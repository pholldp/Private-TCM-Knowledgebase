## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนปัจจุบัน (Conventional Medicine View)
### คำอธิบาย (Description)
Keratitis is an inflammation of the cornea-the clear dome covering the front of the eye. It can be caused by bacterial, viral, fungal, or parasitic infections, or by non-infectious factors like a scratch, contact lens overuse, or severe dry eye. Typical symptoms include eye pain, redness, excessive tearing or discharge, blurred vision, and sensitivity to light. Left untreated, it can lead to corneal scarring and permanent vision loss.
Conventional diagnosis involves a slit-lamp examination to view the cornea, and sometimes a culture or scraping of the eye discharge to identify the specific microorganism. Treatment is urgent and aims to eliminate the infection and reduce inflammation to prevent scarring.

### การรักษาหลัก (Treatments)
Treatment typically involves prescription eye drops-antibiotics for bacterial infections, antivirals for viral keratitis (like herpes simplex), antifungals for fungal cases, and specific antiparasitic agents for Acanthamoeba. Steroid eye drops may be added to calm inflammation, but only after the infection is controlled to avoid worsening it. Severe cases may require oral medications or even corneal transplant if scarring impairs vision. Lubricating drops and pain relievers are also used to manage symptoms.

## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนจีน (Traditional Chinese Medicine View)
### ความเข้าใจเกี่ยวกับการแพทย์แผนจีน (TCM Understanding)
TCM sees keratitis not just as a local eye problem but as a reflection of internal imbalance, often involving the Liver and Lung. The Liver channel opens directly into the eyes, so any Liver disharmony-especially Heat or Fire-can surge upward and inflame the cornea. The Lung governs the body's surface and is vulnerable to external Wind, which can carry Heat into the eyes, triggering an acute attack. This is why a simple cold or a bout of stress can precede a flare-up.
Different patterns produce different symptoms. A sudden onset with redness, burning, tearing, and perhaps a mild fever points to Wind-Heat invading the surface. Intense throbbing pain, a bitter taste, and irritability signal Liver Fire blazing upward.
Thick, sticky yellow discharge and persistent itching suggest Damp-Heat brewing in the Liver channel. Chronic dry, gritty eyes with night sweats and a red, peeled tongue indicate Kidney Yin deficiency with empty heat rising. Recurrent, stabbing pain with a dark purplish tongue points to Blood Stagnation from long-term damage.
The same Western diagnosis of keratitis can have these vastly different roots, which explains why some people respond to antibiotics but keep getting recurrences-the underlying terrain hasn't changed. TCM treatment targets the specific pattern to not only heal the current flare but also reduce future episodes by correcting the constitutional weakness.

### การวินิจฉัยโดยแพทย์แผนจีน (Practitioner Diagnosis)
A TCM practitioner begins by asking how the eye feels-burning, itching, dry, or sharply painful-and what triggered it. The onset, accompanying symptoms, and your overall constitution help narrow down the pattern. They will also examine your tongue and feel your pulse, which provide objective clues about the internal imbalance.
If the keratitis appeared suddenly after exposure to wind or a cold, with redness, burning pain, tearing, and perhaps a mild headache or nasal congestion, it suggests Wind-Heat invading the eyes. The tongue is often red with a thin yellow coat, and the pulse feels floating and rapid, indicating a superficial attack.
Intense redness, severe throbbing pain, extreme light sensitivity, and a bitter taste in the mouth-especially if worsened by stress or anger-point to Liver Fire Blazing upward. The tongue is red with a yellow coat, and the pulse is wiry and rapid, reflecting deep internal heat that needs clearing.
When the eye produces sticky yellow or white discharge and feels persistently itchy, Damp-Heat in the Liver Channel is likely. The tongue coating appears thick and greasy yellow, and the pulse may be slippery and rapid. Digestive heaviness or a feeling of fullness often accompanies this pattern.
In chronic or recurrent keratitis, the eye feels dry and gritty, vision may be blurry, and symptoms worsen with fatigue or at night. This points to Kidney Yin Deficiency With Empty-Heat Blazing. The tongue is red with little or no coating, and the pulse is thin and rapid, signaling a need to nourish yin and cool deficiency fire.
If the pain is fixed and severe, with persistent deep redness and a purplish tongue or dark spots, Blood Stagnation has developed. The pulse may feel choppy. This is usually a later stage or complication of prolonged inflammation, where blood circulation in the eye collaterals is impaired.

## กลุ่มอาการการแพทย์แผนจีนและการรักษา (TCM Patterns and Treatment)

### Wind-Heat
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/wind-heat)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Acute onset of redness and burning pain, Profuse watery tearing, Sensitivity to light (photophobia), Mild fever or chills with headache, Nasal congestion with yellow discharge
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Windy or dusty environments, Spicy, greasy, or fried foods, Alcohol or smoking, Rubbing the eyes, Prolonged screen time or eye strain
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Cool or cold compresses over the eyes, Rest in a dark, quiet room, Chrysanthemum or mint tea, Avoiding wind and bright light
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Keratitis often begins when an external Wind-Heat pathogen invades the body's surface. The eyes are the uppermost portals of the Liver channel and are especially vulnerable to Wind-Heat rising upward. This acute invasion causes the small collaterals around the cornea to become congested and inflamed, producing the hallmark redness, burning pain, and excessive tearing. Because the pathogen is still at the exterior level, you may also experience mild fever, slight aversion to wind, or a headache that accompanies the eye symptoms.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red tip and edges with a thin yellow (or white-to-yellow) coating. Pulse: floating and rapid (浮数 fú shuò), felt easily at the surface.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Wind-Heat is an exterior pathogen that attacks the upper body, so anything that feeds Heat (spicy food, alcohol) or invites more Wind (drafty environments, rubbing) will intensify the redness and burning. Cooling, dispersing measures - like a cool compress, chrysanthemum tea, and resting in a dark room - help release the exterior and clear Heat, which calms the eye's inflamed collaterals and speeds recovery.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Drink plenty of room-temperature water and sip chrysanthemum or mint tea to gently cool the surface. Avoid spicy, fried, or greasy foods, which add internal Heat. Rest your eyes in a dim, clean room and use a cool, damp cloth over closed lids for comfort. Protect your eyes from wind and dust with wrap-around glasses.

### Liver Fire Blazing
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/liver-fire-blazing)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Intense eye redness and burning pain, Bitter taste in the mouth, Irritability and short temper, Throbbing headache at the temples, Sensitivity to light (photophobia)
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Emotional stress, frustration, or anger, Spicy, greasy, or fried foods, Alcohol and coffee, Prolonged screen time or eye strain, Hot, windy weather, Lack of sleep
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Rest in a dark, quiet room, Cool or cold compresses over the eyes, Chrysanthemum tea and cooling foods, Stress reduction and emotional calm
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  The Liver channel opens into the eyes. When intense anger, frustration, or excessive consumption of spicy, greasy foods generates excess Liver Fire, that fire surges upward along the channel, directly attacking the cornea. This causes the intense redness, burning pain, and photophobia characteristic of acute keratitis.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red body with redder sides, dry yellow coating, possible red prickles. Pulse: wiry, rapid, and forceful, especially at the left middle position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Liver Fire flares upward when triggered by emotional stress, especially anger and frustration, or by heating foods and stimulants like alcohol and coffee. These triggers add fuel to the fire, intensifying the surge to the eyes. Conversely, rest, cooling foods, and cold compresses help settle the rising Yang and drain excess Heat, directly counteracting the upward movement of Fire. Stress reduction prevents the initial generation of excess Liver Qi that turns into Fire.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Avoid spicy, greasy, and fried foods, as well as alcohol and coffee, which fuel the fire. Drink chrysanthemum tea or cooling herbal teas, and eat plenty of lightly cooked green vegetables. Manage emotional stress through meditation or gentle exercise like Tai Chi. Rest your eyes frequently, especially if you work on screens, and ensure adequate sleep to allow the Liver to recover.

### Damp-Heat in the Liver Channel
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/damp-heat-in-the-liver-channel)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Thick, sticky yellow or white discharge from the eyes, Itching and a gritty sensation in the eyes, Feeling of heaviness or pressure around the eyes, Bitter taste in the mouth and poor appetite, Redness at the sides of the tongue with a yellow, greasy coating
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Spicy, greasy, or fried foods, Alcohol and heavy sweets, Hot, humid weather, Emotional stress, frustration, or anger
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Cool, light meals (e.g., congee, steamed greens), Keeping the eyes clean with gentle rinsing, Resting in a cool, dry environment
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Dampness and Heat can brew in the Liver and Gallbladder system, often from a diet heavy in greasy or spicy foods, or from lingering external Damp-Heat. This sticky, turbid mixture travels upward along the Liver channel, which opens into the eyes. It settles in the cornea, causing the hallmark thick yellow discharge, itching, and a sensation of grittiness that distinguishes this pattern from dry or simply fiery types of keratitis.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red body, especially at the sides, with a thick, yellow, greasy coating that is heaviest in the center and root. Pulse: wiry, slippery, and rapid.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Damp-Heat in the Liver channel is like a sticky, overheated stew inside the body - rich foods, alcohol, and humidity add more fuel and fluid, making the discharge thicker and the eyes itchier. Cooling, easily digested foods and a calm environment help drain the excess, while frustration and stress stir up Liver Qi, fanning the Heat that rises to the eyes.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Avoid greasy, fried, and heavily spiced foods, as well as alcohol, which all generate Dampness and Heat. Favor light, cooling foods like mung beans, cucumber, and celery. Keep the eye area clean and avoid rubbing, which can worsen irritation.

### Kidney Yin Deficiency With Empty-Heat Blazing
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/kidney-yin-deficiency-with-empty-heat-blazing)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Dry, gritty sensation in the eyes, Blurred vision that worsens with fatigue, Night sweats, Heat in the palms, soles, and chest, Lower back soreness and weakness, Dry throat, especially at night
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Overwork and late nights, Spicy, greasy, or fried foods, Emotional stress, frustration, or anger, Dry, hot weather, Prolonged screen time or eye strain
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Adequate sleep, Cool, humid environment, Moistening foods (pears, goji berries), Gentle eye rest, Hydration with small sips
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When the body's deep Yin reserves run low, the Kidneys lose their ability to cool and moisten the tissues. This creates a deficiency heat that rises upward along the channels to the eyes, drying out the cornea and fueling a slow, smoldering inflammation. Unlike the sudden redness of an acute infection, this type of keratitis feels persistently dry and gritty, and it often flares up after overwork, late nights, or prolonged eye strain.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: Red, thin, dry body with little or no coating, possible cracks. Pulse: Fine (Xi) and Rapid (Shu), weak at the rear (Chi) position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Kidney Yin is the body's cooling and moistening reserve. Overwork, late nights, and stress burn through this reserve, allowing empty-heat to rise and dry out the eyes. Rest and cooling, moistening foods help replenish Yin and calm the heat, while spicy and drying foods deplete it further. A cool, humid environment soothes the dryness and prevents the heat from flaring.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Prioritize rest and avoid overwork, especially late nights, which deplete Yin. Eat moistening foods like pears, black sesame, goji berries, and spinach. Sip warm water throughout the day rather than drinking large amounts at once. Avoid spicy, fried, and overly heating foods that can fan the flames of empty-heat.

### Blood Stagnation
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/blood-stagnation)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Fixed, stabbing eye pain, Pain worse at night, Persistent dark red or purplish eye congestion, Dark purplish tongue with stasis spots, Pain worse with pressure
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Cold environment, Sedentary lifestyle, Emotional stress, frustration, or anger, Prolonged screen time or eye strain, Nighttime
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warm compress, Gentle eye massage, Warm foods and spices, Rest in a dark, quiet room, Light exercise
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When keratitis becomes chronic or recurs repeatedly, the persistent inflammation damages the tiny blood vessels of the eye. Over time, this leads to blood stasis, where blood flow becomes sluggish and stuck in the eye collaterals. The obstructed flow causes severe, fixed, stabbing pain and a dark, purplish congestion that does not easily resolve.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: dark purple body with stasis spots, distended dark sublingual veins, thin white coat. Pulse: choppy (涩 Sè) or wiry (弦 Xián).
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Blood stasis means the blood is not flowing smoothly. Cold and inactivity cause further congealing and stagnation, making the pain worse. Warmth, gentle movement, and massage promote circulation and help break up the stasis. Nighttime is a yin time when blood flow naturally slows, which is why the pain intensifies then; rest in a dark room helps by reducing external stimulation while the body heals.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat warming, blood-moving foods like turmeric, ginger, black fungus, and dark leafy greens. Avoid cold and raw foods that can congeal blood. Gentle exercise like walking or qigong promotes circulation. Use a warm compress over the closed eye to ease pain and encourage local blood flow.

## คำถามที่พบบ่อย (Frequently Asked Questions)
### Can TCM treat viral keratitis like herpes simplex?
Yes. TCM has a long history of managing recurrent viral keratitis. Herbs like Jin Yin Hua and Huang Qin have antiviral properties, and acupuncture can help regulate the immune response. Many patients find that TCM reduces the frequency and severity of flare-ups by strengthening the body's defenses. However, never stop prescribed antiviral medication without consulting your eye doctor.

### Will acupuncture around my eye hurt?
Acupuncture points around the eye, such as Jingming (BL-1) and Zanzhu (BL-2), are needled with extremely fine, single-use needles. Most people feel a mild sensation of pressure or a dull ache rather than sharp pain. The practitioner will ensure you are comfortable, and the brief sensation is usually outweighed by the soothing relief that follows.

### Can I use TCM alongside my prescription eye drops?
Absolutely. Herbal formulas and acupuncture do not interfere with antibiotic, antiviral, or antifungal eye drops. In fact, they may enhance healing and comfort. Always inform both your ophthalmologist and TCM practitioner of all treatments you are using, and never stop prescribed medication without your doctor's guidance.

### How long before I see improvement?
Many patients notice a reduction in redness and pain within the first few days of starting herbal treatment. Acute patterns often improve significantly in a week. Chronic or recurrent conditions may take several weeks to show steady progress, with a full course of treatment lasting 3-6 months to prevent future episodes.

### Does TCM prevent corneal scarring?
By reducing inflammation and promoting healthy blood circulation to the cornea, TCM can help minimize scarring and support clearer healing. Herbs that move blood stasis, such as Dan Shen and Hong Hua, are specifically used in patterns where scarring is a concern. Early treatment is key to the best visual outcome.

### Can dietary changes help my keratitis?
Yes. A diet that reduces internal heat and dampness is essential. Avoid spicy, greasy, and deep-fried foods, as well as alcohol and excessive coffee, which can fan the flames of Liver Fire or create Dampness. Instead, favor cooling and moistening foods like chrysanthemum tea, goji berries, cucumber, pears, and mung bean soup.
