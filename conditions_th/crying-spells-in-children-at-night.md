## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนปัจจุบัน (Conventional Medicine View)
### คำอธิบาย (Description)
In Western medicine, excessive crying in an otherwise healthy infant is often labeled as colic, especially if it follows the "rule of three": crying for more than three hours a day, more than three days a week, for more than three weeks. It typically begins in the first few weeks of life and resolves by three to four months. The exact cause is unknown, but theories include an immature digestive system, gas, overstimulation, or a developing nervous system that struggles to self-soothe.
Diagnosis is clinical, based on the history and a normal physical exam. Night crying that persists beyond infancy may be considered a behavioral sleep issue, night terrors, or a response to teething or illness. Treatment is supportive and rarely involves medication.

### การรักษาหลัก (Treatments)
Conventional management focuses on soothing techniques (rocking, white noise, swaddling, pacifiers), dietary adjustments (for breastfeeding mothers, eliminating dairy or other potential allergens), and sometimes simethicone drops or probiotics to reduce gas. Reassurance that the condition is self-limited is a cornerstone of care. In formula-fed infants, a trial of hypoallergenic formula may be suggested.

## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนจีน (Traditional Chinese Medicine View)
### ความเข้าใจเกี่ยวกับการแพทย์แผนจีน (TCM Understanding)
In TCM, a child's peaceful sleep depends on the Shen (spirit) being calmly anchored in the Heart. Night crying is a sign that something is disturbing the Shen - and that something can be cold, heat, or fright. Because infants' organ systems are still immature, their Spleen and Stomach are easily upset by feeding issues or external temperature, and their Heart Qi is easily shaken by sudden noises or strangers.
When the Shen is unsettled, the child wakes abruptly and cries.
The Spleen is the body's main digestive organ and the source of warmth. When Spleen Yang is deficient, internal cold builds up, causing Qi to stagnate and the abdomen to ache.
At night, Yin energy is strongest and the body's Yang Qi retreats inward, so the cold pain intensifies. The baby cries in a low, whimpering way and is comforted by warmth and gentle pressure on the tummy. This is the Spleen Yang Deficiency pattern.
Alternatively, excess Heat can accumulate in the Heart channel - often from the mother's diet during pregnancy or breastfeeding, or from external warmth. This Heat rises to disturb the Shen, making the child irritable and restless. The cry is loud and persistent, the face is flushed, and the baby may be soothed by a cool, dark room but aggravated by light. This is Heart Fire transmitting to the Small Intestine, a common pattern in colicky infants.
A third pattern arises when a sudden fright - a loud noise, a fall, an unfamiliar face - scatters the still-fragile Heart Qi. The spirit is shaken, and the child wakes with a panicked cry, the face shifting from pale to flushed.
This Heart Qi Deficiency pattern often follows a specific startling event and may recur whenever the child is startled again. TCM treats each of these root causes differently, rather than offering the same remedy for all night crying.

### การวินิจฉัยโดยแพทย์แผนจีน (Practitioner Diagnosis)
A TCM practitioner begins by observing the child and asking the caregiver about the crying. The sound, timing, and what soothes or aggravates the crying are crucial clues. A baby who calms with warmth and gentle belly pressure points toward cold, while one who cries louder when a light is turned on suggests heat. These first impressions guide the diagnostic process.
If the crying is low, intermittent, and the baby curls up, preferring warmth and pressure on the tummy, Spleen Yang Deficiency is likely. Cold in the digestive system causes Qi stagnation and pain that worsens at night. Other signs include cold hands and feet, poor appetite, loose stools, and a pale face. The tongue is pale with a thin white coating, and the pulse feels deep and slow.
When the crying is loud and persistent, especially in a bright room, Heart Fire transmitting to the Small Intestine is suspected. The child appears irritable, with a red face, thirst, dark scanty urine, and constipation. The tongue tip is red with a thin yellow coating, and the pulse is rapid. Excess heat in the Heart channel disturbs the spirit, making the child restless and easily agitated.
Sudden night crying with a startle response points to Heart Qi Deficiency, often triggered by fright. The child seems unsettled, with facial color shifting between pale and bluish. The crying may be high-pitched then low, and the baby is easily startled. The tongue looks normal, but the pulse is rapid. This reflects a temporary depletion of Heart Qi that leaves the Shen (spirit) unanchored.

### Spleen Yang Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/spleen-yang-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Low, intermittent crying, Cold hands and feet, Pale face, Comforted by warmth and pressure on abdomen, Poor appetite, loose stools, Worse at night
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Cold foods or drinks, Cold environment or drafts, Overfeeding, Fright or emotional upset, Late-night feeding with cold milk
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warm compress on tummy, Gentle abdominal massage, Warm room, Breastfeeding on demand, Swaddling
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  The Spleen is the body's main digestive organ and a source of warmth. When Spleen Yang is deficient, internal Cold builds up, causing the Qi to stagnate and the abdomen to ache. At night, Yin energy dominates and the body's Yang Qi is at its weakest, so the cold and pain grow stronger. The baby cries in a low, intermittent way, instinctively seeking warmth and gentle pressure to ease the discomfort.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale, puffy, tender body with teeth marks and a white slippery coating. Pulse: deep, slow, weak.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Spleen Yang Deficiency allows internal Cold to accumulate, causing Qi stagnation and abdominal pain that peaks at night. Cold foods and drafts further damage Yang and worsen the pain, while warmth and gentle massage help move Qi and dispel Cold, easing discomfort. Overfeeding burdens the Spleen, and emotional stress or fright can unsettle the baby's Shen, making crying worse.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Keep the baby's belly warm with a soft belly band or swaddle. Avoid cold foods and drinks; if breastfeeding, the mother should steer clear of raw, cold, or hard-to-digest foods. Gently massage the baby's tummy in a clockwise direction with warm hands. Maintain a calm, warm sleep environment and avoid startling noises.

### Heart Fire transmitting to the Small Intestine
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/heart-fire-transmitting-to-the-small-intestine)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Loud, piercing cry, Irritability and restlessness, Flushed red face, Crying worsens with light, Dark, scanty urine, Thirst for cold drinks
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Bright lights, Spicy or warming foods, Overstimulation or overexertion, Hot weather, Fright or emotional upset
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Cool room temperature, Quiet, dimly lit room, Cooling drinks (if age-appropriate), Gentle rocking or massage, Soothing lullabies
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  In children, the Heart houses the Shen (spirit), and when excess Heat accumulates in the Heart channel, it disturbs the Shen, causing intense restlessness and irritability. This manifests as loud, inconsolable crying at night, especially when the child sees light, because light is a yang stimulus that further agitates the fire. The Heat also travels down to the Small Intestine, leading to dark, scanty, and burning urination, which adds to the child's discomfort and crying.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: Red body with a redder, swollen tip; possible sores on the tongue tip; thin yellow coating. Pulse: Rapid (Shu) and overflowing (Hong), especially at the left Cun (Heart) position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Heart Fire is an excess heat pattern that agitates the Shen (spirit). Bright lights and heat add yang energy, intensifying the fire and making crying worse. Spicy foods and emotional upset also fuel heat. Cooling the environment and offering cooling foods or drinks help drain the excess heat, while dark, quiet settings and gentle touch calm the spirit and allow the child to settle.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Keep the child's sleeping environment cool, dark, and quiet. Avoid overstimulating activities before bedtime, and establish a calming routine. For breastfeeding mothers, avoid spicy, greasy, or excessively warming foods, as these can pass heat to the baby through breast milk. Offer cooling fluids like barley water or plain water if the child is old enough. Gentle massage of the back along the spine can help soothe the nervous system.

### Heart Qi Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/heart-qi-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Sudden crying at night, often after a fright, Startles easily at noises or touch, Face turns pale then flushed during the episode, Fatigue and listlessness during the day, Weak, quiet voice or reluctance to speak
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Sudden loud noises, Fright or emotional upset, Overstimulation or overexertion, Cold environment or drafts
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Quiet, dimly lit room, Gentle, reassuring touch, Predictable bedtime routine, Warm, soft foods
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  In young children the Heart Qi is still fragile. A sudden fright - a loud noise, a fall, or an unfamiliar face - can scatter the Qi and shake the Shen (spirit). When the spirit is unsettled, the child wakes abruptly at night with a panicked cry, and the face may shift from pale to flushed as the Qi struggles to gather itself. Over time, the Heart Qi becomes depleted, leaving the child easily startled, listless during the day, and prone to repeated night-time episodes.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale, puffy, with a thin white coating; a midline crack toward the tip may appear in chronic cases. Pulse: rapid but weak (Empty or Fine), especially at the left Cun position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Fright scatters the Heart Qi and unsettles the Shen, which is already weak in this pattern, so a calm, predictable environment helps the Qi gather and the spirit settle. Warm foods and gentle touch support the body's Yang and reassure the nervous system, while cold and overstimulation drain the fragile Qi further and provoke more startle responses.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Keep the child's environment calm, especially in the evening - dim lights, soft sounds, and a predictable bedtime routine help the Shen settle. Offer warm, easy-to-digest foods like congee with a little red date or goji berry to gently nourish Qi. Avoid overstimulating play right before bed, and respond to night crying with quiet, reassuring touch rather than bright lights or loud voices.

## คำถามที่พบบ่อย (Frequently Asked Questions)
### Is it safe to give Chinese herbs to my baby?
Yes, when prescribed by a qualified TCM practitioner experienced in pediatrics. Infant formulas use gentle, low-dose herbs and avoid any substances considered toxic. The herbs are often prepared as a decoction that can be given by spoon or mixed into a small amount of milk. Never give your child over-the-counter adult herbal products or formulas not specifically prescribed for them.

### How can I tell if my baby's night crying is from cold or heat?
Observe the cry and what soothes it. A cold-pattern cry is low and whimpering; the baby may curl up, have cold hands and feet, and calm down with warmth and gentle belly pressure. A heat-pattern cry is loud and forceful; the baby's face is red, they may seem irritable, and they often cry harder in a bright room or when overly warm. A cool, dark environment tends to help. Your TCM practitioner will also look at the tongue and the vein at the index finger to confirm the diagnosis.

### Can TCM treatment be combined with breastfeeding?
Absolutely. In fact, TCM often treats the breastfeeding mother as well, since what she eats can directly affect the baby. Herbal formulas prescribed for the infant are designed to be safe, and many practitioners will also advise the mother on her own diet to prevent heat or cold from passing through the milk. Always tell your practitioner if you are breastfeeding so they can coordinate care.

### Does my baby need acupuncture?
No. For infants, TCM uses acupressure, gentle massage (pediatric tuina), and herbal medicine - not acupuncture needles. Your practitioner may teach you simple massage techniques to do at home, such as clockwise rubbing around the belly button to warm the Spleen or stroking specific pathways to calm the spirit. These are safe, comforting, and can strengthen the parent-child bond.

### How long until we see improvement?
Many parents notice a difference within the first few days, especially in heat or fright patterns. Cold-deficiency patterns may take a week or two to respond as the digestive warmth is rebuilt. Full resolution typically occurs within two to four weeks of consistent treatment. Your practitioner will adjust the formula as the pattern shifts, so follow-up visits are important.

### What if the crying is accompanied by fever or vomiting?
These are not typical signs of the patterns TCM treats for simple night crying and could indicate an infection or other serious condition. Please see our Safety section for a list of red-flag symptoms that require immediate medical attention rather than TCM care.
