## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนปัจจุบัน (Conventional Medicine View)
### คำอธิบาย (Description)
Western medicine typically diagnoses chronic gastroenteritis as chronic inflammation of the stomach and intestinal lining, often called chronic gastritis or functional dyspepsia. Common symptoms include upper abdominal pain or discomfort, bloating, nausea, early satiety, and changes in bowel habits like diarrhea or constipation. Diagnosis is usually made through endoscopy with biopsy, testing for Helicobacter pylori infection, and ruling out other conditions.

### การรักษาหลัก (Treatments)
Standard treatment focuses on reducing stomach acid with proton pump inhibitors (PPIs) or H2 blockers, eradicating H. pylori with antibiotics if present, and using prokinetic drugs to speed gastric emptying. Antacids may provide temporary relief, and dietary adjustments like avoiding trigger foods are often recommended.

## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนจีน (Traditional Chinese Medicine View)
### ความเข้าใจเกี่ยวกับการแพทย์แผนจีน (TCM Understanding)
In TCM, the digestive system is primarily the domain of the Spleen and Stomach. The Stomach receives food and sends it downward; the Spleen transforms it into Qi and blood. Chronic gastroenteritis arises when this partnership breaks down - often from poor diet, emotional stress, or constitutional weakness. When the Spleen is weak, food sits undigested, causing bloating, fatigue, and loose stools. When the Stomach's downward movement is disrupted, belching, reflux, and nausea appear.
The Liver plays a critical role as the regulator of smooth Qi flow. Emotional stress, frustration, or anger can cause Liver Qi to stagnate and then 'invade' the Stomach, leading to distension, acid reflux, and pain that flares with mood. If the stagnation generates heat, burning epigastric pain and a bitter taste develop. This is why stress is such a powerful trigger - and why calming the Liver is often essential for healing the gut.
Dietary factors create other patterns. Overindulgence in greasy, spicy, or sweet foods can generate Damp-Heat, which clogs the digestive tract with a heavy, sticky sensation, nausea, and a thick yellow tongue coating. Chronic inflammation, on the other hand, can slowly consume the Stomach's Yin fluids, leaving a dry, gnawing ache and a red, peeling tongue. In long-standing cases, poor circulation may lead to Blood Stagnation, causing fixed, stabbing pain and dark stools.
Because these patterns have such different roots, they demand different treatments. A person with Spleen Qi Deficiency needs strengthening and warming; someone with Damp-Heat needs clearing and drying; Liver Qi Stagnation requires smoothing and calming. This is the heart of TCM's approach: not just treating 'chronic gastritis' but treating the specific imbalance that keeps it going.

### การวินิจฉัยโดยแพทย์แผนจีน (Practitioner Diagnosis)
A practitioner begins by asking about the nature and timing of the discomfort. In Spleen and Stomach Qi Deficiency, the hallmark is a dull epigastric fullness and poor appetite, accompanied by general fatigue and loose stools. The tongue is pale and puffy, and the pulse feels weak and thready, reflecting a digestive system too weak to transform food into Qi.
When emotional stress is a clear trigger, attention turns to the Liver. In Liver Qi Stagnation invading the Stomach, the epigastric distension and belching fluctuate with mood, and there may be rib-side tension. The tongue body is often normal with redder edges, and the pulse is wiry, indicating constrained Qi.
If the stagnation persists and generates heat, the picture shifts to Liver Qi Stagnation transforming into Heat. Acid reflux, a bitter taste in the mouth, and a burning epigastric pain become prominent. The tongue is red with a yellow coating, and the pulse is wiry and rapid, showing that fire has flared upward.
A practitioner suspects Damp-Heat in Stomach and Spleen when there is a heavy, stuffy sensation in the upper abdomen, nausea, a sticky bitter taste, and foul-smelling loose stools. The tongue is red with a thick, yellow, greasy coat, and the pulse is slippery and rapid, confirming the accumulation of turbid dampness and heat.
In Stomach Yin Deficiency, the pain is a dry, burning sensation rather than a dull ache. The person complains of constant thirst, a dry mouth, and perhaps a slight feverish feeling. The tongue is red with little or no coating, and the pulse is thin and rapid, signaling that the stomach’s moistening fluids have been depleted.
Long-standing illness may lead to Stomach Blood Stagnation. Here the pain is fixed and stabbing, often worsening at night. The tongue may appear dark purple or show small ecchymosis spots, and the pulse feels choppy or wiry. This pattern indicates that poor circulation has created local blood stasis in the stomach lining.

### Spleen and Stomach Qi Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/spleen-and-stomach-qi-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Poor appetite or early fullness, Bloating that worsens after eating, Loose or unformed stools, Fatigue and heavy limbs, Sallow or pale complexion
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Overeating or large meals, Raw, cold, or iced foods, Overwork and exhaustion, Worry and mental strain
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warm, cooked meals, Small, frequent meals, Rest after eating, Gentle walking or stretching
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  The Spleen is the main engine for turning food into Qi and blood. When it is deficient, food sits in the stomach instead of being transformed, causing bloating and fullness that get worse after eating. The Stomach also loses its ability to send digested food downward, so appetite fades and stools become loose. The whole body feels tired because there simply is not enough Qi being produced to fuel daily life.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale, puffy, and tooth-marked with a thin white coat. Pulse: weak, soft, and especially deficient at the right Guan (middle) position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: The Spleen loves warmth and routine. Cold, raw foods and overeating force it to work harder with already depleted Qi, which worsens bloating and loose stools. Warm, cooked meals and small portions give the Spleen a manageable load it can actually transform. Rest conserves Qi, while gentle movement prevents stagnation without draining energy. Worry and overthinking knot the Spleen Qi, so mental calm is as important as dietary care.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat warm, well-cooked, easily digestible foods like soups, congees, and steamed vegetables. Take small, frequent meals rather than three large ones, and avoid raw, cold, or greasy foods that tax a weak Spleen. Gentle walking after meals helps Qi move, but rest when you are tired - overexertion drains the very Qi you are trying to rebuild.

### Liver Qi Stagnation invading the Stomach
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/liver-qi-stagnation-invading-the-stomach)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Distending pain under the ribs, Frequent belching and acid reflux, Symptoms worsen with stress or anger, Irritability, Sensation of a lump in the throat
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Stress, anger, or frustration, Eating when upset, Irregular meal times, Greasy, fried, or spicy foods, Premenstrual phase
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Emotional calm, Gentle walking or stretching, Warmth on the abdomen, Deep breathing exercises
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When stress, frustration, or unexpressed emotions build up, the Liver's ability to keep Qi moving smoothly becomes impaired. This stuck Liver Qi then invades the Stomach horizontally, disrupting its normal downward movement. As a result, Qi rebels upward, causing bloating, belching, acid reflux, and a distending pain that often radiates to the rib cage. The symptoms tend to come and go with emotional ups and downs.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: Normal or slightly red body, may have redder sides, thin white coating. Pulse: Wiry (xian), especially at the left middle position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Stress and anger are the primary triggers for this pattern because they directly cause Liver Qi to stagnate and attack the Stomach. Calming the mind and engaging in gentle movement help unblock that stuck Qi. Warmth encourages Qi to flow smoothly, while deep breathing helps descend rebellious Stomach Qi. Spicy, greasy foods and irregular eating habits further burden the digestive system and create additional stagnation, making symptoms worse. The premenstrual phase naturally sees an increase in Liver Qi stagnation, which is why symptoms often intensify at that time.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat regular, smaller meals and avoid eating when stressed or upset. Favor lightly cooked, easily digestible foods and limit spicy, greasy, or fried items. Gentle daily movement like walking, stretching, or tai chi helps keep Qi flowing smoothly. Prioritize stress management through breathing exercises or mindfulness.

### Damp-Heat in Stomach and Spleen
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/damp-heat-in-stomach-and-spleen)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Bloating and fullness in the upper abdomen, Bitter taste in the mouth, Sticky, incomplete bowel movements, Heavy, foggy feeling in the head and limbs, Thick, greasy yellow tongue coating
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Greasy, fried, or spicy foods, Alcohol and coffee, Humid or damp weather, Overeating or large meals, Sweet, sugary foods
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Light, easily digestible meals, Dry, cool weather, Gentle walking or stretching, Small, frequent meals
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When the Spleen and Stomach are overwhelmed by dampness and heat-often from a diet heavy in greasy, spicy, or sweet foods-digestion becomes sluggish and inflamed. The dampness creates a heavy, bloated feeling and sticky stools, while the heat adds a bitter taste, bad breath, and a sensation of internal warmth. Over time, this stuck damp-heat irritates the stomach lining and disrupts the normal downward flow of digestion, leading to chronic nausea, poor appetite, and a thick, greasy tongue coating.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red body, possibly swollen with teeth marks, and a thick, yellow, greasy coating (especially at the center and root). Pulse: soggy (Ru) and rapid (Shu), often also slippery (Hua).
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Damp-heat is a sticky, inflammatory combination. Greasy, sweet, and spicy foods directly add dampness and heat to the digestive system, while humid weather invites external dampness to invade. Light, dry, and easily digested foods reduce the burden on the Spleen and Stomach, allowing them to gradually clear the accumulated damp-heat. Gentle movement stimulates the Spleen's function of transporting and transforming fluids, helping to drain dampness.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat light, cooked foods like congee and steamed vegetables. Avoid greasy, fried, spicy, and sugary foods, as well as alcohol. Small, frequent meals are easier on the stomach. Gentle daily movement like walking helps the body process dampness.

### Stomach Yin Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/stomach-yin-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Dull, burning epigastric pain, Dry mouth and throat, thirsty for small sips, Feeling hungry but not wanting to eat, Dry stools or constipation, Red tongue with little or no coating
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Greasy, fried, or spicy foods, Alcohol and coffee, Overwork and exhaustion, Worry and mental strain
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Soft, moist foods (congee, soups), Small, frequent meals, Warm, still water sipped slowly, Rest and early nights
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Chronic inflammation or lingering heat can slowly dry up the Stomach's natural fluids, much like a pot boiling dry. Without enough Yin moisture to protect and nourish the stomach lining, the tissues become irritated and tender, producing a dull, gnawing, or burning ache. The digestive process itself weakens because the Stomach relies on these fluids to break down food, which is why you may feel hungry yet have little desire to eat.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red, thin body, dry centre, little or no coating with possible cracks. Pulse: fine (Xi) and rapid (Shu).
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Stomach Yin is like the cooling, moist lining that protects the stomach wall. Spicy, dry, or heated foods and late nights burn up these fluids, making the burning pain worse. Soft, moist foods and rest help rebuild and conserve that protective moisture, allowing the stomach to heal.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat small, frequent, soft meals like congee, soups, and stewed pears. Avoid dry, crunchy, spicy, or barbecued foods that scrape and heat the stomach. Sip warm water or herbal teas throughout the day rather than gulping large amounts. Go to bed early to protect Yin and avoid overwork or prolonged stress that consumes fluids.

### Stomach Blood Stagnation
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/stomach-blood-stagnation)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Sharp stabbing pain in upper abdomen, Pain fixed in one location, Pain worsens with pressure, Pain worse at night, Dark purple tongue with stasis spots, Black tarry stools
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Stress, anger, or frustration, Raw, cold, or iced foods, Greasy, fried, or spicy foods, Pressure on the stomach, Overeating or large meals
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Rest and early nights, Warmth on the abdomen, Light, easily digestible meals, Gentle walking or stretching
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Long-standing Qi stagnation or deficiency in the digestive system can slow blood flow, leading to blood stasis in the Stomach. This stasis creates a fixed, stabbing pain and disrupts normal digestion. Over time, the stagnant blood may damage blood vessels, causing dark vomit or black tarry stools.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: dark purple body with stasis spots, distended sublingual veins, thin white coat. Pulse: choppy (Se) or wiry (Xian), rough and uneven.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Blood stasis pain is fixed and aggravated by pressure because the stagnant blood obstructs the channels. Rest and warmth help move blood and relax the Stomach. Cold and heavy foods congeal Qi and blood, worsening stasis, while light meals reduce the digestive burden. Stress tightens the Liver Qi, which can further compress the Stomach and worsen pain.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat warm, cooked foods like soups and stews; avoid raw, cold, and greasy items that slow digestion. Gentle movement like walking helps Qi and blood circulation. Manage stress, which can worsen Qi stagnation. Avoid overeating and late-night meals.

### Liver Qi Stagnation that transforms into Heat
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/liver-qi-stagnation-that-transforms-into-heat)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Burning pain behind the breastbone or in the upper belly, Frequent belching and acid reflux, Bitter taste in the mouth, Distending pain under the ribs, Symptoms worsen with stress or anger, Irritability
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Stress, anger, or frustration, Greasy, fried, or spicy foods, Alcohol and coffee
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Emotional calm, Cooling foods and drinks, Gentle walking or stretching
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When frustration or chronic stress knots up the Liver's Qi flow, that stuck energy eventually smolders into Heat. In chronic gastroenteritis, this trapped Heat flares upward through the Stomach channel, causing burning epigastric pain, sour belching, and acid reflux. Meanwhile, the stagnant Liver Qi continues to disrupt the Stomach's downward rhythm, leaving you with bloating, distension, and a bitter taste that gets worse when emotions run high.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red body with redder sides, yellow coating. Pulse: wiry and rapid (弦数 xián shuò).
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Anger and stress directly tighten the Liver Qi, and when that pressure builds, it turns into Heat that rises to burn the Stomach. Spicy and greasy foods add more heat and dampness, making the fire worse. Cooling foods and calming activities help drain the excess heat and relax the tension, allowing the Stomach to settle and the Qi to flow downward again.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Choose cooling, easily digested foods like cucumber, celery, pear, and congee. Strictly limit alcohol, coffee, fried foods, and spicy dishes-they add fuel to the heat. Gentle, regular movement like walking or Tai Chi helps keep Liver Qi flowing. A short wind-down ritual before bed, away from screens, can prevent overnight reflux and restless sleep.

## คำถามที่พบบ่อย (Frequently Asked Questions)
### Can TCM help if I have H. pylori?
Yes. TCM herbs can support the body in clearing H. pylori, especially when combined with conventional antibiotics. Formulas that clear Damp-Heat, such as those containing Huang Lian (Coptis), have antimicrobial properties. However, TCM does not replace antibiotic therapy if your doctor has prescribed it - always complete the full course. The herbal approach also strengthens the digestive system to prevent recurrence.

### Will I need to change my diet completely?
You don't have to overhaul everything overnight, but diet is a cornerstone of TCM treatment. The most important shift is toward warm, cooked foods and away from raw, cold, greasy, and spicy items that burden the Spleen. Small, frequent meals and thorough chewing make a big difference. Your practitioner will guide you on specifics based on your pattern.

### How many acupuncture sessions will I need?
Most treatment plans start with weekly sessions for 8-12 weeks. Acute symptoms often improve after 3-4 sessions, but deeper patterns require consistent treatment to rebuild digestive function. After the initial course, many people shift to maintenance visits every 2-4 weeks, combined with dietary and herbal support.

### Can I take Chinese herbs with my PPI medication?
Generally, yes. There are no known dangerous interactions between common TCM formulas for gastroenteritis and proton pump inhibitors. To be safe, take herbs and medications at least 2 hours apart. Always inform both your doctor and your TCM practitioner about everything you're taking. If you're on blood thinners, special caution is needed because some herbs (like Dang Gui) can affect clotting.

### Why does stress make my stomach worse?
In TCM, the Liver is responsible for the smooth flow of Qi, and emotional stress directly knots up this flow. When Liver Qi stagnates, it often invades the Stomach, disrupting its downward movement and causing bloating, belching, and reflux. This is why acupuncture points on the Liver channel and herbs that soothe the Liver are so effective for stress-related digestive issues.

### Is chronic gastroenteritis curable with TCM?
Many people achieve long-term remission and can manage occasional flare-ups with diet and herbs. For functional dyspepsia and mild chronic gastritis, complete resolution is possible. For deeper structural changes like atrophy, TCM can significantly improve symptoms and quality of life, though ongoing maintenance may be needed. The goal is a comfortable, symptom-free digestive system that stays that way.

### Does acupuncture hurt for stomach issues?
Acupuncture uses hair-thin needles and is generally not painful. You may feel a brief pinch or a dull ache around the point, which is a sign that Qi is being activated. Points on the abdomen and legs are well-tolerated, and most people find sessions deeply relaxing.

## การวิจัยทางวิทยาศาสตร์สมัยใหม่ (Modern Scientific Research)
1. **Efficacy and safety of Chinese herbal medicine for chronic atrophic gastritis: A systematic review and meta-analysis**
   *วารสารวิชาการ: Li S, et al. J Ethnopharmacol. 2022;285:114898.*
   This meta-analysis pooled data from multiple randomized controlled trials and found that Chinese herbal medicine, alone or combined with conventional therapy, significantly improved clinical symptoms, endoscopic findings, and pathological scores in patients with chronic atrophic gastritis. The most frequently used herbs targeted Spleen Qi deficiency and Damp-Heat patterns.

2. **Acupuncture for chronic gastritis: A systematic review of randomized controlled trials**
   *วารสารวิชาการ: Wang Y, et al. Acupunct Med. 2019;37(3):153-162.*
   A systematic review concluded that acupuncture was superior to sham acupuncture and conventional medication in reducing epigastric pain, bloating, and belching. Common points included Zusanli ST-36, Zhongwan REN-12, and Neiguan PC-6. The review noted that the quality of evidence was moderate due to small sample sizes.

3. **Xiang Sha Liu Jun Zi Tang for functional dyspepsia: A randomized, placebo-controlled trial**
   *วารสารวิชาการ: Chen J, et al. Chin J Integr Med. 2020;26(8):589-595.*
   This trial demonstrated that Xiang Sha Liu Jun Zi Tang significantly improved gastric emptying and reduced symptoms of postprandial fullness and early satiety compared to placebo. The formula was well tolerated and particularly effective for patients with a Spleen Qi deficiency pattern.
