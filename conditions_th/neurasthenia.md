## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนปัจจุบัน (Conventional Medicine View)
### คำอธิบาย (Description)
Neurasthenia is a historical diagnosis that describes a state of persistent physical and mental fatigue, often accompanied by irritability, poor concentration, unrefreshing sleep, muscle aches, and emotional fragility. In modern Western medicine, the term is rarely used as a standalone diagnosis; its symptoms are now typically classified under anxiety disorders, depression, or chronic fatigue syndrome. Diagnosis is clinical, based on a patient’s history and the exclusion of other medical conditions such as anemia, thyroid disease, or sleep apnea.

### การรักษาหลัก (Treatments)
Conventional management usually focuses on symptom relief and may include antidepressant or anti‑anxiety medications, cognitive behavioral therapy (CBT), graded exercise therapy, and sleep hygiene counseling. For some patients, stimulants or sleep aids are prescribed short‑term. The approach is largely uniform, treating the collection of symptoms rather than differentiating underlying constitutional types.

## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนจีน (Traditional Chinese Medicine View)
### ความเข้าใจเกี่ยวกับการแพทย์แผนจีน (TCM Understanding)
TCM understands neurasthenia primarily through the Heart, Spleen, Kidneys, and Liver. The Heart houses the Shen (spirit/mind), and when it is well‑nourished by Blood and Yin, the mind is calm and sleep is sound. The Spleen transforms food into the Qi and Blood that feed the Heart; chronic worry, overthinking, or poor diet can drain the Spleen, leaving the Heart undernourished and the mind restless - this produces the classic picture of fatigue with palpitations, poor appetite, and broken sleep.
The Kidneys store the body’s fundamental Yin and Yang. Kidney Yin is the cooling, moistening foundation that anchors the spirit and nourishes the brain (the “Sea of Marrow”). When overwork, late nights, or excessive sexual activity deplete Kidney Yin, the brain loses its nourishment, leading to memory fog, dizziness, and a hot, restless sensation that makes it hard to settle. When Kidney Yang - the body’s warming fire - is exhausted, the entire system loses its drive, resulting in profound cold, mental dullness, and a deep, heavy fatigue that no amount of sleep seems to fix.
Excess patterns are also common. Emotional frustration and unexpressed anger cause Liver Qi to stagnate; this backed‑up energy creates chest distension, irritability, and a tense, wired exhaustion that worsens with stress. When a rich, greasy diet or prolonged Liver stagnation generates Phlegm and Heat, these pathogenic factors rise to harass the Heart, producing restless sleep, a racing mind, and a thick yellow tongue coating. Because the same Western diagnosis can arise from such different roots - deficiency, stagnation, or heat - TCM always treats the person, not the label.

### การวินิจฉัยโดยแพทย์แผนจีน (Practitioner Diagnosis)
When someone comes in with neurasthenia - a mix of fatigue, poor sleep, and emotional fragility - a TCM practitioner first asks about the nature of the tiredness, sleep quality, appetite, and emotional triggers. The tongue and pulse provide crucial confirmation. The patterns fall broadly into deficiency types, where the body lacks Qi, Blood, Yin, or Yang, and excess types, where stagnation or heat disturb the mind.
Two common excess patterns are Liver Qi Stagnation and Phlegm‑Fire harassing the Heart. In Liver stagnation, emotional stress is the clear trigger; the person feels irritable, sighs often, has chest or rib‑side distension, and shows a wiry pulse. In Phlegm‑Fire, the sleep is more restless with vivid nightmares, the tongue has a thick yellow greasy coat, and the pulse is slippery and rapid - often tied to a rich diet or prolonged frustration.
Heart and Spleen Qi and Blood Deficiency is a very common deficiency pattern. Here the fatigue is profound, with poor appetite, pale complexion, and a tendency to worry. Sleep is light and easily broken, with palpitations. The tongue looks pale and puffy with a thin white coat, and the pulse is thin and weak. This pattern points to overthinking depleting both the Spleen's ability to make Blood and the Heart's need for nourishment.
When the Kidneys are involved, the practitioner checks for heat or cold signs. Kidney Yin Deficiency (often with Liver Yin Deficiency) brings dizziness, tinnitus, lower back soreness, night sweats, and a red tongue with little coat; the pulse is thin and rapid. If Liver Yin is also depleted, irritability and dry eyes may appear.
By contrast, Kidney Yang Deficiency shows cold limbs, low libido, mental dullness, and a pale puffy tongue with a deep weak pulse - a picture of depleted warming energy.

## กลุ่มอาการการแพทย์แผนจีนและการรักษา (TCM Patterns and Treatment)

### Heart and Spleen Qi and Blood Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/heart-and-spleen-qi-and-blood-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Palpitations or fluttering chest, Insomnia with excessive dreaming, Poor appetite with bloating after eating, Pale complexion, lips, and nails, Mental and physical fatigue with poor concentration
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Overthinking and worry, Mental overwork, Skipping meals, Cold, raw foods
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Rest and relaxation, Warm, nourishing meals, Gentle walking or tai chi, Quiet, low-stimulation environment
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  The Spleen transforms food into the Qi and Blood that nourish the entire body, while the Heart houses the mind and relies on that nourishment to stay calm. When overthinking and chronic worry drain both organs, the Heart becomes undernourished and the mind grows restless, leading to insomnia, palpitations, and mental fog. At the same time, weakened Spleen Qi fails to produce enough energy, so appetite drops, digestion becomes sluggish, and profound physical tiredness sets in.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale, puffy, teeth-marked body with a thin white coating. Pulse: fine (thin) and weak, especially feeble in the left Cun (Heart) position and soft in the right Guan (Spleen) position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Overthinking and irregular eating directly deplete Spleen Qi and Heart Blood, which are already weak in this pattern, so mental strain and poor diet quickly worsen fatigue and sleeplessness. Rest, warm meals, and gentle movement rebuild the Spleen’s ability to generate Qi and Blood, allowing the Heart to be nourished and the mind to settle.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat warm, cooked meals at regular times; avoid raw, cold, and greasy foods that weaken the Spleen. Include blood-nourishing foods like red dates, longan fruit, and dark leafy greens. Gentle daily movement such as walking or tai chi helps Qi flow without exhausting you. Most importantly, protect your mind: set boundaries around work and worry, and build in quiet time to let your thoughts settle.

### Phlegm-Fire harassing the Heart
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/phlegm-fire-harassing-the-heart)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Mental restlessness and agitation, Insomnia with vivid, disturbing dreams, Chest oppression with thick yellow phlegm, Bitter taste in the mouth, Flushed red face
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Stress, anger, or frustration, Spicy, greasy, or fried foods, Alcohol, Staying up late or insufficient sleep
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Light, bland meals, Cooling herbal teas, Gentle walking or tai chi, Deep breathing and meditation, Quiet, low-stimulation environment
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When the diet is rich, greasy, and spicy, or when emotional stress stagnates, the Spleen and Stomach struggle to transform fluids, leading to Phlegm. Over time, this Phlegm combines with Heat, often from constrained Liver Qi, and rises to disturb the Heart. This is what produces the restless, agitated mind, chest oppression, and a heavy, foggy head that make it impossible to rest.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red body, swollen with a red prickly tip, and a thick yellow greasy coating. Pulse: slippery and rapid, with the left Cun (Heart) position full and overflowing.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Greasy, fried, and spicy foods directly create Phlegm and Heat in the body, which rise to disturb the Heart and cause agitation and chest heaviness. Alcohol and late nights further fuel internal Fire. Stress and anger generate Liver Fire that combines with Phlegm, making the mind race and sleep impossible. Light, cooling foods and gentle exercise help clear Heat and move stagnant Qi, while deep breathing and a calm environment allow the Heart to settle.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Avoid greasy, fried, and spicy foods, as well as alcohol, which all generate Phlegm and Heat. Eat light, cooling foods like pear, radish, mung beans, and leafy greens. Gentle daily movement, such as walking, helps Qi flow and resolves Phlegm. Wind down before bed with deep breathing or meditation to calm the agitated mind.

### Kidney Yin Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/kidney-yin-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Soreness and weakness of the lower back and knees, Five-center heat (palms, soles, chest), Night sweats, Dizziness and tinnitus, Memory loss and poor concentration, Nocturnal emissions or sexual dysfunction
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Overwork and chronic stress, Excessive sexual activity, Spicy, greasy, or fried foods, Staying up late or insufficient sleep
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Adequate rest and sleep, Quiet, low-stimulation environment, Nourishing, moist foods, Gentle walking or tai chi
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Kidney Yin is the body's cooling, nourishing foundation. When it runs low from overwork, chronic stress, or excessive sexual activity, the brain and marrow lose their nourishment, leading to memory fog, dizziness, and poor concentration.
At the same time, the Yin fails to anchor the Heart spirit, and empty heat rises, causing a restless mind, insomnia, and vivid dreams. The lower back and knees, which are the Kidney's domain, feel sore and weak, and the body's fluids dry up, resulting in night sweats, dry mouth, and a feeling of heat in the palms, soles, and chest.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red, thin body with little or no coating, dry surface; possible cracks. Pulse: fine (细 xì) and rapid (数 shuò).
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Kidney Yin is easily depleted by overwork, stress, and excessive sexual activity, which burn through the body's cooling reserves. Spicy and heating foods add more heat, making the empty fire worse. Rest, cooling foods, and a calm environment allow the Yin to rebuild, while gentle movement keeps Qi flowing without draining it further.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Prioritize rest and avoid overwork, especially late nights. Eat cooling, moistening foods like black sesame, walnuts, goji berries, and pears. Avoid spicy, fried, and heating foods that burn up Yin. Gentle movement like tai chi or walking supports Qi flow without exhausting you.

### Liver Qi Stagnation
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/liver-qi-stagnation)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Chest and rib distension, Frequent sighing, Irritability and mood swings, Insomnia that worsens with stress, Feeling of a lump in the throat
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Stress, anger, or frustration, Alcohol and heavy, greasy foods, Prolonged sitting or inactivity
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Deep breathing and meditation, Gentle walking or tai chi, Peppermint or chrysanthemum tea, Talking through frustrations
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Emotional stress, frustration, and unexpressed anger are the primary drivers of Liver Qi Stagnation. When the Liver’s job of keeping Qi flowing smoothly is blocked, Qi backs up in the chest and ribs, causing distension and pressure. Over time, this pent-up Qi can generate heat that rises to disturb the Heart spirit, leading to the mental restlessness, irritability, and insomnia that define neurasthenia.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: normal or slightly dark body, with red or slightly purplish sides and a thin white coat. Pulse: wiry (弦 xián), taut like a guitar string, especially at the left middle position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Stress and frustration directly cause Liver Qi to stagnate, so calming the mind and moving the body help free it. - **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Practice stress management with meditation, gentle yoga, or regular walking. Keep your lower back and feet warm, especially in cold weather. Gentle daily movement such as walking or tai chi helps circulate Yang without exhausting it. Prioritize rest and avoid overwork or staying up late, as these directly drain Kidney Yang.

### How long before I feel less tired?
Most people notice better sleep and a subtle lift in energy within the first 2-3 weeks of treatment. Deep, sustained recovery from deficiency‑type fatigue usually takes 2-6 months, because rebuilding Qi and Blood is like refilling a well - it cannot be rushed. Excess patterns, where the fatigue is more from stagnation and heat, often clear faster.

### Can I combine TCM herbs with my current antidepressant or anti‑anxiety medication?
In most cases, yes, but it must be supervised. Herbs like Suan Zao Ren (sour jujube seed) have mild sedative properties and can enhance the effect of medications that cause drowsiness. Always inform both your prescribing doctor and your TCM practitioner about everything you are taking. Never stop or adjust your prescription medication on your own; if your symptoms improve, work with your doctor to taper gradually.

### Do I need to change my diet?
Diet plays a supporting role. Across all patterns, TCM recommends regular, warm, cooked meals and avoiding greasy, spicy, or overly processed foods that can create Phlegm and Heat. Cold, raw foods and iced drinks can weaken the Spleen and worsen deficiency fatigue. Specific foods - like longan fruit, jujube dates, and bone broths - can be added based on your pattern, but the general rule is to eat simply and consistently.

### Is neurasthenia the same as chronic fatigue syndrome?
There is significant overlap, but they are not identical. Neurasthenia emphasizes mental and emotional exhaustion alongside physical fatigue, often with a prominent sleep disturbance and irritability. In TCM, both conditions are approached by identifying the underlying pattern - so the treatment principles are similar, and many patients with chronic fatigue syndrome find relief through the same pattern‑based methods described here.

### Will the results last after I stop treatment?
When the underlying pattern is corrected, the improvement is lasting. For deficiency patterns, this means the body has genuinely rebuilt its reserves, so you are less vulnerable to relapse. Your practitioner will often recommend a maintenance phase - less frequent acupuncture and a gentle herbal formula - to consolidate the gains and prevent recurrence during stressful periods.
