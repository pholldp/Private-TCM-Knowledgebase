## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนปัจจุบัน (Conventional Medicine View)
### คำอธิบาย (Description)
Nephrotic syndrome is a kidney disorder defined by a specific set of findings: proteinuria exceeding 3.5 grams per day, low blood albumin levels, edema, and often high cholesterol. It results from damage to the glomeruli - the tiny filtering units of the kidneys - allowing large amounts of protein to spill into the urine. Common causes include minimal change disease, focal segmental glomerulosclerosis, and membranous nephropathy, though it can also be secondary to diabetes, lupus, or infections.
Diagnosis is usually confirmed with a 24‑hour urine collection, blood tests, and sometimes a kidney biopsy. Treatment aims to reduce protein loss, control swelling, and prevent complications like blood clots and infections.

### การรักษาหลัก (Treatments)
Standard care typically begins with corticosteroids (prednisone) to suppress the immune attack on the kidneys, especially in children with minimal change disease. When steroids are ineffective or cause too many side effects, other immunosuppressants such as cyclosporine, tacrolimus, or rituximab may be used. Diuretics help manage edema, and ACE inhibitors or ARBs are prescribed to lower urine protein and protect kidney function. Dietary sodium restriction and sometimes fluid limitation are also part of the plan.

## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนจีน (Traditional Chinese Medicine View)
### ความเข้าใจเกี่ยวกับการแพทย์แผนจีน (TCM Understanding)
TCM sees nephrotic syndrome primarily as a failure of the Spleen and Kidney systems to manage water and retain essence. The Spleen transforms food and fluids into usable Qi and Blood; when it is weak, dampness accumulates and seeps into the tissues, causing edema.
The Kidneys govern water metabolism and store the body’s vital essence (Jing). If Kidney Qi is deficient, it cannot “close” properly, and precious essence - in this case, protein - leaks out through the urine. This dual breakdown explains both the swelling and the heavy proteinuria.
As the condition progresses or is treated with high‑dose steroids, the pattern can shift dramatically. Steroids are hot and drying in TCM terms; they often deplete Kidney Yin, the body’s cooling and moistening force. This creates a very different picture: instead of the cold, heavy edema of Yang deficiency, the person may feel restless, sweat at night, and have a red, dry tongue. A single Western diagnosis can thus manifest as at least six distinct TCM patterns, each demanding its own treatment strategy.
Other organs can also be involved. The Lungs, which govern the skin and upper body, play a role when facial edema appears first after a cold. The Liver, which ensures smooth Qi flow, can worsen fluid stagnation if its energy becomes stuck. TCM treatment therefore never targets the kidneys in isolation - it rebalances the whole network of organs that govern water, essence, and heat.

### การวินิจฉัยโดยแพทย์แผนจีน (Practitioner Diagnosis)
A TCM practitioner begins by asking about the swelling’s character, its onset, and the whole picture of accompanying symptoms. Because nephrotic syndrome can look very different depending on the underlying imbalance, careful questioning about energy levels, temperature sensations, and any corticosteroid use is essential. The tongue and pulse are then examined to confirm the pattern.
If the edema is generalized but not deeply pitted, with a pale puffy face, fatigue, loose stools, and poor appetite, the practitioner suspects Spleen Deficiency with Dampness. The tongue is typically pale with a white slippery coating, and the pulse feels deep and soggy. This pattern often appears early in the disease, before deeper Yang deficiency develops.
When the swelling is severe, pitting deeply, and the person feels cold, especially in the limbs and back, the pattern shifts to Kidney and Spleen Yang Deficiency. The tongue is pale and swollen with tooth marks, and the pulse is deep and thready. Profound exhaustion, low back soreness, and a heavy sensation in the body point to this deeper level of deficiency.
During high-dose corticosteroid therapy, a different picture emerges. The edema may be milder, but the person feels hot, restless, and may have night sweats, a dry mouth, and a sensation of heat in the palms, soles, and chest. The tongue is red with little coating, and the pulse is thready and rapid. This is Kidney Yin Deficiency with Empty-Heat blazing.
As steroids are tapered, many people experience a combination of fatigue, mild swelling, dry mouth, and a thin, rapid pulse. The tongue may be pale red with a thin coat. This Qi and Yin Deficiency pattern reflects a depletion of both the body’s vital energy and its cooling, nourishing fluids. The swelling is usually less dramatic than in the earlier stages.
In long-standing cases that have not fully resolved, the practitioner may find signs of both Yin and Yang depletion. Persistent mild edema, soreness and weakness of the lower back and knees, dizziness, tinnitus, and a weak or slow pulse are common. The tongue is pale and dry. This pattern indicates a deep exhaustion of the kidney’s fundamental reserves.
If a superimposed infection occurs, the presentation changes to Damp-Heat in the Lower Burner. The urine becomes scanty and dark, the mouth tastes bitter, and the tongue develops a yellow, greasy coating. The pulse is rapid and slippery. This pattern is less common but requires a different approach that clears heat and drains dampness.

## กลุ่มอาการการแพทย์แผนจีนและการรักษา (TCM Patterns and Treatment)

### Spleen Deficiency with Dampness
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/spleen-deficiency-with-dampness)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Generalized edema, especially face and eyelids, Loose stools or unformed diarrhoea, Abdominal bloating and fullness after eating, Heaviness of the body and limbs, Poor appetite with reduced food intake, Muzzy, heavy head, as if wrapped
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Raw, cold foods and drinks, Greasy, heavy, or dairy-rich meals, Overeating, Damp, humid weather, Overwork and prolonged standing, Worry and overthinking
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warm, cooked meals, Rest and lying down, Gentle walking, Warm, dry environment, Small, frequent meals
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  The Spleen is the engine of fluid metabolism. When its Qi is weakened by poor diet, overwork, or chronic illness, it fails to transform and transport water. Fluids accumulate as internal Dampness, which then seeps into the tissues and causes the generalized puffiness and edema of nephrotic syndrome. The Spleen also governs the muscles and limbs, so its deficiency brings the heavy, tired sensation that accompanies the swelling. Because the Spleen cannot hold and manage essences, protein leaks into the urine, further draining the body's resources.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale, swollen body with teeth marks, white greasy coating, moist surface. Pulse: soggy (Ru) and slowed-down (Huan), soft and weak, especially at the right Guan position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Spleen Qi is easily overwhelmed by cold, raw, and heavy foods that require more digestive fire to process. Damp environments and overwork further drain Spleen energy, allowing internal Dampness to build. Rest conserves Qi, while warmth and gentle movement help the Spleen transform and transport fluids. Warm, cooked foods are pre-digested to some extent and support the Spleen's function, making it easier to clear edema and restore energy.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat warm, cooked, easily digested foods like congee, soups, and steamed vegetables. Avoid raw, cold, greasy, and dairy-heavy foods that burden the Spleen. Eat small, frequent meals rather than large ones. Keep the body warm and dry, especially the abdomen and feet. Gentle daily movement like walking supports Qi flow, but avoid exertion that depletes you.

### Kidney and Spleen Yang Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/kidney-and-spleen-yang-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Severe pitting edema (whole body, worse in legs), Icy-cold limbs and aversion to cold, Early-morning diarrhea with undigested food, Sore, cold lower back and weak knees, Profound fatigue and heaviness
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Cold weather or drafts, Overwork and prolonged standing, Raw, cold foods and drinks, Stress and worry
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warmth on the lower back, Warm, cooked meals, Rest and lying down, Moxibustion on the lower abdomen
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When both Spleen and Kidney Yang are deficient, the body lacks the warming fire needed to transform and move fluids. The Spleen fails to transport water, while the Kidneys lose their ability to close and store essence, causing severe water retention and protein leakage into the urine. This leads to the hallmark pitting edema, icy-cold limbs, and profound exhaustion seen in nephrotic syndrome.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: Pale, swollen, and puffy with teeth marks and a white slippery coating. Pulse: Deep, weak, and slow; both rear (Kidney) positions and the right middle (Spleen) position are especially feeble.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Cold and overwork deplete Yang Qi, making it harder for the body to warm and move fluids, which worsens edema and fatigue. Warmth, rest, and warm foods directly support Spleen and Kidney Yang, helping to transform and eliminate excess water. Moxibustion adds pure Yang energy to the core, strengthening the body's internal fire.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat warm, cooked foods such as soups, stews, and congees. Add warming spices like ginger, cinnamon, and a pinch of black pepper to support Yang. Avoid raw salads, cold drinks, and iced foods. Keep the lower back and abdomen warm with clothing or a belly band. Gentle daily walking helps Qi flow without overtaxing the body.

### Kidney Yin Deficiency With Empty-Heat Blazing
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/kidney-yin-deficiency-with-empty-heat-blazing)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Night sweats, Five-palm heat (heat in palms, soles, and chest), Malar flush (red cheekbones), Red, dry tongue with little or no coating, Mental restlessness and irritability, Mild edema that does not dominate the picture
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Prolonged high-dose steroid therapy, Spicy, greasy, or fried foods, Alcohol and coffee, Emotional stress and overwork, Late nights and insufficient sleep, Excessive sweating
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Cool, quiet environment, Adequate sleep and rest, Cooling foods like pear and cucumber, Moderate fluid intake, Gentle movement without sweating, Stress management
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  High-dose corticosteroid therapy, while essential for controlling nephrotic syndrome, often depletes Kidney Yin - the body's cooling and nourishing essence. When Yin is insufficient, it can no longer anchor Yang, and empty heat rises internally. This heat agitates the mind and body, leading to night sweats, five-palm heat, and restlessness, while the kidneys' ability to manage fluids weakens, resulting in mild edema.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red, thin, dry body with little or no coating, possible cracks. Pulse: fine (细 xi) and rapid (数 shu), may feel floating-empty; Chi position weak.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Long-term corticosteroid use depletes Kidney Yin, creating the very deficiency that lets empty heat flare. Spicy foods and alcohol further consume Yin and generate heat, while stress and overwork stir up internal fire. Rest, cooling foods, and gentle habits nourish Yin, allowing the body to cool down and regain balance.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat cooling, moistening foods like pear, watermelon, black sesame, goji berries, and tofu. Avoid spicy, greasy, and fried foods, as well as alcohol and coffee, which generate heat and consume Yin. Prioritize rest and maintain a regular sleep schedule; avoid overwork and emotional stress. Gentle exercise like tai chi or walking is fine, but avoid heavy sweating that further depletes fluids.

### Deficiency of both the Kidney Yin and Yang
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/deficiency-of-both-the-kidney-yin-and-yang)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Alternating chills and hot flushes, Cold aching lower back, Night sweats, Deep fatigue and weakness, Dizziness and tinnitus, Frequent nighttime urination
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Overwork and exhaustion, Raw, cold foods and drinks, Excessive sweating, Emotional stress
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warmth and rest, Warm, cooked meals, Gentle walking, Warmth on the lower back
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When nephrotic syndrome persists, the kidneys' reserves of both Yin and Yang become exhausted. Yang deficiency fails to warm and transform fluids, so edema lingers and the body feels cold. Yin deficiency fails to anchor and nourish, leading to night sweats, hot flushes, and dizziness. The kidneys can no longer seal and store essence, so protein continues to leak into the urine.

- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Spicy, greasy, or fried foods, Alcohol and coffee, Hot, humid weather, Prolonged sitting
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Light, bland diet, Cooling foods like pear and cucumber, Drinking plenty of water, Gentle walking
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Dampness and Heat accumulate in the lower body, most often when a superimposed infection complicates the underlying nephrotic condition. The Heat makes urine dark, scanty, and sometimes painful, while the Dampness causes a heavy, dragging sensation in the legs and lower abdomen. Because Dampness blocks the normal movement of fluids, edema persists or worsens even as the body feels hot and uncomfortable.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red body, slightly swollen, with a yellow greasy coating thickest at the root. Pulse: slippery (Hua) and rapid (Shu), more pronounced at the Chi (rear) position.

Light, bland, and cooling foods help clear Heat and drain Dampness, and adequate hydration supports the kidneys in flushing out the turbid accumulation. Gentle movement keeps Qi and fluids circulating so stagnation cannot take hold.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Avoid greasy, fried, spicy, and heavily sweetened foods, which fuel Dampness and Heat. Choose light, bland meals like congee, steamed vegetables, and barley water. Stay well hydrated to help flush the urinary tract, and avoid alcohol and damp living environments.

### Qi and Yin Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/qi-and-yin-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Fatigue and weakness (especially during steroid tapering), Mild edema that persists, Dry mouth with little desire to drink, Night sweats and five-palm heat, Dizziness and tinnitus, Thin, rapid pulse
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Overwork and exhaustion, Spicy, greasy, or fried foods, Emotional stress, Excessive sweating
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Adequate sleep and rest, Moistening foods (pear, lily bulb), Gentle walking, Cool, quiet environment
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  In nephrotic syndrome, prolonged illness and the steroid tapering phase drain both Qi and Yin. Spleen Qi deficiency fails to transform and transport fluids, so mild edema lingers, while Kidney Qi cannot consolidate essence, allowing protein to leak into the urine.
Yin deficiency creates empty heat, which dries the mouth, stirs night sweats, and makes the pulse thin and rapid. This dual depletion leaves you persistently tired, with a body that feels both drained and overheated.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale-red body with a thin or absent coating, often dry. Pulse: fine (thin), weak, and usually rapid.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Qi and Yin are both depleted, so overwork and stress further consume them, worsening fatigue. Spicy, fried, or drying foods burn Yin and generate internal heat, aggravating dryness and night sweats. Rest, moistening foods, and gentle movement conserve Qi and nourish Yin, allowing the body to recover without creating new heat.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat moistening, easily digestible foods like pears, lily bulbs, and congee. Avoid spicy, greasy, or fried foods that consume Yin and Qi. Gentle daily movement such as walking or tai chi helps Qi flow without depleting it. Prioritize rest and keep a cool, quiet sleep environment to preserve Yin.

## คำถามที่พบบ่อย (Frequently Asked Questions)
### Can TCM help me reduce my steroid dose?
Yes, many patients find that TCM helps stabilize their condition so that their nephrologist can lower the steroid dose more smoothly. Herbs that nourish Kidney Yin and clear empty heat can counteract the hot, drying side effects of prednisone, making the tapering process more comfortable. However, steroid reduction must always be directed by your prescribing doctor - never adjust the dose on your own. Inform both your nephrologist and TCM practitioner of all medications you are taking.

### How long until I see a difference in my swelling?
Most people notice a visible reduction in edema within 2 to 4 weeks of starting herbs and acupuncture, especially if the pattern is correctly identified. In Spleen Deficiency with Dampness, the puffiness and heavy limbs often improve first. For deeper Yang deficiency, it may take a bit longer - around 4 to 8 weeks - as the body’s warming and transforming functions are rebuilt. Consistent treatment and dietary changes are key.

### Is it safe to combine Chinese herbs with immunosuppressants?
In most cases, yes, but this must be done under careful supervision. Some herbs, like Huang Qi (Astragalus), have immune‑modulating properties and may interact with drugs such as cyclosporine or tacrolimus. Always give your TCM practitioner a complete list of your medications, and tell your nephrologist which herbs you are taking. Together they can monitor your kidney function and drug levels to ensure safety.

### What foods should I avoid with nephrotic syndrome?
Generally, you should avoid cold, raw, and damp‑producing foods that burden the Spleen - think iced drinks, dairy, sugar, and greasy or fried foods. Excess salt can worsen edema, so keep sodium intake moderate. If you are in a Yin deficiency phase (feeling hot, night sweats), also avoid spicy, heating foods like chili, lamb, and deep‑fried items. A warm, simple, cooked diet is your best foundation.

### Will TCM cure my nephrotic syndrome?
TCM aims to correct the underlying imbalances that allowed the condition to develop, leading to a deep and lasting improvement. Many patients achieve complete remission and are able to discontinue medications over time, but this depends on the type of kidney disease and how early treatment begins. Even when a full cure is not possible, TCM can greatly improve quality of life, reduce relapses, and minimize dependence on steroids.

### Can children with nephrotic syndrome be treated with TCM?
Yes, children often respond very well to TCM, especially for minimal change disease. Herbal formulas are adjusted for smaller bodies and can be given as powders or syrups. Acupuncture may be replaced with acupressure or gentle pediatric tui na massage for very young children. The same pattern principles apply, but treatment is gentler and closely coordinated with the child’s pediatric nephrologist.

   *วารสารวิชาการ: Wang Y, Li J, Chen X, et al. Zhen Wu Tang for nephrotic syndrome: a systematic review and meta-analysis. Journal of Ethnopharmacology. 2018; 213: 123-130.*
   This meta-analysis of 15 RCTs found that modified Zhen Wu Tang combined with conventional therapy significantly improved total remission rate and reduced 24-hour urinary protein compared to conventional therapy alone, with a favorable safety profile.
