## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนปัจจุบัน (Conventional Medicine View)
### คำอธิบาย (Description)
In Western medicine, postoperative pain is an expected acute pain resulting from tissue damage during surgery. It is caused by the activation of pain receptors from incisions, inflammation, and nerve injury. The pain is typically managed with medications such as opioids, nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs (NSAIDs), and local anesthetics, often following a multimodal analgesia protocol. While the pain usually subsides as the wound heals, some patients develop chronic postsurgical pain that persists beyond the normal healing period.

### การรักษาหลัก (Treatments)
Standard management includes around-the-clock dosing of acetaminophen and NSAIDs, with opioids reserved for breakthrough pain. Regional nerve blocks, epidural analgesia, and patient-controlled analgesia pumps are common in the immediate postoperative period.
For persistent pain, gabapentinoids or antidepressants may be added. The goal is to control pain enough to allow early mobilization and prevent complications, but side effects like nausea, constipation, drowsiness, and the risk of opioid dependency are significant concerns.

## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนจีน (Traditional Chinese Medicine View)
### ความเข้าใจเกี่ยวกับการแพทย์แผนจีน (TCM Understanding)
Surgery, from a TCM perspective, is a form of physical trauma that directly cuts through the body's network of channels (Jing Luo) and damages the local tissues. This disruption creates a dual problem: first, the flow of Qi and Blood is physically blocked at the incision, leading to stagnation – a pattern of Qi and Blood Stagnation. At the same time, the surgery causes a significant loss of Qi and Blood, leaving the body depleted and vulnerable to deficiency patterns like Qi and Blood Deficiency or Empty-Cold.
Depending on the individual's constitution and the surgical environment, other pathogenic factors such as Damp-Heat or Phlegm can invade the weakened channels, further complicating recovery. This is why TCM does not treat all postoperative pain the same way; the specific pattern of disharmony determines the treatment approach.

### การวินิจฉัยโดยแพทย์แผนจีน (Practitioner Diagnosis)
A TCM practitioner starts by asking about the quality of the pain - is it sharp and stabbing, or dull and achy? When did it begin, and what makes it feel better or worse? The timing after surgery and the accompanying signs like fatigue or a sense of cold are the first clues that point toward one pattern rather than another.
If the pain is fixed, severe, and stabbing with a sensation of fullness, Qi and Blood Stagnation is the most likely pattern, especially in the early days. The tongue may look dark or have purple spots, and the pulse feels wiry or choppy. This tells the practitioner that Qi and Blood are stuck at the surgical site.
When the pain shifts to a dull, lingering ache that worsens with activity or tiredness, and the person feels weak and looks pale, Qi and Blood Deficiency is indicated. The tongue is pale and thin, and the pulse is weak and thready. This suggests the surgery has drained the body’s vital resources.
If cold makes the pain worse and warmth brings relief, and the person has cold hands and feet, Empty-Cold is likely. The tongue is pale with a white coating, and the pulse is deep and slow or weak. This pattern often appears in those with a constitution that tends toward cold.
A burning quality to the pain, with the area feeling hot or looking red, points to Damp Heat in the channels. The person may feel restless, have a bitter taste, and show a yellow, greasy tongue coating. The pulse is rapid and slippery, suggesting inflammation or infection.
When the pain feels heavy and fixed, as if something is pressing, and the tongue coating is thick and greasy with a slippery pulse, Phlegm obstructing the channels is the diagnosis. Numbness or swelling may also be present, especially in those prone to fluid retention.

## กลุ่มอาการการแพทย์แผนจีนและการรักษา (TCM Patterns and Treatment)

### Qi And Blood Stagnation
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/qi-and-blood-stagnation)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Fixed, stabbing pain that worsens with pressure, Dark purplish complexion or lips, Irritability and emotional tension, Tongue with purple spots and distended sublingual veins, Pulse that feels wiry and rough
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Emotional stress, Cold environments or drafts, Overexertion, Cold, greasy foods
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Gentle movement, Warmth applied to the area, Rest, Warm, light meals
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Surgery cuts through the channels and tissues, creating a physical blockage that disrupts the smooth flow of Qi and Blood. Qi cannot move freely, so it stagnates, and Blood pools and congeals into stasis at the site of the wound. This stuck, knotted energy is what produces the fixed, stabbing pain that worsens with pressure, because any touch further obstructs an already blocked channel.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: dark purple body with stasis spots and distended sublingual veins. Pulse: wiry (弦 xián) and choppy (涩 sè), like a taut guitar string scraping bamboo.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Stress and emotional tension cause Qi to stagnate further, intensifying the blockage. Cold congeals the Blood, making stagnation worse and pain sharper. Gentle movement and warmth encourage Qi and Blood to flow, while rest allows the body to heal without additional strain. Light, warm meals support the Spleen's ability to produce healthy Blood, preventing further stagnation.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat warm, easily digestible foods like soups and stews. Include small amounts of turmeric, ginger, and black pepper to gently promote Blood flow. Avoid cold, raw foods and heavy, greasy meals that can worsen stagnation. Gentle walking as soon as medically safe helps Qi move without straining the wound.

### Qi and Blood Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/qi-and-blood-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Dull, lingering ache (not sharp or fixed), Worse with exertion, better with rest, Fatigue, weakness, and shortness of breath, Pale complexion, lips, and nail beds, Poor appetite
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Overexertion, Skipping meals or irregular eating, Cold, raw foods and drinks, Emotional stress, Cold environments or drafts
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Rest, Warm, light meals, Gentle movement, Warmth applied to the area
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Surgery causes significant loss of Qi and Blood, leaving the body depleted. Without enough Qi and Blood to nourish the channels and tissues, the healing site becomes undernourished, resulting in a dull, lingering ache that feels better with rest and worse after any exertion. This is classic "deficiency pain" - the body simply lacks the resources to repair itself properly.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: Pale body, may be thin or teeth-marked, thin white coating. Pulse: Fine (thin) and weak, especially in the right Guan position (Spleen/Stomach).
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Rest and sleep allow the body to conserve and rebuild Qi and Blood, which are the very substances depleted by surgery. Warm, nourishing foods support the Spleen and Stomach to transform food into Qi and Blood, directly addressing the root deficiency. Overexertion drains Qi further, while cold and raw foods damage the Spleen's digestive fire, impairing the production of new Blood. Emotional stress consumes Qi and disturbs the Spleen, worsening fatigue and pain.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Focus on warm, easily digestible foods like congee, bone broth, stewed meats, and cooked vegetables. Include small amounts of dates, goji berries, and black sesame to gently nourish Blood. Avoid cold, raw foods and icy drinks, which weaken the Spleen. Prioritize sleep and gentle walking; avoid pushing through fatigue.

### Empty-Cold
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/empty-cold)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Pain is dull, lingering, and deep rather than sharp or stabbing, Pain clearly worsens with cold and improves with warmth, Cold hands and feet, and a general feeling of cold, Fatigue, pale face, and lack of thirst
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Cold environments or drafts, Cold, raw foods and drinks, Overexertion, Damp, cold environments
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warmth applied to the area, Warm, light meals, Rest, Warm, layered clothing
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Surgery depletes the body's Qi and Blood, leaving it vulnerable to internal Cold. Without enough warming Yang energy, the channels and tissues contract, causing a deep, lingering pain that feels worse when you are cold or in cold weather.
This is not the sharp, fixed pain of blood stasis, but a dull ache that radiates from deep inside. The pain is often accompanied by cold hands and feet, a pale face, and a strong desire for warmth.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale, puffy, and tender body with teeth marks, thin white moist coating. Pulse: deep, slow, and weak.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Empty-Cold is a deficiency of warming Yang. Cold constricts the channels and slows Qi and Blood flow, making the pain worse. Warmth invigorates Yang, relaxes the channels, and eases pain. Rest conserves Qi, allowing the body's weakened Yang to recover and gradually dispel the internal Cold.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat warm, cooked foods like soups, stews, and congees. Avoid raw, cold, or frozen foods and drinks, which can worsen the internal cold. Keep the body warm, especially the surgical area, with a gentle heat pack if comfortable. Rest well, but incorporate gentle daily movement like slow walking to encourage Qi and Blood flow without exhausting yourself.

### Painful Obstruction due to Damp Heat in Channels
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/painful-obstruction-due-to-damp-heat-in-channels)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Burning, hot pain around the incision or joints, Redness and swelling that feels warm to the touch, Pain worsens with warmth or hot compresses, Pain improves with cold applications, Heavy, aching sensation in the limbs, Thirst with desire to drink cool fluids, Irritability and restlessness, Dark, scanty urine
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Hot, humid weather, Spicy or greasy food, Alcohol, Overexertion, Warm compresses or heating pads, Emotional stress
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Cold compresses, Eating bland, cooling foods, Rest, Gentle movement, Keeping the wound dry
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  After surgery, the body's channels are temporarily open and vulnerable. If Dampness and Heat-either from a preexisting internal imbalance or from an external source like a hot, humid environment-invade the surgical site, they can become trapped in the local channels and joints. This Damp-Heat obstruction prevents Qi and Blood from flowing smoothly, creating a burning, swollen, and heavy kind of pain. The Heat makes the area feel hot and look red, while the Dampness adds a sensation of heaviness and swelling.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: Red body, possibly slightly swollen, with a yellow greasy coating. Pulse: Slippery and rapid.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Damp-Heat is a combination of pathogenic moisture and heat. Heat aggravates the condition by inflaming tissues and making pain worse, while Dampness makes the body feel heavy and swollen. Cold compresses and cooling foods directly counteract the Heat, and dry, cool environments prevent Dampness from accumulating. Spicy, greasy, and alcoholic foods generate more internal Damp-Heat, while overexertion and hot weather introduce external Damp-Heat, worsening symptoms.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Avoid greasy, fried, spicy, and sweet foods that create Dampness and Heat. Focus on light, cooling foods like mung beans, barley, cucumber, and bitter greens. Keep the surgical wound clean and dry, and avoid hot, humid environments. Gentle movement can prevent stagnation, but avoid sweating excessively.

### Phlegm in the Channels joints and muscles
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/phlegm-in-the-channels-joints-and-muscles)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Fixed, stabbing pain at the surgical site, Heavy sensation or stubborn numbness in the limbs, Firm swelling or hard nodules near the wound, Thick, white, greasy coating on the tongue, Excessive phlegm or mucus in the throat
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Greasy, heavy, or fried foods, Cold, raw foods and drinks, Damp or humid weather, Prolonged sitting or immobility
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Gentle movement, Warm, dry environment, Warm, light meals, Rest
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Surgery damages the tissues and disrupts the normal flow of Qi and Blood. When the body struggles to process the byproducts of this trauma, fluids can stagnate and congeal into Phlegm - a thick, turbid substance that lodges in the channels around the surgical site. This Phlegm obstruction causes a heavy, fixed, stabbing pain and can create firm swelling or even hard nodules under the skin.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: purple body with stasis spots and a white, greasy coating; distended sublingual veins. Pulse: wiry and choppy (弦涩, Xián Sè) or wiry and slippery (弦滑, Xián Huá).
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Phlegm is a thick, sticky byproduct that forms when fluids stagnate. Greasy and cold foods create more Dampness, which thickens into Phlegm, while a damp environment and inactivity allow it to settle and block the channels. Gentle movement, warmth, and light meals all help keep Qi and fluids flowing, which prevents Phlegm from accumulating and eases the heavy, fixed pain.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Avoid cold, raw, and greasy foods (like dairy, fried items, and ice cream) which create Dampness and Phlegm. Eat lightly and favor warm, cooked meals with aromatic herbs like ginger and cardamom. Gentle, daily movement - such as slow walking - helps Qi circulate and prevents Phlegm from settling, but avoid overexertion while the wound heals.

## คำถามที่พบบ่อย (Frequently Asked Questions)
### Can TCM help with pain immediately after surgery?
Yes, acupuncture and Chinese herbal medicine can be introduced very early in the recovery process, often within hours or days after surgery, provided the patient is stable and the surgeon agrees. Acupuncture points are selected away from the surgical site to avoid any risk of infection. Herbal formulas can help reduce inflammation, move stagnant Blood, and manage pain, potentially lowering the need for strong painkillers.

### Is acupuncture safe near a surgical wound?
Absolutely. TCM practitioners are trained to avoid needling directly into or near a fresh surgical wound to prevent infection. Instead, they use powerful distal points on the arms, legs, and ears that influence the affected area through the channel system. This approach is both safe and effective for managing postoperative pain.

### Can I take Chinese herbs with my pain medication?
In most cases, yes, but you must inform both your surgeon and your TCM practitioner about everything you are taking. Certain herbs like Dang Gui (当归) and Chuan Xiong (川芎) have mild blood-thinning properties and could interact with anticoagulants like warfarin or antiplatelet drugs like aspirin. Your TCM practitioner will select a safe formula, and your medical team can monitor you appropriately.

### Will TCM interfere with my wound healing?
Not at all - in fact, TCM aims to promote healing. Herbs like Huang Qi (黄芪) are used to generate new tissue, while Dang Gui (当归) nourishes Blood to support repair. By improving circulation and resolving stagnation, TCM treatments can help reduce swelling and bring fresh nutrients to the wound, often leading to smoother, faster recovery.

### How long will I need TCM treatment for postoperative pain?
The duration depends on your pattern and the extent of the surgery. Acute stagnation pain often improves within a week. Deficiency or Cold patterns may require ongoing treatment for 3-6 weeks to rebuild the body's reserves. Some patients continue with a maintenance program to prevent the pain from becoming chronic and to restore full vitality.

### Does acupuncture hurt?
Acupuncture needles are extremely fine - much thinner than a typical injection needle. Most people feel a mild sensation, like a dull ache or a brief tingling, but not sharp pain. Many patients find acupuncture deeply relaxing and even fall asleep during treatment. Any discomfort is minimal and temporary.

1. **Effects of Traditional Chinese Medicine-Based Nursing Techniques on Postoperative Wound Pain in Patients Undergoing Open Thoracic Surgery** ([ลิงก์](https://www.scirp.org/journal/paperinformation?paperid=150466))
   *วารสารวิชาการ: Wang, Y., et al. (2023). Effects of Traditional Chinese Medicine-Based Nursing Techniques on Postoperative Wound Pain in Patients Undergoing Open Thoracic Surgery. Open Journal of Nursing, 13, 123-130.*
   This study evaluated a topical herbal patch containing Yan Hu Suo (Corydalis), Bai Zhi (Angelica dahurica), and Chuan Xiong (Ligusticum) applied at Tanzhong CV-17, Neiguan PC-6, and Zusanli ST-36 after thoracic surgery. Patients receiving the patch experienced significantly lower wound pain scores and required less opioid medication compared to controls, with no notable adverse effects.
