## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนปัจจุบัน (Conventional Medicine View)
### คำอธิบาย (Description)
In conventional medicine, nausea after eating is a common symptom that can stem from many conditions - gastroparesis (delayed stomach emptying), gastroesophageal reflux disease (GERD), functional dyspepsia, food intolerances, gallbladder disease, or even anxiety. Diagnosis often involves a careful history, and may include endoscopy, gastric emptying studies, or ultrasound to rule out structural problems.
Treatment typically targets the suspected cause, but in many cases the exact mechanism remains unclear, and management focuses on symptom relief rather than addressing why the stomach reacts this way in the first place.

### การรักษาหลัก (Treatments)
Standard approaches include antacids, proton pump inhibitors, prokinetic agents (to speed stomach emptying), and antiemetics. Dietary changes - smaller meals, avoiding trigger foods - are also recommended. When anxiety is a factor, anti-anxiety medications or therapy may be suggested.

## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนจีน (Traditional Chinese Medicine View)
### ความเข้าใจเกี่ยวกับการแพทย์แผนจีน (TCM Understanding)
TCM views nausea after eating as a sign that Stomach Qi is rebelling upward instead of descending. The Stomach is meant to send food downward; when its normal downward movement is disrupted, Qi rises and triggers nausea. The key question is what is disrupting that downward flow - and that answer points to the specific pattern.
The Stomach doesn't work alone. It partners with the Spleen to transform food into Qi and Blood, and the Liver ensures the smooth flow of Qi throughout the digestive system. When the Liver is stressed or stagnant, it can attack the Stomach and force Qi upward. When the Spleen is weak, it fails to transport fluids, leading to Phlegm-Fluids that obstruct the Stomach. And when the Stomach's own Yang (warming fire) or Yin (moistening fluids) is deficient, it simply can't do its job.
That's why the same Western diagnosis - nausea after eating - can have six different TCM causes. A person who vomits sour, undigested food after a heavy meal has Food Stagnation. Someone who feels nauseated after even a small, bland meal and craves warmth likely has Stomach Yang Deficiency with Cold. If stress reliably brings on the nausea with belching and rib-side tension, the Liver is invading the Stomach. Each pattern requires a completely different treatment strategy.

### การวินิจฉัยโดยแพทย์แผนจีน (Practitioner Diagnosis)
A TCM practitioner begins by asking about the timing and nature of the nausea. Is it immediate after eating or delayed? What does the vomit look like? These details, along with tongue and pulse, help pinpoint the underlying pattern.
If the nausea strikes right after a heavy or greasy meal and you feel a sour taste and uncomfortable fullness, it suggests Food Stagnation in the Stomach. The tongue coating will be thick and greasy, and the pulse will feel slippery, like a rolling bead under the fingers.
When even a small, bland meal triggers nausea and you vomit clear, watery fluid, the digestive fire is weak and cold is present. This Stomach Yang Deficient and Cold pattern shows a pale tongue with a thin white coating and a slow, weak pulse.
Nausea that flares with emotional stress, accompanied by belching and a distended feeling along the ribcage, points to Liver Qi Stagnation invading the Stomach. The tongue edges may be red, and the pulse feels wiry, like a guitar string.
If you experience dry retching after eating, with a parched mouth and a red tongue that looks smooth with little coating, the Stomach lacks the fluids to anchor Qi downward. This Stomach Yin Deficiency pattern often comes with a thin, rapid pulse.
A history of chronic digestive weakness plus nausea with watery phlegm and a heavy sensation in the chest suggests Phlegm-Fluids in the Stomach. The tongue coating is white and greasy, and the pulse is slippery.
Sudden, acute nausea and vomiting after eating, especially following exposure to cold weather or cold foods, along with chills and a thin white tongue coating, indicates Cold invading the Stomach. This pattern often has a tight pulse.

## กลุ่มอาการการแพทย์แผนจีนและการรักษา (TCM Patterns and Treatment)

### Food Stagnation in the Stomach
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/food-stagnation-in-the-stomach)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Sour, rotten-smelling belching and acid regurgitation, Epigastric fullness and bloating that worsens after eating, Aversion to food and its smell, Thick greasy tongue coating, especially in the centre, Nausea relieved after vomiting
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Overeating, Greasy, fried, or rich foods, Eating quickly, Lying down immediately after eating
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Eating smaller, simpler meals, Gentle walking or movement after eating, Fasting for a meal or two, Warm, bland, easily digestible foods, Light abdominal massage
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When you overeat or consume heavy, greasy foods, the Stomach cannot break them down fast enough. The undigested mass sits and ferments, creating turbid Qi that rebels upward instead of descending. This rising Qi directly triggers nausea, sour belching, and a feeling of uncomfortable fullness.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: normal body, thick greasy or curd-like coating, thickest in the centre. Pulse: slippery (滑 huá), right middle (guan) position forceful.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Overeating and heavy foods overwhelm the Stomach's digestive capacity, creating stagnation that turns into turbid Qi-the direct cause of the nausea. Lying down prevents Qi from descending, worsening the upward rebellion. Smaller meals, gentle movement, and warm congee support the Stomach's natural downward flow and are easy to process, giving the digestive system a chance to clear the backlog.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat smaller, simpler meals and chew food thoroughly. Avoid greasy, fried, and raw foods until the nausea settles. A short, gentle walk after eating helps the Stomach Qi descend. Warm, easily digestible foods like rice congee are ideal while recovering.

### Stomach Yang Deficient and Cold
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/stomach-yang-deficient-and-cold)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Vomiting of clear watery fluid after meals, Dull cold pain in upper belly that improves with warmth, Cold hands and feet, Strong preference for hot food and drinks, Loose stools
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Cold or raw foods, Iced drinks, Overeating, Cold weather, Overwork and fatigue
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warm food and drinks, Ginger tea, Warm compress on abdomen, Small, frequent meals, Rest after eating
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Your Stomach relies on a warm digestive fire (Yang) to break down food and send it downward. When that fire is weak and cold prevails, even a small meal can overwhelm the Stomach's ability to process it. The undigested food sits and stagnates, forcing Stomach Qi to rebel upward instead of descending, which you feel as nausea after eating.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale, puffy, tender body with teeth marks; white slippery coating. Pulse: deep, slow, weak, especially at the right Guan (Stomach) position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: This pattern is rooted in a lack of digestive warmth (Stomach Yang). Cold foods and environments further quench that fire, making the Stomach unable to transform food, so Qi rebels upward as nausea. Warmth, whether from food, ginger, or external heat, restores the Stomach's transforming function and allows Qi to descend normally.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat warm, cooked foods like soups, stews, and congee; avoid raw, cold, and iced items completely. Sip fresh ginger tea before meals to ignite the digestive fire. Eat small, frequent meals so you never overburden a weak Stomach. Keep your abdomen warm with a hot water bottle or a belly band, especially in cold weather.

### Liver Qi Stagnation invading the Stomach
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/liver-qi-stagnation-invading-the-stomach)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Nausea that worsens with emotional upset, Belching or acid reflux, Distending pain or fullness under the ribs, Irritability or moodiness, Frequent sighing
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Stress and frustration, Greasy, fried, or rich foods, Cold foods and drinks, Eating in a rush or while angry
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Calm, unhurried meals, Gentle walking or movement after eating, Warm, bland, easily digestible foods, Emotional release (talking, journaling)
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  The Liver is responsible for the smooth flow of Qi throughout the body. When emotional stress or frustration builds up, Liver Qi becomes stuck and then rebels sideways, attacking the Stomach. This disrupts the Stomach's natural downward movement, causing Qi to rise up as nausea, especially after eating when the Stomach is already working hard.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: normal or slightly red body, often with redder sides, and a thin white coat. Pulse: wiry (弦 xián), like a taut guitar string, most noticeable at the left middle position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Emotional stress is the root cause of Liver Qi stagnation, so calming the mind and avoiding anger directly address the pattern. Cold and greasy foods demand more digestive effort, making it harder for the already compromised Stomach to descend Qi. Gentle movement and warm meals encourage a smooth flow of Liver Qi and support the Stomach's downward function, helping to prevent nausea.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat smaller, regular meals in a calm environment. Gentle movement like walking or yoga helps keep Qi flowing. Avoid eating when upset, and consider warm, lightly cooked foods over cold, raw, or greasy dishes.

### Stomach Yin Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/stomach-yin-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Dry retching or nausea after eating, Dull burning discomfort in the stomach, Thirst with a preference for small sips, Dry mouth and throat, Feeling hungry but not wanting to eat, Dry stools or constipation
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Spicy, fried, or dry foods, Overeating, Late-night eating, Overwork and fatigue, Alcohol and coffee
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Small, frequent meals, Warm fluids in small sips, Rest after eating, Moistening foods (pears, congee)
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  The Stomach depends on Yin, its cooling and moistening fluids, to keep Qi moving downward. When Yin runs low, the Stomach becomes dry and under-lubricated, and Qi rebels upward instead of descending. Eating adds a new workload to a parched environment, so the body reacts with dry retching, nausea, and a burning sensation rather than comfortable digestion.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red, thin body, dry in the centre, with little or no coating and possible cracks. Pulse: fine (Xi) and rapid (Shu).
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Stomach Yin is the body's digestive coolant. Spicy, fried, and dry foods, as well as alcohol and coffee, generate heat and consume those precious fluids, making the Stomach even drier and more rebellious. Stress and overwork also drain Yin over time. In contrast, small, moist meals and gentle rest allow the Stomach to rebuild its Yin reserves without being overloaded, while sipping fluids steadily replenishes moisture without shocking the system.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Focus on moist, easily digested foods like congee, steamed pears, and well-cooked vegetables. Avoid spicy, fried, and dry foods that consume Yin. Sip warm water or mild herbal teas throughout the day rather than drinking large amounts at once, and try to eat small, frequent meals to avoid overwhelming a fragile Stomach.

### Phlegm-Fluids in the Stomach and Small intestine
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/phlegm-fluids-in-the-stomach-and-small-intestine)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Splashing water sound when the abdomen is tapped or during movement, Vomiting of thin, clear, watery fluid or phlegm, No thirst, or thirst without desire to drink, Dizziness and a heavy sensation in the body
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Cold or raw foods, Overeating or greasy meals, Cold weather, Sedentary lifestyle
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warm, bland, easily digestible foods, Ginger tea, Gentle walking or movement after eating, Warm compress on abdomen
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When Spleen Yang has been weak for a long time, it fails to transform fluids, and they accumulate as Phlegm-Fluids in the middle burner. These stagnant fluids physically obstruct the Stomach's normal downward movement. After eating, the Stomach tries to push food and Qi downward but the path is blocked, so Qi rebels upward, producing nausea and often vomiting of thin, watery phlegm.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale, swollen body with teeth marks and a white, slippery coating that is excessively moist. Pulse: deep, wiry, and slippery, especially noticeable in the right Guan position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Phlegm-Fluids are a Yin pathogen born from Spleen Yang deficiency and cold. Cold and raw foods directly injure Spleen Yang, making the fluids thicker and more obstructive. Warmth, both in food and on the abdomen, rekindles the digestive fire and helps vaporize the accumulated dampness. Gentle movement encourages Qi flow, which is essential for fluid metabolism, while a sedentary lifestyle allows dampness to settle and congeal further.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Focus on warm, cooked, easily digestible foods like congee, soups, and stewed vegetables. Avoid cold, raw, and greasy foods, as well as dairy and excessive liquids, which can worsen fluid accumulation. Eat small, frequent meals and take a gentle walk afterward to support Qi movement. Keep the abdomen warm with a hot water bottle or warm clothing.

### Cold invading the Stomach
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/cold-invading-the-stomach)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Sudden cramping stomach pain, Nausea or vomiting of clear watery fluid, Pain relieved by warmth, worsened by cold, Feeling of cold in the stomach area
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Iced drinks, Cold or raw foods, Cold weather, Eating quickly
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warm compress on abdomen, Ginger tea, Rest in a warm room, Warm, bland, easily digestible foods
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When external Cold invades the Stomach, it congeals the Stomach's Qi and freezes its normal downward movement. This trapped Qi then rebels upward, causing sudden nausea after eating.
The Stomach is a warm organ that dislikes cold. A sudden chill from cold weather, iced drinks, or raw food contracts the Stomach muscles, leading to cramping pain and a tight sensation. Warmth relaxes the contraction and helps Qi descend again, which is why heat brings relief.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale body with a moist, thin white slippery coating. Pulse: tight (Jin) and possibly slow.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Cold is a contracting, slowing force that freezes Stomach Qi, making it rebel upward as nausea. Warmth relaxes the Stomach and restores its downward movement. Cold foods and environments reinforce the invasion, while warm foods and external heat directly counteract the pathogenic Cold.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat warm, cooked foods and avoid raw, cold, or iced items. Sip warm ginger tea or plain hot water. Keep your abdomen warm with a hot water bottle or warm clothing, especially in cold weather. Avoid eating in a cold environment.

## คำถามที่พบบ่อย (Frequently Asked Questions)
### How quickly can acupuncture relieve nausea after eating?
Many people feel some relief after the first session, especially if the nausea is acute or stress-related. Acupuncture at points like Neiguan (PC-6) and Zusanli (ST-36) can quickly calm rebellious Stomach Qi. For chronic patterns, consistent weekly sessions over 4-8 weeks build lasting change.

### Can I take Chinese herbs with my antacid or anti-nausea medication?
In most cases, yes - but always inform both your TCM practitioner and your doctor about everything you're taking. Some herbs may influence stomach acid or motility, so your practitioner may adjust the formula to avoid overlap. Never stop prescribed medication abruptly without medical guidance.

### Is nausea after eating always a Stomach problem?
Not entirely. While the Stomach is the main organ involved, the Liver and Spleen play big roles. Stress-induced nausea often stems from Liver Qi attacking the Stomach, and chronic watery nausea with bloating points to Spleen weakness creating Phlegm-Fluids. A TCM diagnosis looks at the whole picture.

### What foods should I avoid if I get nausea after meals?
Generally, avoid cold, raw, greasy, and overly spicy foods - they tax the Stomach's digestive fire. Eat warm, cooked meals in small, frequent portions. Ginger tea can help settle the Stomach across many patterns. For pattern-specific advice, see the dietary guidance in each pattern section.

### Can stress really cause nausea after eating?
Yes - in TCM, emotional stress directly disrupts Liver Qi, which then invades the Stomach and forces Qi upward. This is one of the most common patterns we see. Treatment focuses on soothing the Liver and harmonizing the Stomach, often with formulas like Xiao Chai Hu Tang combined with Zuo Jin Wan.

### I get nausea after even a small meal - why?
This often indicates a deficiency pattern. If you also feel cold and crave warmth, it's likely Stomach Yang Deficiency. If you have a dry mouth and burning sensation, it may be Stomach Yin Deficiency. In either case, the Stomach lacks the energy or fluids to process food properly, so even a light meal triggers rebellion of Qi.

### Are there acupuncture points I can press at home for nausea?
Yes - Neiguan (PC-6), on the inner forearm about three finger-widths above the wrist crease, is famous for calming nausea. Apply steady pressure with your thumb for 1-2 minutes. Zusanli (ST-36), below the knee on the outer leg, can also support digestion. These are safe for most people, but see a practitioner for a full diagnosis.

## การวิจัยทางวิทยาศาสตร์สมัยใหม่ (Modern Scientific Research)
1. **Acupuncture for functional dyspepsia** ([ลิงก์](https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD008487.pub2))
   *วารสารวิชาการ: Lan L, Zeng F, Liu GJ, et al. Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews. 2017; Issue 7. Art. No.: CD008487.*
   This Cochrane systematic review included 14 RCTs and found that acupuncture improved symptoms of functional dyspepsia, including postprandial fullness and nausea, compared to sham acupuncture. The quality of evidence was moderate.

2. **Chinese herbal medicine for functional dyspepsia: a systematic review and meta-analysis**
   *วารสารวิชาการ: Hu J, et al. Evidence-Based Complementary and Alternative Medicine. 2019.*
   This meta-analysis of 22 RCTs concluded that Chinese herbal medicine significantly improved global dyspepsia symptoms, including postprandial nausea, compared to placebo or prokinetic drugs, with a favorable safety profile.

3. **Clinical observation on Bao He Wan for functional dyspepsia of food stagnation type**
   *วารสารวิชาการ: Li X, Zhang Y, Wang H. Journal of Traditional Chinese Medicine. 2018;38(4):567-571.*
   In this RCT of 120 patients with postprandial distress syndrome, Bao He Wan significantly reduced nausea, bloating, and early satiety compared to domperidone, with fewer side effects.
