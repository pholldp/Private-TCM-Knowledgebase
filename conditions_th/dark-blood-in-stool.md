## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนปัจจุบัน (Conventional Medicine View)
### คำอธิบาย (Description)
In Western medicine, dark or tarry stool (melena) usually signals bleeding from the upper digestive tract - the esophagus, stomach, or upper small intestine. Blood turns dark as it travels through the intestines and is partially digested. Common causes include peptic ulcers, gastritis, esophageal varices, or the use of certain medications like NSAIDs. Diagnosis typically involves endoscopy, colonoscopy, or stool tests to locate the source and rule out serious conditions such as cancer.

### การรักษาหลัก (Treatments)
Conventional treatment focuses on stopping acute bleeding and healing the underlying lesion. This may include proton pump inhibitors, H2 blockers, or antibiotics if H. pylori is present. In more severe cases, endoscopic procedures can cauterize or clip bleeding vessels. Iron supplements and dietary changes are often recommended to address anemia from chronic blood loss. If a structural cause is found, surgery may be necessary.

## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนจีน (Traditional Chinese Medicine View)
### ความเข้าใจเกี่ยวกับการแพทย์แผนจีน (TCM Understanding)
In TCM, blood is held in the vessels by the Spleen's Qi. Think of the Spleen as the body's 'container' for blood - when its Qi is strong, blood stays put. When Spleen Qi is weakened by overwork, poor diet, or chronic illness, it can no longer hold blood in, and bleeding occurs. Because this blood moves slowly through the digestive tract, it turns dark by the time it leaves the body. This pattern of Spleen not controlling Blood is the most common TCM explanation for dark stool and comes with deep fatigue, a pale puffy tongue, and a weak pulse.
But Spleen weakness isn't the only cause. If Damp-Heat accumulates in the Large Intestine - from greasy, spicy food or alcohol - heat scorches the intestinal vessels and forces blood out. Here the stool is often dark and tarry but accompanied by anal burning, a sticky mouth taste, and a greasy yellow tongue coating. In colder patterns, Spleen Yang Deficiency leads to internal cold that freezes and stagnates blood, causing dark, almost black stool with cold limbs and a deep slow pulse.
Long-term bleeding also drains the body's Blood reserves. Blood Deficiency then makes the vessels even more fragile, creating a cycle where bleeding leads to more bleeding. The tongue becomes pale and thin, and dizziness and a washed-out complexion appear. Each of these patterns requires a different treatment strategy - tonifying Qi, clearing Heat, warming Yang, or nourishing Blood - which is why TCM doesn't offer a one-size-fits-all solution.

### การวินิจฉัยโดยแพทย์แผนจีน (Practitioner Diagnosis)
A TCM practitioner begins by asking about the color and consistency of the blood, when it appears, and what you feel in your body. Dark blood that arrives after the stool points to bleeding higher in the digestive tract, and the accompanying signs-energy level, temperature sensations, appetite, and stool form-help separate one pattern from another.
If the bleeding is chronic, with dull-dark blood and overwhelming fatigue, a pale face, and a weak appetite, the picture strongly suggests Spleen not controlling Blood. The tongue is typically pale and puffy with a thin coat, and the pulse feels thready and weak, confirming that Qi is too depleted to hold blood inside the vessels.
When the stool is dark and tarry but there is a sense of anal burning, a sticky taste in the mouth, and a greasy yellow tongue coating, the cause shifts to Damp-Heat in the Large Intestine. Here the tongue is red with a thick yellow coat, and the pulse is rapid and slippery-signs that heat and moisture are scorching the intestinal lining and forcing blood out.
Less common patterns each carry their own clues. Spleen Yang Deficiency brings dark or black stool with cold hands and feet, a deep-slow pulse, and a pale, wet tongue. Spleen Deficiency with Dampness produces loose, dark stools with a dull blood color and a swollen tongue with a sticky white coat. Blood Deficiency, often after prolonged bleeding, leaves you dizzy with a very pale, thin tongue and a weak pulse, showing that the body has lost the nourishment needed to contain its own blood.

## กลุ่มอาการการแพทย์แผนจีนและการรักษา (TCM Patterns and Treatment)

### Spleen not controlling Blood
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/spleen-not-controlling-blood)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Dark, tarry stools, Overwhelming fatigue and weakness, Pale or sallow complexion, Poor appetite and loose stools, Easy bruising or prolonged bleeding from minor cuts
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Overwork and exhaustion, Raw, cold, or greasy foods, Excessive worry or pensiveness, Damp or cold environments
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Rest and lying down, Warm, cooked, easily digestible meals, Warmth on the abdomen
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  The Spleen is responsible for keeping blood inside the vessels. When Spleen Qi is too weak, it can no longer hold the blood in, allowing it to seep into the digestive tract. Because the blood moves slowly through the stomach and intestines before leaving the body, it turns dark or tarry. This is a chronic, low-grade loss of blood that drains your energy and leaves you pale and exhausted.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: Pale, puffy, and tender body with teeth marks and a thin white coating. Pulse: Fine (thin) and weak, especially at the right middle (Guan) position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: The Spleen governs digestion and blood containment. Overwork, worry, and cold foods deplete its Qi, making it harder to hold blood in. Rest, warmth, and easily digested foods conserve Spleen Qi, allowing it to regain its grip on the blood.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat warm, cooked meals like soups, stews, and congees to ease digestion. Avoid raw salads, iced drinks, and greasy foods, which weaken the Spleen. Gentle daily movement and adequate rest help rebuild Qi and stop the bleeding.

### Damp-Heat in the Large Intestine
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/damp-heat-in-the-large-intestine)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Dark, tarry stools with a foul smell, Burning sensation at the anus during and after bowel movements, Urgent, frequent diarrhoea with a feeling of incomplete evacuation, Thirst and scanty, dark urine, Thick, yellow, greasy tongue coating
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Spicy, greasy, or fried foods, Alcohol, Hot, humid weather, Emotional stress and anger, Overeating or irregular meals
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Eating light, bland meals, Cooling foods like cucumber and mung beans, Staying well hydrated, Gentle walking or exercise, Rest and avoiding overexertion
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Damp-Heat accumulates in the Large Intestine, where the heat scorches the blood vessels and the dampness creates stagnation. This forces blood out of the vessels, leading to dark, tarry stools that are often mixed with foul-smelling mucus. The heat also produces a burning sensation at the anus and an urgent need to defecate.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red body with a thick, yellow, greasy coating, thickest at the centre and root. Pulse: slippery (滑 huá) and rapid (数 shuò).
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: <p>Damp-Heat is a combination of pathological moisture and heat in the gut. Spicy, greasy, and alcoholic foods directly add heat and dampness, making the condition worse. Hot, humid weather also reinforces the damp-heat environment. Cooling, bland foods and hydration help clear heat and drain dampness, while gentle exercise moves Qi and helps the body transform and eliminate dampness. Rest prevents overtaxing the Spleen, which is already struggling to manage fluids.</p>
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Focus on cooling, damp-draining foods like cucumber, celery, mung beans, and barley. Avoid spicy, greasy, fried foods, alcohol, and coffee. Eat smaller, lighter meals and stay hydrated. Gentle exercise like walking or yoga helps move Qi and transform dampness. Manage stress, as emotional upset can worsen heat.

### Spleen Yang Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/spleen-yang-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Dark, tarry stools, Dull abdominal pain relieved by warmth and pressure, Cold hands and feet, feeling chilly easily, Loose, watery stools with undigested food, Poor appetite, bloating after eating
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Cold, raw foods and iced drinks, Overeating or heavy, greasy meals, Cold, damp weather, Excessive physical fatigue
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warmth on the abdomen, Warm, cooked, easily digestible meals, Resting after meals, Ginger or cinnamon tea
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  The Spleen's Yang energy is like a warming fire that cooks food and holds blood inside the vessels. When that fire runs low, cold settles into the middle burner, and the Spleen can no longer keep blood contained. Blood then oozes slowly into the intestines, where the sluggish, cold flow turns it dark or tarry before it appears in the stool.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale, puffy and tender with teeth marks, covered by a white slippery coating. Pulse: deep, slow and weak.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Cold and raw foods directly damage Spleen Yang, making the middle burner even colder and weaker, so blood leaks out more. Warmth and easily digested cooked foods act like adding fuel to a low fire, helping the Spleen recover its holding and warming functions. Rest conserves Qi, while overexertion drains it further.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat warm, cooked foods like congee, soups and stews. Add gentle warming spices such as ginger, cinnamon and a pinch of black pepper. Avoid raw, cold and iced foods completely. Keep your abdomen warm with a belly wrap or hot water bottle, and get gentle daily movement to support Qi flow without exhausting yourself.

### Spleen Deficiency with Dampness
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/spleen-deficiency-with-dampness)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Loose, unformed stools with dull dark blood, Abdominal bloating and fullness after eating, Heaviness in the body and limbs, Greasy or sticky sensation in the mouth, Pale, swollen tongue with teeth marks and a white greasy coating
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Cold, raw foods, Dairy and sweets, Damp, humid weather, Sedentary lifestyle, Overeating
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warm, cooked, easily digestible meals, Gentle walking or exercise, Dry, warm environment, Resting after meals
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  The Spleen is meant to transform and transport fluids. When Spleen Qi is weak, fluids stagnate and turn into dampness, which then sinks downward into the intestines. This heavy, turbid dampness obstructs the middle burner and damages the delicate intestinal vessels, causing blood to ooze out. The blood appears dark and dull - not bright red - because it is not driven by heat, and it mixes with loose, damp stools.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale, swollen body with teeth marks, white greasy coating, moist surface. Pulse: soggy (Ru) and slow (Huan), possibly slippery.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Cold and raw foods further weaken the Spleen’s transforming ability, increasing dampness, while warm, cooked foods support digestion and dampness transformation. Greasy and sweet foods directly generate dampness. Movement helps Qi circulate and dry dampness, whereas sitting still allows dampness to accumulate. Rest after meals conserves Spleen Qi for digestion.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat warm, cooked foods like soups and congees, and avoid raw, cold, and greasy items that create dampness. Incorporate barley, adzuki beans, and lightly cooked ginger to support the Spleen. Gentle daily movement such as walking helps Qi move and transform dampness; avoid sitting for long periods.

### Blood Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/blood-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Dark, tarry or persistent dark blood in stool, Dizziness and blurred vision, Pale face, lips, and nail beds, Heart palpitations, Scanty menstrual flow (in women) with pale blood
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Overwork and exhaustion, Heavy menstrual bleeding, Skipping meals or poor nutrition, Prolonged standing or physical strain
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Adequate rest and sleep, Warm, cooked, easily digestible meals, Iron-rich foods (e.g., spinach, red dates), Gentle walking or exercise
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When the body's Blood is chronically insufficient, the vessels lose their nourishment and integrity. The remaining Blood fails to anchor and contain itself, allowing it to ooze out of the intestinal vessels. This results in persistent, dark blood that appears after the stool, often accompanied by dizziness, pale complexion, and a thin pulse.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: Pale, thin body with thin white coating. Pulse: Fine (细 xì) and weak.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Blood Deficiency arises when the body's blood reserves are depleted, often from chronic bleeding or poor production. Rest and nutrient-dense foods help rebuild blood, while overwork and further blood loss drain it further. Warm, cooked foods support the Spleen's ability to transform nutrients into Blood.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Focus on nutrient-dense, warm, cooked foods that build Blood: red dates (jujube), black sesame, dark leafy greens, and small amounts of organ meats if tolerated. Avoid raw, cold, and overly spicy foods that can weaken digestion. Prioritize rest and avoid overexertion, as exhaustion depletes Blood further.

## คำถามที่พบบ่อย (Frequently Asked Questions)
### What does dark blood in stool mean in TCM?
Dark blood that appears after the stool - called 'distant bleeding' - tells us the bleeding is happening higher in the digestive tract, and the blood has had time to change color. In TCM, this usually points to a weakness in the Spleen's ability to hold blood inside the vessels, or to Damp-Heat scorching the intestinal lining. The exact meaning depends on the full picture of your symptoms, tongue, and pulse.

### Can TCM stop digestive tract bleeding?
Yes, TCM can be very effective at stopping chronic, low-grade bleeding by addressing its root cause. Herbal formulas like Gui Pi Tang tonify Spleen Qi to contain blood, while others like Huai Hua San clear Damp-Heat to cool the blood. However, acute, heavy bleeding is a medical emergency - seek hospital care immediately if you see large amounts of blood or feel faint.

### How long does it take to see improvement?
Many patients notice less blood in the stool within 2-4 weeks, especially if Damp-Heat is the cause. For deficiency patterns where the body's energy needs rebuilding, consistent improvement usually appears after 6-12 weeks. Full resolution and prevention of recurrence may take several months, particularly if Blood deficiency is involved.

### Can I take TCM herbs while on iron supplements or PPIs?
In most cases, yes. Herbal formulas can be taken alongside iron supplements and acid-reducing medications. However, always inform both your TCM practitioner and your medical doctor about everything you are taking. Some herbs that strongly move blood may interact with anticoagulants, so full disclosure is essential.

### What foods should I avoid if I have dark stool?
Generally, avoid raw, cold, and greasy foods that weaken the Spleen, as well as spicy and heating foods that can aggravate Damp-Heat. Alcohol and coffee should be minimized. Focus on warm, cooked, easily digestible meals like congee, steamed vegetables, and soups. Your TCM practitioner will give you specific advice based on your pattern.

### Is acupuncture used for this condition?
Acupuncture is a helpful support, especially for strengthening Spleen Qi and clearing Damp-Heat. Points like Zusanli ST-36 and Sanyinjiao SP-6 are used to tonify the Spleen and stop bleeding. However, herbal medicine is usually the primary treatment for internal bleeding, with acupuncture as a complementary therapy.
