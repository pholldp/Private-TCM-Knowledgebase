## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนปัจจุบัน (Conventional Medicine View)
### คำอธิบาย (Description)
Age spots, also called solar lentigines or liver spots, are harmless, flat patches of darkened skin that appear most often on the face, hands, shoulders, and arms - areas that get the most sun exposure over a lifetime. They are caused by an overproduction of melanin as skin ages and is repeatedly exposed to ultraviolet light. A doctor usually diagnoses them just by looking; they are benign and don't require treatment, but many people seek to fade them for cosmetic reasons.

### การรักษาหลัก (Treatments)
Conventional options focus on lightening or removing the visible spots. Common approaches include topical creams with hydroquinone, retinoids, or mild steroids; procedures like cryotherapy (freezing), laser therapy, intense pulsed light (IPL), and chemical peels. These can fade existing spots but do not prevent new ones from forming, and sun protection is always recommended.

## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนจีน (Traditional Chinese Medicine View)
### ความเข้าใจเกี่ยวกับการแพทย์แผนจีน (TCM Understanding)
In TCM, the face is a mirror of the internal organs. Age spots are understood as an outward sign of deeper changes - most often a decline in Kidney and Liver Yin, sluggish Blood circulation, or the accumulation of Dampness and Phlegm. As we age, the body's cooling, moistening reserves (Yin) naturally thin out. When the skin loses this nourishment, it becomes dry and prone to pigmentation. At the same time, Qi and Blood can become stagnant, especially if stress or a sedentary lifestyle is present, leaving metabolic waste trapped in the skin.
The Spleen also plays a key role. It transforms food into Qi and Blood and manages fluid metabolism. If the Spleen weakens - often from poor diet or overwork - it fails to transport fluids, and Dampness and Phlegm build up. This turbid substance can block the tiny channels in the face, creating thick, raised spots. So, a single Western diagnosis of age spots can stem from several TCM patterns: a dry, deep-brown spot often signals Yin deficiency, while a dark, purplish spot points to Blood stasis, and a thick, greasy-looking spot suggests Damp-Phlegm.
Because age spots develop slowly, TCM sees them as a chronic imbalance. Treatment doesn't just aim to fade the mark - it seeks to restore the organ systems that keep skin healthy. That means nourishing Yin, moving Blood, or resolving Phlegm, depending on the pattern. The spots themselves become a helpful diagnostic clue: their color, texture, and the symptoms that accompany them tell the practitioner exactly where the imbalance lies.

### การวินิจฉัยโดยแพทย์แผนจีน (Practitioner Diagnosis)
A practitioner first looks at the color and texture of the spots and asks about heat sensations. If the spots are deep brown and you often feel hot palms, night sweats, or a dry mouth, the picture points toward a Yin deficiency pattern. Within that group, prominent heat signs like severe night sweats, flushing, and a red tongue with little coating suggest Kidney Yin Deficiency with Empty-Heat Blazing.
When dizziness, dry eyes, lower back soreness, and milder heat signs like occasional night sweats are the main complaints, it leans more toward a combined Kidney and Liver Yin Deficiency. This pattern is very common as both organ systems decline with age, and the skin loses its moisture and luster.
If the spots are distinctly dark purple or have a fixed, stubborn quality, the practitioner suspects stagnation. When chest tightness, mood swings, or a stressful period clearly preceded the spots, it points to Qi and Blood Stagnation - the Liver Qi is stuck, and blood cannot move freely. If emotional triggers are absent but the tongue shows purple spots and the pulse feels choppy, the diagnosis shifts to pure Blood Stagnation, where sluggish circulation itself is the main driver.
A very different story emerges when the spots are pale and light brown, and the person feels drained, looks pale, and has a weak pulse. That signals Qi and Blood Deficiency, where the face simply isn't being nourished enough. This pattern is less common but important to recognize, because tonifying herbs would be needed rather than moving or cooling ones.
Lastly, a practitioner checks for signs of dampness and phlegm. Spots that look thick, raised, or greasy, together with a heavy body, sticky stool, and a thick tongue coating, point to Damp-Phlegm. Here the Spleen is too weak to manage fluids, and the resulting turbidity clogs the skin's channels, often mixing with stasis to create stubborn patches.

### Kidney and Liver Yin Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/kidney-and-liver-yin-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Deep-brown spots with a dull, dry complexion, Dizziness and ringing in the ears, Dry eyes and blurred vision, Night sweats and heat in palms, soles, and chest, Lower back and knee soreness, Insomnia or restless sleep with vivid dreams
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Overwork and late nights, Spicy, greasy, or fried foods, Chronic stress and anger, Hot, dry environments
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Adequate sleep and rest, Cool, temperate climate, Moisturizing, yin-rich foods, Gentle, regular movement
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  As we age, Kidney essence and Liver blood naturally decline. When both Kidney and Liver Yin become depleted, the skin loses its source of moisture and nourishment. This leads to a dull, dry complexion and the formation of deep-brown age spots, often accompanied by dizziness, dry eyes, and night sweats.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red body, thin with cracks, little or no coating, dry surface. Pulse: fine, rapid, and slightly wiry; weak in the Kidney position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Kidney and Liver Yin are easily depleted by overwork, insufficient sleep, and emotional stress, which generate internal heat and dry out the skin. Spicy and greasy foods further consume Yin fluids, worsening the deficiency. Rest, cooling foods, and gentle movement help preserve and rebuild Yin, allowing the skin to regain its luster and reducing pigment accumulation.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat plenty of Yin-nourishing foods like black sesame, walnuts, goji berries, mulberries, and eggs. Avoid spicy, fried, and overly heating foods that consume Yin fluids. Stay well hydrated with warm water or herbal teas. Prioritize rest and avoid overwork; gentle exercises such as Tai Chi or yoga help circulate Qi without depleting Yin.

### Kidney Yin Deficiency With Empty-Heat Blazing
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/kidney-yin-deficiency-with-empty-heat-blazing)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Deep brown spots on face and hands, skin looks dry and thin, Night sweats and feeling of heat in the afternoon or evening, Hot palms and soles, lower back soreness and weakness, Restlessness, irritability, and insomnia with waking at night, Tinnitus or diminished hearing, Dry throat, thirst with desire for small sips
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Overwork and late nights, Spicy, greasy, or fried foods, Emotional stress and frustration, Hot weather or sauna, Excessive sexual activity
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Cool, quiet environment, Adequate sleep and rest, Yin-nourishing foods (pear, black sesame), Gentle, non-sweaty exercise
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  As we age, Kidney Yin-the body’s cooling and moistening reserve-naturally declines. When Yin becomes too weak to nourish the skin, the complexion turns dry and thin, and empty-heat rises to scorch the surface, causing deep brown spots to form. The spots are a visible sign of this deeper deficiency, often accompanied by night sweats and a sensation of heat in the palms and soles.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red, thin, dry with little or no coating, possibly cracked. Pulse: fine, rapid (细数 xì shuò).
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Overwork, late nights, and excessive sexual activity deplete Kidney Yin, the body's cooling and moistening reserve, allowing empty-heat to rise and worsen spots. Spicy and fried foods create additional internal heat. Rest, cooling foods, and a calm environment help replenish Yin and subdue empty-heat, allowing the skin to heal.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Focus on cooling, moistening foods like pear, cucumber, tofu, black sesame, and goji berries. Avoid spicy, fried, and overly warming foods that aggravate empty-heat. Prioritize rest and aim for a consistent sleep schedule, as staying up late depletes Kidney Yin. Gentle exercise like walking or tai chi helps circulation without overheating.

### Blood Stagnation
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/blood-stagnation)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Dark purplish or brown age spots, Dark or dusky facial complexion, Fixed stabbing pain elsewhere in the body, Dark menstrual blood with clots (in women), Dark circles under the eyes
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Sedentary lifestyle, Cold weather or cold foods, Emotional stress or frustration, Greasy, heavy meals
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Gentle daily movement, Warmth (warm drinks, warm weather), Turmeric or ginger in food, Massage or gua sha
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  As we age, blood circulation can become sluggish, especially if Qi is not moving freely. When blood flow slows, metabolic waste and pigment are not cleared away properly, and they settle in the skin as dark, fixed spots. This pattern is often the final common pathway through which other imbalances, like Qi stagnation or deficiency, produce visible age spots.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: dark purple body with stasis spots, distended dark sublingual veins. Pulse: choppy (涩 sè) or wiry (弦 xián).
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Blood needs warmth and movement to flow freely. Cold and inactivity cause it to congeal like a river freezing in winter, which deepens stasis and darkens spots. Emotional stress knots the Liver Qi that pushes blood, so frustration directly worsens stagnation. Gentle movement, warm foods, and massage all act like a gentle current, keeping blood and pigment moving so they don't settle in the skin.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Keep blood moving with gentle daily exercise like walking, tai chi, or yoga. Avoid prolonged sitting. Eat warming, blood-nourishing foods such as dark leafy greens, beets, and a little turmeric or ginger in cooking. Stay well hydrated with warm fluids, and limit cold, raw foods that can congeal blood.

### Qi And Blood Stagnation
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/qi-and-blood-stagnation)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Dark purplish spots on face and hands, Chest tightness or distending pain in the ribs, Irritability or mood swings, Worsens with emotional stress, Dark lips or purplish complexion
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Emotional stress, Sedentary lifestyle, Greasy or heavy foods, Suppressing emotions
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Gentle exercise, Stress reduction, Warm compress on abdomen, Deep breathing
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Emotional tension and chronic stress cause the Liver to lose its ability to keep Qi flowing smoothly. This stagnant Qi then fails to move Blood, leading to Blood stasis that deposits dark pigment in the skin. The result is the appearance of dark, purplish age spots, often accompanied by chest tightness and moodiness.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: purple or dark body with stasis spots, distended sublingual veins. Pulse: wiry (弦 xián) and choppy (涩 sè).
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Stress and frustration directly stagnate Liver Qi, which is the root of this pattern; as Qi stops moving, Blood congeals and dark spots form. Gentle movement and deep breathing help unblock Qi flow, while warm compresses relax the Liver channel and ease stasis. Greasy foods overburden the Spleen and create dampness, which further obstructs circulation, making the spots worse.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Incorporate gentle daily movement like walking, yoga, or tai chi to keep Qi and Blood flowing. Manage stress through meditation or deep breathing. Eat dark leafy greens, turmeric, and small amounts of black vinegar to gently move Blood. Avoid heavy, greasy foods that can bog down digestion and worsen stagnation.

### Qi and Blood Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/qi-and-blood-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Light-brown spots, not dark or purple, Pale or sallow complexion, Persistent fatigue and weakness, Dizziness or lightheadedness
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Overwork and exhaustion, Skipping meals or poor nutrition, Raw, cold foods, Prolonged stress
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Rest and adequate sleep, Warm, cooked meals, Blood-nourishing foods (red dates, liver), Gentle exercise (walking)
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Qi and Blood are the body's fundamental nourishment. When they are deficient, the skin loses its luster and cannot renew itself properly. The Spleen fails to generate enough Qi and Blood to reach the face, leaving pale, light-brown spots where circulation is weakest. This pattern often accompanies a general sense of fatigue and a pale complexion, not the dark or purple spots seen in stasis patterns.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale body, may be thin or have teeth marks, thin white coating. Pulse: fine (thin) and weak, especially at the right Guan position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Qi and Blood are the body's fundamental nourishment. Overwork and poor diet deplete them further, so rest and nourishing foods replenish them. Raw, cold foods weaken the Spleen's ability to generate Qi and Blood, while gentle exercise stimulates circulation without draining reserves.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat warm, nourishing foods like red dates, black sesame, walnuts, and iron-rich greens. Avoid raw, cold foods that weaken the Spleen. Gentle daily movement like walking or Tai Chi helps Qi flow without exhaustion. Prioritize rest and avoid overwork.

### Damp-Phlegm
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/damp-phlegm)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Thick, raised spots with a greasy sheen, Heavy sensation in limbs and body, Sticky or loose stools, Brain fog or head feels wrapped
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Damp, humid weather, Overeating, Greasy or sweet foods, Sedentary lifestyle, Late-night eating
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Light, warm meals, Moderate exercise, Dry environment, Avoiding dairy and fried foods, Regular meal times
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When the Spleen is weak, it fails to transform fluids, leading to internal Dampness and Phlegm. This turbid Phlegm obstructs the channels and collaterals in the face, causing Qi and Blood to stagnate. Over time, the stagnation manifests as thick, raised, greasy-looking age spots, especially in people who are overweight and have a sluggish metabolism.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale, swollen body with teeth marks, thick white greasy coating, wet surface. Pulse: slippery (滑 Huá), like beads rolling in oil; may be soggy in the right middle position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Damp-Phlegm arises from a weak Spleen that cannot transform fluids. Damp-producing foods and humid environments add to the burden, while light, drying foods and exercise help the Spleen function and resolve Phlegm.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat warm, cooked, light meals; avoid dairy, greasy, sweet, and raw cold foods that create Dampness. Incorporate barley, adzuki beans, and winter melon to help drain Dampness. Gentle daily movement like walking or Tai Chi helps Qi flow and prevents stagnation.

## คำถามที่พบบ่อย (Frequently Asked Questions)
### Can TCM really remove age spots?
Yes, but it works differently than a laser. TCM aims to correct the internal imbalance that allowed the spots to form - by nourishing Yin, moving stagnant Blood, or clearing Dampness. Over time, this can lighten existing spots and, more importantly, prevent new ones. Complete removal of every spot is not always possible, but significant fading and a healthier overall complexion are realistic goals.

### How long does it take to see results?
Most people notice a subtle improvement in skin tone and fewer new spots within 2-3 months of consistent herbs and acupuncture. For visible fading of established spots, give it at least 4-6 months. Patience is key - the spots took years to develop, and internal rebalancing is a gradual process.

### Will I need acupuncture on my face?
Sometimes, but not always. Your practitioner may use very fine needles around the spots themselves (local points) to stimulate circulation, but the core of the treatment is on body points - often on the legs, feet, and back - to address the underlying organ imbalance. Facial acupuncture is gentle and not painful.

### Can I combine TCM with my usual skincare or laser treatments?
In most cases, yes. Topical creams and sun protection can be used alongside herbs and acupuncture. If you are planning a laser or chemical peel, tell both your dermatologist and your TCM practitioner. Some Blood-moving herbs can theoretically increase bruising risk, so your acupuncturist may adjust your formula around procedure dates.

### Do I have to change my diet?
Diet plays a supportive role. While no single food will erase spots, eating in a way that matches your pattern makes a real difference. For example, if you have Yin deficiency, hydrating foods like pear and black sesame help; if you have Damp-Phlegm, avoiding dairy and greasy foods is important. Your practitioner will give you specific guidance.

### Will the spots come back after treatment?
TCM aims to correct the root imbalance, so recurrence is less likely than with purely cosmetic approaches. However, aging continues, and if lifestyle or dietary habits slip back, new spots can eventually form. Many people choose to continue a maintenance herbal formula or periodic acupuncture sessions to sustain the results.
