## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนปัจจุบัน (Conventional Medicine View)
### คำอธิบาย (Description)
Hypertension, or high blood pressure, is a chronic condition where the force of blood against artery walls is consistently too high. It is typically diagnosed when systolic pressure exceeds 130 mmHg or diastolic exceeds 80 mmHg on multiple readings. Often called the 'silent killer,' it usually has no symptoms until it damages the heart, brain, or kidneys, though some people experience headaches, dizziness, or nosebleeds.

### การรักษาหลัก (Treatments)
Standard treatment includes lifestyle changes such as reduced sodium intake, regular exercise, and weight loss, combined with medications like ACE inhibitors, beta-blockers, calcium channel blockers, and diuretics. Most patients require lifelong medication to keep blood pressure within a safe range.

## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนจีน (Traditional Chinese Medicine View)
### ความเข้าใจเกี่ยวกับการแพทย์แผนจีน (TCM Understanding)
TCM does not recognize a single disease called hypertension. Instead, it groups the condition under patterns of dizziness, headache, and internal wind. The primary organs involved are the Liver, Kidneys, and Spleen. The Liver stores Blood and ensures the smooth flow of Qi; when emotional stress, frustration, or anger disrupt this flow, Qi can stagnate and transform into Heat or rise as Yang, driving blood pressure upward.
The Kidneys store the body's foundational Yin and Yang. As we age or overwork, Kidney Yin may become depleted and unable to anchor the Liver's Yang, allowing it to surge to the head. In some cases, both Yin and Yang become exhausted, leading to a mixed picture of heat above and cold below. The Spleen transforms food into Qi and Blood; a diet heavy in rich, greasy, or sweet foods weakens the Spleen and generates phlegm-dampness, which can clog the vessels and obstruct the clear senses.
Because the same high blood pressure reading can arise from such different roots, TCM treatment is never one-size-fits-all. A red-faced, irritable person with a pounding headache needs cooling and calming, while a tired, pale person with a heavy head needs warming and draining. The five patterns below capture the most common pathways that lead to hypertension.

### การวินิจฉัยโดยแพทย์แผนจีน (Practitioner Diagnosis)
A TCM practitioner starts by asking about the quality of your dizziness or headache, as well as what makes it better or worse. The nature of the discomfort - sharp, dull, heavy, or throbbing - provides the first clue toward identifying which pattern is driving your blood pressure.
If the dizziness comes with a distending headache, irritability, and a feeling of pressure behind the eyes, Liver Yang Rising (肝阳上亢) is likely. The tongue may be red with a thin coating, and the pulse feels wiry and forceful. This pattern often flares with stress and lack of sleep.
When the symptoms are more intense - a red face, bitter taste in the mouth, constipation, and a throbbing headache - the practitioner suspects Liver Fire Blazing (肝火亢盛). The tongue is deep red with a thick yellow coating, and the pulse is rapid and wiry. This pattern often appears after emotional outbursts or heavy alcohol consumption.
If the head feels heavy, as if wrapped in a cloth, and you experience chest tightness, nausea, or a foggy feeling, Phlegm-Dampness (痰湿中阻) may be the culprit. The tongue has a swollen body with a greasy white coating, and the pulse is slippery. This pattern is common in people who eat rich, greasy foods and lead a sedentary lifestyle.
For long-standing hypertension with stabbing headaches, chest pain, or numbness in the limbs, Blood Stagnation (瘀血阻络) is considered. The tongue appears dark or purple with stasis spots, and the pulse is choppy or wiry. This pattern often develops over years and can accompany any of the other patterns.
In later stages, when fatigue, cold hands and feet, palpitations, and dizziness are prominent, the practitioner looks for Deficiency of both Kidney Yin and Yang (阴阳两虚). The tongue is pale and swollen, and the pulse is deep and weak. The person may feel exhausted even after rest and have a reduced tolerance for both heat and cold.

### Liver Yang Rising
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/liver-yang-rising)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Throbbing or distending headache, especially at the temples, Dizziness with a heavy head and light, unsteady feet, Irritability and quick temper, Soreness and weakness of the lower back and knees, Ringing in the ears (tinnitus), Flushed face and red eyes
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Anger and stress, Alcohol and spicy food, Overwork and lack of sleep, Hot weather or stuffy environments
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Rest in a quiet, dark room, Cooling foods and drinks, Gentle movement or exercise, Deep breathing and meditation
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  The Liver is responsible for keeping Qi flowing smoothly. When Kidney and Liver Yin become depleted-often from aging, chronic stress, or overwork-they lose the ability to anchor the Liver's Yang. The unmoored Yang surges upward like a pot boiling over, carrying heat and pressure to the head. This rise disturbs the blood vessels, producing the high pressure readings, throbbing headaches, and dizziness typical of hypertension.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red body with redder sides, thin dry yellow or scanty coating, possibly trembling. Pulse: wiry (弦 xián), often forceful at the left middle position; may be fine and rapid if Yin deficiency is pronounced.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Stress and anger directly fuel the upward surge of Liver Yang, so calming the mind is essential. Spicy and heating foods add extra heat to an already rising Yang, while cooling, Yin-nourishing foods help replenish the depleted foundation that normally anchors it. Gentle movement keeps Qi circulating without stagnation, and adequate rest allows the body to rebuild the Yin that holds Yang in check.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Favor cooling, Yin-nourishing foods like cucumber, celery, spinach, and pear. Avoid spicy, greasy, and salty foods, and limit coffee and alcohol. Gentle daily movement such as tai chi or walking helps Qi flow without agitating the Yang. Prioritize wind-down time before bed and aim for consistent, restful sleep.

### Liver Fire Blazing
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/liver-fire-blazing)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Throbbing headache at temples or crown, Bitter taste in the mouth, Red, painful or burning eyes, Flushed red face, Intense irritability and quick temper, Constipation and dark yellow urine
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Anger and stress, Spicy or greasy food, Alcohol, Hot weather or stuffy environments, Overwork and lack of sleep
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Cooling foods and drinks, Rest in a quiet, dark room, Stress reduction, Gentle movement or exercise
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When anger, frustration, or prolonged stress are not released, Liver Qi stagnates and eventually turns into Fire. This Fire surges upward along the Liver channel, bringing intense heat to the head, which forcefully drives up blood pressure. The result is a pounding, throbbing headache, a flushed red face, and a bitter taste in the mouth as the heat disturbs the Gallbladder.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red body with redder sides, dry yellow coating, possible red prickles. Pulse: wiry (弦 xián), rapid (数 shuò), full and forceful, especially at the left Guan (Liver) position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Liver Fire is a hot, rising, and aggressive energy. Emotional stress and anger directly fan the flames, while hot and greasy foods add fuel. Cooling foods and rest help drain the excess heat, and calming the mind prevents the Fire from surging upward, naturally easing the pressure in the head.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Avoid spicy, greasy, and fried foods, as well as alcohol and coffee, which add heat. Favor cooling foods like celery, bitter gourd, chrysanthemum tea, and mung beans. Practice stress management through meditation, deep breathing, or gentle walks. Do not suppress anger-find healthy outlets.

### Phlegm-Dampness in the Middle-Burner
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/phlegm-dampness-in-the-middle-burner)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Head feels heavy, as if wrapped in cloth, Dizziness or lightheadedness, Chest tightness or oppression, Nausea or queasiness, Bloating and fullness after eating, Loose, sticky stools, Excessive drowsiness and sluggishness, Greasy, sticky mouth taste with no thirst
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Greasy, fried, or sweet foods, Damp or humid weather, Overeating or large meals, Sedentary lifestyle, Cold, raw foods
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Light, cooked meals, Gentle movement or exercise, Warm, dry environment, Eating small, frequent portions
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When the Spleen is weakened by poor diet or fatigue, it fails to transform fluids, which then accumulate as phlegm-dampness. This heavy, sticky phlegm-dampness blocks the Middle Burner first, causing bloating and nausea, then rises to the head, where it clouds the clear senses and produces a heavy, wrapped feeling. The obstruction and turbidity disrupt the smooth flow of Qi and blood, which can push blood pressure upward as the body struggles to circulate against the sludge.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale, swollen body with scalloped edges (teeth marks) and a thick white greasy coating, especially in the center. Pulse: slippery (滑 huá) or soggy (濡 rú), often slow or moderate.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Phlegm-dampness forms when the Spleen is overburdened; heavy, greasy, and cold foods create more dampness, while warm, light meals and movement help the Spleen transform and transport fluids. A dry environment and small meals prevent stagnation, easing the turbidity that clouds the head and raises pressure.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Avoid rich, greasy, sweet, and cold-damp foods like dairy, fried items, and raw salads, which burden the Spleen. Eat warm, cooked, lightly spiced meals (such as congee with ginger) to support digestion. Gentle daily movement like walking or tai chi helps circulate Qi and dry dampness, but avoid overexertion.

### Blood Stagnation
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/blood-stagnation)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Stabbing, fixed headache, Chest tightness or stabbing chest pain, Pain that worsens at night, Dark or purplish lips and nails, Numbness in the hands or feet
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Cold environment, Sedentary lifestyle, Greasy, heavy foods, Emotional stress
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Gentle movement or exercise, Warmth, Light, cooked meals, Stress reduction
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When blood flow becomes sluggish and stuck, the vessels lose their smooth, open quality. This forces the heart to pump harder to push blood through the narrowed channels, which drives up blood pressure. The result is a distinctive stabbing pain that stays in one spot, often felt in the head or chest, because the blood simply cannot move freely through that area.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: purple or dusky body with stasis spots, distended dark sublingual veins, thin white coat. Pulse: choppy (涩 Sè) or wiry (弦 Xián).
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Cold causes blood to congeal and vessels to constrict, so warmth and gentle movement open the vessels and encourage smooth flow. A sedentary lifestyle allows Qi and blood to pool and stagnate; regular mild exercise keeps the pump working. Greasy foods create turbid phlegm that further obstructs the vessels, while light, cooked foods are easy to digest and do not burden circulation. Emotional stress knots the Liver Qi, which is the main driver of blood movement; when Qi is stuck, blood stagnates, so calming the mind directly eases the pressure.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat warm, lightly cooked meals and include small amounts of blood-moving foods like hawthorn berries, onion, garlic, and turmeric. Avoid greasy, heavy, or very cold foods and drinks, which can thicken the blood and worsen stasis. Gentle daily movement-such as walking, tai chi, or stretching-is essential to keep blood flowing; avoid prolonged sitting. Manage emotional stress with breathing exercises, as pent-up frustration directly tightens the vessels.

### Deficiency of both the Kidney Yin and Yang
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/deficiency-of-both-the-kidney-yin-and-yang)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Deep fatigue and lack of vitality, Alternating chills and hot flushes, Soreness and weakness of the lower back and knees, Dizziness and ringing in the ears, Frequent nighttime urination, Feeling cold in the limbs
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Overwork and lack of sleep, Cold, raw foods, Cold environment, Excessive physical labor, Chronic stress and worry
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Adequate rest and early nights, Warm, nourishing soups and stews, Gentle warmth on the lower back, Gentle movement or exercise
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  In the later stages of hypertension, the body's foundational Yin and Yang reserves in the Kidneys become worn down. Kidney Yin deficiency can no longer anchor the body's upward energy, allowing empty heat to rise and disturb the head, causing dizziness and a sensation of heat. At the same time, Kidney Yang deficiency fails to warm the limbs and transform fluids, leading to internal cold, fluid retention, and a lack of vitality. This dual depletion makes blood pressure unstable and often resistant to treatment, with symptoms of both heat and cold appearing together.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale, puffy-tender body with teeth marks and cracks, thin white coat that may be partially peeled. Pulse: deep, fine, and weak, weakest at the Chi (rear) position; may be slightly rapid if Yin deficiency dominates, or slow if Yang deficiency is more prominent.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: The Kidneys are the root of both Yin and Yang, and they are recharged during sleep and rest. Overwork and late nights drain these reserves, worsening the depletion. Warm foods and warmth on the lower back directly support Kidney Yang, while gentle movement circulates Qi without consuming it. Cold and dampness obstruct Yang and further damage the Kidneys' warming function, so avoiding them is essential.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat warm, easily digestible, nourishing foods like bone broths, stews, and congees. Avoid raw, cold, and iced foods that further deplete Yang. Gentle daily movement such as tai chi or walking helps circulate Qi without exhausting reserves. Prioritize deep rest and avoid staying up late, as nighttime is when the Kidneys recharge.

## คำถามที่พบบ่อย (Frequently Asked Questions)
### Can TCM cure high blood pressure or just manage symptoms?
TCM aims to correct the underlying imbalance so that blood pressure normalizes naturally. It is not a quick fix, but many patients are able to reduce or even eliminate their medication over time under their doctor's supervision. The goal is to restore the body's self-regulating ability, not just to mask symptoms.

### How long does it take for acupuncture to lower blood pressure?
Acupuncture often brings immediate relaxation and a temporary dip in blood pressure during the session. For lasting change, weekly sessions over 4-8 weeks are typical. Some people notice a gradual drop in their average readings within a month, especially when combined with herbs and dietary adjustments.

### Can I take Chinese herbs with my blood pressure medication?
Yes, in most cases TCM herbs can be taken alongside conventional medications. However, you should never stop or adjust your medication without your doctor's guidance. Some herbs have mild blood-thinning or diuretic effects, so always inform both your TCM practitioner and your prescribing physician about everything you are taking.

### What foods should I avoid if I have high blood pressure from a TCM perspective?
In general, avoid excessively salty, greasy, or spicy foods, as they can aggravate dampness and heat. Cooling foods like celery, cucumber, and chrysanthemum tea can help calm Liver Fire, while warming, nourishing foods like bone broth and black beans support Kidney deficiency. Reduce alcohol and caffeine, which can stir up Liver Yang.

### Does stress cause high blood pressure in TCM?
Yes, emotional stress - especially anger, frustration, and prolonged tension - is a major trigger. It directly disrupts the Liver's ability to keep Qi flowing smoothly, leading to stagnation that can transform into Fire or cause Yang to rise. This is why many stress-related hypertension patterns respond well to TCM approaches that calm the Liver.

### Will my blood pressure go back up after stopping herbs?
If the underlying pattern has been fully resolved and lifestyle habits are maintained, blood pressure can remain stable. However, if the root imbalance is deep-seated, some ongoing maintenance with herbs or periodic acupuncture may be recommended. Your practitioner will guide you on a tapering plan rather than abrupt cessation.

## การวิจัยทางวิทยาศาสตร์สมัยใหม่ (Modern Scientific Research)
1. **Acupuncture for essential hypertension**
   *วารสารวิชาการ: Li DZ, Zhou Y, Yang J, et al. Acupuncture for essential hypertension. Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews. 2018, Issue 11. Art. No.: CD008821.*
   A Cochrane systematic review assessing the effects of acupuncture on blood pressure. The review included 22 RCTs and found that acupuncture may modestly reduce systolic and diastolic blood pressure compared to sham acupuncture or no treatment, but the evidence was of low to moderate quality due to small sample sizes and methodological limitations.

2. **Tianma Gouteng Yin for essential hypertension: a systematic review and meta-analysis**
   *วารสารวิชาการ: Zhang Y, Wang C, Liu J, et al. Tianma Gouteng Yin for essential hypertension: a systematic review and meta-analysis. Evidence-Based Complementary and Alternative Medicine. 2020;2020:9185706.*
   This meta-analysis of 18 RCTs involving 1,645 patients found that Tianma Gouteng Yin combined with conventional antihypertensives significantly reduced systolic and diastolic blood pressure compared to antihypertensives alone, and improved TCM symptom scores. The formula was well-tolerated with few adverse events.

3. **Banxia Baizhu Tianma Tang for hypertension with phlegm-dampness: a randomized controlled trial**
   *วารสารวิชาการ: Wang X, Li H, Chen J, et al. Clinical study on Banxia Baizhu Tianma Decoction in treating hypertension of phlegm-dampness type. Journal of Traditional Chinese Medicine. 2019;39(5):723-728.*
   An RCT of 120 patients with phlegm-dampness type hypertension showed that adding Banxia Baizhu Tianma Tang to standard treatment led to greater reductions in blood pressure and improvements in dizziness, heavy head sensation, and greasy tongue coating compared to standard treatment alone after 8 weeks.
