## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนปัจจุบัน (Conventional Medicine View)
### คำอธิบาย (Description)
In Western medicine, unintentional weight loss is defined as a significant drop in body weight that occurs without deliberate changes in diet or exercise. It is often a red flag, prompting investigation for underlying conditions such as hyperthyroidism, diabetes, gastrointestinal malabsorption, chronic infection, cancer, or psychiatric disorders like depression. Diagnosis typically involves blood tests, imaging, and endoscopy to identify an organic cause.

### การรักษาหลัก (Treatments)
Conventional treatment focuses on identifying and addressing the underlying disease. For example, thyroid medication for hyperthyroidism, insulin for uncontrolled diabetes, or nutritional supplements and appetite stimulants for malabsorption and cachexia. If no cause is found, management may include dietary counseling, high-calorie supplements, and monitoring.

## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนจีน (Traditional Chinese Medicine View)
### ความเข้าใจเกี่ยวกับการแพทย์แผนจีน (TCM Understanding)
TCM views unintentional weight loss as a failure of the Spleen and Stomach system, the body's engine for transforming food into Qi and Blood. When this engine is weak, food sits and bloats rather than nourishing, and weight drops gradually despite eating. A pale, puffy tongue and a weak pulse confirm that the digestive fire is too low to extract the building blocks your body needs.
But not all weight loss comes from a weak appetite. If you eat well yet still lose weight, and your mouth feels dry or you have a gnawing hunger, the Stomach's Yin fluids may be depleted. A mild empty heat then burns through nutrients instead of storing them, leaving you thin and restless. The tongue here is often red with little coating, revealing the dryness inside.
Stress plays its own role. When frustration or bottled-up emotions cause the Liver Qi to rebel and attack the Spleen, digestion becomes chaotic - loose stools, bloating, and weight loss that worsens with tension. This pattern shows how closely the mind and gut are linked in TCM, and why the same weight loss can have such different roots.
In deeper cases, the body may simply lack the raw materials. Qi and Blood Deficiency leaves the entire system undernourished, with a pale face, dizziness, and a thin, weak pulse.
And when Kidney Yang - the body's foundational warmth - is deficient along with Spleen Yang, the digestive process becomes cold and sluggish, leading to weight loss with cold limbs and early-morning diarrhea. Each pattern reveals a different story behind the scale.

### การวินิจฉัยโดยแพทย์แผนจีน (Practitioner Diagnosis)
A practitioner starts by asking about appetite and digestion. When weight loss comes with a poor appetite, loose stools, and a sense of heaviness after eating, the focus turns to Spleen and Stomach Qi Deficiency. A pale, slightly swollen tongue and a weak pulse confirm that the body’s ability to extract nourishment from food has slowed down.
If the person is losing weight but still eats well - or even feels hungry - and complains of a dry mouth, thirst, or a gnawing sensation in the stomach, that points toward Stomach Yin Deficiency. Here the tongue is often red with little or no coating, and the pulse feels thin and rapid, showing that a mild internal heat is burning through fluids faster than they can be replenished.
When weight loss is intertwined with stress, sighing, and alternating bowel habits (loose stools one day, constipation the next), the picture shifts to Rebellious Liver Qi invading the Spleen. A wiry pulse and a tongue that may be pale or have teeth marks suggest that emotional tension is disrupting the digestive rhythm, preventing the body from holding onto the nourishment it receives.
In cases where the body looks undernourished across the board - pale face, brittle nails, dizziness, and deep exhaustion - a broader Qi and Blood Deficiency is likely. The tongue is pale and thin, and the pulse is weak and thready. This pattern often develops after prolonged illness or chronic Spleen weakness, when the raw materials for blood simply run low.
A less common but important picture is Kidney and Spleen Yang Deficiency. Here weight loss is accompanied by a constant feeling of cold, especially in the lower back and limbs, and a sallow, slightly puffy appearance rather than just thinness. The tongue is pale and swollen with a white coating, and the pulse feels deep and slow, indicating that the body’s warming and transforming fire has dimmed.

### Spleen and Stomach Qi Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/spleen-and-stomach-qi-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Poor appetite, Bloating after eating, Loose or unformed stools, Fatigue and weakness, Pale or sallow complexion
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Cold or raw foods, Overeating or large meals, Stress and emotional upset, Overwork and lack of sleep, Irregular eating or skipping meals
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warm, cooked meals, Small frequent meals, Rest after eating, Gentle walking or exercise
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Your Spleen and Stomach are the engines that turn food into Qi and Blood, the building blocks of your body. When their Qi is weak, this transformation process sputters. Food sits and bloats rather than nourishing, so you lose weight gradually despite eating, and feel drained of energy.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale, puffy body with tooth marks and a thin white coating. Pulse: weak, soft, and empty in the right Guan position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: The Spleen thrives on warmth and routine. Warm, cooked foods are easier to digest and transform into Qi and Blood, while raw and cold foods dampen the digestive fire. Overeating and irregular meals overwhelm the already weak Spleen, leading to bloating and poor absorption. Rest and gentle movement support Qi circulation without draining energy, allowing the body to rebuild.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat warm, cooked foods like soups, stews, and congees. Avoid raw, cold, and greasy foods that burden the Spleen. Eat small, frequent meals and rest briefly after eating. Gentle exercise like walking or tai chi helps circulate Qi without depleting it.

### Stomach Yin Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/stomach-yin-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Dry mouth and throat, especially at night, Feeling hungry but not wanting to eat, or eating without satisfaction, Dull burning pain or gnawing discomfort in the stomach, Dry stools or constipation, Thirst for small sips of water
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Spicy, greasy, or fried foods, Overwork and lack of sleep, Stress and emotional upset, Excessive talking or singing
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warm, moistening foods (congee, soups), Small sips of water throughout the day, Regular, relaxed meal times, Adequate rest and sleep
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  The Stomach relies on Yin fluids to moisten and break down food. When these fluids run low, a mild empty heat develops that burns through nutrients instead of storing them. This is why someone with Stomach Yin Deficiency can lose weight even when eating normally-the body is essentially cooking without enough water, and the nourishment never reaches the tissues.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red body, thin, with a dry centre and little or no coating; cracks may appear. Pulse: fine (Xi) and rapid (Shu), especially weak at the right middle position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Spicy and dry foods act like a fan on a low flame, intensifying the empty heat and consuming Yin fluids. Late nights and overwork drain the body's reserves, which are already shallow. Conversely, moistening foods and rest replenish the Stomach's cooling, lubricating layer, allowing it to properly transform food into nourishing Qi and Blood. Small, unhurried meals prevent overwhelming the weakened digestive fire.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Focus on moist, easily digestible foods like congee, steamed pears, honey water, and soups. Eat smaller, more frequent meals and avoid dry, spicy, or fried foods that further deplete fluids. Gentle evening walks and a regular sleep schedule help preserve Yin.

### Rebellious Liver Qi invading the Spleen
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/rebellious-liver-qi-invading-the-spleen)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Weight loss with loose stools triggered by emotional upset, Abdominal pain that improves after a bowel movement, Bloating after eating, Frequent sighing and irritability
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Stress and emotional upset, Irregular eating or skipping meals, Cold or raw foods, Spicy, greasy, or fried foods
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Stress reduction and relaxation, Warm, cooked meals, Gentle walking or exercise
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When frustration or stress bottles up, the Liver Qi can rebel and attack the Spleen instead of flowing smoothly. This disrupts the Spleen's job of transforming food into nourishment, so meals pass through poorly absorbed, causing loose stools and bloating. Over time, the body loses its supply of Qi and Blood, and weight drops off even when you eat.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: normal or slightly pale body, possible teeth marks, thin white coating that may be slightly greasy. Pulse: wiry (弦 xián) overall, with the left guan position distinctly wiry and the right guan position softer.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Emotional upset directly agitates Liver Qi, causing it to invade the Spleen and trigger diarrhea and bloating that prevent weight gain. Calming practices smooth the Liver, while warm, cooked foods give the Spleen the gentle support it needs to transform food into steady nourishment.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat warm, cooked meals at regular times; avoid cold, raw, and greasy foods that burden the Spleen. Gentle movement like walking or Tai Chi helps Qi flow, and a daily wind-down practice (deep breathing, journaling) keeps Liver Qi from stagnating. Limit alcohol and spicy foods, which can fan Liver heat.

### Qi and Blood Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/qi-and-blood-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Pale or sallow complexion, Dizziness and lightheadedness, Heart palpitations, Fatigue and weakness, Poor appetite
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Overwork and lack of sleep, Irregular eating or skipping meals, Cold or raw foods, Stress and emotional upset
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Adequate rest and sleep, Warm, cooked meals, Nourishing foods (red dates, beef), Gentle walking or exercise
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When the body's Qi is too weak to transform food into usable energy and Blood is too scanty to nourish muscles and tissues, the whole body becomes undernourished. This leads to gradual, unintentional weight loss, a pale and sallow complexion, and a deep, persistent fatigue that rest alone doesn't fix.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale, may be thin or have teeth marks, with a thin white coating. Pulse: fine and weak.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Qi and Blood are the body's fundamental substances; they are easily depleted by overwork, poor nutrition, and stress, and replenished by rest, warm nourishment, and gentle activity. Cold and raw foods further weaken the Spleen's ability to extract nutrients, while warm, cooked meals support digestion and absorption. Adequate sleep allows the body to restore its reserves, and gentle movement circulates Qi without draining it.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat regular, warm, cooked meals with easily digestible, nourishing foods like congee, stews, red dates, goji berries, and moderate amounts of high-quality protein (chicken, beef). Avoid raw, cold, and greasy foods that tax the Spleen. Prioritize sleep and gentle movement like walking or tai chi to build Qi without depleting it.

### Kidney and Spleen Yang Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/kidney-and-spleen-yang-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Early-morning diarrhea (around 5 AM), Cold hands and feet, especially below the knees, Sore and cold lower back and knees, Undigested food in stools, Puffy, pale face and swollen ankles, Poor appetite with abdominal bloating after eating
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Cold or raw foods, Cold, damp weather, Overwork and lack of sleep, Excessive sexual activity, Stress and emotional upset
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warm, cooked meals, Keeping the abdomen and lower back warm, Gentle walking or exercise, Adequate rest and sleep, Moxibustion on the lower back and abdomen
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  The digestive system depends on Spleen Yang to transform food and Kidney Yang to provide the foundational warmth. When both are deficient, the entire system becomes cold and sluggish, unable to break down food properly. Nutrients are not absorbed, leading to gradual weight loss, while fluids accumulate, giving a puffy, waterlogged appearance despite the wasting. The classic early-morning diarrhea is a sign that Yang is too weak to hold stool until the body warms up.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Pale, swollen, puffy tongue body with teeth marks, white slippery coating; pulse deep, weak, and slow, especially feeble in the Chi and right Guan positions.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Kidney and Spleen Yang are the body's warming and transforming forces. Cold foods and environments directly extinguish this fire, while warmth and rest help preserve and restore it. The digestive system needs heat to break down food; without it, nutrients pass through undigested, leading to weight loss. Moxibustion and warm spices provide the external and internal heat needed to reignite the digestive fire, allowing the body to finally absorb nourishment and rebuild.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat only warm, cooked foods and avoid all cold, raw, or icy items. Incorporate warming spices like ginger, cinnamon, and cloves into soups and stews. Small, frequent meals are easier to digest. Keep the lower back and abdomen warm with clothing or a hot water bottle. Gentle exercise like walking or tai chi can help move Qi without depleting it. Rest deeply and avoid overwork and late nights, which further drain Kidney Yang.

## คำถามที่พบบ่อย (Frequently Asked Questions)
### Can acupuncture really help me gain weight?
Yes. Acupuncture works by strengthening the Spleen and Stomach's ability to transform food into energy and tissue. Points like Zusanli ST-36 and Sanyinjiao SP-6 are used across all patterns to boost digestive function. Combined with herbal formulas, acupuncture helps correct the underlying imbalance so your body can hold onto nourishment again.

### How long until I see results?
Digestive improvements - less bloating, more regular bowel movements, better appetite - often appear within 2-3 weeks. Actual weight gain typically becomes noticeable after 4-8 weeks, though deeper deficiency patterns may take 3-6 months to show sustained change. Consistency with herbs and dietary adjustments is key.

### Do I need to change my diet?
Yes, diet is a central part of treatment. TCM recommends warm, cooked, easily digestible foods like soups, stews, and congee. Raw, cold, and greasy foods should be avoided as they tax the digestive fire. Your practitioner will give you specific guidance based on your pattern, but the general rule is to eat in a way that supports your Spleen.

### What if I have a good appetite but still lose weight?
This often points to Stomach Yin Deficiency or an internal heat pattern. You may feel hungry, even have a gnawing sensation, but the body is burning through nutrients instead of storing them. The tongue is usually red with little coating. Treatment focuses on nourishing Yin and clearing empty heat, rather than simply tonifying Qi.

### Can I combine TCM with my doctor's treatment?
Absolutely. Acupuncture and herbs can safely complement conventional investigation and treatment. Always inform your TCM practitioner of any medications, especially thyroid hormones, insulin, or corticosteroids. If a serious underlying condition is found, TCM works best as a supportive therapy alongside standard medical care.

### Is weight loss in TCM always due to a weak Spleen?
The Spleen is almost always involved, but the root cause varies. It could be Qi deficiency, Yin deficiency, Liver overacting on the Spleen, or a deeper Yang deficiency. That's why a proper pattern diagnosis - including tongue and pulse examination - is essential to choose the right herbs and points.
