## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนปัจจุบัน (Conventional Medicine View)
### คำอธิบาย (Description)
Anhidrosis is the medical term for the inability to sweat normally. Sweating is the body’s primary cooling mechanism; when it fails, overheating can occur. Causes range from nerve damage (autonomic neuropathy, diabetes), skin conditions (burns, psoriasis), certain medications (anticholinergics), to dehydration or genetic disorders. Diagnosis typically involves a thermoregulatory sweat test, skin biopsy, or nerve studies to determine whether the problem is central, peripheral, or glandular.

### การรักษาหลัก (Treatments)
Conventional treatment focuses on managing the underlying cause and preventing overheating. This may include stopping offending medications, treating skin conditions, using cooling vests or air conditioning, and applying topical moisturizers. In some cases, medications that stimulate sweating (like pilocarpine) are tried, but results vary.

## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนจีน (Traditional Chinese Medicine View)
### ความเข้าใจเกี่ยวกับการแพทย์แผนจีน (TCM Understanding)
Sweating in TCM is a delicate dance between the Lung, which governs the skin and pores, the Spleen, which transforms fluids into usable moisture, and the Kidney Yang, which provides the warmth to steam those fluids outward. The defensive Qi (Wei Qi) acts as the gatekeeper, opening and closing the pores in response to temperature and need. When any part of this system falters, sweat can’t form or can’t escape.
The most common reason for a sudden lack of sweating is an external invasion of Cold. Just as a cold wind makes you shiver and close your coat, Cold tightens the pores and traps the defensive Qi at the surface. This is an acute, full-body stop - you feel chilled, achy, and can’t sweat even under blankets. It’s the body’s gate slammed shut.
But not all anhidrosis is acute. Chronic inability to sweat often stems from deeper constitutional weaknesses. If the body’s Yang is deficient, there isn’t enough internal fire to steam the fluids and open the pores - the skin stays cold and dry no matter the weather. If Body Fluids are depleted from chronic heat, overwork, or illness, there’s simply no raw material to make sweat; you may feel hot inside but produce nothing.
And sometimes, the problem isn’t a lack of something, but an excess: Damp-Heat or Phlegm can physically clog the channels and pores, like a steamy fog that won’t lift, causing patchy or heavy non-sweating.
This is why TCM doesn’t treat “anhidrosis” as one condition. The tongue, pulse, and accompanying sensations - chills versus heat, dryness versus stickiness - point to the root. By addressing that root, whether it’s releasing the exterior, warming Yang, nourishing fluids, or clearing obstructions, normal sweating can often be restored.

### การวินิจฉัยโดยแพทย์แผนจีน (Practitioner Diagnosis)
A practitioner first asks whether the inability to sweat came on suddenly or has been a long‑standing issue. The timing, triggers, and what other feelings accompany it-chills, heat, dryness, or stickiness-point toward very different underlying patterns.
If the lack of sweating is abrupt and follows exposure to cold wind, with intense chills, body aches, and a tight‑feeling headache, an Exterior‑Cold pattern is likely. The tongue usually has a thin white coat and the pulse feels floating and tight, like a drum skin. This is the classic acute presentation, where cold has literally shut the pores.
When the problem is more chronic and mild, with a constant sensitivity to drafts and a tendency to catch colds easily, Ying‑Wei Disharmony comes to mind. The tongue is pale with a thin white coating, and the pulse is floating but weak or moderate-a sign that the body’s protective and nourishing forces are out of sync and cannot open the pores properly.
If the person feels cold all the time, has cold hands and feet, looks pale and tired, and simply never breaks a sweat even when warm, a Yang Deficiency is suspected. The tongue is often pale and puffy, and the pulse is deep, slow, and weak. Here, the internal fire that should drive sweat to the surface is simply too low.
A very different picture emerges when the skin is dry but the person feels uncomfortably hot inside, with thirst and a dry throat. This points to Body Fluids Deficiency-there is not enough fluid to form sweat. The tongue is red with little or no coating, and the pulse is thin and rapid, revealing that heat has consumed the body’s moisture.
If lack of sweating comes with a sticky, heavy sensation, skin that looks red or irritated, and a thick yellow tongue coating, Damp‑Heat is blocking the pores. The pulse is often slippery and fast.
In contrast, when the sweat loss is patchy and accompanied by numbness or a heavy, swollen tongue with a greasy coat, Phlegm in the Channels is physically clogging the sweat glands, and the pulse tends to be slippery or wiry.

## กลุ่มอาการการแพทย์แผนจีนและการรักษา (TCM Patterns and Treatment)

### Exterior-Cold
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/exterior-cold)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: No sweating after cold exposure, Strong chills, mild fever, Headache at back of head/neck, Stiff neck, aching upper back, Clear runny nose, sneezing, No thirst, prefers warm drinks
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Cold drafts/AC, Icy/cold drinks, Damp chilly weather, Overexertion while chilled
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warm blankets/clothing, Ginger tea, Rest in warm draft-free room, Light sweating after warm bath
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When a person is suddenly exposed to cold wind, the pores tighten and the body's defensive Qi becomes trapped at the surface. This is like a gate being slammed shut - sweat cannot escape, even if the body tries to push it out. The result is a sudden, complete inability to sweat, accompanied by strong chills and a stiff, aching body.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: normal or slightly pale body with a thin, white, moist coating. Pulse: floating and tight (fu jin), like a taut rope felt easily at the surface.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Cold contracts and closes the pores, which is exactly how this pattern blocks sweating, so warmth is the most direct remedy. Warmth relaxes the surface, opens the pores, and helps the defensive Qi push out the cold pathogen through a gentle sweat. Cold drinks and drafts do the opposite - they tighten the exterior further, trapping the cold inside and prolonging the inability to sweat.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Drink warm ginger tea or scallion water to help the body push out a light sweat. Eat warm, easy-to-digest foods like congee and avoid all cold drinks and raw foods. Bundle up and rest in a warm, draft-free room until sweating returns naturally.

### Ying-Wei Disharmony
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/ying-wei-disharmony)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Sensitive to wind/drafts, Intermittent fever, chills and heat, Fatigue, easily tired, General body aches, Mild lack of sweating
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Wind/draft exposure, Stress, overwork, Cold raw foods, Excessive physical exertion
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warm light clothing, Gentle exercise, Ginger tea, Rest in warm draft-free room
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Your body's defensive Qi (Wei) and nourishing Qi (Ying) normally work together like an outer guard and inner supply. When they fall out of sync-often after an illness, during a weak spell, or from an incomplete recovery-the Wei Qi loses its ability to open the pores properly. The result is a mild, lingering inability to sweat, even when you feel warm or need to cool down, because the signal to release sweat never quite gets through.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Pale-red or slightly pale body with a thin white moist coating. Pulse: floating and moderate (浮缓).
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Wind and drafts disrupt the already weakened Wei Qi, making it harder for the pores to function. Rest and warmth support the body's energy to harmonize Ying and Wei. Gentle exercise stimulates Qi flow without depleting it, while ginger tea warms the exterior and encourages a mild, healthy sweat. Cold foods and overwork drain Qi, worsening the disharmony.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat warm, easily digestible foods like congee with ginger and scallions. Avoid cold, raw foods and icy drinks. Gentle daily movement like walking or tai chi helps circulate Qi, but avoid overexertion. Protect yourself from drafts and wind.

### Yang Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/yang-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Feeling cold, aversion to cold, Cold hands and feet, Fatigue, low vitality, Pale puffy face, Loose stools, undigested food, Frequent clear urine, nighttime urination
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Cold weather/AC, Raw, cold foods and iced drinks, Overwork, exhaustion, Prolonged standing
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warmth, warm drinks, Rest and sleep, Gentle exercise, Moxibustion on lower abdomen
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Yang is the body's warming, transforming fire. When it is deficient, the pores lack the gentle heat needed to open and release sweat. Even in warm conditions, the body cannot convert fluids into sweat, so the skin stays dry and cold.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale, swollen, moist with teeth marks, white slippery coating. Pulse: deep, slow, weak.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Yang Deficiency means the body's warming and transforming power is low. Cold further drains that fire, while warmth and warm foods gently stoke it. Rest conserves Yang, and gentle movement circulates it without depleting reserves. Moxibustion directly adds heat to key points, helping Yang to rise and open the pores.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat warm, cooked foods like soups, stews, and congees. Include ginger, cinnamon, lamb, and walnuts. Avoid raw, cold, and icy foods and drinks. Keep the body warm, especially the lower back and abdomen. Gentle activities like walking or tai chi help move Yang without exhaustion.

### Body Fluids Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/body-fluids-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Dry skin, Thirst, wants water, Dry cracked lips, Scanty dark urine, Dry hard stools, constipation
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Spicy fried foods, Hot dry weather, Excessive sweating/saunas, Alcohol, coffee, Overwork, stress
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Drinking water, Moistening foods like pears, Cool humid environment, Rest and sleep
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Body Fluids are the foundation of sweat. When they are depleted by chronic heat, overwork, or illness, there is simply not enough liquid to form sweat. The pores stay shut because there is nothing to release, even when the body feels hot inside.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red, dry, thin body with cracks, little or no coating. Pulse: fine (thin) and rapid, possibly choppy.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Body fluids are already scarce, so anything that further dries them out-like spicy food, heat, or excessive sweating-worsens the condition. Rest and moistening foods help replenish fluids, while a humid environment reduces insensible water loss, allowing what little fluid remains to be used for essential functions.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Drink plenty of water and enjoy moistening foods like pears, honey, white fungus soup, and lily bulb. Avoid spicy, fried, or drying foods. Use a humidifier in dry environments and avoid excessive sweating or saunas that can further deplete fluids.

### Damp-Heat
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/damp-heat)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Heaviness and sluggishness, Sticky greasy mouth, Chest and upper abdominal stuffiness, Dark yellow scanty urine, Low-grade afternoon fever, Skin sticky but dry
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Hot humid weather, Greasy fried foods, Dairy products, Sedentary lifestyle
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Cool dry weather, Light non-greasy meals, Gentle exercise, Drinking water
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Damp-Heat is like a steamy, humid fog that settles in the body and clogs the skin's pores. The heavy, sticky nature of dampness combines with heat, creating a mugginess that blocks the normal opening and closing of sweat glands. Even when the body is warm and should be sweating, the pores remain sealed, leaving the skin dry but sticky to the touch.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red body with a thick, yellow, greasy coating. Pulse: soggy-rapid (Ru Shu) or slippery-rapid (Hua Shu).
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Damp-heat thrives in humid conditions and is aggravated by heavy, greasy foods that create more dampness and heat. Cooling, dry environments and light foods reduce the damp-heat burden, while gentle movement helps Qi circulation to transform dampness and open pores.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat light, cooling foods like barley, mung beans, and bitter greens. Avoid greasy, spicy, and dairy-heavy meals that create dampness. Gentle exercise like walking or yoga helps move Qi and disperse dampness, but avoid overheating.

### Phlegm in the Channels joints and muscles
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/phlegm-in-the-channels-joints-and-muscles)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Patchy lack of sweat, Heavy numb limbs, Hard swellings around joints/muscles, Purplish skin over affected areas, Chest tightness, excessive phlegm
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Damp humid environments, Greasy heavy sweet foods, Cold raw foods, iced drinks, Sedentary lifestyle
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warmth, dry weather, Gentle exercise, Light cooked spiced foods, Dry body brushing
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When the body fails to transform fluids properly, they can thicken into a sticky substance called Phlegm. This Phlegm can settle deep in the muscles and along the channels that connect to the skin, physically clogging the sweat pores. As a result, sweat cannot escape, leading to patchy or localized areas of dryness, often alongside heavy, numb limbs and firm swellings around the joints.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: purple body with stasis spots, white greasy coating, distended sublingual veins. Pulse: wiry (Xian) and choppy (Se), or wiry and slippery (Hua).
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Phlegm is a Yin pathogen that congeals and thickens with cold and dampness, so warmth and movement help to disperse it and open the pores. Greasy, heavy, and cold foods create more dampness and Phlegm, directly worsening the blockage. Gentle exercise and dry warmth encourage the Qi to move, preventing Phlegm from settling in the channels and allowing sweat to flow again.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Avoid damp-producing foods like dairy, sugar, cold raw salads, and greasy fried dishes. Favor warm, cooked meals with gentle aromatic spices such as ginger, cardamom, and black pepper. Daily gentle movement like walking or stretching is essential to keep Qi and fluids circulating so Phlegm does not settle.

## คำถามที่พบบ่อย (Frequently Asked Questions)
### Why can’t I sweat even when I’m hot or exercising?
In TCM, this points to either a deficiency (not enough fluids or Yang to produce sweat) or an obstruction (pores blocked by Cold, Damp, or Phlegm). A practitioner looks at your other symptoms - do you feel cold or hot inside? Is your skin dry or sticky? - to determine which pattern is at play.

### Is it dangerous not to sweat?
Sweating is your body’s natural cooling system. Without it, you’re at risk of overheating, especially in hot weather or during exercise. If you suddenly can’t sweat and feel feverish, confused, or nauseous, seek emergency care immediately. In chronic cases, the main danger is heat intolerance, so it’s important to stay cool and treat the underlying cause.

### Can acupuncture really make me sweat again?
Yes, acupuncture can help restore the body’s ability to sweat by regulating the nervous system and opening the pores. Points like Hegu (LI-4) and Waiguan (SJ-5) are commonly used to promote sweating in external patterns, while points like Zusanli (ST-36) and Sanyinjiao (SP-6) build the fluids and Yang needed for chronic cases. Many patients notice a subtle warmth and light sweating during or after treatment.

### How long does TCM treatment take for lack of sweating?
It depends on the pattern. A sudden cold-induced stop may resolve in days. A lingering imbalance from illness or constitution may take several weeks. Deep, chronic deficiency or stubborn Phlegm blockages can take a few months. Your practitioner will give you a more precise estimate after your first consultation.

### Are there any foods that help promote sweating?
Yes. Warm, cooked foods and spices like ginger, cinnamon, and green onion can gently encourage sweating, especially if you tend to feel cold. If you’re dry, pears, apples, and soups help build fluids. Avoid raw, cold foods and greasy, heavy meals that can clog the system. (More in the Diet section.)

### Can I use TCM alongside my regular medications?
In most cases, yes. TCM herbs and acupuncture can complement conventional care. However, some formulas contain Ma Huang (Ephedra), which can raise blood pressure and interact with stimulants or heart medications. Always tell both your TCM practitioner and your doctor about everything you’re taking to avoid interactions.
