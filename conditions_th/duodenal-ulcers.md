## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนปัจจุบัน (Conventional Medicine View)
### คำอธิบาย (Description)
A duodenal ulcer is a sore in the lining of the duodenum, the first part of the small intestine just after the stomach. It typically causes a burning or gnawing pain in the upper abdomen that often improves temporarily after eating but returns a few hours later, along with bloating, nausea, and sometimes vomiting.
The most common cause is infection with Helicobacter pylori (H. pylori) bacteria, followed by long-term use of non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs (NSAIDs) like ibuprofen or aspirin. Diagnosis is usually confirmed by endoscopy or a breath test for H. pylori.

### การรักษาหลัก (Treatments)
Standard treatment aims to reduce stomach acid and eliminate H. pylori if present. This typically involves a combination of antibiotics (such as amoxicillin and clarithromycin) and proton pump inhibitors (PPIs) like omeprazole to suppress acid and allow the ulcer to heal. H2 blockers, antacids, and stopping NSAIDs are also part of management. Lifestyle changes such as avoiding spicy foods, alcohol, and smoking are recommended.

## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนจีน (Traditional Chinese Medicine View)
### ความเข้าใจเกี่ยวกับการแพทย์แผนจีน (TCM Understanding)
The Spleen and Stomach are at the center of digestion in TCM. The Spleen transforms food into Qi and blood, and the Stomach receives and ripens food. When these organs are weak - from poor diet, overwork, or chronic illness - the gut lining doesn't get enough nourishment. This leaves it vulnerable to acid damage, causing a dull, achy pain that often feels better after eating or with warmth. This is why Spleen Qi Deficiency and Stomach Yang Deficiency are such common patterns in duodenal ulcer.
The Liver also plays a surprisingly direct role. It is responsible for the smooth flow of Qi throughout the body, and when stress, anger, or frustration block that flow, the pent-up Liver Qi can surge sideways to attack the Stomach. This disrupts the Stomach's downward flow, causing Qi to rebel upward as belching, acid reflux, and pain that flares with emotional stress. This is the Liver Qi Stagnation invading the Stomach pattern, and it explains why stress is such a powerful trigger for duodenal ulcers.

### การวินิจฉัยโดยแพทย์แผนจีน (Practitioner Diagnosis)
A TCM practitioner begins by exploring the nature of the pain, its triggers, and what brings relief. The timing, quality, and associated sensations-such as bloating, belching, or burning-are the first clues. They then examine the tongue and pulse to confirm the underlying pattern, because each pattern leaves a distinct signature on these diagnostic tools. This holistic picture guides treatment, whether it’s strengthening digestion, warming the middle, or soothing the Liver.
If the main complaint is bloating and fullness after meals, along with persistent fatigue and a poor appetite, the practitioner suspects Spleen and Stomach Qi Deficiency. The tongue is often pale and slightly swollen with a thin white coat, and the pulse feels weak and forceless. The pain is typically dull and diffuse, not sharp or localized, and it improves after rest but worsens with overexertion or irregular eating.
When the deficiency deepens into cold, the pain becomes a dull ache that feels better with warmth-like a hot water bottle or a warm meal-and worse after cold food or exposure. The person may regurgitate clear, watery fluid and have a preference for warm drinks. The tongue is pale and moist, and the pulse is deep, slow, and weak. These signs point to a lack of warming Yang energy in the Stomach.
If the pain and distension flare up with stress, anger, or frustration, and is accompanied by frequent belching and a sensation of stuffiness in the chest and sides, the pattern is likely Liver Qi invading the Stomach. The tongue may look normal or slightly red on the edges, and the pulse is wiry, like a guitar string. The pain often moves around and is not fixed, reflecting the erratic nature of stagnant Qi.
A heavy, oppressive fullness in the upper abdomen, along with a bitter taste in the mouth, nausea, and a sticky sensation, suggests Damp-Heat in the Stomach and Spleen. The tongue has a thick, yellow, greasy coating, and the pulse is slippery and rapid. This pattern often arises from rich, greasy, or spicy foods and alcohol, creating a turbid environment that irritates the ulcer. The discomfort is constant and not clearly relieved by eating or fasting.
A gnawing, burning hunger pain that is not soothed by eating, along with a dry mouth and throat, indicates Stomach Yin Deficiency. The tongue appears red and may lack a coating, looking peeled or mirror-like, and the pulse is thin and rapid. This pattern reflects a lack of cooling, moistening fluids in the stomach, often due to chronic inflammation or late nights that consume Yin.
When the pain becomes fixed, stabbing, and worse at night, and the tongue shows purple spots or a dusky color, the practitioner diagnoses Stomach Blood Stagnation. The pulse may feel choppy or wiry. This pattern often develops after long-standing ulcer disease, where poor local circulation leads to stasis. The pain is intense and pinpoint, unlike the diffuse ache of deficiency patterns.

### Spleen and Stomach Qi Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/spleen-and-stomach-qi-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Poor appetite, Abdominal bloating after eating, Loose or unformed stools, Fatigue and physical weakness, Sallow or pale complexion, Mild dull epigastric discomfort
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Overeating or heavy meals, Raw, cold, or greasy foods, Overwork and exhaustion, Irregular meal times
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Small, frequent meals, Warm, cooked foods, Gentle walking after meals, Rest and adequate sleep
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  The Spleen transforms food into Qi and blood. When Spleen Qi is weak, digestion becomes sluggish, food sits and bloats, and the stomach lining doesn't receive the nourishment it needs to repair itself. Over time, this chronic weakness leaves the duodenal mucosa vulnerable to erosion and ulceration, especially after meals when the demand on digestion is highest.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: Pale, puffy body with tooth marks and a thin white coating. Pulse: Weak, soft, and forceless, especially at the right middle (Guan) position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: <p>In Spleen Qi deficiency, the digestive fire is like a low flame. Overeating or cold, heavy foods overwhelm it, causing bloating and loose stools. Small, warm, cooked meals are easier to transform, conserving Qi. Rest and gentle movement prevent Qi from being drained further, while overwork depletes the very Qi needed to heal the ulcer.</p>
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat small, regular meals of warm, cooked foods like congee, soups, and steamed vegetables. Chew thoroughly and avoid raw, cold, greasy, or hard-to-digest items. Gentle walking after meals aids digestion; conserve energy by resting when tired and avoiding overwork.

### Stomach Yang Deficient and Cold
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/stomach-yang-deficient-and-cold)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Dull aching pain relieved by warmth and pressure, Pain worsens with cold food or cold weather, Vomiting of clear watery fluid, Cold hands and feet, Loose stools, No thirst, or only desire for warm drinks
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Cold foods and iced drinks, Raw vegetables and salads, Skipping meals, Cold, damp weather, Overwork and exhaustion
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warmth on the belly, Eating small, warm meals, Hot ginger tea, Gentle rest after eating
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When the Stomach's warming Yang is depleted, internal Cold settles into the digestive tract. The Stomach loses its ability to transform food and fluids, so meals sit heavy and clear watery fluid may rise up. This Cold constricts the local Qi flow, causing a dull, aching pain that feels better when heat and gentle pressure are applied. Over time, the cold-deficiency weakens the protective lining of the stomach and duodenum, making an ulcer more likely.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: Pale, puffy, tender body with teeth marks; white slippery coating. Pulse: Deep, slow, weak; right Guan (middle) position especially weak.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: This pattern is driven by a lack of warming Yang, so anything cold - food, drink, weather - deepens the internal Cold and makes the pain worse. Warmth, both on the skin and from warm cooked foods, directly supports the Stomach's Yang and helps it function, easing the dull ache. Rest conserves Qi and Yang, while overwork and skipping meals drain them further.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat only warm, cooked foods - soups, stews, and congees are ideal. Avoid raw salads, cold drinks, and icy foods completely. Add a little fresh ginger, cinnamon, or a pinch of black pepper to your cooking to gently warm the middle. Eat small, regular meals so the Stomach is never empty for too long. Keep your abdomen warm with a scarf or hot water bottle, especially in cold weather, and avoid overworking or rushing through meals.

### Liver Qi Stagnation invading the Stomach
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/liver-qi-stagnation-invading-the-stomach)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Distending pain that radiates to the ribs, Pain and bloating worsen with stress or anger, Frequent belching and acid reflux, Irritability, moodiness, or easy anger, Feeling of fullness and stuffiness in the chest, A sensation of a lump in the throat
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Stress and frustration, Anger or resentment, Skipping meals, Greasy, fried, or spicy foods
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Deep breathing and relaxation, Gentle walking, Warm, bland meals, Talking through feelings
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Your Liver is responsible for keeping the flow of Qi smooth throughout your body. When stress, frustration, or anger build up, that flow gets stuck, and the pent-up Liver Qi surges sideways to attack the Stomach. This disrupts the Stomach's ability to send Qi downward, causing it to rebel upward as belching, acid reflux, and distending pain that radiates toward the ribs.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: normal or slightly red body with redder sides and a thin white coat. Pulse: wiry (弦 xián), like a taut guitar string, most pronounced at the left middle position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Emotional stress and anger are the main drivers of this pattern because they cause Liver Qi to stagnate and then attack the Stomach. Calming the mind and moving the body gently help unblock that stuck Qi, while warm, regular meals support the Stomach's own downward-moving function. Spicy and greasy foods, on the other hand, create heat and dampness that can further agitate the Liver and overwhelm the Stomach, making the pain and belching worse.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat smaller, regular meals and avoid spicy, greasy, or fried foods that can further irritate the Stomach. Gentle daily movement like walking or tai chi helps keep Liver Qi flowing, but avoid intense exercise when symptoms are active. Prioritize stress management through deep breathing, meditation, or talking things out, because emotional tension directly triggers this pattern.

### Damp-Heat in Stomach and Spleen
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/damp-heat-in-stomach-and-spleen)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Epigastric fullness and bloating, worse after eating, Bitter or sticky taste in the mouth, Loose, sticky bowel movements that feel incomplete, Heavy, tired limbs, Thick yellow greasy tongue coating
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Greasy, fried, or spicy foods, Alcohol and coffee, Damp or humid weather, Overeating or heavy meals, Stress and emotional upset
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Light, bland meals, Gentle walking after meals, Cool, dry environment, Avoiding greasy and spicy foods
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When greasy, spicy foods or damp environments overwhelm the Spleen and Stomach, Dampness and Heat accumulate in the digestive tract. This obstructs the normal descending of Stomach Qi and the Spleen's ability to transform and transport, leading to epigastric fullness, a bitter taste, and sticky stools. The persistent heat and dampness can erode the mucosal lining, contributing to the formation of a duodenal ulcer.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red body, possibly swollen with teeth marks, thick yellow greasy coating especially at centre and root. Pulse: soggy (Ru) and rapid (Shu), often also slippery (Hua).
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Greasy and spicy foods directly introduce Dampness and Heat into the body, aggravating the obstruction in the Stomach and Spleen. Light, bland foods reduce the burden on digestion, allowing the Spleen to transform and transport fluids normally. Gentle movement encourages Qi circulation, which helps drain Dampness, while a dry environment prevents external Dampness from invading. Emotional stress can disrupt Qi flow and contribute to stagnation, making Damp-Heat harder to clear.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Avoid greasy, fried, spicy, and sweet foods, as well as alcohol and dairy, which generate Dampness and Heat. Eat light, easily digestible meals like congee, steamed vegetables, and small portions of lean protein. Gentle daily exercise like walking helps the Spleen move Qi and drain Dampness. Keep living spaces dry and well-ventilated.

### Stomach Yin Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/stomach-yin-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Gnawing, burning upper abdominal pain, Feeling hungry but not wanting to eat, Dry mouth and throat, thirst for small sips, Dry stools or constipation, Red, thin tongue with little or no coating
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Spicy, fried, or dry foods, Alcohol and coffee, Late nights and overwork, Prolonged stress or worry
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Small, frequent moist meals, Warm, soupy foods, Rest and early nights, Gentle, cooling beverages like pear juice
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Stomach Yin is the cooling, moistening fluid that protects the stomach lining. When Yin runs low - often from chronic heat, spicy diets, or prolonged stress - the lining becomes dry and vulnerable. Without enough Yin to buffer it, the mucosal barrier weakens, allowing acid to erode the tissue and form an ulcer. The pain feels gnawing or burning because the empty heat from Yin deficiency irritates the nerves.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red, thin body, dry centre, little or no coating, possible cracks. Pulse: fine (细 xì) and rapid (数 shuò).
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: <p>Stomach Yin is like the body’s internal coolant and moisturizer. Spicy foods, alcohol, and late nights generate heat and dry up these precious fluids, making the ulcer pain worse. Stress and overwork also burn through Yin, leaving the stomach lining unprotected. Conversely, moist, gently cooked foods and rest help replenish Yin, cooling the empty heat and allowing the ulcer to heal.</p>
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Avoid spicy, fried, and dry foods, as well as alcohol and coffee, which further consume Yin. Eat small, frequent meals of moistening foods like congee, steamed pears, soups, and bone broths. Sip warm water throughout the day and go to bed early to allow Yin to regenerate overnight.

### Stomach Blood Stagnation
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/stomach-blood-stagnation)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Sharp, stabbing pain that stays in one place, Pain fixed in a single spot, Pain worsens at night, Dark purplish lips or dull complexion, Black tarry stools or vomiting of dark material
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Pressure on the abdomen, Overeating or heavy meals, Cold or raw foods, Stress and anger, Nighttime
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Gentle walking, Warm compress on the abdomen, Small, frequent meals, Stress reduction
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When a duodenal ulcer persists for a long time, the local blood circulation in the stomach lining becomes sluggish and obstructed. This forms a fixed area of blood stasis that acts like a bruise inside the tissue, causing sharp, stabbing pain that does not move. The stasis also prevents normal tissue repair, making the ulcer slow to heal and prone to bleeding episodes.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: purple or dark dusky body with possible purple stasis spots, distended sublingual veins, thin white coat. Pulse: choppy (涩 sè) or wiry (弦 xián).
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Blood stasis acts like a stagnant pool: it causes fixed, piercing pain that worsens when the area is pressed or burdened by a large meal. At night, when the body's Qi and blood move more slowly, the stasis becomes even more stuck, so pain intensifies. Cold and raw foods congeal blood, while emotional stress knots Qi, both of which trap blood flow further. Gentle walking and warmth encourage circulation, helping to break up the stasis. Small meals reduce the digestive load, and calming the mind prevents the Qi stagnation that leads to blood stasis.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat small, frequent, warm meals that are easy to digest, such as congee and well-cooked vegetables. Avoid cold, raw, and greasy foods that can congeal blood and worsen stasis. Gentle daily walking helps keep Qi and blood moving. Manage stress with deep breathing or meditation, as emotional tension can tighten the chest and abdomen, aggravating pain.

## คำถามที่พบบ่อย (Frequently Asked Questions)
### Can acupuncture really heal a duodenal ulcer?
Yes. Acupuncture restores the smooth flow of Qi in the Stomach and related channels, reduces inflammation, and promotes local tissue repair. It also calms the nervous system, which is especially important for stress-related ulcers. While acupuncture alone may not be enough for deep ulcers, it works well alongside herbs and diet changes to speed healing and reduce pain.

### How long does it take for Chinese herbs to work on an ulcer?
Most people notice a reduction in pain and bloating within 1-2 weeks of starting herbs. However, full ulcer healing typically takes 4-8 weeks, and the herbs should be continued even after symptoms improve to ensure the lining is fully repaired and the underlying imbalance is corrected. Consistency is key - missing doses can slow progress.

### Can I take Chinese herbs while on PPIs or antibiotics?
Yes, TCM herbs and acupuncture can safely complement conventional treatment. They may even help reduce side effects of antibiotics and support the healing process. Always tell both your doctor and your TCM practitioner about all medications and supplements you are taking. Never stop prescribed medications abruptly without consulting your doctor.

### Will I need to change my diet?
Yes, diet is an integral part of TCM treatment for duodenal ulcer. While specific advice depends on your pattern, general guidelines include avoiding raw, cold, spicy, and greasy foods, as well as coffee, alcohol, and smoking. Instead, focus on warm, cooked, easily digestible meals like congee, soups, and steamed vegetables. Eating small, frequent meals and chewing thoroughly also helps.

### Is TCM safe if I have H. pylori?
Absolutely. TCM cannot replace antibiotics for eradicating H. pylori, but it can support the body during and after antibiotic therapy, reduce side effects, and help heal the ulcer. After the bacteria are cleared, TCM works to restore digestive strength and prevent recurrence by addressing the underlying weaknesses that allowed the infection to take hold.

### Can stress cause my ulcer to come back?
In TCM, stress is a major trigger for duodenal ulcers because it directly disrupts the Liver's ability to keep Qi flowing smoothly. This stagnant Liver Qi then attacks the Stomach, leading to pain and bloating. Managing stress - through acupuncture, herbs, and lifestyle changes - is essential not just for healing the current ulcer but for preventing future ones.

### How do I know which TCM pattern I have?
A TCM practitioner will examine your tongue, feel your pulse, and ask detailed questions about your pain, digestion, emotions, and overall health. The type of pain (dull vs. sharp), what makes it better or worse, and associated symptoms like bloating, belching, or cold limbs all point to a specific pattern. You can also take our online quiz for a preliminary indication, but a professional diagnosis is recommended.
