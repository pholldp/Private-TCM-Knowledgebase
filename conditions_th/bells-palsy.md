## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนปัจจุบัน (Conventional Medicine View)
### คำอธิบาย (Description)
Bell's Palsy is an acute, usually one-sided weakness or paralysis of the facial muscles, caused by inflammation of the facial nerve. It appears suddenly - often overnight - making it hard to close the eye, smile, or raise the eyebrow on the affected side. Other symptoms may include drooling, altered taste, increased sensitivity to sound, and pain around the jaw or behind the ear. The exact cause is not fully understood, but reactivation of the herpes simplex virus is suspected in many cases. Diagnosis is made by ruling out other causes such as stroke or Lyme disease.

### การรักษาหลัก (Treatments)
Standard Western treatment aims to reduce nerve inflammation and speed recovery. A short course of oral corticosteroids like prednisone is the cornerstone, ideally started within 72 hours of symptom onset. Antiviral medication may be added if a viral cause is suspected. Eye protection is critical because the eyelid may not close fully - lubricating drops, ointments, and taping the eye shut at night are routine. Most people recover fully within three to six months, though some continue to have mild weakness or involuntary muscle movements.

## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนจีน (Traditional Chinese Medicine View)
### ความเข้าใจเกี่ยวกับการแพทย์แผนจีน (TCM Understanding)
TCM sees Bell's Palsy primarily as an obstruction of Qi and blood in the channels that nourish the face. The face is richly traversed by the Yangming (Stomach and Large Intestine), Taiyang (Small Intestine and Bladder), and Shaoyang (Gallbladder and Triple Burner) meridians. When a pathogenic factor - most often Wind - invades these superficial channels, the flow of Qi and blood stalls, and the facial muscles lose their ability to move. This is why the paralysis often appears so suddenly: Wind in TCM strikes like a gust, swift and unexpected.
The most common scenario is an invasion of external Wind, which often teams up with Cold or Heat. Wind-Cold causes the facial channels to contract and tighten, like pipes freezing in winter. Wind-Heat brings inflammation, redness, and a burning sensation. If the body's internal environment is already damp or phlegmy - often from a weak Spleen - Wind can stir that Phlegm upward into the face, creating a heavy, numb paralysis that feels different from the tightness of Wind-Cold.
Not every case starts from an outside invasion. If the body's Qi is chronically depleted, there may not be enough energy to push blood through the facial channels, leading to a flaccid, lingering droop that does not tighten. In some people, especially those under intense stress or with a tendency to high blood pressure, Liver Yang can surge upward, disrupting the facial channels from within. This is why TCM always asks about the circumstances around the onset: the trigger points directly to the pattern, and the pattern dictates the treatment.

### การวินิจฉัยโดยแพทย์แผนจีน (Practitioner Diagnosis)
A TCM practitioner begins by asking when the facial paralysis appeared and what it feels like. Sudden onset after exposure to cold wind or an open window points one way, while a slower, heavier sensation points another. The quality of discomfort, any accompanying symptoms, and the appearance of the tongue and pulse are the key clues that narrow down the pattern.
If the face feels tight, stiff, and painful, and the person strongly dislikes cold or draughts, this suggests a Wind‑Cold invasion. The tongue often looks pale with a thin white coat, and the pulse feels floating and tight. There is usually no fever or sweating, just a sensation of chilliness and a sudden inability to move one side of the face.
When the paralysed side feels hot, looks slightly red or swollen, and comes with a sore throat, thirst, or mild fever, the pattern is Wind‑Heat. The tongue appears red with a thin yellow coating, and the pulse is floating and rapid. This picture often follows an upper respiratory infection or exposure to hot, stuffy environments.
A heavy, numb, or “cotton‑wool” sensation in the face, along with a feeling of mucus in the throat or dizziness, points to Wind‑Phlegm. The tongue is often puffy with a greasy white coat, and the pulse feels slippery and wiry. This pattern arises when pre‑existing dampness and phlegm are stirred up by an external wind, clogging the facial channels.
If the paralysis has lasted weeks or months and the muscles feel flaccid or wasted, with a dull complexion and general fatigue, the practitioner suspects Qi Deficiency causing Blood Stagnation. The tongue may be pale or dark with stasis spots, and the pulse is thready and hesitant. This pattern reflects a deeper weakness where the body lacks the energy to move blood through the facial vessels.
In rare cases, the facial droop comes with dizziness, a flushed face, irritability, or a pulsing headache. This suggests Liver Yang Rising, often seen in people with high blood pressure or emotional strain. The tongue is red with a thin yellow coat, and the pulse is wiry, rapid, and forceful. Here the root is internal, with upward‑rushing yang disturbing the facial channels.

### Wind-Cold
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/wind-cold)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Sudden onset of facial paralysis, Facial muscle tightness and pain, Aversion to cold and chills, No sweating, Headache and stiff neck, Clear runny nose and sneezing
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Cold, damp, or windy weather and drafts, Cold or raw foods and drinks, Emotional stress, anger, or frustration, Overwork, exhaustion, or lack of sleep, Going out with a sweaty face
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warmth or warm compresses on the face, Rest and relaxation, Warm ginger and scallion tea, Gentle facial massage
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When a sudden draft of cold wind strikes the face while the body's defenses are weak, the Wind-Cold pathogen penetrates the superficial channels. This cold contracts and obstructs the flow of Qi and blood in the Yangming and Taiyang meridians that nourish the facial muscles. The result is a rapid loss of control on one side of the face, with tightness and pain because the channels are literally 'frozen' and unable to relax.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: normal or slightly pale body with a thin, white, moist coating. Pulse: floating and tight (浮紧 fú jǐn).
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Wind-Cold contracts and blocks the facial channels, so warmth and rest relax the muscles and reopen the pathways. Cold, stress, and exhaustion further tighten the blockage, making the droop and pain worse. Gentle heat and warm foods mimic the body's own defensive Qi, helping to expel the invader and restore flow.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Keep the face and neck warmly covered, especially in wind or air conditioning. Sip fresh ginger tea with a pinch of cinnamon to warm the channels from within. Eat warm, cooked soups and stews, and avoid raw, cold, or iced foods and drinks until the paralysis resolves.

### Wind-Heat
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/wind-heat)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Facial redness and warmth, Fever or feeling of body heat, Sore throat and thirst, Sudden onset of facial paralysis
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Greasy, fried, or dairy-rich foods, Hot or stuffy environments, Emotional stress, anger, or frustration, Cold, damp, or windy weather and drafts
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Cool or well-ventilated environment, Drinking chrysanthemum tea, Rest and relaxation, Cool compresses on the face
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Wind-Heat (风热, fēng rè) is an external pathogen that invades the body's surface when the protective Qi is weak. Instead of just causing a cold, it travels along the channels to the face, bringing heat and inflammation that block the flow of Qi and blood. This sudden obstruction starves the facial muscles of nourishment, leading to paralysis with redness and a burning sensation.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red tip and edges with a thin yellow coating (may be thin white early on). Pulse: floating and rapid.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Wind-Heat is an external pathogen that brings heat and inflammation to the facial channels. Spicy and greasy foods add internal heat, while stress stirs up liver fire, both aggravating the condition. Cooling foods and rest help the body clear heat and expel the pathogen, while avoiding wind prevents further invasion. Keeping the environment cool and calm supports the body's natural effort to discharge the exterior pathogen without adding more heat.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat cooling foods like pear, cucumber, and chrysanthemum tea. Avoid spicy, fried, or greasy foods that add internal heat. Rest in a cool, well-ventilated space and protect your face from direct wind or drafts.

### Wind-Phlegm
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/wind-phlegm)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Facial numbness and stiffness, Sensation of heaviness in the face, Dizziness or vertigo, Copious white frothy sputum, Chest stuffiness or fullness
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Cold, damp, or windy weather and drafts, Greasy, fried, or dairy-rich foods, Prolonged inactivity or sedentary lifestyle, Emotional stress, anger, or frustration
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warmth or warm compresses on the face, Gentle exercise or walking, Light, cooked foods, Avoiding dairy and greasy foods, Rest and relaxation
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When the Spleen is weak and fails to transform fluids, dampness accumulates and thickens into phlegm. If an external Wind then invades the body, it stirs this internal phlegm upward into the channels of the face. The mixture of Wind and Phlegm obstructs the facial collaterals, blocking the flow of Qi and blood and causing the muscles to become numb, stiff, and heavy.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: swollen with teeth marks, white greasy coating. Pulse: wiry and slippery (弦滑 xián huá).
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Phlegm forms when the Spleen fails to transform fluids, a process worsened by greasy, cold, and damp-producing foods. Wind stirs this phlegm upward, so emotional stress and exposure to drafts can trigger or aggravate facial paralysis. Warmth, gentle movement, and a clear, light diet help the Spleen function, resolve phlegm, and keep Qi flowing smoothly through the facial channels.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Avoid cold, raw, greasy, and dairy-rich foods that create dampness and phlegm. Favour warm, cooked meals with mild spices like ginger and cardamom to support digestion. Gentle daily movement such as walking or tai chi helps move Qi and resolve phlegm. Keep the face protected from wind and cold drafts, and manage stress to prevent internal wind from stirring.

### Qi Deficiency causing Blood Stagnation
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/qi-deficiency-causing-blood-stagnation)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Flaccid facial muscles (no tightness), Dull or dusky facial complexion, Persistent fatigue and weakness, Long-standing or recurrent facial paralysis, Fixed stabbing pain that worsens with pressure
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Overwork, exhaustion, or lack of sleep, Cold, damp, or windy weather and drafts, Cold or raw foods and drinks, Emotional stress, anger, or frustration, Prolonged inactivity or sedentary lifestyle
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Rest and relaxation, Warmth or warm compresses on the face, Nourishing soups and stews, Gentle facial massage
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  After the acute phase of Bell's Palsy, if the body's Qi remains depleted, there is not enough force to propel blood through the facial channels. This leads to blood stasis, which starves the facial muscles of nourishment, resulting in flaccid paralysis and a persistent droop that does not tighten with cold or wind. The complexion often appears dull or dusky because stagnant blood is not refreshed.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale-dark or pale-purple body, possible stasis spots, teeth marks, distended sublingual veins, thin white coat. Pulse: deep, fine/weak, and choppy (涩 sè).
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Qi deficiency means the body's energy is low, so rest and nourishing foods are essential to rebuild the force that moves blood. Cold and raw foods deplete Spleen Qi and constrict blood vessels, worsening stasis, while warmth and gentle movement promote circulation. Overexertion consumes the little Qi available, leaving facial muscles weaker, and stress disrupts the smooth flow of Qi, further stagnating blood. The reliefs work by supporting Qi and gently activating blood, which is the core strategy of this pattern.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Focus on warm, cooked foods that nourish Qi and blood, such as soups with astragalus, chicken, and red dates. Avoid raw, cold, and greasy foods that can further weaken digestion. Gentle daily walking and facial exercises help stimulate circulation without depleting energy. Protect the face from cold wind with a scarf.

### Liver Yang Rising
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/liver-yang-rising)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Dizziness or vertigo, Throbbing or distending headache, especially at the temples, Flushed face and red eyes, Irritability and quick temper, Sensation of heat rising to the face, Ringing in the ears (tinnitus), Insomnia with vivid dreams
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Emotional stress, anger, or frustration, Greasy, fried, or dairy-rich foods, Alcohol and coffee, Hot or stuffy environments, Overwork, exhaustion, or lack of sleep
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Cool or well-ventilated environment, Rest and relaxation, Cooling foods (cucumber, pear), Gentle exercise or walking, Acupuncture or massage
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  The Liver stores Blood and ensures the smooth flow of Qi. When Liver Yin is deficient, Yang loses its anchor and surges upward, carrying internal Wind and heat to the head. This sudden upward disturbance of Yang disrupts the facial channels, causing muscle paralysis. The pattern often appears in people with high blood pressure or long-standing emotional strain, and the paralysis is accompanied by dizziness, a flushed face, and irritability.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red body with red sides, thin dry yellow or scanty coat. Pulse: wiry (弦 xián), especially forceful at the left Guan (middle) position; may be fine and rapid if Yin deficiency is prominent.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Stress and anger cause Liver Qi to surge upward, directly fueling the rising Yang that disrupts the face. Hot, spicy, or greasy foods add heat to the body, while alcohol and coffee stir Yang and deplete Yin further. In contrast, cooling foods and a calm environment help anchor the Yang, and rest allows the Liver to store Blood and nourish Yin. Gentle movement keeps Qi flowing without provoking upward surges.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat cooling, Yin-nourishing foods like cucumber, pear, tofu, and mung beans. Avoid spicy, greasy, and fried foods, alcohol, and coffee. Prioritize regular, restful sleep and gentle exercise such as walking or Tai Chi. Manage stress with meditation or deep breathing, and keep a calm, cool environment.

## คำถามที่พบบ่อย (Frequently Asked Questions)
### How quickly can acupuncture help Bell's Palsy?
Many patients notice the first subtle changes - such as less stiffness or a tiny flicker of movement - within the first one to two weeks of treatment. Significant visible improvement often appears within two to four weeks for acute external patterns. Acupuncture is typically given two to three times per week initially, and the earlier it is started after symptom onset, the more quickly the nerve tends to respond.

### Can I combine TCM with the prednisone my doctor prescribed?
Yes, TCM treatment works safely alongside corticosteroids. Acupuncture and herbal medicine do not interfere with prednisone, and many patients use both in the acute phase. Always tell both your TCM practitioner and your prescribing doctor about all treatments you are receiving. Do not stop prednisone abruptly - work with your doctor to taper it appropriately as your face recovers.

### Will I get better completely, or will my face stay uneven?
Complete recovery is the goal of TCM treatment, and most patients who start early achieve full or near-full restoration of facial function. Even in cases that have lingered for months or have developed synkinesis, TCM can often improve muscle tone, reduce tightness, and restore more natural movement, though the timeline is longer. The key is treating not just the nerve inflammation but also the underlying pattern that allowed the condition to take hold.

### What foods should I eat to help my face heal?
In general, favor warm, cooked, easy-to-digest foods that support your Spleen Qi, such as soups, congees, steamed vegetables, and small amounts of ginger. Avoid cold, raw foods and icy drinks, which can introduce more Wind and Cold. If your face feels heavy and numb, cut back on dairy, greasy, and fried foods that create phlegm. Your practitioner will refine these guidelines based on your specific pattern.

### Does acupuncture on the face hurt?
Facial acupuncture uses very fine needles and is usually more surprising than painful. You may feel a tiny pinch or a sensation of heaviness or tingling around the point, which is a sign that Qi is arriving. Most patients find the experience deeply relaxing and often notice a softening of facial tension right after the session.

### Can children get Bell's Palsy, and is TCM safe for them?
Yes, children can develop Bell's Palsy, and TCM offers gentle, effective treatment. Acupuncture can be adapted with fewer needles and shorter retention times, and herbal formulas are adjusted for weight and age. Pediatric cases often resolve quickly because children's Qi is more responsive. Always work with a practitioner experienced in treating children.

### Can TCM prevent long-term complications like synkinesis?
By keeping Qi and blood moving freely through the facial channels, TCM may reduce the risk of synkinesis and lingering tightness. Acupuncture and herbs work to restore balanced muscle tone rather than just forcing movement. In patients who already have synkinesis, targeted needling can help retrain the muscles and soften abnormal pulling over time.

## การวิจัยทางวิทยาศาสตร์สมัยใหม่ (Modern Scientific Research)
1. **Acupuncture for Bell's palsy** ([ลิงก์](https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD002914.pub5))
   *วารสารวิชาการ: Chen N, Zhou M, He L, Zhou D, Li N. Acupuncture for Bell's palsy. Cochrane Database Syst Rev. 2015;(8):CD002914.*
   Cochrane systematic review of 14 randomized controlled trials evaluating acupuncture for Bell's palsy. The authors concluded that acupuncture may improve recovery, but the evidence is limited by risk of bias and small sample sizes.

2. **Efficacy of acupuncture for Bell's palsy: a systematic review and meta-analysis of randomized controlled trials** ([ลิงก์](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0121880))
   *วารสารวิชาการ: Li P, Qiu T, Qin C. Efficacy of acupuncture for Bell's palsy: a systematic review and meta-analysis of randomized controlled trials. PLoS One. 2015;10(5):e0121880.*
   Meta-analysis of 14 RCTs including 2,178 patients. Acupuncture significantly improved the cure rate compared with conventional medication (RR 1.14, 95% CI 1.04-1.25), supporting its use as an effective treatment.

3. **Acupuncture for Bell's palsy: a multicenter randomized controlled trial** ([ลิงก์](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11655-012-1102-6))
   *วารสารวิชาการ: Zheng H, Chen M, Huang X, et al. Acupuncture for Bell's palsy: a multicenter randomized controlled trial. Chin J Integr Med. 2012;18(7):493-500.*
   Multicenter RCT of 120 patients comparing acupuncture plus prednisolone to prednisolone alone. The acupuncture group showed significantly better facial nerve function recovery at 6 months (p<0.05).
