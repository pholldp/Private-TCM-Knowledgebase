## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนปัจจุบัน (Conventional Medicine View)
### คำอธิบาย (Description)
Hypovolemic shock occurs when the body loses more than 20% of its blood or fluid supply, making the heart unable to pump enough blood to the organs. Common causes include severe bleeding from trauma, surgery, or gastrointestinal ulcers, as well as dehydration from burns, vomiting, or diarrhea. Without enough circulating volume, tissues are starved of oxygen, leading to rapid heart rate, low blood pressure, cold and clammy skin, confusion, and eventually organ failure.
Diagnosis is based on vital signs, physical exam, and blood tests. Immediate treatment focuses on stopping the source of fluid loss and replacing volume with intravenous fluids and blood products. It is one of the most time-critical emergencies in medicine.

### การรักษาหลัก (Treatments)
Standard emergency treatment for hypovolemic shock centers on rapid fluid resuscitation - typically intravenous crystalloids (like saline or Ringer's lactate) or colloids, and blood transfusions when hemorrhage is the cause. Medications called vasopressors may be used to constrict blood vessels and raise blood pressure if fluids alone are insufficient. Definitive care involves stopping the bleeding, whether through surgery, endoscopy, or other interventions.

## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนจีน (Traditional Chinese Medicine View)
### ความเข้าใจเกี่ยวกับการแพทย์แผนจีน (TCM Understanding)
In TCM, Blood is not just a fluid - it is the material foundation that houses the mind and carries warmth and vitality throughout the body. When a massive hemorrhage occurs, Blood rushes out, and with it goes Qi, the body's animating force. This sudden loss can trigger a collapse of the body's most fundamental energies: Yang, the warming, activating principle; Yin, the cooling, moistening foundation; or both. The result is a state of profound emptiness that TCM calls 血脱 (xuè tuō), or blood desertion.
The key to diagnosis lies in temperature and pulse. If the limbs turn ice-cold, the skin is drenched in cold sweat, and the pulse is barely perceptible or completely hidden, the collapse is primarily Yang - the body's internal fire has gone out. This pattern corresponds to Heart Yang Collapsing or Collapse of Yang and requires immediate warming and revival. If instead the person feels restless, the palms and soles are warm, the skin is hot and oily, and the tongue is deep red and dry, the collapse is Yin - the body's cooling fluids have been drained, leaving unanchored heat. This is Collapse of Yin.
Sometimes the dominant feature is not temperature but a sensation of hollow emptiness: extreme fatigue, a sinking feeling in the abdomen, and shortness of breath so severe the person can barely speak. This is Qi Collapsing, where the Spleen and Stomach's lifting function fails and Qi sinks downward with the blood. Underlying all of these acute patterns, a person with chronic Qi and Blood Deficiency is far more vulnerable to shock because their reserves are already depleted - even a moderate bleed can become catastrophic.
TCM's approach is to identify which vital substance has collapsed and to restore it immediately, using potent herbs and acupuncture points that act within minutes. For Yang collapse, warming, Qi-reviving formulas like Shen Fu Tang (Ginseng and Aconite Decoction) are used. For Yin collapse, cooling, fluid-generating formulas like Sheng Mai San (Generate the Pulse Powder) are given. These interventions aim not just to replace volume but to reignite the body's own capacity to hold and move blood.

### การวินิจฉัยโดยแพทย์แผนจีน (Practitioner Diagnosis)
A TCM practitioner first looks at the person’s overall energy and temperature. They ask whether the limbs feel icy cold or unusually warm, and whether the skin is dry or clammy with sweat. These clues immediately separate patterns where the body’s warming Yang has collapsed from those where cooling Yin fluids are depleted.
If the hands and feet are stone-cold, the face is ashen, and the pulse is barely detectable or very slow, the practitioner suspects Heart Yang Collapsing or Collapse of Yang. Heart Yang Collapse often brings a bluish tinge to the lips and a faint, scattered pulse, while Collapse of Yang shows a deep, thready pulse and profuse cold sweating all over.
When extreme weakness and breathlessness dominate the picture, and the bleeding was sudden and heavy, the focus shifts to Qi Collapsing. Here the voice is barely a whisper, and the person feels too exhausted to move. The tongue is pale and the pulse is weak but not necessarily cold or rapid - the key is that the body’s vital force is sinking away.
If the person complains of thirst, feels restless, and has warm palms and soles despite the blood loss, the practitioner thinks of Collapse of Yin. The tongue will be red with little or no coating, and the pulse will be rapid and thin, like a fine thread. This pattern often appears when blood loss also drains the body’s cooling and moistening yin fluids.
A less acute but important pattern is Qi and Blood Deficiency. This may be the background state before shock, or a lingering aftermath. The person looks pale, tires easily, and may have dizziness. The tongue is pale and slightly dry, and the pulse is consistently weak and thready, without the dramatic cold or heat signs of the collapse patterns.

## กลุ่มอาการการแพทย์แผนจีนและการรักษา (TCM Patterns and Treatment)

### Heart Yang Collapsing
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/heart-yang-collapsing)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Profuse cold sweating, especially on the forehead, Ice-cold limbs past elbows or knees, Blue-purple lips and nail beds, Severe palpitations or fluttering heartbeat, Extremely weak or barely perceptible breathing, Ashen-white or grey complexion
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Cold exposure, Blood loss, Physical exertion
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warmth and blankets, Emergency medical care, Rest
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  The Heart's Yang is the fire that pumps blood through the body. When severe bleeding empties the vessels, the Heart loses its fuel and its Yang collapses, causing the body to shut down. The limbs become icy as Yang withdraws inward, and the pulse becomes faint because there is no force to push blood to the surface.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: Very pale or bluish-purple, short and flaccid, wet with a thin white slippery coat. Pulse: Minute (Wei), barely perceptible, or Hidden (Fu), possibly Knotted (Jie) with slow irregular pauses.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Yang is the body's warming and driving force. Cold and further blood loss drain the last of the Heart's Yang, deepening collapse. Warmth, rest, and moxibustion preserve and gently restore the remaining Yang, helping the body hold on until emergency care arrives.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: This is a medical emergency requiring immediate hospital care. After stabilization, avoid cold raw foods and overexertion. Eat warm, easily digestible soups with a little ginger and ginseng to rebuild Qi and Yang. Keep the body warm and rest completely.

### Collapse of Yang
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/collapse-of-yang)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Profuse cold sweating that does not stop, Ice-cold limbs extending past elbows and knees, Ashen-white or greyish complexion, Blue or purple lips and fingernails, Severe aversion to cold with desire to curl up, Mental apathy, drowsiness, or unconsciousness, Extremely faint or absent pulse
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Cold exposure, Blood loss, Physical exertion, Emotional distress, Cold food and drink
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warmth and blankets, Rest, Warm fluids, Moxibustion on the lower abdomen, Gentle, deep breathing
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When the body loses a massive amount of blood, the foundation of Yang Qi is drained away. Blood and Qi are inseparable; blood carries the warmth and activity of Yang. Without enough blood to anchor it, Yang suddenly scatters and collapses, leaving the body cold, pale, and barely functioning. This is the classic picture of hemorrhagic shock where the life-fire is on the verge of going out.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale, moist, flaccid body with a thin white slippery coating. Pulse: extremely faint (minute, wei) and on the verge of disappearing, or completely impalpable.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Warmth and rest conserve the dwindling Yang Qi, while cold, exertion, and further blood loss drain it further. The body's vital fire is like a small flame; it must be protected from wind and cold and fed with gentle, warm nourishment. Moxibustion directly infuses Yang Qi into the body's core, helping to rekindle the fire.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: This is a medical emergency requiring immediate hospital care. After stabilization, warm, easy-to-digest foods like rice congee with a little fresh ginger and ginseng can help rebuild Qi and Yang. Avoid all cold, raw, or difficult-to-digest foods, and keep the person warm and protected from drafts.

### Qi Collapsing or Qi Sinking
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/qi-collapsing-or-qi-sinking)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Profound fatigue and weakness that feels like the body is sinking, Shortness of breath with a low, weak voice, A bearing-down or empty sensation in the abdomen, Dizziness and lightheadedness that worsens when standing
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Physical exertion, Cold food and drink, Blood loss
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Rest, Warmth and quiet, Small, warm, easily digested meals
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Qi holds blood inside the vessels. When a massive bleed happens, Qi rushes out with the blood - this is called "Qi collapsing with the blood." The Spleen and Stomach Qi, which are responsible for keeping organs and tissues in their proper place, become too weak to lift. The result is a sudden, profound weakness, a feeling of sinking or emptiness inside, and a body that can no longer hold itself up.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale, puffy, and teeth-marked with a thin white coat. Pulse: empty and weak overall, especially at the right Guan position; it may feel as though it disappears under firm pressure.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: When Qi sinks after a major bleed, the body's lifting and holding functions fail. Any activity, especially standing, demands Qi that simply isn't there, worsening the sinking sensation and weakness. Lying flat reduces this demand and helps the remaining Qi stay centered. Cold foods further damage the already depleted Spleen Yang, while warm, simple foods require minimal Qi to digest, allowing the body to direct its energy toward recovery.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: After an acute bleed, absolute rest is essential - even speaking can deplete precious Qi. Once stable, eat small, frequent, warm meals of easily digested foods like congee, well-cooked root vegetables, and bone broths to rebuild the Spleen's strength. Avoid raw, cold, or heavy foods that demand too much digestive energy.

### Collapse of Yin
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/collapse-of-yin)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Profuse hot, sticky sweat that beads like oil, Burning hot body with warm hands and feet, Intense thirst with desire for cold drinks, Intense restlessness and agitation, Deep red, dry, cracked tongue with no coating (mirror tongue), Flushed red face
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Heat or warm environment, Physical exertion, Emotional distress, Spicy, drying foods
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Cool, quiet, dark environment, Sipping cold water slowly, Rest
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When massive blood loss occurs, it drains not only blood but also the body's deep yin fluids-the cooling, moistening foundation that anchors yang. Without yin to hold it down, unanchored yang rises as empty heat, burning up the remaining fluids and creating a state of internal dryness and heat. This is a critical collapse of the body's material substance, leading to organ failure if not reversed.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: deep red or crimson, completely dry, no coating (mirror tongue), possibly cracked and shrunken. Pulse: fine (thin), rapid, and racing, without force, may feel scattered or hollow.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Yin is the body's cooling and moistening resource. Heat and exertion consume the little Yin that remains, allowing unanchored Yang to flare up further. Rest and a cool environment help preserve Yin, while small sips of cold water provide direct moisture without overwhelming the weakened system. Calming the mind prevents the emotional fire from adding to the empty heat.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: This is a medical emergency requiring immediate hospital care. In recovery, sip small amounts of cool water or electrolyte drinks. Once stable, warm bone broths and rice congee gently rebuild fluids and blood. Avoid spicy, dry, or fried foods that consume Yin. Complete rest in a cool, quiet room is essential.

### Qi and Blood Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/qi-and-blood-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Persistent fatigue and lack of energy, Pale or sallow complexion, Dizziness or lightheadedness, Heart palpitations, Poor appetite, Pale lips and nail beds
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Overwork and exhaustion, Skipping meals, Cold food and drink, Blood loss
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Rest, Warm, nourishing foods, Gentle movement, Iron-rich broths
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When Qi and Blood are chronically deficient, the body's ability to withstand blood loss is severely weakened. Qi is the commander of Blood - it holds Blood inside the vessels and moves it through the body. If Qi is already too weak to contain the Blood, even a moderate hemorrhage can cause a dangerous drop in blood volume, leading to shock. At the same time, insufficient Blood fails to nourish the heart and brain, so the person becomes dizzy, pale, and easily collapses when stressed.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale body, may be thin or teeth-marked, thin white coating. Pulse: fine (thin) and weak, especially in the right Guan position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Rest and sleep allow the body to conserve and rebuild Qi, while warm, nourishing foods directly provide the building blocks for Blood. Overwork and exhaustion drain Qi, which is already too weak to hold Blood, making shock more likely. Cold and raw foods damage the Spleen, the source of Qi and Blood production, further weakening the body's ability to recover from or withstand blood loss.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Focus on warm, easily digested foods that build Blood, such as bone broth, congee with red dates and goji berries, and small amounts of well-cooked red meat or liver. Avoid raw, cold, and greasy foods that tax the Spleen. Gentle daily movement like walking or qigong helps Qi circulate, but stop before you feel tired. Prioritize sleep and avoid overwork - rest is the most direct way to conserve and rebuild Qi and Blood.

## คำถามที่พบบ่อย (Frequently Asked Questions)
### Can acupuncture really help in a medical emergency like shock?
Yes, specific acupuncture points have been used for centuries to revive consciousness and support blood pressure in collapse states. Points like Baihui (DU-20) on the top of the head and Zusanli (ST-36) on the leg are known to raise Yang and strengthen Qi. In a modern emergency setting, acupuncture is used as an adjunct - never a replacement - to standard resuscitation. It can help stabilize the patient while fluids and medications are being administered.

### What Chinese herbs are used for severe blood loss?
The most famous emergency formula is Shen Fu Tang, which combines ginseng (Ren Shen) to powerfully restore Qi and prepared aconite (Zhi Fu Zi) to revive collapsed Yang. For Yin collapse with heat signs, Sheng Mai San - ginseng, ophiopogon tuber, and schisandra fruit - is used to generate fluids and anchor Yin. After the acute phase, formulas like Ba Zhen Tang (Eight Treasure Decoction) are used to rebuild both Qi and Blood over weeks to months.

### Is it safe to combine Chinese herbs with blood transfusions and IV fluids?
Generally, yes. The herbs used in acute shock are not known to interfere with transfusions or intravenous fluids. However, always inform your medical team about any herbal treatment you are receiving. Some herbs, particularly those that strongly move blood, could theoretically increase bleeding risk, so they are avoided in active hemorrhage. A qualified TCM practitioner will select formulas appropriate for the emergency context.

### How does TCM help recovery after a major bleed?
After the acute shock is reversed, many patients experience lingering fatigue, cold intolerance, dizziness, and poor concentration. TCM sees this as a state of Qi and Blood Deficiency or residual Yang weakness. Herbal formulas like Ba Zhen Tang or Bu Zhong Yi Qi Tang are used to rebuild the Spleen and Stomach's ability to produce new blood, while acupuncture and moxibustion strengthen the body's core energy. This can shorten recovery time and improve quality of life compared to rest alone.

### What does a 'collapsed pulse' feel like?
In TCM, a collapsed or 'minute' pulse is one that is barely perceptible under the fingers - extremely faint, thready, and often slow or rapid. It feels as though the pulse is about to disappear. This indicates that the body's Qi and Yang are severely depleted and unable to push blood to the surface. It is a critical sign that demands immediate intervention.

### Can moxibustion be used in shock?
Yes, moxibustion - the burning of dried mugwort near specific points - is a classic emergency technique for Yang collapse. Applying moxa to points like Shenque (REN-8, the navel) or Guanyuan (REN-4) can rapidly warm the body and revive Yang. It is especially useful when the patient is too cold and weak for needles alone.

## การวิจัยทางวิทยาศาสตร์สมัยใหม่ (Modern Scientific Research)
1. **Shenfu injection for shock: a systematic review and meta-analysis of randomized controlled trials**
   *วารสารวิชาการ: Zhang Q, Li C, Shao F, et al. Shenfu injection for shock: a systematic review and meta-analysis. J Evid Based Med. 2012;5(3):152-160.*
   This meta-analysis pooled data from multiple RCTs and found that Shenfu injection, when added to standard care, significantly improved systolic blood pressure and reduced 28-day mortality in patients with various types of shock, including hemorrhagic shock. However, the authors noted that trial quality was generally low, and larger, well-designed studies are needed.
