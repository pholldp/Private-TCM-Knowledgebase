## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนปัจจุบัน (Conventional Medicine View)
### คำอธิบาย (Description)
Heavy menstrual bleeding, also called menorrhagia, is defined as excessive blood loss (typically over 80 mL per cycle) that interferes with daily life, or periods lasting longer than 7 days. It can be caused by hormonal imbalances, uterine fibroids, polyps, adenomyosis, bleeding disorders, or sometimes no identifiable cause.
Diagnosis usually involves a detailed history, pelvic exam, ultrasound, and blood tests. In some cases, an endometrial biopsy or hysteroscopy may be recommended to rule out more serious conditions.

### การรักษาหลัก (Treatments)
Common conventional treatments include hormonal contraceptives (pills, patches, or hormone-releasing IUDs) to thin the uterine lining, tranexamic acid to reduce bleeding, and NSAIDs like ibuprofen to ease cramping and lighten flow. For some women, surgical options such as endometrial ablation, myomectomy, or hysterectomy are considered when other treatments don't help or when structural causes are present.

## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนจีน (Traditional Chinese Medicine View)
### ความเข้าใจเกี่ยวกับการแพทย์แผนจีน (TCM Understanding)
TCM sees heavy menstrual bleeding as a problem of the Chong (Penetrating) and Ren (Directing) vessels - the two main channels that supply and regulate the uterus. When these vessels are stable and well-nourished, menstrual flow is moderate and regular. But if the body's internal balance is disturbed, the vessels lose their grip, and blood escapes in excess. The Spleen, Liver, and sometimes the Kidneys are the organ systems most commonly involved, because they govern the blood, the smooth flow of Qi, and the body's holding power.
One classic scenario is Spleen Qi deficiency. The Spleen has the vital job of keeping blood inside the vessels. When it's weakened - often by overwork, poor diet, or chronic worry - it can't hold the blood, and periods become heavy, pale, and watery. The woman feels deeply tired and may have a dragging sensation in the lower belly. This is a deficiency pattern, where the body lacks the strength to contain the flow.
In other cases, excess Heat is the culprit. Heat in the uterus makes the blood reckless, like boiling water overflowing a pot. The flow is heavy, bright or deep red, and thick or sticky, often accompanied by thirst, restlessness, and a feeling of heat.
A third pattern involves Blood Stagnation: when blood gets stuck in the uterine vessels, it backs up and then bursts through, causing heavy, dark, clotted periods with sharp cramps that ease once clots pass.
Finally, Damp-Heat can pour down the Liver channel into the uterus, producing heavy, sticky, foul-smelling flow with genital irritation.
Because each pattern has a completely different root, the same diagnosis of heavy periods can require very different treatments. That's why TCM doesn't just ask "how heavy is your flow?" - it looks at colour, texture, pain, and accompanying symptoms to find the true cause and restore balance.

### การวินิจฉัยโดยแพทย์แผนจีน (Practitioner Diagnosis)
A TCM practitioner starts by asking about the colour and texture of the menstrual blood, because these qualities are the strongest initial clues. If the flow is pale red and thin, it points toward a deficiency pattern, while bright or dark red and thick blood suggests heat, and dark purple blood with clots signals stagnation. The timing and accompanying sensations - such as pain, fatigue, or thirst - further narrow the picture.
In Spleen not controlling Blood, the blood is typically pale, thin, and watery, and the woman often feels deeply tired, short of breath, and may experience a dragging sensation in the lower abdomen. The tongue is pale with a thin white coat, and the pulse feels weak and thready. This pattern reflects a lack of Qi to hold blood in the vessels, so the focus is on signs of general Qi deficiency.
When Heat in Uterus Blood is the cause, the menstrual flow is heavy, bright or deep red, and thick or sticky, sometimes with small clots. The woman often feels hot, thirsty, and restless, with a dry mouth and concentrated urine. The tongue is red with a yellow coating, and the pulse is rapid and slippery. These heat signs - especially the bright red colour and sensation of warmth - distinguish it from the pale, tired picture of Qi deficiency.
Blood Stagnation in the Directing and Penetrating Vessels presents with dark, purplish blood and significant clots, accompanied by cramping or stabbing pain that may be relieved after clots pass. The tongue appears purplish or has dark spots, and the pulse feels choppy or wiry. This pattern stands out because of the pain and the dark, clotted nature of the flow, which is quite different from the thin, pale bleeding of deficiency or the hot, thick bleeding of heat.
The less common Damp-Heat in the Liver Channel pattern adds sticky, malodorous menstrual flow and a sensation of heaviness in the lower body. The tongue coating is yellow and greasy, and the pulse is slippery and rapid. Unlike pure heat, damp-heat brings a turbid, sticky quality and often includes signs like vaginal discharge or a bitter taste in the mouth, helping the practitioner differentiate it from simple heat in the uterus.

### Spleen not controlling Blood
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/spleen-not-controlling-blood)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Heavy periods with pale, thin blood, Extreme fatigue and shortness of breath, Poor appetite and loose stools, Dizziness and lightheadedness
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Overwork and physical exhaustion, Raw, cold, or greasy foods, Worry and mental strain, Cold and damp environments, Prolonged standing
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Rest and adequate sleep, Warmth on the lower belly, Warm, easily digestible foods (soups, congee), Gentle exercise, Rest and emotional calm
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Your Spleen has the important job of holding blood inside the vessels. When Spleen Qi is weak-often from overwork, poor diet, or chronic worry-it can no longer keep the blood in place. This allows blood to leak out, leading to heavy menstrual flow that is pale and thin because there isn't enough Qi to give it rich color and substance.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale, puffy, tender body with teeth marks and a thin white coating. Pulse: fine (thin) and weak, especially weak at the right Guan position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Spleen Qi is easily weakened by overwork, worry, and cold, heavy foods. Rest and warmth conserve Qi, while warm, simple meals provide nourishment without straining the digestive system. Gentle movement keeps Qi flowing without depleting it. Because the Spleen governs the muscles and holding function, standing for long periods consumes Qi and can worsen bleeding, while lying down helps the Spleen recover its grip on the blood.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat warm, cooked, easily digestible foods like soups, congee, and stews. Avoid raw, cold, and greasy foods that tax the Spleen. Prioritize rest and gentle movement such as walking or tai chi. Keep your abdomen warm and avoid overwork, both physical and mental.

### Heat in Uterus Blood
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/heat-in-uterus-blood)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Early menstrual periods (arriving 7&#x2B; days ahead of schedule), Heavy flow with deep red or dark red, thick, sticky blood, Thirst with desire for cold drinks, Feeling of heat in the lower abdomen, Restlessness and irritability
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Spicy, greasy, or fried foods, Anger and emotional stress, Hot weather or overheating
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Cooling foods and drinks, Rest and emotional calm, Cool environment
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When excess Heat enters the Blood level, it stirs the Blood to move recklessly, much like boiling water overflows a pot. In the uterus, this Heat forces the menstrual blood out early and in heavy amounts. The Heat also thickens the blood, turning it deep or dark red and sticky, while consuming the body's fluids to create thirst, dry mouth, and dark urine.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red body, especially at the tip, with possible prickles and a thin yellow dry coat. Pulse: rapid (数 shuò), often wiry (弦 xián) or slippery (滑 huá).
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Heat in the Blood is directly fueled by heating foods, emotional fire like anger, and a hot external environment. These factors stir the Blood to move recklessly, worsening the heavy, early flow. Cooling foods, rest, and a calm mind work in the opposite direction-they clear Heat, soothe the Blood, and allow the body to retain its fluids, which naturally lightens and regulates the menstrual flow.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Focus on cooling, hydrating foods like cucumber, watermelon, mung beans, and leafy greens. Avoid spicy, greasy, or fried foods, alcohol, and coffee, which add Heat. Gentle exercise like walking or yoga can help release pent-up frustration without overheating the body. Prioritize rest during your period and keep your environment cool.

### Blood Stagnation in the Directing and Penetrating Vessels
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/blood-stagnation-in-the-directing-and-penetrating-vessels)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Sharp, stabbing cramps that improve after passing clots, Dark purple or blackish menstrual blood with clots, Fixed, pressing pain in the lower abdomen, Purple or dusky tongue with possible stasis spots
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Cold weather or icy drinks, Prolonged sitting or inactivity, Stress and emotional upset, Heavy, greasy foods
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warmth on the lower belly, Gentle exercise, Passing clots, Warm, cooked meals
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Blood Stagnation in the Directing (Ren) and Penetrating (Chong) vessels acts like a dam inside the uterus. When blood cannot flow freely, it backs up and then bursts through, causing a heavy flow that is dark and clotted. The sharp, fixed cramps are from the stuck blood trying to push its way out, and the pain eases once the clots pass and the blockage is temporarily cleared.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: dark purple or dusky body, possible stasis spots, engorged sublingual veins, thin white coat. Pulse: choppy (rough, uneven) and often wiry, especially at the left middle position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Cold and stillness cause blood to congeal, making the stasis worse, while warmth and gentle movement encourage blood to flow freely. Stress tightens the Liver Qi, which is the main driver of smooth blood movement, so emotional upset directly worsens the blockage. Passing clots temporarily relieves the obstruction, which is why the pain eases afterward.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Avoid cold foods and icy drinks, which congeal blood and worsen stasis. Gentle daily movement like walking or yoga keeps pelvic circulation open. A warm compress or hot water bottle on the lower abdomen during your period can ease cramps and encourage clots to pass smoothly.

### Damp-Heat in the Liver Channel
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/damp-heat-in-the-liver-channel)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Heavy, sticky, foul-smelling menstrual flow, Genital itching or burning, Bitter taste in the mouth, Yellow greasy tongue coating, Dark, scanty urine or painful urination, Feeling of heaviness in the body
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Spicy, greasy, or fried foods, Alcohol, Anger and emotional stress, Hot, humid environments, Prolonged sitting or inactivity
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Cooling foods and drinks, Rest and emotional calm, Gentle exercise, Keeping the genital area clean and dry
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  The Liver channel travels through the genital area. When Damp-Heat builds up in the Liver channel, it pours down into the uterus, heating the blood and making it reckless. The Dampness makes the menstrual flow sticky and foul-smelling, while the Heat causes it to be heavy and possibly bright or dark red.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red body with redder sides, yellow greasy coating thicker at the centre and root. Pulse: wiry (弦 xián), slippery (滑 huá), and rapid (数 shuò).
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Damp-Heat is produced by a combination of dietary and emotional factors. Greasy, spicy foods and alcohol create internal Dampness and Heat, while stress and anger stagnate Liver Qi, which turns into Heat. A cooling diet and gentle movement help clear Heat and dry Dampness, while rest and stress reduction allow Liver Qi to flow smoothly.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Avoid greasy, fried, spicy, and rich foods that generate Damp-Heat. Favour cooling, light foods such as mung beans, cucumber, bitter gourd, and barley. Stay hydrated and avoid alcohol. Gentle exercise like walking or yoga helps move Qi and dry Dampness.

## คำถามที่พบบ่อย (Frequently Asked Questions)
### How long does it take for Chinese herbs to help heavy periods?
Many women begin to see changes within the first full cycle - for example, less clotting or less fatigue during their period. A noticeable reduction in flow volume typically takes 2-3 menstrual cycles of consistent herbal use. If your pattern is primarily excess (like Heat or Stagnation), improvement may come sooner; if it's a deep deficiency, it can take a few months longer to rebuild the body's reserves.

### Can acupuncture alone stop heavy bleeding?
Acupuncture can help regulate the menstrual cycle and reduce flow, especially when used regularly (often weekly). However, for heavy bleeding, it's usually most effective when combined with Chinese herbal medicine, which works internally to address the underlying pattern. Acupuncture is excellent for calming the nervous system, moving Qi, and supporting the uterus, but herbs provide the deeper, sustained nourishment or clearing that heavy bleeding often requires.

### Is it safe to take Chinese herbs if I'm on birth control or other medications?
In most cases, yes - but it's essential to tell both your TCM practitioner and your doctor about everything you're taking. Some herbs in formulas for heavy bleeding (like San Qi or Yi Mu Cao) have blood-moving properties and could theoretically interact with anticoagulant medications. Herbs are generally compatible with hormonal contraceptives, but your practitioner will choose a formula that doesn't interfere with your specific treatment. Never stop prescribed medications without consulting your doctor.

### What foods should I eat if I have heavy periods?
In general, favour warm, cooked foods that support blood production and Spleen Qi - think bone broths, soups, stews, root vegetables, and leafy greens. Iron-rich foods like dark leafy greens, black sesame seeds, and moderate amounts of red meat can help replenish what's lost. Avoid icy drinks, raw salads, and excessive cold or raw foods, which can weaken the Spleen and make bleeding harder to control. Spicy, greasy, and heavily processed foods tend to generate Heat and Dampness, so they're best minimized.

### Will the heavy periods come back after I stop TCM treatment?

## การวิจัยทางวิทยาศาสตร์สมัยใหม่ (Modern Scientific Research)
1. **The utilization of traditional Chinese medicine in patients with dysfunctional uterine bleeding in Taiwan: a nationwide population-based study** ([ลิงก์](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12906-017-1876-9))
   *วารสารวิชาการ: Yen HR, Chen YY, Huang TP, et al. The utilization of traditional Chinese medicine in patients with dysfunctional uterine bleeding in Taiwan: a nationwide population-based study. BMC Complement Altern Med. 2017;17(1):427.*
   This retrospective cohort study analyzed National Health Insurance data from Taiwan and found that over 60% of patients with dysfunctional uterine bleeding used TCM within one year of diagnosis. The most commonly prescribed Chinese herbal formulas were Gui Pi Tang, Bu Zhong Yi Qi Tang, and Si Wu Tang, and Spleen Qi deficiency was the predominant pattern identified. The study highlights the real-world integration of TCM for menstrual disorders.

2. **Treating Gynaecological Disorders with Traditional Chinese Medicine: A Review** ([ลิงก์](https://doi.org/10.3121/cmr.1.3.225))
   *วารสารวิชาการ: Scheid V. Treating Gynaecological Disorders with Traditional Chinese Medicine: A Review. Clin Med Res. 2003;1(3):225-234.*
   This review discusses the TCM approach to common gynecological conditions, including menorrhagia. It outlines key patterns such as Heat in the Blood, Blood Stagnation, and Spleen Qi deficiency, and describes representative formulas. The review emphasizes the importance of pattern differentiation and notes that while clinical evidence is largely based on case series and tradition, TCM offers a coherent framework for individualized treatment.
