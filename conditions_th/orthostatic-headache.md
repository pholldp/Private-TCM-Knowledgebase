## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนปัจจุบัน (Conventional Medicine View)
### คำอธิบาย (Description)
In Western medicine, a headache that worsens upon standing is called an orthostatic headache. It can be caused by a drop in cerebrospinal fluid pressure (spontaneous intracranial hypotension), postural orthostatic tachycardia syndrome (POTS), or other conditions. Diagnosis often involves a neurological exam, MRI, or measuring blood pressure and heart rate changes with posture.

### การรักษาหลัก (Treatments)
Treatment depends on the cause. For low CSF pressure, bed rest, hydration, caffeine, and sometimes an epidural blood patch. For POTS, increased salt and fluid intake, compression stockings, and medications to raise blood pressure or slow heart rate.

## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนจีน (Traditional Chinese Medicine View)
### ความเข้าใจเกี่ยวกับการแพทย์แผนจีน (TCM Understanding)
In TCM, the head is the highest point of the body and is called the 'seat of all Yang.' For it to function clearly, Qi and Blood must rise upward against gravity. Standing upright demands extra effort from the body's lifting forces-primarily Spleen Qi and Kidney Yang. When these systems are weakened, or when something blocks the upward path (like phlegm or blood stasis), the head is starved of nourishment the moment you stand, and pain results.
The Spleen and Stomach produce the Qi and Blood that nourish the head, so if they are deficient, a dull, achy headache appears with fatigue. The Kidneys provide the foundational Yang that warms and lifts, so if Kidney Yang is weak, the head feels hollow and empty. If the Liver's Yang loses its anchor due to stress, it surges upward and creates a throbbing, distending pain. And if dampness or phlegm accumulates, it rises to cloud the head, causing a heavy, muffled sensation.
This is why one Western diagnosis of orthostatic headache can have six or more TCM patterns. The quality of the pain, the tongue appearance, and the pulse all reveal which system is out of balance. By treating the root cause-not just the head-TCM aims to restore the natural upward flow so that standing no longer triggers pain.

### การวินิจฉัยโดยแพทย์แผนจีน (Practitioner Diagnosis)
A practitioner begins by asking about the quality and timing of the headache, as well as what makes it better or worse. The fact that it appears or worsens upon standing is the common thread, but the distinct feel of the pain and the accompanying signs quickly narrow the diagnosis to one pattern.
If the headache is dull and aching, worsens with any exertion, and is accompanied by fatigue, pale complexion, and heart palpitations, the pattern is likely Qi and Blood Deficiency. The tongue is pale with a thin coat, and the pulse is weak. The body simply lacks the resources to nourish the head against gravity.
When the headache feels hollow or empty and is paired with dizziness, cold hands and feet, and a sore lower back, Kidney Yang Deficiency is the probable cause. The tongue appears pale and swollen, and the pulse is deep and thin. Here the warming, lifting function of Kidney Yang has weakened, so standing fails to send clear Qi upward.
A heavy, muffled sensation, as if the head is wrapped in a cloth, points to Damp-Phlegm. Standing encourages turbid phlegm to rise and block the clear orifices. A greasy white tongue coat and a slippery pulse are classic signs, often joined by chest oppression, nausea, or a foggy feeling in the head.
A fixed, stabbing pain that may worsen at night, and sometimes intensifies when upright due to gravitational congestion, suggests Blood Stagnation. The tongue often shows dark spots or a purplish color, and the pulse feels choppy. A history of head injury or chronic pain can be a helpful clue.
If the headache is distending or throbbing, flares with stress or heat, and is accompanied by irritability, Liver Yang Rising is likely. Standing can aggravate the upward surge. The tongue is red with a yellow coat, and the pulse is wiry and rapid. This pattern often has clear emotional triggers.
A headache that feels like an empty, unsupported sensation in the head, with tinnitus, dizziness, and a sore back, points to Kidney Essence Deficiency. The tongue is red with little coating, and the pulse is deep and thin. This reflects a deeper, more chronic depletion than simple Qi and Blood Deficiency, often seen with aging or prolonged illness.

## กลุ่มอาการการแพทย์แผนจีนและการรักษา (TCM Patterns and Treatment)

### Qi and Blood Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/qi-and-blood-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Dull, aching headache, Worsens with standing or exertion, Improves with lying down, Persistent fatigue and weakness, Pale complexion, lips, and nail beds, Dizziness or lightheadedness, Poor appetite
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Prolonged standing, Overwork and exhaustion, Skipping meals, Stress and worry
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Lying down to rest, Rest and sleep, Warm, nourishing meals, Gentle movement or exercise
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Qi and Blood are the fundamental energies that nourish every tissue in the body. When they are deficient, the head-the highest point-receives the least supply, especially when standing, because gravity makes it harder for the depleted Qi to push Blood upward. This leads to a dull, aching headache that improves when lying down, as recumbency allows the meager resources to more easily reach the brain. The persistent fatigue and pale complexion confirm that the root problem is an overall lack of nourishment.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale body, may be thin or have teeth marks, with a thin white coating. Pulse: fine and weak, especially in the right Guan position (Spleen and Stomach).
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: When Qi and Blood are deficient, the body struggles to circulate them against gravity. Standing upright demands that Qi lift Blood to the head; if resources are low, this fails, causing pain. Lying down removes that demand, allowing the depleted supply to reach the brain. Rest and nourishing food replenish Qi and Blood, while overwork and standing deplete them further. Stress and worry consume Spleen Qi, which is responsible for producing Blood, so they worsen the deficiency.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat warm, cooked foods like soups, stews, and congees to support Spleen Qi and Blood production. Include dark leafy greens, red dates, and moderate amounts of animal protein. Avoid cold, raw, and greasy foods that weaken digestion. Prioritize rest and avoid prolonged standing or overexertion.

### Kidney Yang Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/kidney-yang-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Hollow or empty headache sensation, Worse when standing or rising, Cold lower back and knees, Frequent urination, especially at night, Fatigue and low spirits
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Prolonged standing, Cold environments or drafts, Overwork and exhaustion, Cold or raw foods
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warmth on lower back, Lying down to rest, Warm, nourishing meals, Gentle movement or exercise
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Kidney Yang is the body's root of warmth and the force that lifts clear Qi to the head. When it weakens, the head is starved of both nourishment and warmth. Standing upright demands extra Yang to raise Qi upward, but a depleted Kidney cannot supply it, so the head feels hollow, empty, and painful. Lying down allows the body's remaining Yang to rest and settle, which is why the headache eases.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale, puffy, tender body with teeth marks and a wet white coating. Pulse: deep, slow, and weak, especially at the rear (Chi) positions.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Kidney Yang is the body's warming pilot light. Cold weather, cold foods, and overwork drain that fire, making the headache worse. Standing demands Yang to lift clear Qi to the head, which a depleted Kidney cannot do. Rest, warmth, and gentle movement conserve and gently stoke the remaining Yang, allowing the head to receive the warmth and nourishment it needs.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Keep the lower back and feet warm, and avoid cold or raw foods. A diet with warm, cooked meals and modest amounts of lamb, walnuts, and ginger helps sustain Yang. Gentle movement like walking or tai chi supports circulation without exhausting Qi, but rest lying down when the headache strikes.

### Damp-Phlegm
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/damp-phlegm)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Head feels heavy or wrapped, Chest and upper abdominal stuffiness, Nausea or a bland taste in the mouth, Thick, white, greasy tongue coating
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Greasy, rich, or sweet foods, Damp or humid weather, Overeating or skipping meals, Prolonged standing
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Lying down to rest, Light, warm, dry foods, Gentle movement or exercise
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When the Spleen is weak, it fails to transform fluids, and dampness collects into thick, turbid phlegm. This phlegm-dampness rises and obstructs the clear orifices in the head, creating a heavy, muffled sensation. Standing encourages turbid Yin to surge upward, worsening the blockage and triggering the headache. Lying down allows the turbidity to settle, which is why the pain eases when you rest flat.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale, swollen body with teeth marks, thick white greasy coat, wet surface. Pulse: slippery (Hua) or soggy (Ru).
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Phlegm-dampness is a heavy, turbid substance that rises when the body is upright, clogging the head. Lying down helps it settle, relieving the pressure. Warm, dry foods and movement support the Spleen in transforming dampness, while greasy, cold, or damp items directly add to the phlegm burden. Overeating taxes the Spleen, producing more phlegm, so light, regular meals are essential.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat warm, cooked foods and avoid cold, raw, or greasy meals that create dampness. Gentle daily movement like walking helps the Spleen transform fluids. Steer clear of dairy, sweets, and heavy starches until the tongue coating clears.

### Blood Stagnation
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/blood-stagnation)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Sharp, stabbing pain fixed in one location, Pain worsens when standing or at night, Dark or purplish tongue with stasis spots, Dark circles under the eyes or dusky complexion
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Prolonged standing, Cold environments or drafts, Stress or frustration, Night time
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Lying down to rest, Gentle movement or exercise, Warmth on the neck
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Blood is meant to flow smoothly, nourishing every part of the head. When blood stagnates - often after an old injury, long-standing Qi stagnation, or exposure to cold - it forms a fixed blockage in the vessels. Standing upright increases the pull of gravity on the already sluggish blood, intensifying the pressure and causing a sharp, stabbing pain in one spot. Lying down eases the strain, allowing blood to trickle past the obstruction more freely.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: dark purple body, possible stasis spots; distended, dark sublingual veins. Pulse: choppy (Se), rough like a knife scraping bamboo.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Standing pulls blood downward, but stasis creates a bottleneck; the pressure intensifies the stabbing sensation. Lying down removes this gravitational load, letting blood creep past the blockage. Cold congeals blood, making stasis worse, while warmth encourages flow. Stress knots Qi, and where Qi stops, blood stagnates further. Gentle movement and rest both help to gradually reopen the vessels.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Keep the neck and head warm; cold constricts vessels and makes stasis worse. Gentle daily movement like walking or stretching helps blood flow, but avoid prolonged standing. Add a pinch of turmeric or ginger to meals - they gently warm and move the blood.

### Liver Yang Rising
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/liver-yang-rising)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Distending or throbbing headache, often at the temples, Worse with stress, anger, or frustration, Irritability and quick temper, Dizziness or feeling of head heavy and feet light, Flushed face and red eyes
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Stress and anger, Prolonged standing, Heat and stuffy rooms, Spicy food, Alcohol
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Lying down to rest, Cool environment, Calming activities like meditation, Gentle head massage
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  The Liver is meant to anchor rising Yang with its Yin and Blood. When chronic stress, frustration, or overwork depletes that Yin anchor, the unmoored Yang surges upward to the head. This produces a distending, throbbing pain - especially at the temples. Standing up encourages this upward rush, making the headache worse; lying down helps the rising Yang settle back down.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red body with redder sides, thin dry yellow or scanty coating. Pulse: wiry (弦 xián), especially forceful at the left middle position; may be fine and rapid if Yin deficiency is pronounced.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Stress and anger cause Liver Qi to stagnate and transform into heat, which fuels the upward surge of Yang. Standing increases the upward momentum of Qi and blood, pushing the already rising Yang higher. Rest and lying down allow Qi to flow back down, calming the Yang. Cooling foods and environments counteract the heat, while spicy and greasy foods add more heat and worsen the flare-up.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat cooling, Yin-nourishing foods like cucumber, celery, pear, and leafy greens. Avoid spicy, greasy, and fried foods that stir up internal heat. Manage stress with gentle daily movement like walking or tai chi, and protect sleep. Limit alcohol and caffeine, which can further unbalance the Liver.

### Kidney Essence Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/kidney-essence-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Empty, hollow sensation in the head, Worse when standing or upright, better lying down, Poor memory and difficulty concentrating, Soreness and weakness of the lower back and knees, Tinnitus and dizziness
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Prolonged standing, Overwork and exhaustion, Excessive sexual activity, Fear and anxiety, Cold or raw foods
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Lying down to rest, Warmth and gentle heat, Nourishing foods (bone broth, black sesame), Gentle movement or exercise
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  The Kidneys store Essence (Jing), which produces marrow and fills the brain. When Kidney Essence is depleted, the brain loses its deepest nourishment, creating an empty, hollow headache. Standing upright demands more Essence to support the head, so the emptiness intensifies and the pain worsens. Lying down allows the depleted Essence to settle, which is why the headache eases with rest.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale, thin body with little or no coating, possible fine cracks. Pulse: fine, deep, and weak, especially at the Chi (rear) positions.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Standing upright demands more Essence to nourish the brain, so when Essence is already depleted, the headache worsens. Overwork and excessive sexual activity further drain the Kidneys, deepening the deficiency. Cold and raw foods can injure Kidney Yang, compromising the transformation of Essence. Rest, lying down, and nourishing foods allow the Essence to settle and be replenished. Warmth and gentle movement support Kidney function without exhausting it.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat foods that gently nourish Kidney Essence, such as black sesame, walnuts, goji berries, and bone broths. Avoid overwork, late nights, and excessive sexual activity, all of which deplete Essence. Gentle daily movement like Tai Chi or walking in nature supports the Kidneys without draining them.

## คำถามที่พบบ่อย (Frequently Asked Questions)
### Why does my headache only happen when I stand up?
In TCM, standing up requires your body to lift Qi and Blood upward to your head. If your Spleen Qi, Kidney Yang, or Blood is too weak to do this, or if something like phlegm blocks the channel, the head is deprived of nourishment the moment you rise, and pain occurs. Lying down allows the flow to reach the head more easily, so the headache eases.

### Can acupuncture really help with orthostatic headaches?
Yes. Acupuncture can open the channels that carry Qi and Blood to the head, and specific points on the legs and back can strengthen the Spleen and Kidneys to improve the upward lift. Many patients feel relief after just a few sessions, though lasting change requires a course of treatment to correct the underlying pattern.

### How long until I see results?
Excess patterns like Damp-Phlegm often improve within 2-4 weeks. Deficiency patterns take longer-3 to 6 months is common-because the body needs time to rebuild Qi and Blood. You may notice the headache becoming less intense first, then less frequent. Consistency with herbs and acupuncture is key.

### Can I take TCM herbs with my current medications?
In most cases, yes, but you must inform both your TCM practitioner and your doctor. Some herbs that move blood (like Chuan Xiong) can interact with blood thinners. Sedative herbs should be used cautiously with sleep or anxiety medications. Always bring a full list of your medications to your TCM consultation.

### Do I need to change my diet?
Diet plays a supporting role. In general, eat warm, cooked foods to strengthen the Spleen and avoid cold, raw foods that create dampness. Include nourishing foods like bone broth, dark leafy greens, and small amounts of red meat. Stay hydrated but avoid excessive cold fluids. Your practitioner may give more specific advice based on your pattern.

## การวิจัยทางวิทยาศาสตร์สมัยใหม่ (Modern Scientific Research)
1. **Acupuncture for tension-type headache** ([ลิงก์](https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD007587.pub2))
   *วารสารวิชาการ: Linde K, Allais G, Brinkhaus B, et al. Acupuncture for tension-type headache. Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews 2016, Issue 4. Art. No.: CD007587.*
   A Cochrane systematic review of 12 trials involving 2349 adults found that acupuncture is effective for reducing the frequency of episodic and chronic tension-type headache. Participants receiving acupuncture had at least a 50% reduction in headache frequency compared to those receiving routine care or sham acupuncture, with benefits sustained over six months.

2. **Acupuncture for migraine prophylaxis** ([ลิงก์](https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD001218.pub3))
   *วารสารวิชาการ: Linde K, Allais G, Brinkhaus B, et al. Acupuncture for migraine prophylaxis. Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews 2016, Issue 6. Art. No.: CD001218.*
   This Cochrane review of 22 trials with 4985 participants concluded that acupuncture is at least as effective as prophylactic drug treatment for migraine and has fewer adverse effects. Acupuncture reduced migraine frequency by roughly 3 days per month compared to sham acupuncture, supporting its use for headache conditions where conventional medications are unsuitable.
