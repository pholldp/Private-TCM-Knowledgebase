## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนปัจจุบัน (Conventional Medicine View)
### คำอธิบาย (Description)
Chronic urticaria is defined by recurrent, itchy wheals (hives) that appear daily or almost daily for six weeks or longer, often with no obvious trigger. The wheals are caused by mast cells in the skin releasing histamine and other chemicals, which makes tiny blood vessels leak fluid into the surrounding tissue. In many cases the cause remains unknown - so-called chronic spontaneous urticaria - though physical triggers such as pressure, cold, heat, or sunlight can be identified in some people.
Diagnosis is usually made from the history and appearance of the rash; routine lab tests are often normal. Conventional treatment aims to block histamine and calm the immune response, but it does not address why the mast cells are overactive in the first place.

### การรักษาหลัก (Treatments)
Standard management begins with non-sedating antihistamines, often at up to four times the usual dose if needed. When antihistamines are not enough, doctors may add H2 blockers, leukotriene receptor antagonists, or short courses of oral corticosteroids for severe flares. For refractory cases, the monoclonal antibody omalizumab (anti-IgE) or the immunosuppressant ciclosporin may be used. Topical anti-itch lotions and avoidance of known triggers are also recommended.

## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนจีน (Traditional Chinese Medicine View)
### ความเข้าใจเกี่ยวกับการแพทย์แผนจีน (TCM Understanding)
In TCM, chronic urticaria (慢性瘾疹, màn xìng yǐn zhěn) is understood as a battle between the body's protective shield - the Wei Qi - and an invading pathogen called Wind. Wind is the chief culprit behind the sudden, shifting, intensely itchy quality of hives. But Wind does not invade unless the body's defenses are already compromised, which is why chronic hives always point to an underlying weakness. The nature of that weakness - and the type of Wind it invites - determines the pattern.
The Lung system governs the Wei Qi that circulates at the skin surface. When the Lungs are strong, Wind cannot enter. If the Wei Qi is deficient (Protective Qi Deficiency pattern), even a mild draft can trigger pale hives.
The Spleen and Stomach are responsible for transforming food into Qi and Blood; when they are overloaded with greasy, spicy, or sweet foods, they generate Damp-Heat that rises to the skin as red, angry wheals (Damp-Heat in Stomach and Spleen pattern). The Liver stores Blood, and if Blood is chronically deficient, the skin becomes dry and undernourished, generating an internal Wind that itches relentlessly, especially at night (Blood Deficiency with External Wind pattern).
This is why the same Western diagnosis of chronic urticaria can have completely different TCM causes. A person whose hives are pale, brought on by fatigue, and accompanied by spontaneous sweating needs to have their Qi strengthened. Another person whose hives are red, flare after a heavy meal, and come with bloating needs to have Damp-Heat cleared from the digestive tract. A third person with dry, night-time hives, brittle nails, and a thin pulse needs to have their Blood nourished and their Wind calmed. TCM treatment is tailored to the pattern, not the diagnosis.

### การวินิจฉัยโดยแพทย์แผนจีน (Practitioner Diagnosis)
A practitioner begins by asking about the timing and triggers of the hives, their color and shape, and any accompanying symptoms. The tongue and pulse are examined to identify the underlying pattern. Chronic urticaria (慢性瘾疹, màn xìng yǐn zhěn) often reflects a deeper imbalance, so the pattern diagnosis goes beyond the skin.
If hives appear more at night, worsen with fatigue, and the skin is dry and rough, Blood Deficiency with External Wind is likely. The tongue is pale with a thin coat, and the pulse is thin or wiry. This pattern arises when blood is insufficient to moisten the skin and anchor the body’s defenses, allowing wind to invade.
When hives flare after minimal wind exposure, and the person catches colds easily with spontaneous sweating, Protective Qi Deficiency is suspected. The tongue is pale and puffy with a thin white coat, and the pulse is weak. The body’s defensive shield is too weak to repel external pathogens.
Episodic hives accompanied by alternating sensations of chills and heat, with light sweating, point to Ying-Wei Disharmony. The tongue may look normal or slightly pale, and the pulse is often soft or slightly wiry. This pattern reflects a miscommunication between the nutritive and protective qi layers, making the skin pores unstable.
Generalized fatigue, pale complexion, dizziness, and hives that are pale and less itchy suggest Qi and Blood Deficiency. The tongue is pale and thin, and the pulse is weak and thready. Both qi and blood are depleted, failing to nourish the skin and maintain a strong exterior.
Red, hot, intensely itchy hives that appear after eating rich, spicy, or greasy foods indicate Damp-Heat in Stomach and Spleen. There may be abdominal bloating, loose stools or constipation, and a heavy sensation. The tongue is red with a yellow, greasy coat, and the pulse is slippery and rapid.
Chronic dry, itchy hives that worsen in the evening, along with night sweats, dry mouth, and a feeling of heat, suggest Yin and Blood Deficiency. The tongue is red with little or no coating, and the pulse is thin and rapid. Deep yin dryness generates internal wind, causing persistent skin irritation.

### Blood Deficiency with External Wind
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/blood-deficiency-with-external-wind)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Skin itching that worsens at night, Dry, flaking, or rough skin, Pale or sallow complexion, Aversion to wind or drafts, Dizziness or light-headedness, Dull or brittle nails, Scanty or pale menstrual blood
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Exposure to wind or drafts, Emotional stress, Spicy, greasy, or fried foods, Overwork and exhaustion, Dry, cold weather
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Moisturizing the skin, Warm, draft-free environment, Blood-nourishing foods, Rest and adequate sleep, Gentle movement or exercise
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When the body’s blood is chronically deficient, the skin loses its nourishment and becomes dry, creating an internal “wind” that causes itching. At the same time, this deficiency weakens the protective qi (wei qi) at the surface, so external wind can easily invade the skin. The combination of internal dryness and external wind produces hives that appear suddenly, shift location, and feel intensely itchy, especially at night when blood is even more depleted.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale, thin body with a thin white and slightly dry coat. Pulse: fine (thin), wiry at the left middle position, and floating at the superficial level on the right front position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: <p>Blood deficiency creates an internal dryness that generates wind, while also weakening the protective qi (wei qi) at the skin’s surface. External wind easily invades this weakened exterior, triggering the sudden appearance and disappearance of hives. Nourishing blood and avoiding wind helps restore the skin’s resilience. Spicy and greasy foods consume yin and blood, worsening dryness; stress stirs up liver wind, aggravating itching. Rest and gentle movement build qi and blood without overstraining the body.</p>
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat blood-nourishing foods like dark leafy greens, red meat, liver, black sesame, goji berries, and dates. Avoid spicy, greasy, and deep-fried foods that generate heat and dryness. Keep skin moisturized and avoid harsh soaps. Protect yourself from wind and drafts, especially after sweating. Gentle exercise like walking or tai chi can strengthen qi without depleting blood.

### Protective Qi Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/protective-qi-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Pale or skin-colored wheals, Worse with drafts and cold, Spontaneous sweating, worse with activity, Frequent colds and infections, Aversion to wind, Fatigue and low stamina, Bright pale complexion
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Exposure to wind or drafts, Overwork and exhaustion, Cold, raw foods and drinks, Emotional stress, Excessive sweating
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Rest and adequate sleep, Warm, draft-free environment, Warm, cooked meals, Gentle movement or exercise, Moxibustion
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Your Protective Qi (Wei Qi) acts like a shield, guarding the skin against wind and cold. When this shield is weak from chronic Qi deficiency, the skin's defense is easily breached. Even a slight draft can trigger an invasion of wind, causing the skin to react with pale, itchy wheals that come and go.
Because the underlying problem is a lack of energy, the hives tend to flare when you are tired or run-down, and the itching is often mild rather than intensely hot and red. This pattern is marked by repeated episodes and a general feeling of being chilled and easily exhausted.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale, puffy, possibly with teeth marks; thin white coat. Pulse: floating, empty, and soggy (soft and fine at the superficial level).
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Protective Qi is the body's defensive energy, and it is weak in this pattern. Fatigue, wind, and cold further deplete Qi, making it easy for wind pathogens to invade the skin and cause hives. Rest, warmth, and nourishing foods conserve and rebuild Qi, stabilizing the exterior. Gentle exercise strengthens Qi without exhausting it, while moxibustion directly warms and tonifies the body's defensive energy.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat warm, cooked foods like soups and stews to support Spleen Qi. Avoid raw, cold foods and icy drinks. Engage in gentle, consistent exercise like walking or tai chi to build Qi, but avoid exhausting yourself. Protect your neck and back from drafts; wear a scarf in windy weather. Moxibustion at home on Zu San Li can help strengthen your constitution.

### Ying-Wei Disharmony
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/ying-wei-disharmony)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Hives appear after exposure to wind or drafts, Spontaneous sweating with minimal activity, Mild chills or alternating chills and mild heat, Generalised body aches and fatigue, Sensitivity to temperature changes
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Exposure to wind or drafts, Overwork and exhaustion, Cold, raw foods and drinks, Sudden temperature changes
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warm, draft-free environment, Rest and adequate sleep, Warm, cooked meals
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  The protective Wei Qi acts like a shield at the skin surface, while the nourishing Ying Qi moistens and stabilises it from within. In this pattern the two are out of sync, so the pores open and close at the wrong times. This lets external Wind slip in easily, and the body's attempt to push it back out creates the wheals and itching. Spontaneous sweating further depletes the Wei layer, leaving the skin even more vulnerable to drafts and temperature changes.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale-red or slightly pale body with a thin, white, moist coating. Pulse: floating and moderate (浮缓 fú huǎn), sometimes floating and weak.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: <p>Wind directly invades the already disharmonious Wei Qi, triggering the cycle of wheals and itching. Sweating depletes both Ying and Wei, making the skin even more open to attack. Warmth and rest help the body conserve Qi and keep the pores properly closed. Cold and raw foods weaken the Spleen's ability to generate Wei Qi, so avoiding them protects the skin's natural barrier.</p>
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Keep the neck and upper back protected from drafts. Wear layers that you can add or remove as your temperature shifts. Gentle movement like walking or Tai Chi builds Qi without causing heavy sweating. Favour warm, cooked foods and avoid raw, icy, or greasy meals that weaken the Spleen's ability to produce Wei Qi.

### Qi and Blood Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/qi-and-blood-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Pale, pinkish or skin-colored wheals rather than bright red, Deep fatigue and weakness that worsen with exertion, Sallow or pale complexion and pale lips, Dizziness or lightheadedness, Heart palpitations and poor sleep, Poor appetite and spontaneous sweating
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Overwork and exhaustion, Exposure to wind or drafts, Skipping meals or poor nutrition, Emotional stress
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Rest and adequate sleep, Warm, cooked meals, Warm, draft-free environment, Gentle movement or exercise
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When Qi is weak, the body's outer defenses are porous, and wind easily slips in to trigger hives. Blood deficiency means the skin is undernourished and dry, so it itches more readily. Because both the fuel (Qi) and the substance (Blood) are low, the body cannot drive out the wind, and the hives keep coming back - especially when you are tired or run down.

- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat warm, nourishing foods that build Qi and Blood: bone broths, slow-cooked stews, eggs, dark leafy greens, goji berries, and moderate amounts of red meat. Protect yourself from drafts and cold weather, and go to bed by 11 p.m. to support Blood regeneration.

- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Spicy, greasy, or fried foods, Alcohol, Overeating, Damp, humid weather
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Light, bland meals, Cool environment, Avoiding alcohol, Rest after eating
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When the Stomach and Spleen are overloaded with greasy, spicy, or sweet foods, they produce internal Dampness and Heat. This turbid Damp-Heat cannot be properly transformed and instead rises upward and outward, erupting through the skin as red, itchy hives. The digestive discomfort that accompanies the rash-bloating, sticky taste, loose stools-is a direct sign of this middle burner imbalance.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red body with a thick, yellow, greasy coating, especially at the center and root. Pulse: soggy (Ru) and rapid (Shu), often slippery (Hua).
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Rich, greasy, and spicy foods generate Dampness and Heat in the Stomach and Spleen, which then surge into the skin as hives. Alcohol and overeating further tax the middle burner, making the rash worse. Light, cooling foods help clear Damp-Heat, while rest supports the Spleen's ability to transform and transport fluids, reducing the internal turmoil that feeds the skin eruption.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat light, easily digested meals. Avoid greasy, fried, spicy, and heavily sweetened foods, as well as alcohol and dairy. Favor barley, mung beans, bitter greens, and lightly cooked vegetables. Staying well hydrated helps flush Dampness.

- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Overwork and exhaustion, Spicy, greasy, or fried foods, Emotional stress, Hot, dry weather

  When the body's deeper nourishing reserves - Yin and Blood - run low over a long period, the skin loses its natural moisture and resilience. This dryness creates a kind of internal "wind" that stirs under the skin, producing persistent, dry, itchy hives. The mild heat that comes from Yin deficiency further irritates the skin, making the itching worse, especially at night or when the body feels warm.

Rest, moisture, and cooling foods help rebuild Yin and Blood, calming the wind and soothing the skin.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Focus on moistening, cooling foods like pear, black sesame, honey, and leafy greens. Avoid spicy, greasy, and drying foods that consume Yin. Prioritise deep rest and gentle, calming exercise like walking or tai chi to conserve your reserves.

## คำถามที่พบบ่อย (Frequently Asked Questions)
### Can acupuncture help with chronic hives?
Yes. Acupuncture works on two levels: it calms the immediate itch by moving Qi and blood in the skin channels, and it addresses the root imbalance that makes you susceptible to outbreaks. Points like Sanyinjiao (SP-6) and Xuehai (SP-10) are used to cool the blood and stop itching, while Zusanli (ST-36) and Qihai (REN-6) strengthen the body's overall energy and defenses. Many patients notice the itch subsides during or shortly after a session, but lasting change comes with a course of weekly treatments over several weeks.

### How long does it take for Chinese herbs to work for urticaria?
Most people notice the hives become less angry - smaller, less red, less frequent - within one to two weeks of starting a formula matched to their pattern. For excess patterns like Damp-Heat, significant clearing may happen in four to six weeks. For deficiency patterns where the body must rebuild blood or Qi, it can take three to four months before the skin stops flaring altogether. The herbs are taken daily, and consistency is key; stopping too early often leads to a return of symptoms.

### Can I take TCM herbs with antihistamines?
Generally, yes. TCM herbs and antihistamines work through different pathways and can be used together, especially in the early stages when you need relief from severe itching. There are no well-documented dangerous interactions between standard antihistamines and the herbs commonly prescribed for urticaria. However, always tell both your TCM practitioner and your prescribing doctor exactly what you are taking. If you are on immunosuppressants like ciclosporin or omalizumab, extra caution is needed and your TCM practitioner should be fully informed.

### What foods should I avoid if I have chronic hives?
Across all patterns, it is wise to avoid foods that generate internal heat and dampness: deep-fried foods, excessive sugar, alcohol, spicy dishes, and rich dairy. Cold, raw foods and icy drinks can weaken the Spleen's digestive fire, making it harder for the body to clear dampness. If your hives flare after meals, pay attention to shellfish, processed meats, and fermented or aged foods, which can be triggers. A simple, warm, cooked diet of soups, congees, and steamed vegetables gives your digestive system a rest while you heal.

### Is chronic urticaria curable with TCM?
Many people achieve long-term remission where hives stop occurring, or occur only very rarely and mildly. TCM does not frame this as a permanent "cure" in the way antibiotics cure an infection, but rather as a restoration of balance so the body no longer overreacts to wind, food, or stress. For some, especially those with deep-seated constitutional deficiencies, the goal is management: far fewer outbreaks, much less itching, and no reliance on daily medication. The outcome depends on the pattern, how long you have had the condition, and how consistently you follow the treatment plan.

### Do I need to avoid wind and cold if I have chronic urticaria?
If your pattern involves Protective Qi Deficiency or Ying-Wei Disharmony, protecting yourself from drafts and sudden temperature changes is genuinely helpful. A scarf around the neck and covering exposed skin in windy weather can prevent an outbreak. This is not a superstition - in TCM terms, wind is a pathogen that enters through the pores when the exterior is weak. However, if your hives are driven by Damp-Heat or Blood Deficiency with internal wind, external wind is less of a direct trigger, though it can still aggravate the skin.

### How does a TCM practitioner diagnose my pattern?
The practitioner will ask detailed questions about the timing of your hives (day or night, after meals, with stress), their color and shape, and what makes them better or worse. They will look at your tongue - its color, coating, and shape - and feel your pulse at both wrists. These observations reveal which organ systems are out of balance. For example, a pale, puffy tongue with teeth marks points to Qi deficiency; a red tongue with a greasy yellow coat suggests damp-heat. This pattern diagnosis then guides the choice of herbs, points, and dietary advice.

## การวิจัยทางวิทยาศาสตร์สมัยใหม่ (Modern Scientific Research)
1. **Chinese herbal medicine for chronic urticaria: a systematic review and meta-analysis of randomized controlled trials**
   *วารสารวิชาการ: Zhang X, Wang Y, Li J, et al. Chinese herbal medicine for chronic urticaria: a systematic review and meta-analysis. Frontiers in Pharmacology, 2019.*
   This meta-analysis pooled data from multiple RCTs comparing Chinese herbal medicine (often Dang Gui Yin Zi or Yu Ping Feng San-based) to antihistamines. Herbal treatment significantly reduced wheal and itching scores and lowered recurrence rates, with a favorable safety profile.

2. **Unveiling the immunopharmacological mechanisms of Danggui Yinzi (DGYZ) in treating chronic urticaria: insights from network pharmacology and experimental validation** ([ลิงก์](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12147246))
   *วารสารวิชาการ: Liu Y, Chen H, Zhao M, et al. Unveiling the immunopharmacological mechanisms of Danggui Yinzi in treating chronic urticaria: insights from network pharmacology and experimental validation. PMC, 2025.*
   This 2025 study combined network pharmacology with in vitro and in vivo experiments to demonstrate that Dang Gui Yin Zi modulates key inflammatory pathways, inhibits mast cell degranulation, and reduces histamine release, providing a scientific basis for its anti-urticarial effects.
