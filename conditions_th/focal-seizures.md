## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนปัจจุบัน (Conventional Medicine View)
### คำอธิบาย (Description)
Focal seizures (also called partial seizures) begin in a limited area of one cerebral hemisphere. Depending on the location, they can cause motor symptoms like jerking or stiffening, sensory symptoms like tingling or visual distortions, or autonomic and psychic symptoms such as fear or déjà vu. Some focal seizures remain localized (focal aware seizures), while others can spread and impair consciousness (focal impaired awareness seizures) or generalize to a full-body convulsion.
Diagnosis relies on a detailed description of the episodes, often supplemented by EEG to capture abnormal brain activity and MRI to look for structural causes. Standard treatment aims to suppress seizures, usually with anti-seizure medications, and occasionally with surgery or dietary therapies when medications fail.

### การรักษาหลัก (Treatments)
Anti-seizure medications (ASMs) are the first-line treatment, with choices guided by seizure type, side-effect profile, and patient factors. For drug-resistant cases, options include resective surgery, neurostimulation devices, or the ketogenic diet. Acute seizure action plans may involve rescue medications like benzodiazepines. The primary goal is seizure freedom or significant reduction, but many patients continue to experience breakthrough seizures and medication side effects.

## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนจีน (Traditional Chinese Medicine View)
### ความเข้าใจเกี่ยวกับการแพทย์แผนจีน (TCM Understanding)
In TCM, focal seizures are understood as a sudden disruption of the brain's sensory orifices and channels by internal Wind, Phlegm, Fire, or Deficiency. The Liver, Spleen, and Heart are the key organ systems involved. The Liver governs the smooth flow of Qi and stores Blood; when it becomes stagnant or overheated, it can generate internal Wind that rises to the head and agitates the channels controlling movement, causing localized twitching or numbness. The Spleen transforms fluids; when it's weak, fluids accumulate into Phlegm - a sticky, obstructive substance that can block the brain's orifices and trigger a seizure with phlegm in the throat. The Heart houses the mind (Shén); if it's disturbed by Phlegm-Fire or undernourished by Blood deficiency, the mind loses its anchor and a seizure may erupt.
What makes TCM's view so practical is that it differentiates seizures by their accompanying signs. A seizure preceded by dizziness, a gurgling throat, and copious frothy sputum points to Wind-Phlegm obstructing the orifices - a pattern of sticky phlegm stirred by internal Wind. When the seizure comes with a red face, irritability, bitter taste, and thick yellow phlegm, that's Phlegm-Fire harassing the Heart, where Phlegm has combined with Heat to agitate the mind. If the seizure is frequent, accompanied by a throbbing headache and severe dizziness, and triggered by stress or anger, it's Liver Wind agitating internally due to Liver Yang Rising - a pattern of unanchored Yang energy surging upward. These acute excess patterns are the most common drivers of active seizures.
But TCM also recognizes that many people have an underlying deficiency that makes the brain vulnerable. Heart and Spleen Deficiency - often from chronic fatigue, worry, or poor digestion - fails to produce enough Qi and Blood to nourish the brain and calm the mind, leading to seizures that occur when the person is exhausted or stressed. And in some cases, a traumatic injury or long-standing stagnation creates Blood Stagnation in the brain's tiny vessels, causing a fixed, stabbing pain and localized seizures. By identifying which pattern - or mixture of patterns - is at play, TCM practitioners tailor treatment not just to stop the seizure but to rebuild the constitution so the brain becomes less susceptible over time.

### การวินิจฉัยโดยแพทย์แผนจีน (Practitioner Diagnosis)
A TCM practitioner first distinguishes whether the focal seizure is in an acute, stormy phase or a quiet interictal period. Two common acute patterns are Wind-Phlegm and Phlegm-Fire harassing the Heart. Both involve phlegm, but the heat component tells them apart. Wind-Phlegm tends to produce gurgling phlegm sounds, frothy spittle, and a sudden localized convulsion with a tongue that is red with a white greasy coat and a slippery, forceful pulse.
Phlegm-Fire harassing the Heart adds a strong heat signature: the face is red, the person is irritable and restless, and the tongue is red with a thick yellow greasy coat while the pulse is rapid and slippery. The seizure feels more agitated, and there may be bitter taste, constipation, and dark urine. These fire signs are the key clue that shifts the diagnosis away from simple Wind-Phlegm.
Liver Wind agitating Internally due to Liver Yang Rising produces frequent convulsions, dizziness, and a throbbing sensation in the head, often triggered by stress or anger. Phlegm signs are minimal; instead the tongue is red with a thin coat and the pulse is wiry and forceful. This pattern is more about rising yang energy than sticky phlegm, and the practitioner looks for that wiry pulse and emotional triggers to confirm it.
Blood Stagnation is less common but very distinctive: the person describes fixed, stabbing headaches and the tongue appears dark purple with possible stasis spots. The pulse feels choppy or wiry. When a focal seizure follows a head injury or longstanding headaches, this pattern becomes a top suspect. In the quiet phase, Heart and Spleen Deficiency can be the root: fatigue, poor appetite, pale tongue, and a weak pulse point to a lack of nourishment for the mind rather than an excess of wind or phlegm.

### Wind-Phlegm
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/wind-phlegm)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Dizziness or vertigo before the seizure, Gurgling or rattling sound in the throat during the seizure, Copious white or frothy sputum, Chest stuffiness, nausea, or vomiting of phlegm, Heaviness or tingling in the limbs
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Anger or frustration, Greasy, dairy, or sweet foods, Overeating, Damp, humid weather, Overwork and lack of sleep
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Rest and calm environment, Warm, easily digested meals, Ginger tea or warm water, Acupuncture and deep breathing
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Wind-Phlegm forms when internal Wind stirs and carries accumulated Phlegm upward to block the brain's sensory orifices. This sudden obstruction disrupts the local flow of Qi and blood, triggering a focal seizure with jerking, numbness, or sensory distortions in a specific part of the body. The telltale rattling sound in the throat and copious white frothy sputum point directly to Phlegm as the root cause.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: normal or slightly pale body, swollen with teeth marks, white greasy coating. Pulse: wiry and slippery (Xian Hua).
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Emotional stress stirs Liver Wind, which then carries Phlegm upward to the head, provoking a seizure. Greasy, sweet, or heavy foods directly produce more internal Phlegm, thickening the obstruction. Rest and a calm environment settle the Wind, while a light diet and ginger help break down and clear Phlegm, reducing the root cause of the attacks.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Avoid greasy, dairy, and sweet foods that generate Phlegm. Emphasize warm, light, easily digestible meals with ginger to help transform Phlegm. Regular acupuncture and gentle daily movement help keep Qi and fluids moving smoothly.

### Phlegm-Fire harassing the Heart
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/phlegm-fire-harassing-the-heart)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Flushed red face and red eyes, Irritability and mental restlessness, Thick yellow sticky phlegm with throat gurgling, Chest oppression and bitter taste, Focal seizure with a sensation of heat
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Spicy, greasy foods, Alcohol, Emotional stress or anger, Overwork and lack of sleep
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Cooling foods (e.g., pear, cucumber, chrysanthemum tea), Quiet rest in a cool room, Deep breathing exercises, Avoiding greasy and spicy meals
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Phlegm and Fire combine to agitate the Heart and obscure the mind, causing sudden loss of control. The thick, yellow phlegm obstructs the chest and throat, while Fire flares upward, producing a flushed face, irritability, and a bitter taste. When this turmoil reaches the brain, it can trigger a focal seizure-often with a sensation of heat and mental agitation.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red body, swollen with red prickly tip, thick yellow greasy coating. Pulse: slippery and rapid.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Spicy, greasy foods and alcohol create Phlegm and Heat in the body, which directly feeds the Phlegm-Fire pattern. Emotional stress stirs up Liver Fire, which combines with Phlegm to agitate the Heart. Cooling foods, rest, and relaxation help clear Heat and settle the mind, reducing the internal turmoil that triggers seizures.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Avoid greasy, spicy, and heavy foods that generate Phlegm and Heat. Favor cooling, light foods like pear, cucumber, and mung beans. Manage stress with meditation or gentle walks to prevent Fire from flaring upward.

### Liver Wind agitating Internally due to Liver Yang Rising
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/liver-wind-agitating-internally-due-to-liver-yang-rising)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Severe dizziness with a feeling of falling, Throbbing, distending headache, Tremor or twitching of hands, head, or limbs, Numbness or tingling in the limbs, Stiff neck, Flushed face and red eyes, Irritability and easy anger, Insomnia with vivid, disturbing dreams
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Anger or frustration, Alcohol or coffee, Spicy, greasy foods, Overwork and lack of sleep, Stressful situations
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Rest in a quiet, dark room, Cooling foods (e.g., pear, cucumber, chrysanthemum tea), Gentle movement, stretching, or tai chi, Avoiding anger and stress
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When the body's Yin is too weak to anchor the Liver's Yang, that Yang energy surges upward like a wave. This creates internal Wind, which disturbs the brain's normal calm and agitates the channels that control movement. The result is a focal seizure, often with twitching or numbness in one part of the body, accompanied by a throbbing headache and a sense of dizziness or unsteadiness.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red body, stiff or trembling, possibly deviated to one side, with a thin yellow or scanty coating. Pulse: wiry, fine, and often rapid.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Anger and stress surge Liver Qi upward, directly fueling the rising Yang that generates Wind and triggers seizures. Alcohol, coffee, and spicy foods add heat, further agitating Yang and depleting Yin. Rest, cooling foods, and gentle movement help anchor the Yang and nourish Yin, calming the internal Wind and stabilizing the brain.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Avoid alcohol, coffee, and spicy or greasy foods that stir up Liver Yang. Sip chrysanthemum tea and eat more cooling foods like celery, cucumber, and pear. Prioritize calming daily habits like tai chi, gentle stretching, or meditation, and keep a regular sleep schedule to protect Yin.

### Heart and Spleen Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/heart-and-spleen-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Seizures triggered by fatigue, stress, or overthinking, Fatigue and physical exhaustion, even between seizures, Palpitations or awareness of heartbeat, insomnia, vivid dreams, Poor appetite, abdominal bloating after eating, loose stools, Sallow or pale complexion, dizziness
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Worry, overthinking, emotional stress, Overwork and lack of sleep, Cold, raw, or greasy foods, Cold, damp weather
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Adequate, restful sleep, Warm, easily digested meals, Gentle movement, stretching, or tai chi, Quiet, low-stress environments
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When the Heart and Spleen are weak, the body cannot produce enough Qi and Blood to fully nourish the brain and calm the mind. This leaves the nervous system undernourished and unstable, making focal seizures more likely between episodes, especially when you are tired, stressed, or have been worrying a lot. The seizures themselves are often mild - a brief localized twitch, sensory change, or moment of altered awareness - and they occur against a backdrop of deep fatigue, poor appetite, and a tendency to feel anxious or easily startled.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale and tender, often puffy with teeth marks on the edges, and a thin white coating. Pulse: fine (thin) and weak, sometimes slightly choppy; the left cun (Heart) and right guan (Spleen) positions feel especially weak.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: In Heart and Spleen Deficiency, the body's reserves of Qi and Blood are already low. Stress and overthinking directly drain the Heart and Spleen further, while cold, raw foods and damp environments weaken the Spleen's ability to produce energy from food. This depletion makes the brain more vulnerable to seizure activity. ### Blood Stagnation
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/blood-stagnation)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Fixed, stabbing headache that worsens at night, Dark purplish tongue with stasis spots and distended sublingual veins, Pain is worse with pressure, Dark facial complexion or dark circles under the eyes, Possible history of head injury or prolonged emotional stress
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Cold weather or cold foods, Prolonged sitting or inactivity, Emotional stress or anger, Overwork and lack of sleep, Head injury or trauma
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warmth and gentle movement, Warm, lightly spiced foods, Adequate, restful sleep, Stress reduction
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When blood flow becomes sluggish or obstructed in the tiny vessels (collaterals) of the brain, it creates a fixed area of stagnation. This stuck blood acts like a dam, disrupting the normal electrical and energetic balance of that brain region, which can trigger a localized seizure. The stabbing, fixed headache that often accompanies this pattern is a direct signal of the stasis pressing on the collaterals.
This pattern is frequently seen after a head injury, but prolonged emotional stress or Qi stagnation can also eventually congeal blood. The seizure itself is the body's acute attempt to break through the blockage, though it does so in a chaotic way.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: dark purple body with stasis spots, engorged dark sublingual veins, thin white coat. Pulse: choppy (涩, sè) - rough and uneven like scraping bamboo; may also be wiry (弦, xián) if Qi stagnation is prominent.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Blood stasis is a congealing of blood, often caused by cold, Qi stagnation, or trauma. Cold contracts vessels and slows flow, while warmth and gentle movement encourage circulation. Stress constrains Liver Qi, which can lead to blood stasis, so calming the mind and moving the body help break up the obstruction. Restful sleep allows the body to repair and regenerate blood, preventing further stagnation.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Choose warm, lightly spiced foods that encourage circulation, like turmeric, ginger, black pepper, and leafy greens. Avoid icy drinks and raw, cold foods which congeal blood. Gentle daily movement - walking, tai chi, or stretching - keeps blood moving. Prioritize stress reduction and consistent sleep, as emotional tension and exhaustion worsen stasis.

## คำถามที่พบบ่อย (Frequently Asked Questions)
### Can TCM treat focal seizures while I'm on anti-epileptic drugs?
Yes, TCM can be safely combined with most anti-epileptic drugs. Herbal formulas and acupuncture work on your underlying pattern, while your medication helps control breakthrough seizures. Always inform both your TCM practitioner and your neurologist about everything you are taking, as some herbs can affect how your body processes certain AEDs. Never stop or reduce your medication without your neurologist's supervision.

### Will I need to take herbs forever?
Not usually. Once your seizure frequency drops and your pattern stabilizes, your practitioner will gradually reduce the dosage or switch to a maintenance formula taken only a few days a week. The goal is to correct the underlying imbalance so that your brain is less seizure-prone. Some patients with deep-rooted deficiency patterns may take herbs longer, but always with periodic breaks.

### Does acupuncture hurt?
Most people feel only a tiny pinch when the needle is inserted, followed by a dull ache or tingling sensation that quickly fades. Acupuncture points used for focal seizures, such as on the scalp, hands, and feet, are generally very tolerable. The treatment itself is deeply relaxing, and many patients find it helps reduce stress - a common seizure trigger.

### Can children with focal seizures be treated with TCM?
Yes, TCM is commonly used for children with epilepsy. Herbal dosages are adjusted for weight, and acupuncture may be replaced with non-needle techniques like acupressure or pediatric tuina massage for very young or needle-shy children. Always work with a practitioner experienced in pediatrics, and keep your child's neurologist in the loop.

### How quickly will I see results?
Many patients notice a decrease in seizure frequency within 4-8 weeks, especially with excess patterns like Wind-Phlegm or Phlegm-Fire. Some see improvement even sooner. Deficiency patterns take longer because the body needs time to build Qi and Blood. Consistency is key - missing herbal doses or skipping acupuncture sessions can slow progress.

### What should I do during a seizure?
For a focal seizure, stay calm and guide the person to a safe place away from sharp objects. Do not restrain them. Time the seizure. If it lasts longer than 5 minutes, the person has trouble breathing, or they do not return to their usual self afterward, seek emergency medical care. TCM treatment focuses on prevention, not acute seizure management - see our Safety section for more red flags.

### Are there any foods I should avoid?
Yes. In TCM, greasy, fried, and sugary foods create Phlegm, which can worsen seizures. Dairy, processed snacks, and rich meats are common culprits. Spicy and heating foods like chili, alcohol, and coffee can stir up Liver Wind or Fire. Instead, eat warm, simple meals like congee, steamed vegetables, and lean proteins. Your practitioner will give you more specific guidance based on your pattern.

## การวิจัยทางวิทยาศาสตร์สมัยใหม่ (Modern Scientific Research)
1. **Acupuncture for epilepsy** ([ลิงก์](https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD005062.pub4))
   *วารสารวิชาการ: Cheuk DK, Wong V. Acupuncture for epilepsy. Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews 2014, Issue 5. Art. No.: CD005062.*
   Cochrane systematic review evaluating acupuncture as a treatment for epilepsy. The review included 17 randomized controlled trials and found that acupuncture may be beneficial as an add-on therapy for reducing seizure frequency, but the overall quality of evidence was low to moderate due to risk of bias and small sample sizes.

2. **New advances in Traditional Chinese Medicine interventions for epilepsy: a review** ([ลิงก์](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11917061))
   *วารสารวิชาการ: Wang Y, et al. New advances in Traditional Chinese Medicine interventions for epilepsy: a review. Front Pharmacol. 2025;16:1498762.*
   A narrative review summarizing recent preclinical and clinical evidence for TCM therapies in epilepsy, including herbal formulas like Ding Xian Wan and Tian Ma Gou Teng Yin, as well as acupuncture. The review highlights anticonvulsant mechanisms and notes that while clinical studies show promise, most are limited by small sample sizes and lack of blinding.
