## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนปัจจุบัน (Conventional Medicine View)
### คำอธิบาย (Description)
Myofascial Pain Syndrome (MPS) is a chronic pain disorder characterized by sensitive, tight bands of muscle fiber called trigger points. These points can cause local pain or refer pain to other areas of the body, along with stiffness, weakness, and limited range of motion. It commonly affects the neck, shoulders, and back, and is often linked to repetitive strain, poor posture, or stress.
Diagnosis is made through a physical exam where a practitioner palpates for taut bands and reproduces the characteristic pain. There is no single lab test or imaging study that confirms MPS, which is why it is sometimes overlooked or mistaken for other conditions.

### การรักษาหลัก (Treatments)
Standard care usually includes physical therapy, stretching exercises, and trigger point release techniques such as massage or dry needling. Trigger point injections with local anesthetics or corticosteroids are sometimes used. Medications may include NSAIDs, muscle relaxants, and occasionally low-dose antidepressants or anticonvulsants for chronic pain. Lifestyle modifications and stress management are also recommended.

## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนจีน (Traditional Chinese Medicine View)
### ความเข้าใจเกี่ยวกับการแพทย์แผนจีน (TCM Understanding)
In TCM, Myofascial Pain Syndrome is understood as a form of Painful Obstruction (Bi Syndrome), where the smooth flow of Qi and Blood through the muscles and fascia is blocked. The Liver plays a central role because it governs the sinews and ensures the free flow of Qi. When emotional stress, frustration, or tension disrupts the Liver, Qi stagnates and muscles tighten, creating that familiar achy, distending pain.
External pathogens like Wind, Cold, and Dampness can also invade the body's channels, especially when your defenses are low. Cold congeals the flow, causing deep, fixed pain that worsens with cold weather. Dampness creates heaviness and stiffness. Over time, unresolved stagnation can deepen into Blood Stasis-the blood thickens and congeals right inside the muscle fibers, forming the hard, stabbing knots we know as trigger points.
In some cases, the pain has a burning, swollen quality, reflecting a combination of Dampness and Heat clogging the channels. For others, especially older adults or those with chronic illness, the root is a deficiency of the Liver and Kidneys. When these organs are depleted, they cannot properly nourish the sinews and bones, leading to dull, lingering pain and weakness that never fully resolves.
Because the same Western diagnosis can stem from such different TCM patterns, treatment must be personalized. A person whose pain feels better with warmth and worse in damp weather needs a very different strategy than someone whose knots burn and feel better with cold. By reading the subtle clues in your pain, your tongue, and your pulse, a TCM practitioner can identify the pattern and design a treatment that addresses both the local trigger point and the whole-body imbalance that created it.

### การวินิจฉัยโดยแพทย์แผนจีน (Practitioner Diagnosis)
A TCM practitioner begins by asking how the pain feels, where it is, and what makes it better or worse. They also look at your overall state - your energy, mood, and any other symptoms - because myofascial pain can arise from several different underlying imbalances. The tongue and pulse give crucial clues that help separate one pattern from another.
If the pain is a deep, heavy ache that worsens with cold or damp weather and eases with warmth, and you feel stiff and chilly, the pattern is likely Painful Obstruction with Wind-Cold-Damp. The tongue often has a thin white coating, and the pulse feels tight or wiry, reflecting an invasion of external pathogens that have lodged in the channels.
When the pain is sharp, fixed in one spot, and feels like a knife, especially when pressing on a taut band or knot, Blood Stagnation is the main pattern. The tongue may appear purplish or show dark spots, and the pulse is often choppy. This pattern often develops when earlier obstructions were not fully resolved, leading to persistent trigger points.
If the pain has a distending, moving quality and flares up with stress, frustration, or emotional upset, Qi and Blood Stagnation is at play. You might also notice chest tightness or sighing. The tongue can be dusky, and the pulse wiry. Here the liver’s role in smoothing the flow of Qi has been disrupted, causing pain that mirrors the emotional state.
When the painful area feels hot, looks slightly red or swollen, and the pain is burning rather than dull, Painful Obstruction due to Damp Heat in Channels is present. The tongue is red with a yellow coating, and the pulse is rapid. This less common pattern often appears after an injury or when dampness lingers and transforms into heat.
In chronic, nagging cases where pain is worse at night, accompanied by weakness, soreness in the low back and knees, and a sense of depletion, the root is Painful Obstruction with Liver and Kidney Deficiency. The tongue is pale with a thin coat, and the pulse is thin and weak. This pattern is more frequent in older adults or after long illness, reflecting an inability to properly nourish the sinews.

### Painful Obstruction with Wind-Cold-Damp
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/painful-obstruction-with-wind-cold-damp)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Pain is deep, aching, and often fixed in location, Stiffness and pain worsen in cold, damp, or rainy weather, Heaviness or a sensation of weight in the limbs, Aversion to cold and drafts; pain relieved by warmth, Mild swelling or puffiness around the painful area without redness or heat
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Cold, damp, or rainy weather, Sitting in drafts or air conditioning, Cold, raw foods and icy drinks, Prolonged sitting or inactivity, Emotional stress, frustration, or worry
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Applying warmth or warm compresses, Gentle stretching and movement, Dry, warm weather, Warm, cooked meals, Wearing layers to avoid drafts
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Wind, Cold, and Dampness are external pathogens that invade the body when your defenses are low, often after exposure to drafts, cold weather, or damp conditions. They settle into the muscles and fascia, where Cold contracts and congeals the flow of Qi and Blood, while Dampness creates a heavy, sticky stagnation. This combination leads to the tight, ropey bands and tender trigger points that define myofascial pain, with stiffness and aching that feel much worse in cold or wet weather.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale, slightly swollen body with teeth marks, white greasy coating, moist surface. Pulse: wiry and tight, or soggy and slow if Dampness predominates.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Wind, Cold, and Dampness are the root cause of this pattern, so anything that introduces more cold or moisture into the body-like cold weather, drafts, or raw foods-will further congeal Qi and Blood, intensifying pain and stiffness. Warmth and gentle movement do the opposite: they disperse Cold, evaporate Dampness, and encourage a smooth flow of Qi and Blood through the affected channels, which relaxes the taut bands and relieves the trigger points.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Keep the painful areas warm with scarves, wraps, or heat packs, and avoid sitting in drafts or air conditioning. Choose warm, cooked foods like soups and stews, and use warming spices such as ginger, cinnamon, and black pepper. Gentle daily movement like stretching or walking helps prevent stagnation, but avoid overexertion.

### Blood Stagnation
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/blood-stagnation)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Stabbing, fixed pain that feels like a knife, Pain worsens with pressure on the knot, Pain is worse at night, Dark or purplish lips, nails, or complexion, Palpable hard lumps or taut bands in the muscle, Dark menstrual blood with clots (if applicable)
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Emotional stress, frustration, or worry, Cold, damp, or rainy weather, Prolonged sitting or inactivity, Greasy, heavy foods, Direct pressure on the trigger point
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Gentle stretching and movement, Applying warmth or warm compresses, Turmeric or ginger tea, Deep breathing, relaxation, and emotional calm
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When muscle injury, chronic tension, or unresolved pathogenic factors persist, the local circulation of Qi and Blood becomes blocked. Over time, this stagnation deepens into Blood Stasis-the blood thickens and congeals right inside the muscle fibers, forming hard, knotty trigger points that feel like palpable lumps. The pain is sharp and fixed, like a knife, because the stagnant blood presses on the nerves, and it worsens at night when the body's Yin energy naturally thickens the blood further.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: purple or dusky body with stasis spots, distended dark sublingual veins, thin white coat. Pulse: choppy (涩 sè) like a knife scraping bamboo, or wiry (弦 xián) if Qi stagnation is involved.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Stress and frustration cause Liver Qi to stagnate, which then leads to Blood stasis-the very root of this pattern. Cold congeals blood, so cold weather and inactivity slow circulation and worsen the fixed, stabbing pain. Heat and gentle movement, on the other hand, invigorate Blood and help dissolve the knots. Warming spices like turmeric and ginger move Blood, while deep breathing encourages Qi to flow, breaking the cycle of stagnation.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Avoid cold, raw foods and icy drinks, which congeal blood and make stasis worse. Eat warming, blood-moving foods like turmeric, ginger, hawthorn berries, black fungus, and lightly cooked greens. Gentle daily movement-such as walking, tai chi, or stretching-keeps Qi and Blood flowing. Apply a warm compress or heating pad to painful knots, and avoid sitting in one position for too long.

- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Emotional stress, frustration, or worry, Prolonged sitting or inactivity, Cold, raw foods and icy drinks, Cold, damp, or rainy weather

- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When emotional stress or frustration disrupts the Liver’s ability to keep Qi flowing smoothly, that stagnant Qi creates a sensation of distending, tight pain in the muscles. Over time, the stuck Qi prevents Blood from moving properly, leading to fixed, stabbing pain and palpable knots at trigger points. This combination of Qi stagnation and Blood stasis is what makes the pain both mood-sensitive and deep-seated.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: dark purple body with stasis spots and distended sublingual veins. Pulse: wiry (xian) and choppy (se).
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Emotional stress directly tightens the Liver Qi, which is the root of this pattern, so frustration or suppressed anger immediately worsens the distending pain. Gentle movement and warmth help to unblock stagnant Qi and invigorate Blood flow, loosening the taut bands. Cold and inactivity, on the other hand, cause further congealing and stagnation, making the pain more fixed and intense.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Incorporate gentle, rhythmic movement like walking, tai chi, or stretching to keep Qi and Blood flowing. Avoid prolonged sitting and take breaks to move. Manage stress through deep breathing or meditation, as emotional tension directly tightens the muscles. Use warm compresses on painful areas to encourage circulation. Eat lightly cooked, warm foods and include small amounts of spices like turmeric and black pepper; avoid icy drinks and raw, cold foods that congeal stagnation.

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Burning pain in the trigger points, Trigger points feel warm or hot to the touch, Pain eases with cold, worsens with warmth, Heavy, aching sensation in the limbs, Thirst with desire for cold drinks, Irritability and restlessness
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Hot, humid weather, Spicy or fried foods, Alcohol, Overexertion that raises body heat
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Cold compress on the painful area, Cool, dry environment, Light, non-greasy meals, Gentle stretching and movement
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Dampness and Heat combine to clog the channels that run through your muscles and fascia. This creates a hot, heavy obstruction that makes trigger points feel burning and swollen, rather than just stiff. The Heat intensifies pain and makes it worse with warmth, while the Dampness adds a deep, aching heaviness that lingers.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red body, possibly slightly swollen, with a yellow greasy coating. Pulse: slippery (滑 huá) and rapid (数 shuò).
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Damp-Heat is an excess of both moisture and warmth in the body. Heat and humidity in the environment, spicy foods, and alcohol all add more Heat and Dampness, making the obstruction worse. Cooling applications, a dry environment, and light foods help drain Dampness and clear Heat, which unblocks the channels and eases the burning pain.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Avoid spicy, greasy, and deep-fried foods that create more Heat and Dampness. Favor cooling, light foods like cucumber, watermelon, mung beans, and barley. Stay hydrated with plain water or mild herbal teas. Gentle stretching in a cool environment helps, but avoid heavy sweating in hot conditions.

### Painful Obstruction with Liver and Kidney Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/painful-obstruction-with-liver-and-kidney-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Dull, aching pain rather than sharp or burning, Lower back and knee soreness and weakness, Pain worsens in cold or damp weather and improves with warmth, Fatigue, lack of stamina, and a pale complexion, Numbness or tingling in the limbs
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Cold, damp, or rainy weather, Overexertion and prolonged standing, Emotional stress, frustration, or worry, Cold, raw foods and icy drinks
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Applying warmth or warm compresses, Gentle stretching and movement, Warm, cooked meals, Moxibustion on the lower back
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When the Liver and Kidneys become depleted-often from aging, chronic illness, or overwork-they can no longer nourish the sinews and bones. This leaves the muscles and fascia undernourished, causing dull, lingering pain and weakness.
Because the body's underlying strength is weak, it cannot properly expel external pathogens like cold and dampness, which settle into the channels and make trigger points stubborn and slow to heal.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale, thin body with a thin white coating. Pulse: fine, weak, and slow, especially weak in the rear (Kidney) position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Cold and dampness further deplete the body's warming Yang Qi and congeal Blood, making the already undernourished sinews stiffer and more painful. Rest and warmth conserve and support the deficient Kidney Yang, while gentle movement promotes Qi flow without exhausting the reserves. Warm, nourishing foods and moxibustion directly supplement the Liver and Kidneys, helping to rebuild the foundation that keeps muscles and fascia supple.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Favor warm, cooked, and easily digestible foods like bone broths, stews, and congees. Include moderate amounts of kidney-nourishing foods such as black beans, walnuts, and goji berries. Keep the body warm, especially the lower back and knees, and avoid exposure to cold and damp environments. Gentle exercises like tai chi or qigong help circulate Qi and Blood without depleting your reserves.

## คำถามที่พบบ่อย (Frequently Asked Questions)
### Can acupuncture really release my trigger points?
Yes. Acupuncture targets both local Ashi points (the painful knots themselves) and distal points along the affected channels. The needle can elicit a brief local twitch response, which helps release the taut muscle band and restore normal blood flow. Many patients feel immediate, though sometimes temporary, relief after a session.

### How many acupuncture sessions will I need?
Most patients begin with weekly sessions for 4-6 weeks. The exact number depends on your pattern and how long you've had the pain. Acute cases may resolve in 2-4 visits, while chronic, stubborn trigger points often require 10-12 sessions or more. Your practitioner will reassess regularly and adjust the frequency as you improve.

### Can I combine TCM with my physical therapy or medication?
Absolutely. TCM works well alongside physical therapy, massage, and conventional medications. Acupuncture and herbs can enhance the effects of stretching and strengthening exercises. However, always inform both your TCM practitioner and your doctor about all treatments you are receiving, especially if you are taking blood-thinning medications or muscle relaxants.

### Will I need to change my diet?
Basic dietary adjustments can support your recovery. In general, avoid cold and raw foods that constrict the channels. Warm, cooked meals and spices like turmeric and ginger help move Qi and Blood. If your pattern involves Dampness, you may be asked to reduce dairy, greasy foods, and sugar. Your practitioner will give you specific guidance based on your pattern.

### Is it normal to feel sore after acupuncture?
Mild soreness or a bruised sensation at the needle sites is common, especially when trigger points are needled directly. This usually fades within a day and is a sign that the muscle is releasing. It should not be severe or debilitating. Applying a warm compress can help.

### Can TCM help if I've had myofascial pain for years?
Yes, but it requires patience. Long-standing pain often involves deeper Blood Stagnation or underlying deficiency. While some relief may come quickly, fully softening old knots and correcting the root imbalance can take several months of consistent treatment. The goal is not just temporary relief but lasting change in how your muscles hold tension.

## การวิจัยทางวิทยาศาสตร์สมัยใหม่ (Modern Scientific Research)
1. **Moxibustion therapy on myofascial pain syndrome: An evidence-based clinical practice guideline** ([ลิงก์](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7668527))
   *วารสารวิชาการ: Wang X, et al. Moxibustion therapy on myofascial pain syndrome: An evidence-based clinical practice guideline. Medicine. 2020;99(46):e23142.*
   This guideline systematically reviewed the evidence for moxibustion in treating myofascial pain syndrome. The panel found that moxibustion can significantly reduce pain intensity and improve physical function compared to conventional therapies, with a good safety profile. The recommendation was conditional due to the moderate quality of included studies.

2. **Effect of different needling manipulations on myofascial pain syndrome: A randomized controlled trial**
   *วารสารวิชาการ: Chen L, et al. Effect of different needling manipulations on myofascial pain syndrome: a randomized controlled trial. Zhen Ci Yan Jiu. 2024;49(12):1246-1252.*
   This trial compared different acupuncture needling techniques (lifting-thrusting vs. twirling) for myofascial pain syndrome. Both techniques reduced pain and improved local blood flow, but the combination of manipulations tailored to the TCM pattern diagnosis showed superior results in relieving trigger point sensitivity and restoring muscle function.
