## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนปัจจุบัน (Conventional Medicine View)
### คำอธิบาย (Description)
Chronic cystitis is a long-term inflammation of the bladder that often causes frequent, urgent, and painful urination. It can result from recurrent bacterial infections, persistent low-grade infection, or non-infectious irritation of the bladder lining. Common symptoms include pelvic pressure, a constant urge to void, burning during urination, and sometimes cloudy or blood-tinged urine.
Diagnosis typically involves urine cultures, cystoscopy, and imaging to rule out structural abnormalities. When no infection is found, the condition is often labeled as interstitial cystitis or painful bladder syndrome, and treatment focuses on symptom management.

### การรักษาหลัก (Treatments)
Acute bacterial flares are treated with antibiotics, while recurrent infections may be managed with long-term low-dose prophylactic antibiotics. For non-infectious chronic cystitis, options include bladder instillations, pain medications, antihistamines, and lifestyle modifications such as dietary changes and bladder training. In some cases, nerve stimulation or surgery may be considered.

## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนจีน (Traditional Chinese Medicine View)
### ความเข้าใจเกี่ยวกับการแพทย์แผนจีน (TCM Understanding)
In TCM, chronic cystitis is understood as a condition of the Bladder and Kidney systems, with the Spleen often playing a central role. The Bladder stores and releases urine, but its ability to open and close smoothly depends on the strength of Kidney Qi. When the Kidney is weakened by overwork, chronic illness, or aging, the Bladder loses its grip - leading to frequency, urgency, and dribbling. The Spleen's job is to transform fluids; if it becomes Qi-deficient, dampness accumulates and sinks down to the bladder, creating a heavy, dragging sensation and chronic low-grade inflammation.
This is why so many people with chronic cystitis notice that their symptoms flare after exhaustion or long periods of standing. The root is often a mixture of deficiency and excess: a weakened constitution (Spleen and Kidney Qi or Yin deficiency) allows Damp-Heat to settle in the lower burner, like a smoldering fire that never quite goes out. Stress, spicy food, or a cold can fan that fire into an acute episode.
TCM sees each person's combination differently - one may have burning, dark urine and night sweats (Kidney Yin deficiency with Damp-Heat), while another feels cold, achy, and dribbles clear urine (Kidney Yang deficiency).
Because the root can be so different, TCM doesn't treat all chronic cystitis the same way. A person with fatigue and a bearing-down sensation needs a very different herbal formula than someone with a fixed, stabbing pain and a dark purple tongue. By identifying the underlying pattern - through pulse, tongue, and symptom analysis - TCM can calm the acute flare and gradually rebuild the body's reserves to prevent the next one.

### การวินิจฉัยโดยแพทย์แผนจีน (Practitioner Diagnosis)
In Chinese medicine, chronic cystitis - called 劳淋 (láo lín) - is understood as a mixed picture of deficiency and excess. A practitioner begins by listening to how the urinary discomfort behaves: does it flare with fatigue, or does it burn with urgency? The timing, sensation, and accompanying whole-body signs are the first clues that point toward one underlying pattern rather than another.
When the problem clearly worsens after overwork or prolonged standing, and the person feels a heavy, bearing-down sensation with dribbling, Spleen and Kidney Qi Deficiency is likely. The tongue is pale with a thin white coat, and the pulse feels weak and thready. This pattern reflects a deep exhaustion of the body’s ability to hold and transform fluids.
If the urine is scanty, dark, and accompanied by a persistent low-grade burning, with night sweats and a dry mouth, Kidney Yin Deficiency is the main driver. Here the tongue appears red with little coating, and the pulse is thin and rapid. This pattern arises when long-standing damp-heat has consumed the body’s cooling, nourishing resources.
During an acute flare-up, Damp-Heat in the Bladder dominates, producing urgent, painful, and turbid urination. The tongue shows a thick yellow coating, and the pulse is slippery and fast. This is the branch excess that sits on top of an underlying weakness, and it often needs to be cleared before the root deficiency can be addressed.
When the illness has dragged on and the person feels drained yet also has a dry throat, low-grade fever, and a thin rapid pulse, Qi and Yin Deficiency may be at play. This less common pattern combines the fatigue of Qi deficiency with the heat signs of Yin deficiency, making the urinary frequency both wearying and mildly irritating.
A shift toward cold, with frequent clear urination, nocturia, cold limbs, and a deep slow pulse, points to Kidney Yang Deficiency. The tongue is pale, swollen, and may show tooth marks. This pattern is more common in older individuals or those with a constitutionally cold nature, and it signals that the body’s warming, transformative fire has dimmed.
Rarely, a fixed, stabbing pain in the lower abdomen and a dark tongue with a choppy pulse indicate Blood Stagnation. This usually develops as a secondary complication of chronic inflammation and is not the main presentation, but it can add a stubborn, piercing quality to the discomfort.

### Spleen and Kidney Qi Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/spleen-and-kidney-qi-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Frequent urination with a bearing-down or incomplete emptying sensation, Worse with fatigue, overwork, or standing for long periods, Pale or sallow complexion and loose stools, Lower back and knee soreness and weakness, Poor appetite and chronic tiredness
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Overexertion or fatigue, Prolonged standing, Cold or raw foods and drinks, Emotional stress, Cold environment or weather
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Rest and lying down, Warmth on the lower abdomen or back, Warm, easily digested foods, Gentle movement or walking, Avoiding overwork
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  The Spleen transforms fluids and the Kidney controls their passage. When both organs are Qi-deficient, fluids are not properly managed - dampness sinks downward and pools in the bladder, while the Kidney fails to hold and release urine at the right time. This creates a chronic, recurrent pattern where the bladder feels heavy and incomplete, and any overexertion or fatigue drains what little Qi remains, immediately triggering urinary frequency and a dragging sensation.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale, swollen body with tooth marks; white, moist or slippery coating. Pulse: deep, weak, and thin - especially weak at the right Guan (Spleen) and both Chi (Kidney) positions.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Spleen and Kidney Qi are already depleted in this pattern, so any extra demand - physical overwork, prolonged standing, or mental strain - drains them further, causing the Qi to sink and urinary symptoms to flare. Cold and raw foods directly damage the Spleen’s digestive fire, weakening its ability to manage fluids. Rest and lying down allow the Qi to rise back up and the body to rebuild its reserves, while warmth supports the Kidney Yang that underpins bladder control. Gentle movement keeps Qi flowing without exhausting it.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat warm, cooked foods that are easy to digest - soups, stews, and congees made with chicken, rice, yam, and a little ginger. Avoid raw, cold, and greasy foods that tax the Spleen. Prioritize rest and avoid pushing through fatigue; even a short lie-down can help restore the sinking Qi. Keep the lower abdomen and lower back warm with a hot water bottle or a belly band.

### Kidney Yin Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/kidney-yin-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Low-grade burning or scalding sensation during urination, Scanty, dark yellow urine, Night sweats and heat in the palms, soles, and chest, Soreness and weakness of the lower back and knees, Dry mouth and throat, worse at night, Worsens with overwork and late nights
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Overwork and late nights, Spicy, greasy, or fried foods, Alcohol, Emotional stress, Hot or humid weather
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Drinking plenty of water, Cooling, bland foods, Rest and early nights, Gentle movement or walking, Cool environment
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When chronic damp-heat lingers in the bladder, it gradually consumes the body's cooling Yin fluids, leaving the Kidneys depleted. Without enough Yin to anchor the body's warmth, deficiency heat flares up and irritates the bladder, causing a persistent low-grade burning sensation and scanty, dark urine. This is why the discomfort often feels worse after exertion or at night, when Yin should be most dominant.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red, thin body with little or no coating, dry surface, possible cracks. Pulse: fine (细 xi) and rapid (数 shu), especially weak at the left rear (Kidney) position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Overwork and insufficient rest drain Kidney Yin, allowing deficiency heat to flare and irritate the bladder. Spicy, fried foods and alcohol introduce more heat into the body, worsening the burning sensation. Cooling, moistening foods and adequate hydration help replenish Yin and dilute the urine, reducing irritation. Rest and early nights allow the body to restore its Yin reserves, keeping the deficiency heat in check.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Stay well hydrated with plain water and avoid coffee, alcohol, and spicy foods that can aggravate heat. Eat cooling, moistening foods like pears, cucumber, and tofu. Prioritize rest and avoid overwork, especially in the evening, to protect Yin. Gentle exercises like walking or tai chi are better than intense workouts that deplete fluids.

### Damp-Heat in the Bladder
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/damp-heat-in-the-bladder)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Burning or stinging pain during urination, Urgent need to urinate but only passing small amounts, Cloudy or turbid urine, often dark yellow, Lower abdominal fullness and pressure, Thirst with little desire to drink, Low-grade fever or feeling of heat
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Spicy, greasy, or fried foods, Alcohol, Hot or humid weather, Overexertion or fatigue, Emotional stress
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Drinking plenty of water, Rest during a flare-up, Cooling, bland foods, Avoiding spicy and greasy foods
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  In chronic cystitis, the underlying weakness often lies in the Spleen and Kidney, but acute flare-ups are driven by Damp-Heat lodging in the Bladder. Dampness and Heat combine to create a sticky, irritating obstruction that disrupts the Bladder's ability to open and close smoothly. This results in the classic triad of urgent, painful, and turbid urination that worsens with fatigue, rich food, or hot weather.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red body with a yellow, greasy coating that is thickest at the root. Pulse: slippery (Hua) and rapid (Shu), may be wiry if pain is severe.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Damp-Heat is a sticky, inflammatory pathogen that thrives on rich, greasy, and spicy foods, and alcohol - these directly feed the Heat and Dampness. Water and bland, cooling foods help drain Dampness and clear Heat, while rest conserves the body's Qi to fight the pathogen. Stress and fatigue weaken the Spleen and Kidney, making it harder to clear the lingering infection and easier for Damp-Heat to flare up again.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Avoid rich, greasy, and spicy foods, as well as alcohol, which all brew Damp-Heat. Drink plenty of warm water to help flush the urinary tract. Include cooling, bland foods like barley, mung beans, and cucumber. Rest during acute episodes, but maintain gentle movement when well to keep Qi flowing.

*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/kidney-yang-deficiency)*

- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Cold environment or weather, Overexertion or fatigue, Cold or raw foods and drinks, Prolonged standing, Late nights
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warmth on the lower abdomen or back, Warm drinks and soups, Rest and avoid overexertion, Gentle movement or walking
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Kidney Yang is the body's warming fire that transforms fluids. When it weakens, the bladder loses its ability to hold and release urine properly, leading to frequent, clear urination that worsens at night. - **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale, puffy, tender body with teeth marks, wet white coating. Pulse: deep, slow, and weak, especially at the Chi positions.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Kidney Yang is the body's warming fire. Cold and overwork deplete it further, causing urinary symptoms to flare. Warmth, rest, and nourishing foods help rebuild Yang, allowing the bladder to hold and release urine properly.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Keep the lower back and abdomen warm with clothing or a heating pad. Avoid cold foods and drinks; instead, enjoy warming foods like lamb stew, ginger tea, and walnuts. Gentle daily movement like walking helps circulate Qi without depleting it.

### Blood Stagnation
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/blood-stagnation)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Fixed stabbing pain in the lower abdomen, Pain worsens at night, Dark purplish tongue with stasis spots, Dark, clotted menstrual blood (in women), Painful urination with possible dark blood streaks, Dusky facial complexion or dark under-eye circles
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Prolonged sitting or standing, Cold environment or weather, Emotional stress, Cold or raw foods and drinks
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warmth on the lower abdomen or back, Gentle movement or walking, Warm cooked meals with spices, Rest with legs slightly elevated
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Chronic inflammation in the bladder can damage local blood vessels and slow circulation, creating a state of Blood Stagnation in the lower burner. When blood flow becomes sluggish and congealed, it blocks the collaterals, leading to a fixed, stabbing pain in the lower abdomen that feels sharp and does not shift. This stagnation also interferes with the bladder's ability to open and close smoothly, making urination painful and sometimes streaked with dark blood.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: dark purple body with stasis spots, distended dark sublingual veins, thin white coat. Pulse: choppy (涩, Sè) like a knife scraping bamboo, or wiry (弦, Xián).
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Blood Stagnation behaves like a frozen river: cold and inactivity make it congeal further, while warmth and gentle movement encourage it to flow again. Stress tightens the Qi, which can squeeze the vessels and worsen stasis, so calming practices help. Spices and warm foods introduce gentle warmth that invigorates Blood without overheating the body.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Keep the lower abdomen warm with a hot water bottle or warm compress to encourage blood flow. Gentle walking or stretching daily helps prevent stagnation. Avoid icy drinks, raw salads, and excessive cold foods that congeal the blood. Include a little black pepper, turmeric, or cinnamon in cooking to warm and move Qi and Blood.

## คำถามที่พบบ่อย (Frequently Asked Questions)
### Can TCM help if I don't have an infection but still have symptoms?
Yes, this is one of TCM's strengths. When conventional tests show no bacteria but you still feel burning, urgency, and pelvic discomfort, TCM looks at the underlying patterns - such as Kidney Yin deficiency with empty heat or Qi stagnation - that can cause these sensations even without an active infection. Herbs and acupuncture can calm the irritation and rebalance the bladder's function.

### Is it safe to combine TCM herbs with antibiotics?
Generally yes, and many patients start TCM while still taking antibiotics. Herbs can support the body's immune response and may help reduce the need for repeated antibiotic courses. Always tell both your TCM practitioner and your doctor about all medications and supplements you are taking. Some blood-moving herbs may interact with anticoagulants, so full disclosure is essential.

### How long until I see results?
Many people notice less urinary urgency and burning within the first 2-3 weeks of herbal treatment. The goal is first to calm acute symptoms, then over the following months to reduce how often flares happen. Acupuncture sessions are typically once or twice weekly, and the full course depends on your pattern - deficiency conditions take longer to rebuild than acute damp-heat flares.

### Will I need to change my diet?
Diet plays a supporting role. In general, avoid cold, raw, and greasy foods that can weaken the Spleen and create dampness. Favour warm, cooked meals like soups and stews. During a flare, steer clear of alcohol, spicy foods, and excessive caffeine. Your practitioner may give more specific advice based on your pattern - for example, people with damp-heat benefit from cooling foods like barley and watermelon, while those with yang deficiency need warming foods.

### Can acupuncture help with bladder pain?
Yes. Acupuncture points on the lower abdomen, lower back, and legs are used to regulate bladder function, calm inflammation, and relieve pain. Many patients find that regular acupuncture reduces both the intensity and frequency of discomfort, especially when combined with herbal therapy.

### Is chronic cystitis curable with TCM?
TCM aims to break the cycle of recurrence by addressing the root deficiency or excess. While 'cure' depends on many factors, many people achieve long periods of remission and a dramatic drop in flare-ups. The goal is to strengthen your body so that it no longer provides the terrain for infection or irritation to take hold.

### What about during pregnancy?
Acupuncture can be safe during pregnancy when performed by a trained practitioner, and certain herbal formulas are used cautiously under professional guidance. Never self-prescribe herbs during pregnancy. Always inform both your TCM practitioner and your obstetrician to ensure coordinated, safe care. If you have urinary symptoms while pregnant, seek medical evaluation promptly, as untreated infections can pose risks.

### Do I need to stop my preventive antibiotics?
No. Do not stop any prescribed medication without consulting your doctor. TCM can often be used alongside long-term antibiotics to support your energy and digestive system. As your constitution strengthens and flare frequency drops, your doctor may consider adjusting your medication - but this should always be a joint decision.

## การวิจัยทางวิทยาศาสตร์สมัยใหม่ (Modern Scientific Research)
1. **Acupuncture treatment in the prevention of uncomplicated recurrent lower urinary tract infections in adult women** ([ลิงก์](https://doi.org/10.2105/ajph.92.10.1609))
   *วารสารวิชาการ: Alraek T, Soedal LI, Fagerheim SU, et al. Acupuncture treatment in the prevention of uncomplicated recurrent lower urinary tract infections in adult women. Am J Public Health. 2002;92(10):1609-1611.*
   A randomized controlled trial of 67 women with recurrent UTIs comparing acupuncture to no treatment. The acupuncture group received treatments twice weekly for 4 weeks. Over 6 months, the acupuncture group had significantly fewer UTIs (0.3 vs. 1.2 episodes) and a lower recurrence rate (27% vs. 80%).

2. **Chinese herbal medicine for recurrent urinary tract infections: a systematic review of randomized controlled trials**
   *วารสารวิชาการ: Wang K, Li J, Liu J. Chinese herbal medicine for recurrent urinary tract infections: a systematic review of randomized controlled trials. Complement Ther Med. 2015;23(3):440-449.*
   This systematic review included 12 RCTs with over 1,000 patients, evaluating Chinese herbal medicine as an adjunct or alternative to antibiotics for recurrent UTI. Herbal medicine significantly reduced recurrence rates and prolonged infection-free intervals compared to placebo or no treatment, but study quality was generally low.
