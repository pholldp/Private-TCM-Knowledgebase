## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนปัจจุบัน (Conventional Medicine View)
### คำอธิบาย (Description)
Western medicine often attributes menstrual fatigue to hormonal fluctuations, particularly drops in estrogen and progesterone during the luteal phase and menstruation. It can also be linked to iron-deficiency anemia from heavy bleeding, premenstrual syndrome (PMS), or underlying conditions like thyroid disorders. Diagnosis typically involves blood tests to rule out anemia and thyroid issues, and tracking symptoms over cycles.

### การรักษาหลัก (Treatments)
Conventional management focuses on symptom relief: iron supplements for anemia, hormonal contraceptives to regulate cycles, and pain relievers for associated cramps. Lifestyle recommendations often include exercise, stress reduction, and dietary changes like increasing protein and complex carbohydrates. For severe PMS, antidepressants may be prescribed.

## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนจีน (Traditional Chinese Medicine View)
### ความเข้าใจเกี่ยวกับการแพทย์แผนจีน (TCM Understanding)
TCM sees menstrual fatigue as a sign that your body's vital substances - Qi, Blood, Yin, and Yang - are being drained faster than they can be replenished. Each month, menstruation naturally consumes these resources, but when the Spleen and Kidneys are weak, they can't keep up with the demand. The Spleen transforms food into Qi and Blood, while the Kidneys store the essence that fuels all bodily functions. If these systems are compromised, the period leaves you feeling like a battery that won't hold a charge.
The most common pattern is Qi and Blood Deficiency, where the Spleen isn't producing enough energy and blood to begin with. This fatigue feels like a deep, heavy drain, often with pale skin and heart flutters. When Yin fluids are also low, as in Qi and Yin Deficiency, the exhaustion comes with dryness, night sweats, and a sensation of heat. In Kidney and Spleen Yang Deficiency, the body's warming fire is too weak, leading to cold limbs, aching back, and sluggishness that worsens during the flow.
Other patterns involve the Liver. Emotional stress can cause Liver Qi to stagnate, and when combined with Blood Deficiency, you get fatigue mixed with irritability, breast tenderness, and poor appetite. Even the Lungs can be involved in Spleen and Lung Qi Deficiency, where shortness of breath and a weak voice accompany the tiredness. By looking at your unique cluster of symptoms - not just the fatigue - TCM pinpoints which organs need support and how to restore balance.

### การวินิจฉัยโดยแพทย์แผนจีน (Practitioner Diagnosis)
A practitioner starts by asking when the fatigue hits hardest and what other signs travel with it. Menstrual fatigue that feels like a deep drain with pale skin and a thready pulse points toward Qi and Blood Deficiency, the most common picture. Blood loss during the period directly depletes both resources, leaving you washed out and weak.
If the exhaustion comes with a dry mouth, night sweats, or a sensation of heat in the palms, the pattern is more likely Qi and Yin Deficiency. Here the tongue looks red with little coating, and the pulse feels thin and rapid. The body is running low on both motive power and cooling fluids.
When fatigue is accompanied by cold hands and feet, aching lower back, and a heavy sluggishness that worsens during the flow, Kidney and Spleen Yang Deficiency is at play. The tongue is often pale and puffy, and the pulse is deep and slow. The body’s warming fire is too weak to sustain energy through the period.
If irritability, breast tenderness, and a poor appetite mingle with the tiredness, Blood Deficiency with Liver and Spleen disharmony is likely. Emotional stress knots the Liver Qi, while weak digestion fails to build enough blood, so the period leaves you drained and moody. The pulse may feel wiry and fine.
Less often, a person notices shortness of breath and a very soft voice along with the fatigue, pointing to Spleen and Lung Qi Deficiency. The Spleen can’t generate enough Qi, and the Lungs can’t distribute it, so physical demands like menstruation quickly exhaust the system. The tongue is pale and the pulse is weak.

## กลุ่มอาการการแพทย์แผนจีนและการรักษา (TCM Patterns and Treatment)

### Qi and Blood Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/qi-and-blood-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Pale or sallow complexion, pale lips and nail beds, Dizziness or lightheadedness, Heart palpitations, Poor appetite and spontaneous sweating
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Overwork and insufficient rest, Skipping meals or irregular eating, Cold or raw foods, Excessive exercise, Stress and worry
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Rest and sleep, Warm, cooked meals, Gentle movement, Keeping warm
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Menstruation naturally draws on the body's reserves of Qi and Blood. When these reserves are already low, the monthly loss leaves you feeling drained and heavy. The Spleen, which produces Qi and Blood from food, is often weak in this pattern, so it cannot keep up with the demand, and the heart and brain don't get the nourishment they need to keep you feeling alert and strong.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale body, may be thin or have teeth marks, thin white coating. Pulse: fine (thin) and weak, especially weak in the right Guan position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Rest and warm, nourishing food replenish the Qi and Blood that are lost during menstruation. Overwork, skipping meals, and excessive exercise further deplete the Spleen's ability to generate Qi and Blood, deepening the fatigue. Cold and raw foods weaken the Spleen's digestive fire, while warmth supports blood circulation and energy production. Stress knots the Liver Qi, which can disrupt the Spleen's function and worsen the deficit.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat warm, cooked, nourishing foods like congee, bone broth, dark leafy greens, and small amounts of red meat or eggs. Avoid raw, cold foods and excessive exercise during your period. Prioritize rest and gentle movement like walking or restorative yoga.

### Qi and Yin Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/qi-and-yin-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Deep fatigue that intensifies during menstruation, Night sweats, Dry mouth and throat with little desire to drink, Warm palms, soles, and chest, Red, cracked tongue with thin or absent coating
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Overwork and insufficient rest, Spicy, fried, or drying foods, Hot, dry weather, Stress and worry
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Rest and sleep, Cool, quiet environment, Warm, moistening foods like congee, Gentle stretching or yoga
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Menstruation naturally consumes Qi and Blood. When the body already has a deep-seated deficiency of both Qi and Yin fluids, the added demand of the period leaves you drained. The lack of Qi fails to energize, while the lack of Yin leads to a low-grade heat that further dries out fluids, causing the fatigue to be accompanied by dry mouth, night sweats, and a sensation of warmth.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red or pale-red body with thin or absent coating, may be cracked and dry. Pulse: fine (细 xì), weak, and often rapid, especially forceless at the right Guan position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Qi and Yin Deficiency means your body is low on both vital energy and cooling fluids. Overexertion and stress further drain Qi, while heat and drying foods consume Yin, intensifying the fatigue and dry sensations. Rest, nourishing moist foods, and a calm environment help rebuild Qi and Yin, allowing your body to cope with the demands of menstruation without becoming so depleted.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat warm, easy-to-digest, moistening foods like congee, pears, and steamed vegetables. Avoid spicy, fried, and overly drying foods that deplete Yin fluids. Stay hydrated with warm water or herbal teas. Gentle activities like walking or restorative yoga help Qi flow without overtaxing. Prioritize rest, especially during your period, and aim for consistent sleep to rebuild Yin.

### Kidney and Spleen Yang Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/kidney-and-spleen-yang-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Deep tiredness that worsens with menstrual flow, Cold hands and feet, especially below the knees, Sore, aching, cold lower back and knees, Loose stools or urgent early-morning diarrhea, Swollen ankles and legs, Poor appetite and bloating after eating, Frequent urination with clear, pale urine
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Exposure to cold, Cold or raw foods, Overwork and insufficient rest, Prolonged standing or heavy lifting, Stress and worry
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warmth and hot drinks, Rest and gentle warmth on the abdomen, Warm, cooked meals, Moxibustion on the lower back, Gentle movement
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Your body's internal warming system, driven by Kidney and Spleen Yang, is too weak to generate enough energy. When Yang is deficient, the Spleen cannot transform food into the Qi and blood you need, while the Kidneys fail to provide the deep, foundational warmth that fuels all activity. During menstruation, the loss of blood further drains your reserves, and the already feeble Yang cannot keep up, leaving you profoundly tired, heavy, and cold.
Because Yang also manages fluid metabolism, its weakness allows water to accumulate, making you feel bloated and swollen, especially in the lower body. The coldness and lack of fire cause your lower back and knees to ache, and your digestion to become sluggish, with loose stools or early-morning diarrhea that sap even more energy.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale, swollen and puffy with teeth marks on the edges, and a white slippery coating. Pulse: deep, weak, and often slow; both rear (Chi) positions feel particularly feeble.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Yang deficiency means your body lacks the internal fire needed to stay warm and energized. Cold, whether from weather or food, directly damages that fire, making fatigue and aching much worse. Overwork and stress consume the little Yang you have left, while rest and warmth preserve it. Warm, cooked foods and moxibustion act like adding logs to a dying fire, slowly rebuilding the warmth and drive your body needs to overcome the deep exhaustion that comes with your period.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat warm, cooked foods like soups, stews, and congees with ginger, cinnamon, and small amounts of lamb or beef to gently stoke your digestive fire. Avoid raw, cold, and iced foods and drinks, which extinguish Yang. Keep your lower back and abdomen warm with a hot water bottle or a belly wrap, especially during your period. Gentle daily movement like walking or yoga can help circulate Qi, but avoid overexertion. Moxibustion on the lower back and navel area at home can be profoundly warming and restorative.

### Blood Deficiency with disharmony of Liver and Spleen
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/blood-deficiency-with-disharmony-of-liver-and-spleen)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Irritability and mood swings before period, Breast distension or tenderness, Poor appetite and loose stools, Scanty, pale menstrual flow, Dizziness or blurred vision
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Emotional stress or frustration, Skipping meals or irregular eating, Cold or raw foods, Overwork and insufficient rest
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Rest and sleep, Warm, cooked meals, Gentle stretching or yoga, Warm compress on lower abdomen
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Your body's blood supply is already low, so muscles and organs don't get enough nourishment. The Spleen is too weak to produce fresh Qi and blood from food, while emotional stress causes the Liver's energy to stagnate and disrupt smooth flow. When your period arrives, the blood loss deepens the deficiency and the Liver's pent‑up energy creates irritability and breast tension, leaving you profoundly tired.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale and thin, especially pale on the sides, with a thin white coat that may be slightly dry. Pulse: overall weak (Xu), wiry at the left middle (Liver) position, and weak at the right middle (Spleen) position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Emotional stress and frustration aggravate Liver Qi stagnation, which directly feeds the irritability and breast distension that accompany your fatigue. Cold, raw foods and irregular eating weaken the Spleen, making it harder to produce the blood and Qi you need. ### Spleen and Lung Qi Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/spleen-and-lung-qi-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Shortness of breath on exertion, Weak, low voice, Poor appetite with bloating, Catching colds easily, Pale, puffy face
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Overwork and insufficient rest, Cold or raw foods, Exposure to cold, Prolonged talking, Worry and pensiveness
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Rest and gentle warmth, Warm, cooked meals, Deep breathing exercises, Avoiding overexertion
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  The Spleen transforms food into Qi and Blood, while the Lungs govern breathing and distribute Qi throughout the body. When both are weak, there is a constant shortage of usable energy. Menstruation demands extra Qi to move blood, and this added burden depletes already scant reserves, leaving you profoundly tired, short of breath, and speaking in a frail voice during your period.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale, puffy, tender body with teeth marks, thin white slippery coating. Pulse: weak, especially in the right cun and guan positions.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: <p>Overexertion and prolonged talking drain Lung and Spleen Qi, so rest and gentle breathing conserve Qi. Cold and raw foods damage Spleen Yang, impairing digestion and Qi production; warm cooked foods support Spleen function. Emotional stress, especially worry, knots Spleen Qi, so calming activities help prevent further depletion.</p>
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat warm, cooked foods like congee, soups, and steamed vegetables to support Spleen digestion. Avoid raw, cold, and greasy foods. Gentle exercise like walking or tai chi is beneficial, but rest when tired. Keep your chest and abdomen warm, and avoid overexertion or prolonged talking.

## คำถามที่พบบ่อย (Frequently Asked Questions)
### Can acupuncture really help with period fatigue?
Yes, acupuncture can be highly effective for menstrual fatigue. Points like Zusanli ST-36 and Sanyinjiao SP-6 are commonly used to strengthen the Spleen and regulate menstruation, boosting your natural energy production. Many women notice a lift in their vitality after a few sessions, especially when combined with herbal support.

### How long until I feel less tired during my period?
Most women see noticeable improvement within 2-3 menstrual cycles of consistent treatment. Qi and Blood Deficiency patterns often respond faster, with more energy by the second cycle, while deeper deficiencies like Kidney Yang Deficiency may take 3-6 months to rebuild. Your practitioner will track your progress and adjust your formula as needed.

### Is it safe to take Chinese herbs with my birth control pill?
Generally, yes, but always inform both your TCM practitioner and prescribing doctor. Most herbs for menstrual fatigue, like Dang Gui and Bai Shao, do not interfere with hormonal contraceptives. However, open communication ensures your care is coordinated and safe, especially if you take other medications.

### What foods should I eat to reduce menstrual fatigue?
Focus on warm, cooked foods that support Spleen Qi and Blood production, such as soups, stews, congee, root vegetables, and small amounts of high-quality protein. Avoid cold, raw foods and icy drinks, which weaken the Spleen and worsen fatigue. Incorporating black sesame seeds, goji berries, and longan fruit can also help nourish your blood.

### Can stress make my menstrual fatigue worse?
Absolutely. In TCM, emotional stress can cause Liver Qi to stagnate, which disrupts the smooth flow of energy and blood throughout your body. When combined with underlying deficiencies, this stagnation can amplify fatigue, often alongside irritability or breast tenderness. Treatments like Xiao Yao San are designed to soothe the Liver and strengthen the Spleen simultaneously.

### Will the fatigue come back after I stop treatment?
TCM aims to correct the root imbalance, so results can be lasting if your body's reserves are fully rebuilt. However, factors like ongoing stress, poor diet, or overwork can deplete you again. Many women choose periodic maintenance sessions or seasonal herbal formulas to sustain their energy long-term.

1. **Acupuncture for premenstrual syndrome: a systematic review and meta-analysis of randomised controlled trials**

   This systematic review included 10 RCTs and found acupuncture superior to sham acupuncture and medication for reducing overall PMS symptoms, including fatigue. The meta-analysis showed significant improvements in symptom scores, though heterogeneity was present.

   *วารสารวิชาการ: Jang SH, Kim DI, Choi MS. J Altern Complement Med. 2014;20(3):208-215.*
   A randomized trial of 80 women with PMS found that Xiao Yao San significantly reduced fatigue, irritability, and breast tenderness compared to placebo, with few side effects.
