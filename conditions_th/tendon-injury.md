## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนปัจจุบัน (Conventional Medicine View)
### คำอธิบาย (Description)
In Western medicine, a tendon injury refers to damage to the tough, fibrous cords that attach muscle to bone. This can range from acute sprains and strains to chronic conditions like tendonitis or tendinopathy. Typical symptoms include localized pain, swelling, stiffness, and reduced range of motion. Diagnosis is usually made through a physical exam, sometimes supported by ultrasound or MRI to assess the extent of the damage.

### การรักษาหลัก (Treatments)
Standard care often begins with the RICE protocol-rest, ice, compression, and elevation-to manage acute inflammation. Over-the-counter NSAIDs like ibuprofen are used for pain and swelling. Physical therapy is prescribed to restore strength and mobility. For persistent cases, corticosteroid injections may reduce inflammation, and surgery may be considered for severe tears or ruptures.

## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนจีน (Traditional Chinese Medicine View)
### ความเข้าใจเกี่ยวกับการแพทย์แผนจีน (TCM Understanding)
In TCM, tendons and ligaments belong to the 'Jin' (sinews) system, which is nourished by the Liver and supported by the Kidney. The Liver stores Blood, and when Blood is abundant, tendons are supple and strong. The Kidney stores Essence, which provides the deep foundation for all connective tissues. When these organs are depleted-through aging, overwork, or chronic stress-tendons become dry, brittle, and prone to injury.
Acute trauma, like a sudden twist or fall, directly disrupts the local flow of Qi and Blood. Qi stagnation causes distension and pain; Blood stasis leads to bruising and fixed, stabbing pain. This is the Qi and Blood Stagnation pattern, the most common presentation right after an injury. If not fully resolved, or if a person is exposed to cold, damp, or windy conditions, external pathogens can invade the injured area. Cold contracts the sinews, Dampness causes heaviness and swelling, and Wind makes pain move. This Wind-Cold-Damp pattern explains why some tendon injuries ache more in bad weather and feel better with warmth.
When the body lacks the raw materials to repair-due to Qi and Blood Deficiency-the pain becomes a dull, lingering ache with weakness and fatigue. In older adults or after years of repetitive strain, Kidney and Liver Yin Deficiency may be the root, causing chronic soreness, stiffness, and associated symptoms like lower back pain and night sweats. Each pattern requires a different treatment, which is why TCM doesn’t treat all tendon injuries the same way.

### การวินิจฉัยโดยแพทย์แผนจีน (Practitioner Diagnosis)
A TCM practitioner first asks how the injury happened and what the pain feels like. Sudden trauma with sharp, stabbing pain, swelling, and visible bruising almost always points to Qi and Blood Stagnation. The tongue may look dark or have purplish spots, and the pulse often feels wiry or choppy. This is the dominant pattern right after a sprain or strain.
When the pain is described as heavy, sore, and clearly worse in cold or damp weather, the picture shifts to Wind‑Cold‑Damp obstruction. This pattern often appears when an injury is exposed to the elements or when chronic strain meets external pathogens. The tongue coating tends to be white and greasy, and the pulse may feel soft or tight.
If the injury has dragged on for a long time or the person is constitutionally weak, the pain becomes dull and is accompanied by muscle weakness and fatigue. This signals Qi and Blood Deficiency-the body simply lacks the resources to nourish and repair the sinews. The tongue is pale with a thin coat, and the pulse is thready and weak.
In older adults or after years of repetitive strain, persistent soreness in the lower back and knees, perhaps with tinnitus or dizziness, suggests Kidney and Liver Yin Deficiency. Here the sinews and bones lose their moistening and strengthening foundation. The tongue appears red with little coating, and the pulse is thready and rapid.

## กลุ่มอาการการแพทย์แผนจีนและการรักษา (TCM Patterns and Treatment)

### Qi And Blood Stagnation
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/qi-and-blood-stagnation)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Fixed, stabbing pain at the injury site, Pain worsens with pressure, Visible bruising or purple discoloration, Local swelling and distension
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Alcohol and greasy foods, Continuing activity on the injury, Cold or damp environment
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Gentle heat after 48 hours, Rest and elevation, Light massage away from the injury
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When a tendon is suddenly overstretched or struck, the local flow of Qi and Blood is disrupted. This immediate blockage causes Qi to stagnate, creating distension and pain, while Blood stasis leads to the visible bruising and fixed, stabbing sensation. The combination of stuck Qi and congealed Blood is what makes the area swollen, hot, and intensely tender to the touch.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: dark purple body with possible stasis spots. Pulse: wiry (弦 xián) and choppy (涩 sè).
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Alcohol and greasy foods generate internal heat and dampness, worsening stagnation, while cold environments further congeal Blood. Rest and gentle heat, in contrast, encourage Qi and Blood to flow again, which is the core mechanism needed to resolve stasis and heal the sinews.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: In the first 48 hours, use ice and compression to limit swelling, then switch to gentle heat to promote circulation. Eat easily digestible foods and avoid cold, raw items that can congeal Blood; small amounts of turmeric or saffron in cooking can gently move stasis.

### Wind-Cold-Damp
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/wind-cold-damp)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Pain worsens in cold or damp weather, Heaviness and aching of the limbs, Stiffness that improves with warmth or gentle movement, Swelling without redness or heat, Numbness or tingling in the injured area, Aversion to cold and wind
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Cold, damp, or windy weather, Prolonged rest or immobility, Cold foods and iced drinks, Exposure to drafts or air conditioning
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warm compresses or heat, Gentle movement and stretching, Dry, warm weather, Ginger or cinnamon tea
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When a tendon injury is exposed to cold, damp, or windy weather, or when chronic strain meets these external pathogens, the local channels become obstructed by Wind, Cold, and Dampness. Cold contracts the sinews and slows circulation, Dampness creates heaviness and swelling, and Wind causes pain to move around. This combination turns an acute strain into a lingering, weather-sensitive ache that feels deep, heavy, and stiff.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale or slightly swollen body with a white greasy coating and moist surface. Pulse: moderate (Huan) or soggy (Ru), becoming tight (Jin) when Cold predominates.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Wind, Cold, and Dampness are Yin pathogens that contract and obstruct the channels. Warmth and gentle movement disperse these pathogens, restore the flow of Qi and Blood, and relax the sinews. Cold and damp environments do the opposite-they tighten the channels and increase stagnation, which is why the pain and stiffness feel worse.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Keep the injured area warm and dry-use a heating pad or warm compress. Eat warming, easy-to-digest foods like ginger, cinnamon, lamb, and root vegetables; avoid cold, raw, or greasy foods that worsen Dampness. Gentle daily movement helps prevent stiffness, but avoid overexertion in cold or wet weather.

### Qi and Blood Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/qi-and-blood-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Dull, lingering ache rather than sharp pain, General fatigue and weakness, worse after activity, Pale complexion, lips, and nail beds, Muscle weakness or atrophy around the injury, Slow healing, injury lingers for weeks or months
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Overworking or overexercising, Skipping meals or eating cold, raw foods, Prolonged stress and worry, Cold, damp environments, Insufficient sleep
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Rest and adequate sleep, Warm, nourishing meals, Gentle stretching or Tai Chi, Warm compresses on the area, Reducing stress and worry
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Qi and Blood are the body's raw materials for repairing tissue. When they are deficient, the injured tendon does not receive the nourishment it needs to heal fully. The result is a lingering dull ache, muscle weakness around the injury, and a slow, incomplete recovery. The tendon may feel weak and easily fatigued, and the area may look pale or lack vitality.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale body, possibly thin or with teeth marks, thin white coating. Pulse: fine (thin) and weak.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Overexertion and lack of sleep drain Qi and Blood, the very substances needed to mend the tendon. Warm, nourishing foods and rest replenish these resources, while gentle movement encourages circulation without consuming them. Stress and worry damage the Spleen, the organ that produces Qi and Blood from food, so calming the mind and eating well are essential to recovery.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat warm, cooked foods like soups, stews, bone broths, and congees with lean meats, eggs, and dark leafy greens. Avoid raw, cold foods and icy drinks that strain the Spleen. Gentle daily movement such as walking, Tai Chi, or gentle stretching helps Qi and Blood circulate without depleting them. Prioritize deep, restorative sleep and avoid overexertion until strength returns.

### Kidney and Liver Yin Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/kidney-and-liver-yin-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Dull, persistent soreness in tendons, Weakness and aching of the lower back and knees, Dry eyes and blurred vision, Night sweats and heat in palms/soles/chest, Dizziness or ringing in the ears, Gradual onset from overuse or aging
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Overwork and late nights, Spicy, fried, or drying foods, Excessive sweating or heat, Chronic stress and frustration
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Adequate rest and early nights, Moistening, cooked foods, Gentle stretching or Tai Chi, Cool, calm environment
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Your tendons and ligaments depend on the nourishment of Liver Blood and Kidney Essence to stay strong and flexible. When chronic overuse, aging, or long-term stress depletes the Yin of these two organs, the sinews lose their lubrication and become dry, stiff, and fragile. This leads to persistent, dull soreness and weakness rather than the sharp swelling of a fresh injury. The lower back and knees are the first to complain, because the Kidney governs bones and the Liver governs sinews, and both are now undernourished.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red body, thin, with cracks and little or no coating, dry surface. Pulse: fine (thin) and rapid, often wiry; weak in the left Chi (Kidney) position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Kidney and Liver Yin are like the deep water reserves that keep your tendons pliable. Overwork, late nights, and emotional stress drain that water, leaving sinews dry and stiff. Spicy and fried foods add heat, which further evaporates Yin. Rest, moistening foods, and gentle movement slowly refill the reservoir, while cooling and calming habits protect what little Yin remains, allowing tendons to regain their suppleness.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Prioritize rest and avoid overwork, especially late nights, which burn Yin the fastest. Eat cooked, moistening foods like black sesame, walnuts, goji berries, yam, and bone broths. Gentle, consistent movement such as Tai Chi or swimming keeps sinews supple without draining your reserves. Steer clear of spicy, drying foods and alcohol, which fan the flames of deficiency heat.

## คำถามที่พบบ่อย (Frequently Asked Questions)
### Can acupuncture help a fresh tendon injury?
Yes, acupuncture can be started within 24-48 hours after an acute injury, once the initial swelling stabilizes. It helps reduce pain, move stagnant Qi and Blood, and accelerate healing. Distal points are often used first to avoid needling directly into the injured area. Combined with herbal liniments or poultices, recovery is often faster than rest alone.

### How soon after an injury should I start TCM treatment?
For acute sprains or strains, begin TCM as soon as possible-ideally within the first 48 hours. Early treatment with acupuncture and Blood-moving herbs can reduce swelling and bruising and prevent the injury from becoming chronic. Even if the injury is weeks old, TCM can still help resolve lingering pain and stiffness.

### Is it safe to combine Chinese herbs with over-the-counter painkillers?
Many TCM herbs for tendon injury are safe to take alongside NSAIDs like ibuprofen, but you should always inform both your TCM practitioner and your doctor. Some Blood-moving herbs (like Dang Gui or Hong Hua) may increase bleeding risk if you are also taking anticoagulants. Always provide a full medication list at your TCM consultation.

### Will TCM help with chronic tendonitis that hasn't responded to other treatments?
Yes. Chronic tendonitis often involves a mix of lingering Blood stasis and underlying deficiency, which TCM can address. By strengthening the Liver and Kidney to nourish the sinews and dispelling any Wind-Cold-Damp lodged in the area, acupuncture and herbs can reduce pain and restore function even when other treatments have failed. Improvement may take 6-12 weeks.

### Do I need to stop exercising while receiving TCM treatment?
Not necessarily, but you should modify your activity to avoid re-injury. Gentle movement and stretching are often encouraged to promote circulation, but high-impact or repetitive motions that caused the injury should be reduced. Your TCM practitioner will give you specific guidance based on your pattern and stage of healing.

### Can TCM prevent tendon injuries from coming back?
Yes, especially for chronic or recurrent tendon problems. By correcting underlying deficiencies-such as Qi and Blood Deficiency or Kidney and Liver Yin Deficiency-TCM strengthens the sinews and improves resilience. Dietary changes, appropriate exercise, and periodic acupuncture tune-ups can help maintain tendon health long-term.

### What's the difference between a tendon injury and tendonitis in TCM?
In TCM, both are considered disorders of the sinews (Jin). Acute tendon injuries from trauma are typically Qi and Blood Stagnation patterns. Tendonitis, which is inflammation and degeneration, can involve the same stagnation but often includes chronic deficiency patterns or Wind-Cold-Damp obstruction. TCM treats them based on the pattern rather than the Western diagnosis.

## การวิจัยทางวิทยาศาสตร์สมัยใหม่ (Modern Scientific Research)
1. **Acupuncture for chronic Achilles tendinopathy: a randomized controlled trial**
   *วารสารวิชาการ: Zhang BM, Zhong LW, Xu SW, et al. Acupuncture for chronic Achilles tendinopathy: a randomized controlled trial. Acupuncture in Medicine. 2013;31(2):170-175.*
   This trial compared real acupuncture to sham acupuncture in patients with chronic mid‑portion Achilles tendinopathy. The acupuncture group showed significantly greater reductions in pain and improvements in function at 8 and 16 weeks, suggesting acupuncture can be an effective treatment for this stubborn tendon condition.

2. **Acupuncture for lateral epicondylitis: a systematic review**
   *วารสารวิชาการ: Trinh KV, Phillips SD, Ho E, Damsma K. Acupuncture for the alleviation of lateral epicondyle pain: a systematic review. Rheumatology. 2004;43(9):1085-1090.*
   This systematic review evaluated multiple RCTs on acupuncture for tennis elbow. It concluded that acupuncture is effective for short‑term pain relief compared to placebo, though the evidence for long‑term benefit is less clear. The review supports acupuncture as a safe and viable option for lateral epicondylitis.
