## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนปัจจุบัน (Conventional Medicine View)
### คำอธิบาย (Description)
Cardiovascular disease is an umbrella term for conditions that affect the heart and blood vessels, including coronary artery disease, heart failure, arrhythmias, and hypertension. It is often caused by atherosclerosis - the buildup of fatty plaques in the arteries - and can lead to chest pain (angina), shortness of breath, palpitations, fatigue, and swelling. Diagnosis typically involves electrocardiograms (ECG), echocardiograms, stress tests, blood work, and imaging studies.

### การรักษาหลัก (Treatments)
Standard treatment includes medications such as statins to lower cholesterol, beta-blockers and ACE inhibitors to manage blood pressure and heart rate, antiplatelet drugs like aspirin or clopidogrel to prevent clots, and diuretics for fluid retention. For blocked arteries, procedures like angioplasty with stenting or coronary artery bypass surgery may be recommended. Lifestyle changes - diet, exercise, smoking cessation - are also foundational.

## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนจีน (Traditional Chinese Medicine View)
### ความเข้าใจเกี่ยวกับการแพทย์แผนจีน (TCM Understanding)
In TCM, the Heart is the emperor of the body - it governs blood and vessels, and it houses the Shén (spirit), which is responsible for sleep, mental clarity, and emotional calm. When the Heart is balanced, blood flows smoothly and the mind is at peace.
But the Heart doesn't work alone. The Spleen provides the Qi and blood that fuel it, the Liver ensures the free flow of Qi so blood can circulate without obstruction, the Lungs supply Qi and govern the breath, and the Kidneys anchor the body's fundamental Yin and Yang. A problem in any of these organ systems can eventually disturb the Heart.
That's why cardiovascular disease in TCM is never just about the heart muscle or the arteries. It's about what is blocking or weakening the flow of Qi and blood through the chest.
Qi deficiency leaves the Heart too weak to pump forcefully. Blood stagnation creates fixed, stabbing pain. Phlegm - a thick, turbid substance formed when the Spleen fails to transform fluids - can clog the vessels, creating a heavy, suffocating sensation. Liver Yang rising, often from stress or Yin deficiency, sends heat and pressure upward, causing palpitations and high blood pressure. Each pattern feels different, and each needs a different strategy.
Because the same Western diagnosis - say, coronary artery disease or hypertension - can stem from multiple TCM patterns, treatment is always individualized. Two patients with the same cholesterol numbers might receive completely different herbal formulas and acupuncture protocols, because one has a greasy tongue and heavy chest (phlegm) while the other has a purple tongue and stabbing pain (blood stasis). This pattern-based approach is the heart of TCM's effectiveness for chronic cardiovascular conditions.

### การวินิจฉัยโดยแพทย์แผนจีน (Practitioner Diagnosis)
If the chest discomfort is dull and oppressive, and the person is constantly weary with a pale face, the pattern is likely Qi Deficiency causing Blood Stagnation. The tongue may appear pale with dark spots, and the pulse feels weak and thready, reflecting the Heart Qi’s inability to push blood through the vessels.
When the chest pain is sharp and stabbing, fixed in one spot, and flares up with stress or anger, that points to Qi And Blood Stagnation. The tongue often looks dusky purple with stasis spots, and the pulse is wiry or choppy, indicating that emotional tension is blocking the flow of Qi and blood.
A heavy, suffocating sensation in the chest-like a weight pressing down-accompanied by a greasy tongue coating and a slippery pulse suggests Phlegm in the Chest. This pattern often appears in people with obesity or high cholesterol, where turbid phlegm accumulates and obstructs the Heart vessel.
If the main complaint is dizziness, irritability, and a pounding sensation in the head with high blood pressure, Liver Yang Rising is the likely pattern. The tongue may be red with a thin yellow coat, and the pulse is forceful and wiry, showing that Yin is too weak to anchor rising Yang.
When palpitations and chest discomfort come with night sweats, a dry mouth, and a sense of heat in the palms and soles, Qi and Yin Deficiency is at play. The tongue looks red with little or no coating, and the pulse is thin and rapid-classic signs that both the body’s energy and cooling fluids are depleted.
Shortness of breath, a weak cough, and palpitations that worsen with exertion point to Lung and Heart Qi Deficiency. Here the tongue is pale and the pulse is weak, indicating that the Lungs cannot gather enough Qi to support the Heart’s pumping action.

## กลุ่มอาการการแพทย์แผนจีนและการรักษา (TCM Patterns and Treatment)

### Qi Deficiency causing Blood Stagnation
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/qi-deficiency-causing-blood-stagnation)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Fixed stabbing chest pain that worsens with pressure, Persistent fatigue and physical weakness, Shortness of breath with reluctance to speak, Dull or dusky complexion, Spontaneous sweating with mild activity
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Overexertion or lack of sleep, Stress, anger, or frustration, Cold weather or environment, Greasy or heavy foods, Sedentary lifestyle or prolonged bed rest
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Gentle exercise (walking, tai chi), Warmth (warm drinks, warmth on chest), Hawthorn berry tea, Adequate rest, Stress reduction (meditation, deep breathing)
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When Heart Qi is deficient, it lacks the power to pump blood forcefully through the vessels. This sluggish flow allows blood to congeal and form static pools, creating a painful blockage. The result is a mix of fatigue and weakness from the Qi deficiency, alongside fixed, stabbing chest pain from the blood stasis.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale-dark or pale-purple body with stasis spots and teeth marks; thin white coat. Pulse: deep (Chen), fine or weak (Xi/Ruo), and choppy (Se).
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Overexertion drains Qi further, leaving even less force to move blood, while cold constricts vessels and worsens stasis. Stress stagnates Qi, which is already too weak to circulate blood freely. Gentle movement and warmth gently stimulate Qi and blood flow without depletion. Hawthorn moves blood stasis, and rest allows Qi to rebuild, creating a balance that relieves both the fatigue and the pain.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Gentle daily movement, like walking or tai chi, is essential to keep Qi and blood flowing without exhausting you. Eat warm, cooked foods that tonify Qi and move blood, such as hawthorn berry tea, jujube dates, and dark leafy greens. Avoid cold, raw foods and heavy, greasy meals that weaken digestion and promote stagnation.

### Qi And Blood Stagnation
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/qi-and-blood-stagnation)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Fixed, stabbing pain in the chest, Distending or bloating pain in the chest and ribs, Pain worsens with pressure, Irritability or emotional tension, Dark purplish complexion or lips, Painful periods with dark, clotted blood
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Stress, anger, or frustration, Greasy or heavy foods, Sedentary lifestyle or prolonged bed rest, Cold weather or environment
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Gentle exercise (walking, tai chi), Stress reduction (meditation, deep breathing), Warmth (warm drinks, warmth on chest), Light, warm meals
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Emotional stress, frustration, or dietary irregularities cause Qi to stagnate, particularly in the Liver. When Qi flow stalls, it can no longer propel Blood smoothly, leading to Blood stasis. In the chest, this stasis obstructs the Heart vessel, producing fixed, stabbing pain that worsens with pressure and emotional upset.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: purple or dark body with stasis spots, distended sublingual veins, thin white coat. Pulse: wiry (弦 xián) and choppy (涩 sè).
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Emotional stress and anger are the primary drivers of Liver Qi stagnation, which directly leads to Blood stasis in the chest. Heavy, greasy foods and a sedentary lifestyle further bog down Qi and Blood flow. Gentle movement, warmth, and calming practices help unblock stagnation by promoting circulation and relaxing the Liver, which is why they bring relief.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Choose lightly cooked vegetables, whole grains, and small amounts of warming spices like turmeric or ginger to nudge Qi and Blood. Avoid heavy, greasy, or fried foods that create stagnation. Gentle daily movement - a walk, tai chi, or stretching - is one of the most effective ways to keep Qi flowing. Pair this with stress management: deep breathing, journaling, or meditation can ease the emotional tension that feeds this pattern.

### Phlegm in Chest
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/phlegm-in-chest)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Heavy, stuffy chest oppression (not sharp pain), Copious white sticky phlegm, especially in the morning, Greasy white tongue coating, Sensation of something blocking the throat, Nausea and poor appetite, Heaviness in the body and dizziness
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Greasy or heavy foods, Cold, raw foods, Damp, humid weather, Sedentary lifestyle or prolonged bed rest, Overeating, Dairy products
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Light, warm meals, Gentle exercise (walking, tai chi), Dry, well-ventilated rooms, Warm ginger tea
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When the Spleen fails to transform fluids, dampness accumulates and congeals into phlegm. This thick, sticky phlegm rises to the chest and obstructs the flow of Heart Qi and blood, creating a heavy, oppressive sensation and making it hard to breathe deeply. Over time, the ongoing blockage strains the cardiovascular system and can lead to palpitations, dizziness, and a feeling of suffocation.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale, swollen body with teeth marks; thick white greasy coating; moist surface. Pulse: slippery (滑 huá), often with a wiry (弦 xián) quality.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Greasy, fried, cold, and raw foods, as well as dairy, overwhelm the Spleen and generate dampness that thickens into phlegm, directly worsening chest obstruction. Damp weather invades the body and adds external dampness to the internal phlegm, while sitting still allows Qi to stagnate. In contrast, light, warm meals and ginger tea support the Spleen's ability to process fluids without creating more phlegm. Gentle movement activates Qi, which helps the body metabolize and clear phlegm, and a dry environment prevents external dampness from adding to the burden.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat light, warm meals and avoid dairy, greasy, fried, and overly sweet foods that create dampness and phlegm. Include cooked radish, barley, and ginger to help break down phlegm. Gentle daily movement like walking or tai chi moves Qi and helps the body transform phlegm; avoid sitting for long periods.

### Liver Yang Rising
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/liver-yang-rising)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Throbbing temple headache, Dizziness or vertigo, Irritability and quick temper, Palpitations with forceful pulse, Flushed face and red eyes
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Stress, anger, or frustration, Alcohol and caffeine, Spicy or greasy food, Overexertion or lack of sleep
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Rest and quiet environment, Cooling foods and drinks, Stress reduction (meditation, deep breathing), Adequate rest
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When Liver Yin is insufficient, it cannot anchor the Liver Yang, which then surges upward like a flame. This upward rush carries heat and Qi to the head and chest, causing a pounding heartbeat (palpitations), high blood pressure, and a throbbing headache. The heart is disturbed because the flaring Yang agitates the Heart Shen, leading to irritability and insomnia, while the excess Yang overstimulates the heart, making it beat forcefully.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red body with redder sides, thin dry yellow or scanty coat, may tremble slightly. Pulse: wiry (弦 xián), especially forceful at the left middle position; may be fine and rapid if Yin deficiency is prominent.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Stress and anger directly provoke Liver Yang to surge upward, which is the core mechanism of this pattern, so calming the mind and body helps anchor it. Alcohol, caffeine, and spicy foods add heat and further unbalance Yang, while cooling, yin-nourishing foods and rest replenish the Yin that is failing to anchor the Yang.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat cooling, yin-nourishing foods like celery, spinach, and chrysanthemum tea. Avoid alcohol, coffee, and spicy or greasy foods that can fan the flames. Practice stress-reducing activities such as gentle yoga or meditation, and keep a regular sleep schedule to replenish Yin.

### Qi and Yin Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/qi-and-yin-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Heart palpitations, Night sweats, Dry mouth with little desire to drink, Fatigue and lack of strength, Shortness of breath worsened by exertion, Warm sensation in palms, soles, and chest
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Overwork and stress, Spicy or greasy food, Overexertion or lack of sleep, Excessive sweating, Dry, windy weather, Excessive talking or singing
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Adequate rest, Cool environment, Moistening soups, Gentle exercise (walking, tai chi), Deep breathing exercises
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When the body's Qi and Yin are both depleted, the heart lacks the energy to pump blood effectively and the nourishing fluids to keep the vessels moist. This leads to palpitations, chest discomfort, and a thin, rapid pulse. The Yin deficiency also creates a mild internal heat that causes night sweats and a dry mouth.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red or pale-red body with thin or absent coating, possibly cracked and dry. Pulse: fine (thin), weak, and often rapid.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Qi and Yin are already depleted, so anything that further drains them - overwork, late nights, sweating, and drying foods - worsens palpitations and fatigue. Rest, moistening foods, and gentle movement help rebuild Qi and Yin, while a cool environment soothes the empty heat from Yin deficiency.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat warm, moistening foods like congee, steamed pears, and tofu. Avoid spicy, fried, or drying foods that consume Yin. Gentle exercise such as walking or tai chi helps build Qi without overstraining. Prioritize sleep and avoid late nights to preserve Yin.

### Lung and Heart Qi Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/lung-and-heart-qi-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Shortness of breath that worsens with the slightest exertion, Weak cough with clear, thin phlegm, Spontaneous daytime sweating without heat, Palpitations and a fluttering sensation in the chest, Weak, low voice and reluctance to speak, Pale, wan complexion
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Overexertion or lack of sleep, Excessive talking or singing, Cold, raw foods, Cold weather or environment, Worry and grief
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Adequate rest, Deep breathing exercises, Warm, cooked foods, Avoiding overexertion
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  The Lungs govern Qi and respiration, while the Heart governs blood and vessels. When Lung Qi is weak, the body fails to generate enough Qi overall, and the Heart is left without the driving force it needs to pump blood smoothly. This leads to a combination of respiratory weakness and cardiac insufficiency, producing breathlessness, palpitations, and a constant sense of fatigue.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale, puffy, tender body with teeth marks, thin white coat. Pulse: weak and soft overall, especially deficient in the right Cun (Lung) and left Cun (Heart) positions.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Lung and Heart Qi are easily depleted by overexertion, prolonged talking, and the emotions of worry and grief, which directly weaken the Lungs and the Qi-producing Spleen. Cold, raw foods and cold weather further tax the body's warming and transforming functions, making it harder to generate Qi. Rest, gentle movement, and warm, nourishing foods replenish Qi at a pace the weakened body can actually use, allowing the Lungs and Heart to gradually regain their strength.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Prioritize rest and avoid overwork. Gentle, slow breathing exercises and practices like Tai Chi help build Qi without exhausting it. Eat warm, cooked foods such as rice porridge, sweet potato, and chicken soup; a little astragalus root added to broths can gently support Lung and Heart Qi. Steer clear of raw, cold, and difficult-to-digest foods that weaken the digestive fire needed to produce Qi.

## คำถามที่พบบ่อย (Frequently Asked Questions)
### Can TCM help lower high blood pressure?
Yes, especially when the hypertension is driven by a TCM pattern like Liver Yang Rising or Phlegm obstruction. Acupuncture and herbs such as Tian Ma (Gastrodia) and Gou Teng (Uncaria) can help subdue rising Yang and calm the nervous system. However, blood pressure should be monitored regularly, and any medication changes must be coordinated with your doctor.

### Is acupuncture safe if I'm taking blood thinners?
Acupuncture itself is generally safe for patients on anticoagulants, though your practitioner should use thinner needles and avoid deep needling to minimize bruising. The bigger concern is herbal medicine: many blood-moving herbs like Dan Shen or Dang Gui can enhance the effect of warfarin or aspirin, increasing bleeding risk. Always disclose all medications to your TCM practitioner.

### How long does it take to feel better with TCM?
Many people notice improvements in energy, sleep, and chest comfort within the first month. For chronic conditions like coronary artery disease or heart failure, a realistic expectation is 3-6 months of consistent herbs and weekly acupuncture to see lasting change. The goal is to correct the underlying pattern, not just mask symptoms, so patience is key.

### Do I need to change my diet while receiving TCM treatment?
Dietary adjustments are a core part of TCM. For heart disease, the general advice is to eat warm, cooked foods that are easy to digest and avoid greasy, heavy, or cold meals that create phlegm and dampness. Specific foods like hawthorn berry, garlic, and leafy greens support heart health. Your practitioner will give you personalized guidance based on your pattern.

### Can I take Chinese herbs with my heart medications?
In many cases, yes, but it must be done under professional supervision. Some herbs can interact with blood pressure drugs, blood thinners, or anti-arrhythmics. Your TCM practitioner should review your medication list and choose formulas that are safe to combine. Never stop or adjust your prescription medications without consulting your cardiologist.

### What is the best TCM treatment for chest pain?
It depends on the cause. For stabbing, fixed pain that worsens with stress, blood-moving formulas like Xue Fu Zhu Yu Tang are used. For heavy, suffocating chest pain with phlegm, herbs like Gua Lou Xie Bai Ban Xia Tang help open the chest. Acupuncture points such as Neiguan (PC-6) and Shanzhong (REN-17) are effective for many types of chest discomfort. A proper diagnosis is essential to choose the right approach.

## การวิจัยทางวิทยาศาสตร์สมัยใหม่ (Modern Scientific Research)
1. **Effect of Traditional Chinese Medicine on the Cardiovascular Diseases** ([ลิงก์](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8978630))
   *วารสารวิชาการ: Liu Y, et al. Effect of Traditional Chinese Medicine on the Cardiovascular Diseases. Frontiers in Pharmacology. 2022;13:806300.*
   A comprehensive review of molecular mechanisms and clinical evidence for classic TCM formulas (including Xue Fu Zhu Yu Tang, Sheng Mai San, and Zhi Gan Cao Tang) in treating ischemic heart disease, heart failure, and atherosclerosis. The review highlights anti-inflammatory, antioxidant, and anti-apoptotic effects.

2. **Xuefu Zhuyu decoction for coronary heart disease: a systematic review and meta-analysis**
   *วารสารวิชาการ: Zhang H, et al. Xuefu Zhuyu decoction for coronary heart disease: a systematic review and meta-analysis. Chinese Journal of Integrative Medicine. 2020;26(5):362-370.*
   A meta-analysis of 18 RCTs involving 1,562 patients found that Xuefu Zhuyu decoction combined with conventional therapy significantly improved angina symptoms and reduced the frequency of angina attacks compared to conventional therapy alone, with a good safety profile.

3. **Shengmai San for chronic heart failure: a meta-analysis of randomized controlled trials**
   *วารสารวิชาการ: Li J, et al. Shengmai San for chronic heart failure: a meta-analysis of randomized controlled trials. Evidence-Based Complementary and Alternative Medicine. 2018;2018:9381930.*
   This meta-analysis of 12 RCTs demonstrated that Shengmai San as an adjunct to standard heart failure treatment improved left ventricular ejection fraction and 6-minute walk distance, with fewer adverse events. The formula was well-tolerated and may enhance quality of life.
