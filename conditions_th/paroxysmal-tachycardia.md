## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนปัจจุบัน (Conventional Medicine View)
### คำอธิบาย (Description)
Paroxysmal tachycardia is a type of arrhythmia where the heart suddenly starts beating much faster than normal, often exceeding 100 beats per minute, and then just as suddenly returns to a normal rate. The most common form is paroxysmal supraventricular tachycardia (PSVT), which arises from abnormal electrical pathways above the ventricles. Episodes can last from seconds to hours and may cause palpitations, lightheadedness, shortness of breath, or chest discomfort.
Diagnosis typically involves an electrocardiogram (ECG) captured during an episode, or a Holter monitor worn for 24-48 hours to catch the intermittent rhythm disturbance. The condition is often benign in an otherwise healthy heart, but frequent or prolonged episodes can impair quality of life and, in rare cases, lead to more serious complications.

### การรักษาหลัก (Treatments)
Conventional management begins with vagal maneuvers - physical techniques that stimulate the vagus nerve to slow the heart rate. When these fail, medications such as adenosine, beta-blockers, or calcium channel blockers may be used to terminate an acute episode. For recurrent, troublesome tachycardia, daily preventive medications (antiarrhythmics) are prescribed, and catheter ablation - a procedure that destroys the tiny area of heart tissue causing the abnormal circuit - can offer a more permanent solution.

## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนจีน (Traditional Chinese Medicine View)
### ความเข้าใจเกี่ยวกับการแพทย์แผนจีน (TCM Understanding)
In TCM, the Heart is not just a pump - it is the house of the Shen, or mind-spirit. A calm, steady heartbeat depends on the Shen being securely anchored by sufficient Heart Blood and Yin.
When these nourishing substances run low, the Shen becomes unsettled, like a person pacing in an empty room, and the pulse races in response. This is why paroxysmal tachycardia often flares during moments of quiet or at night - when the mind has nothing to distract it, the emptiness inside the Heart is felt most acutely.
But the Heart does not stand alone. The Spleen produces the Blood that anchors the Shen, so poor digestion or chronic worry can starve the Heart and trigger palpitations. The Liver’s smooth flow of Qi prevents emotional heat from flaring upward to disturb the Heart, while the Kidneys supply the Yin and Yang that keep the Heart’s fire in balance. A weakness in any of these organ systems can ultimately destabilize the heartbeat, which is why the same Western diagnosis can have so many different TCM roots.
On the other side of the coin, the Heart can be overwhelmed by pathological factors that should not be there. Phlegm and Fire, born from a rich, greasy diet and emotional frustration, can rise to cloud the Shen and cause a forceful, agitated pounding. Stagnant Blood in the chest vessels forces the Heart to pump harder and faster, producing a stabbing, constricted sensation.
Even excess fluid - when Yang is too weak to transform it - can press on the Heart and make it race whenever you lie down. These excess patterns feel very different from the hollow flutter of a deficiency, and they require a completely different treatment strategy.
This is the core insight of TCM: the quality of your palpitations - whether they feel fluttery, hot, heavy, or stabbing - reveals the underlying imbalance. A practitioner reads not only your description but also the tongue’s color and coating, and the pulse’s depth, width, and rhythm, to identify which pattern is active. That pattern is the true diagnosis, and treatment is built around it, not around a generic label of “tachycardia.”

### การวินิจฉัยโดยแพทย์แผนจีน (Practitioner Diagnosis)
A TCM practitioner begins by asking what the palpitations feel like and when they strike. A fluttering, anxious sensation that worsens with fright or exhaustion often points toward a deficiency pattern, while a sudden, forceful pounding accompanied by restlessness suggests an excess pattern such as Phlegm‑Fire or stagnant Blood.
If the person looks pale, feels dizzy, and complains of poor memory and a timid heart, the practitioner suspects Heart Blood Deficiency. The tongue will be pale and the pulse thin and weak, confirming that Blood is too scanty to anchor the Shen. This pattern is especially common after prolonged stress, heavy menstrual bleeding, or poor nutrition.
When the palpitations come with a feeling of heat - night sweats, warm palms and soles, a dry mouth, and irritability - the picture shifts to Heart Yin Deficiency. The tongue is red with little or no coating, and the pulse is thin and rapid. The empty heat disturbs the Heart Shen, making the heartbeat more noticeable in the evening or during emotional upset.
If fatigue, poor appetite, and loose stools accompany the racing heart, the root lies in the Spleen as much as the Heart. In Heart and Spleen Deficiency the Spleen fails to manufacture enough Blood, so both organs become weak. The tongue is pale and the pulse is thin and forceless. The digestive clues are what separate this pattern from pure Heart Blood Deficiency.
A person who feels a sudden, violent pounding along with chest fullness, a bitter taste, and a sensation of agitation may be dealing with Phlegm‑Fire harassing the Heart. The tongue shows a thick, yellow, greasy coating, and the pulse is slippery and rapid. Phlegm and Fire together create a turbulent, restless energy that triggers paroxysmal episodes, often after rich food or alcohol.
When the palpitations are accompanied by cold hands and feet, an aversion to cold, and a sensation of oppression in the chest, the practitioner considers Heart Yang Deficiency. The tongue appears pale and puffy, and the pulse is deep, weak, or slow. Here the heart lacks the warming, propelling force of Yang, so the beat feels feeble and the body feels chilled.
Stabbing chest pain that accompanies the palpitations, together with a purplish tongue and a choppy or knotted pulse, signals Heart Blood Stagnation. The practitioner will ask about any history of heart disease or trauma. This pattern indicates that Blood is not flowing smoothly through the heart vessels, causing both pain and rhythm disturbance.
Rarely, the palpitations come with dizziness, swelling of the limbs, and a sensation of water rushing upward - the hallmarks of Water Qi intimidating the Heart. The tongue is pale and swollen with a slippery coating, and the pulse is deep and slippery. Fluid accumulation overwhelms the Heart, so the practitioner will also ask about urination and any history of kidney or lung weakness.

## กลุ่มอาการการแพทย์แผนจีนและการรักษา (TCM Patterns and Treatment)

### Heart Blood Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/heart-blood-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Palpitations that worsen with fatigue, Pale or sallow complexion, Dizziness and light-headedness, Insomnia with vivid dreams, Easily startled or anxious
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Overwork and exhaustion, Excessive worry or overthinking, Skipping meals or poor diet, Late nights
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Rest or sleep, Warm, cooked foods or meals, Gentle movement or walking, Deep breathing
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  The Heart relies on a steady supply of Blood to anchor the Shen (mind) and keep its rhythm smooth and steady. When Blood is deficient, the Heart muscle and its vessels are undernourished, so the Shen becomes unsettled - this is what triggers sudden bursts of rapid or pounding beats. The palpitations often come when you're tired or trying to sleep, because rest is when the body would normally draw on Blood to calm the mind, but there isn't enough to go around.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale body, possibly thin, with a thin white coating. Pulse: fine (细 xì) and weak (弱 ruò), sometimes choppy (涩 sè); especially thin at the left Cun (Heart) position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Heart Blood is made from the essence of food and replenished by rest. Overwork and mental strain consume Blood, leaving the Heart unmoored and prone to racing. Rest, sleep, and nourishing foods rebuild Blood and anchor the Shen, calming the rhythm.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Build Blood with regular, warm, nourishing meals - think soups and stews made with dark leafy greens, red dates, goji berries, black sesame, and moderate amounts of red meat or organ meats if they suit you. Avoid skipping meals and cut back on caffeine, alcohol, and spicy foods that can scatter Qi and unsettle the mind. Protect your sleep; aim for a consistent bedtime and a wind-down routine. Gentle daily movement like walking, qigong, or restorative yoga helps Blood circulate without exhausting your reserves.

### Heart Yin Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/heart-yin-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Palpitations that feel hot or fluttering, Worse at night or when resting, Night sweats, Feeling of heat in palms, soles, and chest (five-palm heat), Dry mouth and throat, Mental restlessness and agitation
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Emotional stress, Greasy, fried, or spicy foods, Overwork and exhaustion, Late nights, Hot weather
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Cooling foods and drinks, Rest or sleep, Gentle breathing exercises, Cool environment
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Heart Yin is the cooling, nourishing substance that anchors the mind (Shen). When it runs low, empty heat flares up inside, like a kettle boiling dry. This heat agitates the Heart, causing sudden bursts of rapid, fluttering beats that often feel worse when you lie down at night, because nighttime is when Yin should be strongest.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red, especially at the tip, with little or no coating, dry surface, and possibly a midline crack. Pulse: thin (fine) and rapid, often weak at the deep level, especially at the left cun position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Heart Yin deficiency means the body's cooling and calming reserves are low. Stress, overwork, and late nights burn through Yin even faster, while spicy foods and hot weather add heat, making the Heart more agitated. Cooling foods, rest, and a calm environment help replenish Yin and soothe the empty heat, allowing the Heart to settle.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Favor cooling, moistening foods like pear, lily bulb, goji berries, and black sesame. Avoid spicy, fried, or overly heating foods and drinks, including alcohol and coffee. Prioritize going to bed before 11 p.m., as late nights drain Yin. Gentle practices like meditation or yin yoga help calm the mind without overheating.

### Heart and Spleen Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/heart-and-spleen-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Palpitations that worsen with fatigue, Poor appetite and bloating after eating, Pale or sallow complexion, Fatigue and low energy, Loose or poorly formed stools, Insomnia with vivid dreams
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Excessive worry or overthinking, Raw or cold foods and drinks, Greasy, fried, or spicy foods, Overwork and exhaustion, Skipping meals or poor diet
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Rest or sleep, Warm, cooked foods or meals, Gentle movement or walking, Reducing mental strain, Small frequent meals
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Your Spleen is the engine that turns food into Qi and Blood. When it becomes weak - often from overwork, worry, or poor diet - it cannot produce enough Blood to nourish the Heart. A Heart starved of Blood loses its anchor, so it races and flutters, causing palpitations. At the same time, the Spleen's Qi deficiency leaves you feeling exhausted, with a poor appetite and bloating after meals.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale, tender body with teeth marks along the edges, thin white coating. Pulse: fine (thin) and weak, possibly choppy.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: The Spleen is easily damaged by cold, damp, and mental overstrain. Warm, cooked foods and rest give it the gentle support it needs to rebuild Qi and Blood. Overthinking and worry are the emotional enemies of the Spleen, directly weakening its ability to transform food into nourishment. When the Spleen recovers, it can finally send enough Blood to the Heart, and the palpitations naturally settle.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat warm, cooked foods like soups, stews, and congees that are easy to digest. Avoid raw, cold, and greasy items that tax the Spleen. Small, frequent meals are better than large ones. Gentle movement like walking or tai chi supports Qi flow without depleting you. Prioritize rest and protect your sleep - late nights and overwork directly drain the Spleen and Heart.

### Phlegm-Fire harassing the Heart
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/phlegm-fire-harassing-the-heart)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Sudden onset of palpitations, Chest oppression and fullness, Copious thick yellow sticky phlegm, Mental restlessness and agitation, Bitter taste in the mouth, Flushed red face and red eyes, Dizziness and head heaviness
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Stress and anger, Greasy, fried, or spicy foods, Alcohol and caffeine, Late nights and overwork
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Cooling foods and drinks, Deep breathing, Gentle movement or walking
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Phlegm and Fire are the two culprits here. Phlegm is a sticky, turbid substance that forms when the body’s fluids aren’t processed properly - often from a diet heavy in greasy, rich foods. The result is a sudden, agitated racing of the heartbeat, as the Heart struggles against the obstruction and the Fire agitates the spirit.

- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Avoid greasy, fried, and spicy foods, as well as alcohol and excess coffee-these all create Phlegm and Heat. Wind down before bed with deep breathing or meditation to keep the mind from overheating.

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Palpitations that worsen with exertion, Cold hands and feet, Chest stuffiness or mild chest pain, Pale or sallow complexion, Fatigue and low energy, Spontaneous sweating, Mental listlessness
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Cold weather or drafts, Overwork and exhaustion, Raw or cold foods and drinks, Emotional shock or fear
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Rest or sleep, Warmth applied to body, Gentle movement or walking, Warm, cooked foods or meals
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Heart Yang is the warm, propelling force that keeps blood moving and the rhythm steady. When that fire dims, the Heart struggles to pump effectively, so it races intermittently in an effort to compensate - but the beats feel hollow and weak. Because Yang also warms the body, its deficiency leaves the hands and feet cold, the face pale, and the chest heavy.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale, puffy, tender with teeth marks and a white slippery coating. Pulse: deep, weak, and slow; may be knotted (irregular pauses).
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Heart Yang is the body's internal fire. Cold, whether from weather or food, directly extinguishes that fire, while overexertion drains the already limited Yang, triggering or worsening palpitations. Rest and warmth conserve and gently stoke the Yang, allowing the Heart to beat with more strength and steadiness.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Keep the chest and feet warm; avoid cold drafts. Eat cooked, warming foods like soups with ginger, cinnamon, and a little lamb or chicken. Gentle daily movement such as walking or tai chi helps circulate Yang without exhausting it. Avoid overexertion and emotional shocks.

### Heart Blood Stagnation
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/heart-blood-stagnation)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Stabbing or pricking chest pain, Pain stays in one fixed spot, Purple or dark lips and nails, Cold hands and feet, Feeling of suffocation or tightness in the chest, Anxiety or restlessness during pain episodes
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Cold weather or cold drinks, Stress and anger, Sedentary lifestyle, Greasy, fried, or spicy foods, Overwork and exhaustion
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Gentle movement or walking, Warmth applied to body, Deep breathing, Warm, cooked foods or meals, Rest or sleep
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When Blood stagnates in the vessels that nourish the Heart, it is like a traffic jam inside the chest. The Heart has to pump harder and faster to push through the blockage, which triggers sudden, forceful bouts of rapid heartbeat. The stabbing pain, fixed location, and purple tongue are all signs that Blood is stuck and not flowing smoothly.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: purple or dark-red body with stasis spots, distended sublingual veins, and a thin white coating. Pulse: choppy (涩) or knotted (结), often irregular with pauses, felt most clearly at the left Cun position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Blood stasis is like a frozen river; cold and inactivity thicken it further, while warmth and gentle movement help it flow smoothly. Stress and anger cause Qi to stagnate, which can worsen blood stasis, so relaxation and deep breathing are crucial. Heavy foods burden digestion and create phlegm, which can further obstruct the vessels.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Avoid cold, raw foods and heavy, greasy meals that can congeal Blood. Gentle daily movement like walking or tai chi helps keep blood flowing. Manage stress with breathing exercises, as emotional tension can worsen stagnation. Keep the chest warm and avoid prolonged sitting.

### Water Qi intimidating the Heart
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/water-qi-intimidating-the-heart)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Palpitations worse when lying down, Swelling of lower legs or body, Cold hands and feet, Sensation of something rushing upward from stomach to chest, Pale, swollen, wet tongue with teeth marks
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Lying flat, Cold weather or drafts, Excessive fluid intake, Salty foods, Overwork and exhaustion, Emotional stress
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Sitting up or propped up, Warmth applied to body, Gentle movement or walking, Warm, cooked foods or meals, Rest or sleep
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When Heart Yang and Kidney Yang are weak, the body can no longer transform and move fluids properly. Water builds up internally and surges upward, pressing on the Heart and disrupting its rhythm. This causes the sudden pounding sensation, often worse when lying down because the fluid shifts and presses more heavily on the chest. The coldness and swelling are signs that Yang Qi is too depleted to warm the limbs and manage water.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: Pale, swollen, tender body with teeth marks; white slippery water-slick coating. Pulse: Wiry and slippery (弦滑 xián huá), or deep and fine (沉细 chén xì).
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Lying flat allows pooled fluids to press on the Heart, while sitting up helps gravity shift fluid downward. Coldness further weakens Yang, so warmth supports the body's ability to transform water. Gentle movement encourages Qi flow and fluid metabolism without overtaxing the Heart. Overexertion and stress deplete Yang and stir up internal water, making symptoms worse.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Keep the body warm, especially the lower back and feet, to support Yang Qi. Avoid raw, cold, and overly salty foods, as they burden fluid metabolism. Gentle movement like walking or tai chi can help circulate Qi and fluids without straining the Heart. Limit excessive fluid intake, especially cold drinks, and avoid lying flat immediately after meals.

## คำถามที่พบบ่อย (Frequently Asked Questions)
### Can Chinese herbs interact with my heart medication?
Yes, certain herbs can interact with antiarrhythmics, beta-blockers, or blood thinners. For example, Dang Gui (Angelica sinensis) and Dan Shen (Salvia miltiorrhiza) have mild blood-moving properties that may amplify the effect of anticoagulants like warfarin. Always bring a complete list of your medications to your TCM consultation. Your practitioner will select a formula that is safe alongside your current treatment, and we strongly recommend keeping your cardiologist informed.

### What does acupuncture for palpitations feel like?
Acupuncture for this condition is generally gentle and deeply relaxing. The key points are often on the inner forearm (Neiguan PC-6) and the wrist (Shenmen HT-7), along with points on the back and legs. You may feel a mild ache or a warm, spreading sensation when the needle is inserted, followed by a profound sense of calm. Many patients notice their breathing slows and their heart feels steadier even during the session.

### How long until I feel better?
Most patients notice a reduction in the frequency and severity of their tachycardia episodes within 4-6 weeks of weekly acupuncture and daily herbs. Some feel an immediate calming effect after the first treatment. However, lasting change requires correcting the underlying deficiency or clearing the pathogenic factor, which can take several months, especially for patterns rooted in long-standing Blood or Yang deficiency.

### Do I need to change my diet?
Diet plays a supportive role. In general, avoid stimulants like caffeine, alcohol, and spicy foods that agitate the Heart. Heavy, greasy meals create Phlegm that can cloud the Shen and trigger palpitations. Instead, favor foods that nourish Blood and calm the mind, such as longan fruit, jujube seeds, millet, and lightly cooked leafy greens. Your practitioner will give you more specific dietary guidance based on your pattern.

### Can TCM help if my tachycardia is caused by anxiety?
Absolutely. In TCM, anxiety and palpitations often share the same root - an unsettled Shen due to Heart Blood or Yin deficiency, or Phlegm-Fire disturbing the Heart. By calming the Shen and nourishing the Heart, TCM addresses both the emotional trigger and the physical symptom simultaneously. Many patients find that as their palpitations settle, their overall anxiety level drops significantly.

### Will the palpitations come back after treatment?
TCM aims to correct the underlying imbalance, not just suppress symptoms. When treatment is completed and the body’s reserves are rebuilt, the tendency to develop tachycardia is greatly reduced. However, if the lifestyle or emotional patterns that originally caused the imbalance return, episodes can recur. Your practitioner will help you identify your personal triggers and teach you self-care techniques - acupressure, breathing exercises, dietary adjustments - to maintain your progress.
