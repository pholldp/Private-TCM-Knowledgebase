## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนปัจจุบัน (Conventional Medicine View)
### คำอธิบาย (Description)
In Western medicine, lumbar disc disease refers to a range of problems involving the intervertebral discs in the lower spine, most commonly herniation (where the inner gel-like nucleus pushes through a tear in the outer annulus) or degenerative disc disease (where discs lose hydration and height over time). When a herniated disc compresses a spinal nerve root, it can cause sciatica - pain, numbness, or weakness radiating down the leg. Diagnosis is typically confirmed with MRI or CT scans, and severity is graded by the degree of disc displacement and nerve involvement.

### การรักษาหลัก (Treatments)
Standard treatment usually begins with conservative measures: rest, activity modification, physical therapy, and anti-inflammatory medications (NSAIDs) for pain. If these fail, epidural steroid injections may be used to reduce nerve root inflammation. Surgery (microdiscectomy, laminectomy, or spinal fusion) is reserved for severe cases with progressive neurological deficits or intractable pain.

## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนจีน (Traditional Chinese Medicine View)
### ความเข้าใจเกี่ยวกับการแพทย์แผนจีน (TCM Understanding)
In TCM, the lower back is considered the 'palace of the Kidneys' (yāo wéi shèn zhī fǔ). The Kidneys govern the bones and marrow, and the Liver nourishes the sinews and tendons. When the Kidney essence and Liver blood are depleted - through aging, overwork, or chronic illness - the spinal discs lose their nourishment, becoming brittle and prone to herniation. This is why disc problems are more common with age and why they often feel like a deep, bone-weary ache.
Acute disc herniation after a sudden strain or injury is understood as Qi and Blood Stagnation. The traumatic force obstructs the flow of Qi and blood in the channels of the lower back, causing sharp, stabbing pain that is fixed in one spot. The stagnation leads to local swelling and inflammation, which further compresses nerves and radiates pain down the leg. This pattern is often marked by a purplish tongue and a wiry, choppy pulse.
When dampness and heat combine and settle into the lower back - often in hot, humid weather or in people with a diet rich in greasy, spicy foods - they create a painful obstruction pattern. The pain feels heavy and burning, and it worsens with warmth. This corresponds to the inflammatory flare-ups that many disc patients experience, where the area feels swollen and hot. A red tongue with a yellow, greasy coating is a hallmark.
Because these three patterns have different root causes, the same Western diagnosis of 'lumbar disc herniation' can require completely different TCM treatments: moving blood for stagnation, tonifying the Liver and Kidneys for deficiency, or clearing damp-heat for inflammation. A skilled TCM practitioner distinguishes them through pain quality, accompanying symptoms, and tongue and pulse diagnosis.

### การวินิจฉัยโดยแพทย์แผนจีน (Practitioner Diagnosis)
A TCM practitioner begins by asking what the pain feels like, when it started, and what makes it better or worse. The quality of the ache, its daily rhythm, and any accompanying signs like thirst or fatigue are the first clues that point toward one pattern rather than another. Tongue and pulse examination then confirms the picture, revealing the underlying state of Qi, Blood, and internal organs.
If the pain is sharp and stabbing, fixed in one spot, and began after a sudden strain or injury, Qi and Blood Stagnation is the likely pattern. The lower back often feels stiff and board-like, and the pain may worsen at night. A purplish tongue, possibly with dark spots, and a wiry or choppy pulse support this diagnosis.
When the discomfort is a dull, chronic soreness with deep weakness and fatigue, Painful Obstruction with Liver and Kidney Deficiency is suspected. The pain tends to flare after exertion and ease with rest. Additional clues include weak knees, dizziness, or tinnitus. The tongue is typically pale with a thin white coating, and the pulse is deep, thin, and often weak.
A burning, heavy sensation in the low back that gets worse in warm, humid weather points toward Painful Obstruction due to Damp Heat in Channels. This pattern often brings a feeling of heat in the joints, thirst, and dark, scanty urine. The tongue is red with a thick, greasy yellow coating, and the pulse feels rapid and slippery, reflecting the internal heat and dampness.

## กลุ่มอาการการแพทย์แผนจีนและการรักษา (TCM Patterns and Treatment)

### Qi And Blood Stagnation
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/qi-and-blood-stagnation)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Stabbing, fixed low back pain, Pain worsens with pressure or at night, Dark purple tongue with stasis spots, Stiffness and limited range of motion
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Prolonged sitting or standing still, Cold, damp weather, Heavy lifting or sudden twisting, Emotional stress and tension
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Gentle movement, Applying warmth to the back, Resting
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When the lower back is suddenly strained or injured, the local flow of Qi becomes obstructed, and blood pools in the meridians. This stagnant blood creates a sharp, stabbing pain that is fixed in one spot, because the blocked channels fail to nourish the discs and surrounding tissues. Over time, the stasis can also compress the nerves, causing pain to radiate down the leg.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: dark purple body with stasis spots and distended sublingual veins, thin white coat. Pulse: wiry (弦 xián) and choppy (涩 sè), like a taut string scraping bamboo.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Inactivity and cold cause Qi and blood to congeal, intensifying the stasis that underlies this pattern. Gentle movement and warmth disperse stagnation and restore flow through the blocked channels, which is why they ease the pain. Emotional stress knots the Liver Qi, making the stagnation worse, so staying calm and avoiding strain helps prevent flare-ups.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Avoid cold, raw foods and icy drinks, which can congeal blood and worsen stasis. Gentle, regular movement like walking or tai chi helps keep Qi and blood flowing, but stop any activity that triggers sharp pain. Apply a warm compress to the lower back for 15 minutes a day to promote circulation.

### Painful Obstruction with Liver and Kidney Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/painful-obstruction-with-liver-and-kidney-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Dull, chronic low back ache, Knee soreness and weakness, Fatigue and lack of stamina, Pain worse with cold or damp weather, Numbness or tingling in the legs, Pale or dull complexion
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Cold, damp weather, Overwork, Prolonged standing, Raw, cold foods
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Applying warmth to the back, Resting, Gentle movement, Warm, nourishing foods
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  The Liver and Kidneys are the root of the sinews and bones. When they become depleted - often through aging, overwork, or chronic illness - the lumbar spine loses its nourishment. This weakness invites Cold and Damp to settle in the lower back, causing a deep, dull ache that lingers and worsens with fatigue.
Because the Kidneys govern the lower back and the Liver controls the sinews, deficiency leads to chronic soreness, knee weakness, and a heavy sensation in the legs. The pain is not sharp or fixed like a fresh injury, but rather a persistent, gnawing discomfort that flares up in cold or damp weather.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale, thin body with a thin white coating. Pulse: fine, weak, and slow, especially weak in the Chi (rear) position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: <p>Cold and Damp are the very pathogens that lodge in the weakened lower back, so they directly worsen the obstruction and pain. Overwork and prolonged standing further deplete the already deficient Liver and Kidneys, leaving the sinews and bones even less nourished.</p><p>Warmth and rest help by conserving Qi and driving out Cold, while gentle movement encourages blood and Qi to circulate through the stiff, poorly nourished tissues without causing further strain.</p>
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Keep your lower back warm and avoid sitting or sleeping in drafty, damp places. Gentle, consistent movement like walking or tai chi keeps Qi flowing without draining your reserves. Eat warm, cooked foods - bone broths, stews with black beans, walnuts, and goji berries - and steer clear of raw, cold meals that chill the body.

### Painful Obstruction due to Damp Heat in Channels
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/painful-obstruction-due-to-damp-heat-in-channels)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Burning, hot lower back pain, Heavy, dragging sensation in the back and legs, Pain worsens with heat or humidity, Pain eases with cold compresses, Thirst with dark, scanty urine
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Hot, humid weather, Hot baths or saunas, Spicy, greasy, or fried foods, Alcohol
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Cold compress on the back, Cool, dry weather, Light, cooling foods (e.g., barley, cucumber)
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When dampness and heat combine and settle into the lower back, they block the flow of Qi and blood through the channels that travel to the legs. This creates a heavy, burning pain that feels worse with warmth or humidity, because heat intensifies the fire and dampness makes everything feel swollen and stuck. The inflamed, boggy environment around the disc irritates the nerves, causing radiating pain and stiffness.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red, possibly slightly swollen, with a yellow greasy coating. Pulse: slippery and rapid, possibly soggy.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Damp-heat is a combination of two pathogenic factors: dampness makes the body feel heavy and swollen, while heat creates burning pain. Warm, humid weather and hot applications add more heat and moisture, making the blockage worse. Spicy and greasy foods also generate internal damp-heat. Cold compresses and cooling foods directly counteract the heat, while dry, cool air and light foods help the body drain the excess dampness, easing the pain.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Stay in a cool, dry environment and avoid hot baths or heating pads on the back. Eat light, cooling foods like mung beans, barley, cucumber, and watermelon; cut back on greasy, spicy, and fried foods that generate damp-heat. Gentle movement like walking or swimming helps keep Qi flowing, but avoid sweaty, exhausting workouts.

## คำถามที่พบบ่อย (Frequently Asked Questions)
### Can acupuncture really help with sciatica from a herniated disc?
Yes. Acupuncture works by releasing muscle spasm, improving local blood flow, and reducing inflammation around the compressed nerve root. In TCM terms, it moves stagnant Qi and blood in the Bladder and Gallbladder channels that run from the lower back down the leg. Many patients feel a reduction in radiating leg pain after the first few sessions, though lasting relief typically requires a series of treatments combined with herbs.

### How long until I see results?
For acute injuries, you may feel noticeably better after 1-2 weeks. Chronic, long-standing disc problems usually take 4-8 weeks of weekly acupuncture and daily herbs to see a meaningful shift. If the root is Liver and Kidney deficiency, rebuilding deep reserves can take several months, but you should still experience gradual improvements in pain and function along the way.

### Can TCM replace surgery for a herniated disc?
In many cases, yes - especially when the disc herniation is not causing severe, progressive nerve damage. TCM can reduce pain, inflammation, and muscle guarding, allowing the body to reabsorb the herniated material naturally. However, if you have loss of bladder or bowel control, rapidly worsening leg weakness, or cauda equina syndrome, surgery is necessary and TCM should be used as a complementary support afterward, not a replacement.

### Is it safe to combine TCM with my current medications?
Generally, yes. Acupuncture and most herbal formulas can be used alongside NSAIDs, muscle relaxants, and physical therapy. If you take blood thinners (warfarin, aspirin, clopidogrel), tell your TCM practitioner because some herbs like Dang Gui and Chuan Xiong have mild blood-moving properties. Always keep both your doctor and your herbalist informed of everything you are taking.

### Will I need to change my diet?
Dietary guidance depends on your pattern, but some basics apply to almost everyone with disc problems: avoid cold, raw foods and icy drinks, which can worsen stagnation and dampness. Minimize greasy, fried, and spicy foods that generate damp-heat. Favor warm, cooked meals, bone broths, and foods that nourish the Kidneys like black beans, walnuts, and dark leafy greens.

### Can TCM prevent future disc problems?
Yes - that's one of its strengths. By correcting the underlying pattern (whether it's a tendency to blood stagnation, Kidney weakness, or damp-heat), TCM aims to make your spine more resilient. Many patients find that after a course of treatment, their back feels stronger and minor strains no longer trigger major flare-ups. Lifestyle and dietary advice help maintain those gains long-term.

### Does TCM work if I've already had back surgery?
Absolutely. Acupuncture and herbs can help manage post-surgical pain, reduce scar tissue formation, and improve mobility. They also address the constitutional imbalances that contributed to the original disc problem, which surgery alone does not correct. Just be sure to tell your practitioner about your surgical history so they can adapt point selection and herbal formulas accordingly.

## การวิจัยทางวิทยาศาสตร์สมัยใหม่ (Modern Scientific Research)
1. **Clinical observation on Qishao Huoxue Formula for lumbar disc herniation of Qi stagnation and blood stasis type and its effects on hemorheology and serum inflammatory factors**
   *วารสารวิชาการ: Author(s) not specified. Published in Chinese Journal of Experimental Traditional Medical Formulae, 2022.*
   This RCT evaluated a modified blood-activating formula in 120 patients with Qi and Blood Stagnation pattern LDH. The treatment group receiving Qishao Huoxue Formula showed significantly greater reductions in VAS pain scores and improvements in Japanese Orthopaedic Association scores compared to the control group taking diclofenac. The herbal group also demonstrated favourable changes in hemorheology indices and a decrease in serum inflammatory cytokines, suggesting an anti-inflammatory mechanism.

2. **Acupuncture for chronic low back pain: a Cochrane systematic review** ([ลิงก์](https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD001351.pub3))
   *วารสารวิชาการ: Furlan AD, van Tulder MW, Cherkin DC, et al. Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews, 2015, Issue 1. Art. No.: CD001351.*
   This Cochrane review included 33 RCTs and found moderate-quality evidence that acupuncture is more effective than no treatment or sham acupuncture for short-term pain relief in chronic low back pain. Function improved significantly compared with no treatment. The review covered various types of low back pain, including disc-related pain, and concluded that acupuncture is a reasonable referral option.

3. **Clinical practice guideline for low back pain (lumbar disc herniation) with TCM rehabilitation**
   *วารสารวิชาการ: Chinese Association of Rehabilitation Medicine. Published in Chinese Journal of Rehabilitation Medicine, 2021.*
   This national guideline, developed by the Chinese Association of Rehabilitation Medicine, standardises the TCM diagnosis and treatment of lumbar disc herniation. It outlines several patterns (including Qi and Blood Stagnation, Damp-Heat Obstruction, and Deficiency patterns) and recommends corresponding herbal formulas, acupuncture protocols, and external therapies based on evidence grading. The guideline serves as a key reference for clinical practice in China.
