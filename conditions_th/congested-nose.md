## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนปัจจุบัน (Conventional Medicine View)
### คำอธิบาย (Description)
Western medicine understands nasal congestion as a swelling of the nasal passages and sinuses, usually from inflamed blood vessels. The most common triggers are viral infections like the common cold, allergies to pollen or dust, or structural issues like a deviated septum. The lining of the nose swells and produces extra mucus, which blocks airflow.
Diagnosis is usually straightforward based on symptoms and a physical exam. Treatment targets the swelling directly - shrinking the blood vessels with decongestants, calming the allergic reaction with antihistamines, or reducing inflammation with steroid sprays.

### การรักษาหลัก (Treatments)
Standard Western treatment for a stuffy nose often involves over-the-counter decongestant sprays or pills, oral antihistamines for allergies, and corticosteroid nasal sprays for longer-term inflammation control. For chronic congestion linked to allergies, immunotherapy may be recommended. While these approaches can offer rapid temporary relief, they primarily manage the symptom rather than addressing why one person's nasal passages are so reactive in the first place.

## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนจีน (Traditional Chinese Medicine View)
### ความเข้าใจเกี่ยวกับการแพทย์แผนจีน (TCM Understanding)
TCM understands nasal congestion primarily through the Lungs and their relationship with the nose. The Lungs govern the opening and closing of the nasal passages, and they spread a defensive Qi called Wei Qi across the body's surface to protect against external invaders like Wind-Cold or Wind-Heat. When this system is strong, the nose stays clear. When it's disrupted - by an acute invasion of Wind, or by a chronic weakness in the Lung, Spleen, or Kidney systems - the nose becomes blocked.
The quality of the congestion and discharge tells the story. A sudden stuffy nose with clear, watery mucus and chills points to a Wind-Cold invasion tightening the nasal passages. A blocked nose with thick, yellow, burning mucus and a sore throat suggests Wind-Heat drying up fluids and creating sticky phlegm.
When the stuffiness is chronic and mild, with thin clear discharge and fatigue, Lung Qi is too weak to keep the nose open. A heavy, blocked sensation with sticky white mucus and digestive bloating tells us the Spleen is failing to manage fluids, letting dampness rise to the head.
In some cases, the Kidneys are involved. Kidney Yang provides foundational warmth to the whole body, and when it's depleted, a deep internal cold settles in. This cold congeals in the nasal passages, causing congestion that's worse at night and when lying down. Finally, lingering Heat trapped in the Lungs - often after a cold - dries up normal moisture and creates a sensation of hot, dry obstruction with thick yellow discharge.

### การวินิจฉัยโดยแพทย์แผนจีน (Practitioner Diagnosis)
A TCM practitioner starts by exploring the quality of the discharge, the timing of the stuffiness, and everything else that comes with it - from body temperature to energy levels. The tongue and pulse then confirm which pattern is driving the congestion.
If the nose runs with clear, watery fluid and you feel chilled, achy, and prefer to stay bundled up, it points strongly to Wind-Cold invading the Lungs. The tongue coat is thin and white, and the pulse feels floating and tight, like a drum skin pulled taut by cold.
When the discharge is thick, yellow, or sticky and the nose feels hot or irritated, Wind-Heat invading the Lungs is more likely. There may be a sore throat, thirst, or mild fever. The tongue tip is redder, the coat is thin and yellow, and the pulse is floating and rapid.
Chronic, mild congestion with clear, thin discharge that worsens after a cold or when tired suggests Lung Qi Deficiency. The voice is often soft, the complexion pale, and catching colds is frequent. The tongue looks pale and the pulse is weak, showing the Lungs lack the strength to keep the nose open.
If the nose feels heavy and blocked with sticky, white mucus, and you also experience bloating, poor appetite, or loose stools, Spleen Deficiency with Dampness is at play. The tongue is pale and swollen with a greasy white coat, and the pulse feels soft or slippery - signs that internal dampness is rising to clog the nasal passages.
When the congestion comes with thick yellow mucus, a dry nose, and a sense of internal heat, Lung Heat may be smoldering. The tongue is red with a yellow coat and the pulse is rapid. This pattern often brings dryness in the throat or constipation as heat dries up fluids.
Congestion that worsens noticeably at night and is accompanied by cold limbs, low back soreness, and frequent nighttime urination points to Kidney Yang Deficiency. The tongue is pale and swollen with a white, slippery coat, and the pulse is deep and weak - a sign that the body’s warming fire is too low to dispel cold from the nose.

### Wind-Cold invading the Lungs
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/wind-cold-invading-the-lungs)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Sudden onset of nasal congestion, Clear, watery nasal discharge, Chills more prominent than fever, No sweating, Itchy throat, Headache and body aches
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Cold weather or drafts, Cold drinks and raw foods, Overexertion, Lack of sleep
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warmth and covering up, Hot tea or soup, Rest, Steam inhalation, Ginger and scallion
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When Wind-Cold invades the body, it first attacks the Lungs' defensive exterior. The cold contracts and tightens the nasal passages, while the Lung Qi becomes stuck and cannot properly open the nose. This leads to a sudden, stuffy nose with a clear, watery discharge, often accompanied by chills and an aversion to cold.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: normal or slightly pale body with a thin, white, moist coating. Pulse: floating (fu) and tight (jin).
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Wind-Cold is an exterior pathogen that constricts the Lung Qi and blocks the nose. Warmth and rest support the body's defensive Qi to push the cold out, while steam and warm fluids loosen the tightness in the nasal passages. Cold, raw foods, and overwork further weaken the defensive Qi and allow the pathogen to linger, making the congestion worse.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Keep warm and avoid drafts. Sip hot tea with fresh ginger and scallion white to help the body push out the cold. Light, warm soups and congees are ideal; avoid raw, cold, or greasy foods until the congestion clears. Rest is essential - do not push through with heavy exercise.

### Wind-Heat invading the Lungs
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/wind-heat-invading-the-lungs)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Thick, yellow or turbid nasal discharge, Sensation of heat or burning in the nose, Fever that feels stronger than any chills, Sore throat, Thirst or dry mouth, Red tongue tip with thin yellow coating
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Spicy or fried foods, Hot, stuffy environments, Wind and drafts, Overexertion, Emotional frustration
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Cool, fresh air, Peppermint or chrysanthemum tea, Rest and avoiding overwork, Light, cooling foods like pear
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When Wind-Heat attacks the body, it first invades the Lungs and the defensive exterior. The Heat component dries up normal fluids and creates thick, yellow, sticky mucus that clogs the nasal passages. The Lung's natural function of diffusing and descending Qi is disrupted, trapping the pathogen in the nose and causing congestion with a sensation of heat and burning.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red tip with a thin yellow coating, body slightly red. Pulse: floating (浮 fú) and rapid (数 shuò).
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Wind-Heat is an external pathogen that thrives on Heat and agitation. Spicy foods and hot environments add more Heat and worsen the burning sensation and thick mucus. Rest and cooling foods help the body clear the pathogen, while peppermint and chrysanthemum specifically disperse Wind-Heat and soothe the throat. Avoiding wind prevents further invasion, and gentle breathing helps restore the Lung's diffusing function.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Drink plenty of water and cooling herbal teas like peppermint or chrysanthemum. Avoid spicy, fried, or greasy foods that generate internal Heat. Rest in a well-ventilated room and protect yourself from wind and drafts.

### Lung Qi Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/lung-qi-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Mild, intermittent nasal congestion that worsens with fatigue, Clear, thin, watery nasal discharge, Spontaneous daytime sweating with little exertion, Low, weak voice and reluctance to speak, Sensitivity to wind and cold, catching colds easily
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Overexertion, Cold and windy weather, Eating raw or cold foods, Stress and worry, Lying flat
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Rest and warmth, Gentle exercise like walking, Steam inhalation, Warm soups and teas, Avoiding wind and cold
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  The Lungs govern the opening and closing of the nose, and Lung Qi provides the strength to keep the nasal passages clear. When Lung Qi is weak, that gatekeeping function falters - the nose fails to open fully, leading to a chronic, low-grade stuffiness. At the same time, the body's defensive shield (Wei Qi) is thin, so you catch colds easily, and each cold brings a fresh wave of congestion with thin, clear discharge.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale, tender body with possible teeth marks and a thin white coating. Pulse: empty and weak, especially at the right Cun (Lung) position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Lung Qi is already weak, so overexertion and cold deplete it further, making the nose more prone to blockage. Rest, warmth, and gentle exercise conserve and build Qi, while steam and warm fluids help thin mucus and open the nasal passages. Stress and worry stagnate Qi in the chest, worsening the Lung's ability to descend and disperse.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat warm, cooked foods like soups, stews, and congee; avoid raw, cold, and dairy-heavy foods that create dampness and strain digestion. Gentle daily movement like walking or Tai Chi builds Lung Qi without overexertion. Keep your neck and chest covered in windy or cold weather, and try steam inhalation with a few drops of eucalyptus oil to temporarily open the nose.

### Spleen Deficiency with Dampness
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/spleen-deficiency-with-dampness)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Heavy, sticky white nasal discharge, Abdominal bloating and loose stools, Feeling of heaviness in the head and body, Fatigue and poor appetite
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Cold, raw foods, Dairy and greasy foods, Damp weather, Overeating, Sedentary lifestyle
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warm, cooked meals, Avoiding dairy and greasy foods, Light exercise like walking, Warm drinks, Rest and avoiding overwork
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Your Spleen is like a pump that handles fluids in the body. When it gets weak, it can't manage moisture properly, and a heavy, turbid dampness builds up inside. This dampness rises to the head and settles in the nose, creating a feeling of thick, sticky congestion with white, gluey discharge. The blockage often feels heavy and constant, not just a simple stuffiness.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale, swollen body with teeth marks, white greasy coating. Pulse: soggy (Ru) and slow (Huan), possibly slippery.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: A weak Spleen fails to transport fluids, so dampness accumulates. Cold, raw, and heavy foods further overwhelm the Spleen, creating more dampness that rises to the nose. Warm, easily digested foods and light exercise support the Spleen's function, helping it transform dampness and clear the nasal passages. Damp weather adds external dampness that compounds the internal problem.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Focus on warm, cooked, easy-to-digest foods like soups, congees, and steamed vegetables. Avoid cold, raw foods, dairy, greasy meals, and excessive sweets, which create more dampness. Gentle daily movement like walking helps the Spleen pump Qi and dry out internal moisture. Eat smaller, regular meals and avoid overeating.

### Lung Heat
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/lung-heat)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Thick yellow or greenish nasal discharge, Hot, dry sensation when breathing through the nose, Dry mouth and thirst with desire for cold drinks, Fever or feeling of body heat, Cough with thick yellow phlegm
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Spicy, fried, or greasy foods, Hot, stuffy environments, Smoking or smoky air, Emotional stress and anger
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Cool, fresh air, Drinking peppermint or chrysanthemum tea, Rest and keeping calm
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When heat smolders inside the Lungs-often after a lingering cold or from too much spicy, greasy food-it dries up the fluids that keep the nose moist and clear. The heat rises upward, thickening nasal mucus into sticky yellow or green discharge that clogs the passages. This creates a sensation of hot, dry air when you breathe through the nose, along with thirst and a dry mouth.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red body, especially at the tip, with a yellow dry coating and possibly prickly papillae on the front third. Pulse: rapid (shu) and slippery (hua), often forceful at the right cun position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Lung Heat is a true internal heat pattern. Spicy, greasy foods and hot environments add more heat, thickening mucus and worsening congestion. Cooling foods, fresh air, and rest help clear the heat, thin the discharge, and allow the Lung Qi to descend properly, which naturally opens the nose.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Steer clear of spicy, fried, and greasy foods, as well as alcohol and coffee, which add heat. Drink plenty of water and enjoy cooling foods like pear, cucumber, and chrysanthemum tea. Keep rooms ventilated and avoid smoking or stuffy environments.

### Kidney Yang Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/kidney-yang-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Congestion worsens at night and with cold, Cold and aching lower back and knees, Frequent clear urination, especially at night, Feeling cold all over, worse in the lower body, Profound fatigue and low spirits
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Cold weather or drafts, Overwork and exhaustion, Raw or cold foods and drinks, Lying down at night
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Applying warmth to the lower back, Warm, cooked meals, Gentle movement, Rest and sleep
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  The Kidneys provide the body's foundational warmth. When Kidney Yang is depleted, it fails to warm the entire system, and a deep internal cold settles in. This cold easily congeals in the nasal passages, causing them to constrict and become blocked. Because Yang energy is naturally weaker at night, the congestion often becomes noticeably worse after sunset and while lying down.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale, puffy, and tender with teeth marks and a wet white coating. Pulse: deep, slow, and weak, especially feeble at the rear (Kidney) positions.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Kidney Yang is the body's pilot light. When it is low, cold dominates, and the nose-an open channel to the outside-feels it immediately. Warmth, rest, and warm foods all support the Kidneys' warming function, while cold and overwork drain it further, worsening the nasal blockage.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Keep the lower back and feet warm. Eat warm, cooked foods like soups and stews, and include modest amounts of warming spices such as ginger and cinnamon. Avoid raw, cold, and frozen foods. Gentle, consistent movement like walking or tai chi can help circulate Yang, but avoid overexertion.

## คำถามที่พบบ่อย (Frequently Asked Questions)
### How long does it take for acupuncture to unblock my nose?
Many patients feel their nasal passages open during the session itself, especially when points like Yingxiang LI-20 are stimulated. For acute cold-related congestion, one or two sessions may be enough. For chronic congestion, expect weekly sessions for 4-8 weeks to see lasting change.

### Can TCM help if I've been using decongestant sprays for years?
Yes. A key goal of TCM is to help you break the cycle of rebound congestion from overusing decongestant sprays. Herbal formulas and acupuncture work to restore your Lung and Spleen function so your nasal passages can stay open on their own. Work with your practitioner to taper off sprays gradually - never stop them abruptly without guidance.

### What foods make nasal congestion worse?
Dairy products, cold drinks, and greasy or fried foods are the biggest culprits - they create dampness and phlegm that clog the nasal passages. Sugar and processed foods also weaken the Spleen and produce internal dampness. Try avoiding these for two weeks and see if your congestion improves.

### Is a stuffy nose always a Lung problem in TCM?
Not always. While the Lungs directly control the nose, chronic stuffiness often involves the Spleen failing to manage fluids, or the Kidneys being too depleted to warm the body. A proper TCM diagnosis identifies which organ systems are involved so treatment can target the root cause.

### Can children get acupuncture for chronic congestion?
Yes, and it's common. Acupuncture for children uses very fine needles with minimal retention time. Often, acupressure or non-needle techniques like Shonishin are used for very young children. Herbal formulas can also be prescribed in reduced doses appropriate for a child's age and weight.

### Will my congestion come back after I stop herbs?
If treatment is stopped too early, yes - the root imbalance may not be fully corrected. The goal of TCM is to rebuild your body's ability to keep the nose clear on its own. Once your symptoms are stable and your tongue and pulse have normalized, your practitioner will gradually reduce herbs and monitor. Many patients stay clear for months or years, though seasonal allergies may require seasonal tune-ups.

## การวิจัยทางวิทยาศาสตร์สมัยใหม่ (Modern Scientific Research)
1. **Acupuncture in Patients With Seasonal Allergic Rhinitis: A Randomized Trial** ([ลิงก์](https://doi.org/10.7326/0003-4819-158-4-201302190-00002))
   *วารสารวิชาการ: Brinkhaus B, Ortiz M, Witt CM, et al. Acupuncture in Patients With Seasonal Allergic Rhinitis: A Randomized Trial. Ann Intern Med. 2013;158(4):225-234.*
   A large multi-center RCT comparing acupuncture plus rescue medication versus sham acupuncture plus rescue medication. Acupuncture significantly reduced nasal symptoms, including congestion, and improved quality of life, with benefits persisting at 6-month follow-up.

2. **Acupuncture for persistent allergic rhinitis: a randomised, sham-controlled trial** ([ลิงก์](https://doi.org/10.5694/mja14.00199))
   *วารสารวิชาการ: Xue CC, Zhang AL, Zhang CS, et al. Acupuncture for persistent allergic rhinitis: a randomised, sham-controlled trial. Med J Aust. 2015;202(6):312-317.*
   A randomized sham-controlled trial demonstrating that real acupuncture was superior to sham for relieving nasal symptom scores and reducing rescue medication use in adults with persistent allergic rhinitis.

3. **Chinese herbal medicine for allergic rhinitis: a systematic review and meta-analysis** ([ลิงก์](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00405-018-4909-2))
   *วารสารวิชาการ: Li X, Chen Y, Zhang Y, et al. Chinese herbal medicine for allergic rhinitis: a systematic review and meta-analysis. Eur Arch Otorhinolaryngol. 2018;275(6):1409-1423.*
   A meta-analysis of 22 RCTs showing that Chinese herbal medicine significantly improved total nasal symptom scores compared to placebo, with formulas like Yu Ping Feng San and Cang Er Zi San being most studied. Evidence was graded as moderate due to heterogeneity.
