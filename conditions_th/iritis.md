## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนปัจจุบัน (Conventional Medicine View)
### คำอธิบาย (Description)
Iritis is inflammation of the iris, the colored part of the eye. It is a form of anterior uveitis and typically causes eye pain, redness around the iris, sensitivity to light, and sometimes blurred vision. The pupil may become constricted or irregular. Diagnosis is made by an eye doctor using a slit-lamp examination. Iritis can occur on its own, often without a known cause, or it may be linked to autoimmune conditions, infections, or injury.

### การรักษาหลัก (Treatments)
Standard treatment aims to reduce inflammation and prevent complications. Corticosteroid eye drops are the mainstay, often combined with dilating drops to prevent the iris from sticking to the lens (synechiae). In more severe or recurrent cases, oral steroids or systemic immunosuppressive drugs may be prescribed.

## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนจีน (Traditional Chinese Medicine View)
### ความเข้าใจเกี่ยวกับการแพทย์แผนจีน (TCM Understanding)
In TCM, the eyes are closely connected to the Liver. The Liver channel opens into the eyes, and the Liver is responsible for the smooth flow of Qi and Blood throughout the body. When this flow is disrupted - by an external invasion of Wind and Heat, or by internal emotional stress that stirs up Liver Fire - the pathogenic heat rushes upward along the channel and inflames the delicate tissues of the eye. This is why iritis often involves redness, pain, and a constricted pupil: the Heat contracts the iris and disturbs the clear fluids.
A sudden onset of iritis after exposure to wind or a change in weather, accompanied by a gritty sensation, tearing, and a floating rapid pulse, points to a Wind-Heat pattern. Here the external pathogen has entered the body and lodged in the Liver channel, and the treatment principle is to dispel Wind and clear Heat from the surface. The herbs are light and dispersing, designed to push the pathogen back out.
When the eye pain is intense and throbbing, the pupil is tightly constricted, and the person feels irritable with a bitter taste in the mouth, the pattern is Liver Fire Blazing. This internal Fire is often triggered by anger, frustration, or prolonged stress. The tongue is red with a yellow coating, and the pulse is wiry and rapid. Treatment must clear the Liver Fire and drain the heat downward, using bitter and cold herbs that are much stronger than those used for Wind-Heat.
A less common but stubborn pattern is Damp-Heat in the Liver channel. Here the inflammation feels heavy and sticky, the vision is blurred, and there may be a dull ache around the brow. The tongue has a greasy yellow coating. This pattern often arises from a diet rich in greasy, spicy, or sweet foods that create internal Dampness and Heat. The treatment must not only clear Heat but also dry the Dampness and drain it out through urination, which takes more time.

### การวินิจฉัยโดยแพทย์แผนจีน (Practitioner Diagnosis)
A TCM practitioner begins by asking how the eye discomfort started and what it feels like. The speed of onset, the quality of the pain, and the appearance of the eye are the first big clues. A sudden, dramatic flare-up points toward an external invasion, while a more intense, angry kind of pain suggests an internal fire blazing upward. These early impressions guide the practitioner toward one pattern over another.
If the iritis came on suddenly after exposure to wind or a change in weather, and there is a gritty, painful sensation with lots of tearing and sensitivity to light, the Wind-Heat pattern is most likely. The eye looks red around the iris, but the redness is not as deep and angry as in a fire pattern. The tongue may have a thin white or slightly yellow coating, and the pulse feels floating and rapid, like a breeze moving quickly across the surface of the skin.
When the eye pain is severe and throbbing, the pupil is tightly constricted, and the whole eye looks deeply inflamed, the Liver Fire Blazing pattern is a strong candidate. People with this pattern often feel irritable, have a bitter taste in the mouth, and may be thirsty. The tongue body is red with a yellow coating, and the pulse is wiry and rapid - it feels tight and forceful, like a guitar string vibrating under the finger.
The Damp-Heat in the Liver Channel pattern tends to produce a dull, heavy ache around the brow and temple, not just the sharp pain of pure heat. The pupil may be constricted or even irregularly shaped, and vision can be blurry or hazy. A key sign is a greasy, yellow coating on the tongue, which signals the presence of dampness mixed with heat. The pulse may feel soft yet rapid, or wiry, reflecting the combination of obstruction and heat.

## กลุ่มอาการการแพทย์แผนจีนและการรักษา (TCM Patterns and Treatment)

### Wind-Heat
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/wind-heat)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Sudden onset of eye pain and redness, Distending pain around the eye, Photophobia and tearing, Constricted pupil (&#x77B3;&#x795E;&#x7D27;&#x5C0F;), Mild fever or aversion to wind, Floating, rapid pulse
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Windy weather, Spicy or fried food, Bright light, Alcohol, Emotional stress
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Cool compresses, Resting in a dim room, Chrysanthemum tea, Avoiding wind
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Iritis often begins when an external Wind-Heat pathogen attacks the body and travels along the Liver channel, which opens into the eyes. The Heat rushes upward, inflaming the eye's delicate tissues and causing the pupil to contract painfully. This is why the onset is typically sudden and may follow exposure to wind or a mild cold.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red tip and edges with a thin white or thin yellow coating. Pulse: floating (fú) and rapid (shuò).
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: <p>Wind-Heat is an external pathogen that enters through the skin and nose, easily stirred up by wind and heat. Windy conditions and spicy foods feed that Heat, pushing it upward to the eyes. Cooling compresses and gentle herbal teas help disperse the pathogen and calm the inflammation. Rest and dim light give the eyes a chance to recover without further irritation.</p>
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Avoid spicy, fried, or greasy foods that can generate internal Heat. Protect your eyes from wind, bright light, and screen glare. Cool (not icy) compresses can soothe acute redness. Drink chrysanthemum or mint tea to gently clear Wind-Heat.

### Liver Fire Blazing
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/liver-fire-blazing)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Severe, throbbing eye pain that worsens with pressure, Eye redness concentrated around the iris (ciliary flush), Bitter taste in the mouth, Intense irritability and short temper, Dry mouth and throat, dark urine, constipation
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Anger, frustration, or emotional stress, Spicy, fried, or greasy foods, Alcohol and coffee, Prolonged screen time and eye strain, Hot weather or stuffy environments
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Cool compresses over closed eyes, Rest in a dark, quiet room, Sipping chrysanthemum or mint tea, Calming the mind and avoiding arguments
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When the Liver is overwhelmed by intense Fire, that blaze surges upward along the Liver channel, which opens into the eyes. This heat scorches the iris, causing severe inflammation, pain, and a constricted pupil. The same Fire disturbs the mind, producing the irritability and restless agitation that often accompany the eye symptoms.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: Red body with redder sides, dry yellow coating, possible red prickles. Pulse: Wiry (弦 xián), rapid (数 shuò), and forceful, especially at the left middle position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Liver Fire is a pattern of excess Heat that surges upward. Emotional stress and anger act like wind on a flame, directly feeding the Fire, while spicy, greasy foods, and alcohol add more Heat to the system. Cooling the eyes and the body, resting in darkness, and choosing cooling foods and drinks help to drain the excess Fire and protect the delicate eye tissues. Calming the mind prevents the emotional sparks that fan the flames.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat cooling, light foods - cucumber, pear, watermelon, and leafy greens. Sip chrysanthemum tea daily. Strictly avoid alcohol, coffee, spicy dishes, fried foods, and late-night snacking. Protect the eyes from bright light with sunglasses and take frequent screen breaks. Practice a short daily meditation or slow breathing to keep anger in check.

### Damp-Heat in the Liver Channel
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/damp-heat-in-the-liver-channel)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Constricted or irregularly shaped pupil, Heavy brow ache or temporal pain, Blurry vision with a sensation of fog, Bitter taste in the mouth
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Greasy or spicy food, Damp weather, Alcohol, Emotional stress
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Cool, dry environment, Light, bland foods, Resting the eyes
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When external Wind-Dampness and Heat invade the Liver channel, or when a diet rich in greasy, spicy foods creates internal Damp-Heat, the clear flow of Qi and Blood to the eyes becomes obstructed. This turbid, sticky Heat settles in the iris, causing the pupil to constrict or become irregular, with a heavy, dragging ache around the brow and blurry vision.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red body with red sides and a yellow greasy coating, thicker at the center and root. Pulse: wiry, slippery, and rapid.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Damp-Heat in the Liver channel is aggravated by anything that adds more Dampness or Heat to the body-greasy foods, alcohol, and humid weather all contribute. A cool, dry environment and bland foods help drain Dampness and clear Heat, giving the eyes a chance to recover. Emotional stress stirs up Liver Qi, which can transform into Heat, worsening the condition.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Avoid greasy, fried, and spicy foods, as well as alcohol and coffee, which generate Dampness and Heat. Focus on light, easily digested meals like congee, steamed vegetables, and barley water. Keep the eyes rested and avoid straining in bright light.

## คำถามที่พบบ่อย (Frequently Asked Questions)
### Can TCM treat iritis naturally without steroid eye drops?
In many acute cases, TCM can manage the inflammation effectively with herbs and acupuncture alone, especially when started early. However, if your ophthalmologist has prescribed steroid drops to prevent complications like synechiae, it is often safest to use them as directed while beginning TCM treatment. The goal of TCM is to address the root imbalance so that you can eventually reduce or eliminate the need for steroids, but this should be done gradually and under medical supervision.

### How quickly does acupuncture work for eye inflammation?
Many patients notice a reduction in pain and light sensitivity after the first one or two acupuncture sessions. The full anti-inflammatory effect builds over a series of treatments, typically one to two times per week. For acute Wind-Heat iritis, relief can come within days; deeper patterns like Liver Fire or Damp-Heat may take a couple of weeks before the eye feels significantly calmer.

### Is it safe to take Chinese herbs along with my eye drops?
Yes, herbal formulas are generally safe to use alongside corticosteroid eye drops. There are no known direct interactions between the herbs commonly used for iritis and topical eye medications. Always inform both your TCM practitioner and your ophthalmologist about everything you are taking. Do not stop steroid drops abruptly, as this can cause a rebound flare-up.

### Will changing my diet help prevent future iritis attacks?
Diet plays a supporting role in TCM treatment. Reducing greasy, fried, spicy, and heavily sweet foods can help prevent the buildup of Damp-Heat and Liver Fire that trigger flare-ups. Adding cooling foods like chrysanthemum tea, cucumber, and celery can gently clear heat from the body. While diet alone may not stop an acute attack, it makes a meaningful difference in long-term prevention when combined with herbs and acupuncture.

### Can stress really cause iritis?
In TCM, stress - especially anger, frustration, or long-held resentment - is a major trigger for Liver Fire Blazing, which directly affects the eyes. Many patients notice that their iritis flares up during or after a period of intense emotional strain. TCM treatment not only clears the heat from the eyes but also helps smooth the Liver Qi flow, making you less reactive to stress over time.

### What should I do if my eye suddenly gets worse during treatment?
A mild temporary increase in redness or discomfort can sometimes occur as the body expels pathogens, but any sudden, severe worsening - especially vision loss, intense pain, or an irregular pupil - is a red flag. In that case, stop your herbs and seek urgent ophthalmologic care immediately. Always keep your TCM practitioner informed of any changes.
