## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนปัจจุบัน (Conventional Medicine View)
### คำอธิบาย (Description)
Acute heart failure is a sudden, life-threatening condition where the heart cannot pump enough blood to meet the body’s needs. It often develops over hours or days and causes severe breathlessness, fluid buildup in the lungs and body, extreme fatigue, and sometimes confusion. It’s usually triggered by a heart attack, uncontrolled high blood pressure, an infection, or a sudden worsening of chronic heart failure.
Diagnosis is made through physical exam, blood tests (like BNP), chest X-ray, and echocardiogram. Treatment in the hospital focuses on stabilizing the patient with oxygen, diuretics to remove excess fluid, and medications to support heart function.

### การรักษาหลัก (Treatments)
Standard emergency treatment includes oxygen therapy, intravenous diuretics (such as furosemide) to reduce fluid overload, vasodilators (like nitroglycerin) to ease the heart’s workload, and sometimes inotropic drugs (like dobutamine) to strengthen heart contractions. Once stable, patients are transitioned to long-term medications including ACE inhibitors, beta-blockers, and aldosterone antagonists to prevent recurrence.

## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนจีน (Traditional Chinese Medicine View)
### ความเข้าใจเกี่ยวกับการแพทย์แผนจีน (TCM Understanding)
In TCM, acute heart failure is not a single disease - it’s a crisis that reveals a long-standing imbalance in the body’s vital forces. The heart relies on Yang (warming, driving energy) to pump blood, Qi to move it, and Yin to nourish the organ. When these reserves are depleted by chronic illness, stress, or aging, the heart can no longer circulate blood properly. This leads to a buildup of fluid, blood stasis, or phlegm that suddenly overwhelms the system.
The Kidneys and Spleen play a central role. The Kidneys store the body’s deepest Yang - the pilot light that warms and transforms fluids. When Kidney Yang weakens, water cannot be processed and it floods upward, causing swelling and breathlessness. The Spleen transforms food into Qi and manages fluid metabolism; if it’s weak, it produces phlegm that clogs the Lungs and obstructs breathing.
Because the root deficiency can be Qi, Yang, or Yin - and the branch excess can be blood stasis, phlegm, or water - the same Western diagnosis of acute heart failure can look very different from person to person. Some patients are cold, pale, and waterlogged; others are exhausted, dry, and restless. That’s why TCM identifies six distinct patterns, each with its own treatment strategy.

### การวินิจฉัยโดยแพทย์แผนจีน (Practitioner Diagnosis)
A practitioner first gauges the balance between fatigue and coldness. If exhaustion and chest oppression dominate, with a purplish tongue showing stasis spots, that suggests Qi Deficiency causing Blood Stagnation.
When the same tiredness is joined by dry mouth, night sweats, and a red tongue with a scanty coat, it shifts the picture toward Qi and Yin Deficiency, where both vital energy and cooling Yin fluids are depleted.
Coldness is the next big clue. Heart Yang Deficiency brings cold limbs, aversion to chill, and a pale, swollen tongue, reflecting the heart’s failing warmth.
When that coldness is accompanied by severe edema, scanty urine, and breathlessness, the root has moved deeper to Kidney Yang Deficiency with Water overflowing, because the kidneys can no longer transform fluids, letting water flood upward.
If the person struggles to lie flat and coughs up plenty of thin or frothy sputum, Phlegm-Fluids in the Lungs is likely. The tongue coat is greasy and the pulse feels slippery, distinguishing it from the water pattern, where systemic swelling is more prominent than respiratory congestion.
Finally, Collapse of Yang is unmistakable: profuse cold sweat, extreme cold limbs, and a pulse that is minute or barely palpable. This is not a pattern to differentiate by subtle questions - it signals a critical emergency where Yang is about to extinguish, and it demands immediate intervention.

## กลุ่มอาการการแพทย์แผนจีนและการรักษา (TCM Patterns and Treatment)

### Qi Deficiency causing Blood Stagnation
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/qi-deficiency-causing-blood-stagnation)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Persistent, deep fatigue and weakness, Fixed, stabbing chest pain worse with pressure, Shortness of breath on minimal exertion, Dusky or greyish-purple complexion, Spontaneous sweating with mild activity, Numbness or tingling in the limbs
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Overexertion or physical exertion, Cold, raw foods and iced drinks, Emotional stress and worry, Cold, damp weather or environments, Prolonged standing
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Gentle movement (walking, Tai Chi), Warm food and drinks, Rest and lying down, Warmth application (blankets, warm drinks), Deep, slow breathing
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Qi is the vital force that pushes blood through the vessels. When Qi becomes weak, it loses its ability to move blood briskly, and the circulation slows down, leading to stagnation. In the heart, this means the blood pools and congeals, creating a dull, fixed pain in the chest and a feeling of heaviness. The lack of fresh, oxygenated blood reaching the face and limbs causes a dusky complexion and constant fatigue, while the heart struggles to pump against the stagnant blood, causing palpitations and breathlessness on the slightest exertion.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale-dark or pale-purple body with scattered stasis spots, teeth marks, and distended sublingual veins; thin white coat. Pulse: deep (Chen), fine or weak (Xi/Ruo), and choppy (Se).
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: This pattern is rooted in a deficiency of Qi, which is the body's vital energy for movement and warmth. Overexertion, stress, and cold all consume Qi further, making the blood even more stagnant and worsening chest pain and fatigue. Rest, warmth, and gentle movement conserve and gently stimulate Qi, helping it push blood through the vessels. Nourishing, warm foods directly supply the building blocks for Qi and blood, while deep breathing helps move Qi in the chest, relieving the stagnation that causes the fixed pain.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat small, frequent meals of warm, easily digestible foods like congee with red dates, goji berries, and a little ginger. Avoid raw, cold, and greasy foods that tax the digestive Qi. Gentle daily movement such as slow walking or Tai Chi helps Qi and blood flow without depleting you. Rest after meals and prioritize sleep to rebuild Qi reserves.

### Qi and Yin Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/qi-and-yin-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Persistent fatigue and lack of strength, Shortness of breath worsened by exertion, Dry mouth with little desire to drink, Night sweats, Warm sensation in palms, soles, and chest, Palpitations
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Overexertion or physical exertion, Emotional stress and worry, Spicy, fried, or drying foods, Hot and dry weather, Insufficient sleep
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Rest and lying down, Cool, quiet environment, Moistening foods like pears and congee, Gentle breathing exercises
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  In acute heart failure, the heart relies on Qi to pump blood and on Yin to nourish and cool the organ. When Qi is weakened, the heart lacks the drive to circulate blood properly, leading to fatigue, shortness of breath, and palpitations. At the same time, depleted Yin fluids fail to moisten and anchor the body, causing dry mouth, night sweats, and a warm sensation in the palms and soles. This dual depletion leaves the heart muscle underpowered and overheated, making it vulnerable to acute decompensation.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red body with a thin or absent coating, often dry and possibly cracked. Pulse: fine (thin) and weak, frequently rapid.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Overexertion and lack of sleep directly drain Qi, while stress and worry consume both Qi and Yin by generating internal heat. Spicy or fried foods add heat that further dries up Yin fluids. Rest, moistening foods, and a cool environment do the opposite: they conserve Qi, replenish Yin, and calm the mild deficiency heat, giving the heart the quiet, nourished state it needs to recover.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Prioritize rest and avoid overexertion; even mild activity should be paced. Eat small, frequent meals of easily digestible, moistening foods such as rice congee, pears, and steamed vegetables. Limit spicy, fried, and drying foods that consume Yin. Keep the living space cool and calm, and wind down gently before bed to protect night Yin.

### Heart Yang Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/heart-yang-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Cold hands and feet, Bright pale face, Palpitations worse with exertion, Chest stuffiness and shortness of breath on activity, Aversion to cold
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Cold exposure (weather, drafts), Overexertion or physical exertion, Cold, raw foods and iced drinks
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warmth application (blankets, warm drinks), Rest and lying down, Warm food and drinks
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Heart Yang is the warming, driving force that pushes blood through the vessels. When it weakens, the heart cannot pump with enough strength, so blood and Qi slow down and cold sets in. This leaves the chest feeling stuffy and breathless, while the body's extremities become cold and pale because warm blood is not reaching them.
In acute heart failure, this lack of warm propulsion also allows fluids to accumulate, making breathing even harder. The heart struggles against the cold, leading to palpitations that worsen with any exertion.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale, puffy, and tender with teeth marks, and a white slippery coating. Pulse: deep and weak, especially at the left cun position; may be knotted.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Heart Yang is the body's internal fire. Cold extinguishes that fire, further weakening the heart's pumping action, so warmth is essential. Exertion demands more Qi and blood than a deficient heart can supply, making symptoms worse, while rest conserves the little Yang that remains.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat warm, cooked foods like soups and stews, and use warming spices such as ginger and cinnamon. Keep the body warm, especially the chest and feet, and avoid cold drinks and raw foods. Gentle movement like slow walking is fine, but stop before you feel breathless.

### Kidney Yang Deficiency with Water overflowing
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/kidney-yang-deficiency-with-water-overflowing)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Swelling of legs and ankles, Deep cold feeling in the lower back and legs, Scanty or difficult urination, Breathlessness on exertion, Abdominal bloating and loose stools, Cold hands and feet
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Cold, raw foods and iced drinks, Overexertion or physical exertion, Cold, damp weather or environments, Excessive salt
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warmth application (blankets, warm drinks), Warm food and drinks, Gentle movement (walking, Tai Chi), Moxibustion (heat therapy)
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Your Kidney Yang is the body's pilot light for warming and transforming fluids. When it weakens, the body cannot properly process and eliminate water, so fluid builds up - first in the lower body as swelling, then rising to press on the heart and lungs. That is why you feel breathless, your heart pounds, and your urine becomes scanty.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale, puffy, teeth-marked body with a white slippery coating; surface looks excessively wet. Pulse: deep (Chen), slow (Chi), and weak (Ruo), especially faint at the Chi (proximal) position.

### Phlegm-Fluids in the Lungs
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/phlegm-fluids-in-the-lungs)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Cough with copious thin, frothy white sputum, Must sit upright to breathe (orthopnea), Chest stuffiness that eases after expectoration, Heavy, bloated sensation in the body, Nausea and poor appetite, White, thick, greasy tongue coating
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Lying flat, Cold, raw or dairy foods, Damp or humid weather, Overexertion or physical exertion, Cold exposure (weather, drafts)
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Sitting upright or leaning forward, Coughing up phlegm, Warm, dry environment, Warm food and drinks
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When Spleen Yang is weak, it fails to transform fluids, which then accumulate into phlegm. This phlegm rises to clog the Lungs, blocking the descent of Qi and trapping fluid in the chest. The obstructed Lungs can no longer help the Heart move blood smoothly, so breathlessness, orthopnea, and palpitations worsen rapidly.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale, swollen with teeth marks, thick white greasy or slippery coating. Pulse: slippery (Hua), strongest at the right Cun position; may become wiry (Xian) if cold-fluid retention is severe.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Phlegm-fluids are a Yin pathogen that worsens with cold and dampness, so warmth and dryness help resolve them. Lying flat allows fluids to pool in the chest, making breathlessness much worse, while sitting upright uses gravity to help drain them downward. Expectoration directly removes the obstructing phlegm, temporarily opening the airways and easing chest pressure.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Avoid cold, raw, and dairy foods that increase phlegm. Eat warm, cooked meals like congee with a little fresh ginger. Keep the chest and back warm, and avoid damp or humid environments. Gentle diaphragmatic breathing can help move phlegm and ease breathlessness.

### Collapse of Yang
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/collapse-of-yang)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Profuse cold sweating that does not stop, Ice-cold hands and feet extending past elbows and knees, Faint or impalpable pulse, Ashen-white or greyish complexion, Extreme desire to curl up and severe aversion to cold, Mental apathy or loss of consciousness
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Cold exposure (weather, drafts), Overexertion or physical exertion, Emotional shock or fear, Cold, raw foods and iced drinks, Sudden movements
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warmth application (blankets, warm drinks), Rest and lying down, Warm food and drinks, Moxibustion (heat therapy), Calm environment
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  In acute heart failure, the heart's Yang is the engine that pumps blood and warms the body. When Yang collapses, that engine fails completely - the heart can no longer circulate blood, limbs turn ice-cold, and the pulse becomes barely perceptible.
This is the deepest stage of deficiency, where the body's vital fire is nearly extinguished. The profound weakness, cold sweat, and breathlessness of end-stage heart failure all stem from this near-total loss of warming and activating Yang Qi.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale, moist, and flaccid with a thin white slippery coating. Pulse: minute (微 wēi), extremely faint and on the verge of disappearing, sometimes impalpable.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Yang is the body's warming and activating force. Cold further depletes it, while warmth preserves the little that remains. Rest conserves Qi, and moxibustion directly adds Yang to the core. Any shock or exertion scatters the fragile Yang, accelerating collapse.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: This is a critical emergency requiring immediate hospital care. While awaiting help, keep the person warm with blankets, offer warm fluids if conscious, and avoid cold or raw foods. Do not move them unnecessarily.

## คำถามที่พบบ่อย (Frequently Asked Questions)
### Can TCM treat acute heart failure on its own?
No. Acute heart failure is a medical emergency that requires immediate hospital care. TCM is used alongside conventional treatment - never as a replacement - to support recovery, manage symptoms, and prevent future episodes once the patient is stable.

### Is acupuncture safe for someone with heart failure?
Yes, when performed by a qualified practitioner. Acupuncture points are chosen to calm the spirit, strengthen Qi, and reduce fluid retention. It’s gentle and generally well tolerated. Always inform both your cardiologist and acupuncturist about your full health picture.

### How does TCM help with fluid retention and swelling?
TCM sees edema as a failure of the Kidneys and Spleen to transform and transport fluids. Herbs like Fu Ling and Bai Zhu strengthen the Spleen and drain dampness, while warming herbs like Zhi Fu Zi restore Kidney Yang to move water. Acupuncture points such as Zusanli ST-36 and Guanyuan REN-4 support this process.

### Will I need to take herbs forever?
Not necessarily. In the acute recovery phase, herbs are taken daily to stabilize the condition. As your strength returns, the formula may be adjusted and eventually tapered to a maintenance dose or stopped. Some patients with chronic deficiency may benefit from long-term, low-dose herbal support.

### Can I combine TCM with my heart medications?
Yes, but always with professional guidance. Some herbs can interact with blood thinners or affect blood pressure. Never stop or change your prescribed medications without your doctor’s approval. Bring a list of all your medications to your TCM consultation so your practitioner can ensure safety.

### What dietary changes can support my heart?
Focus on warm, easy-to-digest foods like congee, soups, and stews. Use mild warming spices such as ginger and cinnamon. Avoid raw, cold, and greasy foods, and limit salt and fluid intake if you have swelling. Small, frequent meals are easier on your heart and lungs than large ones.

### How quickly will I feel better with TCM?
Many people notice less breathlessness and fatigue within 2-4 weeks of starting herbs and acupuncture. However, rebuilding deep Qi and Yang reserves takes longer - often 3-6 months or more. Progress depends on the pattern and how long the weakness has been building.

## การวิจัยทางวิทยาศาสตร์สมัยใหม่ (Modern Scientific Research)
1. **Shenfu injection for acute heart failure: A systematic review and meta-analysis of randomized controlled trials**
   *วารสารวิชาการ: Wang L, et al. J Ethnopharmacol. 2018; 225: 18-26.*
   This meta-analysis pooled data from multiple RCTs comparing Shenfu injection plus standard therapy to standard therapy alone in patients with acute heart failure. The addition of Shenfu injection was associated with a significant improvement in left ventricular ejection fraction and a reduction in BNP levels, with no increase in serious adverse events. However, the authors noted a high risk of bias in most included studies.

2. **Effect of Shengmai injection on hemodynamics and clinical outcomes in acute decompensated heart failure: a randomized controlled trial**
   *วารสารวิชาการ: Zhang Y, et al. Chin J Integr Med. 2020; 26(8): 567-573.*
   In this single-center RCT, 120 patients with acute decompensated heart failure were assigned to receive either standard treatment or standard treatment plus Shengmai injection. The Shengmai group showed faster resolution of dyspnea and a trend toward lower 30-day readmission rates, though the difference was not statistically significant. The study was underpowered to detect mortality differences.
