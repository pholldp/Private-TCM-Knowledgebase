## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนปัจจุบัน (Conventional Medicine View)
### คำอธิบาย (Description)
In Western medicine, upper abdominal distension is most often linked to functional dyspepsia, gastritis, delayed gastric emptying (gastroparesis), or irritable bowel syndrome. It may also arise from food intolerances, small intestinal bacterial overgrowth (SIBO), or gallbladder dysfunction. Diagnosis typically involves a symptom history, dietary review, and sometimes endoscopy or breath testing to rule out structural causes or infection.

### การรักษาหลัก (Treatments)
Standard treatment often includes antacids, proton-pump inhibitors (PPIs) to reduce stomach acid, or prokinetic agents to speed gastric emptying. Dietary modifications - such as smaller meals, reducing fatty foods, and avoiding carbonated drinks - are commonly recommended. Stress management and, in some cases, low-dose antidepressants are used for functional dyspepsia. While these approaches can provide temporary relief, they don't always address the deeper, recurring nature of the bloating.

## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนจีน (Traditional Chinese Medicine View)
### ความเข้าใจเกี่ยวกับการแพทย์แผนจีน (TCM Understanding)
TCM understands upper abdominal distension primarily through the Stomach and Spleen, the two organs at the center of digestion. The Stomach is supposed to send food downward; the Spleen transforms it into usable energy and sends clear Qi upward. When this rhythmic rise and fall is disrupted - by overeating, emotional stress, or constitutional weakness - Qi gets stuck in the middle burner, creating that telltale sensation of fullness and pressure.
The Liver plays a crucial but often overlooked role. It's responsible for the smooth flow of Qi throughout the body. When stress, frustration, or anger cause the Liver Qi to stagnate, it frequently invades the Stomach, disrupting its downward movement and causing Qi to accumulate in the upper abdomen. This is why emotional upset so often leads to bloating and indigestion.

### การวินิจฉัยโดยแพทย์แผนจีน (Practitioner Diagnosis)
A practitioner begins by asking when the distension started and what makes it better or worse - after a heavy meal, during a stressful week, or with no obvious trigger. The character of the feeling (sharp, burning, dull, heavy) and the appearance of the tongue and pulse quickly narrow the possibilities.
If the bloating appeared suddenly after overeating or indulging in rich, greasy foods, and the belly feels tight and refuses pressure, Food Stagnation in the Stomach is the likely culprit. The tongue coating will be thick and greasy, and the pulse often feels slippery, confirming that undigested food is sitting in the digestive tract.
When distension spreads into the sides of the ribcage and flares up during times of frustration or worry, the root is Liver Qi Stagnation invading the Stomach. The tongue may look normal or slightly red with a thin white coat, while the pulse takes on a wiry, tense quality that reflects the emotional strain.
A person who feels a chronic, mild fullness that eases with gentle pressure or a warm compress, and who struggles with low appetite and fatigue, is likely dealing with Spleen and Stomach Qi Deficiency. The tongue appears pale and puffy with tooth marks on the edges, and the pulse is weak and thin - signs of a digestive engine that lacks the power to move food.
Damp-Heat in the Stomach and Spleen creates a heavy, burning distension that feels uncomfortable rather than sharply painful. The tongue is red with a sticky yellow coating, and the pulse is slippery and rapid. You may notice a bitter taste in the mouth, thirst, and dark, scanty urine, all pointing to heat and moisture trapped in the middle burner.
Spleen Deficiency with Dampness produces a persistent, soggy bloating that lingers long after meals. The tongue is pale and swollen with a greasy coat, and the pulse feels weak and deep. Stools are loose and sticky, revealing a Spleen too weak to manage fluids, so dampness accumulates and weighs down the abdomen.
Cold-Damp invading the Spleen causes distension with a distinct cold, cramping pain that improves with warmth and worsens after cold drinks or exposure to damp weather. The tongue is pale with a white, slippery coating, and the pulse is slow or deep and slippery - a picture of the digestive fire being smothered by cold and moisture.

### Food Stagnation in the Stomach
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/food-stagnation-in-the-stomach)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Sour, rotten-smelling belching and acid regurgitation, Aversion to food and its smell, with foul flatulence, Thick greasy or curd-like tongue coating, thickest in the centre, Distension and pain that worsen after eating and with pressure, Pain and bloating relieved after vomiting
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Overeating or heavy meals, Eating late at night, Lying down immediately after eating, Cold, raw foods and drinks, Rushed, stressful meals
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Fasting or skipping a meal, Gentle walking or exercise, Drinking warm water or light congee, Vomiting (when nausea is strong), Clockwise abdominal massage
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When you overeat or consume heavy, greasy, or hard-to-digest foods, the Stomach becomes overwhelmed. Food sits and ferments instead of moving downward, creating a physical blockage that traps Qi. The trapped Qi pushes upward and outward, producing the sensation of bloating, fullness, and pressure in the upper abdomen.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: normal body with a thick, greasy or curd-like coating, thickest in the centre. Pulse: slippery (滑 Huá) and full, especially forceful at the right guan (Stomach) position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Overeating or eating greasy, heavy foods directly overwhelms the Stomach’s capacity to transform and transport, leading to physical obstruction and Qi stagnation. Lying down after eating prevents the natural downward movement of Stomach Qi, worsening the distension. Fasting or eating light meals gives the Stomach a break, while gentle walking and abdominal massage encourage Qi to descend and food to move along. Vomiting is the body’s emergency way to expel the stagnation, providing immediate relief.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat smaller, lighter meals and avoid greasy, fried, or raw foods. Chew thoroughly and stop eating before you feel full. A short walk after meals helps Qi descend. Avoid lying down immediately after eating.

### Liver Qi Stagnation invading the Stomach
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/liver-qi-stagnation-invading-the-stomach)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Distension that radiates to the rib area, Worse with emotional stress or frustration, Frequent sighing and belching, Irritability or moodiness
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Emotional stress, Eating when upset, Greasy, fried, or sweet foods, Irregular meal times, Alcohol
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Relaxation and stress reduction, Gentle walking or exercise, Belching or passing gas, Warmth on the abdomen, Deep breathing
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  The Liver is responsible for the smooth flow of Qi throughout the body. When emotional stress, frustration, or anger build up, the Liver Qi becomes stuck. This stagnant energy then invades the Stomach, disrupting its natural downward movement. As a result, Qi accumulates in the upper abdomen, causing distension and bloating that often spreads to the rib area.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: normal or slightly red body with redder sides, thin white coat. Pulse: wiry (弦 xián), like a taut guitar string.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Emotional stress directly causes Liver Qi to stagnate, which then attacks the Stomach, so calming the mind and moving Qi are essential. Gentle exercise and deep breathing encourage Qi to flow smoothly, relieving the pressure. Belching releases trapped Qi from the Stomach. Warmth helps relax the Liver and move stagnant Qi. Heavy, greasy foods and alcohol burden the Stomach and worsen the stagnation, so they are best avoided.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat smaller, regular meals and avoid skipping meals or eating when upset. Reduce greasy, fried, and spicy foods that burden the Stomach. Gentle daily movement like walking or stretching helps keep Qi flowing. Practice stress management techniques such as deep breathing or meditation.

### Spleen and Stomach Qi Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/spleen-and-stomach-qi-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Mild, chronic bloating that worsens after eating, Bloating feels better with gentle pressure and warmth, Poor appetite and loose, unformed stools, Fatigue, weak limbs, and a sallow complexion
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Overeating or heavy meals, Cold, raw foods and drinks, Overwork and fatigue, Emotional stress, Skipping meals or irregular eating
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Rest, Warmth on the abdomen, Warm, cooked meals, Gentle pressure or massage on abdomen, Gentle walking or exercise
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  The Spleen is meant to transform food into Qi and send it upward. When Spleen Qi is weak, this engine runs sluggishly. Food and fluids sit in the stomach instead of being processed, creating a mild, chronic bloated feeling. The distension worsens after eating because the already tired digestive system cannot handle the added load, and it eases with rest and warmth, which help conserve and support the little Qi that remains.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale, puffy, with tooth marks and a thin white coating. Pulse: weak, soft, and empty at the right Guan (middle) position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: A weak Spleen needs warmth and gentle support to function. Overeating or eating cold, heavy foods overwhelms it, causing food to stagnate and bloat. Fatigue and emotional stress consume Qi further. Warmth and gentle pressure provide external support and stimulate Qi movement, while rest conserves the limited Qi. Small, frequent meals reduce the digestive burden, allowing the Spleen to cope without becoming overloaded.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat small, frequent meals of warm, cooked, easily digestible foods like soups, congees, and stewed vegetables. Avoid raw, cold, greasy, or heavy foods that strain the Spleen. Gentle walking after meals helps Qi move. Prioritise rest and avoid overwork, as fatigue directly depletes Spleen Qi.

### Damp-Heat in Stomach and Spleen
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/damp-heat-in-stomach-and-spleen)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Heavy, burning bloating in the upper abdomen, Bitter, sticky taste in the mouth, Sticky, incomplete bowel movements, Heavy feeling in the body and limbs, Thirst with little desire to drink
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Greasy, fried, or sweet foods, Alcohol, Hot, humid weather, Overeating or heavy meals, Sedentary lifestyle
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Light, easily digestible foods, Bitter vegetables (dandelion, endive), Cool, dry weather, Gentle walking or exercise, Barley or mung bean soup
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  The Spleen and Stomach are the organs of digestion. When heavy, rich foods or hot, humid environments overwhelm them, Dampness and Heat accumulate in the middle burner. This sticky, hot mixture blocks the normal flow of Qi, causing the upper abdomen to feel bloated, heavy, and often burning. The distension is persistent and accompanied by a sticky taste, poor appetite, and a sensation of incomplete bowel movements.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red body, possibly swollen with teeth marks, thick yellow greasy coating especially at center and root. Pulse: soggy (Ru) and rapid (Shu), often slippery (Hua), most pronounced at the right Guan position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Greasy, spicy, and sweet foods, along with alcohol, directly create Dampness and Heat in the body, overwhelming the Spleen and Stomach. Hot, humid environments add external Dampness, compounding the internal accumulation. Light, bland meals and bitter vegetables help clear Heat and drain Dampness, while gentle exercise stimulates Qi flow to break up the stagnation. Barley and mung beans are traditional foods that leach out Dampness and cool the system.

### Spleen Deficiency with Dampness
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/spleen-deficiency-with-dampness)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Heavy, persistent bloating that worsens after eating, Loose, sticky or unformed stools, Heaviness in the limbs and body, as if weighed down, Poor appetite with a sticky or greasy taste in the mouth, Fatigue and drowsiness, especially after meals
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Cold, raw foods and drinks, Greasy, fried, or sweet foods, Damp, humid weather, Overwork and fatigue, Skipping meals or irregular eating
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warm, cooked meals, Warmth on the abdomen, Rest, Light, easily digestible foods, Gentle walking or exercise
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  The Spleen is responsible for transforming food into energy and managing fluids. When it becomes weak, it can no longer handle fluids properly, and dampness starts to accumulate like a heavy fog in the middle of the body. This dampness blocks the normal movement of Qi, causing a persistent, heavy upper abdominal bloating that worsens after eating because the weak Spleen struggles even more to process food.
The bloating is not just gas; it's a sensation of heaviness and fullness, often accompanied by loose, sticky stools because the Spleen fails to separate clear from turbid.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale and swollen with teeth marks on the edges, white greasy coating, moist surface. Pulse: soggy (Ru) and slow (Huan), soft and lacking force, especially at the right Guan position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Raw, cold, and greasy foods damage the Spleen's Yang energy, making it even harder to transform fluids, which directly increases dampness and bloating. Damp weather adds external dampness to an already waterlogged system. Overwork depletes Qi, leaving the Spleen with less power to do its job. In contrast, warm, cooked meals and gentle warmth support the Spleen's function and help it burn off dampness. Light movement stimulates Qi flow to break up stagnation, while rest conserves the energy needed for digestion.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat small, frequent meals of warm, cooked foods like soups, stews, and congees. Avoid raw, cold, greasy, and sweet foods, as well as dairy, which all increase dampness. Sip warm water or ginger tea throughout the day. Gentle daily walking helps Qi move and dries dampness. Keep the abdomen warm and avoid sitting in damp, cold environments. Rest after meals and avoid overwork or heavy exercise immediately after eating.

### Cold-Damp invading the Spleen
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/cold-damp-invading-the-spleen)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Distension with a cold, heavy sensation that feels better with warmth, Loose or watery stools, Thick white greasy tongue coating, Heavy feeling in the body and limbs, Poor appetite and bland taste in the mouth
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Cold, raw foods and drinks, Damp, chilly weather, Overeating or heavy meals, Sitting or lying on cold surfaces
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warmth on the abdomen, Gentle pressure or massage on abdomen, Warm, cooked meals, Rest
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When external Cold-Damp or too many raw, cold foods invade the body, they directly chill the Spleen and freeze its ability to transform food and fluids. This congealing effect traps Dampness in the middle burner, blocking the normal rise and fall of Qi. The result is a heavy, cold-feeling distension that sits in the upper abdomen like a weight, often with a dull ache that improves with warmth.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale, swollen body with teeth marks, and a thick white greasy coating that is excessively moist. Pulse: soggy (Ru) or slippery (Hua) and slow (Chi).
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Cold and Dampness are the direct enemies in this pattern - they freeze the Spleen's function and congeal Qi, so anything that adds cold or dampness (raw foods, cold weather) makes the distension heavier. Warmth and warm foods act like thawing ice, restoring the Spleen's ability to move and transform, which lifts the heavy fullness.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat only warm, cooked foods - soups, stews, and congees are ideal. Avoid all raw, cold, and iced items, as well as dairy and greasy foods. Sip warm ginger tea throughout the day and keep a hot water bottle on your belly when the distension feels heavy.

## คำถามที่พบบ่อย (Frequently Asked Questions)
### How quickly can TCM relieve upper abdominal bloating?
For acute bloating after overeating, many people feel relief within 24-48 hours of starting herbs like Bao He Wan. Chronic bloating that has been present for months may take a few weeks to noticeably improve, with the first signs often being less fullness after meals and easier bowel movements. Acupuncture can sometimes provide immediate relaxation of the tight sensation, but lasting change builds over several sessions.

### Can acupuncture really help with bloating?
Yes. Acupuncture points on the abdomen, legs, and arms are chosen to regulate the Stomach and Spleen, calm the Liver, and move stagnant Qi. Many patients describe a sense of movement or warmth in the belly during treatment. While one session may temporarily reduce distension, a course of 6-10 sessions is usually recommended for lasting results.

### Do I need to change my diet while taking Chinese herbs?
Yes, dietary adjustments greatly enhance the herbs’ effectiveness. TCM views certain foods as contributors to bloating - cold drinks, raw salads, greasy or fried foods, and dairy are common culprits. Your practitioner will recommend warm, cooked meals and specific foods that support your pattern, but even simple changes like eating smaller portions and chewing thoroughly can make a noticeable difference.

### Is it safe to take TCM herbs with my acid-reflux or ulcer medication?
In most cases, yes. Many patients safely combine TCM formulas with antacids, H2 blockers, or proton-pump inhibitors. However, to avoid any potential interaction, we generally advise taking herbs and Western medications at least 2 hours apart. Always bring a full list of your medications to your TCM consultation and inform your prescribing doctor that you are using herbal medicine.

### What if my bloating is constant and nothing else has helped?
Constant bloating often signals a deeper imbalance, such as Spleen Qi Deficiency with Dampness or Liver Qi Stagnation that has become chronic. TCM excels at addressing these root causes, not just masking the symptom. Treatment may take longer - often several months - but many patients who have tried conventional approaches without success find that the bloating gradually resolves as their digestive function is rebuilt.

### Can TCM help if my bloating is caused by stress?
Absolutely. The Liver-Stomach connection is a core concept in TCM. When stress causes Qi to stagnate in the Liver, it often ‘invades’ the Stomach, causing bloating, belching, and a tight feeling under the ribs. Herbs and acupuncture that soothe Liver Qi (like Chai Hu Shu Gan San) can be very effective, and many people notice that their bloating improves as their stress levels come down.

## การวิจัยทางวิทยาศาสตร์สมัยใหม่ (Modern Scientific Research)
1. **Acupuncture for functional dyspepsia** ([ลิงก์](https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD008487.pub2))
   *วารสารวิชาการ: Lan L, Zeng F, Liu GJ, et al. Acupuncture for functional dyspepsia. Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews. 2014, Issue 10. Art. No.: CD008487.*
   A Cochrane systematic review evaluating the effectiveness of acupuncture for functional dyspepsia. It included seven trials and found that acupuncture may improve symptoms, including epigastric distension and postprandial fullness, compared to sham acupuncture, but the evidence was limited by small sample sizes and methodological weaknesses.

2. **Efficacy and safety of Xiangsha Liujunzi granules for functional dyspepsia: A multicenter, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled clinical trial** ([ลิงก์](https://doi.org/10.1155/2016/8795062))
   *วารสารวิชาการ: Zhang Y, et al. Efficacy and safety of Xiangsha Liujunzi granules for functional dyspepsia: A multicenter, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled clinical trial. Evid Based Complement Alternat Med. 2016;2016:8795062.*
   This randomized trial assessed the herbal formula Xiangsha Liujunzi granules in patients with functional dyspepsia. The treatment group showed a significant reduction in postprandial fullness and epigastric distension scores compared to placebo, with a favorable safety profile.

3. **Acupuncture for functional dyspepsia: a randomized, sham-controlled trial** ([ลิงก์](https://doi.org/10.1038/ajg.2014.157))
   *วารสารวิชาการ: Zhao L, et al. Acupuncture for functional dyspepsia: a randomized, sham-controlled trial. Am J Gastroenterol. 2014;109(9):1390-1397.*
   A single-blind RCT comparing true acupuncture to sham acupuncture in 712 patients with functional dyspepsia. True acupuncture significantly improved epigastric distension, early satiety, and quality of life, with effects persisting at 12-week follow-up.
