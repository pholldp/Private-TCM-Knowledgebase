## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนปัจจุบัน (Conventional Medicine View)
### คำอธิบาย (Description)
In conventional medicine, hyperactivity is usually seen as a symptom rather than a standalone diagnosis. It most commonly appears as part of attention-deficit/hyperactivity disorder (ADHD) in children and adults, but can also be caused by anxiety disorders, bipolar disorder, hyperthyroidism, or stimulant use. The experience typically involves excessive motor activity, impulsivity, difficulty sitting still, and a feeling of being driven or restless.
Diagnosis is based on clinical history and behavioral assessments, sometimes with thyroid function tests or other lab work to rule out medical causes. Standard treatment often includes behavioral therapy and medications such as stimulants (for ADHD), mood stabilizers, or anti-anxiety drugs, depending on the underlying condition.

### การรักษาหลัก (Treatments)
Conventional management of hyperactivity depends on the suspected cause. For ADHD, stimulant medications like methylphenidate or amphetamines are common, along with behavioral therapy and educational support. Anxiety-related restlessness may be treated with SSRIs or benzodiazepines. Hyperthyroidism is addressed with antithyroid drugs, radioactive iodine, or surgery. In all cases, the aim is to reduce the outward symptoms of overactivity and improve daily functioning.

## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนจีน (Traditional Chinese Medicine View)
### ความเข้าใจเกี่ยวกับการแพทย์แผนจีน (TCM Understanding)
In TCM, hyperactivity is almost always a sign that the mind's anchor - the Shén - has been unsettled. The Liver is often the first organ involved because it governs the smooth flow of Qi and emotions. When stress, frustration, or unexpressed anger build up, Liver Qi stagnates. Over time, that stuck energy can generate heat, which rises to disturb the Heart and mind, producing irritability, a short fuse, and a restless, driven feeling.
But not all hyperactivity comes from excess heat. When the body's Yin - the cooling, nourishing force - becomes depleted, it can no longer hold the active Yang in check. This is like water running low in a kettle; the flame is the same but the water boils faster. In these deficiency patterns, the person often feels tired yet wired, with a racing mind, night sweats, and a hollow, anxious restlessness.
The Kidneys, which store the body's fundamental Yin, and the Heart, which houses the mind, are both deeply involved.
Because the emotional texture and triggers differ from pattern to pattern, a TCM practitioner will listen carefully to your experience. Is the hyperactivity a simmering irritability that flares with stress? A volcanic anger that erupts suddenly? Or a constant low-grade buzz that makes it impossible to relax?
The answer, along with the tongue and pulse, reveals whether the root is Liver Qi Stagnation turning to Heat, Liver Fire Blazing, Liver Yang Rising, Heart and Kidney Yin Deficiency, or a mix of Qi and Yin Deficiency. Each path gets its own treatment.

### การวินิจฉัยโดยแพทย์แผนจีน (Practitioner Diagnosis)
A TCM practitioner starts by listening to how your hyperactivity actually feels. Is it a simmering irritability that flares with stress, a volcanic anger that erupts suddenly, or a restless buzzing that keeps you awake at night? The emotional quality and the triggers are the first clues that point toward one pattern rather than another.
If the agitation is clearly linked to emotional frustration and comes with a sense of tightness in the chest, frequent sighing, and a bitter taste in the mouth, the picture suggests Liver Qi Stagnation that has begun to generate Heat. The tongue may look slightly red on the edges, and the pulse often feels wiry, like a guitar string.
When the hyperactivity is more intense-angry outbursts, a red face, severe restlessness, and a pounding headache-the diagnosis shifts toward Liver Fire Blazing. Here the tongue is typically red with a yellow coating, and the pulse is wiry and rapid. The heat is no longer just brewing; it has fully ignited and is flaring upward.
A person who feels wired but also depleted, with a racing mind, dizziness, and insomnia, may be experiencing Liver Yang Rising. This pattern often stems from a deeper Yin deficiency that fails to anchor the Yang. The tongue tends to be red with a thin or absent coating, and the pulse is wiry, thready, and rapid.
If nighttime restlessness, palpitations, anxiety, and a feeling of heat in the palms and soles dominate, the root is often Heart and Kidney Yin Deficiency. The tongue is red with little coating, and the pulse is thready and rapid. In later stages, when fatigue and low-grade agitation coexist, a practitioner might recognize Qi and Yin Deficiency, where the tongue remains red with scant coating but the pulse feels weak and rapid.

### Liver Qi Stagnation that transforms into Heat
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/liver-qi-stagnation-that-transforms-into-heat)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Irritability with explosive outbursts, Bitter taste in the mouth, Distending or burning rib pain, Flushed face and red eyes, Constipation with dry stools, Difficulty falling asleep from racing thoughts
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Emotional stress, anger, or frustration, Spicy, greasy, or fried foods, Alcohol or caffeine, Hot weather or overheated rooms, Overwork and lack of sleep
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Expressing feelings calmly, Cooling foods and drinks, Gentle movement (walking, tai chi), Deep breathing and meditation
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  The Liver is responsible for the smooth flow of Qi and emotions. When stress, frustration, or unexpressed anger build up over time, the Liver Qi gets stuck and stagnates. That stuck energy eventually heats up, like friction creating fire.
This internal Heat rises to disturb the mind and spirit, leading to a restless, agitated, and hyperactive state. The person feels a constant inner pressure, irritability that can explode into anger, and a mind that races and refuses to settle down.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red body with redder sides, yellow coating. Pulse: wiry and rapid (弦数 xián shuò), especially strong at the left middle position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Emotional stress is the primary trigger because it directly causes Liver Qi to stagnate, which then generates Heat. Spicy and heating foods add external Heat to an already overheated system, making the agitation worse. Calming practices like deep breathing and gentle movement help unblock the Liver Qi so it can flow freely again, while cooling foods and drinks directly counteract the internal Heat. Good sleep allows the spirit to settle and the Liver to restore its smooth function.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Steer clear of spicy, greasy, and fried foods, as well as alcohol and coffee, which add heat and fuel agitation. Emphasize cooling, light foods like cucumber, celery, chrysanthemum tea, and peppermint tea. Regular gentle exercise such as walking, tai chi, or yoga helps keep Qi flowing and prevents stagnation. Build a daily wind-down routine before bed to calm the mind, and practice deep breathing or meditation to release pent-up frustration.

### Liver Fire Blazing
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/liver-fire-blazing)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Throbbing or distending headache at the temples or top of the head, Red, burning eyes, Bitter taste in the mouth, Irritability with explosive outbursts, Restless, driven agitation, Flushed red face, Constipation with dark yellow urine
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Emotional stress, anger, or frustration, Spicy, greasy, or fried foods, Alcohol or caffeine, Hot weather or overheated rooms, Overwork and lack of sleep
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Cooling foods and drinks, Deep breathing and meditation, Cool, quiet, and dark environment, Gentle movement (walking, tai chi)
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When anger, frustration, or long-held resentment build up, Liver Qi can stagnate and then erupt into intense Fire. This Fire surges upward to the head, agitating the mind and spirit, which produces the driven, restless, and hyperactive state. The excess Heat disturbs the Heart, making a person feel constantly on edge and prone to explosive outbursts.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red body with redder sides, dry yellow coating, possible red prickles. Pulse: wiry and rapid, full and forceful, especially at the left middle (guan) position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Liver Fire is fueled by emotional stress, especially anger, which causes Qi to stagnate and then explode into Heat. Spicy, greasy foods and alcohol add external Heat to the body, making the Fire burn hotter. Cooling foods, rest, and calming practices help drain the Fire and soothe the Liver, directly countering the hyperactive state.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Cool the body with foods like cucumber, celery, watermelon, and chrysanthemum tea. Avoid spicy, greasy, and fried foods, as well as alcohol and coffee. Practice calming activities such as meditation, gentle yoga, or deep breathing to soothe Liver Qi. Ensure adequate rest and avoid overwork, which can stoke the Fire.

### Liver Yang Rising
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/liver-yang-rising)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Throbbing or distending headache at the temples or top of the head, Flushed face and red eyes, Irritability and quick temper, Dizziness, heavy head and light, unsteady feet, Insomnia with vivid, disturbing dreams
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Emotional stress, anger, or frustration, Spicy, greasy, or fried foods, Alcohol or caffeine, Overwork and lack of sleep, Hot weather or overheated rooms
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Cool, quiet, and dark environment, Rest and adequate sleep, Cooling foods and drinks, Gentle movement (walking, tai chi)
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When the body's Yin (cooling, nourishing energy) becomes depleted, it can no longer anchor the Liver's Yang (active, rising energy). The unanchored Yang surges upward to the head, creating a state of inner restlessness, irritability, and a racing mind. This rising Yang makes you feel constantly 'on,' unable to settle down, and quick to react with anger.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red body, redder at the sides, with a thin dry yellow or scanty coat, possibly slightly trembling. Pulse: wiry (弦 xián), especially at the left middle position; may be fine and rapid if Yin deficiency is prominent.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Stress and anger directly stir up Liver Yang, making the rising energy more intense. Spicy and greasy foods generate internal heat, while alcohol and caffeine are stimulating and heating, all of which can worsen the upward surge. Overwork and sleep deprivation deplete Yin, leaving Yang even less anchored. Cooling foods and a calm environment help subdue the rising Yang, while rest and gentle movement nourish Yin and allow the mind to settle.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Prioritize cooling, yin-nourishing foods like celery, spinach, cucumber, and chrysanthemum tea. Avoid spicy, greasy, or fried foods, and limit alcohol and caffeine. Regular gentle exercise such as walking or tai chi helps smooth Liver Qi without overheating. Protect your sleep and build in downtime to calm the mind.

### Heart and Kidney Yin Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/heart-and-kidney-yin-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Restless and anxious despite exhaustion, Insomnia with vivid, disturbing dreams, Night sweats and five-center heat, Low back soreness and dizziness, Red tongue with little or no coating
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Overwork and lack of sleep, Emotional stress, anger, or frustration, Spicy, greasy, or fried foods, Excessive mental activity, Hot weather or overheated rooms
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Rest and adequate sleep, Cooling foods and drinks, Cool, quiet, and dark environment, Gentle movement (walking, tai chi), Deep breathing and meditation
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  The Kidneys store the body's fundamental Yin, which anchors the mind and keeps the Heart cool. When Kidney Yin is depleted, the nourishing 'water' that should calm the Heart runs dry. Empty heat rises from this deficiency, agitating the Heart spirit and creating a state of constant mental restlessness - a 'wired but tired' hyperactivity that is often worse at night when Yin should dominate.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red, especially at the tip, thin body, little or no coating, dry surface, possible cracks. Pulse: fine (thin) and rapid, weak at the left Kidney position, left Heart position thin and slightly floating.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Heart and Kidney Yin Deficiency is a state of depleted cooling, nourishing fluids. Overwork, late nights, and mental strain burn through Yin, while spicy and heating foods add more heat. Rest, cooling foods, and calming practices replenish Yin and allow the empty heat to subside, settling the restless spirit.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Prioritize rest and avoid late nights, which further deplete Yin. Eat cooling, moistening foods like black sesame, walnuts, mulberries, and pears; avoid spicy, fried, and stimulating foods. Gentle exercise such as Tai Chi or walking helps without overheating. Practice meditation or deep breathing to settle the mind.

### Qi and Yin Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/qi-and-yin-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Fatigue and weakness, Restless and anxious despite exhaustion, Dry mouth with little desire to drink, Night sweats, Warm sensation in palms, soles, and chest, Heart palpitations
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Overwork and lack of sleep, Spicy, greasy, or fried foods, Emotional stress, anger, or frustration
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Rest and adequate sleep, Cool, quiet, and dark environment, Nourishing, moistening foods (e.g., pear, tofu), Gentle breathing exercises
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When the body's Qi and Yin are both depleted, the nourishing, cooling Yin fluids are too weak to anchor the mind, while the Qi is too weak to sustain calm, steady energy. This creates a paradoxical state of deep fatigue combined with a low-grade inner restlessness - you feel exhausted yet unable to settle. The empty heat from Yin deficiency stirs the spirit, causing mild agitation, palpitations, and a sense of being 'tired but wired.'
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red or pale-red body with a thin or absent coating, dry and possibly cracked. Pulse: fine (thin) and weak, often rapid.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Qi and Yin deficiency means the body lacks both the vital energy and the cooling, nourishing fluids that sustain calm function. Rest and moistening foods rebuild these resources, while overexertion, spicy foods, and emotional strain further consume Qi and dry up Yin, intensifying the tired-but-wired feeling. A cool, quiet environment helps settle the empty heat that stirs the mind.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat small, frequent meals of easily digested, moistening foods like congee, steamed vegetables, pears, and tofu. Avoid spicy, fried, or drying foods. Prioritize rest and protect your sleep. Gentle movement such as Tai Chi or walking is helpful, but stop before you feel tired.

## คำถามที่พบบ่อย (Frequently Asked Questions)
### What does hyperactivity look like in TCM?
In TCM, hyperactivity isn't just physical restlessness. It includes emotional signs like irritability, explosive anger, a racing mind, or feeling 'wired but tired.' The specific combination of symptoms - what triggers it, what time of day it worsens, whether you feel hot or drained - tells the practitioner which internal pattern is at play.

### How is hyperactivity treated with acupuncture and herbs?
Treatment depends on the pattern. For Liver Fire Blazing, cooling herbs like Long Dan Cao and acupuncture points such as Taichong (LR-3) and Xingjian (LR-2) drain excess heat. For Yin deficiency, nourishing formulas like Tian Wang Bu Xin Dan and points like Shenmen (HT-7) and Taixi (KI-3) restore the cooling anchor. The goal is always to address the root imbalance, not just sedate the mind.

### Can TCM help with hyperactivity caused by ADHD?
Yes, many patients with ADHD-related hyperactivity find relief with TCM. The approach is not to treat 'ADHD' as a single label but to identify the specific TCM pattern underneath - often Liver Qi Stagnation with Heat or Yin deficiency. Treatment is highly individualized and can be used alongside conventional medications, though you should coordinate care with both your doctor and TCM practitioner.

### Will I need to take herbs forever?
No. Herbs are typically used for a period of weeks to months until the pattern shifts and the body's balance is restored. Once the root imbalance is corrected, many patients maintain calm without daily herbs, though some may use periodic 'tune-ups' or dietary adjustments. The aim is always to make the treatment temporary.

### Can I combine TCM with my current medications?
In most cases, yes. Acupuncture and many herbs are safe alongside conventional drugs. However, certain sedative herbs may compound the effects of anti-anxiety or sleep medications, so always inform both your TCM practitioner and your prescribing doctor about everything you are taking. Never stop prescribed medication abruptly without medical guidance.

### What dietary changes can help reduce hyperactivity?
Generally, avoid stimulants like caffeine, alcohol, and spicy foods, which can add heat and agitate the mind. Favor cooling, moistening foods such as pear, cucumber, tofu, and leafy greens. Eating regular, calm meals and avoiding heavy, greasy foods also helps. Specific advice will depend on your pattern - your practitioner will guide you further.

### How long does it take to see results?
Many patients notice a shift in their restlessness and mood within 2-4 weeks, especially for excess patterns. Deficiency patterns take longer - often 3-6 months - because the body needs time to rebuild Yin and Qi. Consistency with herbs and acupuncture sessions is key.
