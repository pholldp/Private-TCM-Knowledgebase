## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนปัจจุบัน (Conventional Medicine View)
### คำอธิบาย (Description)
Encephalitis is inflammation of the brain, most often triggered by a viral infection or an autoimmune attack. Typical symptoms include a sudden high fever, severe headache, confusion, seizures, and sensitivity to light. Diagnosis usually involves brain imaging (MRI), a lumbar puncture to analyze cerebrospinal fluid, and blood tests to identify the underlying cause.

### การรักษาหลัก (Treatments)
Treatment depends on the cause. Viral encephalitis may be managed with antiviral medications like acyclovir, while autoimmune forms are treated with corticosteroids, intravenous immunoglobulin, or plasma exchange. Supportive care - including fluids, anticonvulsants for seizures, and sometimes mechanical ventilation - is critical during the acute phase.

## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนจีน (Traditional Chinese Medicine View)
### ความเข้าใจเกี่ยวกับการแพทย์แผนจีน (TCM Understanding)
TCM classifies encephalitis under the umbrella of warm diseases (温病), specifically summerheat warm disease (暑温) or summerheat wind (暑风). It is seen as an invasion of external heat, wind, fire, dampness, and phlegm that attacks the brain. The pathogenic heat can penetrate to the Qi level, the Pericardium, or stir Liver wind, while phlegm and dampness can cloud the orifices. The core pathogenesis is heat toxins clouding the mind, phlegm misting the Heart, and internal wind stirring, with underlying Spleen and Kidney deficiency allowing phlegm and dampness to accumulate.

### การวินิจฉัยโดยแพทย์แผนจีน (Practitioner Diagnosis)
A TCM practitioner begins by asking how the illness started and what the person is feeling right now. Encephalitis often moves through stages, and the pattern changes as the illness deepens. By listening carefully to the description of fever, headache, and mental state, and by examining the tongue and pulse, the practitioner can pinpoint which pattern is dominant and how far the heat has penetrated.
When the pattern is Qi Level Heat, the illness is still at an early stage. The person has a high fever, a pounding headache, and intense thirst, but remains alert and restless. The tongue is red with a yellow coating, and the pulse feels rapid and surging. The absence of confusion or convulsions is a key clue that the pathogen has not yet moved deeper into the body.
If the heat toxins deepen into Heat invading the Pericardium, the picture changes sharply. The fever stays high, but now the mind is affected-there may be delirium, coma, or slurred speech. The tongue turns a deeper red with a yellow greasy coating, and the pulse is rapid and forceful. This shift in consciousness is the main signal that the pericardium is involved and treatment must be more urgent.
When the heat becomes so extreme that it stirs internal Wind, the pattern becomes Liver Wind agitating Internally due to extreme Heat. Convulsions, spasms, or a rigid neck appear alongside high fever. The tongue looks purple or very deep red, and the pulse feels wiry and rapid. This is a critical pattern where the body's own heat has generated a storm-like movement that endangers the brain.
In some cases, the illness takes a different turn with Phlegm Misting the Heart. Here the mental fog is less about acute delirium and more about a heavy, sluggish confusion, sometimes with seizure-like episodes. The tongue coating is thick and greasy, and the pulse is slippery. A practitioner will suspect this pattern when there is a history of phlegm conditions and the mental clouding feels dense rather than fiery.
Another less common pattern is Damp-Heat, where dampness and heat combine. The person may have fever, a heavy head, and possibly jaundice. The tongue coating is yellow and greasy, and the pulse is slippery and rapid. Unlike pure heat patterns, there is a sense of heaviness and stickiness to the symptoms, and the skin or eyes may take on a yellow hue.
During recovery, Empty-Heat caused by Yin Deficiency often appears. The high fever has subsided, but a low-grade heat lingers, along with a dry mouth and throat. The tongue is red with little or no coating, and the pulse is thin and rapid. This pattern reflects that the body's cooling fluids have been depleted, leaving a smoldering inner heat that needs gentle nourishment rather than strong cooling.

### Qi Level Heat
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/qi-level-heat)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: High fever without chills, Intense thirst with desire for cold drinks, Profuse sweating, Irritability and restlessness, Flushed face, Dark scanty urine
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Spicy, greasy, or fried foods, Alcohol and coffee, Overexertion or physical activity, Hot or stuffy environment, Emotional stress and anger
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Cooling drinks or fluids, Cool, quiet environment, Light, easily digestible foods, Cool compresses on forehead, Deep breathing and calming activities
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  In encephalitis, a warm-heat pathogen often bypasses the exterior and penetrates directly into the Qi level, where the body's defensive Qi mounts a fierce battle. This internal clash generates intense heat with no chills, drenching sweats, and a raging thirst for cold drinks as fluids are rapidly consumed. The heat rises to disturb the mind and brain, causing severe headache, irritability, and restlessness.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red body with a yellow, dry coating; possible prickles in the centre. Pulse: overflowing (hong) and rapid (shu), large and forceful.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Qi Level Heat is an interior excess heat condition where the body's defensive Qi battles the pathogen fiercely, generating high fever, thirst, and sweating. Cooling foods and drinks help drain heat and replenish fluids. Rest and a cool environment prevent further heat buildup and agitation. Spicy, greasy foods and alcohol add more heat, while overexertion consumes Qi and fluids, worsening the imbalance. Emotional stress stirs up internal fire, aggravating irritability and headache.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Drink plenty of cool fluids like mung bean soup or chrysanthemum tea to clear heat. Avoid spicy, greasy, and heavy foods that generate internal heat. Rest in a cool, quiet environment to prevent agitation.

### Heat invading the Pericardium
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/heat-invading-the-pericardium)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: High fever that worsens at night, Loss of consciousness or delirium, Cold hands and feet despite high fever, Deep red, stiff tongue, Extreme restlessness and irritability, Flushed face
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Spicy, greasy, or fried foods, Bright lights or loud noises, Emotional agitation or fright, Hot or stuffy environment
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Cool, quiet environment, Cooling drinks or fluids, Deep rest with minimal stimulation
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  In encephalitis, the pathogenic Heat penetrates deeply past the body's outer defenses and directly attacks the Pericardium, the protective envelope of the Heart that houses the mind (Shen). This toxic Heat clouds the orifices of the head, leading to loss of consciousness and delirium. Because the Heat is trapped internally and blocks the circulation of Yang Qi to the limbs, the hands and feet can feel cold despite the raging fever inside.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: deep red or crimson body, stiff with a prickly tip; coating dry yellow or scanty. Pulse: fine (thready) and rapid, possibly wiry.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Heat invading the Pericardium is an interior excess-Hot condition that traps pathogenic Fire deep inside, clouding the mind. Anything that adds external or internal Heat-such as spicy food, hot rooms, or emotional stress-fuels the fire and worsens delirium. Cooling the body with light fluids and a calm environment helps drain Heat and settle the spirit, giving the body a chance to restore consciousness.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: This is a medical emergency requiring hospital care. During recovery, keep the environment cool, quiet, and dimly lit to avoid overstimulating the spirit. Offer small amounts of cooling fluids like mung bean soup or pear juice, and completely avoid spicy, greasy, or fried foods that add internal Heat.

### Liver Wind agitating Internally due to extreme Heat
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/liver-wind-agitating-internally-due-to-extreme-heat)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: High fever with violent convulsions or seizure-like spasms, Neck rigidity and arched-back spasm (opisthotonos), Eyes rolling upward and clenched jaw, Loss of consciousness or delirium, Red flushed face and intense thirst, Dark scanty urine
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: High fever, Spicy, greasy, or fried foods, Bright lights or loud noises, Emotional stress or agitation, Overexertion or physical activity
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Cool, quiet environment, Cool compresses on forehead, Sipping cool water, Light, cooling foods like mung bean soup
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When extreme Heat from a severe infection invades the body, it scorches the Liver's Yin and Blood, depriving the sinews of nourishment. This causes internal Wind to surge upward, which manifests as violent convulsions, rigid neck and limbs, and even an arched-back spasm. The same Heat toxins also cloud the Heart's orifices, leading to loss of consciousness or delirium.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: deep red or crimson body, dry with prickles, stiff, yellow dry coating. Pulse: wiry (弦 xián), rapid (数 shuò), and forceful, especially at the left guan position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: This pattern is driven by extreme internal Heat scorching Liver Yin and generating Wind. Cooling measures help drain Heat and calm Wind, while quiet darkness reduces sensory stimulation that can trigger convulsions. Spicy, hot foods and emotional stress add more Heat and stir Wind, worsening the condition.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: This is a medical emergency; seek hospital care immediately. During recovery, eat cooling, Yin-nourishing foods like pear, watermelon, and mung bean soup. Avoid spicy, fried, and stimulating foods. Rest in a quiet, darkened room to reduce sensory stimulation that can trigger Wind.

### Phlegm Misting the Heart
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/phlegm-misting-the-heart)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Mental confusion or dullness, feeling like you are in a fog, Rattling or gurgling sound in the throat, Dizziness and heavy-headedness
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Damp cold weather, Greasy, dairy-heavy or sugary foods, Sedentary lifestyle, Excessive worry or overthinking
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warm dry environment, Light, easily digestible foods, Gentle movement or exercise
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When the Spleen is weakened-either by a lingering infection, poor diet, or constitutional tendency-it fails to transform fluids, and a thick, turbid Phlegm accumulates. This Phlegm rises and clouds the Heart orifice, which houses the mind and consciousness. The result is the mental dullness, confusion, and clouded awareness typical of this less common encephalitis presentation, where the brain is literally "misted over" rather than inflamed by acute heat.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale swollen body with a thick white greasy coating, may appear stiff. Pulse: slippery (滑, huá) and full, like beads rolling under the fingers.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Phlegm forms when the Spleen's ability to transform fluids is overwhelmed by Dampness. Greasy and heavy foods, damp environments, and a sedentary life all add to that Damp burden, thickening the Phlegm that clouds the mind. Warmth, light eating, and gentle movement activate the Spleen and Lung Qi to transform and expel Dampness, while emotional ease prevents Qi stagnation that would otherwise let Phlegm congeal further.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Stick to warm, cooked, and easily digestible foods like congee and steamed vegetables, and strictly avoid dairy, greasy, fried, or sugary items that generate more Phlegm. Keep living spaces dry and airy, and incorporate gentle daily movement like walking or stretching to keep Qi flowing and help the body clear Dampness.

### Damp-Heat
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/damp-heat)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Jaundice (yellow skin or eyes), Heavy, sluggish sensation in the head and body, Chest and upper abdominal stuffiness, Nausea and poor appetite, Low-grade fever that worsens in the afternoon, Thirst with little desire to actually drink
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Greasy, fried foods, Damp, humid weather, Overexertion or physical activity, Alcohol and sweets
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Light, bland diet, Cool, dry environment, Warm fluids, Rest
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Damp-Heat is a heavy, turbid pathogen that can accumulate when external dampness and heat invade the body, or when a weakened Spleen fails to transform fluids, creating internal dampness that combines with heat. In encephalitis, this turbid mixture rises to obstruct the clear orifices of the head, causing mental cloudiness, a heavy sensation, and fever. The Heat component also disturbs the Liver and Gallbladder, forcing bile to leak into the skin and turning it yellow (jaundice).
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red body with a yellow greasy coating. Pulse: slippery-rapid (滑数 huá shuò).
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Dampness is produced by the Spleen when it is overwhelmed by heavy, greasy, or sweet foods, and external dampness from humid environments directly invades the body. Heat is stirred up by alcohol, overexertion, and rich foods. A light, bland diet and rest give the Spleen the space it needs to transform and drain dampness. A cool, dry environment prevents external dampness from adding to the internal burden, while warm fluids help dissolve turbidity without congealing it the way cold drinks do.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Avoid greasy, fried, sweet, and dairy-rich foods that create more Dampness. Eat light, easily digested meals like congee with barley (Yi Yi Ren) to help the Spleen drain dampness. Sip warm fluids, but avoid iced drinks, which can congeal dampness. Rest in a cool, dry environment and avoid humid places.

### Empty-Heat caused by Yin Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/empty-heat-caused-by-yin-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Low-grade fever worse in the afternoon or evening, Night sweats, Dry mouth and throat with a desire to sip water, Flushed cheekbones (malar flush), Restlessness and difficulty falling asleep, Heat sensation in palms, soles, and chest
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Overwork and staying up late, Spicy, greasy, or fried foods, Hot, dry weather, Emotional stress and worry, Excessive sweating or saunas
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Adequate rest and deep sleep, Cool, quiet environment, Yin-nourishing foods like pear and lily bulb, Gentle movement or exercise, Sipping warm water
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  During the acute phase of encephalitis, high fever consumes the body's Yin, the cooling and moistening foundation. As the infection subsides, the depleted Yin can no longer hold the body's heat in check, so a low-grade empty heat rises up, disturbing the mind and drying the tissues. This is why you may feel lingering warmth, restlessness, and dryness even after the worst of the illness has passed.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red, thin, dry body with cracks, little or no coating. Pulse: fine (thready) and rapid.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Yin deficiency means the body's cooling and moistening reserves are low. Activities that consume Yin-like overwork, late nights, or spicy foods-add more heat and dry out the body further, making the empty heat feel stronger. Rest, a cool environment, and yin-nourishing foods help rebuild those reserves, gently settling the heat and restoring a sense of calm.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Focus on gentle, nourishing foods that build Yin: pears, lily bulb, black sesame, tofu, and congee. Avoid spicy, fried, or drying foods that can further deplete fluids. Prioritize rest and a regular sleep schedule; even mild overexertion can flare the empty heat. Keep the room cool and humidified, and sip warm water throughout the day.

## คำถามที่พบบ่อย (Frequently Asked Questions)
### Can acupuncture be used during the acute phase of encephalitis?
Yes, but it must be administered by an experienced practitioner, ideally in a hospital setting alongside conventional emergency care. Acupuncture points like Dazhui (DU-14) and Quchi (LI-11) are used to clear heat, while Baihui (DU-20) and Yongquan (KI-1) can help calm convulsions and restore consciousness. It is not a replacement for antiviral or supportive medical treatment but a complementary tool to manage symptoms and protect the brain.

### Can TCM help prevent long-term complications after encephalitis?
Many post-encephalitis issues - memory problems, fatigue, tremors, or personality changes - are seen in TCM as lingering Heat, Phlegm, or Yin Deficiency. By clearing residual pathogens and nourishing the body's Yin and Blood, herbal medicine and acupuncture can support neurological recovery and reduce the risk of chronic symptoms. The earlier TCM is introduced after the acute phase, the better the outcomes tend to be.

### Is it safe to combine TCM herbs with antiviral or antiseizure medications?
In most cases, yes, but always inform both your TCM practitioner and your neurologist about all medications you are taking. Some herbs used for encephalitis, such as Shi Gao and Huang Lian, have no known serious interactions with conventional drugs. However, sedative herbs or formulas like An Gong Niu Huang Wan should be used with caution if you are already taking sedatives or anticonvulsants, as they may enhance drowsiness. Never stop prescribed medications without your doctor's guidance.

### What diet should I follow during recovery from encephalitis?
Focus on cooling, easily digestible foods that do not create heat or phlegm. Mung bean soup, pear, lily bulb, and congee are excellent. Avoid greasy, fried, spicy, or heavily processed foods, as well as alcohol and coffee, which can stir up internal heat and dampness. As energy returns, gradually introduce nourishing foods like black sesame, goji berries, and bone broths to rebuild Yin and Blood.

### How long does it take to feel better with TCM after encephalitis?
Many patients notice improvements in energy and mental clarity within 4-6 weeks of regular treatment. However, full recovery from post-encephalitis fatigue, cognitive fog, or motor issues often takes 3-6 months of consistent herbs and acupuncture. The timeline depends on the severity of the initial infection and the pattern involved - Yin Deficiency recovery, for example, is a slower process of rebuilding the body's cooling and nourishing foundation.

### Can children with encephalitis receive TCM treatment?
Yes, pediatric encephalitis is commonly treated with TCM in China, often using gentle herbal formulas and acupuncture tailored to the child's age and constitution. Acupressure or non-needle techniques may be used for very young children. However, any treatment must be coordinated with the child's medical team, and acute symptoms like high fever or seizures require immediate hospital care.
