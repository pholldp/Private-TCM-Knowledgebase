## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนปัจจุบัน (Conventional Medicine View)
### คำอธิบาย (Description)
In Western medicine, eye discharge is often a symptom of conjunctivitis (pink eye), blepharitis (eyelid inflammation), dry eye syndrome, or allergies. It can be watery, mucous-like, or crusty, and may be accompanied by redness, itching, or a gritty sensation. Diagnosis is typically based on the appearance of the eye and discharge, sometimes with swabs to identify bacterial or viral causes.

### การรักษาหลัก (Treatments)
Treatment depends on the cause: antibiotic drops for bacterial infections, antihistamine drops for allergies, artificial tears for dry eye, and warm compresses and eyelid hygiene for blepharitis. In persistent cases, steroid drops or oral medications may be used.

## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนจีน (Traditional Chinese Medicine View)
### ความเข้าใจเกี่ยวกับการแพทย์แผนจีน (TCM Understanding)
In TCM, the eyes are intimately connected to the Liver and the Spleen. The Liver channel opens into the eyes, so emotional stress or Liver heat often shows up as redness and irritation. The Spleen, meanwhile, governs the transformation and transport of fluids - when it's weak, fluids can accumulate as Dampness and rise to the eyes as discharge. So eye discharge isn't just a local eye problem; it's a reflection of what's happening deeper in the body.
The quality of the discharge is a key diagnostic clue. Thin, watery discharge often points to an external Wind invasion or a deficiency of Qi and Blood that fails to hold fluids. Thick, yellow, sticky discharge signals Heat or Damp-Heat brewing internally. Scant, stringy discharge that is worse at night suggests Yin Deficiency, where the body's cooling fluids are too low and empty heat rises to dry the eyes.
Even the timing matters: morning discharge that is heavy and sticky often means Dampness has settled overnight.
This is why one Western diagnosis - like blepharitis - can correspond to several different TCM patterns. A person with red, crusty lids and a greasy coating on the tongue may have Damp-Heat in the Spleen and Stomach, while another with dry, itchy lids and night sweats may have Yin Deficiency. Each requires a fundamentally different treatment strategy, even though the symptom looks similar on the surface.

### การวินิจฉัยโดยแพทย์แผนจีน (Practitioner Diagnosis)
A TCM practitioner starts by asking about the discharge itself - is it scant and sticky, thin and watery, or thick and yellow? The answer immediately narrows the field. Scant, sticky discharge that worsens at night and comes with a dry mouth, night sweats, and a red tongue with little coating points to Empty-Heat from Yin Deficiency. Here the body’s cooling fluids are too low, and the resulting heat rises to the eyes.
If the eyes feel red and gritty with a small amount of sticky discharge, and the person is often irritable or stressed, Liver Qi Stagnation transforming into Heat is likely. The tongue edges may look redder, and the pulse feels wiry. This pattern reflects constrained energy that turns into heat and travels up the Liver channel to the eyes.
When the eyes are dry and tired but the discharge is thin or watery, and the person looks pale, feels exhausted, or has a history of prolonged illness or childbirth, Qi and Blood Deficiency is the root. A pale tongue and a weak, thready pulse confirm that the eyes simply aren’t getting enough nourishment.
Sudden redness, itching, and watery or sticky discharge after exposure to wind or during a cold suggests an external Wind-Heat invasion. The pulse is often floating and rapid, and there may be a sore throat or mild fever. This is an acute pattern, much like conjunctivitis, and it needs to be cleared quickly.

## กลุ่มอาการการแพทย์แผนจีนและการรักษา (TCM Patterns and Treatment)

### Empty-Heat caused by Yin Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/empty-heat-caused-by-yin-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Scant, sticky or stringy discharge, Dry, gritty sensation in the eyes, Worse at night or in the evening, Dry mouth and throat, with desire to sip water, Night sweats and heat in palms, soles, and chest
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Spicy, fried, or drying foods, Late nights and overwork, Hot, dry weather
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Cool drinks and moistening foods, Adequate sleep and rest, Humidified environment
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When the body's cooling and moistening Yin is depleted, empty heat rises upward along the channels to the eyes. This heat dries the normal tear film, leaving the eyes chronically dry and irritated, with only a small amount of sticky, stringy discharge. The symptoms are typically worse at night because Yin is naturally lower at that time, and the lack of moisture is felt as a persistent gritty sensation.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red, thin, dry body with cracks, little or no coating. Pulse: fine (thin) and rapid.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Empty heat arises because Yin is insufficient to anchor the body's warmth. Spicy foods, overwork, and late nights consume the remaining Yin, fanning the heat and worsening dryness. Rest, cooling foods, and moisture-rich environments help restore Yin and calm the empty heat, reducing the sticky discharge and eye irritation.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Emphasize yin-nourishing foods like pear, black sesame, spinach, and millet. Avoid spicy, fried, and overly heating foods that consume Yin. Prioritize rest and sleep, especially before midnight, to allow Yin to regenerate. Use a humidifier in dry environments and take breaks from screens to reduce eye strain.

### Liver Qi Stagnation that transforms into Heat
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/liver-qi-stagnation-that-transforms-into-heat)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Red, gritty eyes with burning sensation, Small amount of sticky discharge, worse with stress, Irritability and explosive anger, Bitter taste in the mouth, Distending pain along the ribcage
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Stress and anger, Spicy or fried foods, Alcohol, Hot weather, Eye strain
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Cool compresses on eyes, Relaxation and stress reduction, Chrysanthemum tea, Gentle exercise like Tai Chi, Avoiding anger
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When emotions like anger and frustration are held in, Liver Qi stagnates. Over time, this stuck Qi generates heat, which travels up the Liver channel to the eyes. The rising heat irritates the eyes, causing redness, a gritty sensation, and a small amount of sticky discharge.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red body with redder sides, yellow coating. Pulse: wiry and rapid (xian shu).
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: <p>Stress and anger are the root cause of Liver Qi stagnation, which generates heat. Managing emotions directly reduces the heat that rises to the eyes. Cooling foods and activities help clear the heat, while spicy and greasy foods add fuel to the fire.</p>
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Avoid spicy, greasy, and fried foods that add heat. Sip chrysanthemum tea to cool the eyes. Practice stress management with meditation or gentle exercise like Tai Chi. Avoid alcohol and late nights, which can fan the flames of Liver heat.

### Qi and Blood Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/qi-and-blood-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Thin, watery eye discharge, Eye dryness and fatigue, Worse with exertion or prolonged reading, Pale complexion and general fatigue, Dizziness or lightheadedness, Poor appetite
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Overwork and physical exhaustion, Prolonged screen use or reading, Skipping meals or poor nutrition, Excessive sweating or blood loss, Chronic stress or worry, Postpartum or post-surgery recovery
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Adequate rest and sleep, Warm, nutrient-rich meals (soups, stews), Gentle exercise (walking, qigong), Warm compress on eyes, Reducing screen time
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When the body’s Qi and Blood are insufficient, the eyes are not properly nourished or moistened. This leads to chronic dryness, a tired sensation, and a thin, watery discharge because the tear film lacks the substance to stay stable. The condition is often worse after prolonged visual concentration or physical exertion, which further drains Qi and Blood.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale body, may be thin or have teeth marks, with a thin white coating. Pulse: fine (thin) and weak, especially in the right Guan position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Qi and Blood are the foundation of the body’s nourishment. Overwork, poor diet, and insufficient rest deplete them, leaving the eyes dry and tired. Rest and nutrient-dense foods replenish Qi and Blood, while gentle movement encourages their circulation. Warmth and reduced visual strain help preserve what little Qi and Blood are available, giving the eyes the moisture and energy they lack.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat warm, nourishing foods such as bone broth, congee, red dates, goji berries, and dark leafy greens. Avoid cold, raw foods and excessive screen time. Gentle exercise like walking or qigong helps build Qi without depleting it. Prioritize rest and sleep to allow Blood regeneration.

### Wind-Heat
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/wind-heat)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Sudden onset of red, itchy eyes, Watery or sticky discharge that may crust, Sore throat and mild fever or chills, Thirst with desire to drink, Floating, rapid pulse
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Exposure to wind or fans, Spicy or greasy food, Rubbing the eyes, Lack of sleep
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Cool compresses on eyes, Chrysanthemum or peppermint tea, Rest in a dark, quiet room, Avoiding wind and drafts
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Wind-Heat is an external pathogen that invades the body through the nose and mouth, then travels upward to the eyes. The Heat irritates the delicate tissues, making the eyes red, itchy, and inflamed, while the Wind drives fluid leakage, producing watery or sticky discharge. Because it is an acute exterior attack, symptoms like sore throat, mild fever, and a floating rapid pulse often appear at the same time.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red tip and edges with a thin yellow (or thin white-to-yellow) coating. Pulse: floating (fú) and rapid (shuò).
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Wind-Heat is an exterior pathogen, so anything that introduces more Wind or Heat (like drafts, spicy food, or fatigue) fuels the attack and worsens eye symptoms. Cooling compresses, gentle herbs that release the exterior, and rest help the body push the pathogen out, soothe inflammation, and restore normal fluid balance in the eyes.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Drink plenty of water and mild chrysanthemum or peppermint tea. Avoid spicy, fried, or greasy foods that generate internal Heat. Rest in a cool, well-ventilated room and shield your eyes from wind and bright light.

### Spleen Deficiency with Dampness
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/spleen-deficiency-with-dampness)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Copious, sticky eye discharge, especially in the morning, Feeling of heaviness in the body and limbs, Poor appetite and loose stools, Pale, puffy tongue with teeth marks and a greasy white coating, Fatigue and drowsiness after eating
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Damp or humid weather, Cold raw foods and iced drinks, Greasy or heavy meals, Dairy products, Overeating or eating late at night
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warm, light meals, Gentle walking after eating, Dry, mild weather, Avoiding overeating, Small, frequent meals
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  The Spleen transforms food into Qi and manages fluids. When it is weak, fluids accumulate as internal Dampness. This Dampness has a heavy, sticky nature and can rise to the eyes, causing a copious, sticky discharge that is worst in the morning when Dampness has settled overnight. The discharge is not yellow or hot because there is no Heat; it is simply turbid fluid that the Spleen cannot manage.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale, swollen body with teeth marks and a white greasy coating. Pulse: soggy (Ru) and slowed-down (Huan), possibly slippery.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Spleen deficiency fails to transform fluids, leading to Dampness. Dampness is aggravated by cold, raw, and greasy foods, as well as humid environments, because they further burden the Spleen. Warm, cooked foods and moderate exercise strengthen the Spleen's transporting function, helping to clear the sticky discharge.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat warm, cooked foods such as congee with a little ginger and barley. Avoid dairy, greasy foods, raw salads, and sweets, which create more Dampness. Gentle daily movement like walking after meals helps the Spleen process fluids, and try not to lie down immediately after eating.

### Damp-Heat in Stomach and Spleen
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/damp-heat-in-stomach-and-spleen)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Thick, yellow, sticky discharge, Worse in the morning or after heavy meals, Upper abdominal bloating and nausea, Sticky or bitter taste in the mouth, Heavy feeling in the body and limbs
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Greasy or fried foods, Damp or humid weather, Heavy, late-night meals, Alcohol
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Light, bland diet, Cool, dry environment, Gentle movement after eating
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Dampness and Heat brew together in the Stomach and Spleen, much like steam rising from a compost pile. This turbid, hot vapor has nowhere to go but upward, following the Stomach channel directly to the face and eyes. The result is a thick, yellow, sticky discharge that feels hot and heavy, reflecting the nature of the underlying digestive imbalance.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red body, possibly swollen with teeth marks, with a thick, yellow, greasy coating at the center and root. Pulse: soggy (濡 ru) and rapid (数 shu), often also slippery (滑 hua).
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Greasy and sweet foods create the very Dampness and Heat that brew in the digestive system, so eating them directly fuels the problem. A light, bland diet and gentle movement help the Spleen do its job of transforming fluids, cutting off the source of the turbid steam that rises to the eyes.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Avoid greasy, fried, sweet, and dairy-rich foods that create more Dampness and Heat. Eat lightly cooked vegetables, whole grains like barley, and small amounts of cooling proteins like mung beans. A short walk after meals helps the Spleen process food and prevents stagnation.

## คำถามที่พบบ่อย (Frequently Asked Questions)
### Can TCM help with chronic eye discharge that keeps coming back?
Yes, this is where TCM excels. Recurrent eye discharge often stems from an underlying weakness, such as a Spleen that isn't managing fluids properly or a Yin Deficiency that creates chronic dryness and irritation. By addressing the root pattern with herbs and acupuncture, TCM aims to reduce the frequency and severity of flare-ups over time rather than just suppressing the symptom.

### Is it safe to use TCM herbs alongside my prescription eye drops?
In most cases, yes. Herbal formulas taken orally work systemically and generally do not interfere with topical eye medications. However, always inform both your TCM practitioner and your eye doctor about all treatments you are using. If you are taking blood-thinning medications, be especially cautious as some herbs may have mild blood-moving effects.

### How quickly can I expect my eye discharge to improve with acupuncture?
Many people notice less irritation and discharge within the first few sessions, especially for acute conditions like Wind-Heat. For chronic patterns, improvement is more gradual, often becoming noticeable after 2-4 weekly sessions, with continued progress over several weeks as the underlying imbalance is corrected.

### What foods should I avoid if I have eye discharge?
In general, avoid foods that create Dampness and Heat, such as greasy, fried, and very spicy foods, as well as excessive dairy, sugar, and alcohol. These can worsen thick, sticky discharge. If your discharge is thin and watery, you may need to avoid raw, cold foods that further weaken the Spleen. Your practitioner will give you specific dietary advice based on your pattern.

### Can TCM treat eye discharge in children?
Yes, TCM offers gentle approaches for children, including mild herbal formulas, pediatric tui na (massage), and non-needle acupuncture techniques. However, children's bodies respond quickly, so always consult a qualified pediatric TCM practitioner for appropriate dosing and treatment.

### Does the color of the discharge really matter in TCM diagnosis?
Absolutely. Clear or white watery discharge usually indicates Wind-Cold or deficiency. Yellow or green sticky discharge points to Heat or Damp-Heat. Scant, stringy, or dry discharge often signals Yin Deficiency. The color, consistency, and timing are all clues that help your practitioner identify the exact pattern and choose the right herbs and points.
