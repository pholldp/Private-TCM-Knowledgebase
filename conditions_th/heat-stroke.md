## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนปัจจุบัน (Conventional Medicine View)
### คำอธิบาย (Description)
Western medicine classifies heat-related illnesses on a spectrum from mild heat cramps to life-threatening heat stroke. Heat exhaustion involves heavy sweating, weakness, nausea, headache, and a core temperature that may be normal or slightly elevated. If untreated, it can progress to heat stroke, defined by a core body temperature above 40°C (104°F), hot dry skin, confusion, and potential organ failure. Diagnosis is based on symptoms and temperature measurement, and treatment focuses on rapid cooling and fluid replacement.

### การรักษาหลัก (Treatments)
For mild heat exhaustion, treatment includes moving to a cool environment, rest, and drinking water or electrolyte solutions. Severe heat stroke is a medical emergency requiring aggressive cooling (ice packs, cold IV fluids, cooling blankets) and careful monitoring for organ damage. There is no standard medication for the lingering fatigue and heat sensitivity that often follow a heat illness.

## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนจีน (Traditional Chinese Medicine View)
### ความเข้าใจเกี่ยวกับการแพทย์แผนจีน (TCM Understanding)
In TCM, summer heat is not just a temperature-it is an external pathogen, a fierce Yang evil that invades the body when the weather is hot and the body's defenses are weak. Unlike a simple sunburn or dehydration, this pathogen penetrates through the skin and breathing, forcing the pores open, driving out sweat, and rapidly consuming the body's Qi and Yin fluids. The result is a cascade of symptoms that depend on how deeply the heat has invaded and which organ systems it has disrupted.
When summer heat first strikes and stays at the surface or in the Stomach channel (the Bright Yang level), the body mounts a strong reaction: high fever, drenching sweat, and intense thirst. If the heat pushes deeper to the Pericardium-the protective sac around the Heart that houses the mind-consciousness becomes clouded and confusion sets in. At the deepest Blood level, the heat can generate internal Wind, causing convulsions and rigidity. Meanwhile, when humidity mixes with heat, a different pattern emerges: the dampness blocks the pores, trapping heat inside while weighing down the limbs and upsetting the digestion.
This is why TCM sees heat stroke not as one disease but as a family of patterns. The same hot day can produce blazing Bright Yang Stomach Heat in a robust person, heavy Summer Heat with Dampness in a humid climate, or lingering Qi and Yin Deficiency in someone already depleted. The pattern diagnosis tells the practitioner exactly which herbs and points will clear the heat, restore fluids, and rebuild the energy that the heat has stolen.

### การวินิจฉัยโดยแพทย์แผนจีน (Practitioner Diagnosis)
A TCM practitioner begins by asking about the circumstances and the first symptoms. Sudden high fever with drenching sweat and intense thirst right after heavy sun exposure points to Bright Yang Stomach Heat. A more gradual onset of fatigue, mild fever, and thirst after a long hot day suggests a general Summer-Heat pattern that has not yet settled deep into the body.
If the heat combines with humidity, the picture changes to Summer Heat with Dampness. The person feels heavy, nauseous, and their head and limbs ache as if wrapped in a wet blanket. The tongue coating is thick and greasy, and the pulse feels soggy rather than just rapid. Digestive upset, like loose stools, is common.
When the heat has drained the body’s resources, Qi and Yin Deficiency emerges. The fever becomes low-grade or intermittent, the mouth is dry, and profound exhaustion sets in. The tongue is red with little coating, and the pulse is weak and thin. This pattern often appears during recovery or after prolonged mild exposure.
If the heat penetrates deeper, it can disturb the mind, causing Heat in the Pericardium. Confusion, delirium, or loss of consciousness are the red flags, even while the fever stays high. A very red tongue tip and a rapid, thready pulse confirm the diagnosis. In the most severe cases, heat generates internal wind, leading to convulsions and rigid limbs - a medical emergency.

### Bright Yang Stomach Heat
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/bright-yang-stomach-heat)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: High continuous fever that does not improve with sweating, Profuse sweating, Intense thirst with craving for cold water, Flushed red face, Irritability and restlessness, Scanty dark urine
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Hot, humid weather, Strenuous physical activity in hot weather, Heavy, greasy, or spicy foods, Alcohol and coffee
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Cool environment, Drinking cool water or electrolyte drinks, Rest in a cool, shaded area, Light, loose clothing
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Summer heat is a fierce Yang pathogen that invades the body directly through the skin and breathing. When it penetrates deeply, it settles in the Stomach channel (Bright Yang), which is the body's great reservoir of Qi and fluids. The trapped heat blazes like a furnace, pushing the body's temperature dangerously high, forcing out sweat that fails to cool, and rapidly consuming fluids, which creates the raging thirst.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red body with a yellow, dry coating; early cracks may appear. Pulse: overflowing and surging (Hong), rapid (Shu), like a powerful wave that rises and falls.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Heat stroke from Bright Yang Stomach Heat is a pure excess heat condition; anything that adds heat to the body worsens it, while cooling measures directly counteract the pathogen. A cool environment and cold drinks help drain the internal fire, and rest prevents the generation of more internal heat through movement. Hot weather and exertion stoke the furnace, and heating foods or stimulants pour fuel on the flames.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Move to a cool, shaded place immediately. Sip cool water or watermelon juice frequently-never ice-cold, as the shock can trap heat. Eat light, watery foods like cucumber, mung bean soup, and pear. Avoid alcohol, coffee, spicy foods, and heavy, greasy meals that generate internal heat.

### Summer-Heat
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/summer-heat)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Profuse sweating, Intense thirst with desire for cold drinks, Irritability and mental restlessness, Dizziness and heavy limbs, Fever, worse after sun exposure
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Prolonged sun exposure, Strenuous physical activity in hot weather, Heavy, greasy, or spicy foods, Dehydration
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Rest in a cool, shaded area, Drinking cool water or electrolyte drinks, Cooling the skin with a damp cloth or compresses, Eating watermelon or cucumber
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Summer-Heat is an external pathogen that invades the body during hot weather, especially when a person is overexerted or unprotected. It penetrates the skin and pores, forcing them open and causing profuse sweating that drains fluids and Qi. The intense heat disturbs the Heart and Spirit, leading to restlessness, while the loss of fluids creates unquenchable thirst and a heavy, exhausted feeling.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red body, redder tip, yellow greasy or sticky coat, dry surface. Pulse: rapid, soggy (soft and slightly floating) or overflowing (Hong) if heat intense.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Summer-Heat is an external heat pathogen that depletes fluids and disturbs the Heart. Rest in cool shade and drinking fluids help clear heat and replenish Yin, while sun exposure and exertion intensify the heat invasion and further damage Qi and fluids. Cooling foods like watermelon directly clear summer heat and generate fluids, while heavy meals tax the Spleen and produce internal damp-heat, worsening the condition.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Avoid direct sun exposure during peak hours and rest in a cool, well-ventilated space. Drink plenty of water or electrolyte-rich fluids, and eat cooling, hydrating foods like watermelon, cucumber, and mung bean soup. Avoid heavy, greasy meals and alcohol, which can generate more internal heat.

### Summer Heat with Dampness
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/summer-heat-with-dampness)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Fever that stays despite sweating, Heavy, aching limbs and body heaviness, Stuffy chest and bloated upper abdomen, Thirst with little desire to drink, Nausea or vomiting, Loose, sticky stools
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Hot, humid weather, Heavy, greasy, or spicy foods, Iced drinks and raw salads, Strenuous physical activity in hot weather
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Cool, dry environment, Light, easily digested meals, Warm barley or mung bean tea, Gentle rest
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Summer heat is a blazing yang pathogen, but when the air is humid, it often mixes with dampness. The external dampness blocks the skin pores, trapping the heat inside, while the heat forces moisture to accumulate in the body. This double burden weighs down the limbs, smothers the appetite, and creates a sticky, heavy feeling that ordinary sweating cannot relieve.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red body with a white, greasy (sticky) coating - may turn yellow and greasy if heat intensifies. Pulse: soggy (Ru) and rapid (Shu), like pressing a wet cotton ball.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Dampness is heavy and sticky; it smothers the Spleen and traps heat. Cold drinks and raw foods further damage Spleen Yang, making it even harder to transform dampness. A dry, cool environment and light cooked foods support the Spleen’s ability to drain dampness, while gentle rest conserves Qi so the body can clear the lingering summer heat.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat light, cooked foods like congee, mung bean soup, and lightly steamed vegetables. Avoid dairy, fried foods, and sugar, which create more dampness. Sip warm water or mild barley tea; avoid ice-cold drinks that shock the Spleen. Rest in a cool, dry place with a fan - but not directly in an air-conditioning draft.

### Qi and Yin Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/qi-and-yin-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Fatigue and lack of strength, Dry mouth with little desire to drink, Spontaneous sweating, Night sweats, Lingering low-grade fever, Shortness of breath
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Prolonged sun exposure, Strenuous physical activity in hot weather, Excessive sweating, Heavy, greasy, or spicy foods, Emotional stress or agitation
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Rest in a cool, shaded area, Eating watermelon or cucumber, Light congee, Small frequent sips of water
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Summer heat is a Yang pathogen that easily consumes Qi and Yin. When exposure is prolonged or the body's defenses are weak, heat gradually depletes the vital energy and fluids, leaving you exhausted, dry, and unable to cool down. This is why heat stroke can linger as a low-grade fever with fatigue rather than a sudden high fever.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red or pale-red body, thin or absent coating, possibly cracked and dry. Pulse: fine (thin) and weak, possibly rapid.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Heat and exertion further consume Qi and Yin, deepening the deficiency. Rest and cooling fluids help replenish what was lost, while moistening foods rebuild Yin and clear lingering heat without taxing the digestion.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Rest in a cool, shaded environment and avoid further heat exposure. Sip small amounts of room-temperature water or herbal teas like chrysanthemum throughout the day. Eat light, moistening foods such as watermelon, cucumber, pear, and rice congee. Avoid spicy, fried, or overly sweet foods that can further dry out your fluids.

### Heat in Pericardium
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/heat-in-pericardium)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Loss of consciousness or delirium, Cold hands and feet despite high fever, Crimson, stiff tongue, Fine and rapid pulse, High fever worse at night, Incoherent speech or inability to speak
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Prolonged sun exposure, Heavy, greasy, or spicy foods, Emotional stress or agitation, Strenuous physical activity in hot weather
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Cool environment, Eating watermelon or cucumber, Complete rest, Acupuncture on Pericardium channel points
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When summer heat is not cleared in time, it can penetrate beyond the body's outer defenses and invade the Pericardium, the protective sac around the Heart that houses the mind (Shen). This intense heat disturbs the Shen, leading to confusion, delirium, and loss of consciousness. At this deep Nutritive (Ying) level, the heat consumes Yin fluids, causing a crimson stiff tongue and a fine rapid pulse, while the body's extremities may paradoxically feel cold because the heat is trapped internally.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: crimson (deep red) body, stiff or shortened, with a yellow dry coating or little coating. Pulse: fine (xi) and rapid (shu).
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: This pattern arises when summer heat penetrates deep into the Nutritive level and the Pericardium, trapping heat internally and disturbing the mind. Cooling measures and rest help dissipate that trapped heat and preserve Yin fluids, while acupuncture on the Heart and Pericardium channels directly clears the organ-level heat. Conversely, any additional heat exposure, spicy foods, or emotional agitation adds more fire, worsening the mental disturbance and fluid depletion.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Move to a cool, shaded, or air-conditioned environment immediately. Sip cooling fluids like watermelon juice, mung bean soup, or chrysanthemum tea to replenish Yin and clear Heat. Avoid spicy, greasy, or hot-natured foods that add internal Heat. Rest completely until the fever subsides and mental clarity returns.

### Heat victorious stirring Wind
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/heat-victorious-stirring-wind)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Violent convulsions or seizures, Stiffness and rigidity of the neck and limbs, Eyes rolling upward or fixed gaze, Loss of consciousness or delirium, High fever that does not break
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Prolonged sun exposure, Strenuous physical activity in hot weather, Dehydration, Wearing heavy, non-breathable clothing
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Cool environment, Rest in a cool, shaded area, Cooling the skin with a damp cloth or compresses, Drinking cool water or electrolyte drinks
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When summer heat is extreme and the body's defenses are overwhelmed, the heat penetrates deep into the Blood level. It scorches the body's Yin fluids, making the sinews and vessels dry and brittle. This extreme Heat then generates internal Wind, which erupts like a violent storm, causing the muscles to convulse, the neck and limbs to stiffen, and consciousness to be lost.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: deep red or crimson, stiff and dry, possibly with prickles, yellow dry coating. Pulse: wiry and rapid, forceful.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Extreme heat is the root cause, driving pathogenic Fire deep into the Blood and generating internal Wind. Cooling the body, resting, and hydrating help clear that Heat, preserve Yin fluids, and prevent the Wind from flaring up. Any further heat stress or dehydration will only intensify the internal Fire and worsen convulsions.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: This is a medical emergency; immediate cooling and hospitalization are critical. While waiting for help, move to shade, remove excess clothing, and apply cool water to the skin. After recovery, avoid heat exposure and drink plenty of cooling fluids like watermelon juice or mung bean soup to replenish Yin.

## คำถามที่พบบ่อย (Frequently Asked Questions)
### Can TCM treat heat stroke at home?
For mild heat exhaustion with symptoms like heavy sweating, fatigue, and thirst but a clear mind, TCM cooling herbs and rest can be very effective. However, if there is any confusion, high fever that won't come down, or fainting, this is a medical emergency and you must go to the hospital immediately. TCM can be used alongside hospital care for recovery.

### What is the best herb for heat stroke?
There is no single best herb for everyone, because the right herb depends on your pattern. For intense thirst and high fever, Shi Gao (gypsum) is a powerful cooling mineral. For exhaustion and fluid loss, Xi Yang Shen (American ginseng) and Mai Dong (ophiopogon) nourish Qi and Yin. Watermelon (Xi Gua) is a food that acts like a cooling herb-its rind is actually used in the formula Qing Shu Yi Qi Tang. A TCM practitioner will combine herbs specifically for your presentation.

### How quickly can acupuncture help with heat stroke symptoms?
Acupuncture can bring rapid relief for headaches, nausea, and muscle cramps associated with heat stroke. Many people feel a drop in the sensation of internal heat and a calming of the mind within minutes of treatment. Points like Dazhui (DU-14) and Quchi (LI-11) are used to release heat from the body. However, severe heat stroke with organ involvement requires emergency medical care, not acupuncture alone.

### Can I use TCM to prevent heat stroke?
Yes. TCM can strengthen your body's ability to handle summer heat. If you tend to get heat exhaustion easily, a practitioner may give you a mild formula to take during hot weather that boosts Qi and generates fluids. Simple dietary habits-like eating watermelon, avoiding heavy meals, and drinking chrysanthemum tea-also help. Acupuncture can be used preventively to balance your system before the summer season.

### What should I eat after recovering from heat stroke?
Stick to light, easy-to-digest foods for a few days: congee, steamed vegetables, and cooling fruits like pear and watermelon. Avoid fried, spicy, and greasy foods that can rekindle internal heat. Gradually reintroduce normal foods as your appetite and energy return. If you experience ongoing fatigue, a practitioner may recommend adding small amounts of nourishing foods like Chinese yam or lotus seed to your congee.

### Is it safe to take TCM herbs with Western fever reducers?
Generally, cooling TCM herbs do not interact dangerously with over-the-counter fever reducers like acetaminophen or ibuprofen. However, some herbs can have mild blood-thinning or diuretic effects, so if you are on blood pressure medications, blood thinners, or have kidney issues, inform both your doctor and TCM practitioner. Always maintain a gap of at least 1-2 hours between taking herbs and Western medications to minimize any potential interaction.
