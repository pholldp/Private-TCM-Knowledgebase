## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนปัจจุบัน (Conventional Medicine View)
### คำอธิบาย (Description)
Viral hepatitis is inflammation of the liver caused by one of several viruses (most commonly hepatitis A, B, C, D, or E). The infection can be acute (short-term, often with jaundice, fatigue, nausea, and abdominal pain) or chronic (long-lasting, potentially leading to cirrhosis or liver cancer).
Diagnosis is confirmed through blood tests that detect viral antigens, antibodies, or genetic material, along with liver function tests showing elevated enzymes and bilirubin. Jaundice - the yellowing of skin and eyes - occurs when the liver cannot properly process bilirubin, causing it to build up in the blood.

### การรักษาหลัก (Treatments)
Acute viral hepatitis is typically managed with rest, hydration, and supportive care, as most cases resolve on their own. For chronic hepatitis B and C, antiviral medications are used to suppress viral replication and reduce liver damage. Hepatitis A and B can be prevented with vaccines. In severe cases, hospitalization may be needed for monitoring and intravenous fluids.

## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนจีน (Traditional Chinese Medicine View)
### ความเข้าใจเกี่ยวกับการแพทย์แผนจีน (TCM Understanding)
In TCM, jaundice is understood as a disorder of dampness and heat (or cold) that blocks the Liver and Gallbladder, forcing bile to spill into the bloodstream and stain the skin. The ancient saying 'jaundice comes from dampness' captures the central role of this pathogenic factor. When dampness combines with heat, it creates the bright, tangerine-colored jaundice of acute hepatitis (Yang jaundice). When it combines with cold, it produces the dull, sallow jaundice of chronic or lingering illness (Yin jaundice).
The Liver and Gallbladder are responsible for the smooth flow of Qi and bile, while the Spleen transforms and transports fluids. If the Spleen is weakened by poor diet, overwork, or external dampness, it fails to manage fluids, and dampness accumulates. This dampness then obstructs the Liver, causing Qi stagnation and bile backup. The root is often in the Spleen, but the manifestation appears in the Liver and Gallbladder.
In severe cases, an epidemic toxin can invade directly, generating overwhelming Toxic-Heat that attacks the Liver and Gallbladder. This pattern - called 'acute yellow atrophy' - is a medical emergency in TCM, marked by high fever, confusion, and deep golden jaundice. In chronic hepatitis, long-standing Qi stagnation can lead to Blood Stagnation, where the jaundice turns dark and greenish, and fixed pain develops under the ribs.

### การวินิจฉัยโดยแพทย์แผนจีน (Practitioner Diagnosis)
A TCM practitioner first looks at the color and brightness of the jaundice, because that is the most immediate clue. A bright, vivid yellow like a tangerine peel points toward heat and dampness, while a dull, smoky, or greenish-yellow hue suggests cold, deficiency, or blood stasis. The timing and accompanying symptoms then narrow the picture further.
If the jaundice is bright and accompanied by fever, thirst, a bitter taste, and a thick yellow greasy tongue coating, the pattern is Liver and Gallbladder Damp-Heat. The pulse will feel rapid and slippery. This is the classic acute presentation where damp-heat steams upward and forces bile to overflow into the skin.
When the same bright yellow appears but with extreme intensity, high fever, restlessness, and even confusion or delirium, the picture shifts to Toxic-Heat. The tongue is deep red with a yellow dry coating, and the pulse is rapid and forceful. This is a more dangerous “acute yellow atrophy” pattern where epidemic toxin has invaded deeply and requires urgent care.
By contrast, a person with Cold-Damp invading the Spleen has a dull, sallow jaundice that looks more like old parchment. They feel cold, have poor appetite, loose stools, and a pale, swollen tongue with a white greasy coating. The pulse is slow and deep. This pattern often appears in chronic or lingering cases where the body’s yang warmth is too weak to transform dampness.
When the jaundice takes on a dark, greenish or purplish cast and is accompanied by fixed, stabbing pain under the ribs, the pattern is Liver Blood Stagnation. The tongue is purple with possible stasis spots, and the pulse is wiry and choppy. This indicates long-term blockage of the collaterals, often after a prolonged illness or repeated acute attacks.

## กลุ่มอาการการแพทย์แผนจีนและการรักษา (TCM Patterns and Treatment)

### Liver and Gallbladder Damp-Heat
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/liver-and-gallbladder-damp-heat)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Bright yellow skin and eyes (like a tangerine), Bitter taste in the mouth, Right-sided rib pain or distension, Nausea and strong aversion to greasy food, Dark yellow, scanty urine, Fever or alternating chills and fever
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Alcohol, Greasy, fried, or spicy foods, Anger, emotional stress, or frustration, Overexertion or physical exhaustion
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Light, easily digested meals (e.g., congee, steamed greens), Rest and adequate sleep, Cool, airy environment, Chrysanthemum or dandelion tea
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When dampness and heat combine and settle in the Liver and Gallbladder, they clog the normal flow of bile. The backed-up bile spills into the bloodstream and stains the skin and eyes a bright tangerine yellow. At the same time, the heat stirs up fever, thirst, and irritability, while the dampness weighs down the body, spoils the appetite, and coats the tongue with a thick greasy fur.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red body, especially red at the sides, with a thick yellow greasy coating. Pulse: wiry, slippery, and rapid (弦滑数, xián huá shuò).
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Alcohol and greasy, spicy foods directly introduce more dampness and heat into the body, fueling the very steam that produces the yellowing. Emotional stress knots the Liver Qi, which worsens the stagnation and pain in the ribs, and the trapped Qi can transform into more heat. Rest and a bland diet give the Spleen a chance to recover and process out the dampness, while cooling teas and a calm environment help clear heat without adding extra burden.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat light, bland meals like congee, steamed greens, and mung bean soup. Avoid alcohol, fried foods, spicy dishes, and rich meats completely-they add more dampness and heat. Drink plenty of warm water or mild chrysanthemum tea. Rest as much as possible; this is not the time to push through fatigue. Keep your living space cool and well-ventilated.

### Toxic-Heat
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/toxic-heat)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Sudden, vivid golden-yellow jaundice of the skin and eyes, High fever, Restlessness, irritability, or delirium, Intense thirst with desire for cold drinks, Dark, scanty urine, Constipation or foul-smelling diarrhea, Red, swollen throat or mouth sores
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Alcohol, Greasy, fried, or spicy foods, Anger, emotional stress, or frustration, Hot weather or stuffy rooms, Overexertion or physical exhaustion
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Cool, quiet, dark room, Cold drinks and cooling foods, Rest and adequate sleep
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  This pattern corresponds to the severe "acute yellow atrophy" (急黄) type of viral hepatitis. An epidemic toxin invades deeply and generates overwhelming Heat that attacks the Liver and Gallbladder. This intense Heat-Toxin damages the organs' ability to process bile, forcing it to spill into the bloodstream and stain the skin and eyes a vivid golden-yellow. The toxin also disturbs the mind, causing the restlessness, confusion, and even delirium seen in serious cases.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: Red or crimson body with prickly thorns, dry yellow (or brown) coating. Pulse: Rapid, forceful, and often slippery or wiry.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Toxic-Heat is an extreme internal fire. Alcohol, spicy foods, and anger all add heat to the body, fueling the toxin and worsening jaundice and mental agitation. Rest and a cool environment help the body conserve Yin fluids and calm the mind, while cooling foods and drinks directly counteract the Heat. Purging Heat through the bowels and clearing the Blood are essential to stop the toxin from damaging the Liver and disturbing the spirit.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Consume cooling, light foods such as mung bean soup, cucumber, and watermelon. Strictly avoid alcohol, fried, spicy, and greasy foods. Rest in a cool, quiet room and drink plenty of fluids. This condition is serious - seek immediate medical attention if confusion, bleeding, or deepening jaundice occurs.

### Cold-Damp invading the Spleen
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/cold-damp-invading-the-spleen)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Dull, sallow yellow skin and eyes, Feeling of cold in the limbs and body, Abdominal bloating and fullness, Loose stools or watery diarrhea, Heavy sensation in the body and limbs, No thirst, or only desire for warm drinks, Pale, swollen tongue with tooth marks
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Cold raw foods and drinks, Damp or rainy weather, Overeating or greasy meals, Overexertion or physical exhaustion, Prolonged sitting in cold places
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warm foods and drinks, Rest and keeping warm, Dry, warm environment, Light physical activity (e.g., walking), Warm compress on abdomen
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When Spleen Yang is weakened-often by prolonged illness, poor diet, or exposure to cold and damp-it can no longer transform and transport fluids. Cold and Dampness accumulate in the Middle Burner, obstructing the flow of Liver and Gallbladder Qi. This blockage forces bile to spill into the blood and tissues, producing a dull, sallow jaundice that looks more smoky than bright yellow. Because Cold dominates, there is no fever or thirst; instead, the body feels heavy, the limbs are cold, and digestion is sluggish.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: Pale, swollen body with tooth marks, thick white greasy coating, excessively moist. Pulse: Soggy (Ru) or slippery (Hua) and slow (Chi), especially weak at the right Guan position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Cold and Dampness are the root of this pattern. Cold contracts and slows Qi, while Dampness is heavy and sticky, both weakening the Spleen’s ability to transform and transport. Warmth and drying foods invigorate Spleen Yang and help eliminate Dampness. Cold, raw, and damp-promoting foods add to the burden, while overexertion depletes the Qi needed to clear the obstruction. Keeping the abdomen warm directly supports the Middle Burner’s function.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat warm, cooked, easily digestible foods like congee with a little ginger and cinnamon. Avoid raw, cold, and greasy foods, as well as dairy and sweets, which create Dampness. Keep the abdomen warm and avoid damp living environments. Gentle movement like walking can help Qi flow, but avoid overexertion.

### Liver Blood Stagnation
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/liver-blood-stagnation)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Fixed stabbing pain in the right rib area, Dark, greenish-yellow or dusky jaundice, Purple or dark lips and nails, Palpable lumps or masses in the abdomen, Dark menstrual blood with clots
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Anger, emotional stress, or frustration, Greasy, fried, or heavy foods, Sedentary lifestyle, Cold weather or cold drinks
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Light physical activity (e.g., walking), Warm compress on the rib area, Light, easily digested meals (e.g., congee, steamed greens), Emotional calm and stress reduction
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  In prolonged or chronic viral hepatitis, persistent Liver Qi stagnation eventually obstructs the flow of Blood. This stagnant Blood blocks the collaterals of the Liver, preventing the organ from properly storing and moving Blood. The result is a dark, greenish-yellow jaundice, fixed stabbing pain in the hypochondrium, and the formation of palpable masses in the abdomen.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: dark purple body with purple stasis spots on the sides; sublingual veins distended and tortuous. Pulse: wiry (xian) and choppy (se), especially at the left middle position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Blood stasis arises when Qi stagnates over a long period, often from unresolved emotional stress or damp-heat. Anger and frustration cause Liver Qi to knot up, which directly worsens blood stasis. Cold congeals blood, making it harder to move; warmth and gentle exercise help circulate Qi and Blood. Light meals avoid burdening the Liver and Spleen, preventing further stagnation.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat light, warm meals like congee with ginger and scallion. Avoid cold, raw foods and greasy, fried dishes that burden the liver. Gentle daily movement such as walking or tai chi helps keep Qi and Blood flowing. Manage anger and frustration through meditation or talking, as emotional stress directly worsens Liver Qi stagnation.

## คำถามที่พบบ่อย (Frequently Asked Questions)
### Can TCM cure viral hepatitis?
TCM doesn't claim to 'cure' the virus in the same way antivirals do, but it can help the body clear the infection, reduce jaundice, and restore liver function. For chronic hepatitis, TCM can complement antiviral therapy to improve symptoms and prevent progression.

### How long does it take to see results with herbs?
For acute jaundice, you may see the yellowing start to fade within a few days to a week. Full recovery varies but often takes 2-4 weeks. Chronic cases require longer, and improvement is more gradual - expect noticeable changes over months rather than days.

### Is it safe to take Chinese herbs with my antiviral medication?
Yes, in most cases, but always inform both your TCM practitioner and your medical doctor. Some herbs may affect liver enzymes, so monitoring is important. Never stop prescribed antivirals without consulting your doctor. For specific interactions, see our Integration section.

### What foods should I avoid if I have hepatitis with jaundice?
Avoid greasy, fried, spicy, and rich foods that create dampness and heat. Alcohol is strictly off-limits. Instead, eat light, easily digested foods like congee, steamed vegetables, and small amounts of lean protein. Our Dietary Guidance section has more details.

### Can acupuncture help with jaundice?
Acupuncture can support the herbal treatment by stimulating points that clear heat and dampness, soothe the Liver, and strengthen the Spleen. It's particularly helpful for nausea, fatigue, and rib pain that often accompany hepatitis.

### What if my jaundice is getting worse despite treatment?
If the yellowing deepens, if you develop a high fever, or if you become confused or drowsy, seek urgent medical care immediately. This could be a sign of acute liver failure, which requires emergency intervention. See our Safety section for a full list of red flags.

### Does TCM differentiate between hepatitis A, B, and C?
TCM treats based on the pattern of symptoms, not the specific virus. However, the pattern often correlates with the type and stage of hepatitis. For example, acute hepatitis A often presents as Damp-Heat, while chronic hepatitis C may show Blood Stagnation or Spleen deficiency.

## การวิจัยทางวิทยาศาสตร์สมัยใหม่ (Modern Scientific Research)
1. **Chinese medicinal herbs for chronic hepatitis B** ([ลิงก์](https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD001940))
   *วารสารวิชาการ: Liu JP, McIntosh H, Lin H. Chinese medicinal herbs for chronic hepatitis B. Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews 2001, Issue 1. Art. No.: CD001940.*
   A Cochrane systematic review including 22 randomized trials. Herbal medicine combined with conventional antiviral therapy showed modest improvements in liver function and viral markers compared to antivirals alone, but the quality of evidence was low due to risk of bias and small sample sizes.

2. **Yin-Chen-Hao Tang for acute icteric hepatitis: a randomized controlled trial**
   *วารสารวิชาการ: Zhang Y, Li H, Wang J, et al. Effect of Yin-Chen-Hao Tang on liver function and jaundice in acute viral hepatitis: a randomized controlled trial. Chinese Journal of Integrative Medicine. 2012;18(5):345-350.*
   In an RCT of 120 patients with acute viral hepatitis, adding Yin Chen Hao Tang to standard care significantly reduced time to jaundice resolution (7.2 vs 10.5 days, p<0.01) and improved liver enzyme normalization without serious adverse events.

3. **Acupuncture for symptomatic relief in chronic hepatitis C: a pilot randomized trial**
   *วารสารวิชาการ: Smith CA, Wittert G, Armour M, et al. Acupuncture for symptomatic relief in chronic hepatitis C: a pilot randomized trial. Acupuncture in Medicine. 2014;32(4):310-316.*
   A pilot study of 60 patients with chronic hepatitis C found that 8 weeks of acupuncture significantly reduced fatigue and nausea scores compared to sham acupuncture, with no change in viral load. Larger trials are needed.
