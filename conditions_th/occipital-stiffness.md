## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนปัจจุบัน (Conventional Medicine View)
### คำอธิบาย (Description)
In conventional medicine, occipital stiffness is typically understood as tension or spasm in the muscles at the base of the skull, often linked to poor posture, prolonged screen time, stress, or cervical spine disorders like arthritis or disc problems. Diagnosis is based on physical examination, and imaging (X-ray, MRI) may be used if nerve compression or structural damage is suspected. It is important to distinguish common muscle stiffness from nuchal rigidity, a sudden, severe stiff neck with fever that can indicate meningitis and requires emergency care.

### การรักษาหลัก (Treatments)
Standard treatment includes rest, heat or ice therapy, over-the-counter pain relievers (NSAIDs, acetaminophen), muscle relaxants, and physical therapy to improve posture and strengthen neck muscles. In persistent cases, trigger point injections or corticosteroid injections may be considered. Lifestyle modifications like ergonomic adjustments and stress management are also recommended.

## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนจีน (Traditional Chinese Medicine View)
### ความเข้าใจเกี่ยวกับการแพทย์แผนจีน (TCM Understanding)
TCM views the back of the neck as a crossroads where the body's defensive energy meets the outside world. The Greater Yang (Taiyang) channels—the Bladder and Small Intestine meridians—run along the nape and are the first line of defense against external pathogens like Wind, Cold, and Dampness. When these invaders strike, they obstruct the flow of Qi and blood, causing sudden stiffness, tightness, and pain. This is why a draft or a change in weather can trigger an acute episode: your body's protective Qi is battling an invader at the gate.
But not all occipital stiffness comes from the outside. Internal imbalances can also clog the channels. A previous neck injury, years of poor posture, or chronic stress can lead to Blood Stagnation—a kind of local traffic jam in the vessels that nourish the muscles. Similarly, when the Spleen fails to process fluids properly, it can generate Phlegm-Dampness, a sticky, obstructive substance that lodges in the neck, creating a heavy, stubborn stiffness that feels like something is stuck.
Finally, the deepest reserves of the body—the Kidney and Liver Yin—are responsible for moistening and nourishing the sinews. As we age or after prolonged overwork, these reserves can run low, leaving the neck dry, tight, and chronically uncomfortable.
This type of stiffness tends to creep in gradually and worsens with fatigue, reflecting an internal drought rather than an external attack. That’s why the same Western diagnosis of occipital stiffness might require warming herbs to expel Cold, blood-moving herbs to clear stasis, or nourishing herbs to replenish Yin—each pattern demands a fundamentally different strategy.

### การวินิจฉัยโดยแพทย์แผนจีน (Practitioner Diagnosis)
A TCM practitioner begins by asking when the stiffness started and what brings it on. Sudden onset after exposure to wind or cold points toward an external invasion, while gradual, lingering stiffness suggests an internal imbalance or chronic strain.
If the neck is stiff with sweating and a mild aversion to wind, the pattern is likely Greater Yang Attack of Wind. The pulse is often floating and moderate, and the tongue may have a thin white coat. This is a classic exterior deficiency pattern where the body’s protective Qi is slightly compromised.
When the stiffness is severe, with no sweating and a marked aversion to cold, it points to Greater Yang Attack of Cold. The pulse is floating and tight, and the tongue coat is thin and white. This is an exterior excess pattern where Cold firmly obstructs the channels.
A history of chronic neck issues that flare in cold or damp weather suggests Painful Obstruction with Wind-Cold-Damp. The stiffness is often heavy and aching, and the tongue may have a white greasy coat. The pulse is typically slow or slippery, reflecting the lingering dampness.
If the stiffness follows a specific injury, poor posture, or repetitive strain, Blood Stagnation is likely. The pain is fixed and stabbing, and the tongue may show purple spots. The pulse is often choppy, signaling that blood is not moving smoothly through the neck channels.
When the neck feels heavy and numb, as if something is stuck, Phlegm in the Channels may be the cause. The tongue is swollen with a greasy coat, and the pulse is slippery. This pattern often accompanies a feeling of foggy headedness.
A dull, persistent stiffness that worsens with fatigue and is accompanied by dizziness, tinnitus, or a dry mouth points to Kidney and Liver Yin Deficiency. The tongue is red with little coat, and the pulse is thin and rapid. This is a deeper, long-term pattern where the body’s nourishing Yin is depleted.

### Greater Yang Attack of Wind
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/greater-yang-attack-of-wind)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Stiffness and pain at the back of the neck, Mild sweating with strong aversion to wind and drafts, Mild chills and low-grade fever, Nasal congestion with clear runny discharge, Floating and moderate (relaxed) pulse
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Exposure to cold wind or drafts, Overexertion or overwork, Eating cold or raw foods, Emotional stress or frustration
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warm ginger tea, Rest and relaxation, Massage or acupressure, Applying warmth to the neck, Light, warm congee
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When Wind and Cold invade from the outside, they first attack the Greater Yang (Taiyang) channels that run along the back of the neck and head. Because the defensive Qi is slightly weak in this pattern, the pores fail to close tightly, leading to mild sweating, while the pathogen obstructs the flow of Qi and blood in the neck, causing stiffness and pain.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: normal body colour with a thin white coat that may be slightly moist. Pulse: floating (Fu) and moderate/relaxed (Huan).
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Wind and Cold are the direct cause of this pattern; they invade the body's surface and block the Greater Yang channels that run through the neck. Anything that warms and protects the neck helps the defensive Qi push the pathogen out, while cold drafts drive it deeper. Resting allows the body to focus its Qi on fighting the external invasion, and gentle warmth relaxes the tightened channels. Warm drinks like ginger tea support the exterior-releasing action of the body, while cold foods and overexertion further deplete the defensive Qi, making symptoms worse.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Keep the neck covered and avoid wind drafts. Drink warm ginger tea to help the body expel the cold. Rest and avoid strenuous activity until the sweating and stiffness subside. Eat easily digestible, warm foods like congee.

### Greater Yang Attack of Cold
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/greater-yang-attack-of-cold)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Sudden onset of severe, board-like neck stiffness, No sweating at all, even with fever, Strong chills and shivering, worse with cold, Widespread body aches, especially back and neck, Thin white tongue coating
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Exposure to cold wind or drafts, Eating cold or raw foods, Lack of sweating, Overexertion or overwork
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Applying warmth to the neck, A mild sweat, Warm ginger tea, Rest and relaxation
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  The Greater Yang (Taiyang) channels run along the back of the neck and head. When a strong external Cold invades the body, it directly attacks these channels, causing the defensive Qi to contract and the pores to close tightly. This blocks the flow of Qi and blood in the neck, leading to sudden, severe stiffness and pain that feels like a board, without any sweating to release the pathogen.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: normal body color, thin white moist coating. Pulse: floating and tight (浮紧 fú jǐn), tight across all positions.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Cold contracts and obstructs the channels, sealing the pores and trapping the pathogen, which directly stiffens the neck. Warmth, ginger, and rest help the body's defensive Qi to push outward, opening the pores and allowing a sweat to release the cold, thus relaxing the neck and easing pain.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Keep the neck warmly covered, especially after exposure to cold or wind. Drink hot ginger and scallion tea to promote a mild sweat and help release the pathogen. Avoid cold drinks and raw foods, which can worsen the cold obstruction. Rest and avoid drafts until sweating occurs and symptoms ease.

### Painful Obstruction with Wind-Cold-Damp
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/painful-obstruction-with-wind-cold-damp)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Aching, heavy pain in the neck and shoulders, Stiffness worsens in cold, damp, or rainy weather, Heaviness or numbness in the neck and upper back, Limited range of motion, worse after rest and better with gentle movement
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Cold and damp weather, Exposure to cold wind or drafts, Prolonged sitting or inactivity, Eating cold or raw foods
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Applying warmth to the neck, Gentle stretching or movement, Warm, dry weather
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Wind, Cold, and Dampness are external evils that can invade when the body's defenses are low. They lodge in the channels of the neck, where Cold contracts the muscles and sinews, causing tight stiffness, while Dampness brings heaviness and swelling. Together they block the smooth flow of Qi and blood, creating a chronic, weather-sensitive stiff neck that feels better with warmth and worse on cold, damp days.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale, slightly swollen with teeth marks, white greasy coating, moist surface. Pulse: wiry (弦 xián) and tight (紧 jǐn), or soggy (濡 rú) if Dampness predominates.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Cold and Dampness are the primary pathogens in this pattern. Cold contracts and tightens the channels, while Dampness is heavy and sticky, obstructing the flow of Qi and blood. Warmth and movement help to disperse Cold, dry Dampness, and promote circulation, which relieves stiffness. Cold, damp environments and foods introduce more of these pathogens, worsening the condition.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Keep the neck warm with a scarf, especially in damp or windy weather. Avoid cold, raw foods and icy drinks that burden the Spleen and create Dampness. Gentle daily stretching and movement like tai chi or walking help keep Qi and blood flowing. Dry, warm environments are beneficial.

### Blood Stagnation
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/blood-stagnation)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Fixed, stabbing pain in the neck, Stiffness that worsens at night, Dark or purplish complexion, lips, or nails, Pain that feels worse with pressure, History of neck injury or repetitive strain
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Cold and damp weather, Prolonged sitting or inactivity, Repetitive strain or injury, Emotional stress or frustration
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Applying warmth to the neck, Gentle stretching or movement, Massage or acupressure, Adequate sleep
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When the neck is injured, overused, or held in one position for too long, the local circulation of Qi and Blood can become stagnant - much like a traffic jam in the narrow vessels of the nape. This stagnation creates a fixed, stabbing pain and a persistent stiffness that feels as though the muscles are locked. Because Blood is a Yin substance, the discomfort often worsens at night or during rest when movement slows, allowing the stasis to settle in.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: purple or dusky body, often with dark stasis spots; distended, dark sublingual veins. Pulse: choppy (涩, sè) like a knife scraping bamboo, sometimes wiry (弦, xián) if Qi stagnation is also present.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Blood Stagnation is a state of congealed, sluggish flow. Cold constricts the vessels and thickens the blood, making stasis worse, while warmth and gentle movement dilate the channels and encourage fresh Blood to push through. Stress and emotional tension cause Qi to knot, which in turn worsens Blood stasis, so calming the mind and relaxing the neck muscles helps restore circulation.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Keep the neck warm and avoid exposure to cold drafts. Gentle daily movement - like slow neck rolls or walking - helps keep Blood flowing. Include moderate amounts of spices such as turmeric, ginger, and a little black pepper in your food; they gently warm the channels. Avoid prolonged sitting or looking down at screens without breaks.

### Phlegm in the Channels joints and muscles
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/phlegm-in-the-channels-joints-and-muscles)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Heavy, numb sensation in the neck, Sensation of a lump or obstruction in the neck, Puffy eyelids or a feeling of chest tightness, Excessive phlegm or mucus, Greasy white coating on the tongue
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Damp, rainy weather, Greasy, sweet, or dairy-rich foods, Prolonged sitting or inactivity, Cold and damp weather
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warm, dry weather, Gentle stretching or movement, Light, non-greasy foods, Applying warmth to the neck
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Phlegm-Dampness is a thick, sticky substance that forms when the Spleen fails to transform fluids properly. When this phlegm lodges in the channels and collaterals of the neck, it blocks the free flow of Qi and blood, creating a heavy, numb stiffness that feels like something is stuck. Over time, the obstruction can lead to blood stasis, causing a fixed, stabbing pain in the neck. This pattern often develops gradually in people with chronic dampness and a sedentary lifestyle, and the stiffness is persistent and difficult to shake off.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: purple body with stasis spots, white greasy coating, distended sublingual veins. Pulse: wiry (弦) and choppy (涩) or wiry and slippery (滑).
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Phlegm-Dampness is heavy and sticky, and it worsens with dampness and rich foods that burden the Spleen. Warmth and movement help Qi circulate, which transforms Dampness and clears the channels, while cold and stillness cause it to congeal further.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Avoid dairy, greasy, and sweet foods that create Dampness and Phlegm. Eat warm, light meals with ginger, garlic, or radish to help transform Phlegm. Gentle daily movement like walking or neck stretches keeps Qi flowing. Keep the neck warm and dry, especially in damp weather.

### Kidney and Liver Yin Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/kidney-and-liver-yin-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Dull, persistent aching stiffness, not sharp or acute, Stiffness worsens with overwork and fatigue, improves with rest, Dizziness and ringing in the ears (tinnitus), Dry eyes and blurred vision, Night sweats and heat in the palms, soles, and chest, Soreness and weakness in the lower back and knees
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Overexertion or overwork, Spicy and fried foods, Late nights, Dry environment, Emotional stress or frustration
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Adequate sleep, Gentle stretching or movement, Moistening foods like pears or broths, Moderate, not hot, temperature, Rest and relaxation
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  The Liver and Kidneys are responsible for nourishing the sinews and bones. When their Yin fluids run low, the tendons and channels in the neck lose their natural moisture and suppleness. This creates a chronic, dull stiffness that feels worse when you are tired and better after gentle movement and rest.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red, thin body with cracks, little or no coating, dry surface. Pulse: fine (thin), rapid, and wiry; weak in the left Chi (Kidney) and Guan (Liver) positions.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Kidney and Liver Yin deficiency means the body's cooling, moistening reserves are low. Overwork, late nights, and stress further consume Yin, while spicy and dry foods create internal heat that dries the tendons. Rest, gentle movement, and nourishing foods help replenish Yin, easing the dull stiffness.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Avoid spicy, fried, and drying foods that deplete Yin. Prioritize rest and avoid overwork, especially late nights. Gentle stretching and moderate exercise like Tai Chi can help maintain flexibility without exhausting Yin. Include moistening foods such as black sesame, goji berries, pears, and bone broths.

## คำถามที่พบบ่อย (Frequently Asked Questions)
### What does a stiff neck mean in Chinese medicine?
It means the flow of Qi and blood through the channels of the neck is blocked. The cause can be external-like an invasion of Wind and Cold that tightens the muscles-or internal, such as old injuries causing Blood Stagnation, or a long-term deficiency of Yin fluids that fails to nourish the sinews. The specific meaning depends on your full symptom picture, which a TCM practitioner will assess through questions, tongue, and pulse diagnosis.

### Can acupuncture really help with a stiff neck at the back of my head?
Yes. Acupuncture points like Fengchi (GB-20) and Dazhui (DU-14) are located right in the occipital region and are highly effective at releasing muscle tension and restoring circulation. Many patients feel immediate relief during the session, and regular treatments can reduce both the frequency and intensity of episodes.

### How long will it take to see results with herbs and acupuncture?
Acute stiffness from a recent cold or draft often improves within 1-2 weeks. Chronic patterns like Blood Stagnation or Wind-Cold-Damp Obstruction typically show meaningful progress in 4-8 weeks. If the root is a deep Yin deficiency, symptom relief may begin within a few weeks, but full rebuilding of the body's reserves can take 3-6 months of consistent care.

### Is it safe to combine TCM with my usual painkillers or muscle relaxants?
Generally yes, but always inform both your TCM practitioner and your doctor about all medications and supplements you are taking. Some blood-moving herbs may have mild blood-thinning effects, so coordination is especially important if you take anticoagulants. Never stop prescribed medication without medical guidance.

### My stiff neck gets worse in cold weather-why?
In TCM, Cold has a contracting nature. If your stiffness is caused by an underlying pattern of Wind-Cold-Damp Obstruction or an old Cold invasion that never fully cleared, cold weather will naturally aggravate the contraction and blockages in the channels. Keeping the neck warm and using warming herbs and foods can make a big difference.

### When should I worry about a stiff neck and go to the hospital instead?
If your stiff neck appears suddenly with a high fever, severe headache, sensitivity to light, or a rash, seek emergency care immediately-these can be signs of meningitis. Also go to the ER if the stiffness follows an injury, or is accompanied by numbness, weakness, or loss of bladder control. For a full list of red flags, see the Safety section above.

## การวิจัยทางวิทยาศาสตร์สมัยใหม่ (Modern Scientific Research)
1. **Acupuncture for neck disorders**
   *วารสารวิชาการ: Trinh K, Graham N, Gross A, et al. Acupuncture for neck disorders. Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews. 2016;(5):CD004870.*
   Cochrane systematic review of 27 trials (n=5,462) evaluating acupuncture for chronic neck pain. Found moderate evidence that acupuncture relieves pain and improves function better than sham acupuncture or no treatment immediately after treatment and at short-term follow-up.

2. **Effectiveness of acupuncture for chronic neck pain: a randomized controlled trial**
   *วารสารวิชาการ: Witt CM, Jena S, Brinkhaus B, et al. Acupuncture for patients with chronic neck pain. Pain. 2006;125(1-2):98-106.*
   Large German trial (n=14,161) comparing acupuncture plus routine care to routine care alone. The acupuncture group had significantly greater improvements in neck pain and disability at 3 months, with benefits sustained at 6 months.
