## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนปัจจุบัน (Conventional Medicine View)
### คำอธิบาย (Description)
Facial paralysis is the sudden weakness or inability to move the muscles on one side of the face. The most common form is Bell’s palsy, thought to arise from inflammation or viral infection of the facial nerve (cranial nerve VII). It typically develops over hours to days, causing drooping of the mouth, difficulty closing the eye, drooling, and sometimes altered taste or hearing. Diagnosis is made by physical examination, and most people recover partially or fully within weeks to months, though some may experience incomplete healing or synkinesis (unwanted muscle movements).

### การรักษาหลัก (Treatments)
Standard care includes corticosteroids such as prednisone to reduce nerve swelling, and possibly antiviral medications if a viral cause is suspected. Protecting the eye is crucial - artificial tears, lubricating ointments, and taping the eyelid shut at night. Physical therapy and facial exercises may be recommended to maintain muscle tone. Most patients are monitored with the expectation of natural recovery, but severe or non-resolving cases may require surgical decompression or nerve grafting.

## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนจีน (Traditional Chinese Medicine View)
### ความเข้าใจเกี่ยวกับการแพทย์แผนจีน (TCM Understanding)
In TCM, acute facial paralysis is primarily seen as an invasion of external Wind, which penetrates the body’s defenses and lodges in the channels of the face - especially the Yangming (Stomach and Large Intestine) and Shaoyang (Gallbladder) channels that run across the cheeks, mouth, and eyes. Wind is a ‘yang’ pathogen that moves quickly and targets the upper body, which is why the face is so vulnerable. Once the channels are blocked, Qi and blood cannot reach the facial muscles, leading to paralysis.
The critical distinction is the nature of the Wind. If it is Wind-Cold, the muscles feel tight and achy, the face looks pale, and the person feels chilly and prefers warmth. If it is Wind-Heat, the face feels hot or swollen, the mouth is dry, and the person may have a sore throat or fever. These two patterns account for the majority of early cases, and the treatment strategy is opposite: warming for Cold, cooling for Heat.
Sometimes the Wind carries phlegm - a sticky, obstructive substance that forms when the Spleen is weak and fails to transform fluids. In Wind-Phlegm, the face feels numb and heavy, there is profuse white phlegm in the throat, and the tongue is swollen with a greasy coating. This pattern often occurs in people who have a tendency toward dampness, and it responds to herbs that both dispel wind and transform phlegm.
When facial paralysis lingers for weeks or months without full recovery, the problem shifts from an external invasion to an internal deficiency. Qi becomes too weak to move blood, and stagnation sets in. The face droops, the muscles feel flaccid, and the complexion turns dusky. This Qi Deficiency with Blood Stagnation pattern requires a different approach - boosting Qi and invigorating blood - and recovery may be slower but still achievable with consistent treatment.

### การวินิจฉัยโดยแพทย์แผนจีน (Practitioner Diagnosis)
To see if Wind-Cold is the culprit, a practitioner will ask whether the facial weakness came on suddenly after exposure to cold or wind, and whether you feel chilled and prefer warmth. The face may feel tight and achy rather than burning, and you might notice a general aversion to drafts. The tongue is pale with a thin white coating, and the pulse feels floating and tight - both confirming an external cold invasion.
If instead the face feels hot, swollen, or tender, and you have a dry throat, thirst, or even a low fever, the pattern leans toward Wind-Heat. The practitioner will ask about recent sore throats or feeling overheated. A red tongue with a yellow coating and a floating, rapid pulse distinguish this from Wind-Cold. The onset is still acute, but the quality of the sensation is fiery rather than chilly.
When the face feels numb or stiff rather than just weak, and you notice a heavy sensation with plenty of phlegm in the throat, Wind-Phlegm is likely. A practitioner will ask about a history of dampness, bloating, or a sluggish feeling. The tongue appears swollen with a thick greasy white coating, and the pulse feels wiry and slippery - both signs that phlegm is obstructing the channels along with wind.
If the facial paralysis has lingered for weeks or months without full recovery, and you also feel deeply tired with a dull, dark complexion, the pattern may have shifted to Qi Deficiency causing Blood Stagnation. The practitioner will ask about long-standing fatigue and whether the facial muscles have become lax or atrophied. A purplish dark tongue with visible stasis spots and a thready, choppy pulse confirm that blood is not moving well.

## กลุ่มอาการการแพทย์แผนจีนและการรักษา (TCM Patterns and Treatment)

### Wind-Cold
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/wind-cold)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Sudden onset of eye and mouth deviation, Facial tightness or pain, Strong aversion to wind and cold, No sweating, Clear watery nasal discharge, No thirst or preference for warm drinks
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Exposure to cold wind or drafts, Cold or raw foods, Washing face with cold water, Overwork
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warmth on the face, Ginger tea, Rest, Gentle facial massage with warm oil, Avoiding cold wind
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Wind-Cold is an external pathogen that invades the body’s surface. When it attacks the face, especially the Yangming and Shaoyang channels, the cold contracts the muscles and blocks the normal flow of Qi and blood. This sudden obstruction causes the eye and mouth to deviate, and the face to feel tight or painful. The cold also produces the characteristic chills, aversion to wind, and absence of sweating.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: normal or slightly pale body with a thin, white, moist coating. Pulse: floating and tight (浮紧, fú jǐn).
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Cold has a contracting and obstructing nature. When Wind-Cold invades the face, it tightens the channels and blocks the flow of Qi and blood, causing paralysis. Warmth relaxes the channels, promotes circulation, and helps expel the pathogen. Warm foods and drinks, like ginger tea, support the body's Yang Qi and aid in driving out cold. Rest conserves Qi, which is essential to fight the external invasion. Avoiding cold wind and cold water prevents further cold from entering and worsening the condition.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Keep the face and neck warmly covered, especially when outdoors or in drafts. Use a warm compress or a gentle heat source on the affected side to relax tight muscles. Sip warm ginger and scallion tea to help dispel cold from the surface. Eat warm, cooked foods like soups and stews, and avoid cold or raw foods and iced drinks. Rest in a calm, draft-free room and avoid overexertion.

### Wind-Heat
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/wind-heat)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Facial burning or swelling, Sore throat, Thirst with a desire to drink, Fever with mild chills, Red, dry eyes
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Exposure to wind or heat, Spicy, greasy, or fried foods, Overwork, Stress
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Cool environment, Rest, Drinking plenty of fluids, Cool compresses on the face
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When an external Wind-Heat pathogen invades, it often travels along the Yangming and Shaoyang channels that run across the face. The combination of Wind and Heat creates local inflammation, swelling, and a burning sensation along the facial nerves. This disrupts the normal flow of Qi and blood, causing the muscles to lose their ability to move, resulting in sudden deviation of the eye and mouth. The Heat also consumes body fluids, leading to dry throat, thirst, and a red tongue.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red at the tip and edges with a thin yellow coating (may be thin white-to-yellow in early stages). Pulse: floating (浮 fú) and rapid (数 shuò).
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Wind-Heat is an external pathogen that invades the body's surface. Heat and wind exposure worsen it because they reinforce the pathogen. Spicy and greasy foods create internal heat, adding to the burden. Rest and cool environments help the body's defensive Qi to expel the pathogen, while fluids cool internal heat and prevent further fluid consumption.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Stay in a cool, well-ventilated space and avoid direct wind or drafts. Drink plenty of water and eat cooling foods like mung beans, cucumber, and pear. Avoid spicy, greasy, and fried foods that create internal heat. Rest is essential to allow the body to clear the pathogen.

### Wind-Phlegm
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/wind-phlegm)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Facial numbness or stiffness, Copious white, frothy sputum, Heavy sensation in the head or body, Chest stuffiness or nausea, Dizziness or vertigo
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Damp or humid weather, Cold or raw foods, Spicy, greasy, or fried foods, Exposure to wind, Sedentary lifestyle
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warm, dry environment, Light, cooked meals, Gentle walking or stretching, Avoiding dairy, Ginger tea
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When the Spleen is weak and fails to transform fluids, internal phlegm-dampness accumulates. If external wind then invades, it can stir up this phlegm and carry it upward to obstruct the facial channels. The result is numbness, stiffness, and deviation of the mouth and eye, often with a heavy sensation and copious phlegm.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: normal or slightly pale body, swollen with teeth marks, white greasy coating. Pulse: wiry and slippery (Xian Hua).
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Phlegm is a damp, heavy pathogen that worsens with cold and dampness, so warm, dry conditions and light, cooked foods help the Spleen transform it. Wind stirs up phlegm and invades the channels when the body is exposed to drafts; keeping the face covered and avoiding wind prevents this. Gentle movement keeps Qi and fluids moving, preventing stagnation from turning into phlegm.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Avoid cold, raw, and greasy foods that create phlegm. Eat warm, cooked meals with ginger and lightly spiced dishes to support Spleen Qi. Protect the face from wind and drafts. Gentle daily movement like walking helps Qi flow and transforms dampness.

### Qi Deficiency causing Blood Stagnation
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/qi-deficiency-causing-blood-stagnation)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Facial muscle laxity or sagging, Dusky or dark facial complexion, Persistent fatigue and lack of strength, Numbness or tingling on the affected side, Fixed, dull pain on the face, worse with pressure
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Overwork, Cold or raw foods, Exposure to cold wind or drafts, Stress
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Gentle walking or stretching, Warm, nourishing soups, Rest, Warmth on the face, Moxibustion on Zusanli
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When the body's Qi is weak from overwork or chronic illness, it loses the strength to push blood through the tiny vessels that feed the facial muscles. Blood slows down and stagnates, leaving the muscles undernourished and flaccid. This is why the face droops persistently and the complexion turns dusky, and why recovery is slow without also rebuilding Qi.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale-dark or pale-purple body with stasis spots, swollen with teeth marks, thin white coat. Pulse: deep, thready or weak, and choppy (涩 sè).
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: This pattern is rooted in Qi deficiency, so overexertion and cold further drain Qi and congeal blood. Rest and gentle movement rebuild Qi, while warmth and nourishing foods support blood circulation. Moxibustion adds external warmth to stimulate the flow of Qi and blood through the facial channels.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat warm, nourishing meals like chicken soup with astragalus root, dates, and ginger. Gentle daily walks and facial exercises help move blood without exhausting Qi. Avoid cold foods and overwork. Moxibustion at home on Zusanli can gently warm and tonify.

## คำถามที่พบบ่อย (Frequently Asked Questions)
### How quickly can acupuncture help my facial paralysis?
In many cases, people begin to notice slight movement returning within 2-3 weeks of starting acupuncture and herbal treatment. The speed depends on the pattern: Wind-Cold and Wind-Heat tend to respond faster than Wind-Phlegm or long-standing deficiency patterns. Consistency matters - attending sessions 2-3 times per week in the acute phase gives the best results.

### Can I combine acupuncture with the steroids my doctor prescribed?
Yes, absolutely. Acupuncture and herbs can safely be used alongside corticosteroids and antiviral medications. In fact, starting TCM right away may support nerve recovery and reduce inflammation. Do not stop your prescribed medication without consulting your doctor. Let both your physician and your acupuncturist know about all treatments you are receiving.

### Do I need to change my diet during treatment?
Diet plays a supportive role. Avoid cold, raw foods and icy drinks, as cold can contract the channels and slow healing. Reduce dairy, greasy, and sweet foods that create phlegm, especially if you have a Wind-Phlegm pattern. Warm soups, congee, and ginger tea are excellent choices to help dispel wind and nourish Qi.

### Is it safe to use herbs while pregnant or breastfeeding?
Many herbs used for facial paralysis are considered safe during pregnancy, but some - particularly strong blood-moving herbs like Chi Shao or Tao Ren - should be used with caution or avoided. Always tell your TCM practitioner if you are pregnant, planning to become pregnant, or breastfeeding. They will adjust the formula to ensure safety for both you and your baby.

### What if my face doesn’t fully recover after months?
If facial weakness persists beyond 3-6 months, TCM still has much to offer. The focus shifts to the Qi Deficiency and Blood Stagnation pattern, using formulas like Bu Yang Huan Wu Tang to boost energy and move blood. Recovery may be gradual, but many people continue to see improvements in muscle tone and symmetry with ongoing acupuncture and herbs. Patience and consistency are key.

### Will I need to do facial exercises?
Gentle facial exercises can complement acupuncture by encouraging muscle re-education. Your TCM practitioner may recommend specific movements like trying to raise the eyebrow, smile, or puff out the cheek. However, avoid forceful or repetitive exercises that cause fatigue or pain. The combination of acupuncture, herbs, and gentle exercises often yields the best results.

### Does acupuncture on the face hurt?
Most people find facial acupuncture surprisingly comfortable. The needles used are extremely thin, and the sensation is often a mild tingling or pressure rather than pain. Points on the face are close to the surface, so the insertion is shallow. Many patients actually feel a pleasant release as tight muscles relax.

### Can stress cause facial paralysis?
In TCM, stress can weaken the body’s defensive Qi, making you more susceptible to external wind invasion. Emotional strain, especially frustration or anger, can stir internal wind and contribute to facial paralysis. While stress alone may not be the direct cause, it often sets the stage. Addressing stress through acupuncture, herbs, and lifestyle is an important part of both treatment and prevention.

## การวิจัยทางวิทยาศาสตร์สมัยใหม่ (Modern Scientific Research)
1. **Acupuncture for Bell's palsy** ([ลิงก์](https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD002914.pub5))
   *วารสารวิชาการ: Chen N, Zhou M, He L, Zhou D, Li N. Acupuncture for Bell's palsy. Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews 2010, Issue 8. Art. No.: CD002914.*
   A Cochrane systematic review evaluating the effectiveness of acupuncture for Bell's palsy. It included randomised controlled trials and concluded that acupuncture may be effective, though the quality of evidence was limited by methodological flaws in the included studies. The review highlighted the need for larger, well-designed trials.

2. **Electroacupuncture combined with Qianzhengsan decoction for the treatment of peripheral facial paralysis: A retrospective study** ([ลิงก์](https://doi.org/10.1097/MD.0000000000030134))
   *วารสารวิชาการ: Li Y, et al. Electroacupuncture combined with Qianzhengsan decoction for the treatment of peripheral facial paralysis: A retrospective study. Medicine (Baltimore). 2022 Sep 2;101(35):e30134.*
   This retrospective study analysed the effects of electroacupuncture combined with the Chinese herbal formula Qianzhengsan (Qiān Zhèng Sǎn) in patients with peripheral facial paralysis. The combination therapy significantly improved facial nerve function scores and recovery rates compared to conventional medication alone, with a good safety profile.
