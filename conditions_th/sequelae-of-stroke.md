## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนปัจจุบัน (Conventional Medicine View)
### คำอธิบาย (Description)
A stroke occurs when blood flow to part of the brain is interrupted, either by a clot (ischemic stroke) or bleeding (hemorrhagic stroke). The resulting brain damage can cause lasting deficits in movement, sensation, speech, and cognition, depending on the area affected. Rehabilitation typically begins soon after the acute event and may continue for months or years.
Conventional post-stroke care focuses on physical therapy to improve mobility and strength, occupational therapy for daily tasks, speech therapy for communication and swallowing, and medications to prevent another stroke. While many patients make significant gains, some continue to struggle with persistent weakness, spasticity, or fatigue.

### การรักษาหลัก (Treatments)
Standard rehabilitation includes physical therapy to retrain movement and balance, occupational therapy to relearn daily activities, and speech-language therapy for aphasia or dysphagia. Medications such as antiplatelets (aspirin, clopidogrel) or anticoagulants (warfarin, DOACs) are prescribed to reduce the risk of another stroke, along with statins and blood pressure drugs. Spasticity may be managed with muscle relaxants or botulinum toxin injections.

## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนจีน (Traditional Chinese Medicine View)
### ความเข้าใจเกี่ยวกับการแพทย์แผนจีน (TCM Understanding)
In TCM, a stroke (中风, zhòng fēng) is seen as a sudden attack of internal Wind, often combined with Phlegm, Fire, or Stasis, that rushes upward and obstructs the brain's orifices and channels.
After the acute crisis, the body is left with damaged channels and collaterals, and a deep underlying deficiency-usually of Qi, Blood, or Yin-that allowed the Wind to arise in the first place. The lingering symptoms are the result of this unresolved blockage and the body's struggle to nourish and move the affected limbs.
The recovery phase is characterized by a mix of deficiency and excess. The most common pattern, Blood and Qi Deficiency with Blood Stagnation, occurs when the body's energy and Blood are too weak to properly circulate, leading to stagnation in the vessels. This produces a limb that feels limp and weak, accompanied by fatigue and a pale or purplish tongue. Treatment must both tonify and invigorate.
Another frequent picture is Empty-Wind agitating in the Interior, where the Yin fluids of the Liver and Kidneys are so depleted that they cannot anchor Yang, and internal Wind stirs up, causing tremors, dizziness, and a red, dry tongue. This pattern often emerges in patients who were already Yin-deficient before the stroke. Phlegm patterns-whether Wind-Phlegm or Phlegm-Heat-add heaviness, numbness, and a greasy tongue coating, reflecting poor fluid metabolism.
Because these patterns rarely appear in isolation, a skilled TCM practitioner will assess the tongue, pulse, and symptom details to identify the dominant imbalance. The same Western diagnosis of post-stroke hemiplegia may be treated very differently: one patient may need heavy doses of Qi-tonifying herbs, while another needs Yin-nourishing and Wind-extinguishing herbs. This pattern differentiation is the cornerstone of effective TCM rehabilitation.

### การวินิจฉัยโดยแพทย์แผนจีน (Practitioner Diagnosis)
A practitioner begins by asking what the lingering symptoms actually feel like and when they began relative to the stroke. The character of the weakness or stiffness, whether the limbs feel heavy or flaccid versus tight and resistant, gives the first clues that point toward one pattern over another.
If the dominant picture is one of profound fatigue, a pale face, and a limb that feels limp and weak rather than tight, the practitioner suspects Qi and Blood deficiency with stasis. The tongue is usually pale or purplish, and the pulse feels weak and rough. This is the most common pattern seen in recovery, where the body lacks the energy to move Blood properly and stagnation results.
When tremors, dizziness, or a sense of internal restlessness are prominent, the practitioner looks for Empty-Wind agitating in the Interior. This pattern arises when Yin fluids are too depleted to anchor Yang. The tongue appears red with little or no coating, and the pulse is thin and rapid, confirming that the root is a Yin deficiency that allows Wind to stir.
If numbness or slurred speech comes with a feeling of chest oppression, a greasy tongue coating, and a slippery pulse, the obstruction is likely Wind-Phlegm. The Phlegm combines with internal Wind to block the channels. Here the tongue body may be swollen with a greasy white coating, distinguishing it from the dry, red tongue of Yin deficiency.
When irritability, a bitter taste, and constipation dominate alongside the motor symptoms, the practitioner considers Liver Yang Rising. The tongue is red with a yellow coating and the pulse feels wiry and forceful. This indicates that Yang is still surging upward, keeping the channels tight and tense.
If the person complains more of lower back and knee soreness, dizziness, and tinnitus than of tension or heat, the root is likely Kidney and Liver Yin Deficiency. The tongue is red with little coating, the pulse is fine and weak, and the limbs feel profoundly unrooted rather than merely stiff.
Finally, when sticky yellow phlegm, bad breath, and constipation accompany the motor signs, the pattern is Phlegm-Heat obstructing the interior. The tongue is red with a thick, yellow, greasy coating, and the pulse is slippery and rapid. This points to a hot, obstructive condition that needs clearing rather than simply tonifying.

### Blood and Qi Deficiency with Blood Stagnation
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/blood-and-qi-deficiency-with-blood-stagnation)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Fixed stabbing pain in the paralyzed or numb area, Unilateral limb weakness and numbness, Pale or sallow complexion with a dusky undertone, Fatigue and shortness of breath that worsen with any exertion, Reluctance to speak due to lack of Qi, Numbness and tingling in the affected side
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Overwork or excessive exertion, Emotional stress, Cold or damp environments, Greasy or fried foods, Prolonged sitting or inactivity
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Rest and adequate sleep, Warmth, Gentle movement or exercise, Warm, light meals
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  After a stroke, the body's Qi and Blood are severely drained, leaving the channels and collaterals undernourished and weak. This deficiency fails to propel blood, allowing it to stagnate and block the vessels that supply the limbs. The result is a combination of profound fatigue and weakness with hemiplegia, numbness, and fixed stabbing pains on the affected side.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale-purple body with possible stasis spots, thin white coat, and distended sublingual veins. Pulse: fine (细 xì) and choppy (涩 sè), like a knife scraping bamboo.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: This pattern is a mix of deficiency and stagnation. Rest and nourishing foods rebuild Qi and Blood, which are the foundation for recovery, while gentle movement and warmth encourage blood to flow through the blocked channels. Overexertion drains the already deficient Qi, leaving the body too weak to move blood; stress stagnates Liver Qi, which in turn worsens blood stasis. Cold and dampness constrict the vessels and impede circulation, while raw or greasy foods weaken the Spleen's ability to produce new Qi and Blood, so avoiding them protects the recovery process.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat warm, easily digestible foods like rice porridge with a little meat or bone broth, and include blood-nourishing ingredients such as red dates, goji berries, and dark leafy greens. Avoid cold, raw, and greasy foods that weaken digestion. Gentle daily movement like Tai Chi or slow walking helps Qi and Blood flow without depleting your reserves. Prioritize rest and sleep to rebuild your vital energy.

### Empty-Wind agitating in the Interior
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/empty-wind-agitating-in-the-interior)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Trembling or twitching of hands and feet, Severe fatigue and listlessness, Dry mouth and throat, Dizziness and blurred vision, Malar flush, Night sweats
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Overwork or excessive exertion, Greasy or fried foods, Emotional stress, Hot environments
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Rest and adequate sleep, Cool environment, Yin-nourishing or cooling foods, Gentle movement or exercise, Hydration
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  After a stroke, the body's Yin fluids can become deeply depleted, especially in the Liver and Kidneys. Without enough Yin to anchor Yang, internal Wind stirs up, causing tremors, twitching, and dizziness. This pattern often emerges when the acute phase has passed but the body remains dry and exhausted.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: crimson, thin, dry, cracked, with little or no coating (mirror-like), may tremble. Pulse: fine (Xi) and rapid (Shu), wiry at the left Guan position, lacks force on deep palpation.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Rest and Yin-nourishing foods help replenish the depleted Yin fluids, cooling the internal environment and calming the stirring Wind. Overexertion, stress, and heating foods further consume Yin and provoke the upward surge of Wind, worsening tremors and fatigue.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat moistening, Yin-nourishing foods such as pears, tofu, congee, and black sesame seeds. Avoid alcohol, coffee, and spicy or fried foods that dry out the body. Prioritize rest and gentle movement like Tai Chi, avoiding overexertion. Keep the living environment cool and humidified.

### Wind-Phlegm
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/wind-phlegm)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Numbness or tingling in the limbs, Slurred or difficult speech, Copious white frothy sputum, Feeling of heaviness in the body, Dizziness with a spinning sensation, Chest stuffiness
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Greasy or fried foods, Cold or damp environments, Emotional stress, Overwork or excessive exertion
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Gentle movement or exercise, Warm, light meals, Warmth, Rest and adequate sleep
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  After a stroke, the Spleen is often weakened, failing to transform fluids, which accumulate into sticky Phlegm. When this Phlegm combines with internal Wind - often stirred by Liver imbalance - it can suddenly obstruct the channels and collaterals. This block disrupts the flow of Qi and Blood to the limbs and tongue, causing numbness, heaviness, slurred speech, and dizziness.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: slightly pale and swollen with teeth marks, white greasy coating. Pulse: wiry and slippery (Xian Hua).
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Dampness and cold congeal fluids into Phlegm, while stress stirs Liver Wind, so avoiding these helps calm the pattern. Gentle movement and warmth keep Qi flowing and prevent Phlegm from stagnating.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat warm, light meals and avoid dairy, fried foods, and cold drinks that create Dampness and Phlegm. Gentle daily movement like walking helps Qi flow and prevents stagnation. Keep the neck and head warm to avoid triggering Wind.

### Liver Yang Rising
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/liver-yang-rising)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Throbbing or distending headache, especially at the temples, Irritability and a quick temper, Dizziness and a sensation of heaviness in the head with unsteady feet, Bitter taste in the mouth and constipation, Flushed face and red eyes
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Emotional stress, Alcohol, Overwork or excessive exertion, Hot environments
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Rest and adequate sleep, Yin-nourishing or cooling foods, Gentle movement or exercise
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  After a stroke, the body's Yin and Blood are often depleted. If the Yin of the Liver and Kidneys is too weak to anchor the Yang, Liver Yang surges upward unchecked. This rising Yang brings heat and agitation to the head, causing the throbbing headaches, dizziness, and irritability that can slow recovery.
The upward rush disturbs the mind and senses, while the underlying Yin deficiency leaves the lower body weak and unsteady. The result is a classic pattern of excess above and deficiency below, which keeps the nervous system in a tense, reactive state.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red body with redder sides, a thin dry yellow coating or scanty coating, and may tremble slightly. Pulse: wiry (弦 xián), rapid (数 shuò), and often forceful, especially at the left middle position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Anger and stress directly fan Liver Yang upward, worsening headache and irritability, so emotional calm is essential. Alcohol and spicy foods add internal heat that feeds the rising Yang, while cooling foods and rest help anchor it. Gentle movement encourages Qi to descend and circulate rather than stagnate and surge.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Avoid alcohol, coffee, spicy foods, and fried or greasy meals, which add heat and stir up Yang. Favor cooling, moistening foods like pear, celery, and mung beans. Keep a calm daily rhythm, wind down before bed, and try gentle movement like walking or tai chi to help Qi flow downward.

### Kidney and Liver Yin Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/kidney-and-liver-yin-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Soreness and weakness of the lower back and knees, Dizziness and ringing in the ears, Dry eyes with blurred vision, Night sweats and heat in palms, soles, and chest, Dry mouth and throat, especially at night
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Overwork or excessive exertion, Greasy or fried foods, Emotional stress, Hot environments
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Rest and adequate sleep, Yin-nourishing or cooling foods, Gentle movement or exercise
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Kidney and Liver Yin are the body’s cooling, nourishing reserves. When they run low, they can no longer anchor the body’s active Yang energy, which allowed internal Wind to rise and trigger the stroke in the first place. After the acute event, this deep Yin deficit persists, leaving the lower back and knees weak and achy, the head dizzy and ringing, and the body subtly overheated at night. It is the root-deficiency that sets the stage for chronic post-stroke fatigue and instability.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red, thin body with cracks, little or no coating, dry surface. Pulse: fine (thready), rapid, and wiry; weak at the left Chi (Kidney) position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Kidney and Liver Yin are like the body’s coolant and lubricant. Overwork, late nights, and emotional heat burn them up, while spicy and fried foods add drying heat. Rest, cooling foods, and gentle movement conserve these fluids, allowing the body to slowly rebuild its deep reserves and stabilize the dizziness, weakness, and nighttime heat.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat moistening, Yin-nourishing foods like black sesame, goji berries, pears, and bone broths. Avoid spicy, fried, or drying foods and limit coffee and alcohol. Prioritize restful sleep and gentle movement like tai chi rather than exhausting workouts. A short afternoon rest can help protect your Yin reserves.

### Phlegm-Heat
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/phlegm-heat)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Coughing up thick, sticky, yellow phlegm, Constipation with bad breath, Heaviness in the limbs and sluggish thinking, Restlessness and irritability
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Greasy or fried foods, Alcohol, Hot environments, Emotional stress, Constipation
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Cool environment, Warm, light meals, Hydration, Regular bowel movements, Gentle movement or exercise
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  After a stroke, the normal movement of Qi and fluids is disrupted, allowing Phlegm and Heat to accumulate. This sticky, hot Phlegm clogs the channels and orifices, leaving the limbs heavy and the mind sluggish. The Heat dries out the bowels, causing stubborn constipation and bad breath, while the Phlegm rises to the chest, producing thick yellow mucus.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red body, swollen, thick yellow greasy coating. Pulse: slippery (滑 huá) and rapid (数 shuò).
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Phlegm-Heat is a thick, hot, sticky obstruction. Greasy and spicy foods add more Phlegm and Heat, while stress stirs up internal Heat. Cooling foods, hydration, and regular bowel movements help clear Heat and drain Phlegm. A calm environment and gentle movement prevent stagnation without fanning the flames.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat light, cooling foods like leafy greens, cucumber, and pear. Avoid greasy, fried, spicy, and sweet foods that create Phlegm and Heat. Drink plenty of warm water to help thin mucus, and keep your bowels regular with gentle fiber-rich foods. Gentle daily movement like walking or Tai Chi prevents stagnation without overheating the body.

## คำถามที่พบบ่อย (Frequently Asked Questions)
### Can acupuncture really help with post-stroke paralysis?
Yes, acupuncture is widely used in stroke rehabilitation to stimulate nerve recovery and improve muscle function. By inserting needles at specific points along the affected channels, acupuncture can help re-establish Qi and Blood flow to weakened limbs. Many studies and clinical experience support its role in enhancing motor recovery, especially when combined with physical therapy.

### When can I start TCM treatment after a stroke?
TCM treatment can often begin as soon as the patient is medically stable, sometimes within days or weeks after the stroke. In the acute phase, treatment focuses on clearing Wind and Phlegm; later, the emphasis shifts to tonifying Qi and Blood and moving stasis. Always coordinate with your medical team before starting acupuncture or herbs.

### Will Chinese herbs interact with my blood-thinning medication?
Some herbs commonly used in post-stroke formulas, such as Dang Gui (Angelica sinensis) and Chuan Xiong (Ligusticum), have mild blood-moving properties that could theoretically increase bleeding risk when combined with anticoagulants or antiplatelet drugs. It is crucial to inform both your TCM practitioner and your prescribing doctor about all medications and supplements. A qualified practitioner will adjust the formula to ensure safety.

### How often do I need acupuncture for stroke recovery?
Typically, acupuncture is recommended 2-3 times per week initially, with frequency tapering as function improves. Herbal medicine is taken daily. The total duration depends on the severity and pattern, but a course of 3-6 months is common. Progress is gradual, and patience is key.

### Does TCM help with speech problems after a stroke?
Yes, TCM can support speech recovery. Acupuncture points on the head and neck are often used to stimulate language function. Herbal formulas that resolve Phlegm and open the orifices may be prescribed when speech problems are linked to Phlegm obstruction. Combined with speech therapy, many patients see improvement.

### Can TCM prevent another stroke?
TCM addresses the underlying imbalances that contributed to the stroke-such as Liver Yang Rising, Phlegm, or Qi deficiency-to reduce the risk of recurrence. By strengthening the body's constitution and improving circulation, herbs and acupuncture can be part of a comprehensive prevention plan. However, they should complement, not replace, conventional preventive medications like blood pressure control and antiplatelets.

### Is it safe to do TCM alongside physical therapy?
Absolutely. TCM and conventional rehabilitation work well together. Acupuncture can be done on the same day as physical therapy, and herbs can be taken alongside standard medications with proper monitoring. In fact, many rehabilitation hospitals in China integrate acupuncture into daily therapy sessions. Just ensure all providers are informed of your full treatment plan.

## การวิจัยทางวิทยาศาสตร์สมัยใหม่ (Modern Scientific Research)
1. **Acupuncture for stroke rehabilitation: a systematic review and meta-analysis** ([ลิงก์](https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD004131.pub3))
   *วารสารวิชาการ: Yang A, Wu HM, Tang JL, Xu L, Yang M, Liu GJ. Acupuncture for stroke rehabilitation. Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews 2016, Issue 8. Art. No.: CD004131.*
   This systematic review pooled data from multiple RCTs and found that acupuncture, when combined with conventional rehabilitation, significantly improved motor function and reduced neurological deficit scores compared to rehabilitation alone. The effect was most pronounced for limb motor recovery.

2. **Efficacy and safety of Buyang Huanwu Tang for post-stroke disability: a systematic review and meta-analysis**
   *วารสารวิชาการ: Wei J, Zhang Y, Jia Q, et al. Efficacy and safety of Buyang Huanwu Tang for post-stroke disability: a systematic review and meta-analysis. Front Pharmacol. 2021;12:692564.*
   This meta-analysis evaluated 15 RCTs and concluded that Buyang Huanwu Tang significantly improved neurological function and activities of daily living in patients with ischemic stroke sequelae. The formula was well tolerated with few adverse events.

3. **Acupuncture for post-stroke spasticity: a systematic review of randomized controlled trials**
   *วารสารวิชาการ: Park J, White AR, James MA, et al. Acupuncture for spasticity after stroke: a systematic review and meta-analysis of randomized controlled trials. Evid Based Complement Alternat Med. 2014;2014:870398.*
   This review found that acupuncture, especially electroacupuncture, reduced spasticity and improved motor function in post-stroke patients. The most commonly used points were on the affected limbs and the scalp. The quality of evidence was moderate.
