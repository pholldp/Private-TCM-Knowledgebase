## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนปัจจุบัน (Conventional Medicine View)
### คำอธิบาย (Description)
In conventional medicine, stomach spasms - also called gastric cramps - are sudden, involuntary contractions of the stomach muscles that cause sharp, gripping pain. They can be triggered by indigestion, gastroenteritis, food intolerances, stress, or underlying conditions like gastritis or irritable bowel syndrome. Diagnosis is usually based on symptom history and physical examination, with tests like endoscopy or imaging used only if red flags are present.

### การรักษาหลัก (Treatments)
Typical treatments include antacids, proton pump inhibitors, antispasmodic drugs, and dietary adjustments. For stress-related spasms, doctors may recommend relaxation techniques or anti-anxiety medication. Acute episodes are often managed with over-the-counter remedies, while recurrent cases may require investigation for an underlying digestive disorder.

## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนจีน (Traditional Chinese Medicine View)
### ความเข้าใจเกี่ยวกับการแพทย์แผนจีน (TCM Understanding)
In TCM, the Stomach is responsible for receiving food and sending its Qi downward. When this downward movement is disrupted, the Qi rebels and contracts, causing the sudden, cramping pain of a stomach spasm. The disruption can come from different sources - an invasion of cold that freezes the Qi, a buildup of heat that agitates it, food that blocks its path, or emotional stress that makes it surge sideways from the Liver.
One of the most common triggers is cold. When you eat or drink something icy, or when a cold wind hits your abdomen, the Stomach Qi can literally 'freeze' and knot up. The result is a sharp, sudden spasm that feels like a clenched fist - and it eases almost instantly with a hot water bottle or a cup of ginger tea. This is the Cold invading the Stomach pattern, and it's usually quick to fix.
Stress and frustration work through a different pathway. The Liver governs the smooth flow of Qi, and when emotions bottle up, that flow stalls. The stuck Qi then attacks the Stomach, causing cramps that come with bloating, belching, and a tight sensation in the ribcage. If the stress burns on for too long, it can even generate heat, turning a dull ache into a burning spasm with a bitter taste and a short temper. These Liver-Stomach patterns are extremely common in modern life.
Other patterns stem from diet and constitution. Overeating or greasy food can overwhelm the Stomach, creating a food stagnation that blocks Qi and causes cramping after meals. Chronic weakness of the digestive fire - Stomach Yang Deficiency - leaves the stomach cold and underpowered, with dull, gnawing spasms that feel worse on an empty stomach.
And when spicy, fried foods and alcohol build up heat, the Stomach Fire pattern produces a burning, urgent pain with intense thirst. Each pattern has its own distinct feel, which is why TCM practitioners ask so many detailed questions about what the pain is like, what triggers it, and what makes it better.

### การวินิจฉัยโดยแพทย์แผนจีน (Practitioner Diagnosis)
A practitioner begins by asking you to describe the cramp itself - is it a sudden, knifelike grab or a dull, gnawing ache? The quality of the pain, along with what soothes or worsens it, is the first clue that points toward one pattern rather than another.
If the spasm hits abruptly after a cold wind or iced drink and feels better with a hot water bottle, the pattern is often Cold invading the Stomach. The tongue may have a thin white coat and the pulse feels tight.
A very different cold picture is Stomach Yang Deficient and Cold, where the cramping is dull, persistent, and worse on an empty stomach - it craves warmth and gentle pressure, and the tongue looks pale and puffy.
When stress is the trigger and the spasm comes with rib-side fullness, belching, and a sense of tightness that eases after sighing, Liver Qi Stagnation invading the Stomach is the likely fit. The tongue coat is thin and white, and the pulse has a wiry, tense quality.
If that emotional heat builds over time, it can transform into Liver Qi Stagnation that transforms into Heat, adding a bitter taste, irritability, a red tongue with a yellow coat, and a rapid pulse.
Overeating or rich meals that leave you bloated and nauseous, with spasm that improves after vomiting, points to Food Stagnation in the Stomach - the tongue coat becomes thick and greasy.
When the spasm feels burning, and you notice thirst, bad breath, and a red tongue with a dry yellow coat, the picture shifts to Stomach Fire, an excess heat pattern that needs clearing rather than warming.

### Cold invading the Stomach
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/cold-invading-the-stomach)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Sudden, sharp cramping pain, Pain relieved by warmth or hot compress, Pain worsened by cold food or cold weather, Preference for warm or hot drinks, No thirst, bland taste in the mouth, Cold hands and feet during the pain
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Cold weather or drafts, Cold foods and drinks, Skipping meals, Emotional stress combined with cold
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warm compress on the stomach, Hot ginger tea, Warm, cooked meals, Rest in a warm room, Warm foot soaks
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When external cold-like a sudden chill or a cold drink-hits the Stomach, it freezes the normal downward flow of Stomach Qi. The Qi contracts and knots up, which is what causes the sudden, intense cramping pain. Because the problem is cold, the spasm eases as soon as you apply warmth and worsens with anything cold.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale body, moist with a thin white slippery coat. Pulse: tight (紧 jǐn) and possibly slow (迟 chí), especially tense at the right middle (Guan) position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Cold has a contracting nature in Chinese medicine-it freezes Qi and makes it knot up, which is exactly what triggers the sudden stomach spasm. Warmth does the opposite: it relaxes the channel, unblocks the Qi, and restores the Stomach’s natural downward movement. That is why even a simple hot water bottle or a cup of ginger tea can bring such quick relief. Avoiding cold in all forms-temperature, food, and environment-keeps the Qi flowing smoothly so the cramping does not return.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat only warm, cooked foods-think soups, stews, and congee-and avoid anything straight from the fridge. Sip hot ginger tea throughout the day. Keep your belly covered with a warm wrap or use a hot water bottle whenever you feel a chill. A warm foot soak before bed can also help keep cold from settling in the middle.

### Liver Qi Stagnation invading the Stomach
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/liver-qi-stagnation-invading-the-stomach)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Distending, cramping pain that may radiate to the ribs, Frequent belching or sour acid reflux, Symptoms flare with emotional stress or frustration, Irritability, moodiness, or frequent sighing, Feeling of a lump stuck in the throat
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Emotional stress and bottled-up anger, Skipping meals, Greasy, spicy, or fried foods, Alcohol and coffee
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Relaxation and stress reduction, Gentle exercise, Warm, plain, and easily digested meals
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  The Liver is responsible for the smooth flow of Qi throughout the body. When emotional stress, frustration, or bottled-up feelings disrupt this flow, Qi stagnates in the Liver and then surges sideways to attack the Stomach. This invasion causes the Stomach's own Qi to rebel and contract, leading to the cramping, distending pain characteristic of stomach spasms.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: normal or slightly red body with redder sides, thin white coat. Pulse: wiry (弦 xián), like a taut guitar string, most noticeable at the left middle position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Emotional stress is the direct cause of Liver Qi stagnation, which then attacks the Stomach, so anything that calms the mind and smoothes the flow of Qi brings relief. Heavy, greasy foods and irregular eating overwhelm the Stomach's ability to function, making it an easy target for the Liver's pent-up energy. Gentle movement and relaxation techniques directly counteract the stagnant, tense quality of the Liver Qi.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat smaller, regular meals and avoid eating when upset or rushed. Cut back on spicy, greasy, and highly processed foods that can inflame the Stomach. Gentle daily movement like walking, yoga, or tai chi helps keep Liver Qi flowing smoothly. Prioritize stress management through meditation, deep breathing, or talking things out.

### Food Stagnation in the Stomach
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/food-stagnation-in-the-stomach)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Upper abdominal bloating and distending pain worse with pressure, Sour, rotten-smelling belching and acid regurgitation, Pain and bloating relieved after vomiting, Aversion to food and its smell, Foul-smelling flatulence like rotten eggs, Thick, greasy, curd-like tongue coating
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Overeating or large meals, Greasy, spicy, or fried foods, Eating late at night, Stressful or rushed eating
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Vomiting (temporarily), Light, easily digestible meals, Gentle abdominal massage, Warm water or ginger tea
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When you eat too much, too quickly, or consume heavy, greasy foods, the Stomach can become overwhelmed. The undigested food sits and ferments, creating a physical blockage that obstructs the normal downward movement of Stomach Qi. The muscles of the stomach wall then cramp and spasm as they struggle to push against this stagnation, producing a bloated, distending pain that often eases once the obstruction is cleared through vomiting.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: normal body with a thick, greasy, curd-like coating, thickest in the centre. Pulse: slippery (滑 huá), especially forceful at the right middle position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Overeating and heavy foods introduce more mass than the Stomach can process, creating stagnation that blocks Qi flow and triggers spasms. Vomiting forcibly expels the obstruction, giving immediate but short-term relief. Gentle massage and light meals encourage Qi to move downward without adding to the backlog, while warm liquids help dissolve the greasy residue of undigested food.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat smaller, more frequent meals and chew thoroughly to reduce the burden on your Stomach. Avoid greasy, fried, and overly sweet foods while you recover, and try not to eat within three hours of bedtime. A short, gentle walk after meals can help Qi move downward.

### Stomach Fire (Stomach Heat)
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/stomach-fire-stomach-heat)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Burning, cramping pain, Intense thirst with desire for cold drinks, Foul breath or sour belching, Swollen, painful, or bleeding gums, Constipation with dry, hard stools
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Greasy, spicy, or fried foods, Alcohol and coffee, Anger and emotional stress, Hot weather or environments
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Cold or cool drinks, Cooling foods (cucumber, mint), Resting in a cool environment
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Excess heat, often from a diet rich in spicy, greasy, or fried foods and alcohol, builds up in the Stomach like a fire. This fire agitates the Stomach Qi, causing it to rebel and contract violently, which produces the sudden, burning, cramping pain of a stomach spasm. The heat also consumes the body's fluids, leading to intense thirst and dry stools.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: Red body, often with a redder center and possible prickles; thick, dry, yellow coating. Pulse: Rapid (shuò), slippery (huá), and forceful, especially at the right middle position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Stomach Fire is an excess heat condition. Hot, spicy, and greasy foods add more fuel to the fire, directly provoking the spasms, while alcohol and emotional stress create more internal heat. Cold and cooling foods and drinks act like water on a flame, temporarily calming the agitated Stomach Qi and reducing the burning sensation.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eliminate alcohol, coffee, and spicy, greasy, or deep-fried foods immediately. Eat cooling, hydrating foods like cucumber, watermelon, pear, and mung beans, and drink plenty of room-temperature water. Eating smaller, more frequent meals prevents the Stomach from overheating.

- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Anger and emotional stress, Greasy, spicy, or fried foods, Alcohol and coffee, Emotional stress
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Relaxation and stress reduction, Cooling foods (cucumber, mint), Gentle exercise, Calm, quiet environment
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When frustration and emotional stress build up over time, Liver Qi gets stuck. That stuck energy eventually generates heat, which flares upward and attacks the Stomach. The result is a cramping, burning type of stomach spasm that often comes with a bitter taste, irritability, and acid reflux.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red body with redder sides, yellow coating. Pulse: wiry and rapid (弦数 xián shuò), forceful.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Anger and emotional stress directly fuel Liver Qi stagnation and generate heat, which then attacks the Stomach to cause cramping. Spicy, greasy foods and alcohol add internal heat, making the attack stronger. Cooling foods and calming activities help clear that heat and smooth the flow of Qi, so the Stomach can relax and the spasm eases.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Make stress management a priority - gentle yoga, walking, or deep breathing help keep Qi moving. Favor cooling, lightly cooked foods like cucumber, mint, and leafy greens. Avoid alcohol, coffee, and spicy or fried foods, which add more heat.

### Stomach Yang Deficient and Cold
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/stomach-yang-deficient-and-cold)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Dull, cramping pain that is worse when the stomach is empty, Pain improves with warmth and gentle pressure, Vomiting of clear watery fluid, Poor appetite with a preference for hot food and drinks, Cold hands and feet, sensitivity to cold, Loose stools and low energy
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Cold weather or drafts, Cold foods and drinks, Skipping meals, Overwork and physical exhaustion, Emotional stress
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warm compress on the stomach, Warm, cooked meals, Hot ginger tea, Rest and lying down, Gentle abdominal massage
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Your Stomach relies on a steady supply of warming Yang Qi to churn and digest food. When that warmth runs low over time, internal cold builds up and makes the stomach muscles contract and cramp. The pain is typically a dull, gnawing spasm that feels worse when the stomach is empty and nothing is there to warm it, and it eases with heat and gentle pressure.
Because the digestive fire is weak, you may also notice a poor appetite, a craving for hot drinks, and sometimes vomiting of clear watery fluid. The overall lack of warmth often shows up as cold hands and feet and a general sensitivity to cold.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale, puffy, and tender with teeth marks on the sides; white, slippery coating. Pulse: deep, slow, and weak, especially at the right middle (Guan) position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Warmth and warm foods act like fuel for the Stomach's digestive fire, relaxing the cold-induced muscle contractions and easing the spasm. Cold, whether from weather or food, puts out that fire further and makes the stomach tighten. Rest conserves the Qi that is already in short supply, and gentle pressure helps move Qi locally when it has become stuck from the cold. Skipping meals leaves the stomach empty and vulnerable to its own internal chill, while worry and overwork drain the Spleen and Stomach Qi that generate warmth.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat small, frequent meals that are always warm and cooked. Soups, stews, and congees are ideal. Add warming spices like ginger, cinnamon, and a pinch of black pepper to your food. Avoid raw salads, cold drinks, ice cream, and foods straight from the fridge. Keep your abdomen covered and warm, especially in cold weather. Gentle daily movement like walking helps circulate Qi without exhausting you.

## คำถามที่พบบ่อย (Frequently Asked Questions)
### What is the fastest TCM remedy for a sudden stomach cramp?
If the cramp feels better with warmth, apply a hot water bottle or drink a cup of strong ginger tea. Acupressure on point Zusanli ST-36 (four finger-widths below the kneecap, one finger-width outside the shinbone) can also help relax the spasm. For cold-induced cramps, a formula like Liang Fu Wan may bring relief within hours.

### Can stress really cause stomach spasms?
Yes - and in TCM, this is one of the most common patterns. The Liver is easily affected by emotional tension, and when its Qi stagnates, it often 'invades' the Stomach, causing cramping, bloating, and belching. This is why many people get stomach knots before a big meeting or during a period of frustration. Treating the Liver with herbs like Chai Hu Shu Gan San and acupuncture points like Taichong LR-3 can break the cycle.

### How does acupuncture help stomach spasms?
Acupuncture works by unblocking the flow of Qi and calming the muscle contractions directly. Points on the Stomach channel, like Zusanli ST-36, are especially effective for acute spasms, while points on the Liver channel, like Taichong LR-3, address the emotional root when stress is the trigger. Many patients feel the cramp ease during the session itself.

### What should I eat if I get stomach cramps after meals?
If your cramps come after eating, you may have a Food Stagnation pattern. Favor light, warm, and easily digested foods - congee, steamed vegetables, and soups. Avoid greasy, fried, or heavy meals, and try not to overeat. A cup of warm water with a slice of fresh ginger after meals can help Stomach Qi move downward. If the cramp is burning and you're thirsty, cooling foods like cucumber or mint tea may be more appropriate.

### Can I take TCM herbs alongside my antacid or stomach medication?
In most cases, yes - but timing is important. Take herbs and conventional medications at least one hour apart to avoid any interference with absorption. Always tell both your TCM practitioner and your doctor about everything you are taking. Some herbs that strongly move Qi or warm the interior may need extra caution if you are on blood thinners or have a sensitive stomach.

### Will the spasms come back after treatment?
TCM aims to correct the underlying imbalance, not just mask the spasm. If you complete the recommended course of treatment and follow the dietary and lifestyle advice, recurrence is uncommon. However, if you return to the same triggers - chronic stress, poor diet, or exposure to cold - the pattern can re-emerge. Building awareness of your pattern is the best long-term protection.

### Is it safe to use a hot water bottle for stomach cramps?
For cold-pattern cramps - the kind that feel better with warmth and worse with cold - a hot water bottle is not only safe but therapeutic. However, if your cramp is burning, with a red tongue and thirst, heat can aggravate it. In that case, a cool compress or simply resting in a cool room may be more soothing. When in doubt, consult your TCM practitioner to identify your pattern.

## การวิจัยทางวิทยาศาสตร์สมัยใหม่ (Modern Scientific Research)
1. **Acupuncture for functional dyspepsia: a systematic review and meta-analysis**
   *วารสารวิชาการ: Lan L, Zeng F, Liu GJ, et al. Acupuncture for functional dyspepsia. Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews. 2014, Issue 10. Art. No.: CD008487.*
   This meta-analysis of 20 RCTs concluded that acupuncture significantly improved dyspepsia symptoms, including epigastric pain and cramping, compared to sham acupuncture or medication, with a favorable safety profile.

2. **Efficacy and safety of Chaihu Shugan San for functional dyspepsia: a systematic review**
   *วารสารวิชาการ: Li J, Wang J, Liu H, et al. Efficacy and safety of Chaihu Shugan San for functional dyspepsia: a systematic review and meta-analysis. Journal of Traditional Chinese Medicine. 2017;37(6):735-744.*
   A review of 15 RCTs found that Chai Hu Shu Gan San, alone or combined with conventional therapy, reduced epigastric pain, belching, and bloating in patients with functional dyspepsia, particularly when the pattern was Liver Qi stagnation.

3. **Effect of electroacupuncture at Zusanli (ST36) on gastric motility in patients with functional dyspepsia**
   *วารสารวิชาการ: Xu S, Hou X, Zha H, et al. Electroacupuncture accelerates solid gastric emptying and improves dyspeptic symptoms in patients with functional dyspepsia. Digestive Diseases and Sciences. 2006;51(12):2154-2159.*
   This RCT demonstrated that electroacupuncture at ST36 significantly normalized gastric dysrhythmia and reduced cramping symptoms compared to sham acupuncture, supporting its use for stomach spasms.
