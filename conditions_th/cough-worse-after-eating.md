## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนปัจจุบัน (Conventional Medicine View)
### คำอธิบาย (Description)
Conventional medicine often links a cough that worsens after eating to gastroesophageal reflux disease (GERD), where stomach acid flows back into the esophagus and triggers a cough reflex. It can also be related to laryngopharyngeal reflux (silent reflux), where the acid reaches the throat, or to post-nasal drip that worsens with certain foods. Diagnosis typically involves symptom history, trials of acid-suppressing medications, and sometimes endoscopy or pH monitoring.

### การรักษาหลัก (Treatments)
Standard treatments aim to reduce stomach acid with proton pump inhibitors (PPIs) or H2 blockers, and to protect the esophagus with antacids or alginates. Lifestyle changes - avoiding trigger foods, eating smaller meals, not lying down for three hours after eating, and elevating the head of the bed - are also recommended. In some cases, prokinetic drugs are prescribed to speed stomach emptying.

## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนจีน (Traditional Chinese Medicine View)
### ความเข้าใจเกี่ยวกับการแพทย์แผนจีน (TCM Understanding)
In TCM, the Lung and Stomach share a close functional relationship because both organs must send Qi downward. The Stomach pushes food down through digestion, while the Lung disseminates Qi downward to the rest of the body. When Stomach Qi rebels and surges upward - often due to overeating, stress, or weak digestion - it rushes into the chest and provokes the Lung, triggering a cough. This is why eating, especially a heavy meal, can immediately set off coughing in susceptible people.
The Spleen plays a central role in transforming food into usable Qi and fluids. If the Spleen is weak, fluids accumulate as dampness, which eventually congeals into phlegm. This phlegm often settles in the Lungs, creating a chronic, productive cough. Eating - particularly rich, greasy, or cold foods - places an extra burden on the Spleen, causing it to generate even more phlegm and worsening the cough.
Food stagnation offers a more direct mechanism: when you eat more than the Stomach can process, or consume hard-to-digest foods, the food sits and ferments, creating a blockage. This obstructs the normal downward movement of Stomach Qi, forcing it upward. The resulting belching, bloating, and acid reflux irritate the throat and trigger a cough.
Because TCM sees these distinct root causes, a cough after eating is not one disease but three possible patterns - each with its own characteristic phlegm, tongue coating, and pulse, and each requiring a fundamentally different treatment approach.

### การวินิจฉัยโดยแพทย์แผนจีน (Practitioner Diagnosis)
A practitioner starts by asking what the cough feels like and exactly when it strikes in relation to eating. The timing, type of phlegm, and any digestive discomfort offer the first clues. Because food-related cough always involves the stomach and lung, the practitioner pays special attention to appetite, bloating, belching, and bowel habits to separate the three main patterns.
If the person complains of a heavy, bloated feeling in the upper abdomen, sour or putrid belching, and a cough that flares soon after a meal, Food Stagnation in the Stomach is the likely picture. The tongue often shows a thick, greasy coating, and the pulse feels slippery. Here the cough is driven by undigested food causing stomach Qi to rebel upward and irritate the lung.
When the cough is chronic, produces lots of white, sticky phlegm, and worsens after eating rich or greasy foods, Damp-Phlegm in the Lungs is the main suspect. The tongue may be swollen with teeth marks and a thick white greasy coating, and the pulse is slippery. The person often feels chest congestion, heaviness, and a foggy head, because the spleen is too weak to manage fluids and eating adds more dampness.
If the cough is triggered by eating and comes with heartburn, acid reflux, nausea, or frequent loud belching, Rebellious Stomach Qi is central. The tongue coating is usually greasy, and the pulse can feel wiry or slippery. In this pattern, the stomach fails to send Qi downward normally, so it rushes upward and provokes the lung, making the cough feel urgent and directly tied to meals.

## กลุ่มอาการการแพทย์แผนจีนและการรักษา (TCM Patterns and Treatment)

### Food Stagnation in the Stomach
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/food-stagnation-in-the-stomach)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Cough triggered or worsened by eating, Upper abdominal bloating and distension, Sour, rotten-smelling belching, Thick, greasy, curd-like tongue coating, Nausea or vomiting of undigested food, Relief after vomiting
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Overeating or heavy meals, Greasy, fried, or rich foods, Cold, raw, or sweet foods/drinks, Lying down after eating, Stressful or rushed eating
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Eating smaller, lighter meals, Eating warm, light, easily digestible foods, Gentle movement after eating, Vomiting (temporary relief)
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When you eat more than your stomach can handle, or consume heavy, greasy foods that are hard to digest, the food sits and stagnates rather than being processed. This blockage obstructs the normal downward movement of Stomach Qi, causing it to rebel upward. The rebellious Qi rushes up to the chest and disturbs the Lungs, triggering a cough that is especially noticeable after meals because eating adds more burden to an already overloaded stomach.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: normal body with a thick, greasy, curd-like coating, thickest in the centre. Pulse: slippery (滑 huá), full, and most forceful at the right middle (Stomach) position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Overeating and greasy foods directly overload the Stomach, creating the stagnation that causes Qi to rebel upward and disturb the Lungs. Eating smaller, warm, easily digestible meals reduces this burden, allowing Stomach Qi to descend normally and stop provoking a cough. Gentle movement after meals helps Qi flow downward, while lying down encourages it to rise, so staying upright and walking aids digestion and prevents the upward rush of Qi.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat smaller, lighter meals and chew thoroughly. Favour warm, cooked foods like soups and congee, and avoid greasy, fried, cold, or raw foods that are hard to digest. Take a gentle walk after eating to encourage downward movement of Qi, and avoid lying down immediately after meals. Consistent meal times and not eating when stressed also help the stomach function smoothly.

### Damp-Phlegm in the Lungs
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/damp-phlegm-in-the-lungs)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Cough with copious white sticky phlegm, easy to bring up, Chest tightness and a feeling of heaviness, Poor appetite, bloating, and loose stools, Body heaviness and fatigue, Thick, white, greasy tongue coating
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Overeating or heavy meals, Cold, raw, or sweet foods/drinks, Damp or humid weather, Lying down after eating, Dairy products
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Eating warm, light, easily digestible foods, Avoiding dairy and greasy foods, Gentle movement after eating, Warmth on the chest, Dry, well-ventilated rooms
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When the Spleen is weak, it fails to transform fluids properly, and dampness accumulates, eventually congealing into phlegm that settles in the Lungs. Eating, especially heavy or rich food, places an extra burden on the Spleen, causing it to produce even more phlegm. This excess phlegm then obstructs the Lung's ability to descend Qi, triggering a cough that is worse after meals.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale, swollen body with teeth marks and a thick, white, greasy coating. Pulse: slippery (滑 huá), like beads rolling under the fingers, and may feel slightly soggy.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Heavy, cold, and damp foods overwhelm the Spleen, causing it to produce even more phlegm, which then clogs the Lungs and triggers coughing. Warm, light meals and gentle movement support the Spleen's ability to transform food and fluids properly, reducing the phlegm burden. A dry environment and chest warmth help keep the Lung Qi open and descending, while lying down after eating allows phlegm to stagnate and rise upward.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat warm, cooked foods and avoid dairy, cold drinks, raw salads, greasy, and sweet foods, which all generate dampness and phlegm. A bowl of congee or a light soup is easier on the Spleen than a heavy meal. Gentle walking after eating helps Qi move and prevents stagnation. Keep the chest and neck warm, and avoid damp, humid environments.

### Rebellious Stomach Qi
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/rebellious-stomach-qi)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Cough triggered or worsened by eating, Belching, Nausea or vomiting, Acid reflux or sour regurgitation, Feeling of Qi rushing up from stomach, Upper stomach fullness
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Overeating or heavy meals, Cold, raw, or sweet foods/drinks, Lying down after eating, Stress and frustration, Stressful or rushed eating
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Eating smaller, lighter meals, Eating warm, light, easily digestible foods, Sitting upright after meals, Belching to release trapped Qi, Warmth on the abdomen
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Normally, Stomach Qi moves downward to push food through digestion. When this Qi rebels and surges upward instead, it can rush into the chest and throat, directly provoking the lung. This upward counterflow triggers a cough, especially after eating when the stomach is full and the rebellious movement is strongest.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: normal or slightly pale body with a white coating (may be yellow if heat is present). Pulse: wiry (xián) or slippery (huá), especially at the right middle position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Rebellious Stomach Qi is a counterflow condition: the stomach's natural downward movement is reversed, causing Qi to rise. Overeating, heavy or cold foods, and rushing meals all burden the stomach and disrupt its descent, triggering the upward rush that provokes a cough. Emotional stress, especially anger or frustration, can make Liver Qi invade the stomach, further pushing Qi upward. Lying down after eating also encourages reflux and upward movement. In contrast, smaller, warm, cooked meals and sitting upright support the stomach's natural descending function. Belching releases trapped Qi, and abdominal warmth soothes and relaxes the stomach, helping Qi move downward again.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat smaller, regular meals and avoid overeating. Choose warm, cooked foods and steer clear of greasy, spicy, or very cold items that burden the stomach. Sit upright after eating and avoid lying down for at least an hour. Gentle walking after meals can help Qi descend.

## คำถามที่พบบ่อย (Frequently Asked Questions)
### Why do I cough after eating?
In TCM, a cough that strikes after meals is a sign that the stomach and lung are out of sync. Instead of moving downward, stomach Qi rebels upward, irritating the throat and triggering a cough. The exact reason depends on your pattern: undigested food blocking the stomach, excess phlegm flooding the lungs, or pure Qi rebellion. Once the pattern is corrected, the cough stops.

### Can TCM stop the cough completely?
Yes, by addressing the root cause. Unlike acid suppressors that only reduce stomach acid, TCM aims to restore the normal downward movement of stomach Qi, clear phlegm, and strengthen digestion. Most patients see significant improvement within a few weeks, and many achieve lasting relief once the underlying imbalance is resolved.

### How long does it take to work?
Many people notice less coughing within 1-2 weeks of starting herbs and acupuncture. Full resolution depends on the pattern: food stagnation and rebellious Qi respond fastest (often 2-3 weeks), while damp-phlegm with a weak spleen may take 4-6 weeks or more. Chronic, long-standing phlegm can require a few months of consistent treatment.

### Do I have to change my diet?
Yes, diet is crucial. The foods that trigger your cough are often part of the pattern. Avoiding greasy, fried, dairy, and cold raw foods while eating smaller, warmer meals gives the stomach a break and speeds recovery. Your practitioner will give you specific guidance based on your pattern, but these general rules apply to almost everyone with this condition.

### Can I take TCM herbs with my acid reflux medication?
Generally yes, but always tell both your TCM practitioner and doctor about all medications you're taking. Some herbs that promote digestion might affect how acid-suppressing drugs are absorbed - it's wise to take them at least an hour apart. With proper monitoring, herbs and PPIs can be used safely together.

### Is coughing after eating a sign of something serious?
Usually it's a functional digestive imbalance, not a dangerous disease. However, if you cough up blood, have severe chest pain, unexplained weight loss, or difficulty swallowing, seek urgent medical evaluation (see Safety section). TCM can address the root once serious causes are ruled out.

### Will the cough come back after treatment?
If the underlying pattern is fully resolved and you maintain healthy eating habits, the cough is unlikely to return. TCM aims to correct the imbalance permanently, not just suppress symptoms. Some people may need periodic tune-ups if they slip back into old dietary patterns.

## การวิจัยทางวิทยาศาสตร์สมัยใหม่ (Modern Scientific Research)
1. **Efficacy and safety of Bao He Wan for functional dyspepsia: a systematic review and meta-analysis**
   *วารสารวิชาการ: Zhang Y, Li J, Wang C, et al. Efficacy and safety of Bao He Wan for functional dyspepsia: a systematic review and meta-analysis. J Tradit Chin Med. 2019;39(4):457-465.*
   This systematic review pooled data from 12 randomized controlled trials involving over 1,200 patients with functional dyspepsia. Bao He Wan significantly improved global symptom scores, postprandial fullness, and early satiety compared to placebo or prokinetic drugs, with fewer adverse events. The study supports the formula’s role in resolving food stagnation and harmonizing the Stomach, mechanisms directly relevant to food-induced cough.

2. **Acupuncture for gastroesophageal reflux disease: a systematic review and meta-analysis**
   *วารสารวิชาการ: Zhu J, Guo Y, Liu S, et al. Acupuncture for gastroesophageal reflux disease: a systematic review and meta-analysis. Acupunct Med. 2016;34(5):349-356.*
   This meta-analysis of 12 RCTs found that acupuncture significantly reduced reflux symptoms, including regurgitation and heartburn, and decreased the frequency of reflux events on pH monitoring. Since GERD is a common trigger for postprandial cough, these findings support acupuncture’s use for cough after eating by addressing the upward rebellion of Stomach Qi.

3. **Chinese herbal medicine for chronic cough: a systematic review of randomized trials**
   *วารสารวิชาการ: Chen X, Liu H, Yang M, et al. Chinese herbal medicine for chronic cough: a systematic review of randomized trials. Evid Based Complement Alternat Med. 2018;2018:6398042.*
   This review included 28 RCTs evaluating various Chinese herbal formulas for chronic cough. Formulas that resolved phlegm and harmonized the Stomach - including Er Chen Tang modifications - were among the most frequently studied and showed significant improvement in cough severity and sputum production compared to standard antitussive medications. The review highlights the clinical relevance of treating the Spleen and Stomach in chronic cough.
