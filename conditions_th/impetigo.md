## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนปัจจุบัน (Conventional Medicine View)
### คำอธิบาย (Description)
Impetigo is a highly contagious bacterial skin infection, most commonly caused by Staphylococcus aureus or Streptococcus pyogenes. It typically begins as red sores or blisters, usually around the nose and mouth, that quickly rupture, ooze fluid, and form a characteristic honey-colored crust. The condition is most common in children, especially in warm, humid weather, and spreads easily through close contact or shared towels and clothing. Diagnosis is usually made by a doctor based on the appearance of the sores, and occasionally a swab is taken to confirm the bacteria.

### การรักษาหลัก (Treatments)
Standard treatment involves topical antibiotic ointments such as mupirocin or fusidic acid for mild, localized infections. More widespread or severe cases are treated with oral antibiotics like cephalexin or dicloxacillin. Good hygiene - gentle washing of the sores, keeping nails short, and avoiding scratching - is essential to prevent spreading the infection to other parts of the body or to other people.

## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนจีน (Traditional Chinese Medicine View)
### ความเข้าใจเกี่ยวกับการแพทย์แผนจีน (TCM Understanding)
In TCM, the skin is considered the exterior of the body and is closely linked to the Lungs, which govern the opening and closing of pores. However, impetigo specifically points to the interplay of Dampness and Heat - two pathological factors that can arise from external weather or internal organ imbalance. When hot, humid summer weather invades the body, or when the Spleen is too weak to transform fluids, Dampness accumulates. Combined with Heat, it creates the moist, oozing, yellow-crusted blisters characteristic of impetigo.
The Spleen is the organ most often at the root of chronic or recurrent impetigo. Its job is to transform food into Qi and to manage fluids. If the Spleen is weak - from poor diet, overwork, or constitutional tendency - it fails to separate clean from turbid fluids, and Dampness overflows to the skin. This explains why some people get impetigo repeatedly even with good hygiene: the internal environment is hospitable to the pathogen.
Emotions also play a role. The Liver ensures the smooth flow of Qi. When stress and frustration cause Qi to stagnate, it can generate Heat that flares upward and outward, creating painful, inflamed lesions that don't heal easily. And when the condition drags on, unresolved Dampness and Heat can congeal into Blood Stasis, making the sores hard, dark, and stabbing. So a single Western diagnosis masks at least five distinct TCM patterns, each requiring a different approach.

### การวินิจฉัยโดยแพทย์แผนจีน (Practitioner Diagnosis)
A practitioner starts by asking when the rash appeared and what the lesions look like. Impetigo that erupts during hot, sticky summer weather, with clusters of yellow, honey-crusted pustules surrounded by a red halo, points strongly to Summer Heat with Dampness. The tongue is red with a thick, greasy yellow coat, and the pulse feels slippery and rapid. This pattern is the classic early-stage picture of the condition.
If the redness is much more intense, the blisters are thick with pus, and the person feels feverish, thirsty, and restless, the diagnosis shifts to Toxic-Heat. Here the damp-heat has deepened into a toxic invasion. The tongue is still red with a yellow coat, but the pulse is even more rapid and forceful. The practitioner is looking for signs that the body is fighting a more aggressive fire.
When lesions ooze and heal very slowly, and the person tends to feel tired, has a poor appetite, and a pale, puffy tongue with a thin white coat, Spleen Deficiency with Dampness is considered. The pulse is weak and soft. This pattern often appears in people who get recurrent outbreaks because their digestive energy is too weak to transform and clear the dampness, so the skin stays wet and vulnerable.
Emotional stress can create a different picture. If the sores are hard, painful, and slow to resolve, and the person feels irritable with a sensation of fullness under the ribs, the practitioner suspects Liver Qi Stagnation transforming into Heat. The tongue may be redder on the sides, and the pulse is wiry and rapid. The heat here is stirred by stuck emotions rather than just the weather.
Finally, when old lesions turn dark, feel hard, and cause a stabbing pain, Qi and Blood Stagnation is at play. The tongue looks dusky with possible purple spots, and the pulse is wiry and rough. This is usually a complication after the acute infection has lingered, leaving local stagnation rather than an active heat process.

## กลุ่มอาการการแพทย์แผนจีนและการรักษา (TCM Patterns and Treatment)

### Summer Heat with Dampness
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/summer-heat-with-dampness)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Dense clusters of yellow, oozing pustules with red halos, Worse or first appears during hot, humid summer months, Body heaviness and fatigue, Stuffiness in the chest and upper abdomen, Thirst with little desire to drink, Greasy tongue coating
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Hot, humid weather, Eating greasy, spicy, or sweet foods, Excessive sweating, Stress and frustration
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Cool, dry weather, Light, cooling foods (mung bean soup), Gentle washing with mild soap, Rest and avoiding sweating
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Impetigo in this pattern is caused by external Summer Heat and Dampness invading the skin during hot, humid weather. These two pathogens combine to create a hot, sticky environment that clogs the pores and generates pus-filled blisters with a characteristic yellow ooze. The same Dampness and Heat can also affect the Spleen and Stomach internally, leading to the systemic symptoms of heaviness, poor appetite, and a greasy tongue coating.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red body with a white greasy coating that may shift to yellow greasy if Heat intensifies. Pulse: soggy (Ru) and rapid (Shu), like pressing a wet cotton ball.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Summer Heat and Dampness are the direct external causes of this pattern, so hot, humid weather and heavy sweating worsen the condition by feeding the pathogens. Greasy, spicy, and sweet foods generate internal Dampness and Heat, adding fuel to the fire. Cooling, drying foods and a calm, cool environment help the body clear the invading pathogens and restore balance. Gentle hygiene removes pus and crusts without damaging the skin, allowing healing.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat light, cooling foods that drain Dampness, such as mung bean soup, bitter melon, and cucumber. Avoid greasy, fried, spicy, and very sweet foods, which create more internal Dampness and Heat. Stay in a cool, dry environment and wear loose, breathable cotton clothing to reduce sweating and friction on the sores.

### Toxic-Heat
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/toxic-heat)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Pus-filled blisters with thick yellow or honey-colored crusts, Intense redness, heat, and swelling around the sores, Fever and a sensation of body heat, Intense thirst with a desire for cold drinks, Dark, scanty urine or constipation
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Spicy, greasy, or fried foods, Hot, humid weather, Scratching or picking at sores
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Cool compresses, Cooling foods and drinks, Rest in a cool, airy room
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When damp-heat from the environment or diet lingers on the skin, it can deepen and concentrate into a fiery toxin. This toxic heat rises to the surface, causing the skin to erupt in angry, red, pus-filled blisters that quickly crust over with thick, honey-colored scabs. The body's internal thermostat becomes overwhelmed, leading to fever, intense thirst, and a flushed appearance as the toxin attempts to push out through the skin.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red body with prickly thorns, dry yellow coating. Pulse: rapid, forceful, and slippery.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Toxic heat is a fiery, aggressive pathogen that flares up with anything that adds heat to the body-like spicy food, hot environments, or friction from scratching. Cooling foods and compresses directly counter that fire, while rest allows the body's Qi to focus on clearing the toxin rather than fighting other demands.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat plenty of cooling, water-rich foods like cucumber, watermelon, and mung bean soup. Strictly avoid spicy, greasy, or fried foods, as well as alcohol and coffee, which add fuel to the internal fire. Keep the skin clean and dry; wash gently with mild, cooling herbal washes (such as diluted honeysuckle tea) and resist the urge to scratch or pick at the crusts.

### Spleen Deficiency with Dampness
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/spleen-deficiency-with-dampness)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Lesions ooze clear or slightly yellowish fluid and heal slowly, Recurrent episodes, especially after fatigue or dietary indiscretion, Fatigue, lack of energy, and drowsiness after meals, Poor appetite, abdominal bloating, and loose stools, Heaviness of the body and limbs
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Cold, raw foods and dairy, Overwork and fatigue, Damp, humid weather
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warm, easily digestible foods, Rest and adequate sleep, Dry, warm environment
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When the Spleen is weak, it cannot properly transform food and fluids, so dampness builds up inside. This internal dampness overflows to the skin, creating the moist, oozing blisters of impetigo. Because the Spleen also supports the body's defensive Qi, the lesions tend to heal slowly and come back again and again, especially after fatigue or eating the wrong foods.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale and swollen with teeth marks, white greasy coating, moist surface. Pulse: soggy (Ru) and slow (Huan), may feel weak and slightly slippery.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: A weak Spleen fails to transform fluids, creating internal Dampness that surfaces as oozing impetigo. Warm, cooked foods and rest support the Spleen's Qi, helping it dry up the dampness, while cold, raw foods and overwork further weaken the Spleen, allowing dampness to accumulate and the lesions to linger or recur.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat warm, cooked foods like congee, stews, and soups. Avoid cold, raw, and greasy foods that weaken the Spleen. Gentle daily movement such as walking helps circulate Qi, but avoid overexertion. Keep the skin clean and dry.

### Liver Qi Stagnation that transforms into Heat
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/liver-qi-stagnation-that-transforms-into-heat)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Painful, hard, inflamed nodules, Irritability and explosive anger, Distending pain in the ribcage, Bitter taste in the mouth
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Anger and frustration, Spicy food, Alcohol, Hot weather
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Stress reduction, Cooling foods, Gentle exercise, Cold compresses
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When emotional stress and frustration become chronic, the Liver's ability to keep Qi flowing smoothly is compromised. This stagnation generates internal heat and fire, which can rise and flare at the skin. The combination of stagnant Qi and fire produces painful, hard, inflamed nodules that are slow to heal, especially on the trunk or rib-side areas.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red body with redder sides, thin yellow coating. Pulse: wiry and rapid (xian shu), especially at the left Guan position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Emotional stress and anger cause Liver Qi to stagnate and generate fire, which directly triggers this pattern. Spicy and greasy foods add heat to the body, worsening inflammation. Cooling foods and stress relief help clear heat and smooth the Liver Qi, reducing flare-ups.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Prioritize stress management through gentle exercise like walking or yoga. Avoid spicy, greasy, and fried foods, as well as alcohol, which add heat. Eat cooling foods such as cucumber, chrysanthemum tea, and mung beans. Keep the affected area clean and avoid scratching to prevent secondary infection.

### Qi And Blood Stagnation
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/qi-and-blood-stagnation)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Stabbing pain in the lesions, Hard, dark or purplish nodules, Dark purple tongue with stasis spots, Irritability or chest distension, Wiry and choppy pulse
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Emotional stress, Prolonged inactivity, Cold exposure
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warm compress on lesions, Gentle exercise, Stress reduction
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When impetigo drags on, unresolved damp-heat can congeal into blood stasis. Qi and Blood stagnation in the skin makes the lesions hard, dark, and stabbing in pain. Emotional stress (Liver Qi stagnation) often worsens this, further impairing circulation and turning the sores into stubborn, painful nodules that resist healing.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: dark purple body with stasis spots, distended sublingual veins, thin white coat. Pulse: wiry (弦 xián) and choppy (涩 sè), like a knife scraping bamboo.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Stress stagnates Liver Qi, which in turn worsens blood stasis, making lesions harder and more painful. Cold congeals blood, while warmth and gentle movement promote circulation and help soften the nodules.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Avoid cold, raw foods and heavy greasy meals that can further congeal blood. Include small amounts of turmeric, hawthorn, and black fungus to gently move blood. Light exercise like walking or stretching helps Qi flow.

## คำถามที่พบบ่อย (Frequently Asked Questions)
### Can TCM cure impetigo without antibiotics?
For mild to moderate cases, yes - especially when treated early. Herbal washes and internal formulas that clear Heat and drain Dampness can resolve the sores as effectively as topical antibiotics in many people. However, if the infection is widespread, accompanied by fever, or not improving within 48 hours, antibiotics may be necessary. TCM can still be used alongside them to speed healing and reduce recurrence.

### How quickly will the sores heal with Chinese medicine?
With the right herbal wash and internal formula, you can expect the oozing to decrease and crusts to start drying within 2-3 days. New blisters usually stop forming after the first day or two of treatment. Complete healing of the skin typically takes 5-10 days for an acute outbreak. Chronic sores or those complicated by underlying Spleen weakness may take a little longer.

### Is impetigo contagious while using herbal treatment?
Yes, the infection can still be contagious until the sores have dried and crusted over, usually 24-48 hours after starting effective treatment. Keep the affected areas covered with clean bandages, avoid sharing towels or clothing, and wash hands frequently. Herbal washes help reduce bacterial load on the skin, but good hygiene remains essential to prevent spreading it to others.

### Can I use herbal washes alongside antibiotic cream?
Absolutely. You can apply a herbal wash or compress first to clean and cool the area, then apply the antibiotic cream after the skin is dry. Just be sure to use a clean cloth or cotton ball each time. Let both your TCM practitioner and your doctor know what you are using so they can coordinate care.

### Does my child need to change their diet to prevent impetigo?
Diet plays a big role in preventing recurrence. In TCM, sugar, dairy, and greasy foods create internal Dampness and Heat, which make the skin more vulnerable. Cutting back on sweets, sugary drinks, and fried snacks while increasing cooling foods like cucumber, mung beans, and lightly cooked vegetables can make a noticeable difference. Even small changes help, especially in children who get impetigo every summer.

### Are the herbs safe for children?
Yes, when prescribed by a qualified TCM practitioner. Pediatric doses are carefully adjusted for the child's age and weight. Many of the herbs used for impetigo, such as Jin Yin Hua (honeysuckle) and Huang Qin (scutellaria), are gentle and have a long history of safe use in children. Always inform your practitioner of any medications your child is taking, and never give adult-dose formulas to a child.

### Will acupuncture help with the pain and itching?
Acupuncture can significantly reduce itching, pain, and inflammation, often within the first session. Points are selected to clear Heat and Dampness from the affected channels and to calm the mind, which reduces the urge to scratch. For children who are afraid of needles, acupressure or gentle massage on the same points can be used instead, with good results.
