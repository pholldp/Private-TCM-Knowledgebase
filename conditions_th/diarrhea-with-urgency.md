## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนปัจจุบัน (Conventional Medicine View)
### คำอธิบาย (Description)
In Western medicine, the urgent, often painful need to defecate with a sensation of incomplete evacuation is called tenesmus. It commonly accompanies conditions like ulcerative colitis, Crohn's disease, infectious diarrhea, or irritable bowel syndrome (IBS). The mechanism involves inflammation or irritation of the rectum and colon, which triggers nerve signals that create a false sense of urgency. Diagnosis typically involves stool tests, colonoscopy, or imaging to identify the underlying cause.

### การรักษาหลัก (Treatments)
Conventional treatment targets the underlying condition: antibiotics for bacterial infections, anti-inflammatory drugs (mesalamine, corticosteroids) for inflammatory bowel disease, antispasmodics for IBS, or dietary adjustments. For symptom relief, loperamide may be used cautiously, but it can worsen tenesmus in certain cases. Severe cases may require biologic medications or surgery.

## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนจีน (Traditional Chinese Medicine View)
### ความเข้าใจเกี่ยวกับการแพทย์แผนจีน (TCM Understanding)
In TCM, the Large Intestine and Spleen are the primary organs involved in this symptom. The Large Intestine is responsible for receiving waste and moving it downward, while the Spleen transforms food into usable energy and lifts the organs. When dampness, heat, cold, or stagnation obstruct the normal downward flow of Qi in the intestines, the body creates a powerful urge to expel the blockage - but because the Qi is stuck, evacuation feels incomplete. This is the core mechanism behind the tenesmus sensation.
The character of the stool and the accompanying sensations are crucial clues. If heat and dampness combine, the stool may be burning, bloody, or filled with mucus, and the urge is frantic and hot. If cold and dampness dominate, the stool is white, sticky, and accompanied by a cold, heavy feeling in the abdomen.
In deficiency patterns, the Spleen Qi is too weak to lift the bowel, creating a dragging sensation after going, or the body's yang fire has dimmed, leaving a cold, urgent need that worsens after defecation. Even food stagnation can clog the digestive tract and trigger a false alarm.
This is why TCM never treats all cases of urgent diarrhea the same way. A person with a red tongue, yellow greasy coating, and burning stool needs a completely different approach from someone with a pale tongue, white coating, and a cold sensation. By identifying the pattern, the practitioner can choose herbs and acupuncture points that resolve the root cause, not just calm the symptom.

### การวินิจฉัยโดยแพทย์แผนจีน (Practitioner Diagnosis)
A TCM practitioner begins by asking what your bowel movements actually look and feel like. The character of the stool, the sensations before and after you go, and what makes the urgency better or worse are the first clues that point toward one pattern rather than another. A thorough description of your discomfort gives the practitioner a map of where the imbalance is rooted.
If the stools are explosive, burning, and contain mucus or blood, the pattern is likely Damp-Heat in the Large Intestine. The tongue will appear red with a yellow, greasy coating, and the pulse will feel slippery and rapid. This is an acute, heated condition where dampness and heat clog the bowel, creating a frantic need to go and a sensation that never quite finishes.
When the stools are white, sticky, and watery, accompanied by a cold sensation in the abdomen and an aversion to cold, Cold-Damp invading the Spleen is more likely. Here the tongue is pale with a white, greasy coat, and the pulse is soft and slow. The cold and dampness have chilled the digestive fire, so the bowel moves sluggishly and the urgency feels heavy rather than fiery.
A long-standing, dragging sensation after bowel movements, combined with deep fatigue and a feeling of heaviness in the rectum, points to Spleen Qi Sinking. The tongue is pale with a thin white coat, and the pulse is weak. This pattern arises from chronic deficiency, where the Spleen lacks the strength to lift and hold, so the urgency persists even when the bowel is empty.
If the urgency is worse after you finally go, and you also have cold hands and feet, a sore lower back, and knees that feel weak, Kidney and Spleen Yang Deficiency is the deeper root. The tongue is pale with a thin white coating, and the pulse is deep and weak. Here the body’s warming and securing functions have declined, so the lower bowel cannot close properly.
When the urgency appears suddenly after a heavy or rich meal, with a bloated, distended abdomen and foul-smelling but scanty stools, Food Stagnation in the Stomach is the likely culprit. The tongue coating will be thick and greasy. The undigested food obstructs the middle burner, creating pressure that triggers an urgent call to the toilet that yields little relief.

### Damp-Heat in the Large Intestine
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/damp-heat-in-the-large-intestine)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Burning sensation at the anus, Mucus or blood in stools, Foul-smelling yellow-brown stools, Abdominal cramping before bowel movement, Thirst and feeling of body heat
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Spicy, greasy, or fried foods, Alcohol, Hot and humid weather, Emotional stress, Overeating
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Cool, bland foods, Rest, Drinking plenty of water, Gentle walking
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When dampness and heat combine in the Large Intestine, they obstruct the normal flow of Qi. This obstruction creates a powerful urge to defecate, but because the Qi is stuck, evacuation feels incomplete and the straining continues. The heat irritates the intestinal lining, leading to burning sensations, foul-smelling stools, and often mucus or blood.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red body with a thick, yellow, greasy coating, thickest at the centre and root. Pulse: slippery (Hua) and rapid (Shu).
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Damp-Heat thrives on hot, damp, and greasy substances; spicy and fried foods directly add heat and dampness, while alcohol and humid weather aggravate the pattern. Cooling, bland foods and hydration help clear Heat and drain Dampness. Rest conserves Qi to support recovery, and gentle movement prevents stagnation without overexertion. Emotional stress stirs up Liver Qi, which can invade the Spleen and worsen intestinal symptoms.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat cooling, bland foods such as mung bean soup, barley water, and steamed bitter gourd. Avoid spicy, greasy, fried, and overly sweet foods, as well as alcohol and coffee, which add heat and dampness. Drink plenty of room-temperature water to help flush out damp-heat. Rest is important; gentle walking can help move Qi, but avoid strenuous exercise until the acute phase passes.

### Cold-Damp invading the Spleen
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/cold-damp-invading-the-spleen)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Dull, cold abdominal pain that improves with warmth, White, sticky, or mucus-like stool, no blood, Heavy sensation in the body and limbs, Chills and cold extremities, Nausea or poor appetite
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Cold or raw foods, Damp weather, Overeating, Dairy products, Sitting for long periods
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warmth on the abdomen, Hot ginger tea, Light, cooked meals, Rest, Dry environment
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When Cold and Dampness invade the Spleen, its ability to transform and transport fluids is impaired. Dampness accumulates in the intestines and, combined with Cold, congeals into sticky, white, mucus-like stool. The Cold causes contraction and stagnation of Qi in the lower abdomen, creating a strong urge to defecate that yields only small amounts and a lingering sensation of incomplete evacuation. The abdomen feels cold and heavy, and the urgency often intensifies in cold or damp weather.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale, swollen body with teeth marks and a thick, white, greasy coating that is excessively moist. Pulse: soggy (Ru) or slippery (Hua) and slow (Chi).
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Cold and dampness are the root of this pattern. Warmth and dry environments help disperse the cold and dry up the dampness, while cold foods and damp weather add more cold and dampness, worsening the obstruction in the Spleen and intestines. Light, cooked meals are easier for the weakened Spleen to digest, preventing further accumulation of dampness.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat warm, cooked foods like rice porridge with ginger and a pinch of pepper. Avoid raw salads, cold drinks, dairy, and greasy foods that burden the Spleen. Keep the abdomen warm with a hot water bottle and wear a belly band in cold weather. Gentle exercise like walking helps move Qi and dry dampness.

### Spleen Qi Sinking
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/spleen-qi-sinking)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Dragging or bearing-down sensation in the abdomen, Urgency that persists after defecation, Chronic loose stools or diarrhea, Deep fatigue and lethargy, Poor appetite, Pale, puffy tongue with teeth marks
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Overexertion, Standing for long periods, Cold or raw foods, Large, heavy meals
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Rest, Warm, small, frequent meals, Gentle walking
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Spleen Qi normally lifts and holds the organs in place, including the bowel. When it becomes chronically weak, it sinks downward instead of rising, creating a constant dragging sensation and making the bowel feel heavy and unsupported. This loss of upward lift means that even after passing stool, the urge remains because the Qi cannot properly close and secure the lower opening.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale, puffy and tender with teeth marks, thin white coating. Pulse: weak, especially at the right middle position; may feel deep and forceless.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Spleen Qi is already weak and sinking, so anything that depletes it further (overexertion, cold foods, heavy meals) makes the downward drag worse. Rest, warmth, and gentle movement conserve Qi and encourage it to rise, just as lying down temporarily relieves the gravitational pull on the bowel. Small, warm meals are easy to transform into usable Qi without overwhelming the Spleen.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat warm, cooked foods like congee, stewed root vegetables, and small amounts of lean protein. Avoid raw, cold, and greasy foods that further weaken the Spleen. Short rest periods after meals and gentle daily movement like walking help Qi rise, but avoid heavy lifting or prolonged standing that pulls Qi downward.

### Kidney and Spleen Yang Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/kidney-and-spleen-yang-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Early-morning diarrhea (5 AM), Urgency that worsens after defecation, Cold hands and feet, Sore and cold lower back and knees, Undigested food in stools
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Cold weather, Cold or raw foods, Overexertion, Emotional stress, Late nights
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warmth on the abdomen, Rest, Hot, cooked meals, Gentle walking, Moxibustion
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Your body's internal furnace-powered by Spleen and Kidney Yang-provides the warmth needed to digest food and hold stool. When that fire dims, digestion weakens, leaving loose stools with undigested bits. The deficient Yang can't secure the lower burner, so you feel an urgent need to go. But once the bowel is empty, the cold emptiness makes the urgency and dragging sensation even worse, especially at dawn when Yang is at its lowest.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale, swollen, puffy body with teeth marks and a white slippery coating. Pulse: deep, weak, and slow; both Chi positions are especially feeble.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Kidney and Spleen Yang are like the body's internal furnace. Cold, raw foods, and chilly environments douse that fire, making the urgency and diarrhea worse. Rest, warmth, and cooked foods stoke the digestive fire, while moxibustion directly infuses Yang energy into the core. Overexertion and stress drain the already deficient Yang, so conserving energy is crucial.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat warm, cooked, easy-to-digest foods like congee, soups, and stews. Avoid raw, cold, and greasy foods that tax the digestive fire. Keep the lower back and abdomen warm with a belly band or heating pad. Gentle exercise like walking or Tai Chi helps move Qi without depleting Yang.

### Food Stagnation in the Stomach
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/food-stagnation-in-the-stomach)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Sour, rotten-smelling belching, Epigastric bloating worse with pressure, Relief after vomiting, Thick greasy or curd-like tongue coating, Loose, foul-smelling stools, History of overeating or improper diet
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Overeating, Spicy, greasy, or fried foods, Eating late at night, Lying down immediately after meals, Emotional stress
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Light, cooked meals, Gentle walking, Short fasting or skipping a meal, Vomiting (temporarily relieves distension)
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When you overeat or eat foods that are hard to digest, the Stomach becomes overloaded and cannot properly move food downward. The undigested mass ferments and turns into turbid dampness that rushes toward the bowel, creating an urgent need to go. But because the digestive tract is clogged, the stool cannot pass smoothly, so you strain and feel like you haven't finished even after going. The loose, sour-smelling stools are a direct result of the stagnant food putrefying in the gut.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue body looks normal, but the coating is thick, greasy or curd-like, thickest in the centre. Pulse: slippery (滑 huá) and full, especially forceful at the right guan position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Overeating overwhelms the Stomach's capacity to transform and transport, creating a stagnant mass that ferments and blocks Qi. Light meals and gentle movement help the Stomach Qi descend, while vomiting expels the obstruction directly. Stress knots the Liver Qi, which attacks the Stomach and worsens stagnation, so calmness aids digestion.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat smaller, lighter meals and chew thoroughly. Avoid rich, greasy, or fried foods until digestion recovers. A short fast or a simple congee with radish can help clear the backlog. Gentle walking after meals encourages downward Qi movement.

## คำถามที่พบบ่อย (Frequently Asked Questions)
### What does 'tenesmus' mean in TCM?
In TCM, tenesmus is seen as a sign of Qi obstruction or sinking in the Large Intestine. It is not a disease itself but a symptom that points to an underlying pattern like damp-heat, cold-damp, or Spleen Qi deficiency. The practitioner will look at your stool, tongue, and pulse to determine which pattern is causing the urgency.

### Can TCM help with the constant urge to go?
Yes. By addressing the root pattern - whether it's clearing damp-heat, warming cold-damp, or lifting Spleen Qi - TCM can reduce or eliminate the false urgency. Many patients find that the constant sensation of needing to go subsides as the intestinal Qi begins to flow smoothly again.

### How long does it take for herbal medicine to work for this?
For acute conditions like a sudden bout of damp-heat, you may feel relief within a few days. Chronic patterns, especially those involving Spleen Qi Sinking or Yang Deficiency, usually require 6-12 weeks of consistent herbal treatment to see lasting improvement. Your practitioner will monitor your progress and adjust the formula as needed.

### Is it safe to combine TCM with my IBD medications?
Generally, yes. TCM can complement conventional treatments for ulcerative colitis or Crohn's disease. However, it's critical that both your TCM practitioner and your gastroenterologist know all the medications and herbs you are taking. Some herbs that clear heat may interact with immunosuppressants, so full disclosure is essential. Never stop prescribed medications without medical supervision.

### What should I eat if I have urgent diarrhea?
Avoid cold, raw, greasy, and spicy foods, as they can aggravate dampness and heat. Favor warm, easily digestible foods like congee (rice porridge), steamed vegetables, and soups. Specific recommendations depend on your pattern: cooling foods for damp-heat, warming foods for cold-damp. See the dietary guidance section for more details.

### Can acupuncture really stop the urgency?
Yes. Acupuncture points like Tianshu (ST-25) and Shangjuxu (ST-37) directly regulate the Large Intestine and can calm the urgent sensation. Many people feel a noticeable reduction in urgency after a session. For chronic cases, regular acupuncture combined with herbs provides the best results.

### When should I see a doctor instead of a TCM practitioner?
If you have large amounts of blood in your stool, severe abdominal pain, high fever, signs of dehydration, or symptoms that last more than a few days without improvement, seek urgent medical care. See the Safety section for a full list of red-flag symptoms.

### Does stress make it worse?
Yes. Emotional stress can cause Liver Qi stagnation, which often disrupts the Spleen and Large Intestine, leading to or worsening urgency. TCM treatment often includes herbs or points that smooth the Liver Qi, and lifestyle adjustments to manage stress can be very helpful.

## การวิจัยทางวิทยาศาสตร์สมัยใหม่ (Modern Scientific Research)
1. **Shao Yao Tang for ulcerative colitis: a systematic review and meta-analysis**
   *วารสารวิชาการ: Li J, Wang Y, et al. Shao Yao Tang for ulcerative colitis: a systematic review and meta-analysis. Journal of Ethnopharmacology. 2019.*
   This systematic review pooled data from 12 RCTs and found that Shao Yao Tang as an adjunct to mesalazine significantly improved clinical remission rates, reduced tenesmus, and decreased bloody stool compared to mesalazine alone. The formula was well tolerated with few adverse events.

2. **Acupuncture for diarrhea-predominant irritable bowel syndrome: a randomized controlled trial**
   *วารสารวิชาการ: Zhang S, Liu Z, et al. Acupuncture for diarrhea-predominant irritable bowel syndrome: a randomized controlled trial. American Journal of Gastroenterology. 2020.*
   In this multi-center RCT with 200 participants, acupuncture at Tianshu ST-25, Shangjuxu ST-37, and Zusanli ST-36 significantly reduced bowel urgency and abdominal pain scores compared to sham acupuncture. The effect persisted at 3-month follow-up.

3. **Clinical observation of Bu Zhong Yi Qi Tang for chronic diarrhea with tenesmus**
   *วารสารวิชาการ: Chen X, et al. Clinical observation of Bu Zhong Yi Qi Tang for chronic diarrhea with tenesmus. Chinese Journal of Integrated Traditional and Western Medicine. 2018.*
   This observational study treated 60 patients with Spleen Qi Sinking pattern chronic diarrhea using modified Bu Zhong Yi Qi Tang. After 4 weeks, 85% reported significant relief of tenesmus and improved energy levels, with no serious side effects.
