## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนปัจจุบัน (Conventional Medicine View)
### คำอธิบาย (Description)
In conventional medicine, abdominal pain that improves with eating, gentle pressure, or warmth is often linked to conditions like functional dyspepsia, peptic ulcer disease, or chronic gastritis. The discomfort is typically described as a dull ache or gnawing sensation in the upper or central abdomen. When food enters the stomach it can buffer excess acid, temporarily relieving pain, while pressure may soothe muscle tension or visceral sensitivity.
Diagnosis usually involves a review of symptoms, sometimes with endoscopy or imaging to rule out structural problems. However, many cases are labeled as functional-meaning no visible damage is found-and treatment focuses on symptom management rather than identifying why the pain pattern developed in the first place.

### การรักษาหลัก (Treatments)
Standard treatments include over-the-counter antacids, H2 blockers (like famotidine), and proton pump inhibitors (such as omeprazole) to reduce stomach acid. For functional pain, doctors may also prescribe low-dose antidepressants to modulate gut-brain nerve signals. Dietary advice often centers on avoiding trigger foods, eating smaller meals, and reducing stress.

## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนจีน (Traditional Chinese Medicine View)
### ความเข้าใจเกี่ยวกับการแพทย์แผนจีน (TCM Understanding)
TCM sees the digestive system as a cooking pot. The Spleen and Stomach supply the fire (Yang) that transforms food into Qi and blood. When that fire weakens, cold accumulates in the abdomen, causing a dull, persistent ache. The fact that the pain feels better with warmth, gentle pressure, or eating is a hallmark of this internal cold-you're temporarily adding the heat and energy the body lacks.
Where the cold lodges and how deeply it penetrates determines the pattern. If the pain is centered around the navel and comes with fatigue and loose stools, Spleen Yang Deficiency is likely the core issue-the Spleen's warming power has dimmed. When the discomfort sits higher in the stomach, with a cold sensation and relief from warm drinks, the problem is Stomach Yang Deficient and Cold. In more stubborn cases, the cold sinks deeper into the body's core Yang (Kidney Yang), leading to severe, cramping pain and ice-cold limbs-a pattern called Lesser Yin Cold Transformation.
This is why two people with the same Western diagnosis can receive completely different TCM treatments. One may need to gently rebuild the Spleen with sweet, warming herbs, while another requires stronger, more penetrating formulas to rekindle the body's fundamental fire. The tongue and pulse give the practitioner precise clues about which organ is most affected and how deep the cold has settled.

### การวินิจฉัยโดยแพทย์แผนจีน (Practitioner Diagnosis)
A TCM practitioner begins by asking about the nature of the pain - is it dull and persistent, or sharp and cramping? The fact that the pain improves with pressure and eating is already a strong clue that the underlying problem is a deficiency of Yang, the body’s warming and activating force. The next step is to identify which organ system is most affected and how deeply the cold has penetrated.
If the pain is a dull, nagging ache around the navel that eases significantly after meals and with gentle pressure, Spleen Yang Deficiency is the most likely pattern. The person often feels chronically tired, has loose stools, and may have a poor appetite. The tongue is typically pale and puffy with a thin white coating, and the pulse feels deep and weak, reflecting the Spleen’s inability to generate warmth and energy.
When the discomfort is centered more in the upper abdomen, accompanied by a sensation of coldness in the stomach, and is relieved by small, warm meals, the pattern shifts toward Stomach Yang Deficient and Cold. Here, the digestive fire is too weak to transform food, so eating or applying warmth brings temporary relief. Nausea, a preference for hot drinks, and cold hands and feet are common. The tongue is pale with a thin white coat, and the pulse is deep and slow.
If the cold sensation is more profound, extending to the lower abdomen and accompanied by severe fatigue, an aversion to cold, and watery diarrhea, the pattern may be a deeper internal cold known as Lesser Yin Cold Transformation. This indicates that the body’s core Yang is depleted, and the pain may feel more persistent and exhausting. The tongue is pale and moist, and the pulse is deep and faint. This pattern requires urgent warming and strengthening of the body’s foundational Yang.

## กลุ่มอาการการแพทย์แผนจีนและการรักษา (TCM Patterns and Treatment)

### Spleen Yang Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/spleen-yang-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Dull, persistent abdominal pain, Pain improves with warmth and pressure, Loose, watery stools with undigested food, Cold hands and feet, dislikes cold, Fatigue and heaviness in the limbs, Pale, puffy tongue with tooth marks
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Cold or raw foods, Iced drinks, Overwork or fatigue, Cold weather or drafts, Skipping meals
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warmth, Gentle abdominal massage, Rest after eating, Eating warm, cooked meals, Moxibustion on the navel
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  The Spleen needs Yang to transform food into energy and warmth. When Spleen Yang is weak, the digestive fire dims, and cold settles in the abdomen. This internal cold causes a dull, persistent ache. Eating provides a brief burst of warmth that temporarily strengthens the Yang, and gentle pressure offers a sense of support, both of which ease the pain.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale, puffy, and tender body with scalloped edges (tooth marks) and a white, slippery coating. Pulse: deep (沉 chén), slow (迟 chí), and weak (弱 ruò), especially at the right Guan position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Spleen Yang is like a digestive pilot light. Cold, raw foods and exhaustion smother that flame, allowing internal cold to accumulate and cause pain. Warmth, rest, and gentle pressure feed the flame, restoring the Spleen's ability to generate warmth and move Qi, so the ache subsides. Eating a warm meal provides a direct, short-term boost of Yang, which is why the pain often eases right after a meal.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat warm, cooked foods like soups, stews, and congees. Avoid raw salads, cold smoothies, and iced drinks, which douse the digestive fire. Use gentle warming spices such as ginger and cinnamon. Keep your abdomen warm with a scarf or hot water bottle. Rest after meals and avoid overwork, which depletes Yang.

### Stomach Yang Deficient and Cold
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/stomach-yang-deficient-and-cold)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Dull, cold pain in the upper belly that improves with warmth and pressure, Vomiting of clear watery fluid, Poor appetite with preference for hot food and drinks, Feeling of fullness or bloating after eating small amounts, Cold hands and feet, Loose stools, Belching
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Cold or raw foods, Cold weather or drafts, Skipping meals, Overwork or fatigue, Stress and worry
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warmth, Eating warm, cooked meals, Gentle abdominal massage, Rest after eating, Warm drinks
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Stomach Yang is the digestive fire that warms and transforms food. When it becomes deficient, cold accumulates in the middle burner, causing a dull, achy pain that feels better with warmth and gentle pressure. Eating temporarily adds warmth and energy, soothing the pain, but the relief is short-lived because the underlying Yang is still weak.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale, puffy, tender body with teeth marks; white slippery coating. Pulse: deep, slow, weak, especially in the right middle (Guan) position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Stomach Yang is the digestive fire that warms and transforms food. Cold and raw foods extinguish this fire, causing pain, while warm foods and external heat temporarily restore warmth and ease the pain. Pressure and rest help by gently stimulating Qi flow without exhausting the weakened Yang. Overwork and stress further deplete Qi and Yang, exacerbating the deficiency.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat warm, easily digestible meals like soups, stews, and congee. Add warming spices such as ginger, cinnamon, and fennel. Avoid raw, cold, and iced foods that further weaken Stomach Yang. Keep the abdomen warm with a belly band or hot water bottle, especially after meals.

### Lesser Yin Cold Transformation
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/lesser-yin-cold-transformation)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Deep, cramping pain that eases with warmth and pressure, Ice-cold hands and feet, Overwhelming drowsiness and desire to sleep, Diarrhea with undigested food, Extreme fatigue and curling up in bed
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Cold weather or drafts, Cold or raw foods, Overwork or fatigue, Skipping meals
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warmth, Rest, Warm drinks, Gentle abdominal massage, Eating warm, cooked meals
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  In Lesser Yin Cold Transformation, the body's core Yang-especially Kidney Yang-is severely depleted. This deep internal cold fails to warm the digestive organs, causing the abdominal muscles and vessels to contract and congeal. The resulting pain feels deep and cramping. Eating, warmth, and gentle pressure temporarily supply a small boost of Yang and Qi to the middle burner, which is why the pain eases with these measures.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale, puffy, and tender with tooth marks, covered by a white slippery coating. Pulse: fine, faint, deep, and slow.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Cold of any kind-weather, food, or exhaustion-further drains the already deficient Yang and intensifies the internal cold, making the pain worse. Warmth, rest, and warm food all provide a temporary supply of Yang energy that warms the middle burner and relaxes the cold-induced contraction. Gentle pressure and eating work similarly by delivering a small, immediate boost of Qi and warmth to the digestive organs, which is why the pain is soothed.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat only warm, cooked foods and sip warm drinks like ginger tea. Avoid all raw, cold, or chilled items. Keep the abdomen covered and use a hot water bottle or moxibustion over the navel area daily. Prioritize rest and avoid overexertion.

## คำถามที่พบบ่อย (Frequently Asked Questions)
### Why does my stomach pain feel better after I eat?
In TCM, this is a strong sign that your digestive fire (Yang) is weak. Eating provides a temporary burst of warmth and energy, much like adding fuel to a dying flame. The relief is often short-lived, though, because the underlying cold hasn't been corrected. That's why warming herbs and foods are central to treatment.

### Is this the same as having a stomach ulcer?
Not necessarily. While some people with ulcers notice relief after eating, many with this symptom have no structural damage at all-their pain is functional. TCM doesn't diagnose based on whether an ulcer is present; instead, we look at the pain's behavior, your digestion, and your overall energy to identify the underlying pattern of cold and deficiency.

### Can TCM help if I've had this pain for years?
Yes. Chronic, lingering pain that improves with warmth and pressure is a classic indication for TCM's warming, tonic approach. Long-standing cases often involve deeper Yang deficiency, so treatment may take a few months, but many people experience steady improvement in both pain and overall vitality.

### What foods should I avoid?
Avoid raw, cold, and iced foods and drinks-they add more cold to an already chilly digestive system. Dairy, greasy foods, and large meals that are hard to digest also tax your weakened Spleen and Stomach. Instead, favor warm, cooked foods like soups, stews, and congee, and use gentle spices like ginger and cinnamon.

### Will acupuncture hurt?
Acupuncture for this condition is generally very gentle. Points on the abdomen and legs are needled with a light touch, and moxibustion (the burning of dried mugwort) is often used to add deep, penetrating warmth. Most people find the treatment deeply relaxing and comforting.

### Can I combine TCM with my antacid medication?
Yes, in most cases. Herbal formulas and acupuncture can be used alongside over-the-counter antacids or prescription acid reducers. Always tell both your TCM practitioner and your doctor what you're taking, and never stop prescribed medications abruptly. If your symptoms improve, your doctor can help you taper safely.

## การวิจัยทางวิทยาศาสตร์สมัยใหม่ (Modern Scientific Research)
1. **Jianzhong Decoction for Peptic Ulcer: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis of 58 Randomized Controlled Trials**
   *วารสารวิชาการ: Authors not verified. Jianzhong Decoction for Peptic Ulcer: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis of 58 Randomized Controlled Trials. 2017.*
   This meta-analysis pooled 58 RCTs evaluating Jianzhong decoctions (primarily Xiao Jian Zhong Tang and Huang Qi Jian Zhong Tang) for peptic ulcer. The combination of herbal medicine with conventional therapy significantly improved ulcer healing rates and reduced symptom recurrence compared to conventional therapy alone, with a good safety profile.

2. **Acupuncture for functional dyspepsia** ([ลิงก์](https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD008487.pub2))
   *วารสารวิชาการ: Lan L, Zeng F, Liu GJ, et al. Acupuncture for functional dyspepsia. Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews 2018, Issue 10. Art. No.: CD008487.*
   A Cochrane systematic review of seven RCTs (542 participants) found that acupuncture may moderately improve symptoms of functional dyspepsia compared to sham acupuncture, though the evidence was downgraded due to risk of bias and imprecision. No serious adverse events were reported.
