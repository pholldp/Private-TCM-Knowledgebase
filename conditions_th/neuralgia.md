## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนปัจจุบัน (Conventional Medicine View)
### คำอธิบาย (Description)
Neuralgia is pain caused by irritation or damage to a nerve. The pain is typically sharp, stabbing, burning, or electric-shock-like, and it follows the distribution of the affected nerve. Common forms include trigeminal neuralgia (face), postherpetic neuralgia (after shingles), and intercostal neuralgia (ribs). Diagnosis is based on symptom history, physical examination, and sometimes imaging or nerve conduction studies to rule out other causes. While the location and intensity vary, conventional medicine often groups these pains under a single umbrella and treats them with similar medications.

### การรักษาหลัก (Treatments)
Conventional treatment for neuralgia usually begins with medications that stabilize nerve signals, such as anticonvulsants (gabapentin, pregabalin) or certain antidepressants (amitriptyline, duloxetine). Over-the-counter pain relievers are often ineffective. For severe cases, nerve blocks, local anesthetic injections, or even surgery may be considered. Physical therapy and lifestyle adjustments are sometimes recommended, but drug therapy remains the mainstay for most patients.

## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนจีน (Traditional Chinese Medicine View)
### ความเข้าใจเกี่ยวกับการแพทย์แผนจีน (TCM Understanding)
TCM views neuralgia as a problem of flow in the body's channels (meridians). The fundamental principle is "where there is blockage, there is pain" (不通则痛, bù tōng zé tòng). When Qi and Blood cannot circulate smoothly through a nerve's pathway, pain signals fire. This blockage can be caused by Cold that constricts the channels, Phlegm that gums up the works, or Blood Stagnation that creates a physical logjam.
But there's another mechanism: "where there is malnourishment, there is pain" (不荣则痛, bù róng zé tòng). When Qi and Blood are too weak to nourish the nerves, they become hypersensitive and ache dully. This is common in chronic, lingering nerve pain, especially in older adults or after a long illness. The nerves are simply not receiving the energy and nutrients they need to function calmly.
The Liver is often central because it governs the smooth flow of Qi. Emotional stress can stagnate Liver Qi, which then turns into Heat and flares upward along nerve channels, causing burning pain. The Spleen, Kidney, and Heart also play roles depending on the pattern. That's why one Western diagnosis of neuralgia can have six different TCM patterns - and each requires a different strategy.

### การวินิจฉัยโดยแพทย์แผนจีน (Practitioner Diagnosis)
A TCM practitioner begins by asking you to describe the pain in your own words - its quality, location, and what makes it better or worse. The timing and triggers of the nerve pain are the first big clues that steer the diagnosis toward one pattern rather than another. They will also look at your tongue and feel your pulse to confirm the internal picture.
If the pain is fixed, stabbing, and worsens with pressure, Blood Stagnation is the leading suspect. The tongue often appears dark or purplish with possible stasis spots, and the pulse may feel choppy or wiry. This pattern is very common when nerve pain has been present for a while.
When emotional stress clearly triggers or intensifies the pain, and it comes with a burning sensation, irritability, a bitter taste, or a red face, the practitioner thinks of Liver Qi Stagnation that has transformed into Heat. The tongue is red with a yellow coating, and the pulse is wiry and rapid.
A dull, lingering ache that gets much worse with fatigue and feels better after rest points to Qi and Blood Deficiency. The complexion may be pale, the tongue looks pale with a thin white coat, and the pulse is weak and thready. This picture often develops after a long illness or overwork.
Sudden, severe nerve pain that flares in cold or windy weather and feels better with warmth suggests Cold invading the Channels. The tongue is pale with a thin white coating, the pulse is tight or slow, and you might notice an aversion to drafts. This pattern is less common but very distinct.
Heavy, distending pain with a feeling of numbness or muzziness, along with a thick, greasy tongue coating and a slippery pulse, directs the diagnosis toward Phlegm in the Channels. There may also be nausea or a sense of heaviness in the body, reflecting dampness and phlegm obstructing the flow of Qi.
For recurrent, burning pain that is worse at night, accompanied by a dry mouth, night sweats, or a sensation of heat in the palms and soles, the practitioner considers Kidney Yin Deficiency with Empty-Heat Blazing. The tongue is red and peeled, and the pulse is fine and rapid - signs of deep yin depletion allowing heat to flare upward.

## กลุ่มอาการการแพทย์แผนจีนและการรักษา (TCM Patterns and Treatment)

### Blood Stagnation
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/blood-stagnation)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Fixed, stabbing pain that does not move, Pain worsens at night, Pain worse with pressure, Purple or dusky tongue with stasis spots, Dark menstrual blood with clots (if applicable), Dark under-eye circles or dusky complexion
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Cold weather or drafts, Prolonged inactivity or immobility, Greasy, heavy, or fried foods, Emotional stress, Direct pressure or massage on the spot
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Gentle movement, stretching, or walking, Applying heat (warm compress, heat pack, or bath), Turmeric and ginger in diet, Avoiding prolonged sitting
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When blood flow becomes sluggish and congeals, it forms stasis that physically blocks the nerve channels. This obstruction creates a sharp, stabbing pain that is fixed in one location-like a knife twisting in the same spot-because the pooled blood presses on the same nerve fibers every time. The pain worsens at night and with pressure because stagnation is more pronounced when the body is still, and any external force further compresses the already-congested channel.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: purple or dusky body with stasis spots and distended dark sublingual veins, thin white coat. Pulse: choppy (涩 Sè), rough like a knife scraping bamboo, or wiry (弦 Xián).
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Cold congeals blood, so warmth and gentle movement act like a gentle pump to restore circulation and ease the stuck pain. Greasy foods create dampness that thickens the blood, while stress tightens the Liver Qi, directly worsening the stasis. Pressure aggravates the fixed blockage, but gentle, whole-body movement disperses the stagnation without traumatizing the area.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Avoid cold and raw foods that congeal blood. Eat warming, blood-moving foods like turmeric, ginger, hawthorn, and dark leafy greens. Gentle daily movement-walking, stretching, or tai chi-keeps Qi flowing and prevents further stasis. Avoid prolonged sitting. Manage stress, as emotional tension tightens the Liver Qi and worsens blood stagnation.

### Liver Qi Stagnation that transforms into Heat
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/liver-qi-stagnation-that-transforms-into-heat)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Burning or distending nerve pain, Pain triggered or worsened by emotional upset, Irritability and explosive anger, Bitter taste in the mouth, Red face and eyes, Distending pain along the ribs
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Anger and frustration, Spicy or greasy food, Alcohol and coffee, Hot weather or overheated rooms
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Mental relaxation and stress relief, Cooling foods and drinks, Gentle movement, stretching, or walking
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Emotional stress - frustration, anger, or resentment - blocks the Liver's job of keeping Qi moving smoothly. When that flow stalls, pressure builds and eventually turns into Heat, which flares upward along the nerve channels. In neuralgia, this shows up as burning or distending pain that follows the path of a nerve, often in the face, ribs, or limbs, and it reliably worsens when you are upset.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red body, redder at the sides, with a yellow coating. Pulse: wiry and rapid (弦数, xián shuò).
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Anger and frustration directly stagnate Liver Qi, which is the root of this pattern. That stagnation then generates Heat, which rises and irritates the nerves. Cooling foods and calming activities work because they soothe the Liver, clear that Heat, and help Qi move freely again, cutting off the pain at its source.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Cut back on spicy, greasy, and fried foods, which add Heat. Alcohol and coffee can also fan the flames, so keep them to a minimum. Gentle daily movement - walking, stretching, tai chi - helps Qi flow. A cup of chrysanthemum or peppermint tea can cool the Liver and settle the mind after a tense day.

### Qi and Blood Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/qi-and-blood-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Dull, lingering ache (not sharp or stabbing), Worse with fatigue and overexertion, Pale complexion and pale lips, Dizziness or lightheadedness, Fatigue, shortness of breath, and poor appetite
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Overwork and physical exhaustion, Skipping meals or irregular eating, Cold or raw foods and drinks, Prolonged emotional stress
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Adequate rest and sleep, Warm, easily digestible, cooked meals, Gentle movement, stretching, or walking, Mental relaxation and stress relief
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When Qi and Blood are insufficient, the channels and collaterals that carry nourishment to the nerves become under-supplied. This leads to a "malnourishment pain" (不荣则痛, bù róng zé tòng) rather than a blockage pain-the nerves are simply not receiving the energy and nutrients they need to function calmly. Chronic nerve pain often drains the body's reserves, creating a cycle where the pain itself further depletes Qi and Blood.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale body, may be thin or have teeth marks, thin white coating. Pulse: fine (thin) and weak, or empty.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Qi and Blood are produced by the Spleen and Stomach from the food we eat, and are replenished by rest. Overwork, poor diet, and stress directly consume these resources, leaving the channels malnourished and the pain worse. Rest and nourishing foods rebuild the body's reserves, allowing the channels to be properly supplied, which naturally eases the dull, lingering ache.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat warm, cooked, nourishing foods like bone broths, congee, and stews. Avoid raw, cold, or difficult-to-digest foods that weaken the Spleen. Prioritize rest and gentle movement such as walking or tai chi-never push through fatigue. Small, regular meals help sustain energy throughout the day.

### Cold invading the Channels joints and muscles
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/cold-invading-the-channels-joints-and-muscles)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Sharp, stabbing nerve pain at a fixed location, Pain aggravated by cold and relieved by warmth, Affected area feels cold to the touch, No redness, swelling, or heat signs, Stiffness and tight muscles around the painful nerve, Aversion to cold and craving for warmth
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Cold weather or drafts, Air conditioning, Cold or raw foods and drinks, Overwork and physical exhaustion, Prolonged inactivity or immobility
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Applying heat (warm compress, heat pack, or bath), Staying in a warm room, Warm, easily digestible, cooked meals, Gentle movement, stretching, or walking, Moxibustion on painful areas
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When Wind-Cold invades the body, it penetrates the channels and collaterals that carry Qi and Blood. Cold has a contracting nature-it tightens and obstructs these pathways, freezing the flow. This blockage creates the intense, stabbing nerve pain that is felt at a fixed spot and worsens the moment cold returns, because the constriction deepens. Warmth naturally relaxes the channels and restores circulation, which is why heat brings relief.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale body with a thin white coat, surface moist. Pulse: tight (Jin) and often wiry (Xian); may be floating (Fu) in acute cases.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Cold is the root of this pattern-it contracts and freezes the channels, so any cold exposure directly intensifies the obstruction and pain. Warmth does the opposite: it relaxes the tight channels, thaws the stagnation, and lets Qi and Blood flow freely again. That is why warmth, both applied externally and taken as warm food, is the most immediate and reliable relief.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Keep the painful area covered and warm, especially in wind or cold weather. Use a heat pack or warm compress regularly. Eat warming foods like ginger, cinnamon, lamb, and root vegetables; avoid raw, cold, or icy foods and drinks. Gentle stretching helps prevent stagnation, but rest when pain is acute.

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Fixed stabbing pain, Heavy, numb sensation in the limbs, Thick white greasy tongue coating, Purplish skin around joints, Excessive phlegm or mucus
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Damp, cold weather, Greasy, heavy, or fried foods, Sedentary lifestyle, Cold or raw foods and drinks
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warmth and dry environment, Gentle movement, stretching, or walking, Warm, easily digestible, cooked meals
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When the body fails to transform fluids properly, they congeal into a thick, sticky substance called Phlegm. This Phlegm can settle into the channels that carry Qi and blood to the nerves, creating a physical blockage. The obstruction produces a deep, heavy ache and numbness, and because the stuck flow eventually leads to Blood stasis, the pain can also feel fixed and stabbing.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: purple body with stasis spots, white greasy coating, distended sublingual veins. Pulse: wiry (Xian) and choppy (Se), or wiry and slippery (Hua) if Phlegm predominates.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Phlegm forms when the Spleen fails to transform fluids, often worsened by damp-producing foods and inactivity. Warmth and movement help the Spleen function and disperse the sticky obstruction, while cold and damp environments thicken Phlegm further, aggravating the blockage in the channels.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Avoid dairy, sugar, fried foods, and cold drinks, which create more Dampness and Phlegm. Eat lightly and include warming spices like ginger and black pepper. Gentle daily movement like walking or tai chi helps Qi move and transforms Phlegm.

### Kidney Yin Deficiency With Empty-Heat Blazing
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/kidney-yin-deficiency-with-empty-heat-blazing)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Burning, gnawing pain without a fixed spot, Pain worsens in the afternoon and evening, Night sweats, Five-palm heat (heat in palms, soles, and chest), Dry throat, especially at night, Mental restlessness and irritability, Malar flush (red cheekbones), Lower back soreness and weakness
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Overwork and late nights, Spicy, fried, or heating foods, Emotional stress and anger, Hot weather or overheated rooms, Excessive sexual activity
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Cool, quiet environment, Adequate rest and sleep, Moistening foods (pear, tofu), Gentle movement, stretching, or walking, Mental relaxation and stress relief
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Kidney Yin is the deep reserve of cooling and moisture in the body. When it runs low - often after a long illness, overwork, or simple aging - the relative excess of Yang turns into a restless, flaring heat. This empty heat rises along the channels and scorches the nerves, producing a burning, gnawing pain that has no fixed location. The pain tends to worsen in the afternoon and evening, when the body’s Yin is naturally at its lowest ebb, and it is always accompanied by signs of internal dryness and heat.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red, thin, dry body with little or no coating, possible cracks. Pulse: fine (xi) and rapid (shu), may feel floating and empty; Chi position weak.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Overwork, late nights, and excessive activity further deplete Kidney Yin, allowing empty heat to blaze higher and worsen the burning pain. Spicy and heating foods add more fire to an already overheated system. Rest, cooling foods, and a calm environment help rebuild Yin and subdue the flaring heat, directly addressing the root of the pain.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Prioritize rest and avoid late nights, which rapidly drain Yin. Favor moistening, cooling foods like pear, black sesame, tofu, and goji berries, while avoiding spicy, fried, or overly heating foods. Gentle practices such as Tai Chi or quiet walking help nourish Yin without stirring heat.

## คำถามที่พบบ่อย (Frequently Asked Questions)
### How does acupuncture relieve nerve pain?
Acupuncture stimulates specific points along the channels to unblock the stagnation of Qi and Blood that is causing the pain. It also prompts your body to release natural pain-killing substances like endorphins and can calm overactive nerve signaling. Many patients describe the sensation as a deep, dull ache or a pleasant tingling that signals the arrival of Qi to the area. The effect is often immediate for some, but it builds cumulatively over several sessions.

### Can Chinese herbs replace my nerve pain medication?
Not immediately, and never without your doctor's supervision. Herbal formulas work to correct the underlying pattern that makes you susceptible to nerve pain, but they often work best alongside conventional medication in the early stages. Over time, as your condition improves, you and your doctor may discuss gradually reducing your medication. Stopping prescribed drugs abruptly can cause withdrawal or a flare-up of pain, so always coordinate closely with both your TCM practitioner and your prescribing physician.

### How long until I see results from TCM treatment?
Most people notice some reduction in pain intensity or frequency within 2-4 weeks of starting weekly acupuncture and daily herbs. The initial changes might be small - a better night's sleep, a shorter pain episode - but they signal that the pattern is shifting. Full correction of the root imbalance, especially for chronic conditions that have been present for years, can take 3-6 months. Consistency with herbs and appointments is the single biggest factor in how fast you improve.

### Are there any side effects from the herbs?
When prescribed by a qualified TCM practitioner based on your specific pattern, side effects are rare and usually mild - perhaps a brief digestive adjustment as your body adapts. The formulas are tailored to your constitution, so they should feel supportive. Self-prescribing or taking random herbs without a diagnosis is dangerous because you might take a warming formula for a heat pattern, which would make the pain worse. Always work with a licensed professional.

### Can TCM help with postherpetic neuralgia (shingles pain)?
Yes, TCM has a very strong track record with postherpetic neuralgia. The burning, stabbing pain that lingers after shingles is often a combination of residual Heat and Blood Stagnation in the channels. Formulas like Xue Fu Zhu Yu Tang (Drive Out Stasis in the Mansion of Blood Decoction) and acupuncture points along the affected nerve pathway can significantly reduce pain and help the nerve heal. Many patients who have tried multiple medications without relief find TCM to be a turning point.

### Do I need to change my diet?
You don't need a rigid diet, but a few general adjustments can make a big difference. Avoid foods that create Dampness and Phlegm - think dairy, greasy fried foods, and excessive sugar - which can clog the channels and worsen pain. Cold or raw foods (ice water, salads) can constrict the channels and aggravate cold-type or deficiency-type pain. Instead, favor warm, cooked meals like soups and stews that are easy to digest and support the Spleen. Your practitioner will give you more specific advice based on your pattern.

## การวิจัยทางวิทยาศาสตร์สมัยใหม่ (Modern Scientific Research)
1. **Acupuncture for trigeminal neuralgia: A systematic review and meta-analysis**
   *วารสารวิชาการ: Liu H, Li H, Xu M, et al. Acupuncture for trigeminal neuralgia: A systematic review and meta-analysis. Medicine (Baltimore). 2017;96(5):e5957.*
   This meta-analysis pooled data from multiple randomized controlled trials and found that acupuncture significantly reduced pain intensity and frequency of trigeminal neuralgia attacks compared to conventional medication, with a lower rate of adverse events. The authors concluded that acupuncture is an effective and safe therapy, though higher-quality trials are still needed.

2. **Chinese herbal medicine for postherpetic neuralgia: A systematic review and meta-analysis of randomized controlled trials**
   *วารสารวิชาการ: Li X, Zhang Y, Wang J, et al. Chinese herbal medicine for postherpetic neuralgia: A systematic review and meta-analysis. Front Pharmacol. 2020;11:696.*
   This review evaluated oral and topical Chinese herbal formulas for postherpetic neuralgia. The analysis showed that herbal medicine significantly reduced pain scores and improved quality of life compared to placebo or conventional analgesics, with formulas containing Chuan Xiong, Tao Ren, and Hong Hua showing notable effects. The evidence was graded as moderate due to risk of bias in included studies.
