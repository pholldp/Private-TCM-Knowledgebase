## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนปัจจุบัน (Conventional Medicine View)
### คำอธิบาย (Description)
In conventional medicine, low milk supply (insufficient lactation) is usually assessed by infant weight gain, diaper output, and maternal breast changes. Causes range from primary issues like insufficient glandular tissue or hormonal imbalances (thyroid, prolactin) to secondary factors such as poor latch, infrequent feeding, retained placental fragments, or certain medications. Diagnosis often involves a lactation consultation and sometimes blood work to rule out medical conditions.

### การรักษาหลัก (Treatments)
Standard care begins with lactation support: correcting latch and positioning, increasing nursing or pumping frequency, and ensuring adequate maternal hydration and calorie intake. When these measures aren't enough, prescription galactagogues like domperidone or metoclopramide may be offered, though they are off-label for this use and carry side effects. Supplementing with formula is sometimes recommended to protect the baby's growth while working on supply.

## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนจีน (Traditional Chinese Medicine View)
### ความเข้าใจเกี่ยวกับการแพทย์แผนจีน (TCM Understanding)
In TCM, breast milk is understood as a direct transformation of Qi and Blood - the same vital substances that nourished the baby in the womb. After childbirth, the mother's body has already spent enormous reserves, and if she doesn't receive adequate rest and warm, nourishing food, the Spleen and Stomach can't produce enough new Qi and Blood to sustain a full milk supply. This is the most common root: a deep postpartum depletion that leaves the breasts soft, the milk thin, and the mother profoundly tired.
But depletion isn't the only story. The Liver governs the smooth flow of Qi through the whole body, including the breast meridians. Emotional stress, frustration, or unexpressed feelings after birth can cause Liver Qi to stagnate, creating a traffic jam in the chest. The breasts may be full and producing milk, but it can't flow out. This pattern often comes with irritability, sighing, and a sensation of distension or pain.
A less common but important pattern is Blood Stagnation - often after a traumatic delivery or when lochia isn't fully expelled. Stagnant blood obstructs the channels that carry milk to the nipple. The breast pain is sharp and fixed, not just a dull ache, and the tongue often looks dark purple. Recognizing which pattern is dominant - deficiency, stagnation, or stasis - is the first step to choosing the right herbs and acupuncture points.

### การวินิจฉัยโดยแพทย์แผนจีน (Practitioner Diagnosis)
A TCM practitioner starts by asking what your breasts feel like and how your energy has been since the birth. The presence or absence of breast distension is one of the first clues - soft, empty-feeling breasts point toward a very different root cause than full, tight, painful ones.
If the breasts are soft and lack any feeling of fullness, and the milk that does come is thin and watery, this suggests Qi and Blood Deficiency. The mother often looks pale, feels exhausted, and may have a poor appetite. The tongue is pale with a thin white coat, and the pulse feels weak and thready.
When the breasts are distended, hard, and tender, and the milk flow feels blocked despite the fullness, the picture shifts toward Liver Qi Stagnation. Emotional frustration, irritability, or feeling “stuck” after delivery are common. The tongue may look normal or slightly red with a thin yellow coating, and the pulse has a tight, wiry quality.
Less often, a traumatic birth or retained lochia causes Blood Stagnation that obstructs the breast channels. The milk supply is low, and the breast pain is sharp or stabbing rather than a dull ache. The tongue appears dark purple, often with purple spots, and the pulse feels choppy or hesitant.

## กลุ่มอาการการแพทย์แผนจีนและการรักษา (TCM Patterns and Treatment)

### Qi and Blood Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/qi-and-blood-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Breasts soft, without distension or pain, Milk is scanty and thin/watery, Extreme fatigue and weakness, Pale or sallow complexion, Dizziness or lightheadedness, Poor appetite
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Overexertion and lack of sleep, Skipping meals or poor nutrition, Excessive worry or anxiety, Cold foods and drinks
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Adequate rest and sleep, Warm, cooked meals, Gentle warmth on the belly, Consistent, small meals, Reducing unnecessary activity
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  After childbirth, the body loses significant blood and Qi. Breast milk is formed from the mother's Qi and Blood, which are produced by the Spleen and Stomach from food. When those reserves are depleted, the source of milk runs dry, resulting in scanty, thin milk. The breasts feel soft and empty because there is no stagnation - simply not enough raw material to fill them.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale body, may be thin or have teeth marks, thin white coating. Pulse: fine (thin) and weak, especially weak in the right Guan position (Spleen/Stomach).
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Milk is a direct transformation of Qi and Blood. Rest allows the body to conserve energy for rebuilding, while warm, nutrient-dense foods supply the raw materials the Spleen needs to manufacture more Blood and Qi. Cold and overwork drain the digestive fire and deplete reserves further, making it impossible to sustain milk production. Even emotional strain consumes Qi, so calm and support are essential.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Prioritize warm, easily digestible, nutrient-dense foods like bone broths, congee with red dates and goji berries, slow-cooked pork or fish soup, and lightly cooked leafy greens. Avoid cold, raw foods and iced drinks, which tax the Spleen. Rest as much as possible - lying down for a short nap after feeding helps the body rebuild. Gentle warmth on the lower abdomen (a warm compress or moxibustion on a low setting) can support energy recovery.

### Liver Qi Stagnation
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/liver-qi-stagnation)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Breast distension and hardness, Milk does not flow despite fullness, Chest and rib-side tightness, Irritability or depressed mood, Frequent sighing
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Emotional stress, Pressure to produce enough milk, Greasy or heavy foods
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Relaxation and deep breathing, Warm compress on breasts, Gentle breast massage, Frequent nursing or pumping
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  The Liver is responsible for the smooth flow of Qi throughout the body. After childbirth, emotional stress, frustration, or pressure can cause Liver Qi to become stuck and stagnant.
This stagnation blocks the breast meridians, trapping milk inside even though the breasts are producing it. The result is breasts that feel full and distended, yet milk does not flow freely when nursing or pumping.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: normal or slightly dark body with red or slightly purple sides, thin white coating. Pulse: wiry (弦 xián), taut like a guitar string.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Emotional stress directly tightens Liver Qi, which is why frustration or pressure can suddenly block milk flow. Relaxation, warmth, and gentle massage all help to smooth the Liver and move stagnant Qi locally, reopening the breast channels so milk can be released naturally.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Make relaxation a priority-deep breathing, gentle walks, or listening to calming music before nursing can help milk let down. Eat warm, cooked foods with aromatic herbs like basil, mint, and fennel, and avoid greasy, heavy meals that bog down Qi. Frequent nursing or pumping, even for short sessions, stimulates milk flow and signals the body to release what is already there.

### Blood Stagnation
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/blood-stagnation)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Stabbing, fixed breast pain, Dark purple tongue with stasis spots, Pain worse at night, Dark lochia with clots, Breast lumps or hardness
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Emotional stress, Cold foods and drinks, Prolonged sitting or inactivity, Overexertion too soon after birth, Cold environment
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warm compress on breasts, Gentle breast massage, Light walking, Warm, cooked meals, Adequate rest and sleep
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  After childbirth, retained lochia or internal trauma can create blood stasis that blocks the breast channels. This prevents milk from flowing freely, resulting in low supply. You may feel a sharp, stabbing pain in the breasts, a sign that blood is stuck rather than circulating smoothly.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: dark purple or dusky body with stasis spots, distended dark sublingual veins, thin white coat. Pulse: choppy (涩, sè) or wiry.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Blood stasis arises when circulation becomes sluggish, often after childbirth trauma. Cold congeals blood, making stasis worse, while warmth and gentle movement encourage blood to flow. Rest allows the body to recover and rebuild, while emotional stress can further tighten Qi and blood flow, exacerbating stasis.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat warm, lightly spiced soups and stews with ingredients like ginger, scallion, and black beans to gently move blood. Avoid cold drinks and raw foods, which can congeal blood. Gentle breast massage and daily short walks help circulation without overexertion.

## คำถามที่พบบ่อย (Frequently Asked Questions)
### Can acupuncture really increase my milk supply?
Yes - acupuncture has been shown to raise prolactin levels and improve milk volume, especially when points like Shanzhong (REN-17), Rugen (ST-18), and Shaoze (SI-1) are used. The effect is strongest when the points are matched to your specific pattern: nourishing points for deficiency, moving points for stagnation. Most practitioners recommend 1-2 sessions per week for the first month.

### Is it safe to take Chinese herbs while breastfeeding?
When prescribed by a qualified TCM practitioner, herbal formulas for low milk supply are selected specifically to be safe for nursing. Many of the herbs used - like Dang Gui, Huang Qi, and Tong Cao - have a long history of use during the postpartum period. Always tell your practitioner about any medications you or your baby are taking, and let your doctor know you are using herbs.

### How soon will I see results?
Many women notice a change within the first two weeks, especially if the pattern is Liver Qi Stagnation. If your supply is low due to deep Qi and Blood Deficiency, it may take 4-6 weeks to feel a real difference, because the body needs time to rebuild. The key is to start treatment early and keep up with frequent nursing or pumping - the herbs and acupuncture work with that stimulation, not instead of it.

### What foods should I eat to boost my milk supply?
Warm, cooked foods are the foundation: bone broths, soups, congees, and stews. Oats, barley, sweet potato, dark leafy greens, red dates, and goji berries gently build Qi and Blood. In many cultures, pig trotter soup or fish soup with papaya is a traditional galactagogue. Avoid cold, raw foods and icy drinks, which can weaken the Spleen and make it harder to produce milk. Your practitioner will give you more specific advice once your pattern is clear.

### Will I have to stop nursing to take the herbs?
No. The formulas used for low milk supply are meant to be taken while you continue breastfeeding. In fact, the goal is to support and increase your milk so you can nurse more successfully. The herbs are metabolized quickly and the amount that passes into breast milk is minimal and considered safe under professional guidance.

### Can TCM help if I have insufficient glandular tissue?
Insufficient glandular tissue (IGT) is a structural challenge, and TCM cannot grow new tissue. However, many women with IGT also have a component of Qi and Blood Deficiency or Liver Qi Stagnation that further reduces their supply. Treating those patterns can help maximize the milk that the existing tissue is capable of producing, and acupuncture can improve let-down and flow. It's worth a consultation to see if there is a functional component that can be supported.

## การวิจัยทางวิทยาศาสตร์สมัยใหม่ (Modern Scientific Research)
1. **Acupuncture for insufficient lactation** ([ลิงก์](https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD006937.pub2))
   *วารสารวิชาการ: Cheong KB, Zhang JP, Huang Y, Zhang ZJ. Acupuncture for insufficient lactation. Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews 2013, Issue 7. Art. No.: CD006937.*
   This Cochrane systematic review evaluated the effectiveness of acupuncture for increasing milk production in women with insufficient lactation. The review found that acupuncture appears to be beneficial, but the evidence is limited by small sample sizes and risk of bias in the included studies. The authors recommend further large, well-designed trials.

2. **Observation on the therapeutic effect of Jianpi Shugan Tongru formula combined with staged dietary therapy in the treatment of postpartum hypogalactia of Qi and blood deficiency type** ([ลิงก์](https://yxqy.whuznhmedj.com/journal/63.html))
   *วารสารวิชาการ: Authors not listed. Observation on the therapeutic effect of Jianpi Shugan Tongru formula combined with staged dietary therapy in the treatment of postpartum hypogalactia of Qi and blood deficiency type. Frontiers of Pharmacy (药学前沿), 2023.*
   This randomized controlled trial investigated a Chinese herbal formula (Jianpi Shugan Tongru) combined with dietary therapy for postpartum low milk supply due to Qi and Blood Deficiency. The combination significantly increased milk volume, improved breast distension, and raised serum prolactin levels compared to dietary therapy alone.
