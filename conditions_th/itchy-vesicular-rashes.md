## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนปัจจุบัน (Conventional Medicine View)
### คำอธิบาย (Description)
Eczema (atopic dermatitis) is a chronic inflammatory skin condition that causes dry, intensely itchy skin with red, scaly patches and sometimes small blisters that weep clear fluid. It often begins in childhood and can flare periodically throughout life. The exact cause is not fully understood, but it involves a combination of genetic factors, a compromised skin barrier, immune system dysregulation, and environmental triggers like allergens, irritants, or stress.
Diagnosis is usually made by a doctor based on the appearance of the rash, its location, and the patient's history. Standard tests are rarely needed unless an infection is suspected.

### การรักษาหลัก (Treatments)
Conventional management focuses on moisturizing the skin barrier (emollients), reducing inflammation during flares (topical corticosteroids or calcineurin inhibitors), and controlling itching (oral antihistamines). For moderate to severe eczema, doctors may prescribe systemic immunosuppressants, biologic drugs, or phototherapy. The goal is to control symptoms and prevent flares, though a definitive cure remains elusive.

## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนจีน (Traditional Chinese Medicine View)
### ความเข้าใจเกี่ยวกับการแพทย์แผนจีน (TCM Understanding)
In TCM, the skin is seen as an extension of the internal organs, particularly the Lungs, Spleen, and Liver. The Lungs govern the skin's opening and closing (the pores), the Spleen transforms food into Qi and manages fluid metabolism, and the Liver ensures the smooth flow of Qi and Blood. When these organs are out of balance, pathogenic factors like Dampness, Heat, and Wind can accumulate internally and then overflow onto the skin, producing the characteristic rash and itch of eczema.
The type of eczema tells the story of what's happening inside. Acute, weepy, red rashes point to an excess of Damp-Heat or an invasion of Wind-Heat - the body is trying to push out something it doesn't need. Chronic, dry, thickened skin, on the other hand, usually signals a deficiency of Blood or Yin, meaning the skin isn't getting enough nourishment.
A third common pattern involves a weak Spleen that generates persistent, low-grade dampness, leading to dull, oozing eczema that never fully clears. This is why two people with the same Western diagnosis of eczema can need completely different TCM treatments. A red, oozing rash with a yellow tongue coating calls for clearing Heat and draining Dampness, while a dry, scaly rash with a pale tongue requires nourishing Blood and moistening dryness. By reading the skin, the tongue, and the pulse together, a TCM practitioner identifies the unique pattern behind each person's eczema.

### การวินิจฉัยโดยแพทย์แผนจีน (Practitioner Diagnosis)
A TCM practitioner begins by looking at the rash itself: is it red, wet, and oozing, or dry, scaly, and thickened? Acute, weepy eruptions with heat point toward patterns involving Damp-Heat or Wind-Heat, while chronic dry skin suggests Blood or Yin deficiency.
If the rash is bright red with blisters and yellow oozing, and the tongue is red with a thick yellow greasy coating, the picture is Liver and Gallbladder Damp-Heat. This pattern often flares quickly, accompanied by a feeling of heaviness, thirst, and dark urine. The pulse will feel slippery and rapid.
When the rash appears suddenly with red papules and intense itching that worsens with heat, and the tongue is red with a thin yellow coating, Wind-Heat is likely. The pulse will be floating, indicating the pathogen is on the surface. This pattern often follows exposure to wind or allergens and may come and go quickly.
In subacute or lingering eczema, the skin looks dull, less red, and weeps only a little. If the person also complains of poor appetite, bloating, loose stools, and fatigue, the practitioner suspects Spleen Deficiency with Dampness. The tongue is pale and swollen with a white greasy coating, and the pulse feels soft and slow.
For chronic, dry eczema with thick, rough, pigmented skin and itching that is worse at night, Blood Deficiency with External Wind is the key pattern. The tongue is pale with a thin white coat, and the pulse is fine. The body lacks enough nourished blood to moisten the skin, allowing internal wind to stir up itch.
A similar but deeper dryness appears in Yin and Blood Deficiency, where the skin is extremely dry and flaky, often with a burning sensation, dry mouth, and constipation. The tongue is red with little or no coating, and the pulse is fine. This pattern reflects a longer-term depletion of fluids, common in older adults or after prolonged illness.

### Liver and Gallbladder Damp-Heat
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/liver-and-gallbladder-damp-heat)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Red, blistering, oozing rash with yellow discharge, Burning and intense itching, Bitter taste in the mouth, Irritability and restlessness, Dark, scanty urine, Distension or discomfort under the ribs
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Anger and frustration, Spicy, greasy, or fried foods, Alcohol, Hot, humid weather, Scratching and friction
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Cool, dry environment, Cool compresses, Light, bland meals, Stress reduction and calm mood, Gentle sweating through movement
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When the Liver and Gallbladder become overwhelmed by Dampness and Heat, the body tries to push the excess out through the skin. This internal stew of Damp-Heat surges upward and outward, flooding the skin with hot, sticky fluids that produce the classic acute eczema picture: red, swollen skin, weeping blisters, and intense burning itch.
The Liver’s normal job of smoothing Qi flow is disrupted by the Heat, so frustration and irritability often flare alongside the rash. The greasy yellow coating on the tongue and the bitter taste in the mouth are telltale signs that the trouble is rooted in the Liver and Gallbladder, not just the skin’s surface.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red body with redder sides, covered by a thick, yellow, greasy coating. Pulse: wiry, slippery, and rapid (弦滑数, xián huá shuò).
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Anger and frustration cause Liver Qi to stagnate and generate more Heat, which is already the core problem, so calming the mind directly cools the skin. Spicy, greasy foods and alcohol are damp and hot in nature - they add to the internal Damp-Heat and make the rash weep and burn. A cool, dry environment and bland foods help the body drain the excess Damp-Heat, while gentle movement opens the pores to release some of the trapped Heat without over-stimulating it.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Avoid alcohol, coffee, spicy foods, and greasy fried dishes, as they all add fuel to the Damp-Heat fire. Fill your plate with cooling, water-rich vegetables like cucumber, celery, and mung bean sprouts. Sip chrysanthemum tea to gently cool the Liver. Keep the skin clean and dry, but use lukewarm water - hot water will worsen the itch. Wear loose, breathable cotton clothing and try not to scratch, which breaks the skin and invites more Heat.

### Spleen Deficiency with Dampness
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/spleen-deficiency-with-dampness)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Dull, oozing skin lesions with little redness, Less intense itching than acute eczema, Poor appetite and bloating after meals, Loose, unformed stools or diarrhoea, Heaviness in the body and limbs, Fatigue and lack of energy, Thirst with no desire to drink
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Cold, raw, or greasy foods, Damp or humid weather, Fatigue and overexertion, Worry and overthinking
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warm, cooked meals, Dry, comfortable environment, Gentle sweating through movement, Rest and avoiding overwork
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When the Spleen is weak, it fails to transform food and fluids properly. This leads to an accumulation of internal dampness, which is heavy and turbid. Since the skin is governed by the Spleen, this dampness can overflow into the skin, forming dull, oozing vesicles that itch. The lack of strong Qi also means the skin lacks nourishment and the rash heals slowly, often lingering in a subacute or chronic phase.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale, swollen body with teeth marks, white greasy coating, moist surface. Pulse: soggy (Ru) and slow (Huan), possibly slippery.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: The Spleen dislikes cold and dampness; raw, cold, and greasy foods overwhelm its ability to transform fluids, directly worsening internal dampness and skin oozing. Damp weather adds external dampness to the internal burden. Fatigue and excessive mental strain further weaken Spleen Qi, making it unable to manage fluids. Conversely, warm, cooked foods are easy to digest and support Spleen function, while rest and gentle movement restore Qi and help the body clear dampness naturally.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat warm, cooked, easily digestible foods like soups, congee, and steamed vegetables. Avoid cold, raw, and greasy foods, as well as dairy and sugar, which create dampness. Gentle daily movement like walking or stretching helps the Spleen move Qi and clear dampness. Avoid overwork and get enough rest-fatigue directly weakens the Spleen.

### Blood Deficiency with External Wind
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/blood-deficiency-with-external-wind)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Dry, flaky, rough skin with scaling and fissures, Burning and intense itching, Pale or sallow complexion with dull nails and dry hair, Aversion to wind or drafts with rashes that come and go, Dizziness, poor sleep, or scanty periods
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Wind and drafts, Dry environment, Fatigue and overexertion, Spicy or drying foods, Night time, Excessive sweating
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Moisturizing the skin, Warm, cooked meals, Rest and avoiding overwork, Avoiding drafts, Gentle sweating through movement
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When the body's Blood is deficient, the skin loses its nourishment and becomes dry, rough, and prone to cracking. This weakness allows external Wind to invade the skin's surface, triggering intense itching and rashes that come and go suddenly. The constant scratching can lead to small blisters and thickened, scaly patches, especially in chronic cases.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale, thin body with a thin white and slightly dry coat. Pulse: fine (thin) and wiry, possibly floating at the superficial level.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Blood Deficiency leaves the skin undernourished and vulnerable to Wind invasion, which triggers itching and rashes. Stress and overwork consume Blood further, while dry environments and wind aggravate the external pathogen. Night time is when Blood naturally returns to the Liver, so if Blood is deficient, the skin becomes even drier and itchier. Nourishing foods and rest replenish Blood, while moisturizing and avoiding drafts protect the skin from Wind, allowing it to heal.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat warm, nourishing foods that build Blood, such as bone broths, dark leafy greens, red meat, and black sesame seeds. Avoid raw, cold, and spicy foods that can damage the Spleen or dry out the skin. Keep the skin well-moisturized with gentle, fragrance-free creams after bathing. Protect your skin from wind and dry air by wearing soft, breathable clothing and using a humidifier indoors. Gentle exercise like walking or tai chi can help circulate Blood, but avoid heavy sweating that depletes fluids.

- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Wind-Heat invades from the outside, striking the skin and the Lung system. The Wind carries the Heat pathogen to the surface, where it fights with the body's defensive Qi, producing red, itchy papules and tiny vesicles. Because the pathogen is still at the exterior level, the rash often appears suddenly and flares up with heat, wind, or stress.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red tip and edges with a thin yellow coating. Pulse: floating and rapid.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Wind-Heat attacks the exterior, so anything that adds heat or stirs up wind will flare the rash. Cooling the body, avoiding hot and spicy triggers, and using acupuncture to release the exterior all help dispel the pathogen and calm the skin. Gentle sweating opens the pores to let the wind-heat escape, while cold compresses constrict the surface to reduce itching temporarily.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Avoid spicy, greasy, and deep-fried foods, as well as alcohol, which can generate internal heat. Wear loose, breathable cotton clothing and keep the skin cool. Gentle sweating through exercise like walking or yoga can help release the exterior, but avoid overheating. Peppermint or chrysanthemum tea can soothe the skin from within.

### Yin and Blood Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/yin-and-blood-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Dry, flaky, rough skin with scaling and fissures, Burning and intense itching, Dry mouth and throat with desire to sip water, Night sweats and five-centre heat, Dizziness, poor sleep, or scanty periods
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Spicy, greasy, or fried foods, Hot baths or saunas, Fatigue and overexertion, Dry environment, Late nights and insufficient sleep
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Cool, dry environment, Hydrating foods like pear juice, Adequate sleep and rest, Moisturizing the skin
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Yin and Blood are the body's cooling moisture and nourishment. When they run low, the skin loses its natural hydration and becomes dry, thin, and cracked. The mild internal heat that accompanies Yin Deficiency rises to the surface, causing an intense itch-especially at night when Yin should be keeping the body cool and settled.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale to pale-red, thin, dry body with cracks, scanty or peeled coating. Pulse: fine (细 xì) and rapid (数 shuò).
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Yin and Blood are the body's coolant and moisturizer. Spicy foods, heat, and overwork consume them further, making skin drier and itchier. Cooling, moistening, and restful habits replenish Yin and Blood, calming the internal heat and restoring the skin's natural barrier.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat moistening, cooling foods like pears, black sesame, spinach, and bone broths. Avoid spicy, fried, or drying foods. Keep skin well-hydrated with gentle, fragrance-free creams. Avoid long hot showers; use lukewarm water. Gentle exercise like Tai Chi or restorative yoga supports Yin without overheating.

## คำถามที่พบบ่อย (Frequently Asked Questions)
### Can TCM help if topical steroids haven't worked?
Yes, many patients turn to TCM when conventional creams provide only temporary relief. Because TCM addresses the internal imbalance driving the eczema, it can reduce the frequency and severity of flares rather than just suppressing symptoms. It often works well alongside your existing skincare routine, and some patients find they need less steroid cream over time.

### How long does it take to see results?
Itching often decreases within the first two weeks of treatment. Visible skin healing takes longer - acute, wet eczema may clear significantly in 4 to 6 weeks, while chronic dry eczema can take 3 to 6 months of consistent herbs and acupuncture. The key is patience; the goal is lasting change, not a quick fix.

### Do I need to change my diet?
Diet plays a supporting role in TCM treatment. Generally, you'll be advised to avoid foods that create internal dampness and heat - such as spicy dishes, deep-fried foods, alcohol, and excessive sugar. Instead, focus on lightly cooked vegetables, whole grains, and cooling foods like mung beans or cucumber. Your practitioner will tailor specific advice to your pattern.

### Can acupuncture really stop the itching?
Yes, acupuncture is very effective at relieving itch. Points like Quchi (LI-11) and Xuehai (SP-10) are used specifically to cool the blood and calm the skin, often providing immediate, though temporary, relief. Over time, regular acupuncture helps correct the underlying imbalance so the itch doesn't return as strongly.

### Is it safe to use Chinese herbs with my prescription creams?
In most cases, yes. TCM herbs are taken internally and work systemically, so they don't interfere with topical medications. However, always tell both your TCM practitioner and your dermatologist about everything you're using. Some herbs can interact with immunosuppressant drugs, so a coordinated approach is essential.

### Will my eczema come back after TCM treatment?
The aim of TCM is to resolve the root imbalance, not just suppress symptoms. Many patients find their eczema goes into long-term remission and stays away, or returns only as a mild, manageable patch during times of extreme stress or dietary slip. Some may need occasional maintenance treatments to stay clear, but overall the condition becomes far less burdensome.

### Can TCM treat eczema in babies or children?
Yes, TCM can be adapted for children. Herbal doses are adjusted for weight, and acupuncture can be replaced with gentle acupressure or pediatric tui na massage. Dietary adjustments are often the first and safest step. Always work with a practitioner experienced in pediatric care.

### What does my tongue tell you about my eczema?
Your tongue is a map of your internal health. A red tongue with a thick yellow coating suggests Damp-Heat, while a pale, puffy tongue with teeth marks points to Spleen deficiency. A dry, cracked tongue indicates Yin or Blood deficiency. These signs help your practitioner choose the right herbs and acupuncture points for your specific eczema pattern.

## การวิจัยทางวิทยาศาสตร์สมัยใหม่ (Modern Scientific Research)
1. **Acupuncture for atopic eczema: a systematic review and meta-analysis** ([ลิงก์](https://doi.org/10.1136/acupmed-2012-010150))
   *วารสารวิชาการ: Lee MS, Choi TY, Kim JI, et al. Acupuncture for atopic eczema: a systematic review and meta-analysis. Acupuncture in Medicine. 2012;30(4):298-303.*
   This systematic review included 6 RCTs and found that acupuncture significantly reduced itch intensity and improved overall eczema severity compared to conventional medication. The authors called for larger, more rigorous trials to confirm the findings.

2. **Chinese herbal medicine for atopic eczema** ([ลิงก์](https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD008642.pub2))
   *วารสารวิชาการ: Gu S, Yang AW, Xue CC, et al. Chinese herbal medicine for atopic eczema. Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews. 2013, Issue 9. Art. No.: CD008642.*
   A Cochrane systematic review assessing oral and topical Chinese herbal medicine for atopic eczema. The review found some evidence that certain herbal preparations may reduce eczema severity and corticosteroid use, but noted that the overall quality of evidence was low due to small study sizes and methodological limitations.

3. **Efficacy and safety of a Chinese herbal medicine formula (Xiao Feng San) for atopic dermatitis: a randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial** ([ลิงก์](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1365-2133.2007.08036.x))
   *วารสารวิชาการ: Hon KL, Leung TF, Ng PC, et al. Efficacy and safety of a Chinese herbal medicine formula (Xiao Feng San) for atopic dermatitis: a randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial. British Journal of Dermatology. 2007;157(2):357-363.*
   This RCT evaluated Xiao Feng San in 85 children with atopic dermatitis. After 12 weeks, the herbal group showed significantly greater improvement in SCORAD index and reduced topical corticosteroid use compared to placebo, with no serious adverse events.
