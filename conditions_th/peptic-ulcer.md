## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนปัจจุบัน (Conventional Medicine View)
### คำอธิบาย (Description)
Peptic ulcers are open sores that develop on the lining of the stomach or the upper part of the small intestine (duodenum). The most common cause is infection with Helicobacter pylori bacteria, followed by long-term use of nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs (NSAIDs) like ibuprofen. The hallmark symptom is a burning or gnawing pain in the upper abdomen, often between meals or at night, that may temporarily improve with eating or antacids. Diagnosis is typically confirmed via endoscopy or tests for H. pylori.

### การรักษาหลัก (Treatments)
Standard treatment involves a combination of antibiotics to eradicate H. pylori if present, and medications that reduce stomach acid production - proton pump inhibitors (PPIs) like omeprazole, or H2-receptor blockers. Antacids may provide short-term symptom relief. Patients are also advised to avoid NSAIDs, alcohol, and smoking. In severe cases, endoscopic procedures or surgery may be needed for bleeding or perforation.

## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนจีน (Traditional Chinese Medicine View)
### ความเข้าใจเกี่ยวกับการแพทย์แผนจีน (TCM Understanding)
In TCM, the Stomach is the 'sea of grain and water,' responsible for receiving food and sending it downward. When its Qi flows smoothly downward, digestion is comfortable. But if something blocks that downward movement - whether emotional stress, cold, or undigested food - Qi stagnates and rises, causing pain, bloating, and acid reflux. An ulcer is seen not just as a physical sore, but as a sign that the Stomach's ecosystem is out of balance.
The Liver plays a central role because it controls the smooth flow of Qi throughout the body, including the Stomach's downward movement. Emotional stress, frustration, or chronic anger can cause Liver Qi to stagnate, which then 'invades' the Stomach (wood overacting on earth). This is why stress is a major trigger for ulcer pain - it directly disrupts the Stomach's rhythm, leading to distending pain that radiates to the ribs, belching, and acid reflux.
On the other hand, when the Stomach's warming Yang is deficient - from chronic illness, aging, or overconsumption of cold foods - the digestive fire weakens. Cold accumulates, congealing Qi and Blood, producing a dull, constant ache that feels better with warmth and gentle pressure. This pattern is like a fire that's gone out; the Stomach can't process food properly, leading to poor appetite and loose stools.
Diet is another major factor. Overeating, eating too quickly, or consuming greasy, spicy, or sugary foods can overwhelm the Spleen and Stomach, creating Dampness and Heat. This Damp-Heat smolders in the stomach lining, causing burning pain, a sticky bitter taste, and a thick yellow tongue coating. Similarly, acute food stagnation from a heavy meal can cause sudden bloating, sour belching, and relief only after vomiting - a temporary but intense pattern.
In chronic cases, long-standing inflammation can consume the Stomach's Yin, the moistening and nourishing aspect. This leaves the stomach lining dry and vulnerable to 'empty heat,' causing a burning hunger-pain that's worse with eating. When Qi stagnation or Cold persists for years, Blood flow slows and congeals, leading to fixed, stabbing pain - a sign of deeper damage that requires moving Blood and healing the tissue.

### การวินิจฉัยโดยแพทย์แผนจีน (Practitioner Diagnosis)
A TCM practitioner first explores the character of the epigastric pain-whether it is sharp, dull, burning, or stabbing-and what makes it better or worse. The timing, dietary triggers, emotional state, and sensations like cold or heat are the first clues that guide the diagnosis toward one of several distinct patterns.
When the pain is a dull ache that feels better with warmth and gentle pressure, and worsens after cold food or fatigue, Stomach Yang Deficient and Cold is likely. The person often feels chilly, has loose stools, and shows a pale tongue with a white coating and a deep, slow pulse.
If the discomfort is a burning sensation with a feeling of hunger that is not relieved by eating, accompanied by dry mouth and a red tongue with little or no coating, Stomach Yin Deficiency is the picture. This pattern often develops after long-standing inflammation that has consumed the stomach’s moistening fluids.
A sudden, sharp pain that strikes right after exposure to cold or drinking iced beverages, and eases with a hot compress, signals Cold invading the Stomach. The tongue coating is white, the pulse is tight, and the pain is intense but transient-it reflects a temporary congelation of Stomach Qi.
When the pain is linked to emotional stress, with distension and belching that may radiate to the sides of the ribs, Liver Qi Stagnation invading the Stomach is the key pattern. The tongue looks normal or has a thin white coating, and the pulse feels wiry, especially on the left side.
If the epigastric area feels full and distended, with sour regurgitation, a thick greasy tongue coating, and pain that improves after vomiting, Food Stagnation in the Stomach is present. This pattern follows overeating or eating rich, hard-to-digest foods, and the pulse often feels slippery.
A heavy, burning pain with nausea, a bitter taste, and a sensation of fullness that does not ease after bowel movements points to Damp-Heat in Stomach and Spleen. The tongue is red with a thick, yellow, greasy coating, and the pulse is rapid and slippery, reflecting inflammation and fluid retention in the middle burner.
When the pain is fixed, stabbing, and worse at night, and may be accompanied by dark, tarry stools, Stomach Blood Stagnation has developed. The tongue appears purple or has dark spots, and the pulse feels choppy. This pattern often arises from long-term Qi stagnation or chronic ulceration.

### Liver Qi Stagnation invading the Stomach
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/liver-qi-stagnation-invading-the-stomach)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Distending, bloating pain in the upper abdomen, Pain that radiates to the rib area, Symptoms flare with emotional stress or frustration, Frequent belching or acid reflux, Irritability, moodiness, or frequent sighing, Feeling of a lump or blockage in the throat
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Emotional stress, anger, or frustration, Skipping meals or eating irregularly, Greasy, fried, or spicy foods, Alcohol, Rushed or tense mealtimes
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Stress management and relaxation, Warm, cooked meals, Gentle walking or light exercise, Deep breathing and sighing, Small, frequent meals
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When stress, frustration, or bottled-up emotions cause the Liver Qi to stagnate, it can overact on the Stomach - wood attacking earth. This disrupts the Stomach's normal downward movement, trapping Qi in the epigastrium and creating the distending pain that is typical of this pattern. Over time, the persistent Qi stagnation can erode the stomach lining and contribute to the formation of an ulcer.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: normal or slightly red body, with redder sides; thin white coat. Pulse: wiry (弦 xián), especially at the left middle position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Emotional stress is the root trigger - it causes Liver Qi to stagnate and then attack the Stomach, so calming the mind and smoothing the Qi directly undo the mechanism of the pain. Irregular eating and hard-to-digest foods further burden the Stomach and worsen the stagnation, while warm, cooked, regular meals support its descending function. Gentle movement and deep breathing help disperse stuck Liver Qi, relieving the pressure that drives the distension and pain.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat small, warm, cooked meals at regular times, and avoid eating when you are angry or rushed. Steer clear of greasy, spicy, and cold foods, as well as alcohol. Gentle daily movement like walking, tai chi, or yoga helps keep Liver Qi flowing smoothly. Make stress management a priority - deep breathing, meditation, or simply taking time to unwind can be as important as diet.

### Stomach Yang Deficient and Cold
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/stomach-yang-deficient-and-cold)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Dull, aching epigastric pain that improves with warmth and pressure, Worsened by cold weather or cold foods, Vomiting of clear watery fluid, Poor appetite with preference for hot drinks, Loose stools, Cold hands and feet, Fatigue and low energy
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Cold or raw foods and iced drinks, Cold weather, drafts, or air conditioning, Overwork and late nights, Skipping meals or eating irregularly, Emotional stress, anger, or frustration
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warm food and drinks, Warm compress on the abdomen, Gentle abdominal pressure, Resting after meals, Small, frequent meals
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When the Stomach's warming Yang is deficient, the digestive fire becomes too weak to transform food and fluids. Cold accumulates in the middle burner, congealing Qi and blood flow in the stomach lining. This results in a chronic, dull, nagging pain that feels better when heat is applied or the area is pressed, because warmth and pressure temporarily restore movement and soothe the spasm. Over time, the lack of nourishment and persistent cold can damage the mucosa, allowing an ulcer to form.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale, puffy, tender with teeth marks; white slippery coating. Pulse: deep, slow, weak, especially in the right Guan (middle) position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Cold and raw foods further deplete the Stomach's already weak Yang, causing Qi to congeal and pain to intensify. Warmth and gentle pressure invigorate Yang Qi, helping it to move and transform, which eases the dull ache. Rest conserves Qi and allows the Spleen and Stomach to recover. Overwork and stress consume Spleen Qi, while emotional strain can cause Liver Qi to invade the Stomach, worsening the deficiency. Small, warm meals provide gentle fuel without overwhelming the weakened digestive fire.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat warm, cooked, easily digestible foods like soups, stews, and congee. Use warming spices such as ginger, cinnamon, and fennel. Avoid raw, cold, and iced foods and drinks. Eat small, frequent meals to avoid overburdening the Stomach. Keep the abdomen warm with a scarf or hot water bottle. Gentle daily movement like walking supports Qi flow, but avoid overexertion.

### Damp-Heat in Stomach and Spleen
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/damp-heat-in-stomach-and-spleen)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Burning pain in the upper abdomen, Feeling of fullness and bloating after eating, Thick, yellow, greasy coating on the tongue, Sticky or bitter taste in the mouth, Nausea or sour regurgitation
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Greasy, fried, or spicy foods, Alcohol, Overeating or heavy meals, Damp, humid weather, Emotional stress, anger, or frustration
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Light, bland meals, Sipping warm water, Cool, calm environment, Gentle walking or light exercise, Resting in a quiet place
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Damp-Heat builds up in the stomach and spleen when rich, greasy, or spicy foods, alcohol, and irregular eating habits overwhelm digestion. The heavy, sticky Dampness obstructs the middle burner, while Heat scorches the stomach lining, leading to burning epigastric pain, fullness, and nausea. The combination creates a slimy coating on the tongue and a bitter, unpleasant taste in the mouth.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: Red body, possibly swollen with teeth marks, thick yellow greasy coating especially at centre and root. Pulse: Soggy (Ru) and rapid (Shu), often slippery (Hua).
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Greasy, spicy, and sugary foods, along with alcohol, directly add Dampness and Heat to the body, making the stomach lining more irritated and the tongue coating thicker. Light, bland foods and barley tea gently drain Dampness and clear Heat without burdening digestion. Gentle movement helps Qi flow, which is essential to transform the sticky Dampness. A cool, dry environment counteracts the heavy, humid nature of this pattern, while rest allows the digestive system to recover. Stress and emotional upset can cause Liver Qi to attack the Stomach, further agitating the Heat, so keeping calm is important.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat light, easily digestible meals like congee, steamed vegetables, and moderate amounts of bitter greens. Avoid greasy, fried, spicy, or sugary foods, as well as alcohol and cold raw drinks that can trap Dampness. Stay in a cool, dry environment and take gentle walks to help Qi circulate and transform moisture.

### Food Stagnation in the Stomach
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/food-stagnation-in-the-stomach)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Epigastric bloating and distending pain that worsens after eating, Sour, rotten-smelling belching, Pain relieved by vomiting, Aversion to food and its smell, Foul-smelling flatulence like rotten eggs, Thick, greasy, curd-like tongue coating
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Overeating or heavy meals, Greasy, fried, or heavy foods, Eating too quickly, Lying down right after eating
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Vomiting, Skipping a meal or light fasting, Gentle walking or light exercise, Sipping warm water
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When you eat too much, too quickly, or consume rich, greasy foods that are hard to digest, the Stomach becomes overwhelmed. The undigested food sits and stagnates, blocking the normal downward movement of Stomach Qi. This obstruction causes bloating, distending pain, and sour belching - and if the pressure builds enough, vomiting may bring relief by physically clearing the backlog.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: normal body with a thick, greasy, curd-like coating, thickest in the centre. Pulse: slippery (滑 huá) and full (实 shí), especially forceful at the right middle position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Overeating and rich foods directly create the food stagnation that blocks Stomach Qi, so they worsen bloating and pain. Vomiting expels the accumulated food, allowing Qi to descend again, which is why it brings such noticeable relief. Gentle movement after meals helps Qi circulate and break down the stagnation, while fasting or eating lightly gives the Stomach time to clear the backlog.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat smaller, more frequent meals and chew thoroughly. Avoid heavy, greasy, and fried foods, especially late at night. If you feel bloated after a meal, try a short, gentle walk rather than lying down. Consider a light fasting day or replace a meal with a simple congee to give your Stomach a rest.

### Cold invading the Stomach
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/cold-invading-the-stomach)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Sharp, cramping pain that comes on suddenly, Pain eases with warmth and worsens with cold, No thirst; prefers warm drinks, Vomiting clear watery fluid, Cold feeling in the stomach and cold hands/feet
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Cold or raw foods and iced drinks, Cold weather, drafts, or air conditioning
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warm compress on the abdomen, Ginger tea, Rest and stillness, Warm, easily digestible foods like congee
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When external Cold suddenly invades the Stomach - through icy foods, cold weather, or a chilled environment - it congeals the Qi and Blood in the gastric lining. This constriction triggers an acute spasm, producing the sharp, cramping pain typical of a peptic ulcer flare. The Stomach's normal downward movement is blocked, leading to nausea, belching, and a cold sensation that only warmth can ease.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale, moist with a thin white slippery coat. Pulse: tight (Jin) or wiry and slow (Chi).
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Cold constricts and freezes Qi and Blood in the Stomach, causing sudden cramping pain. Warmth and warm foods relax the vessels and promote Qi flow, relieving the spasm. Rest allows the body's Yang to recover and fight the Cold, while avoiding cold prevents further constriction.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Avoid all cold and raw foods and drinks. Eat warm, cooked meals like soups, stews, and congee. Keep your abdomen warm with a hot water bottle or a scarf, especially in cold weather. Sip ginger tea throughout the day to gently warm the Stomach.

### Stomach Yin Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/stomach-yin-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Dull burning pain or gnawing discomfort, Feeling hungry but not wanting to eat, Dry mouth and throat, Thirst with a preference for small sips, Dry stools or constipation, Weight loss or thin body frame
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Spicy, fried, or roasted foods, Alcohol and coffee, Skipping meals or eating irregularly, Overwork and late nights, Emotional stress, anger, or frustration
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Small, frequent meals, Warm, moist foods like congee, Sipping warm water, Rest and adequate sleep, Cool, calm environment
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When the stomach lacks Yin, its lining loses the moisture and nourishment it needs to stay intact. This dryness creates a low-grade "empty heat" that burns the delicate tissues, much like a pot left on a stove with no water. Over time, this can erode the stomach wall and lead to an ulcer, causing a dull, gnawing pain that feels better with small sips of fluid.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red, thin body, dry centre, little or no coating with possible cracks. Pulse: fine (Xi) and rapid (Shu), especially weak at the right middle position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Stomach Yin is like the protective, moist lining of the digestive tract. Drying and heating influences-spicy foods, alcohol, late nights, and stress-deplete this fluid, allowing empty heat to rise and erode the tissue. Gentle, moistening foods and rest help replenish Yin, cool the heat, and allow the ulcer to heal naturally.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Choose moistening, easy-to-digest foods like congee, steamed pears, tofu, and bone broths. Eat small, frequent meals and avoid skipping breakfast. Steer clear of spicy, fried, or roasted foods, as well as alcohol and coffee, which dry out the stomach. Sip warm water or herbal teas like chrysanthemum throughout the day, and protect your sleep-late nights consume Yin.

### Stomach Blood Stagnation

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Sharp, stabbing or cutting pain, Pain fixed in one spot that does not move, Pain worse at night, Black tarry stools or vomit that looks like coffee grounds, Dark or purplish lips and complexion
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Heavy, greasy meals, Cold or raw foods and iced drinks, Emotional stress, anger, or frustration, Lying still for long periods, Night time

- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When Qi stagnation or Cold lingers in the Stomach for a long time, it can slow the flow of Blood until it congeals and forms stasis in the Stomach’s tiny vessels. This creates a fixed, knife‑like pain that is worse with even light pressure because the stuck Blood cannot move. Over time, the stasis can damage the vessel walls, leading to dark, tarry stools or coffee‑ground vomit.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: dark, dusky or purple body, often with purple stasis spots and swollen, twisted veins underneath; thin white coat. Pulse: choppy (Se) like a knife scraping bamboo, or wiry (Xian) and tight.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Blood stasis in the Stomach means the blood is stuck and cannot flow freely, causing a fixed, stabbing pain. Gentle movement and warmth encourage circulation, which helps break up the stasis and eases the pain. Heavy or cold meals congeal Qi and Blood, making the blockage worse. Stress tightens the Liver Qi, which originally drove the stasis, so calming the mind helps. At night, the body’s Yin nature slows Blood flow, so the pain intensifies.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat small, warm, soft meals and avoid raw, cold, or greasy foods that congeal Blood. Gentle daily walking or tai chi helps keep Qi and Blood flowing. Steer clear of alcohol and smoking, which worsen stasis and irritate the ulcer. Manage stress with deep breathing or quiet time, since emotional tension tightens the Qi and makes Blood stasis worse.

## คำถามที่พบบ่อย (Frequently Asked Questions)
### Can TCM heal a peptic ulcer without antibiotics?
If your ulcer is caused by H. pylori, antibiotics are the standard of care to eradicate the infection. TCM can support this process by reducing inflammation, protecting the stomach lining, and alleviating side effects like nausea and diarrhea. In cases where ulcers are primarily due to NSAID use or stress, TCM alone can often heal the ulcer by addressing the root imbalance. Always complete any prescribed antibiotic course, and discuss integrating herbs with your doctor.

### How does acupuncture help stomach ulcers?
Acupuncture works by regulating the nervous system and improving blood flow to the stomach lining, which promotes healing and reduces pain. Specific points on the abdomen, legs, and arms are used to calm the Stomach, move stagnant Qi, and harmonize the Liver. Many patients feel immediate relief from bloating and cramping during a session. Regular treatments also help manage stress, a key trigger for ulcer flares.

### Will I need to change my diet?
Yes, dietary adjustments are a cornerstone of TCM ulcer treatment. The general rule is to eat warm, cooked, easy-to-digest foods and avoid raw, cold, greasy, and spicy items that burden the Stomach. Specific recommendations depend on your pattern - for example, ginger tea may help Cold patterns but aggravate Heat patterns. Your practitioner will give you tailored guidance, but even basic changes often bring rapid relief.

### Is it safe to combine TCM herbs with PPIs?
Generally, yes. Many patients use herbs alongside acid-suppressing medications without issue. However, some herbs like licorice root (Gan Cao) can affect blood pressure and potassium levels with prolonged use, so monitoring is important. Always tell both your TCM practitioner and your doctor about all medications and supplements you're taking. Never stop PPIs abruptly - work with your doctor to taper if you wish to reduce dependency.

### How long until I feel better?
Most people notice a reduction in pain and bloating within the first 1-2 weeks of herbs and acupuncture. Acute patterns like Food Stagnation or Cold Invasion may resolve in days. Chronic patterns, especially those involving deficiency or Blood stasis, take longer - often 2-3 months for lasting change. The ulcer itself can heal in 4-8 weeks, but continued treatment strengthens the stomach to prevent recurrence.

### Can stress really cause an ulcer?
From a TCM perspective, absolutely. Stress and unexpressed emotions cause the Liver Qi to stagnate, which then disrupts the Stomach's function - a classic 'Liver invading Stomach' pattern. This leads to the distending pain, acid reflux, and bloating that many ulcer patients know well. Managing stress through acupuncture, herbs, and mind-body practices is often just as important as dietary changes.
