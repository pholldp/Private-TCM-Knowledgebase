## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนปัจจุบัน (Conventional Medicine View)
### คำอธิบาย (Description)
A post-infectious cough is a cough that persists for more than three weeks after a respiratory infection, even after other symptoms like fever and congestion have resolved. It's often caused by lingering inflammation in the airways, post-nasal drip, or temporary airway hypersensitivity. The cough is usually dry or may produce a small amount of phlegm, and it can be triggered by talking, cold air, or deep breaths.
Doctors typically diagnose it based on your history and a physical exam, ruling out other causes like asthma, reflux, or lingering bacterial infection. For most people, the cough gradually fades on its own within eight weeks, but the persistent hacking can be exhausting and disruptive.

### การรักษาหลัก (Treatments)
Conventional treatment focuses on symptom relief while the body heals. Over-the-counter cough suppressants (dextromethorphan) or expectorants (guaifenesin) may be suggested, along with throat lozenges and humidifiers. If airway hyperreactivity is suspected, a doctor might prescribe an inhaled bronchodilator or a short course of inhaled corticosteroids. Antibiotics are used only if a bacterial infection is confirmed. Often, the main recommendation is watchful waiting and reassurance that the cough will eventually stop.

## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนจีน (Traditional Chinese Medicine View)
### ความเข้าใจเกี่ยวกับการแพทย์แผนจีน (TCM Understanding)
In TCM, the Lungs are the most delicate organ, directly open to the outside world through the nose and throat. When a cold or flu invades, the pathogen - usually Wind, carrying Cold, Heat, or Dryness - enters the Lungs and disrupts their natural rhythm of descending and diffusing Qi.
A cough is the body’s attempt to force the intruder out. If the pathogen isn’t fully cleared, it lingers like an unwelcome guest, keeping the Lung Qi in a state of rebellion and the cough alive.
Which pathogen stays behind shapes everything about the cough. Residual Wind-Cold produces a tight cough with thin white phlegm and chills. Lingering Wind-Heat brings a frequent, urgent cough with sticky yellow phlegm and a sore throat. Dryness leaves the airways parched, causing a dry, scratchy cough with little to no phlegm.
In other cases, the infection may have weakened the Spleen, allowing Dampness and Phlegm to accumulate - leading to a heavy, rattling cough with lots of phlegm. TCM reads the phlegm’s color, thickness, and the sensations of hot or cold as a map to the exact imbalance.
Sometimes the cough isn’t just about the Lungs. The Liver can rebel and send Fire upward to scorch the Lungs, triggering explosive coughing fits that come with anger or stress. Or a prolonged cough can drain the Lung’s Yin, the moistening and cooling aspect, resulting in a dry, tickling cough that worsens at night. That’s why one Western diagnosis of "post-infectious cough" can have so many different TCM faces - and why pattern-based treatment is essential.

### การวินิจฉัยโดยแพทย์แผนจีน (Practitioner Diagnosis)
A TCM practitioner begins by asking what the sputum looks like and how the cough feels. The color, thickness, and amount of phlegm - together with the sensations of hot or cold - are the first big clues that point toward one pattern rather than another. Even a mild, lingering cough after an infection tells a clear story when these details are gathered.
If the cough brings up thin, white, watery sputum and you feel chilled, achy, and averse to wind, the picture is Wind‑Cold invading the Lungs. The tongue coating will be thin and white, and the pulse feels floating and tight, like a taut string bobbing on water. This pattern often appears when the initial cold symptoms never fully cleared.
When the cough is frequent and produces sticky yellow sputum, accompanied by a sore throat, thirst, and a sensation of heat, Wind‑Heat is still lodged in the Lungs. The tongue tip is redder with a thin yellow coating, and the pulse is floating and rapid. This pattern is more common when the original infection involved a high fever or a very sore throat.
A dry, hacking cough with little or no sputum, a scratchy throat, and a dry nose points to Exterior Dry Cold invading the Lungs. The tongue may look dry with a thin coating, and the pulse often feels floating. This pattern is especially likely in autumn or in heated indoor air, where dryness damages the lung fluids and leaves the airways irritated.
If the cough is wet and productive but the sputum is copious, white, and sticky - and you feel a heavy sensation in the chest - Damp‑Phlegm has accumulated in the Lungs. The tongue coating is thick, white, and greasy, and the pulse feels slippery, like pearls rolling on a plate. This pattern arises when the body’s fluid metabolism has been weakened after the illness.
A lingering dry cough that is worse in the evening or at night, with very little sputum, night sweats, and a dry mouth, suggests Lung Yin Deficiency. The tongue is red with a scanty or absent coating, and the pulse is thin and rapid. This pattern typically appears later in recovery, after prolonged coughing has consumed the lung’s nourishing fluids.
When the cough comes in violent, barking bursts triggered by stress or anger, and the sputum is yellow and difficult to expel, Liver Fire is insulting the Lungs. The tongue is red with a thin yellow coating, and the pulse is wiry and rapid. Flank pain or a bitter taste in the mouth often accompanies this pattern, linking emotional strain to the respiratory symptom.

### Wind-Cold invading the Lungs
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/wind-cold-invading-the-lungs)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Cough with thin, white, watery phlegm, Chills more prominent than fever, Itchy throat, Clear watery nasal discharge, Absence of sweating, Worse on exposure to cold or wind
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Cold air or wind exposure, Iced drinks and raw foods, Dairy and cold-natured foods, Overexertion in cold weather
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warm drinks (ginger tea), Steam inhalation, Rest and staying warm, Warm compresses on the chest
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  After a respiratory infection, traces of Wind and Cold can linger in the body when the pathogen isn't fully expelled. Cold tightens and contracts, so it traps Lung Qi, preventing it from descending and diffusing normally. The result is a persistent cough with thin, white phlegm, often accompanied by chills and a sensation of tightness in the chest.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: normal or slightly pale body with a thin, white, moist coating. Pulse: floating (fu) and tight (jin).
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Wind-Cold tightens and constricts the Lung Qi, trapping it and causing cough. Warmth, whether from drinks, steam, or compresses, relaxes this constriction, helps the exterior open, and encourages the Lung Qi to descend again. Cold and raw substances do the opposite, aggravating the tightening and making the cough worse.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Keep the chest, neck, and back warm, especially in windy or cold weather. Drink plenty of warm fluids like ginger tea with a little honey; avoid iced drinks, raw salads, and cold dairy products. Gentle steam inhalation (plain water or with a drop of eucalyptus oil) can help soothe the throat and loosen phlegm.

### Wind-Heat invading the Lungs
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/wind-heat-invading-the-lungs)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Cough with thick yellow or sticky phlegm, Sore or painful throat, thirst, Fever stronger than chills, mild sweating, Nasal congestion with yellow or turbid mucus, Red tip of the tongue with thin yellow coating
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Spicy, fried, or greasy foods, Hot, stuffy rooms, Wind exposure, Overexertion, Smoking or pollution
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Cool, fresh air, Drinking cooling fluids (pear juice, chrysanthemum tea), Rest and quiet, Light, bland foods, Menthol or peppermint lozenges
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  After an infection, Wind and Heat can linger in the lungs, disrupting the normal downward movement of Lung Qi. The trapped Heat dries out the body’s fluids, turning phlegm thick and yellow, while the rising Qi triggers a frequent, urgent cough. The Heat also irritates the throat, causing soreness and thirst.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red tip, thin yellow coating, body slightly red. Pulse: floating (Fu) and rapid (Shu).
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Wind-Heat is an exterior hot pathogen. Spicy and fried foods add internal Heat, making the cough and sore throat worse. Hot, stuffy environments trap the pathogen inside, while cool, fresh air helps the body release Heat outward. Rest allows the defensive Qi to concentrate on expelling the invader, and cooling fluids replenish the fluids that Heat has dried up, soothing the throat and thinning phlegm.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Sip cooling fluids like chrysanthemum tea, pear juice, or loquat syrup to soothe the throat and replenish fluids. Avoid spicy, fried, and greasy foods that generate internal Heat. Keep the neck and chest lightly covered to shield from wind, and rest as much as possible so the body’s defenses can clear the pathogen.

### Exterior Dry Cold invading the Lungs
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/exterior-dry-cold-invading-the-lungs)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Dry, hacking cough with scant or no phlegm, Dry nose and scratchy throat, Chills without sweating, Thin white dry tongue coating, Mild headache
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Cold, dry air or wind, Cold or raw foods, Spicy or fried foods, Overexertion, Dry indoor heating
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warm, moist air, Warm fluids like ginger tea or broth, Rest and staying warm, Covering the chest and neck, Light, easy-to-digest soups
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  After an infection, the body's defenses are weakened, and Wind-Dryness can easily invade the Lungs. This external pathogen combines cold and dryness, which constricts Lung Qi and dries up the fluids that normally keep the airways moist. The result is a persistent, dry, hacking cough with very little phlegm, a scratchy throat, and a dry nose-like a cold, crisp wind chapping the lungs from the inside.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: normal body colour with a thin white dry coating. Pulse: floating and wiry (浮弦 fú xián), especially taut at the right cun (Lung) position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: External Dry Cold directly damages Lung fluids and tightens the Lung Qi, leading to a dry cough. Warmth and moisture counteract both the cold constriction and the dryness, helping Lung Qi descend smoothly. Cold, dry environments and foods perpetuate the pathogen, while rest conserves the body's defensive energy to expel it.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Keep the air moist with a humidifier and avoid dry, windy environments. Drink warm fluids like ginger tea with honey or pear juice to soothe the throat. Eat moistening foods such as pears, white fungus, and almonds. Avoid cold, raw, or spicy foods that can further dry out the lungs.

### Lung Yin Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/lung-yin-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Dry, hacking cough with scanty or sticky phlegm, Night sweats, Dry throat and mouth, Afternoon tidal heat or low-grade fever
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Dry, heated indoor air, Spicy or fried foods, Overwork and late nights, Emotional stress, Smoking or alcohol
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Cool, humidified air, Sipping warm water or pear juice, Rest and lying down, Avoiding talking
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  After a respiratory infection, lingering heat or a prolonged cough can gradually consume the Lung's Yin and fluids. Without enough moisture to keep the airways supple, the Lungs become dry and irritable, triggering a persistent dry cough that produces little to no phlegm. This is a pattern of deficiency, where the body lacks the cooling, nourishing resources to fully calm the cough reflex.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red, thin body with cracks, little or no coating, dry surface. Pulse: fine (thin) and rapid, especially weak at the right cun (inch) position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Lung Yin is easily consumed by dryness, heat, and excessive activity. Rest and moistening foods replenish Yin and fluids, soothing the irritable airway. Dry air, spicy foods, and overwork further deplete fluids and stir up empty heat, triggering the dry cough. Avoiding these preserves the little Yin that remains, allowing the Lungs to recover.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat moistening foods like pears, white fungus, lily bulb, and honey. Avoid spicy, fried, and drying foods, as well as alcohol and smoking. Rest well, especially in the afternoon, and keep indoor air humidified.

### Liver Fire insulting the Lungs
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/liver-fire-insulting-the-lungs)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Sudden, forceful coughing fits, Pain or distension along the ribs, worse with coughing, Irritability and quick temper, Bitter taste in the mouth, Face turning red during coughing, Sticky phlegm that is hard to expectorate
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Anger and frustration, Spicy, fried, or greasy foods, Alcohol and coffee, Stressful situations, Hot, stuffy environments
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Calm, stress-free environment, Cooling foods and drinks, Bitter greens (dandelion, chicory), Gentle breathing exercises
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When stress, frustration, or lingering heat after an infection stagnates Liver Qi, it can transform into Fire. This Fire travels upward along the Liver channel, invading the Lungs and disrupting their natural downward movement of Qi. The result is sudden, explosive coughing fits, often triggered by emotional upset, with a sensation of Qi rushing up and sticky phlegm that is hard to expel.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: Red body with distinctly redder sides, thin dry yellow coating. Pulse: Wiry and rapid, with the wiry quality strongest at the left middle position (Liver).
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Emotional stress and anger cause Liver Qi to stagnate and turn into Fire, which then flares upward to attack the Lungs, directly triggering coughing fits. Spicy, greasy, and heating foods add fuel to the Fire, while alcohol and coffee stir up internal Heat. Cooling foods, bitter greens, and a calm environment help clear Liver Fire and soothe the upward rush of Qi, allowing the Lungs to resume their natural downward movement.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Avoid spicy, greasy, and fried foods, as well as alcohol, which can fuel Liver Fire. Sip chrysanthemum tea or eat steamed pears to cool and moisten the Lungs. Gentle stress-management practices like walking or meditation are essential, as emotional upset directly triggers this type of cough.

## คำถามที่พบบ่อย (Frequently Asked Questions)
### How long does a post-infectious cough usually last with TCM treatment?
Many people see a noticeable improvement within the first week, especially if the cough is from a lingering Wind-Cold or Wind-Heat pattern. A full resolution typically takes 2-4 weeks, though a dry, deficient cough may need longer to rebuild lung moisture. Your practitioner will adjust your herbal formula as your cough changes, which helps speed recovery.

### Can I use TCM alongside my regular cough medicine?
Yes, TCM can safely complement over-the-counter cough syrups or lozenges. Tell your practitioner about any medications you’re taking, including prescription inhalers or antibiotics. Herbal formulas often reduce the need for suppressants over time. Never stop prescribed medications abruptly - work with your doctor to adjust dosages as your cough improves.

### What foods should I avoid if I have a lingering cough?
In general, steer clear of cold, raw, and greasy foods, which can hamper digestion and create phlegm. Dairy products tend to increase phlegm production. Spicy and fried foods can irritate the throat and worsen heat-type coughs. Focus on warm, easy-to-digest meals like congee, soups, and steamed vegetables. Pears, especially cooked, are excellent for soothing a dry cough.

### Why does my cough get worse at night?
In TCM, a cough that worsens at night often points to Yin deficiency or dryness. Yin, the body's cooling and moistening aspect, naturally becomes quieter at night, so a lung already low on Yin fluids can feel even drier and more irritable, triggering the cough. Other patterns, like Damp-Phlegm, may also worsen when lying down because phlegm pools. Your practitioner can identify the specific reason and treat accordingly.

### Does TCM work for a dry cough with no phlegm?
Absolutely. A dry, hacking cough is a classic sign of either Wind-Dryness invading the Lungs or Lung Yin Deficiency. TCM uses moistening, Yin-nourishing herbs like Mai Dong and Bai He, along with acupuncture points that help the lungs retain moisture. Many patients with stubborn dry coughs find TCM particularly effective because it addresses the root dryness rather than just suppressing the urge to cough.

### Is acupuncture safe for a cough after a cold?
Yes, acupuncture is very safe for post-infectious cough when performed by a licensed practitioner. Points on the arms, back, and legs are used to regulate Lung Qi and expel lingering pathogens. The needles are hair-thin and cause minimal discomfort. If you have a bleeding disorder or are on blood thinners, inform your practitioner beforehand.

### Can TCM help if I’ve had the cough for months?
Yes, a chronic cough that has lasted for months often involves deeper patterns like Lung Yin Deficiency, Damp-Phlegm, or even Liver Fire. While it may take longer to resolve - usually 4-8 weeks or more - TCM can still be very effective. The key is a precise diagnosis and consistent treatment with herbs and acupuncture to gradually rebalance the body.
