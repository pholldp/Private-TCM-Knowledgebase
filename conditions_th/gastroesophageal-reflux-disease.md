## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนปัจจุบัน (Conventional Medicine View)
### คำอธิบาย (Description)
Gastroesophageal reflux disease (GERD) is a chronic digestive disorder where stomach acid or bile flows back into the esophagus, irritating its lining. Common symptoms include heartburn, regurgitation, chest pain, difficulty swallowing, and a sensation of a lump in the throat. Diagnosis is typically based on symptom patterns and may be confirmed with endoscopy, pH monitoring, or a trial of acid-suppressing medication.

### การรักษาหลัก (Treatments)
Standard treatments include lifestyle modifications (weight loss, elevating the head of the bed, avoiding trigger foods), antacids, H2 receptor blockers, proton pump inhibitors (PPIs), and in severe cases, surgical procedures like fundoplication. PPIs are the mainstay for reducing acid production and allowing esophageal healing.

## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนจีน (Traditional Chinese Medicine View)
### ความเข้าใจเกี่ยวกับการแพทย์แผนจีน (TCM Understanding)
In TCM, GERD is understood not as a simple excess of stomach acid, but as a problem of direction. The Stomach is designed to send its contents downward - when this downward movement fails and Qi rebels upward, acid, food, or bland fluids rise into the esophagus and throat. This rebellious Stomach Qi (胃气上逆, wèi qì shàng nì) is the final common pathway for all reflux, but the reason it rebels varies from person to person.
The Liver is often the hidden driver. This organ is responsible for the smooth flow of Qi throughout the body, including the Stomach's downward motion. Emotional stress, frustration, or suppressed anger can cause Liver Qi to stagnate. Over time, this stagnation can generate Heat or directly invade the Stomach, pushing its contents upward and creating the classic burning reflux with a bitter taste. That's why many people notice their symptoms flare during tense periods.
The Spleen plays a different but equally important role. When the digestive system is weakened - by poor diet, overwork, or constitutional tendency - it fails to transform and transport fluids properly. These stagnant fluids thicken into Phlegm, which then hitches a ride on rebellious Stomach Qi. This produces the sensation of a lump in the throat, chest stuffiness, and bland regurgitation without the intense burning. In some cases, the digestive fire itself is simply too weak, leading to Cold patterns where reflux is watery and the stomach craves warmth.
This is why one Western diagnosis - GERD - can manifest as five distinct TCM patterns, each with its own characteristic sensations, tongue appearance, and pulse quality. The stress-triggered burning, the lump-in-the-throat that comes and goes with mood, and the chronic bland reflux with fatigue are not variations of one disease; they are different diseases in TCM terms, and they require different treatments.

### การวินิจฉัยโดยแพทย์แผนจีน (Practitioner Diagnosis)
A TCM practitioner starts by asking what the reflux actually feels like - burning or bland, constant or intermittent - and what makes it better or worse. The tongue body and coating, along with the pulse quality, provide the critical clues that point toward one pattern rather than another.
If the reflux burns intensely, leaves a bitter taste, and flares with emotional stress, the practitioner suspects Liver Qi Stagnation that transforms into Heat. The tongue is often red with a yellow coating, and the pulse feels wiry and rapid. Chest tightness and irritability are common, and the whole picture is one of heat and tension rising upward.
When stress triggers acid regurgitation and a bloated, full sensation in the upper abdomen but the burning is less pronounced, Liver Qi Stagnation invading the Stomach is more likely. The tongue may look normal or only slightly red with a thin white coat, and the pulse is wiry. The key difference is the absence of strong heat signs - belching and epigastric distension dominate.
A sensation of a lump in the throat that cannot be swallowed, together with frequent belching and chest tightness, points to Qi-Phlegm. The tongue coating is thick and greasy, and the pulse is slippery. Phlegm obstructs the descent of Stomach Qi, so the reflux often feels more like a blockage than pure burning, and the throat feels constantly uncomfortable.
When the reflux is bland rather than burning, and the person feels cold in the stomach with a craving for warm drinks and external warmth, the pattern is Stomach Yang Deficient and Cold. The tongue is pale with a white coating, and the pulse is weak and slow. Symptoms improve with warmth and worsen with cold foods or chilly weather, reflecting an internal lack of warming Yang.
Chronic mild reflux that occurs alongside fatigue, poor appetite, and loose stools suggests Spleen and Stomach Qi Deficiency. The tongue is pale and possibly swollen with a thin white coat, and the pulse is weak. The digestive system lacks the strength to hold Stomach Qi down, so reflux is persistent but not severe, and the whole body feels depleted.

  When stress and frustration build up, Liver Qi gets stuck and eventually turns into Heat. This trapped Heat combines with rebellious Stomach Qi, pushing acid and bitter fluids upward into the chest and throat. The result is the classic burning reflux, sour belching, and a bitter taste in the mouth that defines this pattern.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red body, especially redder on the sides, with a yellow coating. Pulse: wiry and rapid (xian shu).

Spicy, greasy foods and alcohol add Heat to the body and burden the Stomach, making it more likely to rebel upward. Cooling foods and relaxation help clear Heat and smooth the flow of Liver Qi, while small meals and upright posture support the Stomach's natural downward movement.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Avoid spicy, greasy, and fried foods, alcohol, and caffeine. Eat smaller, more frequent meals and avoid lying down for at least two hours after eating. Manage stress with gentle exercise like walking or tai chi, and practice deep breathing or meditation to calm the mind.

### Qi-Phlegm
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/qi-phlegm)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Sensation of a lump in the throat that cannot be swallowed or coughed up, Chest and upper abdominal oppression or stuffiness, Symptoms worsen with emotional stress, Frequent sighing or need to clear the throat, Nausea or queasiness without strong burning
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Emotional stress, Greasy or dairy-heavy food, Cold or raw foods and drinks, Overeating or large meals
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Gentle exercise, Warm, cooked meals, Stress reduction, Deep breathing and relaxation
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When stress or emotional upset knots the Liver Qi, it fails to keep fluids moving smoothly. These stagnant fluids thicken into Phlegm, which then hitches a ride on the rebellious Stomach Qi and rises into the throat and chest. The result is the classic sensation of a lump that cannot be swallowed, along with acid reflux and a tight, stuffy feeling behind the breastbone.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: normal-coloured body, possibly slightly swollen, with a white greasy or slippery coating. Pulse: wiry (Xian) and slippery (Hua).
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Emotional stress knots the Liver Qi, which is the first step in creating the stagnant fluids that turn into phlegm. Greasy and cold foods generate dampness and phlegm directly, adding fuel to the fire. Gentle movement and deep breathing unblock Qi, while warm cooked foods support the digestive system's ability to process fluids, preventing phlegm from forming and lodging in the throat.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Avoid greasy, dairy-heavy, and cold raw foods that promote dampness and phlegm. Gentle daily movement like walking or yoga helps keep Qi flowing. Practice slow, deep breathing to release chest tension, and address the emotional stressors that tend to tighten the throat.

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Sour regurgitation without strong burning, Epigastric distension that radiates to the ribs, Worse with emotional stress and frustration, Frequent belching and sighing, Feeling of a lump in the throat, Irritability and moodiness
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Emotional stress, Skipping meals or irregular eating, Cold or raw foods and drinks, Constrained, rushed eating

  The Liver is responsible for the smooth flow of Qi throughout the body, including the downward direction of Stomach Qi. When emotional stress, frustration, or suppressed anger cause Liver Qi to stagnate, it can attack the Stomach horizontally. This invasion disrupts the Stomach's normal descending movement, forcing Qi and acid to rebel upward into the esophagus, producing acid reflux, belching, and a tight, distending feeling in the upper abdomen.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: normal or slightly red body, may have redder sides, thin white coat. Pulse: wiry (弦 xián), especially at the left middle position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Stress and anger cause Liver Qi to stagnate and invade the Stomach, directly triggering reflux and distension. Relaxation and smooth movement help unblock Qi, while regular, warm meals support the Stomach's downward push.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Avoid eating when upset; take a few slow breaths before meals. Eat smaller, regular meals and avoid raw, cold, or greasy foods that burden the Stomach. Gentle daily movement like walking or tai chi helps keep Liver Qi flowing smoothly.

### Stomach Yang Deficient and Cold
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/stomach-yang-deficient-and-cold)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Dull, cold pain in the upper belly that feels better with warmth and gentle pressure, Regurgitation of clear, bland watery fluid (not sour or burning), Poor appetite with a strong preference for hot food and drinks, Cold hands and feet, and sensitivity to cold environments, Fatigue, lack of energy, and loose stools
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Cold or raw foods and drinks, Exposure to cold weather, Overwork and fatigue, Emotional stress, Skipping meals or irregular eating
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warmth on the stomach, Hot drinks like ginger tea, Warm, small meals, Rest and lying down, Gentle pressure or massage
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Stomach Yang is the digestive fire that drives the downward movement of food and Qi. When this fire is weak, internal Cold develops and the Stomach loses its ability to descend. Instead, Qi rises upward, carrying bland watery fluid into the throat and causing a dull, cold ache in the upper belly that craves warmth.
This is a slow-burning pattern, often seen after years of eating cold or raw foods, or when constitutional weakness is present. The reflux here is not the sharp, burning acid of Heat patterns, but a bland, watery regurgitation that reflects the lack of digestive warmth.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale, puffy and tender body with teeth marks and a white, slippery coating. Pulse: deep, slow and weak, especially at the right Guan position (Stomach/Spleen).

### Spleen and Stomach Qi Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/spleen-and-stomach-qi-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Bland or watery acid reflux, not burning, Poor appetite with early satiety, Bloating and abdominal distension after eating, Chronic fatigue and weak, heavy limbs, Loose or unformed stools
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Overeating or large meals, Cold or raw foods and drinks, Overwork and fatigue, Emotional stress
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warm, small meals, Rest after eating, Gentle exercise, Warm compresses on the abdomen
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  The Spleen and Stomach work as a team: the Spleen sends clear Qi upward while the Stomach sends food downward. When Spleen Qi is too weak to lift, the Stomach loses its downward direction and Qi rebels upward, carrying acid and stomach contents into the throat. Because there is no excess heat, the reflux is usually bland or mildly sour, not intensely burning, and it comes with fatigue, poor appetite, and bloating after eating.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: Pale, puffy, tooth-marked body with thin white coating. Pulse: Weak, soft, right Guan position empty or deficient.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Spleen Qi is weak and easily overwhelmed. Overeating, cold foods, and fatigue further deplete it, causing Qi to stagnate and rebel upward as reflux. Warm, small meals and rest conserve Qi, allowing the Spleen to transform food properly and keep Stomach Qi moving downward. Gentle movement helps Qi flow, while worry knots the Qi and worsens stagnation.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat small, frequent meals of warm, cooked, easily digestible foods like congee, soups, and steamed vegetables. Avoid raw, cold, and greasy foods that tax the Spleen. Chew thoroughly and rest briefly after eating to conserve Qi. Gentle daily movement such as walking supports Qi circulation without draining energy.

## คำถามที่พบบ่อย (Frequently Asked Questions)
### Can TCM really stop acid reflux without PPIs?
Yes, many patients are able to reduce or eliminate their reliance on PPIs after a course of TCM treatment. Instead of simply suppressing acid production, TCM works to correct the underlying directional problem - the rebellious Stomach Qi - by soothing the Liver, strengthening the Spleen, or warming the digestive fire as needed. As the pattern resolves, the reflux naturally subsides. However, PPIs should never be stopped abruptly; always work with your doctor to taper under supervision once your symptoms have stabilized.

### How long does it take for acupuncture to work for GERD?
Many patients feel a noticeable reduction in heartburn and regurgitation within the first 2-3 weekly sessions. Acupuncture works quickly to calm the nervous system and redirect Stomach Qi downward. For lasting change, a full course of 8-12 sessions is typical, combined with daily herbal medicine. Excess patterns (like Liver Heat) often respond faster than deficiency patterns (like Spleen Qi Deficiency), which may require a few months of consistent treatment to rebuild digestive strength.

### What does the TCM diet for reflux look like?
The universal TCM dietary principle for GERD is to support the Stomach's downward function. This means eating warm, cooked, easily digestible foods - think congee, soups, steamed vegetables, and well-cooked grains. Cold, raw foods and iced drinks are avoided because they shock the digestive fire. Greasy, fried, and heavily spiced foods are also limited, as they generate Dampness and Heat. Eating smaller, regular meals and staying upright for at least two hours after eating are essential habits. Beyond these basics, your practitioner will tailor dietary advice to your specific pattern - for example, adding warming spices for a Cold pattern or cooling foods for a Heat pattern.

### Can I take Chinese herbs alongside my PPI medication?
In most cases, yes. Many patients begin TCM while still taking their prescribed acid-suppressing medication. Chinese herbal formulas are generally safe to combine with PPIs, but timing is important - herbs are usually taken 1-2 hours apart from other medications to ensure proper absorption. Always bring a complete list of your medications to your TCM consultation, and keep both your gastroenterologist and your TCM practitioner informed. Certain herbs, like Huang Lian (Coptis), have a direct acid-reducing effect, and your practitioner will adjust the formula as your need for PPIs decreases.

### Why does my reflux get worse when I'm stressed?
In TCM, stress directly affects the Liver, which governs the smooth flow of Qi. When you are tense or frustrated, Liver Qi stagnates and can attack the Stomach horizontally, disrupting its normal downward movement. This is why your reflux flares during arguments, deadlines, or emotional upset - the Liver is literally pushing Stomach Qi upward. Treatment therefore often focuses on soothing the Liver with herbs and acupuncture points that release tension, which is why many patients notice not only less reflux but also a calmer mood.

### Is that lump-in-the-throat sensation really related to reflux?
Yes, it is a classic symptom of what TCM calls Qi-Phlegm - a pattern where stagnant Liver Qi combines with thickened fluids to create a sensation of obstruction in the throat, often called globus sensation. Unlike the burning of acid reflux, this feels like a plum pit stuck in the throat that cannot be swallowed or coughed up, and it often comes and goes with emotional state. TCM treats it by moving Liver Qi and resolving Phlegm, and many patients find this sensation resolves even when conventional tests show nothing wrong.

### Will my reflux come back after I stop TCM treatment?
The goal of TCM is to correct the underlying pattern, not just suppress symptoms. When treatment is completed properly, the constitutional tendency toward reflux is significantly reduced, and many patients remain symptom-free for years. However, if the original triggers - chronic stress, poor diet, irregular eating - return, the pattern can re-emerge. Your practitioner will provide lifestyle and dietary guidance to help you maintain the results, and occasional follow-up treatments can keep things in balance.
