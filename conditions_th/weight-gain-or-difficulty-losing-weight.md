## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนปัจจุบัน (Conventional Medicine View)
### คำอธิบาย (Description)
From a Western medical perspective, weight gain occurs when caloric intake exceeds energy expenditure over time, leading to fat accumulation. It is commonly assessed using Body Mass Index (BMI) and associated with conditions like metabolic syndrome, insulin resistance, and hormonal imbalances such as hypothyroidism or PCOS. Diagnosis involves blood tests, dietary assessment, and sometimes imaging to rule out underlying pathology.

### การรักษาหลัก (Treatments)
Conventional treatment typically centers on dietary modification, increased physical activity, and behavioral therapy. Medications such as GLP-1 agonists may be prescribed to suppress appetite or reduce fat absorption, and in severe cases bariatric surgery is considered. The focus is on creating a sustained calorie deficit and increasing metabolic rate.

## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนจีน (Traditional Chinese Medicine View)
### ความเข้าใจเกี่ยวกับการแพทย์แผนจีน (TCM Understanding)
TCM understands weight gain primarily through the Spleen and Stomach, the organs responsible for transforming food into Qi and separating clear fluids from waste. When this system is overwhelmed - by rich food, fatigue, or emotional strain - it cannot process fluids properly. Those fluids stagnate and congeal into a heavy, sticky substance called Dampness or Phlegm, which accumulates in the body as excess weight. This is why many people feel heavy and waterlogged rather than simply larger.
The Liver also plays a key role. Its job is to keep Qi flowing smoothly. When stress, frustration, or emotional pressure causes Qi to stagnate, the Liver attacks the Spleen, disrupting digestion and leading to bloating and weight gain that fluctuates with mood. This explains why stress-eaters often gain weight in specific patterns and why the weight feels stubborn and unresponsive to simple calorie cutting.
Finally, the Kidneys provide the body's warming, transformative fire. When Kidney Yang is deficient, the body becomes cold and metabolism slows dramatically, like a stove that has gone out. Fluids accumulate, and weight settles low in the belly and hips. This pattern explains the person who is always cold, exhausted, and gains weight even while eating very little - a picture that conventional medicine often labels as a slow metabolism but TCM sees as insufficient Yang.

### การวินิจฉัยโดยแพทย์แผนจีน (Practitioner Diagnosis)
A practitioner begins by asking about appetite, digestion, and energy. Someone with a ravenous appetite, constant thirst, and a hot, bloated sensation likely has Damp-Heat in the Stomach and Spleen. In contrast, a person who gains weight despite eating little and feels chronically tired and heavy is more likely to have Spleen Deficiency with Dampness. These clues about eating and energy point the diagnosis in very different directions.
Next they explore the sensation of heaviness and where fat accumulates. A body that feels waterlogged, with puffy limbs and a swollen tongue with teeth marks, points to Phlegm-Dampness in the Middle-Burner. If the person also feels cold, has a slow pulse, and carries weight low in the belly and hips, the picture shifts toward Kidney and Spleen Yang Deficiency, where the body’s metabolic fire is too weak to transform fluids.
Emotional patterns are a key differentiator. If weight fluctuates with stress, and bloating and irritability flare during tense periods, Liver Qi Stagnation is a strong suspect. The tongue may be dusky and the pulse wiry. This pattern often overlaps with digestive sluggishness because the stuck Liver Qi attacks the Spleen, creating a cycle of emotional eating and slowed metabolism.
Finally, tongue and pulse examination brings clarity. A pale, swollen, wet tongue with a thin white coat and a slow, weak pulse suggests Yang deficiency or Spleen Qi weakness. A red tongue with a thick yellow, greasy coat and a rapid, slippery pulse confirms Damp-Heat. A thick white greasy coat and a slippery pulse point to Phlegm-Dampness. These signs allow the practitioner to distinguish between patterns that can feel similar.

## กลุ่มอาการการแพทย์แผนจีนและการรักษา (TCM Patterns and Treatment)

### Phlegm-Dampness in the Middle-Burner
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/phlegm-dampness-in-the-middle-burner)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Heavy, sluggish feeling in the body and limbs, Bloating and distension in the abdomen after eating, Greasy or sticky sensation in the mouth, Drowsiness and excessive sleepiness, Thick, greasy, white coating on the tongue
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Fatty, greasy, or fried foods, Dairy and sugar, Damp, humid environments, Cold drinks and iced beverages, Overeating or late-night snacking
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Eating warm, cooked meals, Gentle, sweating exercise, Ginger or cardamom tea, Dry, warm weather
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Your body's digestive core - the Spleen and Stomach - normally transforms food into Qi and separates clear fluids from waste. When this system is overwhelmed by rich food or weakened by fatigue, it cannot process fluids properly. Those fluids congeal into a sticky, heavy substance called Phlegm-Dampness, which saturates the body's tissues and directly accumulates as excess fat. This is the most direct TCM mechanism for weight gain: Phlegm-Dampness physically builds up in the middle burner, creating the sensation of a permanently full, bloated belly and a heavy, sluggish body.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale, puffy body with scalloped edges and a thick, white, greasy coating. Pulse: slippery (滑 huá), and possibly slow or soggy, especially at the right middle position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Phlegm-Dampness is fundamentally a problem of too much cold, sticky fluid that the digestive system cannot process. Greasy, cold, and sugary foods are the raw material for this dampness, so they directly worsen the condition, making the body feel heavier and the tongue coating thicker. Warm, cooked foods and spices like ginger and cardamom have an opposite, drying, and warming effect, helping the Spleen to 'burn off' the excess dampness. Gentle exercise and sweating are like opening a window in a damp house - they provide an exit path for the accumulated moisture, which is why it brings immediate relief from the heavy, sluggish sensation.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Avoid cold, raw, and greasy foods, which directly create more dampness. Eat warm, cooked meals like soups and congees with ginger and cardamom. Gentle, consistent exercise that produces a light sweat is crucial to help the body discharge dampness through the skin.

### Spleen Deficiency with Dampness
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/spleen-deficiency-with-dampness)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Heavy, sluggish feeling in the body and limbs, Bloating and distension in the abdomen after eating, Loose, unformed stools, Poor appetite with reduced food intake, Foggy head, as if wrapped, Thirst with no desire to drink
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Raw, cold foods and salads, Dairy and sugar, Overeating or late-night snacking, Damp, humid environments, Sitting for long periods
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Eating warm, cooked meals, Ginger or cardamom tea, Gentle, sweating exercise, Rest and adequate sleep, Dry, warm weather
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Your Spleen is in charge of transforming food and fluids into Qi and blood, and of keeping things moving upward. When it becomes weak, it cannot do this job properly. Instead of rising, the refined fluids sink and become dampness. This dampness is heavy, sticky, and hard to shift, which slows your whole metabolism and leads to gradual weight gain and a feeling of heaviness.
This pattern is often called "deficient obesity" because the body is carrying extra weight but feels soft and puffy, not hard and solid. The dampness is a pathological byproduct of the weak Spleen, not simply overeating. Because the Spleen cannot transform fluids, even a moderate diet can cause weight gain, and losing it feels impossible.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale, swollen body with teeth marks on the edges and a white, greasy coating. Pulse: soggy (Ru) and soft, or slowed-down (Huan), especially weak at the right Guan position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: In this pattern, the Spleen's digestive fire is already weak. Cold and raw foods extinguish that fire further, so dampness accumulates and weight sticks. Warm, cooked foods are easier to transform and give the Spleen less work to do. Gentle movement moves Qi without depleting it, which helps the Spleen's natural upward push. Rest and warmth conserve the Yang energy needed to dry out dampness.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat warm, cooked foods like soups, stews, and congees. Avoid raw salads, cold drinks, dairy, and greasy or fried foods, which all damage the Spleen and create more dampness. A little grated ginger in warm water first thing in the morning helps wake up the digestive fire. Gentle, consistent movement like walking after meals is better than exhausting exercise, which can further deplete Qi.

### Damp-Heat in Stomach and Spleen
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/damp-heat-in-stomach-and-spleen)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Strong appetite with cravings for rich or greasy food, Greasy or sticky sensation in the mouth, Bloating and distension in the abdomen after eating, Heavy, sluggish feeling in the body and limbs, Loose, unformed stools
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Fatty, greasy, or fried foods, Dairy and sugar, Damp, humid environments, Overeating or late-night snacking
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Eating warm, cooked meals, Cooling foods like cucumber and mung beans, Gentle, sweating exercise
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When Stomach and Spleen are overwhelmed by Dampness and Heat, the digestive fire becomes overactive yet ineffective. The Heat drives a strong appetite and cravings for rich foods, while the sticky Dampness clogs the Spleen's ability to transform food into usable Qi. Instead of nourishment, the body produces more pathological Dampness and Phlegm, which accumulates as fatty tissue. This creates a frustrating cycle where you feel hungry and eat more, yet the body holds onto everything because the Spleen cannot transport and metabolize properly.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red body, possibly swollen with teeth marks, with a thick yellow greasy coating thickest at the centre and root. Pulse: soggy (Ru) and rapid (Shu), often also slippery (Hua).
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Greasy, fried, and spicy foods introduce more Heat and Dampness into the Stomach, directly fueling the pattern. Alcohol and dairy are especially cloying and Dampness-producing. Hot, humid weather mirrors the internal environment and makes it worse. Conversely, light, cooling, and easily digested foods reduce the burden on the Spleen, allowing it to clear Dampness. Gentle exercise moves Qi, which is crucial for draining Dampness - when Qi moves, Dampness cannot settle and accumulate as easily.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Focus on cooling, Dampness-draining foods like mung beans, celery, cucumber, and barley. Avoid greasy, fried, and spicy foods, as well as dairy and alcohol, which worsen Dampness and Heat. Eat smaller, more frequent meals to avoid overwhelming the Spleen, and stop eating before you feel completely full. Gentle daily movement like brisk walking helps Qi flow and Dampness transformation.

### Liver Qi Stagnation
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/liver-qi-stagnation)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Distension in the abdomen and sides, Weight gain tied to stress, Irritability and frequent sighing, Irregular or painful periods
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Emotional stress and frustration, Fatty, greasy, or fried foods, Sitting for long periods, Premenstrual period
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Rest and adequate sleep, Gentle, sweating exercise, Eating warm, cooked meals
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Your Liver is responsible for keeping the flow of Qi smooth throughout your body. When stress, frustration, or emotional pressure builds up, that flow gets stuck. - **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat smaller, regular meals and avoid rich, greasy food to support your digestion. ### Kidney and Spleen Yang Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/kidney-and-spleen-yang-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Puffy, waterlogged appearance rather than dense fat, Feeling cold, especially in the lower back, knees, and feet, Loose, unformed stools, Swelling of the legs and ankles, Foggy head, as if wrapped, Poor appetite with reduced food intake
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Cold drinks and iced beverages, Exposure to cold environments, Overwork and exhaustion, Excessive worry and mental strain, Emotional stress and frustration
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Eating warm, cooked meals, Dry, warm weather, Gentle, sweating exercise, Rest and adequate sleep
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Your body's warming, transformative fire comes from Kidney and Spleen Yang. When this Yang weakens, the body becomes cold and damp, like a kitchen stove that has gone out. The Spleen fails to transform food and fluids into usable Qi, and the Kidneys cannot warm the body or manage water metabolism. The result is a sluggish, cold metabolism where fluids accumulate as dampness and fat, especially around the lower belly and hips, making weight loss extremely difficult.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale, swollen, and puffy with teeth marks on the edges and a white, slippery coating. Pulse: deep, weak, and slow, especially weak in the rear (Kidney) and middle-right (Spleen) positions.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Kidney and Spleen Yang are like your body's internal heating system. Cold foods and environments extinguish that fire, while overwork and mental strain drain it. Warm foods and gentle heat support the Yang, and light movement helps it circulate without depleting it further. In this pattern, the metabolism is fundamentally cold, so every choice revolves around protecting and gradually rebuilding that inner warmth.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat mostly warm, cooked foods like soups, stews, and congees. Avoid raw, cold, and iced foods completely. Gentle daily movement like walking helps move Qi and warm the body, but avoid intense exercise that drains energy. Keep the lower back and abdomen warm, and go to bed early to preserve Yang.

## คำถามที่พบบ่อย (Frequently Asked Questions)
### How does TCM explain weight gain differently from my doctor?
Western medicine focuses on calories and energy balance. TCM looks deeper at why your body is storing weight - is your digestive fire too weak, is stress tangling your Liver Qi, or is your body's warming Yang too cold to burn off fluids? This means two people with the same BMI might get completely different herbal formulas because their root causes differ.

### Will I need to follow a strict diet?
Yes, but the diet is tailored to your pattern rather than just cutting calories. Someone with Dampness and cold needs warm, cooked foods like soups and stews with ginger. Someone with Damp-Heat benefits from cooling foods like mung beans and cucumber. The goal is to support your digestion, not starve it.

### How quickly will I see results with acupuncture and herbs?
Most people feel less bloated and heavy within the first 2-3 weeks. Actual weight loss is slower and steadier - typically 1-2 pounds per week once the underlying imbalance begins to correct. Excess patterns respond faster than deficiency patterns, which may take months but offer lasting change.

### Can TCM help with weight gain from hormonal issues like PCOS or menopause?
Yes, TCM is particularly effective here because it treats the underlying pattern - often a combination of Kidney deficiency and Phlegm-Dampness - that disrupts hormones and metabolism. Many women find their cycles regulate and weight begins to shift as the deeper imbalance is addressed.

### Is it safe to combine TCM herbs with my prescription weight loss medication?
Generally yes, but you must inform both your prescribing doctor and your TCM practitioner about everything you are taking. Some herbs can amplify or interfere with medications, especially those affecting metabolism or blood sugar. Coordination is essential.

### Does TCM treat the emotional side of weight gain?
Absolutely. When weight gain is linked to stress, frustration, or emotional eating, TCM sees this as Liver Qi Stagnation attacking the Spleen. Formulas and acupuncture points are chosen specifically to smooth the Liver, calm the Shen, and restore digestive harmony - addressing both the emotion and its physical consequences.

## การวิจัยทางวิทยาศาสตร์สมัยใหม่ (Modern Scientific Research)
1. **Effectiveness of acupuncture for weight loss: an overview of systematic reviews**
   *วารสารวิชาการ: Kim SY, Shin IS, Park YJ. Effectiveness of acupuncture for weight loss: an overview of systematic reviews. Acupuncture in Medicine. 2018;36(5):327-333.*
   This overview of 12 systematic reviews concluded that acupuncture is superior to placebo or lifestyle modification alone for reducing body weight, BMI, and waist circumference. Auricular acupuncture showed the most consistent positive effect, though the authors noted that the quality of included primary studies was generally low.

2. **Effect of a Chinese herbal medicine, Bofu-tsusho-san, on obesity in patients with metabolic syndrome: A randomized controlled trial**
   *วารสารวิชาการ: Hioki C, Yoshimoto K, Yoshida T. Effect of a Chinese herbal medicine, Bofu-tsusho-san, on obesity in patients with metabolic syndrome: A randomized controlled trial. Obesity Research & Clinical Practice. 2010;4(3):e183-e190.*
   A Japanese RCT found that the Kampo formula Bofu-tsusho-san (Fang Feng Tong Sheng San) significantly reduced visceral fat area and waist circumference compared to placebo in obese patients. This formula is indicated for a pattern of internal Heat and Dampness, often with constipation.

3. **Beneficial Effect of Linggui Zhugan Tang on Weight Loss in Obese Patients: A Randomized, Double-Blind, Placebo-Controlled Clinical Trial**
   *วารสารวิชาการ: Hsu CH, Hwang KC, Chao CL, Chou P. Beneficial Effect of Linggui Zhugan Tang on Weight Loss in Obese Patients: A Randomized, Double-Blind, Placebo-Controlled Clinical Trial. Evidence-Based Complementary and Alternative Medicine. 2015;2015:421092.*
   This clinical trial demonstrated that Ling Gui Zhu Gan Tang, a formula for Phlegm-Dampness in the Middle-Burner, combined with lifestyle modification, led to a significantly greater reduction in body weight and BMI compared to placebo with lifestyle modification alone. The formula was well-tolerated.
