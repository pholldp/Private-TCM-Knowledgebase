## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนปัจจุบัน (Conventional Medicine View)
### คำอธิบาย (Description)
Western medicine defines post-infectious fatigue as persistent tiredness, brain fog, and reduced physical capacity that lingers for weeks or months after an acute infection - most commonly viral illnesses like influenza, COVID-19, or glandular fever. Diagnosis is based on symptoms and the exclusion of other causes (anemia, thyroid disorders, depression), as routine lab tests often return normal. The condition can significantly impair daily function, yet its exact mechanisms remain unclear, with theories involving lingering inflammation, mitochondrial dysfunction, or autonomic nervous system dysregulation.

### การรักษาหลัก (Treatments)
Standard care focuses on symptom management and gradual return to activity. Doctors typically recommend adequate rest, pacing (balancing activity with rest to avoid crashes), graded exercise therapy, and cognitive behavioral therapy to manage frustration and sleep disruption. If specific deficiencies like iron or vitamin B12 are identified, supplements may be prescribed. There are no approved medications that directly treat post-infectious fatigue, and the main advice is often to 'give it time.'

## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนจีน (Traditional Chinese Medicine View)
### ความเข้าใจเกี่ยวกับการแพทย์แผนจีน (TCM Understanding)
After an infection, your body's defensive Qi (Wei Qi) has been in a fierce battle to expel the pathogen. That battle consumes a huge amount of your core energy - what TCM calls Zheng Qi, or upright Qi. Once the fever breaks and the acute illness passes, you're left with a deficit. If that deficit isn't properly refilled through rest and nourishment, you feel drained, and that's the root of post-infectious fatigue.
The Spleen and Lung are the organs most likely to be depleted. The Spleen transforms food into Qi and Blood - your body's fuel. When it's weak, you lose appetite and your limbs feel heavy. The Lung governs respiration and spreads Qi to every cell; if it's weakened, even gentle movement leaves you breathless. These two systems often falter together after a respiratory illness.
Sometimes the Heart is also involved. The Heart houses the mind (Shen), and it depends on Blood to stay anchored. When the Spleen can't produce enough Blood, the Heart becomes undernourished, leading to palpitations, restless sleep, and mental fog - the classic brain fog of post-viral fatigue. This is why TCM asks not just 'how tired are you?' but 'what else is happening?'
Because the same infection can hit different people differently, post-infectious fatigue splits into several distinct patterns. One person might have pure Qi deficiency with a weak voice and spontaneous sweating; another might have Qi and Blood deficiency with a pale face and dizziness; a third might have Spleen and Lung Qi deficiency with poor appetite and breathlessness.
Each pattern calls for a different combination of herbs and acupuncture points, because you're not just 'tired' - you're tired in a very specific way.

### การวินิจฉัยโดยแพทย์แผนจีน (Practitioner Diagnosis)
A TCM practitioner begins by asking about the quality of the fatigue and the symptoms that come with it. Simple Qi Deficiency after an infection typically shows as a drained feeling with a weak voice and spontaneous sweating, but the complexion stays fairly clear. The tongue is pale and the pulse feels weak, pointing to a straightforward depletion of the body’s vital Qi.
When the fatigue is joined by a noticeably sallow or pale face, dizziness, blurred vision, or scanty menstruation, the picture shifts to Qi and Blood Deficiency. Here a prolonged illness has consumed both the body’s functional force and its nourishing Blood. The tongue is pale with a thin coat, and the pulse is thin and weak, confirming the double deficit.
If poor appetite, loose stools, and a heavy sensation in the limbs appear alongside shortness of breath and a weak cough, the practitioner suspects Spleen and Lung Qi Deficiency. The Spleen fails to generate Qi and the Lung cannot distribute it, so the whole body feels under‑powered. The tongue is pale with tooth marks, and the pulse is weak, especially at the Lung and Spleen positions.
A more isolated Lung Qi Deficiency is likely when the main lingering issue is a low voice, a slight persistent cough, and breathlessness on mild exertion, while digestion stays relatively normal. This often follows a respiratory infection that specifically weakened the Lung. The tongue is pale with a thin white coat, and the pulse is deep and weak.
When the fatigue is accompanied by palpitations, poor memory, insomnia, and anxiety, the diagnosis turns to Heart and Spleen Qi and Blood Deficiency. The Heart is not nourished by Blood, and the Spleen cannot generate enough Qi, so mental and emotional restlessness join the physical tiredness. The tongue is pale and puffy, and the pulse is thin and weak.

### Qi Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/qi-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Fatigue that worsens with activity, Spontaneous daytime sweating, Shortness of breath on exertion, Reluctance to speak and a weak voice, Pale complexion, lips, and nails
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Overexertion, Emotional stress and worry, Skipping meals or irregular eating, Cold or raw foods and drinks
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Rest and sleep, Warm, easily digestible food and drinks, Gentle movement or breathing exercises, Warmth
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Fighting an infection is hard work for the body. Your defensive Qi is mobilized to expel the pathogen, which consumes a large amount of vital energy. Once the illness passes, the body's reserves are depleted, leaving you with insufficient Qi to power daily activities, which is why you feel drained and weak.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale, puffy, tender body with teeth marks; thin white coating. Pulse: empty (虚 xū) or weak (弱 ruò), soft and thin, lacking force, hollow at deep pressure.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Overexertion and stress directly consume Qi, while raw or cold foods weaken the Spleen's ability to produce new Qi. Rest and sleep allow the body to regenerate its vital energy, and warm, cooked meals support the Spleen in transforming food into Qi. Gentle movement circulates Qi without depleting it, helping you rebuild strength gradually.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat warm, cooked meals like soups and congees, and avoid raw, cold foods that tax the Spleen. Gentle daily movement such as walking or qigong helps circulate Qi without draining it. Prioritize rest and sleep to let the body rebuild its reserves.

### Qi and Blood Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/qi-and-blood-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Pale complexion, lips, and nails, Dizziness or lightheadedness, Heart palpitations or fluttering, Numbness or tingling in limbs, Poor appetite
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Overexertion, Skipping meals or irregular eating, Cold or raw foods and drinks, Emotional stress and worry
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Rest and sleep, Warm, easily digestible food and drinks, Gentle movement or breathing exercises, Consistent daily routine
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  After a significant infection, the body's Qi and Blood are both heavily drained. Qi deficiency leaves you feeling profoundly tired and weak, while Blood deficiency fails to nourish the heart and brain, leading to dizziness, palpitations, and a pale, sallow complexion. The Spleen, which produces Qi and Blood, is often weakened in the aftermath, creating a cycle where new energy cannot be generated.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale body, may be thin or show teeth marks, thin white coating. Pulse: fine (thin) and weak, may feel empty or moderate, especially weak at the right Guan (Spleen) position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Rest and a steady routine allow the body to conserve and rebuild Qi and Blood. Overexertion directly depletes both, just as emotional strain consumes Qi and disturbs Blood. Warm, nourishing foods support the Spleen in generating new Qi and Blood, while cold, raw foods weaken the digestive fire, making it harder to recover. Gentle movement helps circulate Qi without draining reserves, gradually restoring vitality.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat warm, easily digestible, nutrient-dense foods like bone broths, congees with red dates and goji berries, and lightly cooked leafy greens. Avoid raw, cold, and greasy foods that tax the Spleen. Gentle daily movement like walking or qi gong helps circulate Qi without draining it, and prioritize consistent, restful sleep.

### Spleen and Lung Qi Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/spleen-and-lung-qi-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Shortness of breath on exertion, Poor appetite with bloating after eating, Reluctance to speak and a weak voice, Spontaneous daytime sweating, Weak cough with thin, watery phlegm, Pale, puffy face
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Overexertion and too much talking, Cold or raw foods and drinks, Cold or damp weather and drafts, Emotional stress and worry
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Rest and sleep, Warm, easily digestible food and drinks, Gentle movement or breathing exercises, Warmth
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  After an infection the body’s Qi is heavily taxed. When the Spleen is weakened it can no longer transform food into the Qi and Blood needed for energy, while a weakened Lung fails to distribute that Qi to the muscles and surface. The result is a deep fatigue that comes with breathlessness, poor appetite, and a weak voice.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale, puffy, tender body with teeth marks, thin white slippery coating. Pulse: weak and forceless; right-side Cun (Lung) position empty, right-side Guan (Spleen) position soft and deficient.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: The Spleen and Lung are easily depleted by overwork, excessive speaking, and poor diet. Warmth and rest allow the Spleen to slowly rebuild Qi from food, while gentle breathing and avoiding cold protect the Lung’s function of distributing that Qi. Overexertion and cold foods directly weaken these organs, prolonging the fatigue.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat warm, cooked, easily digestible meals like congee, soups, and stewed vegetables. Avoid raw, cold, and greasy foods that further tax the Spleen. Gentle daily movement such as walking or qigong is helpful, but stop before you feel tired. Protect the neck and chest from wind and cold, and prioritize rest.

### Lung Qi Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/lung-qi-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Weak cough with thin, watery phlegm, Shortness of breath on exertion, Reluctance to speak and a weak voice, Spontaneous daytime sweating, Sensitivity to wind and cold, Clear, thin, watery phlegm
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Overexertion, Cold or damp weather and drafts, Talking too much
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Rest and sleep, Warm, easily digestible food and drinks, Gentle movement or breathing exercises, Warmth
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  After a febrile illness, the Lung's Qi is directly consumed. The Lung governs respiration and the exterior, so when its Qi is deficient, the body fails to generate enough energy, leading to persistent fatigue that worsens with even slight exertion. The weakened Lung also cannot distribute Qi to the voice and skin, causing a weak voice, spontaneous sweating, and susceptibility to catching colds.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale and tender body, possibly with teeth marks, thin white coating. Pulse: empty or weak overall, especially weak in the right Cun (Lung) position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Lung Qi deficiency means the body has less energy to power breathing and defend against external pathogens. Overexertion and talking further deplete Qi, while cold and wind invade the weakened exterior. Rest conserves Qi, warmth supports Yang, and gentle exercise gradually builds Lung capacity without overtaxing it.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Rest is essential; avoid overexertion and talking for long periods. Keep warm and avoid drafts, especially on the upper back and neck. Gentle breathing exercises like pursed-lip breathing can help strengthen the Lung. Eat warm, easily digestible foods such as congees and soups, and include qi‑tonifying ingredients like astragalus, ginger, and rice.

### Heart and Spleen Qi and Blood Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/heart-and-spleen-qi-and-blood-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Heart palpitations or fluttering, Insomnia with excessive dreaming, Forgetfulness and poor concentration, Poor appetite with bloating after eating, Pale complexion, lips, and nails, Loose stools
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Emotional stress and worry, Skipping meals or irregular eating, Cold or raw foods and drinks, Lack of sleep, Prolonged mental strain
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warm, easily digestible food and drinks, Gentle movement or breathing exercises, Calm, quiet environment, Consistent daily routine
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  After a serious infection, the body's Qi is severely drained, especially in the Spleen, which is responsible for extracting energy from food. When the Spleen cannot produce enough Qi and Blood, the Heart-which relies on this nourishment to house the mind-becomes undernourished. This leads to the hallmark combination of deep physical fatigue, poor digestion, and mental-emotional unrest like palpitations and restless sleep.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale, puffy body with teeth marks and a thin white coating. Pulse: fine (thin) and weak overall, especially empty at the left front (Cun) position and soft at the right middle (Guan) position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Overthinking and emotional strain directly consume Heart Blood and Spleen Qi, which are already depleted after illness, making fatigue and mental fog worse. Rest, nourishing warm foods, and gentle movement allow the Spleen to rebuild Qi and the Heart to be replenished with Blood, gradually restoring physical energy and mental clarity.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Focus on warm, easily digested meals like congees, soups, and stews. Include small amounts of blood-nourishing foods such as red dates, longan fruit, and lean meat. Avoid raw, cold, and greasy foods that tax the Spleen. Prioritize a regular sleep schedule and gentle, restorative movement like walking or qigong to rebuild strength without depleting reserves.

## คำถามที่พบบ่อย (Frequently Asked Questions)
### Why am I still so tired weeks after my flu or COVID?
In TCM, that lingering fatigue is a sign your Qi and possibly your Blood haven't been fully restored. Fighting an infection consumes a huge amount of your body's vital energy, and if you returned to normal activity too quickly or didn't nourish yourself properly, the deficit persists. The specific pattern - whether it's just Qi deficiency or also involves the Lung, Spleen, or Heart - determines how long it takes to bounce back and what treatment will help most.

### How can acupuncture help with post-infectious fatigue?
Acupuncture works by stimulating points that strengthen the organs most depleted after your illness. Points like Zusanli (ST-36) and Qihai (REN-6) directly boost Qi production; Feishu (BL-13) and Taiyuan (LU-9) support the Lung; Pishu (BL-20) and Sanyinjiao (SP-6) reinforce the Spleen. The needles prompt your body to redirect resources where they're needed, and many patients feel a gentle lift in energy right after a session, with cumulative improvement over several weeks.

### What foods should I eat to recover faster?
Focus on warm, cooked, easy-to-digest foods that don't tax your weakened Spleen. Think congee with a little ginger, steamed vegetables, soups with lean protein, and small amounts of well-cooked grains like rice or oats. Foods that particularly build Qi include sweet potato, carrot, dates, and mushrooms. Avoid cold, raw salads, iced drinks, greasy fried foods, and too much sugar, which all dampen digestive fire and slow recovery. Small, frequent meals are better than large ones.

### Can I take Chinese herbs alongside my regular medications?
In most cases, yes, but you must tell both your TCM practitioner and your medical doctor everything you're taking. Some Qi-tonifying herbs like Huang Qi (astragalus) are generally safe, but herbs that move Blood (like Dang Gui) could interact with blood thinners. Always bring a complete list of your prescriptions, supplements, and over-the-counter products to your TCM consultation so any potential interactions can be checked.

### Is it safe to exercise while I'm this tired?
Gentle movement like walking or qigong can help circulate Qi and prevent stagnation, but strenuous exercise that leaves you exhausted is counterproductive when you're in a deficiency state. TCM advises listening to your body: if activity makes you feel better afterwards, it's likely beneficial; if it wipes you out for the rest of the day, you're depleting reserves you haven't yet rebuilt. Your practitioner can guide you on the right balance as your energy returns.

### Will the fatigue come back after treatment?
When the underlying deficiency is properly corrected, the fatigue shouldn't return unless you go through another major illness or period of severe overwork. TCM treatment aims to rebuild your constitutional strength, not just mask the symptom. That said, it's wise to maintain good habits - regular meals, adequate sleep, and avoiding pushing yourself to exhaustion - to protect the gains you've made. Some patients choose to continue a maintenance herb formula or occasional acupuncture during stressful periods.
