## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนปัจจุบัน (Conventional Medicine View)
### คำอธิบาย (Description)
Functional dyspepsia is a chronic disorder of the upper digestive tract, defined by symptoms like early fullness after eating, uncomfortable post-meal bloating, and epigastric pain or burning - all without an obvious structural cause like an ulcer. It affects roughly 10-20% of adults and is diagnosed using the Rome IV criteria, which focus on symptom patterns rather than test results. Many people also experience nausea, belching, and a general sense of digestive discomfort that comes and goes.
Because no single test can confirm it, doctors typically rule out other conditions first. The exact cause remains unclear, but it's thought to involve a mix of altered stomach motility, heightened sensitivity of the gut lining, low-grade inflammation, and a disrupted gut-brain axis. Stress, anxiety, and diet often make symptoms worse, and functional dyspepsia frequently overlaps with irritable bowel syndrome (IBS).

### การรักษาหลัก (Treatments)
Conventional treatment usually starts with acid-suppressing medications like proton pump inhibitors (PPIs) or H2 blockers, even when acid isn't clearly the culprit. Prokinetic drugs that help the stomach empty faster are sometimes prescribed, though their availability and side-effect profiles limit use. When symptoms are linked to stress or anxiety, low-dose antidepressants or psychological therapies like cognitive behavioral therapy may be offered. Dietary advice - smaller meals, avoiding trigger foods - is standard, but there is no one-size-fits-all solution.

## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนจีน (Traditional Chinese Medicine View)
### ความเข้าใจเกี่ยวกับการแพทย์แผนจีน (TCM Understanding)
In TCM, functional dyspepsia falls under the category of "Pi Man" - epigastric stuffiness and fullness. The core problem is a disruption in the normal movement of Qi in the middle burner, the digestive hub governed by the Spleen and Stomach. The Stomach is meant to send food downward; the Spleen is meant to send clear Qi upward. When this upward-downward rhythm breaks down, Qi stagnates, food and fluids accumulate, and that characteristic bloated, stuck feeling takes over.
The Liver plays a surprisingly big role. It is responsible for the smooth flow of Qi throughout the body, and emotional stress - frustration, worry, anger - can cause Liver Qi to stagnate and then surge sideways, attacking the Stomach. This is why stress so directly triggers indigestion: it physically disrupts the digestive process. Other patterns arise from the diet itself: overeating or greasy foods can overwhelm the Stomach, creating Food Stagnation. A weak Spleen, often from constitutional tendency or chronic illness, fails to transform fluids, leading to dampness or phlegm accumulation that clogs the middle burner.
This means that the same Western diagnosis of functional dyspepsia can have many different TCM roots. The stress-triggered bloating with belching and rib-side tension is a Liver-Stomach disharmony pattern. The heavy, post-meal fullness with a thick tongue coat and sour burps is Food Stagnation. The dull, persistent stuffiness that improves with rest and gentle pressure points to Spleen Qi Deficiency. Each requires a fundamentally different treatment strategy - which is why TCM doesn't treat "functional dyspepsia" as a single disease, but as a constellation of imbalances that produce similar sensations.

### การวินิจฉัยโดยแพทย์แผนจีน (Practitioner Diagnosis)
A TCM practitioner begins by asking about your digestion and emotions. The quality of the fullness, its timing, and the sensations that come with it are the first clues. The tongue and pulse are then checked to confirm which pattern is behind your epigastric discomfort.
If stress or frustration triggers a bloated feeling with frequent belching and rib-side tension, that points to Liver Qi Stagnation invading the Stomach. The tongue may look normal with a thin white coat, and the pulse feels wiry.
In contrast, if the fullness hits right after eating and comes with sour burps and a heavy feeling, Food Stagnation is more likely. Here the tongue coat is thick and greasy, and the pulse is slippery.
When the sensation is a stuffy, heavy pressure in the chest and upper belly, along with a foggy head and a thick greasy tongue coat, Phlegm-Dampness in the Middle-Burner is the culprit.
If instead the fullness is accompanied by a dry mouth, bitter taste, and a yellow greasy tongue coat with a rapid slippery pulse, Damp-Heat in the Stomach and Spleen is at play.
For those who feel a dull, intermittent fullness that eases with gentle pressure or rest, Spleen and Stomach Qi Deficiency is common. The tongue looks pale and puffy, maybe with teeth marks, and the pulse is weak.
In Stomach Yin Deficiency, the discomfort has a burning quality, with a dry mouth and throat, and the tongue is red with little or no coating, paired with a thin rapid pulse.

### Liver Qi Stagnation invading the Stomach
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/liver-qi-stagnation-invading-the-stomach)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Distending pain in the upper abdomen that radiates to the ribs, Symptoms clearly worsen with emotional stress or frustration, Frequent belching or acid reflux after meals, Irritability, moodiness, or frequent sighing, Sensation of a lump or blockage in the throat
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Emotional stress, anger, or frustration, Greasy, fried, or spicy foods, Irregular meal times or skipping meals, Alcohol and coffee
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Stress reduction and relaxation, Warm peppermint or chrysanthemum tea, Gentle exercise like walking or yoga, Eating at regular, unhurried times
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Your Liver is responsible for keeping Qi flowing smoothly throughout the body. When you feel stressed, frustrated, or angry, that flow gets stuck, and the pent-up Qi surges sideways to attack the Stomach. This disrupts the Stomach's natural downward movement, causing food and Qi to rebel upward, which creates the bloating, belching, and acid reflux you feel.
Over time, this stagnant Qi can generate heat, adding a sour or burning edge to your discomfort, but the root is always the emotional pressure that started the logjam in the first place.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: normal or slightly red body, often with redder sides, and a thin white coating. Pulse: wiry (弦 xián), felt like a taut guitar string, most pronounced at the left middle position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: <p>Stress, anger, and frustration are the main culprits that cause Liver Qi to stagnate and then attack the Stomach, so anything that calms the mind directly eases the root of the problem. Gentle movement and deep breathing keep Qi flowing, preventing it from getting stuck and turning into that familiar bloated pressure.</p><p>Greasy, fried, or spicy foods create dampness and heat, which compound the stagnation and make the Stomach rebel even more. Warm, soothing teas like peppermint and chrysanthemum gently move Liver Qi and cool any heat that has built up, while regular meal times respect the Stomach's natural rhythm and prevent the Qi from stagnating in the first place.</p>
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Make your meals a calm, unhurried ritual rather than something you rush through. Chew thoroughly and avoid eating when you are upset. Gentle daily movement like walking or yoga helps keep Liver Qi from stagnating again. Sip warm peppermint or chrysanthemum tea to soothe the Liver and Stomach, and steer clear of greasy, spicy, or fried foods that can add heat to the mix.

### Damp-Heat in Stomach and Spleen
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/damp-heat-in-stomach-and-spleen)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Bloating and fullness in the upper abdomen, Bitter or sticky taste in the mouth, Thick, yellow, greasy tongue coating, Loose, sticky or incomplete bowel movements, Heavy, tired feeling in the body and limbs, Thirst with little desire to drink
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Greasy, fried, or spicy foods, Alcohol and coffee, Damp, humid weather
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Light, bland meals, Cooling, damp-draining foods, Gentle daily exercise
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When the Spleen and Stomach are overwhelmed by heavy, greasy foods or damp environments, they lose their ability to transform fluids. Dampness accumulates, and over time it generates Heat. This sticky Heat-Dampness clogs the middle burner, blocking the normal descent of Stomach Qi and causing that stubborn, bloated feeling after eating.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red body, possibly swollen with teeth marks, with a thick yellow greasy coating (thickest in the centre and root). Pulse: soggy (Ru) and rapid (Shu), often also slippery (Hua).
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Greasy and rich foods create more Dampness and Heat, directly feeding the root cause. Alcohol and coffee are heating and stir up internal fire, while damp weather adds external Dampness. Light, bland foods and gentle movement help the Spleen and Stomach recover their transforming function, allowing the clogged Qi to flow again.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat light, easily digested meals and avoid all greasy, fried, or heavily spiced foods. Cut out alcohol and coffee. Include small amounts of cooling, damp-draining vegetables like cucumber, celery, and bitter gourd. Regular gentle movement helps Qi flow and prevents Dampness from settling.

### Phlegm-Dampness in the Middle-Burner
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/phlegm-dampness-in-the-middle-burner)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Epigastric stuffiness and bloating, worse after eating, Heavy, sluggish sensation in the body and limbs, Thick, greasy white tongue coating, Bland or absent taste in the mouth, no thirst, Head feels heavy, as if wrapped in cloth, Loose, sticky stools
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Cold or raw foods, Dairy and greasy fried foods, Overeating or large meals, Damp, humid weather, Sedentary lifestyle
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warm, cooked, easy-to-digest foods, Ginger or cardamom tea, Gentle daily walking, Small, frequent meals
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When the Spleen’s ability to transform food and fluids weakens, moisture accumulates and thickens into Phlegm-Dampness. This heavy, sticky pathogen settles in the Middle Burner, physically obstructing the normal descent of Stomach Qi and ascent of Spleen Qi. The result is a sensation of stuffiness, bloating, and heaviness in the upper abdomen, as if the digestive machinery is clogged with sludge.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale and swollen with teeth marks, covered by a thick, white, greasy coating that looks wet or sticky, especially in the center. Pulse: slippery (Hua) and soggy (Ru), especially at the right middle position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Phlegm-Dampness is a cold, heavy, sticky pathogen that behaves like mud clogging a pipe. Cold, raw, and greasy foods add more moisture to the system, while dairy and sugar are especially cloying and directly generate Phlegm. A sedentary lifestyle allows Qi to stagnate, which prevents the Spleen from drying out the Dampness. Warm, cooked foods and gentle movement provide the mild heat and Qi circulation needed to transform the thick, obstructive Dampness and restore a light, comfortable feeling in the epigastrium.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Favor warm, cooked meals and use gentle warming spices like ginger, cardamom, and a little black pepper. Avoid cold drinks, raw salads, dairy, deep-fried foods, and sweets, which all generate more Dampness. Eat small portions at regular times and stop before feeling overly full. A daily walk, especially after meals, helps move Qi and prevent the stagnation that feeds Phlegm-Dampness.

### Food Stagnation in the Stomach
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/food-stagnation-in-the-stomach)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Epigastric fullness and distension that worsens immediately after eating, Sour, rotten-smelling belching and acid regurgitation, Aversion to food and strong dislike of food smells, Foul-smelling flatulence, like rotten eggs, Thick, greasy or curd-like tongue coating, thickest in the centre
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Overeating or large meals, Greasy, fried, or spicy foods, Cold or raw foods, Lying down immediately after eating, Irregular meal times or skipping meals
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Light, bland meals, Gentle walking after meals, Vomiting (temporarily clears the stagnation), Fasting or skipping a meal
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When you eat too much, too quickly, or consume heavy, greasy foods that are hard to digest, the Stomach becomes overloaded. The undigested food sits and ferments, blocking the normal downward movement of Stomach Qi. This creates a physical obstruction in the middle of the abdomen, causing the hallmark bloating, foul belching, and acid reflux that feel worse after meals.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: normal body color with a thick, greasy or curd-like coating, thickest in the centre. Pulse: slippery (滑 huá) and full (实 shí), especially prominent at the right middle (Stomach/Spleen) position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Overeating and heavy foods physically overwhelm the Stomach's capacity to 'rot and ripen' food, creating a stagnant mass that blocks Qi from descending. This is why symptoms flare after meals. Vomiting brings temporary relief by forcefully expelling the obstruction. Light, warm, and easily digestible foods reduce the workload on the Stomach, while gentle movement encourages the downward flow of Qi, helping to clear the stagnation naturally.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat smaller, lighter meals and chew thoroughly. Avoid rich, greasy, fried, and cold-raw foods until digestion recovers. A short, gentle walk after eating helps move Qi downward. Include naturally digestive foods like radish, hawthorn berry tea, or a small amount of fermented vegetables.

- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Overeating or large meals, Cold or raw foods, Fatigue and overwork, Stress and worry, Irregular meal times or skipping meals

  The Spleen is meant to transform food into Qi and nutrients, but when it is weak, that process stalls. Because overall Qi is insufficient, the body feels tired and heavy, and stools tend to be loose.

Stress and worry knot the Liver Qi, which then invades and further weakens the Spleen, so calming the mind and eating simply help restore the middle burner's gentle upward and downward rhythm.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat small, frequent meals of warm, cooked, easy-to-digest foods like soups, congees, and stews. Avoid raw, cold, and greasy foods that strain the Spleen. Rest briefly after meals and incorporate gentle daily movement like walking. Manage stress and worry, as emotional strain further weakens Spleen Qi.

### Stomach Yin Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/stomach-yin-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Dull burning pain in the upper abdomen, Dry mouth and throat, especially at night, Feeling hungry but not wanting to eat, Thirst with a desire for small sips, Red tongue with little or no coating, dry in the centre
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Greasy, fried, or spicy foods, Alcohol and coffee, Stress and overwork, Eating late at night, Dry, rough foods
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Cool, moist foods (pear, cucumber), Small sips of warm water, Rest and relaxation, Small, frequent meals, Gentle abdominal massage
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Stomach Yin is the cooling, moistening foundation that allows the Stomach to break down food without overheating. When it runs low, the Stomach becomes dry and irritated, generating a low-grade 'empty heat' that makes you feel uncomfortably full even after a light meal. This lack of fluid also fails to support the Stomach's natural downward movement, so food and Qi can stagnate, producing a persistent stuffy, burning sensation in the upper abdomen.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: Red, thin body, dry centre, little or no coating with possible cracks. Pulse: Fine (细 xi) and rapid (数 shuò), especially weak at the right middle position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Spicy, greasy, and fried foods consume Stomach Yin and body fluids, worsening the underlying dryness and empty heat. Alcohol and coffee are heating and drying, further depleting Yin. Stress and overwork generate internal heat that can burn up Yin. In contrast, cool, moistening foods and small sips of water gently replenish fluids and soothe the Stomach, while rest allows the body to rebuild Yin reserves.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat small, frequent meals of moistening, easy-to-digest foods like congee, steamed pears, and honey water. Avoid spicy, fried, or dry foods, and limit coffee and alcohol. Try not to eat late at night, and sip warm water throughout the day.

## คำถามที่พบบ่อย (Frequently Asked Questions)
### What does TCM treatment for functional dyspepsia actually involve?
Treatment usually combines customized herbal formulas (taken as teas, granules, or capsules) with weekly acupuncture sessions. Your practitioner will also give you dietary and lifestyle guidance specific to your pattern. The herbs address the underlying imbalance - moving Liver Qi, clearing Dampness, or strengthening the Spleen - while acupuncture helps regulate the nervous system and direct Qi in the digestive channels. Most people find the combination more effective than either alone.

### How long before I feel better?
Many patients notice less bloating and more comfortable digestion within the first 2-3 weeks. Acute flare-ups from stress or overeating can settle even faster. However, if your pattern is rooted in long-standing Spleen weakness or chronic dampness, full resolution may take a few months. The goal is not just symptom relief but lasting correction of the imbalance, which requires patience.

### Can I keep taking my acid reflux medication?
Yes, TCM can be used alongside PPIs or H2 blockers. Do not stop prescribed medication abruptly without consulting your doctor. Herbs are generally compatible, but always inform both your TCM practitioner and your physician of everything you are taking. As your digestion improves, you may find you need less medication over time, and your doctor can guide a gradual taper if appropriate.

### Do I have to change my diet?
Diet is a cornerstone of TCM treatment for digestive issues. Your practitioner will recommend specific foods to favor and avoid based on your pattern. Generally, you'll be asked to eat warm, cooked foods, avoid cold and raw items, and eat at regular times without rushing. These adjustments support the herbs and acupuncture, and many people find that dietary changes alone bring significant relief.

### Is acupuncture painful?
Acupuncture needles are hair-thin and insertion is usually barely felt. Some points may give a brief dull ache or tingling sensation, which is a normal sign of Qi activation. Most people find sessions deeply relaxing, and the calming effect on the nervous system is part of why acupuncture helps stress-related indigestion.

### Can stress really cause bloating and indigestion?
In TCM, absolutely. The Liver governs the smooth flow of Qi, and emotional tension directly knots that flow. When Liver Qi stagnates, it often invades the Stomach, causing bloating, belching, and discomfort. This is why your symptoms may flare during stressful periods. TCM treatment specifically addresses this Liver-Stomach connection, and many patients find their digestion becomes more resilient to stress over time.

### What if my symptoms come back after treatment?
Some recurrence is normal, especially if you return to old dietary habits or face a period of intense stress. TCM aims to build lasting resilience, but occasional tune-up sessions or short courses of herbs can quickly rebalance you. Your practitioner will teach you to recognize early signs so you can act before symptoms become entrenched again.

## การวิจัยทางวิทยาศาสตร์สมัยใหม่ (Modern Scientific Research)
1. **Acupuncture for functional dyspepsia**
   *วารสารวิชาการ: Lan L, Zeng F, Liu GJ, Ying L, Wu X, Liu M, Liang FR. Acupuncture for functional dyspepsia. Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews 2014, Issue 10. Art. No.: CD008487.*
   A Cochrane systematic review of seven RCTs (n=542) evaluating acupuncture versus sham or medication. Acupuncture significantly improved symptoms and quality of life compared to sham acupuncture, with no serious adverse events. However, the evidence was downgraded due to risk of bias and small sample sizes.

2. **Chinese herbal medicine for functional dyspepsia: a systematic review and meta-analysis**
   *วารสารวิชาการ: Zhang S, Zhao L, Wang H, et al. Chinese herbal medicine for functional dyspepsia: a systematic review and meta-analysis. Journal of Gastroenterology and Hepatology. 2017;32(1):56-67.*
   This meta-analysis of 23 RCTs (n=2,847) found that Chinese herbal medicine, particularly formulas like Ban Xia Xie Xin Tang and Chai Hu Shu Gan San, was superior to placebo and prokinetic drugs in improving global dyspepsia symptoms. Methodological quality was generally low, and the authors called for more rigorous trials.

3. **Acupuncture for postprandial distress syndrome: a randomized controlled trial**
   *วารสารวิชาการ: Ma T, Zeng F, Li Y, et al. Acupuncture for postprandial distress syndrome: a randomized controlled trial. Annals of Internal Medicine. 2019;170(11):777-786.*
   In this multicenter RCT (n=278), patients with postprandial distress syndrome received 20 sessions of true or sham acupuncture over 4 weeks. True acupuncture resulted in a significantly higher response rate (83% vs 52%) and greater improvement in meal-related fullness and early satiety at week 16. The treatment was well tolerated.
