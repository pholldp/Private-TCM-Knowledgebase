## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนปัจจุบัน (Conventional Medicine View)
### คำอธิบาย (Description)
Western medicine defines proctitis as inflammation of the mucosa lining the rectum. This can result from infections, inflammatory bowel disease (like ulcerative colitis), radiation therapy for pelvic cancers, or reduced blood flow. The hallmark symptoms are rectal bleeding, a persistent feeling of needing to empty the bowels (tenesmus), and the passage of mucus or pus.
Diagnosis is typically made through a combination of symptom history, a digital rectal exam, and direct visualization with a proctoscope or colonoscope. Standard treatment depends on the cause, ranging from antibiotics for an infection to anti-inflammatory suppositories or systemic medications for chronic inflammatory conditions.

### การรักษาหลัก (Treatments)
Conventional treatment is directed at the assumed cause. For infectious proctitis, antibiotics or antivirals are used. For inflammatory causes, doctors prescribe 5-aminosalicylic acid (5-ASA) suppositories, corticosteroids, or biologic medications. While these are often effective at calming the acute inflammation, they primarily work by suppressing the local immune response rather than addressing why the tissue became vulnerable to inflammation in the first place.

## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนจีน (Traditional Chinese Medicine View)
### ความเข้าใจเกี่ยวกับการแพทย์แผนจีน (TCM Understanding)
TCM understands proctitis primarily as a disorder of the Spleen and Large Intestine, often complicated by Dampness and Heat. The Spleen is responsible for transforming the food and fluids you consume into usable energy. When the Spleen is weakened by poor diet, stress, or fatigue, it cannot do its job properly.
Instead of being processed, fluids accumulate into pathological Dampness, which is heavy and turbid. This Dampness naturally sinks downward, pooling in the lower body and settling in the Large Intestine.
Once Dampness becomes trapped, it can combine with Heat, creating a hot, sticky environment that irritates the intestinal lining. This is the most common acute pattern, leading to the classic burning sensation, urgent diarrhea, and mucus or blood in the stool. However, the Heat can also be absent, leaving a pattern of Cold-Damp, where the pain is cramping and relieved by warmth.
Chronic cases often involve a weakening of the Kidney Yang, the body's internal pilot light, leading to a deep, chronic type of Dampness that causes diarrhea first thing in the morning.
Emotional stress plays a major role too. The Liver is responsible for the smooth flow of Qi. When you're frustrated or stressed, Liver Qi can become stuck. This stuck energy often attacks the Spleen, disrupting digestion and triggering a flare-up of proctitis symptoms.
This is why one person's proctitis might flare with spicy food, while another's is triggered by a stressful deadline. By identifying which of these patterns is dominant, TCM treats the root cause, not just the inflamed tissue.

### การวินิจฉัยโดยแพทย์แผนจีน (Practitioner Diagnosis)
A TCM practitioner begins by asking about the bowel movements themselves - their consistency, color, odor, and whether they contain mucus or blood. The timing of diarrhea, the type of abdominal pain, and what makes it better or worse are the first clues that point toward one pattern rather than another. The tongue and pulse are then examined to confirm the underlying imbalance.
When stools are urgent, foul-smelling, and contain mucus or blood, with a burning sensation around the anus and a feeling of incomplete evacuation (tenesmus), this points to Damp-Heat in the Large Intestine. The tongue is typically red with a yellow, greasy coating, and the pulse feels rapid and slippery. This acute pattern often follows spicy, greasy food or an infection.
If diarrhea is watery, accompanied by cramping abdominal pain that feels better with warmth, and there is an aversion to cold, the pattern is likely Cold-Damp invading the Spleen. The tongue appears pale with a white, greasy coating, and the pulse is slow and soggy. This pattern often arises after exposure to cold or eating cold, raw foods.
Loose stools that contain undigested food, along with fatigue, poor appetite, and a heavy sensation in the body, suggest Spleen Deficiency with Dampness. The tongue is pale and puffy with a greasy coating, and the pulse is weak. This chronic pattern develops when the digestive system is weakened over time, often by poor diet or overwork.
If abdominal pain and diarrhea flare up during times of stress, frustration, or anxiety, the pattern is Rebellious Liver Qi invading the Spleen. The pain often moves around and is relieved by passing gas or stool. The tongue may be red on the edges with a thin coating, and the pulse feels wiry, especially on the left side. Emotional triggers are the hallmark.
Early-morning diarrhea, where the person must rush to the bathroom upon waking, accompanied by a cold sensation in the lower abdomen and lower back, points to Kidney Yang Deficiency. The tongue is pale, swollen, and may have teeth marks, while the pulse is deep and thin. This pattern typically appears in long-standing cases where the body’s warming fire has weakened.

### Damp-Heat in the Large Intestine
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/damp-heat-in-the-large-intestine)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Urgent straining with a feeling of incomplete evacuation (tenesmus), Burning sensation at the anus during and after bowel movements, Foul-smelling, yellow-brown stools that may contain mucus or blood, Abdominal cramping and pain that improves slightly after passing stool, Feeling of body heat, thirst, and scanty dark urine
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Spicy, greasy, or deep-fried foods, Alcohol and coffee, Hot, humid weather, Emotional stress or frustration
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Cooling, bland foods (congee, mung beans, barley), Plenty of water at room temperature, Rest, Cool compress on the lower abdomen
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When Dampness and Heat combine and settle in the Large Intestine, they create a hot, sticky environment that irritates the intestinal lining. This causes the gut to become inflamed and overly sensitive, triggering urgent, crampy bowel movements. The Heat scorches the fluids and blood vessels, leading to burning pain, foul-smelling stools, and the passage of mucus or blood.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red body with a thick, yellow, greasy coating, thickest in the centre and root. Pulse: slippery (Hua) and rapid (Shu).
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Damp-Heat thrives on hot, sticky, and hard-to-digest foods that add more "fuel" to the fire and moisture to the swamp. Spicy and greasy foods directly introduce Heat and Dampness, while alcohol and stress fan the flames. Cooling, bland foods and rest help the body drain the excess Dampness and clear the Heat, giving the inflamed gut a chance to recover.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Stick to light, bland, easily digestible foods like congee, steamed vegetables, and mung bean soup. Avoid all greasy, fried, spicy, or heavily sweetened foods, as well as dairy and alcohol, which create more Dampness and Heat. Drink plenty of room-temperature water, and rest during acute flare-ups to allow the gut to heal.

### Spleen Deficiency with Dampness
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/spleen-deficiency-with-dampness)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Loose stools with undigested food, Abdominal bloating, worse after eating, Fatigue and heaviness in the body and limbs, Poor appetite or lack of taste, Pale, puffy tongue with teeth marks and a greasy white coating
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Cold or raw foods, Greasy or heavy foods, Overeating, Damp or humid weather, Prolonged sitting
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warm, cooked foods, Rest, Small frequent meals, Warmth on the abdomen, Gentle movement or walking
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When the Spleen is weak, it cannot properly transform food and fluids. This leads to an accumulation of Dampness, which is heavy and turbid. The Dampness sinks down into the Large Intestine, disrupting its function and causing loose stools, often with undigested food and mucus. The chronic nature of this pattern leaves the body feeling heavy and fatigued, with bloating that worsens after eating because the weakened Spleen struggles even more with each meal.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale and swollen, often with teeth marks on the sides, and a white greasy coating that feels moist. Pulse: soggy (Ru) and slow (Huan), sometimes with a slippery (Hua) quality.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: The Spleen thrives on warmth and dryness. Cold and raw foods douse its digestive fire, while greasy, heavy foods create the very Dampness that clogs its function. Overeating simply gives an already weak Spleen too much to handle. Rest and warmth conserve Qi and help the Spleen work more efficiently, while gentle movement encourages Qi to move and Dampness to be dispersed. Small meals are easier to transform, so the Spleen can keep up without becoming overloaded.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat warm, cooked, and easily digestible foods like soups, congees, and steamed vegetables. Avoid raw, cold, and greasy foods, as well as dairy, which all create more Dampness. Eat smaller meals more frequently so the Spleen is not overwhelmed. Gentle daily movement like walking or stretching helps Qi flow and clears Dampness, but avoid overexertion. A warm compress on the abdomen can soothe bloating.

### Cold-Damp invading the Spleen
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/cold-damp-invading-the-spleen)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Watery, loose stools without strong odor, Dull, cold abdominal pain that eases with warmth, Aversion to cold, body feels heavy, Poor appetite and bland taste in the mouth, Thick, white, greasy tongue coating
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Cold or raw foods, Greasy or heavy foods, Cold, damp weather, Overeating
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warmth on the abdomen, Warm, cooked foods, Ginger tea, Gentle abdominal massage
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When Cold and Dampness invade from the outside or build up from a diet heavy in raw, cold foods, they overwhelm the Spleen's ability to transform fluids. The Spleen loses its grip, and Dampness pours downward into the intestines instead of being processed upward. This floods the bowel with unprocessed fluids and Cold, producing watery, urgent diarrhea with cramping pain.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale and swollen with teeth marks, thick white greasy coating. Pulse: soggy (Ru) or slippery (Hua) and slow (Chi), especially soft at the right Guan position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Cold-Damp invading the Spleen is exactly what it sounds like-the digestive system is overwhelmed by Cold and moisture that clogs its function. Cold contracts and slows movement, causing the cramping pain, while Dampness creates the watery stools and heavy sensation.
Warmth-whether from cooked food, ginger tea, or a hot compress-directly counteracts the Cold by restoring movement and thawing the stagnation. Avoiding raw, cold, and greasy foods stops more Dampness from entering an already waterlogged system. Gentle abdominal massage helps move Qi locally, relieving the dull, fixed pain that comes with Cold-Damp obstruction.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Warm, cooked, and easily digested foods are your foundation-think congee, soups, and stews. Avoid raw, cold, and greasy foods entirely during recovery, including salads, smoothies, ice water, and dairy. A warm compress or hot water bottle on the abdomen can bring immediate relief during cramps.

### Rebellious Liver Qi invading the Spleen
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/rebellious-liver-qi-invading-the-spleen)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Diarrhea triggered by emotional upset, Pain along the ribs or flanks, Irritability or quick temper, Pain relief after bowel movement, Alternating constipation and loose stools
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Emotional stress or frustration, Cold or raw foods, Irregular eating habits
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Stress relief, Gentle movement or walking, Warm, cooked foods
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  In this pattern, emotional stress and frustration cause the Liver's energy to become stuck. Instead of flowing smoothly, it surges sideways and attacks the Spleen. This disrupts the Spleen's ability to transport fluids and food, creating a cycle of tension, pain, and unpredictable bowel movements.
When the Liver Qi rebels, the rhythm of the intestines becomes chaotic. The abdominal pain and diarrhea are a direct result of this internal conflict between the Liver and Spleen, often flaring up exactly when a person feels most pressured or upset.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: normal or slightly pale body, possible teeth marks, thin white coating. Pulse: wiry (弦 xián) overall, especially on the left guan position, with a softer feel on the right guan position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Emotional stress is the key trigger because it directly causes Liver Qi to stagnate, which then attacks the Spleen. This is the very mechanism of this pattern, so the pain and diarrhea are a direct result of this internal conflict. Warm, cooked foods support the Spleen's weakened digestive function, while cold and raw foods further damage it. Gentle exercise helps unblock the stagnant Liver Qi, allowing it to flow smoothly instead of surging sideways into the digestive system.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat small, warm meals at regular times and avoid cold or raw foods that further weaken the Spleen. Gentle daily exercise, like walking, helps keep Liver Qi flowing smoothly.

### Kidney Yang Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/kidney-yang-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Early&#x2011;morning diarrhea (before dawn), Cold sensation or pain in the lower abdomen that feels better with warmth, Soreness and cold pain in the lower back and knees, Feeling cold all over, especially in the lower body, Fatigue and low spirits
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Cold weather or exposure, Cold or raw foods, Overwork and chronic fatigue, Excessive sexual activity
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warmth on the abdomen, Warm, cooked foods, Gentle movement or walking, Rest
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Your Kidneys supply the body's warming fire. When that fire dims, the Spleen cannot properly transform food and fluids, leading to loose stools. In proctitis, this shows up as chronic diarrhea that strikes especially in the early morning, because Kidney Yang is at its lowest ebb just before dawn. The rectum feels cold and unable to hold stool, reflecting the deeper failure of the body's warming and holding functions.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale, puffy, and tender with teeth marks, wet white coating. Pulse: deep, slow, and weak, especially at the rear position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Kidney Yang Deficiency means the body's warming fire is low. Cold foods and environments further drain that fire, while warmth, rest, and nourishing foods gently stoke it. Gentle movement helps Yang Qi rise without exhausting it, which is why moderate activity feels good but over‑exertion sets you back.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Keep the lower abdomen and lower back warm - a belly wrap or hot water bottle helps. Eat warm, cooked, easily digested foods like congee with ginger, and avoid raw, cold, or iced items. Gentle morning movement like walking supports Yang Qi, but avoid over‑exerting yourself.

## คำถามที่พบบ่อย (Frequently Asked Questions)
### Can Chinese herbs really stop the bleeding and mucus?
Yes, specific herbs are very effective at cooling the blood and stopping bleeding. In TCM, bleeding in proctitis is usually seen as Heat in the Large Intestine scorching the blood vessels. Herbal formulas like Shao Yao Tang or Bai Tou Weng Tang contain herbs like Huang Qin and Huang Lian that clear Heat and dry Dampness, which addresses the root cause of the bleeding. Many patients see a noticeable decrease in blood and mucus within the first two weeks of treatment.

### I've had this for years. Is it too late for TCM to help?
No, it is not too late. While acute Damp-Heat cases respond fastest, TCM excels at treating chronic, recurrent proctitis. Chronic cases often involve an underlying weakness of the Spleen or Kidneys. By using herbal formulas to strengthen these organ systems, TCM can help break the cycle of flare-ups and remissions, reducing their frequency and severity over time.

### Do I have to change my diet for the herbs to work?
Yes, diet is a central part of the treatment. Dampness in TCM is often created by what we eat. During a flare-up, it is critical to avoid cold, raw, greasy, spicy, and sugary foods which all contribute to Dampness and Heat. Your practitioner will guide you to eat warm, cooked foods that are easy to digest, which allows the herbs to work more effectively. Without dietary changes, the Dampness will simply return.

### Can TCM help with the urgent, 'gotta go right now' feeling?
Yes, that sensation of urgency and incomplete evacuation (tenesmus) is a classic sign of Damp-Heat in the Large Intestine. The herbs in formulas like Shao Yao Tang work to clear this Dampness and Heat, which directly reduces the irritation and spasms causing the urgency. As the inflammation subsides, the urgent feeling typically calms down.

### Is acupuncture used directly on the inflamed area?
No, acupuncture needles are not inserted into the rectum. Acupuncture points on the abdomen, legs, and back are used to regulate the function of the Spleen, Stomach, and Large Intestine. Points like Tianshu ST-25 and Zusanli ST-36 are very effective at calming abdominal pain and regulating bowel function from a distance, without causing any irritation to the inflamed tissue itself.

### Can I combine TCM with the suppositories my doctor gave me?
In most cases, yes. TCM herbal treatment can be safely combined with conventional anti-inflammatory suppositories or oral medications. This is a common strategy to get the acute inflammation under control quickly while the herbs begin their deeper, corrective work. Always inform both your TCM practitioner and your gastroenterologist about all treatments you are using.
