## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนปัจจุบัน (Conventional Medicine View)
### คำอธิบาย (Description)
In conventional medicine, hemiplegia is defined as total or partial paralysis of one side of the body, typically caused by a cerebrovascular accident (stroke) that damages the motor pathways in the brain. Other causes include traumatic brain injury, brain tumors, or conditions like cerebral palsy. Diagnosis is confirmed through neurological examination and imaging such as CT or MRI scans. The primary focus of treatment is rehabilitation: physical therapy to maintain muscle strength and prevent contractures, occupational therapy to relearn daily activities, and speech therapy when needed. Medications may be used to manage spasticity, and in some cases, surgical interventions like tendon release are considered.

### การรักษาหลัก (Treatments)
Standard rehabilitation includes physical therapy to improve mobility and prevent complications like joint stiffness, occupational therapy to regain independence in daily tasks, and speech-language therapy for communication and swallowing difficulties. For spasticity, muscle relaxants such as baclofen or botulinum toxin injections are commonly prescribed. Supportive devices like braces, walkers, or wheelchairs are used as needed. The goal is to maximize function and quality of life through repetitive, task-oriented training.

## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนจีน (Traditional Chinese Medicine View)
### ความเข้าใจเกี่ยวกับการแพทย์แผนจีน (TCM Understanding)
In TCM, hemiplegia is understood as the aftermath of a "wind-stroke" (中风) that has left the body's vital substances in disarray. The key organ systems involved are the Liver (which governs the sinews), the Spleen (which governs the muscles and produces Qi and Blood), the Kidneys (which store essence and nourish the bones and marrow), and the Heart (which circulates blood and houses the mind). After a stroke, Qi becomes severely depleted and fails to move blood, leading to stagnation. At the same time, phlegm and internal wind may obstruct the channels that carry energy and blood to the limbs, causing paralysis.
The most important clue for a TCM practitioner is the quality of the paralysis. A floppy, weak, "flaccid" limb with severe fatigue and a pale, slightly dark tongue points to Qi Deficiency causing Blood Stagnation. Here, the body simply lacks the energetic force to push blood through the channels.
In contrast, a stiff, contracted, "spastic" limb with muscle wasting, dry skin, and a red, cracked tongue suggests Yin and Blood Deficiency or Kidney and Liver Yin Deficiency-the sinews are undernourished and have become tight and unyielding.
Other patterns include Wind-Phlegm, where a sudden obstruction by phlegm and internal wind causes heavy, weak limbs and slurred speech, and Heart and Spleen Deficiency, where poor blood production leaves the limbs and mind undernourished, leading to lingering weakness with anxiety and poor appetite.
Each pattern arises from a different root imbalance, which is why TCM does not treat all hemiplegia the same way. Through acupuncture, herbal formulas, and dietary therapy, TCM aims to unblock the channels, replenish what is deficient, and calm internal wind-addressing not just the paralysis but also the fatigue, insomnia, and emotional distress that often accompany it.

### การวินิจฉัยโดยแพทย์แผนจีน (Practitioner Diagnosis)
A practitioner first asks about the quality of paralysis: is the limb floppy and weak (flaccid) or stiff and resistant to movement (spastic)? The timing after the stroke also matters - acute sudden paralysis suggests Wind-Phlegm, while chronic lingering weakness points toward deficiency patterns.
If the paralysis is flaccid with severe fatigue, a pale complexion, and a weak pulse, Qi Deficiency causing Blood Stagnation is likely. The tongue may be pale or slightly dark with a thin white coat. This pattern reflects the body’s inability to push blood through the channels after the initial stroke, leaving the limb limp and heavy.
When the affected limb feels tight, spasms easily, and there is visible muscle wasting with dry skin, Yin and Blood Deficiency is the key. The tongue is pale-red with little coating, and the pulse is thin and rapid. Here the sinews are undernourished, so stiffness dominates over pure weakness, and gentle movement may temporarily ease the tension.
If the person also complains of dizziness, tinnitus, a sore lower back, and a sensation of heat in the palms or chest, then Kidney and Liver Yin Deficiency is the root. The tongue is red without coating, and the pulse is fine and rapid. This deep insufficiency allowed internal wind to rise, triggering the stroke and leaving the limbs uncoordinated and poorly controlled.
In the early post-stroke phase, sudden hemiplegia accompanied by slurred speech, facial droop, and a heavy or numb sensation points to Wind-Phlegm. The tongue is swollen with a greasy white coat, and the pulse is slippery. This acute pattern requires clearing phlegm and extinguishing wind to reopen the obstructed channels.
In chronic cases with poor recovery, if the person looks pale, feels palpitations, has a poor appetite, and loose stools, Heart and Spleen Deficiency may be hindering rehabilitation. The tongue is pale and puffy with a thin white coat, and the pulse is weak. This shows the body lacks the resources to generate Qi and blood for healing.

### Qi Deficiency causing Blood Stagnation
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/qi-deficiency-causing-blood-stagnation)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Flaccid limb weakness, not spastic, Fatigue and lack of strength, Dusky or pale-grey facial complexion, Numbness or tingling in the affected side, Spontaneous sweating with mild exertion
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Overexertion, Cold and raw foods, Stress and worry, Damp or cold weather
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Gentle exercise (walking, tai chi), Warmth on the affected side, Nourishing soups and stews, Adequate rest
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  After a stroke, the body's Qi is often severely depleted. Qi is the force that moves blood, so when it is weak, blood slows down and stagnates in the vessels. This stasis blocks the collaterals that nourish the muscles and sinews on one side of the body, leading to the weakness or flaccid paralysis characteristic of this pattern.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale-dark or pale-purple body with stasis spots, teeth marks, thin white coat. Pulse: deep (Chen), fine/weak (Xi/Ruo), and choppy (Se).
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Qi deficiency is the root of this pattern, so overexertion and emotional stress further drain Qi, while cold and raw foods congeal blood and worsen stasis. Rest and warmth conserve Qi and encourage blood to move. Nourishing foods build Qi and blood from the inside, and very gentle movement activates the channels without depleting energy, helping to gradually restore function.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat warm, nourishing foods like chicken soup with astragalus, dark leafy greens, and dates. Avoid cold, raw foods that can worsen stagnation. Gentle daily movement such as slow walking or tai chi helps Qi flow without depleting it, while adequate rest is equally important.

### Yin and Blood Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/yin-and-blood-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Spastic paralysis with muscle tightness, Muscle wasting (atrophy), Dry mouth and throat, Night sweats, Five-centre heat (palms, soles, chest), Dizziness and lightheadedness
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Overwork and stress, Spicy, fried foods, Dry, hot weather, Excessive sweating, Late nights
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Rest and adequate sleep, Moist, nourishing foods, Gentle stretching, Cool, humid environment, Massage with oils
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  The body's sinews and muscles rely on the cooling, moistening nourishment of yin and blood to stay supple. When these reserves run low-often after a prolonged illness like a stroke-the sinews become dry and tight, leading to spastic paralysis, stiffness, and muscle wasting. This is a chronic, wasting presentation where the limbs feel rigid and contracted, accompanied by signs of internal dryness and heat.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale to pale-red, thin, dry body with cracks and scanty coating. Pulse: fine (Xi) and rapid (Shu), weak at the left guan (Liver) and both chi (Kidney) positions.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Yin and blood are the body's cooling, moistening reserves. Rest and nourishing foods directly replenish them, while gentle movement keeps the sinews supple without further depleting these resources. Overwork, stress, and heating or drying habits consume yin and blood, worsening the dryness and spasticity.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Focus on moist, nourishing foods like bone broth, congee, black sesame, goji berries, and dark leafy greens. Avoid drying foods such as spicy dishes, excessive coffee, and alcohol. Gentle, fluid movements like Tai Chi or restorative yoga can help maintain flexibility without depleting energy. Prioritize rest and early nights to conserve yin.

### Kidney and Liver Yin Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/kidney-and-liver-yin-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: One-sided limb weakness or spasticity, Dizziness and ringing in the ears, Dry eyes with blurred vision, Soreness and weakness of the lower back and knees, Night sweats with heat in the palms, soles, and chest
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Overwork and staying up late, Spicy, fried, or drying foods, Emotional stress and anger, Hot weather or overly warm rooms
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Adequate rest and sleep, Cool, quiet environment, Eating black sesame, walnuts, and goji berries, Gentle stretching or Tai Chi
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  The Liver and Kidneys are the root of the tendons and bones. When their Yin becomes depleted, the sinews lose their nourishment, leading to weakness, stiffness, and poor motor control. At the same time, Yin deficiency allows Liver Yang to flare upward, generating internal Wind that can suddenly obstruct the channels and cause one-sided paralysis. After the acute phase, the ongoing Yin deficiency impairs recovery, leaving the affected limbs weak, spastic, or poorly coordinated.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red, thin body with cracks, little or no coating, dry surface. Pulse: fine, rapid, and wiry; left Chi (Kidney) position weak or barely palpable, left Guan (Liver) position thin and slightly wiry.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Yin is the body's cooling, moistening, and nourishing energy. Overwork, late nights, and emotional stress consume Yin and stir Liver Yang, worsening spasticity and weakness. Spicy and drying foods generate internal heat, which further depletes Yin fluids. Rest, a cool environment, and Yin-nourishing foods replenish what is missing, while gentle movement keeps the channels open without exhausting the body's reserves.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Emphasize deeply nourishing, Yin-building foods such as black beans, sesame seeds, walnuts, goji berries, spinach, and yam. Avoid spicy, fried, or drying foods that consume Yin fluids. Prioritize adequate rest and a regular sleep schedule; overwork and late nights directly deplete Kidney Yin. Gentle, flowing exercises like Tai Chi or qigong help circulate Qi without straining the depleted Yin.

### Wind-Phlegm
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/wind-phlegm)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Sudden onset of one-sided weakness, Heavy, numb sensation in the limbs, Slurred or difficult speech, Copious white, frothy sputum, Dizziness or vertigo
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Greasy or fried foods, Damp, cold weather, Stress and emotional upset
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Light, warm meals, Gentle movement, Warm, dry environment
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  After a stroke, the body's fluids can congeal into a sticky obstruction called Phlegm, while internal Wind stirs upward. When Wind and Phlegm combine, they block the channels that control movement and speech, leading to sudden one-sided paralysis. This is why the limbs feel heavy and weak, speech can become slurred, and there may be a sensation of phlegm rattling in the throat.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: normal or slightly pale body, swollen with teeth marks, white greasy coating. Pulse: wiry and slippery (弦滑, xián huá).
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Greasy and damp-producing foods generate more Phlegm, which directly worsens the blockage. A warm, dry environment helps the body transform dampness, while gentle movement encourages Qi to push through the obstructed channels. Stress can stir Liver Wind, aggravating the dizziness and paralysis.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Avoid greasy, fried, and dairy foods that create dampness and phlegm. Choose warm, cooked meals with plenty of vegetables and light grains. Once the acute stage passes, gentle daily movement like walking or tai chi helps Qi flow and prevents phlegm from settling.

### Heart and Spleen Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/heart-and-spleen-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Flaccid weakness of the affected side, Palpitations or awareness of heartbeat, Difficulty falling asleep or staying asleep, Poor appetite with bloating after eating, Fatigue and lack of energy, Sallow or pale complexion, Loose or poorly formed stools
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Overexertion and fatigue, Worry and overthinking, Cold, raw foods, Irregular meal times, Lack of sleep
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Adequate sleep and rest, Warm, easily digestible meals, Gentle, consistent movement, Emotional support and calm
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  The Spleen produces the Qi and Blood that nourish every muscle and sinew. After a stroke, if the Spleen remains weak, it cannot generate enough of these vital resources. The Heart, which governs blood and houses the mind, also becomes undernourished, so circulation to the limbs is poor and mental drive for rehabilitation is low. This dual deficiency leaves the affected side persistently weak, flaccid, and slow to recover.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: Pale, tender body with teeth marks, thin white coating. Pulse: Fine, weak, may be choppy; left cun (Heart) and right guan (Spleen) positions are weak.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: The Spleen needs warm, regular meals to generate Qi and Blood; cold and raw foods damage Spleen Yang and slow recovery. Overthinking and worry consume Heart Blood and stagnate Spleen Qi, deepening the deficiency. Adequate rest and sleep allow the Heart and Spleen to rebuild, while gentle movement encourages Qi flow without further draining the body’s reserves.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat warm, easily digestible foods like congee, well-cooked grains, and root vegetables to support the Spleen. Add blood-nourishing ingredients such as red dates, longan fruit, and small amounts of lean meat. Avoid cold, raw foods and irregular meal times. Gentle daily movement like tai chi or short walks encourages Qi flow without depleting reserves. Prioritize rest and a calm mind, as overthinking and poor sleep directly weaken the Heart and Spleen.

## คำถามที่พบบ่อย (Frequently Asked Questions)
### Can acupuncture really help with post-stroke paralysis?
Yes, many clinical studies and centuries of practice show that acupuncture can improve motor function, reduce spasticity, and enhance overall quality of life. It works by stimulating nerve pathways, increasing local blood flow, and rebalancing the body's energy to unblock the affected channels. Results vary from person to person, but it is most effective when started early and combined with a comprehensive rehabilitation program.

### How soon after a stroke should I start TCM treatment?
As soon as the person is medically stable-often within days to weeks after the stroke. Early intervention with acupuncture and tailored herbal formulas can support the body's natural repair processes and may reduce the severity of long-term disability. Always coordinate with your neurologist and medical team to ensure it is safe to begin.

### Will I need to take herbs forever?
No. Herbal formulas are typically prescribed in courses of several weeks to months, then adjusted or discontinued as your condition improves. The goal is to correct the underlying imbalance so your body can maintain function on its own. Some people with chronic deficiency patterns may use a maintenance formula long-term, but this is not always necessary and is decided on a case-by-case basis.

### Can I combine TCM with my regular physical therapy?
Absolutely. TCM complements physical therapy beautifully. Acupuncture can help relax tight muscles before a therapy session, making stretching and exercises more effective. Herbs can boost energy levels and reduce fatigue, helping you engage more fully in your rehab. Just be sure to inform both your therapist and your TCM practitioner about all treatments you are receiving.

### Are there any foods I should avoid?
In general, it is best to avoid cold, raw, and greasy foods, which can damage the Spleen's digestive function and create phlegm-a sticky obstruction that TCM considers a major factor in paralysis. Also limit spicy and heating foods if you have signs of Yin deficiency (such as dry mouth or night sweats). Focus on warm, cooked meals like soups and stews that are easy to digest and deeply nourishing.

### Is TCM safe if I'm on blood thinners?
Many herbs used for hemiplegia, such as Dang Gui (Angelica sinensis) or Chuan Xiong (Ligusticum wallichii), can have mild blood-moving effects. It is crucial to tell your TCM practitioner about all medications you take, especially warfarin, aspirin, or clopidogrel. Your practitioner can adjust the formula to avoid interactions, and your doctor may need to monitor your blood clotting times more closely. Never stop or change your medication without medical advice.
