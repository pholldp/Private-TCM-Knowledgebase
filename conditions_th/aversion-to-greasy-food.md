## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนปัจจุบัน (Conventional Medicine View)
### คำอธิบาย (Description)
In conventional medicine, aversion to greasy food is often a clue pointing to digestive trouble. It can signal gallbladder problems like gallstones or inflammation, liver conditions such as fatty liver or hepatitis, or pancreatic issues. Doctors may also consider functional dyspepsia, pregnancy, or a side effect of medications. Diagnosis typically involves blood tests, ultrasound, or endoscopy to rule out structural causes. When no clear physical cause is found, the symptom may be attributed to a sensitive digestive system or psychological factors.

### การรักษาหลัก (Treatments)
Treatment depends on the underlying cause. If gallstones are present, surgery may be recommended. For fatty liver, lifestyle changes like weight loss and diet modification are primary. Medications to manage nausea, acid reflux, or pain may be prescribed. If no specific disease is found, advice often centers on eating smaller, low-fat meals and avoiding trigger foods.

## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนจีน (Traditional Chinese Medicine View)
### ความเข้าใจเกี่ยวกับการแพทย์แผนจีน (TCM Understanding)
TCM sees digestion as a team effort, with the Spleen as the chief organ of transformation and transportation. When Spleen Qi is weak, it can't properly process rich, oily foods, leading to internal dampness - a heavy, sticky byproduct that clogs the digestive system. The body's aversion is a natural warning that the digestive fire isn't hot enough to "cook" heavy meals, and forcing them down only creates more dampness and discomfort.
The Liver and Gallbladder are equally crucial. The Liver ensures the smooth flow of Qi, and its partner Gallbladder stores and excretes bile, which in TCM is seen as a form of essence that breaks down fats. When damp-heat lodges in these organs - often from a rich diet, alcohol, or emotional stress - the flow becomes sluggish. Greasy food then feels repulsive, often accompanied by a bitter taste, rib-side fullness, and irritability.
Because multiple organ systems can be involved, TCM distinguishes several distinct patterns. Spleen Deficiency with Dampness leads to bloating and a heavy, drained feeling after eating, while Liver and Gallbladder Damp-Heat causes a strong bitter taste and emotional tension. Phlegm-Dampness creates a foggy sensation and a thick tongue coating. Each pattern requires a different treatment strategy, which is why a one-size-fits-all approach often fails.

### การวินิจฉัยโดยแพทย์แผนจีน (Practitioner Diagnosis)
When a practitioner suspects Liver and Gallbladder Damp‑Heat, they look for a bitter taste in the mouth and a feeling of fullness or pain under the ribs. This pattern often makes greasy food actively repulsive. The tongue typically shows a thick, yellow, greasy coating, and the pulse feels wiry and rapid. Questions about stress, alcohol intake, or rich meals help confirm this picture, because symptoms often flare after overindulgence or emotional upset.
If the aversion to greasy food comes with bloating, loose stools, and a heavy, tired feeling, the practitioner thinks of Spleen Deficiency with Dampness. Here the spleen is too weak to handle fats, so even a small amount of oil feels burdensome. The tongue is pale and puffy with a white greasy coat, and the pulse is weak or thready. The person often has a generally poor appetite and may crave sweets instead.
When damp‑heat directly attacks the spleen, the dislike of oily food is strong and often accompanied by nausea or a sticky sensation in the mouth. The tongue coat is yellow and greasy, and the pulse feels slippery and rapid. This pattern can arise after eating contaminated or overly rich food, especially in humid weather. The practitioner asks about recent travel, food history, and any feeling of heaviness in the limbs.
In Phlegm‑Dampness obstructing the middle burner, the aversion to grease is more persistent and less acute. The person may describe a constant feeling of chest fullness or a lump in the throat. The tongue has a thick, greasy coating that can be white or yellow, and the pulse is slippery. This pattern often develops slowly from long‑term dietary habits and a sedentary lifestyle, and the practitioner will ask about mucus, phlegm, and digestive sluggishness.
When emotional stress is the trigger, the practitioner looks for Liver Qi Stagnation turning into Heat. Greasy food becomes unappealing, and there is often irritability, a bitter taste, and a tendency to sigh or feel pent‑up. The tongue is red with a thin yellow coating, and the pulse is wiry and rapid. Questions about mood, sleep, and menstrual cycles (in women) help distinguish this from simple damp‑heat, because the emotional component is central.
Phlegm‑Heat is a rare but distinct pattern where dampness and heat combine into a sticky, hot phlegm that creates a strong distaste for oily foods. The person may also feel thirsty, have a sticky yellow tongue coating, and a rapid, slippery pulse. The practitioner will ask about coughing of yellow phlegm, restlessness, and whether the aversion to grease is accompanied by a sense of internal heat. This pattern needs careful differentiation from pure damp‑heat.

## กลุ่มอาการการแพทย์แผนจีนและการรักษา (TCM Patterns and Treatment)

### Liver and Gallbladder Damp-Heat
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/liver-and-gallbladder-damp-heat)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Bitter taste in the mouth, Distension or pain under the ribs, especially on the right side, Dark yellow, scanty urine, Thick, yellow, greasy tongue coating, Irritability and restlessness
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Greasy, fried, or rich foods, Alcohol, Spicy, heating foods, Emotional stress, anger, or frustration, Hot, humid weather
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Light, bland meals, Bitter greens (dandelion, arugula), Cooling herbal teas, Stress reduction and rest, Gentle exercise or movement
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When dampness and heat get stuck in the Liver and Gallbladder, the Liver's ability to smooth the flow of Qi and bile is disrupted. Bile helps break down fats, so this stagnation makes digesting greasy food feel heavy and impossible. The body develops a strong aversion because rich, oily foods simply add to the internal dampness and heat that are already overwhelming the system.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red body with redder sides, thick yellow greasy coating. Pulse: wiry (弦 xián), slippery (滑 huá), and rapid (数 shuò).
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Greasy and spicy foods generate more dampness and heat, directly worsening the underlying Liver and Gallbladder stagnation. Alcohol and emotional stress both stir up Liver Fire, making the bitter taste and irritability spike. In contrast, bitter and cooling foods help drain damp-heat, while gentle movement and stress relief keep Liver Qi flowing smoothly so the body can process fats without revolt.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Focus on light, bland meals with plenty of bitter greens like dandelion, arugula, and celery. Avoid alcohol, coffee, and spicy or deep-fried foods. Gentle daily movement like walking or yoga helps Qi flow and prevents dampness from settling. Manage stress with breathing exercises or quiet time, since frustration directly fuels Liver stagnation.

### Spleen Deficiency with Dampness
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/spleen-deficiency-with-dampness)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Pale, swollen tongue with a white greasy coating, Bloating and fullness, especially after eating, Loose or unformed stools, Feeling of heaviness in the body and limbs, Fatigue and lack of energy, No bitter taste in the mouth
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Greasy, fried, or rich foods, Cold or raw foods, Overeating or large meals, Dairy and sweets, Damp or humid weather
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warm, cooked meals, Ginger tea, Small, frequent portions, Gentle exercise or movement, Stress reduction and rest
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Your Spleen is responsible for transforming food into energy and managing moisture in the body. When the Spleen is weak, it cannot properly process rich, heavy foods, so they stagnate and create internal Dampness. This Dampness makes you feel heavy and bloated, and your body naturally rejects greasy foods to avoid adding more of a burden it cannot handle.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale, swollen body with teeth marks and a white greasy coating that looks moist. Pulse: soggy (Ru) and slow (Huan), soft and lacking force, especially at the right wrist position corresponding to the Spleen.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: A weak Spleen cannot transform heavy, greasy foods, so they immediately worsen bloating and aversion. Cold and raw foods further damage the Spleen's warming function, allowing Dampness to accumulate. Warm, cooked foods and ginger gently support the Spleen's digestive fire, while small meals and light movement help Qi circulate to clear Dampness. Rest conserves the Spleen's limited energy, preventing the exhaustion that makes the heaviness feel worse.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat warm, cooked, and easily digestible foods like soups, congees, and steamed vegetables. Avoid cold, raw, and greasy foods as well as dairy and sugar, which create more Dampness. Eat smaller, frequent meals and take a short walk after eating to help Qi move. Ginger tea can warm the digestion and reduce heaviness.

### Damp-Heat invading the Spleen
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/damp-heat-invading-the-spleen)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Heavy, dragging sensation in the limbs, Nausea and queasiness around greasy food, Sticky or greasy taste in the mouth, Upper abdominal bloating and fullness, Thirst with little desire to drink
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Greasy, fried, or rich foods, Dairy and sweets, Damp or humid weather, Overeating or large meals, Alcohol
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Light, bland meals, Barley or coix seed tea, Gentle exercise or movement, Dry, well-ventilated environment
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  The Spleen is responsible for transforming food into energy and managing fluids. When damp-heat-from a humid climate, improper diet, or internal imbalance-invades the digestive system, it creates a sticky, heavy obstruction in the middle burner. This clogs the Spleen's ability to process rich, oily foods, causing a strong aversion that is often accompanied by nausea and a sensation of fullness. The body rejects greasy foods because they would only add more dampness and heat to an already overwhelmed system.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red body with a thick, yellow, greasy coating, especially in the center and root. Pulse: soggy (ru) and rapid (shu), or slippery (hua) and rapid.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Dampness and heat clog the spleen, making it sluggish. Greasy, heavy foods add more dampness and heat, overwhelming digestion and causing rejection. Light, bland foods and herbs that drain dampness reduce the burden, while gentle movement helps the spleen's qi flow. A dry environment prevents external dampness from adding to the internal accumulation.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Avoid greasy, fried, and rich foods; focus on light, easily digestible meals like congee, steamed vegetables, and small portions of lean protein. Drink warm barley tea or coix seed tea to drain dampness. Gentle daily movement like walking or qigong helps the spleen move qi and transform dampness. Keep your living space dry and well-ventilated to avoid external dampness.

### Phlegm-Dampness in the Middle-Burner
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/phlegm-dampness-in-the-middle-burner)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Thick, white, greasy tongue coating, Feeling of fullness and stuffiness in the chest and upper abdomen, Heavy sensation in the head and limbs, as if wrapped in a cloth, No thirst or desire to drink, Loose, sticky stools, Drowsiness and excessive sleepiness
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Greasy, fried, or rich foods, Dairy and cold drinks, Overeating or large meals, Damp or humid weather, Sedentary lifestyle
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warm, cooked meals, Ginger tea, Gentle exercise or movement, Dry, well-ventilated environment
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When the Spleen is too weak to transform fluids, dampness accumulates and, over time, congeals into sticky phlegm that obstructs the middle burner. Greasy food is heavy, damp, and hard to digest, so it further burdens this sluggish system. The body instinctively rejects it, creating a persistent aversion to anything rich or oily.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale, swollen body with teeth marks, covered by a thick, white, greasy coating that is excessively moist. Pulse: slippery (滑, huá) and often slow or moderate (缓, huǎn), with a soggy (濡, rú) quality, especially at the right middle position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Greasy, heavy, and cold foods are the very substances that create phlegm-dampness, so they immediately worsen the obstruction. Warm, light meals and ginger gently warm the Spleen and Stomach, helping them transform dampness rather than letting it accumulate. Movement is essential because it keeps qi and fluids circulating; sitting still allows dampness to settle and congeal. A dry environment reduces external dampness that can further burden the body.

Regular, gentle movement such as walking or stretching helps qi flow and prevents stagnation.

- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Emotional stress, anger, or frustration, Spicy or fried foods, Alcohol
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Stress reduction and rest, Cooling foods (cucumber, celery), Gentle exercise or movement
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Emotional stress, especially anger and frustration, causes the Liver's Qi to become stuck. Over time, this stuck Qi generates heat that rises upward like a flame. That internal heat makes rich, greasy foods feel repulsive and heavy, while also leaving a bitter taste in the mouth and a sour, burning sensation in the stomach.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red body with redder sides and a yellow coating. Pulse: wiry and rapid (xián shuò).
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Emotional stress is the root cause of this pattern-it knots the Liver Qi, which then generates heat. That heat makes rich foods intolerable and rises to create a bitter taste and irritability. Cooling foods and calming activities directly counteract this heat and help the Qi flow smoothly again, so the digestive system settles and greasy food no longer feels so repulsive.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat simple, lightly cooked meals with cooling vegetables like cucumber, celery, and bok choy. Avoid alcohol, coffee, and spicy or fried foods that add more heat. A short daily walk or gentle stretching helps move stuck Liver Qi, and a few minutes of deep breathing before meals can settle the stomach.

### Phlegm-Heat
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/phlegm-heat)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Nausea or vomiting after eating greasy food, Chest oppression or epigastric fullness, Thirst with desire for cool drinks, Sticky sensation in the mouth, Restlessness and irritability, Coughing up thick yellow phlegm
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Greasy, fried, or rich foods, Alcohol and spicy foods, Hot, humid weather
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Light, bland meals, Cooling herbal teas, Gentle exercise or movement
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When dampness and heat combine, they form a sticky, obstructive phlegm-heat that clogs the digestive system. This makes rich, greasy foods feel heavy and nauseating because the body is already overloaded with thick, stagnant fluids. The heat component also stirs up restlessness and thirst, making oily foods even less appealing.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red body, swollen, with a thick yellow greasy coating. Pulse: slippery (滑 huá) and rapid (数 shuò).
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Greasy and spicy foods add more heat and dampness, fueling the very phlegm-heat that makes you feel sick. Cooling, bland foods and light activity help drain dampness and clear heat, easing the digestive blockage and the strong dislike for oily meals.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Avoid greasy, fried, and dairy-rich foods that generate more phlegm and heat. Embrace light, cooling meals like steamed vegetables, congee, and radish. Sip warm water or chrysanthemum tea throughout the day. Gentle daily movement like walking or stretching helps the body clear phlegm and move stagnant Qi.

## คำถามที่พบบ่อย (Frequently Asked Questions)
### Why do I suddenly find greasy food repulsive?
In TCM, a sudden aversion often signals that your digestive system is overwhelmed by dampness or heat. It's your body's way of protecting itself from foods it can't properly process. The underlying cause could be a weak Spleen, a sluggish Liver and Gallbladder, or a buildup of phlegm-dampness. A TCM practitioner will look at your tongue, pulse, and other symptoms to pinpoint the exact pattern and restore balance.

### Can acupuncture and herbs really help me tolerate fatty foods again?
Yes. Acupuncture helps regulate the flow of Qi and clear damp-heat from the organs involved, while herbal formulas target the root imbalance - whether that's strengthening the Spleen, draining dampness, or soothing the Liver. As your digestion improves, your body's tolerance for fats typically returns. Many patients notice they can gradually reintroduce healthy fats without discomfort.

### Do I have to avoid all fats and oils during treatment?
Not all fats, but you'll likely need to favor easy-to-digest, cooked foods initially. Raw, cold, and deeply fried foods are hardest on a weak Spleen and should be minimized. Your practitioner will guide you on when to reintroduce richer foods as your digestion strengthens. The goal is to heal your system so you can eat a balanced diet, not to stay on a restrictive diet forever.

### Is this just a gallbladder problem?
In Western medicine, a sudden aversion to greasy food often points to the gallbladder. In TCM, the Gallbladder is indeed a key player, but it's never seen in isolation. The Liver and Spleen are almost always involved. Even if your gallbladder tests are normal, TCM can identify functional imbalances in the Liver and Gallbladder channel that cause the same symptoms. So while it could be a gallbladder issue, TCM looks at the whole digestive team.

### How long will it take to feel better?
Many people notice less nausea and bloating within the first 2-4 weeks of treatment. If your pattern is primarily excess (like Damp-Heat), relief may come faster. If there's a deeper deficiency (like Spleen Qi weakness), it can take 4-8 weeks to rebuild digestive strength, and a full return of tolerance for rich foods may take a few months. Consistency with herbs and dietary changes is key.

### Can I combine TCM with my regular medical treatment?
Generally yes, and it's often complementary. If you're on medications for gallbladder issues, liver conditions, or acid reflux, tell both your doctor and your TCM practitioner. Herbs can sometimes interact with medications, so your TCM practitioner will adjust formulas accordingly. Never stop prescribed medication without consulting your doctor.

### Are there any red flags I should watch for?
Yes. An aversion to greasy food can occasionally signal a serious condition. If you experience severe abdominal pain, jaundice (yellowing of skin or eyes), unexplained weight loss, or vomiting blood, seek urgent medical care. For a full list of warning signs, see our Safety section.
