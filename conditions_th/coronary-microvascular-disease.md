## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนปัจจุบัน (Conventional Medicine View)
### คำอธิบาย (Description)
Western medicine defines coronary microvascular disease as dysfunction of the heart's tiny resistance vessels, leading to reduced blood flow and typical angina-like chest pain. It is often diagnosed after an angiogram shows no significant blockages in the large coronary arteries. Symptoms include chest pressure or tightness, shortness of breath, and fatigue, especially during exertion or emotional stress. Diagnosis may involve stress testing, cardiac MRI, or invasive coronary reactivity testing.

### การรักษาหลัก (Treatments)
Standard treatment includes medications to relax blood vessels (nitrates, calcium channel blockers), reduce heart workload (beta-blockers), and improve microvascular function (ranolazine). Lifestyle changes such as smoking cessation, stress management, and exercise are also recommended. For some patients, low-dose tricyclic antidepressants or xanthine derivatives are used to modulate pain perception when typical anti-anginal drugs fail.

## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนจีน (Traditional Chinese Medicine View)
### ความเข้าใจเกี่ยวกับการแพทย์แผนจีน (TCM Understanding)
In TCM, chest pain and tightness are understood as a form of "chest obstruction" (胸痹, xiōng bì), a condition described in ancient texts as a blockage of Qi and blood in the chest. The Heart is the emperor organ, responsible for pumping blood and housing the Shen (spirit). When the vessels that nourish the Heart become obstructed, pain follows. But the obstruction can arise from many different sources - emotional stress causing Qi to stagnate, a diet rich in greasy foods generating phlegm, cold invading the chest and congealing blood, or a fundamental weakness of the Heart's Yang or Yin.
The Liver plays a key role because it ensures the smooth flow of Qi. When stress, frustration, or worry disrupt the Liver, Qi backs up into the chest, creating the distending, wandering pain of Qi stagnation. If this stagnation persists, it can progress to blood stasis, where the pain becomes fixed and stabbing - much like the microvascular dysfunction seen in cardiac syndrome X. The Spleen is responsible for transforming food into clear fluids; when it is weakened, it produces phlegm instead, which can rise to clog the chest, causing a heavy, suffocating sensation.
Deficiency patterns are also important. The Kidney provides the body's fundamental Yang (warming fire) and Yin (cooling fluid). When Kidney Yang is low, the Heart lacks the warmth to push blood through the vessels, and cold constricts the microcirculation, leading to pain that worsens in cold weather. When Kidney Yin is depleted, the Heart vessels lose their moistening and become fragile, causing dull, lingering discomfort with night sweats and palpitations. This is why one Western diagnosis - microvascular angina - can require five different TCM treatment strategies.

### การวินิจฉัยโดยแพทย์แผนจีน (Practitioner Diagnosis)
When someone comes in with chest discomfort, a TCM practitioner first asks about the quality and triggers of the pain. Is it a dull ache or a sharp stab? Does it wander or stay fixed? Does cold weather or a rich meal make it worse? These clues begin to separate the patterns of chest obstruction (胸痹, xiōng bì).
If the main feeling is distension and a wandering pain that comes and goes, especially after emotional upset, and the person sighs often, the pattern is likely Qi Stagnation in the Chest. The tongue may look normal or have a thin coating, and the pulse feels wiry, like a guitar string.
When the pain becomes fixed and stabbing, like a knife in one spot, and the lips or tongue appear dark purple with possible spots, the Qi stagnation has progressed to Heart Blood Stagnation. The pulse is often choppy or wiry, reflecting obstructed blood flow.
A heavy, suffocating sensation as if a weight is on the chest, along with a feeling of phlegm in the throat, a thick greasy tongue coating, and a slippery pulse points to Phlegm in the Chest. This pattern often follows a diet rich in greasy or sweet foods, or a weak digestive system.
If the chest pain gets much worse with cold exposure and the hands and feet feel icy, Heart Yang Deficiency is likely. The tongue is pale and swollen, and the pulse is deep and slow. This pattern reflects a lack of the warming, propelling force of Yang.
Finally, in long-standing or late-stage cases, a dull, lingering chest pain accompanied by night sweats, a dry mouth at night, a red tongue with little coating, and a thin, rapid pulse suggests Heart and Kidney Yin Deficiency. The body's cooling, nourishing fluids are depleted, leaving the Heart vessel malnourished.

### Qi Stagnation in Chest
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/qi-stagnation-in-chest)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Chest distension or bloating sensation, Worsens with emotional upset, Frequent sighing, Belching that temporarily relieves discomfort, Irritability or depressed mood, Sensation of a lump in the throat
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Emotional stress, Overeating rich, heavy foods, Prolonged inactivity or sedentary lifestyle, Suppressing emotions, Irregular eating habits
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Gentle movement or exercise, Deep breathing exercises, Warmth on the chest, Light, warm, easily digestible meals, Stress reduction practices
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  The Liver is responsible for the smooth flow of Qi throughout the body. When emotional stress, frustration, or worry cause the Liver Qi to stagnate, this stuck energy can rise and accumulate in the chest. The result is a sensation of fullness, distension, and oppression that comes and goes, because the root problem is the movement of Qi, not a fixed physical blockage.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: normal body colour with a thin white coating, may be slightly dusky on the sides. Pulse: wiry (弦 xián), especially at the left middle position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Emotional stress directly causes Liver Qi to stagnate, which then obstructs the flow of Qi in the chest. Movement and deep breathing help to physically mobilise Qi, while warmth relaxes the constriction. Light meals avoid overburdening the digestive system, which can create further stagnation. Managing emotions and expressing them healthily prevents the buildup of stagnant Qi.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Prioritise gentle daily movement like walking or yoga to keep Qi flowing. Practice deep breathing to help move chest Qi. Avoid heavy, greasy meals that can further stagnate Qi; instead eat light, warm, cooked foods. Manage stress through mindfulness or talking to someone you trust.

### Heart Blood Stagnation
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/heart-blood-stagnation)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Stabbing chest pain in a fixed location, Purple or dark lips and nails, Palpitations with anxiety, Cold hands, Pain that radiates to the shoulder or inner left arm, Insomnia or disturbed sleep
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Cold exposure or weather, Emotional stress, Greasy, heavy, or fried foods, Prolonged inactivity or sedentary lifestyle
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warmth, Gentle movement or exercise, Rest and calm, Blood-moving foods
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When emotional stress or other factors cause Qi to stagnate for a long period, the blood flow in the heart’s tiny vessels gradually slows down and congeals, forming stasis. This stasis blocks the microvessels that nourish the heart muscle, producing a sharp, fixed pain that feels like a knife prick. The blood stasis also prevents fresh blood from reaching the extremities, leading to purple lips and nails and cold hands.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: dark purple or dark-red body with stasis spots, distended sublingual veins, and a thin white coat. Pulse: choppy (涩 sè) or knotted (结 jié), reflecting blood stasis obstructing the heart.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Warmth and gentle movement help invigorate blood and prevent stasis from congealing. Cold constricts vessels and worsens obstruction, while stress and emotional upset further stagnate Qi and blood. Rest and calming the mind reduce the heart’s burden, allowing blood to flow more smoothly.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat warming, blood-moving foods like hawthorn berry, turmeric, ginger, and black fungus; avoid cold and raw foods that can congeal blood. Gentle daily movement like walking or tai chi promotes circulation, while managing stress with meditation or deep breathing helps prevent Qi stagnation from worsening the stasis.

### Phlegm in Chest
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/phlegm-in-chest)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Heavy, stuffy sensation in the chest, Coughing with abundant white sticky phlegm, Thick, greasy tongue coating, Feeling of heaviness in the body, Poor appetite and nausea, Sensation of something blocking the throat
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Greasy, heavy, or fried foods, Dairy products, Cold or raw foods and drinks, Damp weather, Prolonged inactivity or sedentary lifestyle
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Light, warm, easily digestible meals, Moderate exercise, Deep breathing exercises, Ginger or cardamom tea, Small, frequent meals
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When the Spleen is weakened by a rich, heavy diet or damp surroundings, it fails to transform fluids properly. Those fluids turn into a sticky, turbid substance called Phlegm, which rises to the chest and clogs the vessels and airways. This obstruction creates the heavy, suffocating chest tightness and shortness of breath that mark this type of chest discomfort.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale, swollen body with teeth marks; thick white greasy coating; moist surface. Pulse: slippery (Hua), often with a wiry (Xian) quality.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Phlegm forms when the Spleen fails to transform fluids, often due to a diet heavy in greasy, cold, or dairy foods. These foods overwhelm the digestive system, creating dampness that congeals into phlegm and obstructs the chest. Warm, light meals and aromatic herbs strengthen the Spleen and dry dampness. Exercise and deep breathing move Qi, which helps the body clear phlegm and open the chest.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Avoid heavy, greasy, and dairy foods that create dampness. Eat warm, lightly cooked meals with aromatic herbs like ginger and cardamom. Gentle daily movement like walking or tai chi helps move Qi and clear phlegm.

### Heart Yang Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/heart-yang-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Dull, tight chest pain or stuffiness, Worse with cold exposure, better with warmth, Cold hands and feet, disliking cold, Bright pale or white face and lips, Fatigue, mental listlessness, and spontaneous sweating
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Cold exposure or weather, Cold or raw foods and drinks, Overexertion or overwork, Emotional stress
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warmth on the chest, Gentle movement or exercise, Warm drinks and soups, Rest and avoiding overwork, Moxibustion on back points
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Heart Yang is the warming fire that propels blood through the vessels. When it becomes weak, cold energy dominates, causing the vessels to constrict and blood flow to slow down.
This leads to a dull, constricting chest pain that feels worse with cold exposure, as if the chest is being squeezed. The person feels chilled, especially in the hands and feet, because the Heart's Yang can no longer reach the limbs.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale, puffy, and tender with teeth marks, white slippery coating. Pulse: deep and weak, especially at the left Cun (inch) position; may be knotted (slow with irregular pauses).
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Heart Yang is the body's internal fire. Cold weather and raw foods directly drain this fire, making the vessels constrict and pain worse. Overexertion and stress further deplete Yang, leaving the Heart too weak to pump blood smoothly. Warmth, rest, warm foods, and moxibustion all gently feed and protect the Heart's Yang, helping blood flow freely and easing the chest.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat warm, cooked foods like soups, stews, and congees. Avoid raw, cold, and iced items that chill the body. Keep the chest and back warm, especially in cold weather. Gentle daily movement such as walking supports Yang without depleting it; avoid overexertion. Moxibustion at home on the lower back or abdomen can help sustain warmth.

### Heart and Kidney Yin Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/heart-and-kidney-yin-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Dull, lingering chest discomfort rather than sharp pain, Night sweats, often drenching, Palpitations with mental restlessness and anxiety, Low back soreness and tinnitus, Warm palms, soles, and chest (five-centre heat)
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Overexertion or overwork, Emotional stress, Spicy, fried, or heating foods, Alcohol and coffee, Hot, dry weather
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Rest and adequate sleep, Cool, quiet environment, Moistening foods like pear and tofu, Gentle movement or exercise
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  In chronic chest obstruction, the body's Yin fluids become depleted. The Heart relies on Yin to nourish and cool its vessels; when Kidney Yin - the root of all Yin - is insufficient, the Heart vessels lose their moistening and become fragile. This deficiency causes dull, lingering chest discomfort, while the empty heat that arises from Yin deficiency rises to disturb the Heart spirit, producing palpitations, insomnia, and night sweats.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red, especially at the tip, with a thin body, little or no coating, and a dry surface. Pulse: thin (fine) and rapid.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Yin is the body's cooling, moistening resource. Overwork and emotional stress consume Yin and stir up empty heat, making chest discomfort and night sweats worse. Rest, a cool environment, and moistening foods help preserve and replenish Yin, calming the empty heat. Gentle movement keeps Qi flowing without further depleting fluids, which is essential when the body's reserves are low.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Prioritize rest and avoid overwork; eat cooling, moistening foods like pears, cucumber, and black sesame. Avoid spicy, fried, and stimulating foods that consume Yin. Gentle movement such as tai chi or walking helps move Qi without depleting fluids.

## คำถามที่พบบ่อย (Frequently Asked Questions)
### Can TCM help if my angiogram is normal?
Yes. In TCM, a normal angiogram doesn't rule out obstruction at the level of Qi and blood in the microvessels. Many patients with cardiac syndrome X find that acupuncture and herbal formulas reduce pain by addressing the underlying pattern - whether it's Qi stagnation, blood stasis, or phlegm - that conventional tests don't measure.

### How does acupuncture work for chest pain?
Acupuncture points like Neiguan PC-6 and Shanzhong REN-17 are thought to regulate the nervous system, improve microcirculation, and release natural pain-relieving substances. In TCM terms, they open the chest and move Qi and blood. Many patients feel a sense of release or warmth in the chest during treatment.

### Can I take Chinese herbs with my heart medications?
Generally yes, but some herbs (like Dan Shen and Chuan Xiong) have mild blood-thinning effects. If you are on aspirin, clopidogrel, warfarin, or any anticoagulant, you must inform both your cardiologist and TCM practitioner so they can coordinate care. Never stop prescribed medications without your doctor's guidance.

### How long until I see results?
Most patients notice improvement within 4-6 weeks. If your pain is driven by stress or phlegm, you may feel relief sooner. If the root is a deeper deficiency like Yang or Yin weakness, it can take 3-6 months to rebuild the body's reserves and see stable, lasting change.

### Is it safe to exercise with microvascular angina?
Gentle exercise like walking, tai chi, or qigong is often encouraged because it promotes Qi and blood circulation. However, you should avoid pushing into pain and always follow your cardiologist's advice. If exercise triggers chest pain, let your TCM practitioner know - it's a diagnostic clue.

### Do I need to change my diet?
Diet plays a role, especially if your pattern involves phlegm. In general, avoid heavy, greasy, and cold foods that bog down digestion and create stagnation. Warm, cooked meals with ginger, garlic, and onions support circulation. Your practitioner will give more specific guidance based on your pattern.

### Can stress really cause chest pain?
In TCM, absolutely. The Liver is easily affected by emotional stress, and when Liver Qi stagnates, it can directly cause chest tightness and pain. This is one of the most common patterns behind microvascular angina, and it often improves significantly with herbs that move Qi and acupuncture that calms the mind.
