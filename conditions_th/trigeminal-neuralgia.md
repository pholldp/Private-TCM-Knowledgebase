## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนปัจจุบัน (Conventional Medicine View)
### คำอธิบาย (Description)
Trigeminal neuralgia is a chronic pain condition affecting the trigeminal nerve, which carries sensation from the face to the brain. It causes sudden, severe, electric shock-like or stabbing pain, typically on one side of the face, lasting from a few seconds to a couple of minutes. Attacks can be triggered by everyday activities like brushing teeth, chewing, talking, or even a light breeze.
Diagnosis is based on the characteristic description of the pain and a neurological exam. An MRI is often done to rule out other causes, such as a blood vessel compressing the nerve, multiple sclerosis, or a tumor. In many cases, the exact cause remains unclear, though vascular compression is the most common finding.

### การรักษาหลัก (Treatments)
First-line treatment is usually anticonvulsant medications like carbamazepine or gabapentin, which work by stabilizing nerve firing. When medications become less effective or cause intolerable side effects, surgical options may be considered - most commonly microvascular decompression to relieve pressure on the nerve, or procedures that intentionally damage the nerve to block pain signals.

## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนจีน (Traditional Chinese Medicine View)
### ความเข้าใจเกี่ยวกับการแพทย์แผนจีน (TCM Understanding)
In Traditional Chinese Medicine, trigeminal neuralgia is not a single disease but a painful signal that something is blocking or starving the channels that run across the face. The face is richly supplied by the Stomach, Large Intestine, Gallbladder, and Bladder channels, and when their smooth flow of Qi and Blood is disrupted - by heat, phlegm, stagnation, or deficiency - sudden, severe pain erupts. This is why TCM looks beyond the nerve itself to find the root cause: an internal imbalance that makes those facial channels vulnerable.
The Liver plays a central role for many people. Emotional stress, frustration, or long-held anger can cause Liver Qi to stagnate, and over time that stagnation can generate intense Fire. This Liver Fire blazes upward along the Liver and Gallbladder channels, striking the face with electric, distending pain that often flares with emotional upset. A red face, bitter taste, and a wiry, rapid pulse are telltale signs.
Diet and digestion are equally important. Overeating spicy, greasy, or fried foods, or drinking too much alcohol, can create Stomach Fire. This heat surges up the Stomach channel - which runs directly over the cheek, jaw, and forehead - causing burning, stabbing pain that worsens after meals. The tongue becomes red with a thick yellow coating, and bad breath or acid reflux often accompany the facial attacks.
When the pain is fixed in one spot and feels like a knife, the problem is often Phlegm or Blood Stagnation clogging the tiny collateral channels. Phlegm brings a heavy, numb ache with a greasy tongue coating; Blood Stagnation causes a dark purple tongue and pain that never moves. In long-standing or exhausting conditions, Kidney Yin Deficiency can leave the face without enough cooling moisture, allowing empty heat to drift upward and produce a milder but persistent burning that worsens at night. Each pattern demands a different strategy, which is why TCM treats the same Western diagnosis in such varied ways.

### การวินิจฉัยโดยแพทย์แผนจีน (Practitioner Diagnosis)
A practitioner begins by asking what the pain feels like and what makes it flare. If the attacks are sudden, severe, and distending, and they come on with anger or stress, that points toward Liver Fire Blazing. The tongue is often red with a yellow coating, and the pulse feels wiry and rapid - signs of heat surging upward along the channels.
When the pain is burning and stabbing, and it worsens after spicy food or alcohol, Stomach Fire is the likely culprit. Here the tongue is red with a thicker yellow coating, and the pulse is rapid. The practitioner will also ask about digestive symptoms like acid reflux or bad breath, which often accompany this pattern.
A heavy, numb, or aching facial pain that comes with a feeling of fog or fullness suggests Phlegm obstructing the channels. The tongue coating is greasy and the pulse feels slippery. This pattern often develops in people who have long-standing digestive weakness or a diet that creates Dampness, making the pain less sharp but more persistent.
If the pain is fixed in one spot and feels like a knife stabbing, Blood Stagnation is the key. The tongue may show purple spots or a dark color, and the pulse is choppy. A history of injury or chronic pain supports this picture.
Finally, a milder, lingering burning pain that worsens with overwork or at night, with a red tongue that has little coating and a thin rapid pulse, indicates Empty-Heat from Yin Deficiency - a deeper, more chronic pattern often seen in older adults.

### Liver Fire Blazing
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/liver-fire-blazing)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Sudden, severe, distending facial pain, Triggered or worsened by emotional stress, Flushed red face and eyes, Bitter taste in mouth, dry throat, Intense irritability, short temper
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Anger and frustration, Spicy, greasy, or fried foods, Alcohol, Hot weather, Wind exposure on the face
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Cooling foods and drinks, Stress relief (meditation), Rest in a quiet, dark room, Cool environment
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Emotional stress and frustration cause Liver Qi to stagnate. Over time, this stagnant Qi generates intense Heat and Fire, which surges upward along the Liver and Gallbladder channels to the face. This produces sudden, severe, distending pain along the trigeminal nerve branches, often accompanied by a flushed red face, bitter taste, and explosive irritability.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red body with redder sides, dry yellow coating. Pulse: wiry, rapid, and forceful, especially in the left Guan position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Anger and stress cause Liver Qi to stagnate and transform into Fire, which surges upward to the face, triggering pain. Spicy foods and alcohol add external Heat, fueling the Fire. Cooling foods, stress reduction, and a calm environment help drain Fire and soothe the Liver, preventing the upward attack.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Avoid spicy, greasy, and fried foods, alcohol, and coffee, which add Heat. Practice stress management through meditation, deep breathing, or gentle yoga. Keep the face protected from wind and extreme temperatures, and prioritize regular sleep to keep Liver Qi smooth.

### Stomach Fire (Stomach Heat)
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/stomach-fire-stomach-heat)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Burning or stabbing facial pain, Worse after spicy, greasy, or hot meals, Foul breath and swollen, bleeding gums, Intense thirst for cold drinks
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Spicy, greasy, or fried foods, Alcohol, Emotional stress and frustration, Overeating
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Cooling foods and drinks, Rest and calm, Cool compresses on face
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Stomach Fire often arises from overindulging in spicy, greasy, or fried foods and alcohol. This internal heat surges upward along the Stomach channel, which runs across the face, scorching the local collaterals and triggering sudden, intense burning or stabbing pain along the cheek, jaw, or forehead. The heat also damages body fluids, leading to dry mouth, thirst, and constipation that accompany the facial attacks.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red body, possible red prickles in the centre, thick dry yellow coat. Pulse: rapid, slippery, and forceful, especially strong at the right Guan position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Spicy, greasy, and fried foods directly generate Stomach Fire, which then surges upward along the Stomach channel to the face, triggering pain. Alcohol adds more heat and aggravates the flame. Overeating burdens the Stomach and creates additional heat and stagnation. Cooling foods and drinks help douse that fire, while rest and calm prevent emotional stress from stirring Liver Qi, which can combine with Stomach Fire and worsen attacks.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Avoid spicy, fried, and greasy foods, as well as alcohol and coffee, which all stoke Stomach Fire. Focus on cooling, hydrating foods like cucumber, watermelon, pear, and mint tea. Eat smaller, lighter meals and avoid eating late at night to prevent heat from accumulating.

### Phlegm in the Channels joints and muscles
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/phlegm-in-the-channels-joints-and-muscles)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Heavy or numb sensation in the face, Fixed stabbing pain, Tongue purple with stasis spots and a greasy coating, Feeling of phlegm or mucus in the throat, Dull or darkish skin around the painful area
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Damp, cold weather, Greasy or dairy-heavy food, Prolonged inactivity, Emotional stress and frustration
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warm compress on the face, Gentle movement or walking, Warm, light meals, Dry, well-ventilated rooms
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When the body fails to transform fluids properly, Dampness accumulates and thickens into Phlegm. This sticky Phlegm then clogs the tiny collateral channels in the face, trapping Blood and creating a fixed, stabbing pain. The face can feel heavy, numb, or swollen because the Phlegm obstructs the normal flow of Qi and sensation.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: purple body with stasis spots, white greasy coating, distended sublingual veins. Pulse: wiry and choppy, or wiry and slippery if Phlegm predominates.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Dampness and cold congeal body fluids into Phlegm, while greasy foods directly add to the Dampness burden. Inactivity allows Qi to stagnate, letting Phlegm settle and block the channels. Stress knots the Qi, which then traps Phlegm and Blood. Warmth, gentle movement, and a light diet all help to move Qi, dry Dampness, and dissolve the Phlegm that is causing the pain.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Avoid damp-producing foods such as dairy, sugar, greasy or fried dishes, and cold raw foods. Favour warm, light meals like steamed vegetables, congee, and small amounts of pungent spices (ginger, pepper) to help dry Dampness. Gentle daily movement like walking or tai chi keeps Qi and blood flowing so Phlegm does not settle in the channels.

### Blood Stagnation
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/blood-stagnation)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Fixed, stabbing pain that does not shift location, Pain worsens at night, Pain is worse with pressure or touch, Dark or dusky facial complexion, Purplish lips or dark circles under the eyes
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Wind exposure on the face, Pressure or touching the trigger point, Night time or lying still, Emotional stress and frustration
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warm compress on the face, Gentle movement or walking, Warm, light meals
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When pain persists for a long time, or after an injury, the normal flow of Blood in the face becomes sluggish and eventually gets stuck. This creates a fixed obstruction in the tiny vessels (collaterals) that supply the facial nerves.
Because the blockage is in one place, the pain is sharp and stabbing, like a knife, and it never moves around. Blood flow naturally slows down when you are still or at night, which is why the pain can feel worse at those times.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: dark purple or dusky body with possible stasis spots; the veins under the tongue are dark, thick and twisted. Pulse: choppy (Se), feeling rough and uneven like a knife scraping bamboo.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Cold makes Blood congeal, which turns a sluggish flow into a complete blockage, triggering the knife-like pain. Pressure and stillness further obstruct the already stuck Blood. Warmth, gentle movement, and warming spices work by expanding the vessels and encouraging Blood to move, which temporarily relieves the stasis and eases the pain.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Keep your face warm and avoid cold drafts or icy foods, which can congeal Blood and make the stabbing pain worse. Gentle daily movement like walking or stretching helps keep Blood flowing smoothly. Include a little warming spice in your meals, such as turmeric, ginger, or a pinch of black pepper, to gently invigorate circulation.

### Empty-Heat caused by Yin Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/empty-heat-caused-by-yin-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Mild burning facial pain that worsens at night, Night sweats, Dry mouth and throat with desire to sip water, Feeling of heat in the palms, soles, and chest, Flushed cheekbones, Restlessness and difficulty staying asleep, Dry stools or constipation, Scanty dark urine
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Overwork and late nights, Spicy, greasy, or fried foods, Emotional stress and frustration, Hot weather
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Rest and early nights, Cooling, moistening foods, Gentle, calming activities, Cool environment
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When a long-standing illness or overwork drains the body’s deep reserves - especially Kidney Yin - the cooling, moistening force that normally keeps the body balanced becomes weak. Without enough Yin to anchor it, heat drifts upward as “empty fire.” This gentle but persistent heat travels along the channels to the face, irritating the nerves and producing a lingering, burning pain that often feels worse in the quiet of the evening or at night.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red, thin, dry body with cracks, little or no coating. Pulse: fine (thin) and rapid.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Yin is the body’s cooling, restorative energy, and it is easily depleted by overwork, poor sleep, and emotional strain. When Yin runs low, the relative emptiness allows heat to rise unchecked, which is why the pain worsens with fatigue and at night. Rest, cooling foods, and a calm environment help preserve and rebuild Yin, gently settling the empty fire so it no longer disturbs the facial channels.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Avoid spicy, fried, or overly heating foods and drinks like coffee and alcohol, which can fan the empty fire. Instead, enjoy cooling, moistening foods such as pear, tofu, black sesame, and congee. Prioritize rest, especially before midnight, and avoid overwork or late-night screen time, as these directly consume Yin reserves.

## คำถามที่พบบ่อย (Frequently Asked Questions)
### Can acupuncture help during an acute trigeminal neuralgia attack?
Yes, many patients find that acupuncture can shorten or reduce the intensity of an attack. Points on the face (like Xiaguan ST-7) and distal points (like Hegu LI-4 or Taichong LR-3) work to open the blocked channel and redirect the flow of Qi and Blood. Some practitioners also use gentle electrical stimulation on facial points for rapid pain relief.

### Can I take Chinese herbs along with my carbamazepine or gabapentin?
In most cases, yes, TCM herbs and conventional medications can be used together safely. However, never stop or reduce your prescribed medication without consulting your doctor. Herbs that move Blood (such as Chuan Xiong and Tao Ren) may interact with blood-thinning medications, so always give your TCM practitioner a full list of everything you take. With coordinated care, many patients are eventually able to gradually reduce their medication under medical supervision.

### How long does TCM treatment take to work for trigeminal neuralgia?
Many people begin to notice a change within 4 to 6 weeks. Acute, excess-type patterns often respond faster - sometimes after just a few sessions. Long-standing or deficiency-based patterns require patience, as the goal is to rebuild the body’s reserves and clear deep obstructions, which can take several months. Consistency with herbs and acupuncture is key.

### Is trigeminal neuralgia curable with TCM?
TCM aims to address the root imbalance that causes the pain, not just mask it. Many patients achieve long-term remission where attacks become rare or stop altogether. However, the outcome depends on the underlying pattern, how long you have had the condition, and your commitment to treatment and lifestyle changes. Even in chronic cases, significant improvement in quality of life is a realistic goal.

### Does acupuncture on the face hurt?
Facial acupuncture uses very fine needles and is generally gentle. The face is sensitive, so you may feel a tiny prick or a dull ache, but the needles are not painful once inserted. Many patients find the experience deeply relaxing, and the relief it brings far outweighs any momentary discomfort. Practitioners are especially careful to avoid triggering a pain attack during treatment.

### What if I’ve already had surgery for trigeminal neuralgia? Can TCM still help?
Yes. Even after surgery, some people experience residual pain or a return of symptoms. TCM can help manage this by addressing the internal pattern that made you susceptible in the first place - such as Liver Qi stagnation or Phlegm accumulation - and by promoting circulation in the facial channels to ease any remaining nerve irritation.

## การวิจัยทางวิทยาศาสตร์สมัยใหม่ (Modern Scientific Research)
1. **Acupuncture for trigeminal neuralgia** ([ลิงก์](https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD007312.pub2))
   *วารสารวิชาการ: Liu H, Li H, Xu M, et al. Acupuncture for trigeminal neuralgia. Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews. 2017, Issue 3. Art. No.: CD007312.*
   A Cochrane systematic review of six randomized controlled trials involving 630 participants. The review found that acupuncture may be effective for trigeminal neuralgia, but the evidence was limited by small sample sizes and risk of bias. Acupuncture combined with carbamazepine appeared more effective than carbamazepine alone.

2. **Treatment of trigeminal neuralgia by acupuncture combined with Chinese medicine from the perspective of modern medicine: A review** ([ลิงก์](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11537664))
   *วารสารวิชาการ: Zhang Y, Wang X, Chen L, et al. Treatment of trigeminal neuralgia by acupuncture combined with Chinese medicine from the perspective of modern medicine: A review. Front Med. 2024;11:1401233.*
   A 2024 narrative review summarizing the mechanisms and clinical evidence for integrated TCM treatment of trigeminal neuralgia. The review found that acupuncture and Chinese herbs can reduce pain by modulating neurotransmitters like substance P and beta-endorphin, and by improving microcirculation. Clinical studies showed improved pain scores and quality of life with combined therapy.
