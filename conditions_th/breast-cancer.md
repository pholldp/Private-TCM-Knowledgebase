## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนปัจจุบัน (Conventional Medicine View)
### คำอธิบาย (Description)
Breast cancer is a malignant tumor that forms in the cells of the breast, most commonly in the ducts or lobules. It is the most frequently diagnosed cancer in women worldwide. Typical symptoms include a new lump or mass, skin dimpling, nipple retraction or discharge, and changes in breast shape or size. Diagnosis is confirmed through imaging (mammogram, ultrasound, MRI) and biopsy. Treatment depends on stage and subtype, and often involves surgery, radiation, chemotherapy, hormone therapy, or targeted biological agents.

### การรักษาหลัก (Treatments)
Standard Western treatment may include lumpectomy or mastectomy, radiation therapy to destroy remaining cancer cells, chemotherapy to kill rapidly dividing cells, hormonal therapies like tamoxifen or aromatase inhibitors for hormone-receptor-positive cancers, and targeted drugs such as trastuzumab for HER2-positive disease. Treatment plans are tailored based on tumor biology and stage.

## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนจีน (Traditional Chinese Medicine View)
### ความเข้าใจเกี่ยวกับการแพทย์แผนจีน (TCM Understanding)
In TCM, the breasts are intimately connected to the Liver, Spleen, and Kidney organ systems, as well as the Chong (Chōng) and Ren (Rèn) meridians that traverse the chest. The Liver is responsible for the smooth flow of Qi and Blood; when emotional stress, frustration, or long-held anger disrupt this flow, Qi stagnates. Over time, this stagnation can generate Heat, congeal fluids into Phlegm, and slow Blood circulation - creating a fertile ground for masses to form.
The Spleen transforms food into Qi and Blood and manages fluid metabolism. When the Spleen is weakened - often by worry, poor diet, or overwork - it fails to transport fluids, leading to Dampness and Phlegm accumulation. This Damp-Phlegm can combine with stagnant Liver Qi to form lumps in the breast. As the condition deepens, the stuck Qi and Phlegm can further obstruct Blood flow, resulting in hard, fixed masses characteristic of Blood stasis.
If these pathogenic factors persist without resolution, they can smolder and transform into Toxic-Heat, an aggressive pattern where the mass may ulcerate, become foul-smelling, and cause severe burning pain. After surgery, chemotherapy, or radiation, many patients experience a profound depletion of Qi and Blood, leaving them exhausted, pale, and with weakened immunity - what TCM calls Qi and Blood Deficiency. Thus, the same Western diagnosis of breast cancer can manifest as very different TCM patterns, each requiring a distinct treatment approach.

### การวินิจฉัยโดยแพทย์แผนจีน (Practitioner Diagnosis)
A TCM practitioner begins by listening to your story and examining the nature of the lump or area of concern. How long it has been there, what it feels like - fixed, hard, or mobile - and what makes it feel better or worse are all clues. These details help separate early stagnation from more entrenched patterns like Blood stasis or Toxic-Heat.
If the breast lump is accompanied by mood swings, chest and rib-side distension, sighing, and a feeling of emotional tightness, the root is likely Obstruction of the Spleen by Dampness with Liver Qi Stagnation. The tongue may have a thin white or slightly yellow coating, and the pulse often feels wiry (like a guitar string). This pattern reflects stress disrupting the digestive and fluid metabolism systems.
When that stuck energy deepens and generates heat, the pattern shifts to Liver Qi Stagnation transforming into Heat. The breast area may feel hot, tender, and more distinctly distended. The person may taste bitterness in the mouth and feel unusually irritable. The tongue becomes red with a thin yellow coat, and the pulse shifts from wiry to wiry and slippery or rapid, signaling heat.
If the lump feels especially hard, fixed in place, and produces a stabbing or boring pain, Blood Stagnation is dominant. The tongue often appears dark red or purple with possible stasis spots, and the pulse may feel wiry, rough, or tight. A history of prolonged emotional strain or unresolved Qi stagnation often precedes this change.
When the lump becomes ulcerated, weeping, or foul-smelling - similar to a local infection that will not heal - Toxic-Heat has taken hold. The tongue is typically red with a dry yellow coating, and the pulse is rapid and forceful. This reflects deep-seated inflammation and toxicity from longstanding stagnation.
After surgeries, chemotherapy, or radiation, many people present with Qi and Blood Deficiency. Here fatigue, pale complexion, poor appetite, breathlessness, and a weak, thready pulse are the dominant signs rather than the lump itself. The tongue is usually pale with a thin white coat, and the whole picture is one of exhaustion and depleted reserves.

### Obstruction Of the Spleen By Dampness with Liver Qi Stagnation
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/obstruction-of-the-spleen-by-dampness-with-liver-qi-stagnation)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Sticky or incomplete bowel movements, Abdominal bloating after meals, Heaviness in the body and limbs, Low mood or emotional frustration, Poor appetite, Distending discomfort along the ribs, Breast lump with a distending, movable sensation
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Emotional stress, Heavy, greasy foods, Cold raw foods, Worry and overthinking, Damp weather
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warm, easily digestible meals, Gentle exercise, Stress reduction, Avoiding dairy and sweets, Warmth on the abdomen
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When long-term emotional frustration or stress causes Liver Qi to stagnate, it disrupts the Spleen's ability to transform food and fluids. This leads to an accumulation of Dampness and Phlegm in the body. Over time, this Dampness and Phlegm congeals along the breast channels, forming a palpable lump that feels distending rather than fixed, and is often accompanied by digestive sluggishness and heaviness.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: Pale, swollen body with teeth marks and a white, greasy, excessively moist coating. Pulse: Slippery and wiry overall, with a soggy right guan position and a wiry left guan position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Emotional stress directly aggravates Liver Qi stagnation, which is the root of this pattern, while heavy, cold, or greasy foods overwhelm the Spleen and generate more Dampness. Warm, light meals and gentle movement support the Spleen’s digestive function and help move stagnant Qi and Dampness, preventing further congealing of Phlegm in the breast.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat warm, cooked foods and avoid cold, raw, or greasy items that create Dampness. Gentle daily movement like walking helps Qi flow and supports digestion. Manage stress through relaxation techniques to prevent Liver Qi from stagnating.

### Liver Qi Stagnation that transforms into Heat
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/liver-qi-stagnation-that-transforms-into-heat)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Breast distension and burning pain, Worse with stress and anger, Irritability and explosive anger, Bitter taste in the mouth, Red face and eyes
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Anger and frustration, Spicy or greasy food, Alcohol, Overwork and late nights
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Cooling foods and drinks, Stress reduction, Gentle exercise, Adequate rest and sleep
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Emotional stress, frustration, or long-held anger cause the Liver Qi to stagnate. Over time, this stagnant Qi generates Heat, which rises and travels along the Liver channel directly into the breasts, producing a sensation of distension, warmth, and burning pain. The Heat can also congeal body fluids into Phlegm, contributing to the formation of a palpable lump.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red body with redder sides and a yellow coating. Pulse: wiry and rapid (弦数 xián shuò).
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Stress and anger are the primary forces that stagnate Liver Qi and generate Heat, so emotional calm and rest are direct antidotes. Spicy, greasy food and alcohol introduce extra Heat into the body, fanning the flames. Cooling foods and gentle movement help disperse Qi and clear Heat, while rest allows the Liver to store Blood and recover its smooth flow.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Favour cooling, bitter, and green foods like cucumber, celery, chrysanthemum tea, and mint. Avoid spicy, greasy, and fried foods, as well as alcohol and coffee, which add Heat. Gentle daily movement like walking or tai chi helps keep Qi flowing; a short wind-down routine before bed eases nighttime restlessness.

### Blood Stagnation
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/blood-stagnation)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Sharp, stabbing pain worse at night and with pressure, Hard, fixed lump that feels immovable under the skin, Dark purplish tongue with stasis spots or engorged veins, Pain fixed in one spot and does not move
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Emotional stress, Cold weather or cold drinks, Prolonged sitting or inactivity, Cold, greasy, or fatty foods
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Gentle exercise, Warmth on the chest, Stress reduction, Warm, cooked meals with spices
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When Liver Qi stagnates over a long period, it cannot push Blood through the breast channels properly. The Blood slows down, thickens, and eventually congeals into hard, immovable lumps. This stasis creates the sharp, stabbing pain that is fixed in one spot and worsens at night, when circulation naturally slows.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: dark purple or dusky body with stasis spots; the sublingual veins are often engorged and dark. Pulse: choppy (涩 sè), rough and uneven like a knife scraping bamboo; may also be wiry (弦 xián) if Qi stagnation is present.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Blood Stasis is like a frozen river-cold and stillness make it solid, while warmth and movement get it flowing again. Stress tightens the Liver Qi, which further constricts the vessels and traps Blood, so calming the mind and moving the body gently are essential to break the cycle of stasis and pain.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Keep the chest warm and avoid cold, raw foods that can congeal Blood. Gentle daily movement like walking or tai chi helps keep Blood flowing. Manage stress with deep breathing or meditation to prevent Qi stagnation from feeding the stasis.

### Toxic-Heat
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/toxic-heat)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Breast mass ulcerating with foul discharge, Severe burning or stabbing pain in the breast, Red, swollen, hot skin over the mass, High fever or feeling of heat, Intense thirst with desire for cold drinks, Restlessness and irritability
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Spicy or fried foods, Alcohol, Anger and frustration, Overwork and fatigue, Hot weather or saunas
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Cooling foods and drinks, Rest in a cool environment, Stress reduction, Local cool compresses, Avoiding alcohol and spicy foods
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When Liver Qi stagnation, Phlegm, and Blood stasis persist for a long time without resolution, they can smolder and transform into intense Heat. This Heat then becomes toxic, aggressively attacking the breast tissue. As a result, the mass may break open, producing a foul-smelling discharge, severe burning pain, and vivid redness - signs that the disease has entered an aggressive, inflammatory stage.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red body with prickly thorns, dry yellow coating. Pulse: rapid (shu) and forceful (you li), often full or slippery.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Spicy, fried, and heating foods introduce external heat that combines with the existing toxic heat, worsening the ulceration and pain. Emotional stress stirs up Liver fire, which is a primary source of the heat. Cooling foods and rest help disperse heat, protect body fluids, and allow the body's own cooling mechanisms to calm the inflammation.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Avoid spicy, greasy, and fried foods, as well as alcohol, which add heat to the body. Eat cooling, detoxifying foods such as mung beans, bitter gourd, cucumber, and watermelon. Get plenty of rest and avoid emotional stress, which can fan the flames of heat. Cool compresses may soothe local burning.

### Qi and Blood Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/qi-and-blood-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Profound fatigue and weakness, Pale complexion, lips, and nail beds, Poor appetite, Heart palpitations and dizziness, Shortness of breath on exertion
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Overwork and fatigue, Worry and overthinking, Cold, greasy, or fatty foods, Skipping meals or irregular eating, Excessive talking or mental strain
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Adequate rest and sleep, Warm, easily digestible meals, Gentle exercise, Stress reduction
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  After prolonged illness or aggressive treatments like chemotherapy and radiation, the body's Qi and Blood become severely depleted. This deep exhaustion weakens the body's ability to heal and fight the disease, leaving the breast tissue undernourished and the constitution too frail to clear lingering pathogens. The result is persistent fatigue, poor appetite, and a general decline that allows the cancer to linger or recur.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale body, may be thin or have teeth marks, thin white coating. Pulse: fine (thin) and weak, especially at the right Guan position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Qi and Blood are the body's fundamental resources; they are depleted by overwork, poor nutrition, and emotional strain. Rest and nourishing, warm foods allow the Spleen to rebuild these reserves, while stress and exhaustion drain them further. Gentle movement stimulates circulation without taxing the already depleted system, and a calm environment prevents further emotional consumption of Qi.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Focus on warm, easily digestible, nutrient-dense foods like bone broths, congees, and well-cooked vegetables. Eat small, frequent meals to support the Spleen without overwhelming it. Avoid raw, cold, or greasy foods that tax digestion. Gentle daily movement such as walking or tai chi helps Qi flow without depleting reserves. Prioritize rest and a calm, low-stress environment to conserve energy.

## คำถามที่พบบ่อย (Frequently Asked Questions)
### Can acupuncture or herbs cure my breast cancer?
TCM is not a substitute for conventional cancer treatment. It is used as a complementary therapy to support your body during and after chemotherapy, radiation, or surgery, helping to manage side effects like fatigue, nausea, and pain, and to address the internal imbalances that may have contributed to the disease. Always follow your oncologist's primary treatment plan.

### Is it safe to take Chinese herbs during chemotherapy?
Some herbs can interact with chemotherapy drugs or affect liver enzymes. It is crucial to inform both your oncologist and your TCM practitioner about all medications and supplements you are taking. A qualified TCM practitioner will select herbs that support your immune system and digestion without interfering with your treatment. Never self-prescribe.

### How does acupuncture help with breast cancer?
Acupuncture can relieve pain, reduce chemotherapy-induced nausea and vomiting, alleviate hot flashes from hormone therapy, and improve energy levels. It works by regulating Qi flow and calming the nervous system. Many cancer centers now offer acupuncture as part of integrative care.

### What foods should I avoid if I have breast cancer?
From a TCM perspective, it's best to avoid greasy, fried, and highly processed foods that create Dampness and Phlegm. Also limit sugar, dairy, and alcohol, which can burden the Spleen and generate Heat. Focus on warm, cooked meals with plenty of vegetables, whole grains, and moderate amounts of lean protein.

### Can TCM prevent breast cancer recurrence?
While no therapy can guarantee prevention, TCM aims to correct the underlying imbalances - such as Liver Qi stagnation and Spleen deficiency - that are believed to contribute to tumor formation. By improving emotional well-being, digestion, and immune function, TCM may help create an internal environment less conducive to recurrence. Regular check-ups with your oncologist remain essential.

### How long does TCM treatment take to work?
This depends on your pattern and whether you are in active conventional treatment. Many patients feel improvements in energy and mood within the first month. For deeper patterns like Blood Stasis or Toxic-Heat, consistent treatment over several months is typical. Qi and Blood rebuilding after chemotherapy may take 6 months or longer.

### What are the common TCM patterns for breast cancer?
The five main patterns we see are: Liver Qi Stagnation transforming into Heat, Obstruction of the Spleen by Dampness with Liver Qi Stagnation, Blood Stagnation, Toxic-Heat, and Qi and Blood Deficiency. Each has distinct symptoms, tongue and pulse findings, and herbal formulas - which you can explore in the patterns section below.

## การวิจัยทางวิทยาศาสตร์สมัยใหม่ (Modern Scientific Research)
1. **Acupuncture for cancer-related fatigue in patients with breast cancer: a pragmatic randomized controlled trial** ([ลิงก์](https://doi.org/10.1200/JCO.2012.43.4514))
   *วารสารวิชาการ: Molassiotis A, Bardy J, Finnegan-John J, et al. Journal of Clinical Oncology. 2012;30(36):4470-4476.*
   This trial randomized 302 breast cancer patients to receive acupuncture plus usual care or usual care alone. After six weeks, the acupuncture group reported significantly less fatigue and better quality of life, with benefits maintained at 18 weeks. The study supports acupuncture as an effective adjunct for managing persistent fatigue after breast cancer treatment.

2. **Acupuncture for aromatase inhibitor-induced arthralgia in breast cancer patients: a randomized controlled trial** ([ลิงก์](https://doi.org/10.1200/JCO.2009.27.4777))
   *วารสารวิชาการ: Crew KD, Capodice JL, Greenlee H, et al. Journal of Clinical Oncology. 2010;28(7):1154-1160.*
   In this trial, 51 breast cancer patients with joint pain from aromatase inhibitors were randomized to true acupuncture or sham acupuncture. After six weeks, the true acupuncture group had significant reductions in pain and stiffness compared to sham. Acupuncture appears to be a safe and effective option for this common side effect.

3. **Chinese herbal medicine for breast cancer: a systematic review of randomized controlled trials** ([ลิงก์](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10549-013-2688-9))
   *วารสารวิชาการ: Chen S, Flower A, Ritchie A, et al. Breast Cancer Research and Treatment. 2013;140(3):471-483.*
   This systematic review included 15 randomized trials evaluating Chinese herbal medicine as an adjunct to conventional breast cancer treatment. Herbal therapy was associated with improved quality of life, reduced treatment side effects, and possibly prolonged survival, but the quality of the trials was generally low. The authors called for more rigorous research.
