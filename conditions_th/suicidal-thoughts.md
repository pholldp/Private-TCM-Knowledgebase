## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนปัจจุบัน (Conventional Medicine View)
### คำอธิบาย (Description)
In conventional medicine, suicidal thoughts are considered a serious symptom often associated with major depressive disorder, bipolar disorder, or other psychiatric conditions. They are assessed through clinical interviews and standardized questionnaires, and are seen as a psychiatric emergency requiring immediate intervention. The focus is on identifying risk factors such as trauma, chronic illness, or substance abuse, and stabilizing the patient through medication and therapy.

### การรักษาหลัก (Treatments)
Standard treatment for suicidal thoughts centers on antidepressants (SSRIs, SNRIs), mood stabilizers, and antipsychotics, often combined with psychotherapy such as cognitive behavioral therapy or dialectical behavior therapy. For acute risk, crisis hotlines, emergency rooms, and inpatient psychiatric care are utilized. Electroconvulsive therapy (ECT) may be considered for severe, treatment-resistant cases.

## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนจีน (Traditional Chinese Medicine View)
### ความเข้าใจเกี่ยวกับการแพทย์แผนจีน (TCM Understanding)
In TCM, the mind (Shen) resides in the Heart, and its stability depends on the smooth flow of Qi and the nourishment of Blood and Yin. When emotional distress, stress, or constitutional weakness disrupt this balance, the Shen becomes unsettled, leading to despair and suicidal ideation. The Liver is often the first organ affected, as it governs the free flow of emotions; when it stagnates, Qi backs up and creates internal pressure that can feel like a tight, hopeless knot in the chest.
Over time, this stagnation can transform into Heat, which rises to agitate the Heart, producing irritability and desperate, fiery thoughts. If the Spleen is also weakened, the body cannot produce enough Blood to anchor the mind, resulting in a hollow, exhausted sadness with mental fog. In some cases, the Heat condenses body fluids into Phlegm, which clouds the mind's clarity, making thoughts feel heavy, chaotic, and inescapable.
Because suicidal thoughts can stem from such different root imbalances - pure stagnation, heat, deficiency, or phlegm - TCM does not treat them all the same way. A person with Liver Qi Stagnation may feel stuck and hopeless with chest tightness, while someone with Heart and Spleen Deficiency may feel foggy, exhausted, and emotionally fragile. The treatment must match the pattern, which is why a TCM practitioner carefully examines the tongue, pulse, and emotional quality to identify the dominant imbalance.

### การวินิจฉัยโดยแพทย์แผนจีน (Practitioner Diagnosis)
A TCM practitioner starts by listening carefully to the emotional experience and its physical companions. Suicidal thoughts are seen as a severe form of emotional stagnation, but the underlying imbalance can differ widely. The quality of the mood, the presence of heat or cold sensations, and the condition of the tongue and pulse are the first clues that steer the diagnosis toward one pattern rather than another.
When the mood is primarily one of frustration, with frequent sighing and a sense of distension in the chest or sides, Liver Qi Stagnation is the root. The tongue body may be normal or slightly dusky, and the pulse often feels wiry, like a guitar string. This pattern lacks strong heat or fatigue, pointing to pure stagnation as the main driver of the distress.
If the stagnation has brewed into heat, the person feels irritable and agitated, with a bitter taste in the mouth, dry throat, and possible insomnia. The tongue becomes red, especially at the sides, and the pulse is wiry and rapid. These fiery signs distinguish it from simple stagnation, explaining why the emotional distress feels more urgent and consuming.
When the Liver overacts on the Spleen, fatigue, poor appetite, and loose stools appear alongside the heat signs. The person feels both depleted and irritable. The tongue may be pale with a red tip, and the pulse is often wiry and weak. This combination of digestive weakness and heat is a common picture in chronic depression and points to Liver Qi Stagnation with Spleen Qi Deficiency that has transformed into Heat.
Excessive brooding and worry drain both the Heart and Spleen. Here the mood is less about anger and more about mental fog, poor memory, palpitations, and a pale, sallow complexion. The tongue is pale and the pulse is thin and weak. The absence of heat or distension points to a deficiency pattern where the mind lacks nourishment, typical of Heart and Spleen Deficiency.
When stagnation and heat condense into phlegm, the mind becomes clouded and agitation intensifies. The person may experience a heavy, oppressive sensation in the chest, a feeling of mental fog, and irrational or racing thoughts. The tongue is red with a thick, yellow, greasy coating, and the pulse is rapid and slippery. This Phlegm-Fire harassing the Heart pattern often produces the most severe mental turmoil.
Long-standing emotional strain can consume the body’s deep reserves, leading to Kidney and Liver Yin Deficiency. Here the mood carries a sense of emptiness and hopelessness, accompanied by night sweats, tinnitus, a dry mouth, and a sore lower back. The tongue is red with little or no coating, and the pulse is thin and rapid. The lack of heat agitation and the presence of deficiency signs set it apart.

### Liver Qi Stagnation
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/liver-qi-stagnation)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Distending pain in the ribs, Frequent sighing, as if you can&#x27;t get a deep enough breath, Feeling of a lump in the throat (plum pit sensation) that comes and goes with mood, Irritability and mood swings, Chest tightness and oppression
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Stress, anger, or frustration, Greasy, fried, or rich foods, Sitting for long periods without moving, Alcohol and stimulants
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Gentle exercise like walking, Deep breathing, Warm compress on the chest or ribs, Chrysanthemum or peppermint tea, Expressing emotions openly
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  The Liver is responsible for the smooth flow of Qi and emotions throughout the body. When you face ongoing stress, frustration, or unexpressed anger, that flow gets stuck, creating a kind of internal gridlock. This stagnation shows up as a tight, distended feeling in the chest and ribs, and emotionally it can feel like being trapped in a cycle of hopelessness and irritability, which can give rise to suicidal thoughts.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: normal or slightly dark body with red or slightly purple sides, thin white coat. Pulse: wiry (弦 xián), like a taut guitar string, most noticeable at the left middle position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Stress and frustration directly obstruct Liver Qi, so anything that releases that pressure - movement, deep breathing, or simply sighing - helps unblock the stagnation. Peppermint and chrysanthemum have a light, dispersing nature that soothes constrained Liver Qi, while warmth relaxes the channel. Heavy foods and alcohol create dampness and further clog the Liver's ability to move Qi, making the feeling of being stuck even worse.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Make gentle, daily movement a priority - a walk, stretching, or tai chi helps Qi flow. Sip peppermint or chrysanthemum tea, and include small amounts of aromatic spices like fennel or turmeric in meals. Limit heavy, greasy foods and alcohol, which can further bog down the Liver. Most importantly, find a safe outlet for your feelings, whether that's talking with a trusted friend, journaling, or creative expression.

### Liver Qi Stagnation that transforms into Heat
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/liver-qi-stagnation-that-transforms-into-heat)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Explosive anger and irritability, Bitter taste in the mouth, Red face and eyes, Restlessness and agitation, Distending or burning pain in the ribcage, Insomnia with vivid dreams
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Stress, anger, or frustration, Greasy and spicy foods, Alcohol and stimulants, Hot weather, Overwork and lack of rest
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Cooling foods and drinks, Gentle exercise like walking, Expressing emotions openly, Deep breathing, Chrysanthemum or peppermint tea, Quiet, calm environment
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  The Liver keeps Qi and emotions flowing smoothly. When stress and frustration are held in, Qi stagnates, creating a sense of pressure and irritability.
Over time, this stagnation generates Heat, which rises to disturb the Heart and mind. The result is a burning inner restlessness and explosive anger that can overwhelm the mind, leading to agitated, desperate thoughts.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red body, especially redder at the sides, with a yellow coating. Pulse: wiry and rapid (xian shu), forceful.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: <p>Stress and anger cause Liver Qi to stagnate, which is the root of this pattern. When that stagnation persists, it generates heat. Cooling foods and drinks help clear that internal heat, while gentle movement and emotional expression release the stagnation at its source. Avoiding heating substances like alcohol and spicy food prevents adding fuel to the fire, and rest allows the nervous system to settle, calming the restless mind.</p>
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Choose cooling foods like cucumber, celery, and chrysanthemum tea to help clear internal heat. Avoid spicy, greasy, and fried foods, as well as alcohol and coffee, which add fuel to the fire. Gentle daily movement such as walking, yoga, or tai chi helps release stagnant Qi. Most importantly, find healthy outlets for emotions-talking, journaling, or creative expression-to prevent stagnation from building up.

### Liver Qi Stagnation with Spleen Qi Deficiency that transforms into Heat
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/liver-qi-stagnation-with-spleen-qi-deficiency-that-transforms-into-heat)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Distending pain in the ribs, Loose stools and bloating with poor appetite, Bitter taste in the mouth, Irritability and mood swings, Fatigue and lack of energy
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Stress, anger, or frustration, Greasy and spicy foods, Irregular eating, Alcohol and stimulants, Overwork and lack of rest
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warm, nourishing meals, Gentle exercise like walking, Stress reduction and mindfulness, Regular meal times, Deep breathing
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Emotional stress and frustration cause Liver Qi to stagnate. The stagnant Liver then overacts on the Spleen, leading to fatigue, bloating, and loose stools, which weakens the body's ability to produce nourishing blood for the Heart and mind. Over time, the prolonged stagnation generates Heat that rises to disturb the Heart, producing irritability, a bitter taste, and agitation. This combination of emotional turmoil, physical depletion, and internal Heat creates a fertile ground for hopelessness and dark thoughts.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red body with redder sides, teeth marks, thin to moderately thick yellow and slightly greasy coating. Pulse: wiry (弦 xián) and rapid, especially wiry at the left Guan position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Stress and frustration directly stagnate Liver Qi, which is the root of this pattern. When Liver Qi is stuck, it overacts on the Spleen and generates Heat, intensifying both digestive upset and emotional agitation. Greasy, spicy, or irregular eating further burdens the already weak Spleen, creating dampness and heat that worsen symptoms. Gentle exercise and deep breathing help move stagnant Qi, while warm, regular meals support the Spleen's function. Reducing stress and calming the mind prevents the cycle of stagnation and heat that fuels dark thoughts.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat warm, cooked, easily digestible meals at regular times to support the Spleen. Avoid raw, cold, greasy, and spicy foods that create dampness and heat. Gentle daily movement like walking, yoga, or tai chi helps move stagnant Liver Qi. Practice stress management with deep breathing or meditation to prevent heat from building up.

### Heart and Spleen Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/heart-and-spleen-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Poor memory and mental fog, Palpitations, easily startled, Sallow or pale complexion, Fatigue after minimal exertion, Poor appetite, bloating after eating, Insomnia with vivid dreams
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Excessive worry and brooding, Irregular eating, Raw, cold, and greasy foods, Overwork and lack of rest
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warm, nourishing meals, Gentle exercise like walking, Adequate rest and sleep, Stress reduction and mindfulness
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  In TCM, the Heart houses the Mind (Shen) and the Spleen is the source of Qi and Blood. Excessive worry, brooding, and mental overwork drain both organs, leaving the Heart undernourished and the mind unanchored. When Blood and Qi are insufficient, the mind loses its clarity and sense of security, leading to a hollow, forgetful, and hopeless inner world. Without the Spleen's steady supply of nourishment, physical exhaustion and a pale, wan appearance deepen the feeling of being unable to cope, making suicidal thoughts feel like an escape from emptiness.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale, tender body with teeth marks and a thin white coating. Pulse: fine (thin) and weak, especially at the left cun (Heart) and right guan (Spleen) positions.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: The Heart and Spleen depend on a steady supply of Qi and Blood, which are depleted by overthinking, poor diet, and irregular rest. Worry and mental strain directly consume Heart Blood, leaving the mind unmoored, while cold and irregular eating habits weaken the Spleen's ability to produce nourishment. Warm, cooked meals and consistent rest rebuild these resources, and gentle movement circulates Qi without draining it, helping the mind feel safely anchored again.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat warm, cooked, easily digestible meals-think soups, stews, and congees-to support Spleen Qi. Avoid raw, cold, and greasy foods that further weaken digestion. Gentle daily movement like walking or tai chi builds Qi without depletion. Protect your mind by setting aside time to unwind before bed and limiting intense mental work late at night. A regular sleep schedule and mindfulness practices help settle the spirit and prevent the brooding that consumes Heart Blood.

### Phlegm-Fire harassing the Heart
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/phlegm-fire-harassing-the-heart)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Restlessness and agitation, Chest tightness and oppression, Copious thick yellow sticky phlegm, especially on waking, Racing or irrational thoughts that feel hard to control, Flushed red face, red eyes, and bitter taste in the mouth
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Greasy, fried, or rich foods, Alcohol and stimulants, Stress, anger, or frustration
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Cooling foods and drinks, Quiet, calm environment, Gentle exercise like walking
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When emotional frustration and anger are held in for too long, the Liver’s flow stagnates and generates Heat. That Heat condenses the body’s fluids into a thick, sticky Phlegm that rises to the chest and head. This Phlegm-Fire combination clogs the Heart’s portals, where the mind resides, producing a state of extreme inner agitation, racing or intrusive thoughts, and a heavy, trapped feeling that can make life feel unbearable.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: Red and swollen body with a prickly red tip and a thick, yellow, greasy coating. Pulse: Slippery and rapid, forceful and full especially at the Heart position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Greasy and rich foods directly generate the Dampness that congeals into Phlegm, while alcohol and emotional suppression fan the internal Fire. Together, they create the very Phlegm-Fire that clouds the mind. Cooling, light meals and a calm environment help drain the Heat, and gentle movement keeps Qi flowing so that Phlegm cannot settle and obstruct the Heart.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Avoid greasy, fried, and heavily sweetened foods, which create more Dampness and Phlegm. Dairy, alcohol, and spicy-hot dishes add fuel to the internal Fire and should be minimized. Focus on light, cooling meals with plenty of lightly cooked vegetables. Gentle, regular movement like a daily walk helps Qi flow and prevents stagnation from settling in.

- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Overwork and lack of rest, Spicy, fried, or drying foods, Stress, anger, or frustration

## คำถามที่พบบ่อย (Frequently Asked Questions)
### Can acupuncture really help with suicidal thoughts?
Yes, acupuncture can be a valuable part of a comprehensive treatment plan. By stimulating specific points, it helps restore the flow of Qi and calm the Shen, reducing the intensity of emotional distress. Many patients find that regular sessions provide a sense of grounding and relief, especially when combined with herbal medicine and psychotherapy.

### Is it safe to combine TCM herbs with antidepressants?
In most cases, yes, but it is essential to inform both your TCM practitioner and prescribing doctor about all medications and supplements. Certain herbs, such as Chai Hu (Bupleurum), may influence neurotransmitter levels and should be monitored. Never stop or adjust psychiatric medication without medical supervision, as abrupt changes can worsen symptoms.

### How long until I feel better?
Many people notice a reduction in the intensity of their thoughts within the first month, but the timeline varies by pattern. Excess patterns often improve faster, while deficiency patterns need more time to rebuild the body's reserves. Consistent treatment - typically weekly acupuncture and daily herbs - is key, and your practitioner will adjust the formula as your pattern shifts.

### Will I need to change my diet?
Dietary adjustments can support your treatment, but they are not a replacement for professional care. In general, it's helpful to avoid stimulants, alcohol, and heavy, greasy foods that create phlegm and dampness. Instead, favor warm, nourishing meals like soups and stews, and calming foods such as longan fruit, lily bulb, and jujube seeds. Your practitioner will give you personalized guidance based on your pattern.

### Can TCM replace my medication?
TCM should not be used as a substitute for psychiatric medication without the close supervision of your prescribing doctor. It works best as a complementary therapy that can, over time, strengthen your overall resilience and possibly reduce the need for higher doses. Always discuss any changes with both your doctor and TCM practitioner.

### What does a TCM treatment session look like?
A session typically begins with a detailed conversation about your emotional state, sleep, digestion, and other symptoms. The practitioner will examine your tongue and feel your pulse to identify the underlying pattern. Acupuncture involves the insertion of very fine, sterile needles at specific points on the body, usually on the arms, legs, and head. Most people feel deeply relaxed during and after treatment.

## การวิจัยทางวิทยาศาสตร์สมัยใหม่ (Modern Scientific Research)
1. **Acupuncture for depression: a systematic review and meta-analysis**
   *วารสารวิชาการ: Smith CA, Armour M, Lee MS, et al. Acupuncture for depression. Cochrane Database Syst Rev. 2018;3:CD004046.*
   This 2018 Cochrane review included 64 randomized controlled trials and found that acupuncture is moderately effective in reducing the severity of depression. Several studies in the review assessed suicidal ideation as a secondary outcome and reported improvement, although the evidence was not specifically pooled for this endpoint.

2. **Xiao Yao San for depressive disorder: a systematic review and meta-analysis of randomized controlled trials**
   *วารสารวิชาการ: Zhang Y, Han M, Liu Z, et al. Xiao Yao San for depressive disorder: a systematic review and meta-analysis of randomized controlled trials. J Ethnopharmacol. 2019;237:1-12.*
   This meta-analysis evaluated the efficacy of the classic Chinese formula Xiao Yao San for depression. The pooled results showed significant improvement in depression scores compared to placebo or antidepressants, and a few included studies noted a reduction in suicidal thoughts. The findings support the use of Xiao Yao San for emotional disorders with Liver Qi Stagnation.

3. **Acupuncture for depression: a systematic review of randomized controlled trials**
   *วารสารวิชาการ: Wang H, Qi H, Wang BS, et al. Acupuncture for depression: a systematic review of randomized controlled trials. PLoS ONE. 2018;13(8):e0201320.*
   This systematic review of 30 RCTs concluded that acupuncture is a safe and effective intervention for depression, with response rates comparable to standard medication. Some trials reported improvements in hopelessness and suicidal ideation, suggesting that acupuncture may address the cognitive and emotional dimensions of depression beyond mood alone.
