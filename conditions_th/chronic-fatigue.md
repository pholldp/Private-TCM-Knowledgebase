## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนปัจจุบัน (Conventional Medicine View)
### คำอธิบาย (Description)
Western medicine defines chronic fatigue as persistent, unexplained tiredness lasting six months or more that is not relieved by rest and significantly impairs daily functioning. When accompanied by other symptoms such as cognitive difficulties, unrefreshing sleep, and post-exertional malaise, it may be diagnosed as myalgic encephalomyelitis/chronic fatigue syndrome (ME/CFS). Diagnosis is typically one of exclusion, after ruling out other medical or psychiatric conditions through blood tests and clinical evaluation. The exact cause remains unclear, though theories include viral triggers, immune dysfunction, and neuroendocrine abnormalities.

### การรักษาหลัก (Treatments)
Conventional management focuses on symptom relief and may include cognitive behavioral therapy (CBT) to address coping strategies, graded exercise therapy (GET) to gradually increase activity tolerance, and medications such as low-dose antidepressants or sleep aids for associated symptoms. However, no single drug is approved specifically for chronic fatigue, and treatment plans are highly individualized.

## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนจีน (Traditional Chinese Medicine View)
### ความเข้าใจเกี่ยวกับการแพทย์แผนจีน (TCM Understanding)
TCM views chronic fatigue as a state of deficiency, where the body's fundamental substances - Qi, Blood, Yin, and Yang - are insufficient to power daily life. Unlike Western medicine, which often sees fatigue as a single symptom, TCM differentiates it into distinct patterns based on which substance is lacking and which organ system is most affected. The Spleen, as the source of Qi and Blood from food, and the Kidneys, as the root of constitutional Yin and Yang, are the two most commonly involved systems. When they falter, the entire body runs on empty.
Qi Deficiency, often rooted in the Spleen and Lungs, produces a fatigue that worsens with any exertion and comes with breathlessness, spontaneous sweating, and a pale puffy tongue. When the Spleen is specifically weak, digestive symptoms like bloating and loose stools join the picture, because the body can't transform food into usable energy. This is the most superficial level of depletion and often responds well to Qi-tonifying herbs and acupuncture.
Blood Deficiency goes deeper. Blood nourishes the mind and body; when it is insufficient, fatigue is accompanied by a pale face, dizziness, heart palpitations, and restless sleep. The tongue is pale and thin, the pulse fine. This pattern often arises from prolonged Qi Deficiency (Qi makes Blood) or from blood loss, and requires a different treatment strategy - nourishing the Blood and calming the Spirit.
At the deepest level lie Kidney Yin and Kidney Yang Deficiency. Kidney Yin Deficiency causes a dragging fatigue that worsens in the afternoon and evening, with night sweats, dry mouth, and a red tongue with little coating. Kidney Yang Deficiency brings bone-deep weariness that feels worse in the cold, with sore lower back, frequent nighttime urination, and an overwhelming desire for warmth. These patterns take longer to rebuild but are central to many long-standing fatigue cases.

### การวินิจฉัยโดยแพทย์แผนจีน (Practitioner Diagnosis)
A TCM practitioner starts by asking what the tiredness feels like and what else comes with it. The quality of the fatigue and the symptoms that travel alongside it are the first clues that separate Qi Deficiency from deeper patterns like Blood Deficiency or Yin and Yang Deficiency.
If the fatigue is a general lack of get‑up‑and‑go with spontaneous sweating and breathlessness on mild exertion, Qi Deficiency is the leading picture. The tongue is often pale with a thin white coat, and the pulse feels weak and empty, confirming that the body’s vital force is simply running low.
When the same fatigue is paired with poor appetite, bloating, and loose stools, the focus shifts to Spleen Qi Deficiency. Here the digestive engine is weak, so even eating doesn’t restore energy. The tongue remains pale, but the pulse is especially weak at the middle position, which corresponds to the Spleen.
If the person also complains of a pale face, dizziness, heart palpitations, and trouble sleeping, Blood Deficiency is likely. The fatigue comes from a lack of nourishment rather than just a lack of drive. The tongue looks pale and the pulse is thin, sometimes with a choppy quality, reflecting that the blood is not filling the vessels properly.
When the tiredness is accompanied by heat signs-night sweats, a dry mouth, a feeling of warmth in the palms and soles, and a thin, rapid pulse-Kidney Yin Deficiency is usually at play. The tongue is red with little coating, showing that the body’s cooling, moistening reserves are depleted. In contrast, fatigue with cold signs like low‑back soreness, feeling chilled, and frequent urination points to Kidney Yang Deficiency; the tongue is pale and puffy with tooth marks, and the pulse is deep and slow.

## กลุ่มอาการการแพทย์แผนจีนและการรักษา (TCM Patterns and Treatment)

### Qi Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/qi-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Fatigue that worsens with activity, Spontaneous sweating without physical effort, Shortness of breath on mild exertion, Reluctance to speak or speaking in a low voice, Pale, puffy tongue with teeth marks, Weak or empty pulse
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Overexertion or overwork, Skipping meals or poor diet, Raw, cold foods or iced drinks, Excessive worry or stress, Irregular routine
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Rest and adequate sleep, Warm, nourishing cooked meals, Gentle movement like walking, Deep breathing exercises
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Qi is the body's vital force that fuels every movement and function. When Qi is deficient, the entire system runs low on power, causing deep, persistent fatigue that worsens with even light activity.
The Lungs fail to properly manage breathing and the skin's pores, leading to shortness of breath and spontaneous sweating. The Spleen's ability to extract energy from food is impaired, so appetite drops, digestion becomes sluggish, and limbs feel heavy and weak. The protective Wei Qi is also weak, making you more vulnerable to frequent colds and infections.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale, puffy, tender body with teeth marks; thin white coating. Pulse: empty (Xu) or weak (Ruo), soft and yielding, often thin and lacking force, hollow at deeper levels.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Qi is the body's fuel; activity consumes it, so rest is essential to rebuild reserves. Overexertion and stress rapidly deplete Qi, leading to deeper exhaustion. The Spleen produces Qi from food, so it thrives on warm, regular meals and is damaged by raw, cold, or irregular eating. Gentle movement helps Qi circulate smoothly, preventing stagnation while avoiding further depletion. Deep breathing strengthens Lung Qi, which governs the rhythm of energy in the body.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat small, frequent meals of warm, cooked foods like soups, stews, and congees to support the Spleen. Avoid raw, cold, and greasy foods that dampen digestive fire. Incorporate gentle, consistent movement such as walking, Tai Chi, or Qi Gong to circulate Qi without depleting it. Prioritize rest and avoid pushing through exhaustion, as overexertion further drains Qi.

### Spleen Qi Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/spleen-qi-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Poor appetite, Abdominal bloating worse after eating, Loose, soft stools, Heaviness or weakness in the limbs, Dull pale complexion
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Overeating, Raw, cold foods or iced drinks, Excessive worry or stress, Overexertion or overwork, Damp weather
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warm, nourishing cooked meals, Small frequent meals, Rest after eating, Gentle movement like walking, Abdominal warmth
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Your Spleen is responsible for turning food into energy (Qi) and blood. When Spleen Qi is weak, digestion falters and the body can't produce enough energy, leaving you chronically tired, especially after meals. The undigested food can also create dampness, causing heaviness and bloating.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale, puffy, tender body with tooth marks, thin white coating. Pulse: overall deficient and soft, especially weak at the right Guan (Spleen/Stomach) position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: The Spleen transforms food into Qi. Warm, cooked foods are easy to digest and support this transformation, while raw, cold foods dampen the digestive fire. Overeating overwhelms a weak Spleen, leading to bloating and fatigue. Worry knots Spleen Qi, and overwork depletes it. Rest and gentle movement allow Qi to recover and circulate, preventing stagnation.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat warm, cooked foods like soups, stews, and congee. Avoid raw, cold, and greasy foods that tax the Spleen. Eat small, frequent meals and chew thoroughly. Gentle daily movement, like walking or tai chi, helps Qi flow without depleting it. Rest after meals and avoid overworking or excessive worry.

### Blood Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/blood-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Pale or sallow complexion, Pale lips and nail beds, Dizziness or light-headedness, Heart palpitations, Difficulty falling or staying asleep, Poor memory or difficulty concentrating, Numbness or tingling in hands and feet
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Overwork and chronic stress, Heavy menstrual bleeding, Prolonged screen time, Skipping meals or poor diet, Excessive worry or stress
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Rest and adequate sleep, Warm, nourishing cooked meals, Gentle movement like walking, Iron-rich foods (meat, dark greens)
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Blood is the material foundation that nourishes every cell, muscle, and organ in the body. When Blood is deficient, the brain, heart, and limbs are under-supplied, leading to a deep, persistent fatigue that rest alone often does not fix. Without enough Blood to anchor the mind, sleep becomes restless and unrefreshing, and the heart struggles to pump adequately, causing palpitations and dizziness. This is a fatigue of emptiness-the body simply lacks the vital substance it needs to generate energy.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: Pale, thin body with a thin white coating. Pulse: Fine (细 xi) and weak, may feel choppy (涩 se).
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Blood is a tangible substance that must be built through nourishment and rest. Overwork, mental strain, and heavy blood loss directly consume it, while warm, nutrient-dense meals and adequate sleep give the Spleen the raw material and time it needs to manufacture new Blood. Gentle movement encourages circulation without draining reserves, but pushing through exhaustion only deepens the deficit.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Build Blood with warm, cooked foods like bone broths, slow-cooked red meats, dark leafy greens, beets, black sesame, and goji berries. Avoid excessive raw, cold, or iced foods that weaken the Spleen's ability to produce Blood. Prioritize rest and a regular sleep schedule; even gentle exercise like walking should be moderate, never to exhaustion. Reducing screen time can help preserve the Blood that nourishes the eyes and mind.

### Kidney Yin Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/kidney-yin-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Soreness and weakness of the lower back and knees, Night sweats and heat in the palms, soles, and chest, Fatigue that worsens in the afternoon and evening, Dry mouth and throat with dark scanty urine, Dizziness and ringing in the ears
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Spicy, greasy, or fried foods, Overwork and staying up late, Excessive sweating from intense exercise, Hot, dry weather, Excessive sexual activity, Excessive worry or stress
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Cool, quiet environment, Eating moistening foods like pears and black sesame, Going to bed early, Gentle movement like walking, Staying well hydrated
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  The Kidneys store the body's fundamental Yin, which cools, moistens, and deeply nourishes every tissue. When Kidney Yin is depleted, the body lacks this vital sustenance, leading to a persistent, dragging fatigue that feels worse in the afternoon and evening. At the same time, the lack of cooling Yin allows empty heat to rise, causing night sweats, dry mouth, and a restless feeling that further drains energy and disturbs sleep.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red, thin body with little or no coating, dry surface, possible cracks. Pulse: fine (细 xì) and rapid (数 shuò), especially weak at the left chi position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Kidney Yin is the body's cooling reserve. Overwork, late nights, and excessive sexual activity directly drain this reserve, while spicy foods and intense exercise generate more heat, worsening the empty heat symptoms. Cooling, moistening foods and a restful lifestyle help replenish Yin, allowing the body to cool down and restore deep energy. Gentle movement keeps Qi flowing without further consuming Yin, while hydration and early sleep align with the body's natural rhythm of Yin restoration at night.

### Kidney Yang Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/kidney-yang-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Deep fatigue that is worse with cold, Sore, cold lower back and knees, Frequent urination, especially at night, Feeling cold all over, especially lower body, Low libido or sexual dysfunction
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Exposure to cold weather or drafts, Overwork and pushing through fatigue, Raw, cold foods or iced drinks, Excessive sexual activity, Chronic fear or anxiety
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warmth, such as a heating pad on the lower back, Rest and adequate sleep, Warm, nourishing cooked meals, Gentle movement like walking, Moxibustion therapy
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Kidney Yang is the body’s pilot light - it provides warmth and the spark for all metabolic activity. When it runs low, the entire system slows down, resulting in a deep, bone-weary fatigue that feels worse with cold and improves with warmth. The Kidneys govern the lower back and control urination, so the fatigue is typically accompanied by a sore, cold lower back and frequent, clear urination, especially at night, which further depletes energy.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale, puffy, tender body with teeth marks, wet white coating. Pulse: deep, slow, and weak, especially at the Chi (rear) positions; the right Chi is particularly feeble.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Kidney Yang is the body's foundational fire; cold environments and raw foods directly extinguish this fire, worsening fatigue and coldness. Overwork and excessive sexual activity drain the Kidney essence, further depleting Yang. Warmth, rest, and warm foods stoke the fire, while moxibustion and gentle movement help circulate Yang without exhausting it. Emotional fear is said to descend and congeal Qi, directly impacting the Kidneys, so calm reassurance is protective.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Warm, cooked foods are essential; think soups, stews, and congees with ginger, cinnamon, and black pepper. Avoid raw, cold, and frozen foods that dampen the digestive fire. Keep the lower back and feet warm, and consider gentle activities like walking or Tai Chi to gradually build stamina without depleting energy. A midday nap can help conserve Yang when it is naturally at its peak.

## คำถามที่พบบ่อย (Frequently Asked Questions)
### How does TCM treat chronic fatigue differently from Western medicine?
TCM does not treat all fatigue the same way. Instead, it identifies the specific pattern of deficiency - whether Qi, Blood, Yin, or Yang is depleted, and which organ system (Spleen, Kidney, Heart) is involved. Treatment then uses customized herbal formulas and acupuncture points to rebuild those exact reserves. Western medicine often focuses on symptom management with medications or behavioral therapy, while TCM aims to correct the underlying imbalance so the body can generate its own energy again.

### Can acupuncture really help with chronic fatigue?
Yes, many people find acupuncture helpful for chronic fatigue. Acupuncture works by stimulating specific points that strengthen the Spleen, Kidney, and other organ systems, encouraging the body to produce and circulate Qi and Blood more efficiently. Points like Zusanli ST-36 and Qihai REN-6 are commonly used to boost overall energy. Most patients feel a subtle but real lift after a few sessions, though deeper deficiencies require regular treatment over weeks or months.

### How long will it take to feel better?
Improvement is typically gradual. For Qi or Spleen Qi deficiency patterns, many patients notice increased energy within 2-4 weeks of starting herbs and weekly acupuncture. Blood Deficiency may take 6-8 weeks. Kidney Yin or Yang deficiency, which are deeper, often need 3-6 months of consistent treatment. The timeline also depends on how long the fatigue has been present and how closely you follow dietary and lifestyle recommendations.

### Will I need to change my diet?
Diet plays a crucial role in TCM treatment for chronic fatigue. Since the Spleen extracts Qi and Blood from food, eating warm, cooked, easily digestible meals is essential. Cold, raw foods and iced drinks strain the digestive fire and can worsen fatigue. Your practitioner will likely recommend soups, congees, and lightly cooked vegetables, and may suggest avoiding excessive sugar, dairy, and greasy foods that create dampness and further burden the Spleen.

### Can I combine TCM with my current medications or other treatments?
In most cases, yes. TCM herbal medicine and acupuncture can be safely combined with conventional treatments. However, it is essential to inform both your TCM practitioner and your medical doctor about all medications and supplements you are taking. Some herbs may interact with blood thinners or sedatives, so a full disclosure ensures safe integration. Never stop prescribed medications without consulting your doctor, even if you start feeling better.

### Is chronic fatigue the same as 'adrenal fatigue' in TCM?
While TCM does not use the term 'adrenal fatigue,' the concept overlaps with Kidney deficiency patterns, particularly Kidney Yang or Kidney Yin deficiency. The Kidneys in TCM govern the body's constitutional energy and hormonal reserves, much like the adrenal glands in Western physiology. Symptoms like deep exhaustion, low back pain, and cold intolerance align with Kidney Yang deficiency, while afternoon fatigue, night sweats, and dry mouth point to Kidney Yin deficiency. TCM offers a more detailed framework for understanding and treating these states.

## การวิจัยทางวิทยาศาสตร์สมัยใหม่ (Modern Scientific Research)
1. **Acupuncture for chronic fatigue syndrome: a systematic review and meta-analysis**
   *วารสารวิชาการ: Wang T, Xu C, Pan K, et al. Acupuncture for chronic fatigue syndrome: a systematic review and meta-analysis. Acupuncture in Medicine. 2019;37(4):205-216.*
   This meta-analysis of 31 RCTs found that acupuncture significantly reduced fatigue severity compared with sham acupuncture and usual care. Improvements in quality of life and sleep were also noted. The authors called for larger, more rigorous trials to confirm the findings.

2. **Effect of Bojungikgi-tang (Bu Zhong Yi Qi Tang) on chronic fatigue: a randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial**
   *วารสารวิชาการ: Kim HG, Cho JH, Yoo SR, et al. Effect of Bojungikgi-tang on chronic fatigue: a randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial. Evidence-Based Complementary and Alternative Medicine. 2016;2016:9130979.*
   In this 4-week trial of 96 patients with chronic fatigue, those receiving Bu Zhong Yi Qi Tang showed significant reductions in fatigue severity and improvements in vitality compared to placebo. The formula was well tolerated with no serious adverse events.
