## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนปัจจุบัน (Conventional Medicine View)
### คำอธิบาย (Description)
Surgical adhesions are bands of fibrous scar tissue that form between internal organs and tissues after surgery, most commonly in the abdomen. They can tether structures that should move freely, leading to chronic pelvic or abdominal pain, bowel obstruction, and in some cases infertility. Diagnosis is often based on symptoms and imaging such as ultrasound or CT, though definitive identification may require laparoscopy. Adhesions can form within days of surgery and may cause problems months or even years later.

### การรักษาหลัก (Treatments)
Conventional management typically focuses on symptom control with pain relievers and anti-inflammatory medications. When adhesions cause severe bowel obstruction or intractable pain, surgery (adhesiolysis) may be performed to cut or remove the scar tissue. However, surgery itself carries a high risk of creating new adhesions, leading to a cycle of repeat procedures. Non-surgical options like physical therapy and dietary modifications are sometimes recommended to manage symptoms.

## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนจีน (Traditional Chinese Medicine View)
### ความเข้าใจเกี่ยวกับการแพทย์แผนจีน (TCM Understanding)
TCM views surgery as a significant physical trauma that cuts through tissues and disrupts the body's energetic pathways. The immediate consequence is local Blood Stagnation (瘀血, yū xuè) - blood that congeals and fails to circulate properly, forming the fibrous bands we recognize as adhesions. This stagnant blood blocks the flow of Qi, causing the sharp, fixed pain that is the hallmark of this pattern. Over time, the stagnation can generate heat or combine with dampness, further complicating the picture.
But the trauma of surgery doesn't stop at the incision. Blood loss and the prolonged bed rest of recovery often deplete the Spleen's Qi, the energy that transforms food into usable nourishment. A weakened Spleen fails to move fluids, allowing dampness and phlegm to accumulate - substances that in TCM are thick, sticky, and contribute directly to the formation of adhesions and bloating. This is why many patients experience fatigue, loose stools, and a heavy sensation in the abdomen alongside their pain.
Emotional stress plays a powerful role as well. The anxiety, frustration, and worry that accompany surgery and recovery can cause the Liver's Qi to stagnate. Because the Liver channel runs through the abdomen and rib area, stuck Liver Qi creates distending pain that moves around, frequent sighing, and symptoms that flare with emotional upset. When Liver Qi invades the Stomach, it disrupts digestion, leading to acid reflux, belching, and upper abdominal pain. This is why one person's post-surgical adhesions might feel like a dull, constant ache while another's is a cramping, stress-related pain - each pattern demands a different treatment.

### การวินิจฉัยโดยแพทย์แผนจีน (Practitioner Diagnosis)
A TCM practitioner begins by understanding the nature of your pain and how your digestion has changed since surgery. Because surgical adhesions are seen as a disruption of Qi and Blood flow, the quality, location, and triggers of your discomfort offer the first clues. They will also ask about energy, mood, appetite, and bowel habits to map which organ systems are most affected.
If the pain is sharp, fixed, and stabbing-often worsening with pressure-it points toward Blood Stagnation, the core pathology after surgical trauma. The tongue may appear purplish or show dark spots, and the pulse often feels wiry or choppy. This pattern reflects local blockage of circulation where scar tissue forms.
When fatigue, a dull abdominal ache, and bloating dominate, especially with poor appetite and loose stools, Spleen Qi Deficiency is likely. Prolonged bed rest and the body's effort to heal can weaken the Spleen's digestive power. The tongue is usually pale with a thin white coat, and the pulse feels weak and thready.
Distending pain that moves around, belching, sighing, and symptoms that flare with stress suggest Liver Qi Stagnation. The emotional toll of surgery can knot the Liver's smooth flow, causing pressure and discomfort. The edges of the tongue may be slightly red, and the pulse takes on a wiry quality.
If bloating is intense and you notice foul belching or a sense of food just sitting in the stomach, Food Stagnation in the Stomach is at play. Sluggish bowel function after surgery often leads to this. A thick, greasy tongue coating and a slippery pulse are typical signs.
For distending pain in the upper abdomen that radiates to the ribs, with belching and acid reflux, the pattern may be Liver Qi Stagnation invading the Stomach. Here, pent-up Liver Qi disrupts the Stomach's downward movement. The tongue edges may be red, and the pulse is wiry.
Less commonly, severe cramping pain that feels better with warmth and is accompanied by a sensation of cold in the abdomen points to Large Intestine Cold. This can arise from a cold constitution or exposure after surgery. The tongue is pale with a white coating, and the pulse is deep and slow.

## กลุ่มอาการการแพทย์แผนจีนและการรักษา (TCM Patterns and Treatment)

### Blood Stagnation
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/blood-stagnation)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Fixed stabbing pain that does not move, Pain worsens at night, Pain worse with pressure, Dark or purplish complexion, lips, or nails, Palpable hard lumps or masses at surgical site
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Cold weather or cold drinks, Stress and frustration, Prolonged inactivity, Heavy greasy foods
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warmth, Gentle movement, Rest, Warm foods
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Surgery is a form of trauma that cuts tissues and disrupts the smooth flow of Qi and Blood. In TCM, this kind of injury causes local Blood Stagnation (瘀血, yū xuè) - blood that congeals and fails to circulate properly.
When blood stagnates in the abdomen, it forms fibrous bands and adhesions that tether organs together, leading to fixed, stabbing pain. The stagnation also blocks the movement of Qi, causing distension and worsening discomfort with pressure or at night when circulation naturally slows.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: dark purple body with possible stasis spots and distended sublingual veins, thin white coat. Pulse: choppy (Se) or wiry (Xian).
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: <p>Cold and inactivity congeal blood further, while warmth and gentle movement promote circulation. Stress causes Qi stagnation, which worsens blood stasis. Rest allows the body to heal, but too much inactivity stagnates Qi, so a balance of light activity and rest is essential.</p>
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat warm, cooked foods and avoid cold, raw, or greasy meals that can congeal blood. Gentle daily movement like walking or stretching helps Qi and Blood flow to prevent further adhesions. Avoid prolonged sitting or lying down. Apply a warm compress to the abdomen to ease pain.

### Spleen Qi Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/spleen-qi-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Dull, aching abdominal discomfort rather than sharp stabbing pain, Bloating that worsens after eating, Loose stools, Fatigue and lack of energy, Heaviness in limbs
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Raw, cold foods, Overeating, Overexertion, Prolonged bed rest, Stress
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warm, cooked meals, Small, frequent meals, Gentle walking, Rest, Abdominal warmth
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Surgery and blood loss deplete Spleen Qi, the energy that transforms food and fluids. When Spleen Qi is weak, it fails to move fluids properly, leading to dampness and phlegm that accumulate and worsen adhesions. This also slows intestinal movement, causing bloating, loose stools, and chronic fatigue that linger long after the operation.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale, puffy, tender body with tooth marks, thin white coating. Pulse: deficient, soft, and notably weaker at the right guan (Spleen) position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: The Spleen transforms food into Qi and blood; warm, cooked, easily digested meals support its function, while raw, cold, and heavy foods further deplete Spleen Qi. Rest and gentle movement help Qi flow without exhausting the Spleen, but overexertion and prolonged inactivity both harm Qi. Stress and emotional tension consume Spleen Qi, worsening fatigue and digestion.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat warm, cooked, easily digestible meals such as soups and congees. Avoid raw, cold, and greasy foods that further weaken the Spleen. Eat small, frequent meals to avoid overloading digestion. Gentle daily walking helps Qi circulation and digestion. Rest adequately but avoid prolonged bed rest, which can worsen Qi stagnation.

### Liver Qi Stagnation
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/liver-qi-stagnation)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Distension and bloating that moves around the abdomen, Pain or discomfort worse with emotional stress, Frequent sighing, Irritability or mood swings, Rib-side or flank discomfort, Belching
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Emotional stress, Anger or frustration, Greasy, heavy meals, Sedentary rest, Irregular eating schedule
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Gentle walking, Deep breathing, Stress management, Warm compress on abdomen, Eating small, frequent meals
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Surgery disrupts the body's Qi flow, and the emotional stress of recovery - worry, frustration, or anxiety - easily causes Liver Qi to stagnate. The Liver channel runs through the abdomen, so when Qi gets stuck, it creates distension and pain that can move around. This stagnation also impairs the Spleen's digestive function, leading to bloating, poor appetite, and slow return of bowel activity.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: normal or slightly dark body, red or slightly purple sides, thin white coat. Pulse: wiry (弦 xián), taut like a guitar string.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Emotional stress and frustration directly cause Liver Qi to stagnate, which is the root mechanism of this pattern. Gentle movement and deep breathing help to smooth the flow of Qi, while warm compresses relax the abdominal muscles and promote circulation. Greasy and heavy foods burden the Spleen and create further stagnation, so light, warm meals support digestion and Qi movement.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat warm, cooked meals in small, regular portions to avoid burdening the Spleen. Gentle daily movement like walking or tai chi helps move Liver Qi. Practice stress-reduction techniques such as deep breathing or journaling. Avoid cold, raw, and greasy foods that can worsen stagnation.

### Food Stagnation in the Stomach
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/food-stagnation-in-the-stomach)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Upper abdominal bloating and distension that worsens after eating, Sour, rotten-smelling belching and acid reflux, Aversion to food and its smell, Thick, greasy tongue coating (especially in the centre), Foul-smelling flatulence like rotten eggs, Bloating relieved after vomiting or passing gas
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Overeating or large meals, Greasy, heavy, or fried foods, Lying down immediately after eating, Raw, cold, or difficult-to-digest foods, Emotional stress that disrupts digestion
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Light, easily digestible meals, Gentle walking after eating, Warm compresses on the abdomen, Digestive spices like ginger, Small, frequent portions
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  After surgery, the digestive system's Qi is weakened and peristalsis slows. Food that is not properly digested accumulates in the stomach and intestines, creating a blockage of Qi. This stagnation causes distension, bloating, and pain, and the retained food produces turbid dampness and foul-smelling gas. The pressure from the accumulated food can also pull on healing tissues and worsen adhesions.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: normal body colour, thick greasy or curd-like coating, thickest in the centre. Pulse: slippery (滑 huá) and full, with the right guan (Stomach) position especially prominent.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: In this pattern, the digestive system is already sluggish after surgery, so overloading it with heavy food creates a backlog that blocks Qi and produces foul-smelling gas. Light, warm, and easily digestible foods give the stomach less work to do, while gentle movement and digestive spices help push food downward. Lying down after meals allows food to sit and stagnate, whereas staying upright and moving gently encourages peristalsis and the descent of Stomach Qi.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat small, frequent meals of easy-to-digest foods like congee, soups, and steamed vegetables. Avoid heavy, greasy, raw, or cold foods that tax the digestive system. Chew food thoroughly and avoid lying down for at least 30 minutes after eating. Gentle walking after meals helps stimulate peristalsis. Use digestive spices like fresh ginger, cardamom, or a little fermented tea to help break down food.

### Liver Qi Stagnation invading the Stomach
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/liver-qi-stagnation-invading-the-stomach)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Distending upper abdominal pain that radiates to the ribs, Frequent belching or acid reflux, Symptoms worsen with emotional stress or frustration, Irritability and moodiness, Sensation of a lump in the throat (plum pit qi)
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Emotional stress or anger, Greasy or spicy foods, Large meals, Alcohol
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Gentle walking, Deep breathing and relaxation, Warm compress on abdomen, Smaller, more frequent meals
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  After surgery, the physical trauma and emotional stress can cause Liver Qi to stagnate. The Liver channel runs through the rib area, and when its Qi is stuck, it tends to invade the Stomach, disrupting its downward-moving function. This leads to bloating, distending pain in the upper abdomen that radiates to the ribs, acid reflux, and belching. The adhesions themselves are a form of Qi and Blood stagnation, and the Liver's inability to maintain smooth flow worsens the local blockage, creating a vicious cycle of pain and digestive upset.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: normal or slightly red body, with redder sides; thin white coat. Pulse: wiry (弦 xián), especially at the left middle position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Emotional stress and anger are the primary drivers of Liver Qi stagnation, which then attacks the Stomach. Greasy and spicy foods add heat and dampness, further agitating the Liver and burdening the digestive system. Large meals overwhelm the already compromised Stomach Qi. Conversely, gentle movement encourages the smooth flow of Liver Qi, while relaxation techniques calm the mind and prevent the upward surge that causes reflux and pain. Warmth helps to relax the abdominal muscles and move Qi, while small meals reduce the workload on the Stomach.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat small, frequent meals and avoid rich, greasy, or spicy foods that can overstimulate the Liver and Stomach. Incorporate gentle movement like walking or Tai Chi to keep Qi flowing, but avoid overexertion. Practice deep breathing or meditation to manage stress, which directly triggers this pattern. A warm compress on the upper abdomen can help ease distension.

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Dull aching pain in the lower belly, Pain relieved by warmth and gentle pressure, Cold hands and feet, Chronic loose stools or watery diarrhea, Preference for warm food and drink, Feeling of coldness in the abdomen, Rumbling or gurgling intestinal sounds
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Cold weather, Raw or cold food and drinks, Eating too much at once, Overexertion
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Hot water bottle on the belly, Warm, cooked meals, Gentle abdominal massage with warmth, Rest and lying down
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Surgery depletes Qi and Blood, and if your constitution is already on the cold side or you are exposed to cold after the operation, the Spleen's warming power weakens further. This allows internal Cold to settle in the Large Intestine, where it congeals and slows everything down. The resulting stagnation of Qi and Blood not only causes the dull, persistent ache of adhesions but also undermines the gut's ability to absorb fluids, leading to chronic loose stools.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale, swollen, with teeth marks on the edges and a white slippery coating. Pulse: deep, slow, and weak.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Cold constricts and slows the flow of Qi and Blood, making adhesions feel tighter and more painful. Warmth relaxes the tissues, dilates the vessels, and helps the Spleen's Yang energy move again, which is why heat and warm foods bring relief. Cold foods and environments add more Cold to an already cold gut, worsening the stagnation and loosening the stools.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat only warm, cooked foods like soups, stews, and congees. Avoid all raw, cold, or iced items. Keep your abdomen covered and warm, and use a hot water bottle when pain flares. Gentle daily walking helps move Qi without exhausting you.

## คำถามที่พบบ่อย (Frequently Asked Questions)
### Can acupuncture break up surgical adhesions?
Acupuncture cannot physically cut or dissolve scar tissue in the way surgery can, but it can significantly improve the environment around adhesions. By increasing local blood circulation, reducing inflammation, and relaxing tight tissues, acupuncture often relieves pain, improves bowel function, and may help prevent further adhesion formation. Many patients find it a valuable tool for managing symptoms and supporting overall healing.

### How soon after surgery can I start TCM treatment?
In most cases, you can begin acupuncture and gentle herbal therapy once your surgical wound is fully closed and you are medically stable, typically 2-4 weeks after the operation. It is essential to get clearance from your surgeon first, especially if you had major abdominal surgery. Acupuncture points are chosen away from the incision site until healing is complete.

### Will TCM help with chronic pain from old adhesions?
Yes. Even adhesions that formed years ago can respond to TCM. Chronic pain often involves a mix of Blood Stagnation and underlying deficiency, and a skilled practitioner can tailor herbs and acupuncture to address both. While old scar tissue may not disappear, reduced inflammation and improved Qi flow can lead to significant pain reduction and better quality of life.

### Do I need to change my diet?
Diet plays a supportive role. In general, TCM recommends warm, cooked foods that are easy to digest to protect the Spleen's energy. You'll want to avoid cold, raw foods, heavy dairy, and greasy items that create dampness and phlegm. Your practitioner will give you personalized guidance based on your specific pattern.

### Can TCM prevent adhesions from forming after future surgery?
While no approach can guarantee prevention, TCM can strengthen your body's healing response and reduce the tendency to form excessive scar tissue. By addressing underlying patterns like Spleen Qi Deficiency or Blood Stagnation before and after surgery, you may lower the risk of problematic adhesions. Many patients use TCM proactively when planning a necessary repeat operation.

### Is it safe to combine TCM herbs with my pain medications?
Generally, yes, but you must inform both your TCM practitioner and your medical doctor. Certain herbs that invigorate blood, such as Dang Gui (当归) and Chuan Xiong (川芎), may interact with anticoagulant or antiplatelet drugs like warfarin or aspirin. Your TCM practitioner can adjust the formula to avoid interactions and ensure safety.
