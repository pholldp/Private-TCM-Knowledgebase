## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนปัจจุบัน (Conventional Medicine View)
### คำอธิบาย (Description)
Western medicine defines a runny nose (rhinorrhea) as excess nasal mucus production, often triggered by allergies, viral infections, cold air, or irritants. It's typically diagnosed through a history and physical exam, sometimes with allergy testing when symptoms are chronic. The discharge can be clear, cloudy, or colored, and may accompany congestion, sneezing, and postnasal drip.

### การรักษาหลัก (Treatments)
Standard treatments include antihistamines, decongestants, and corticosteroid nasal sprays, along with trigger avoidance. For bacterial infections, antibiotics may be used. Chronic allergic rhinitis is sometimes managed with immunotherapy. These approaches aim to reduce symptoms and inflammation.

## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนจีน (Traditional Chinese Medicine View)
### ความเข้าใจเกี่ยวกับการแพทย์แผนจีน (TCM Understanding)
In TCM, the nose is the opening of the Lungs, and the Lungs govern the dispersal and descent of fluids. When the Lungs are weak or attacked by external pathogens like Wind-Cold or Wind-Heat, they lose control over these fluids, and the nose runs. But the Lungs don't work alone - the Spleen transforms and transports moisture, and the Kidneys provide the warmth needed to process fluids. A weakness in any of these organs can cause chronic, watery discharge, while heat in the Lungs thickens mucus into yellow phlegm.
The color and consistency of nasal discharge are diagnostic clues. Clear, watery mucus typically signals cold or deficiency - either an external invasion of Wind-Cold or an internal deficiency of Spleen and Lung Qi, or Kidney Yang. Thick, yellow discharge points to heat, whether from a Wind-Heat invasion or lingering Lung Heat. The timing also matters: a sudden onset after a chill suggests an acute external attack, while a persistent drip that worsens with fatigue or cold weather indicates a deeper internal weakness.
Because the same runny nose can stem from such different root causes, TCM doesn't have a single treatment. Instead, it identifies the pattern - is this an acute invasion that needs to be expelled, or a chronic deficiency that needs to be strengthened? - and tailors acupuncture and herbal formulas accordingly. This is why two people with seemingly identical symptoms may receive completely different treatments.

### การวินิจฉัยโดยแพทย์แผนจีน (Practitioner Diagnosis)
The practitioner starts by asking about the color, thickness, and how long you have had the nasal discharge. Clear, watery mucus points toward deficiency or external cold, while yellow, thick mucus suggests heat. The sudden onset after a chill or a lingering, chronic drip helps separate acute invasions from internal imbalances. Tongue and pulse examination then confirms the specific pattern.
The most common chronic pattern is Spleen and Lung Qi Deficiency (肺脾气虚, fèi pí qì xū). Here the body’s Qi fails to manage fluids, causing a persistently runny nose with clear, watery discharge. You may also feel fatigued, look pale, and have a weak pulse. This pattern often underlies long-standing allergic rhinitis.
If the clear discharge is accompanied by a deep ache in the lower back, cold hands and feet, and a very deep, weak pulse, it points to Kidney and Spleen Yang Deficiency (脾肾阳虚, pí shèn yáng xū). This deeper cold pattern means the body’s warming power is too weak to transform fluids, leading to a thin, watery drip that is worse in cold weather.
An acute attack of clear, watery discharge with lots of sneezing, chills, and a floating tight pulse is Wind-Cold invading the Lungs (风寒袭肺, fēng hán xí fèi). This external pattern comes on suddenly after exposure to wind or cold and reflects the Lung’s inability to disperse and keep fluids in check.
When the discharge turns yellow and thick and appears suddenly with a fever, sore throat, and a floating rapid pulse, it is Wind-Heat invading the Lungs (风热犯肺, fēng rè fàn fèi). The external heat pathogen inflames the nasal passages, causing congestion and yellow mucus.
Yellow, sticky nasal discharge that lingers without obvious external symptoms, but with a red tongue, yellow coating, and rapid pulse, indicates Lung Heat (肺热, fèi rè). Here internal heat stagnation thickens fluids into a persistent, often foul-smelling discharge.
A less common pattern is the Lesser Yang stage (少阳证, shào yáng zhèng), where a lingering pathogen causes nasal discharge that worsens at night. You may also feel chest tightness, a bitter taste in the mouth, and have a wiry pulse. The discharge can be clear or yellow, but the nighttime worsening and the wiry pulse are key differentiators.

### Spleen and Lung Qi Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/spleen-and-lung-qi-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Chronic clear watery nasal discharge, Fatigue and weakness that worsen after activity, Poor appetite, bloating, and loose stools, Pale face without lustre, Easily catches colds
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Cold, raw foods and iced drinks, Overexertion or overwork, Damp or cold weather, Stress and excessive worry
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warmth and warm environments, Rest and adequate sleep, Eating warm cooked meals, Gentle exercise like walking
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  The Lungs govern the nose and are responsible for dispersing fluids. The Spleen transforms and transports moisture. When both organs are Qi-deficient, the Lung cannot hold back nasal secretions and the Spleen fails to manage fluids, so thin clear phlegm accumulates and overflows from the nose.
This is why the discharge is watery and persistent, especially after exertion or in cold weather, which further drains Qi.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale, puffy, tender body with teeth marks and a thin white slippery coating. Pulse: weak and forceless, especially at the right-side Lung and Spleen positions.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Cold and damp weaken Spleen Yang and promote fluid accumulation, while raw foods directly tax the digestive fire, so avoiding them stops the source of phlegm. Overexertion and stress drain Qi, leaving the Lung and Spleen even more depleted, which is why rest and gentle movement are protective. Warmth and cooked foods are easy to digest and support the body's ability to transform and transport fluids, keeping the nose clear.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat warm, cooked meals like soups, stews, and congees. Avoid cold, raw, and greasy foods that burden the Spleen. Gentle daily movement such as walking or tai chi helps Qi flow without depletion. Prioritize rest and keep the neck and upper back covered in windy weather.

### Wind-Cold invading the Lungs
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/wind-cold-invading-the-lungs)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Clear watery nasal discharge, Chills and aversion to cold, No sweating, Sneezing, Headache and body aches, Thin white tongue coating
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Cold weather, drafts, or wind, Cold, raw foods and iced drinks, Overexertion or overwork
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warm fluids, soups, ginger tea, or hot soup, Steam inhalation, Rest and staying warm
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Wind-Cold is an external pathogen that attacks the body's surface and lodges in the Lungs. The Lungs normally diffuse and descend Qi and fluids, but Cold contracts and obstructs this function. Trapped fluids then overflow as a sudden clear, watery nasal discharge, while the blocked defensive Qi causes chills and an aversion to cold.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: normal or slightly pale body with a thin white moist coating. Pulse: floating (fú) and tight (jǐn).
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Wind-Cold invades the body by tightening the pores and constricting the Lung Qi, causing fluids to stagnate and run from the nose. Warmth and sweating open the pores and release the pathogen, while cold and dampness close them further, worsening symptoms. Rest conserves Qi to fight the external invasion.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Drink plenty of warm fluids like fresh ginger tea or hot water with lemon. Avoid cold drinks, raw salads, and icy foods. Use steam inhalation with a few drops of eucalyptus or peppermint oil to open nasal passages. Keep your neck and chest covered and avoid drafts. Rest until the acute phase passes.

### Wind-Heat invading the Lungs
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/wind-heat-invading-the-lungs)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Yellow or turbid nasal discharge, Fever with mild chills, Sore or painful throat, Thirst or dry mouth, Cough with thick yellow phlegm, Red tongue tip with thin yellow coating
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Greasy, spicy, or fried foods, Hot weather or stuffy rooms, Wind exposure, Overexertion or overwork
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Cool fresh air, Warm fluids, soups, ginger tea, or hot soup, Rest, Peppermint tea
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When Wind-Heat invades the body, it attacks the Lungs first because the Lungs open into the nose and govern the skin. The Heat disrupts the Lung’s ability to disperse and descend fluids, causing them to stagnate and turn into thick, yellow mucus that drains from the nose. The body also mounts a fever to fight the external pathogen, which is why you feel warmer than chilled.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red tip, thin yellow coating, body slightly red. Pulse: floating and rapid, especially at the right cun position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Wind-Heat is an external pathogen that thrives on warmth and stagnation. Spicy foods and hot environments add more Heat, worsening the thick yellow discharge. Cool air and rest help the body's defensive Qi push the pathogen out. Warm fluids keep the Lung's fluids moving without adding coldness, which could trap the pathogen.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Drink plenty of warm water and light fluids like chrysanthemum tea. Avoid spicy, greasy, or fried foods that generate heat. Rest and keep the room ventilated but not drafty.

### Lung Heat
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/lung-heat)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Thick yellow or greenish sticky nasal discharge, Feeling of body heat, thirst for cold drinks, Cough with thick yellow phlegm, Sore throat or hot sensation when breathing through the nose
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Greasy, spicy, or fried foods, Hot weather or stuffy rooms, Smoking or polluted air, Emotional stress, frustration, or anger
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Cool air or cold drinks, Rest and calm environment, Light, cooling foods like cucumber
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When Heat builds up inside the Lung meridian, it acts like a simmering pot that thickens the body's fluids. This turns normal mucus into dense, yellow, sticky phlegm. Because the Lung opens into the nose, that heated phlegm rises and comes out as thick yellow nasal discharge. The same Heat also dries out the mouth and throat, making you feel thirsty and irritable.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red body, yellow dry coating, possibly with prickly red papillae on the front third. Pulse: rapid (shu), possibly slippery (hua) or overflowing (hong), especially forceful at the right cun (Lung) position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Lung Heat is an internal excess heat pattern. Heat is aggravated by heating factors like spicy foods, hot environments, and emotional stress (which stirs up fire). Cooling measures, such as cold drinks and cool air, help counteract the heat and soothe the Lung, reducing the transformation of fluids into thick discharge.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Avoid spicy, greasy, and fried foods that generate internal Heat. Drink plenty of cool water and eat cooling foods like pear and cucumber. Keep indoor air clean and use a humidifier if the air is dry. Avoid smoking and second-hand smoke.

### Kidney and Spleen Yang Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/kidney-and-spleen-yang-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Clear, watery nasal discharge that is often profuse, Cold hands and feet, especially below the knees, Sore, aching, and cold sensation in the lower back and knees, Early-morning diarrhea or chronic loose stools with undigested food, Frequent urination with clear, pale urine, Aversion to cold and feeling chilly all over
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Cold weather, drafts, or wind, Cold, raw foods and iced drinks, Overexertion or overwork, Damp living conditions
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warmth and warm environments, Warm fluids, soups, ginger tea, or hot soup, Moxibustion on the lower back, Rest and lying down
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  The Kidneys are the root of the body's warmth and govern the transformation of fluids. The Spleen transports and transforms water. When the Yang of both organs is weak, they cannot provide enough heat to process the body's liquids. This leaves cold, unprocessed fluid to accumulate and overflow upward as a persistent, clear, watery nasal discharge.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale, swollen, puffy body with teeth marks and a white slippery coating. Pulse: deep, weak, and slow, especially feeble in the rear (Chi) positions.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: This pattern is rooted in a deficiency of warming Yang. Cold and dampness further deplete the body's already weak fire, making fluids accumulate and overflow as nasal discharge. Warmth, warming foods, and rest help preserve and gently restore Yang, allowing the body to transform and transport fluids properly again.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat warm, cooked foods like soups, stews, and congees. Add warming spices such as ginger, cinnamon, and black pepper. Avoid raw, cold, and frozen foods, as well as iced drinks. Keep the lower back, abdomen, and feet warm, especially in cold weather. Gentle exercise like walking or tai chi can help move Qi, but avoid overexertion. Moxibustion on the lower back and lower abdomen can be very helpful.

### Lesser Yang stage
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/lesser-yang-stage)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Nasal discharge worse at night or early morning, Bitter taste in the mouth, Chest tightness or rib-side fullness, Alternating chills and fever, Irritability and dizziness
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Emotional stress, frustration, or anger, Cold weather, drafts, or wind, Greasy, spicy, or fried foods, Overexertion or overwork
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Rest and staying warm, Light, simple meals, Gentle breathing exercises, Keeping the neck and shoulders covered
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  In the Lesser Yang stage, a pathogen lingers in the body's 'half-exterior half-interior' layer, where it gets trapped between the surface and the deep interior. The Gallbladder channel, which runs through the sides of the head and face, becomes congested and stagnant. At night, when the body's Yang Qi retreats inward, the pathogen becomes more active, causing nasal discharge that may alternate with dryness. The stagnant Qi can also generate heat, producing a bitter taste in the mouth and a feeling of tightness in the chest.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: normal or slightly red body, possible red sides, thin white or thin yellow coating. Pulse: wiry (弦 xián) and fine.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Stress and frustration cause Liver Qi to stagnate, which directly affects the Lesser Yang and makes the trapped pathogen more active, worsening nasal discharge. Cold drafts further obstruct the half-exterior half-interior layer, while warmth helps Qi flow freely. Greasy foods create dampness that clogs the channels, and overwork depletes Qi, leaving the body less able to expel the lingering pathogen. Rest and light eating allow Qi to recover and harmonize the Lesser Yang.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Avoid heavy, greasy foods that create dampness and clog the Lesser Yang channels. Eat light, regular meals and include gently cooked vegetables. Keep the neck and shoulders covered to protect the Gallbladder channel from drafts. Gentle movement like walking or stretching helps Qi flow smoothly.

## คำถามที่พบบ่อย (Frequently Asked Questions)
### Can TCM stop a runny nose from allergies?
Yes. Allergic rhinitis in TCM is often rooted in Spleen and Lung Qi Deficiency or Kidney Yang Deficiency, where the body's defensive Qi is too weak to handle normal environmental triggers. Herbs and acupuncture work to strengthen these organ systems, reducing sensitivity and the overflow of clear mucus. Over time, many patients need less medication and experience fewer flare-ups.

### How long does it take for Chinese herbs to work for a runny nose?
For an acute cold with a runny nose, herbal formulas can bring relief within 24-48 hours. For chronic, long-standing nasal discharge, you'll typically notice a difference in 2-4 weeks, with more substantial and lasting improvement after 6-12 weeks of consistent treatment.

### Is acupuncture effective for a constantly runny nose?
Yes. Acupuncture uses local points like Yingxiang LI-20 to directly open the nasal passages, and distal points like Hegu LI-4 and Zusanli ST-36 to address the underlying organ imbalance. Many patients feel their nose dry up and breathing improve during the session itself, and regular treatments reduce the frequency and severity of discharge.

### What does clear vs yellow nasal discharge mean in TCM?
Clear, watery discharge usually indicates cold or deficiency - either an external Wind-Cold invasion or an internal weakness of the Lungs, Spleen, or Kidneys. Yellow, thick, or sticky discharge signals heat, such as Wind-Heat or Lung Heat, where fluids have been thickened by internal warmth. This distinction is crucial for choosing the right herbs and acupuncture points.

### Can children use TCM for a runny nose?
Yes, TCM is very gentle for children. Pediatric acupuncture often uses non-insertive techniques or very shallow needling, and herbal formulas are prescribed in child-appropriate doses. Always consult a qualified TCM practitioner experienced in pediatrics to ensure safety and proper dosing.

### Can I take TCM herbs with my allergy medication?
Generally, yes, but you must inform both your doctor and TCM practitioner about all medications you are taking. Some herbs may interact with sedating antihistamines or decongestants. A qualified practitioner will choose a formula that complements your current treatment without causing unwanted side effects.

### What foods should I avoid if I have a runny nose?
Dairy products, cold and raw foods, and greasy or fried items tend to create phlegm and dampness in the body, making nasal discharge worse. Instead, favor warm, cooked meals and drink warm water or ginger tea. For chronic clear discharge, warming spices like cinnamon and cardamom can be especially helpful.

## การวิจัยทางวิทยาศาสตร์สมัยใหม่ (Modern Scientific Research)
1. **Acupuncture for allergic rhinitis** ([ลิงก์](https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD006475.pub3))
   *วารสารวิชาการ: Chen J, et al. Acupuncture for allergic rhinitis. Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews 2016, Issue 2. Art. No.: CD006475.*
   A Cochrane systematic review including 30 randomized controlled trials found that acupuncture significantly reduced nasal symptom scores and improved quality of life compared to sham acupuncture and antihistamines, with no serious adverse events.

2. **Acupuncture in Patients with Seasonal Allergic Rhinitis: A Randomized Trial** ([ลิงก์](https://doi.org/10.7326/0003-4819-158-4-201302190-00002))
   *วารสารวิชาการ: Brinkhaus B, et al. Acupuncture in Patients with Seasonal Allergic Rhinitis: A Randomized Trial. Annals of Internal Medicine 2013;158(4):225-234.*
   This multicenter RCT of 422 patients showed that acupuncture plus rescue medication led to greater improvement in rhinitis-specific quality of life and reduced antihistamine use compared to sham acupuncture plus rescue medication.

3. **Chinese herbal medicine for allergic rhinitis: a systematic review and meta-analysis** ([ลิงก์](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jep.2018.04.019))
   *วารสารวิชาการ: Wang S, et al. Chinese herbal medicine for allergic rhinitis: A systematic review and meta-analysis. Journal of Ethnopharmacology 2018;216:47-56.*
   A meta-analysis of 21 RCTs concluded that Chinese herbal medicine, particularly formulas targeting Spleen and Lung Qi Deficiency, improved nasal symptom scores and reduced relapse rates compared to Western medication alone, though study quality was moderate.
