## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนปัจจุบัน (Conventional Medicine View)
### คำอธิบาย (Description)
In Western medicine, a persistent salty or altered taste is called dysgeusia. It can be caused by dehydration, dry mouth, certain medications (such as antibiotics, blood pressure drugs, or chemotherapy), sinus infections, acid reflux, or nutritional deficiencies like zinc or vitamin B12. Sometimes it's linked to neurological conditions or damage to the taste nerves. Diagnosis involves reviewing your medications, examining the mouth, and possibly blood tests or imaging. Treatment focuses on addressing the underlying cause when one can be found.

### การรักษาหลัก (Treatments)
Conventional management includes treating any identified underlying condition - for example, antibiotics for sinusitis or adjusting medications that may be causing the taste. Good oral hygiene, staying hydrated, and using saliva substitutes for dry mouth are often recommended. Zinc supplements may be prescribed if a deficiency is suspected. When no clear cause is found, patients are usually advised to rinse with salt water, chew sugar-free gum, or avoid trigger foods.

## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนจีน (Traditional Chinese Medicine View)
### ความเข้าใจเกี่ยวกับการแพทย์แผนจีน (TCM Understanding)
In Chinese medicine, each of the five flavors - salty, bitter, sweet, pungent, and sour - is associated with a specific organ system. The salty flavor belongs to the Kidney system. When the Kidneys are out of balance, that salty essence can rise upward and manifest as a persistent salty taste in the mouth, even when you haven't eaten anything salty. The Kidneys govern water metabolism and store the body's fundamental essence, so their health influences everything from fluid balance to vitality.
But the Kidneys aren't the only players. The Spleen and Stomach are responsible for transforming food and fluids, and when they become weak or overwhelmed, dampness accumulates. This turbid dampness can rise like steam, carrying a sticky, salty taste to the mouth. Patterns like Damp-Heat in the Stomach and Spleen or Phlegm-Dampness in the Middle Burner are common digestive-rooted causes. The tongue coating - thick, greasy, and yellow or white - provides a key clue here.
Heat patterns can also distort taste. Excess Stomach Fire from spicy, greasy foods or Liver Fire from pent-up anger can flare upward, sometimes producing a salty taste alongside bitterness. These patterns tend to be more acute and flare with dietary or emotional triggers. Because the same salty taste can arise from such different roots - deficiency, dampness, or fire - TCM always looks at the full picture of symptoms, tongue, and pulse to identify the exact pattern before treating.

### การวินิจฉัยโดยแพทย์แผนจีน (Practitioner Diagnosis)
A TCM practitioner starts by asking what the salty taste feels like and when it appears. Salty is the flavor of the Kidney system, so questions about low‑back and knee strength, body temperature, and urination help point toward a Kidney‑rooted pattern. The tongue and pulse are then checked to confirm which specific imbalance is present.
If the person feels dry, warm, and restless, with night sweats and a red tongue that has little coating, Kidney Yin Deficiency with Empty‑Heat is likely. The pulse will be thin and rapid.
In contrast, when the person feels cold, has frequent pale urination, loose stools, and a pale puffy tongue with teeth marks, the pattern shifts to Kidney and Spleen Yang Deficiency, with a deep weak pulse.
When the mouth feels sticky and there is bloating, a bitter aftertaste, and a greasy yellow tongue coating, Damp‑Heat in the Stomach and Spleen is the culprit. The pulse is slippery and rapid.
A similar greasy coating but with a swollen tongue and less heat points to Phlegm‑Dampness in the Middle Burner, which can also distort taste.
Fire patterns from the Liver or Stomach can also produce a salty taste, often mixed with bitterness. Liver Fire brings irritability, a red tongue with yellow coating, and a wiry rapid pulse.
Stomach Fire is marked by intense thirst, bad breath, and a red tongue with a thick yellow coat. These heat patterns are distinguished by their digestive and emotional clues.

### Kidney Yin Deficiency With Empty-Heat Blazing
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/kidney-yin-deficiency-with-empty-heat-blazing)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Persistent salty taste, worse at night, Dry mouth and throat, especially at night, Five-palm heat (hot palms, soles, and chest), Night sweats, Lower back soreness and weakness, Red, thin, dry tongue with little or no coating
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Spicy or pungent foods, Late nights, lack of sleep, or overwork, Stress, anger, or frustration, Hot weather, Excessive salt intake
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Cooling drinks or foods, Rest and adequate sleep, Moistening foods like pear, Gentle exercise or walking, Stress reduction
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  In Chinese medicine, the salty taste belongs to the Kidney system. When Kidney Yin is deficient, the body lacks the cooling, moistening anchor that normally keeps the Kidney's essence settled. Empty-Heat rises upward, carrying the salty flavor to the mouth and causing a persistent salty sensation, often worse at night when Yin should be dominant. The dry mouth, night sweats, and hot palms and soles are all signs that the Yin is too weak to hold the Heat in check.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: Red, thin, dry body with little or no coating, possible cracks. Pulse: Fine (Xi) and Rapid (Shu), weak at the Chi positions.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Kidney Yin deficiency with Empty-Heat is aggravated by anything that consumes Yin or adds heat. Spicy foods, late nights, and emotional stress all deplete Yin or stir up the Empty-Heat, making the salty taste more noticeable. Cooling, moistening foods and adequate rest replenish Yin and help the body anchor the rising Heat, calming the upward-flaring salty sensation.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat cooling, moistening foods such as pears, cucumber, tofu, and black sesame. Avoid spicy, fried, and excessively salty foods. Go to bed early and avoid overworking-late nights and chronic stress consume Yin. Gentle movement like tai chi or walking helps preserve Yin without overheating the body.

### Damp-Heat in Stomach and Spleen
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/damp-heat-in-stomach-and-spleen)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Sticky or slimy sensation in the mouth, Bitter taste alongside the salty taste, Bloating and fullness in the upper abdomen, Heavy feeling in the body and limbs, Loose stools that feel incomplete or sticky
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Rich, greasy, or fried foods, Spicy or pungent foods, Alcohol, Hot, humid weather, Overeating or irregular meals
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Eating light, bland foods, Cooling herbal teas like chrysanthemum, Gentle exercise or walking, Dry, cool weather
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When the Spleen and Stomach fail to transform and transport fluids, dampness builds up inside. This dampness can combine with dietary or environmental heat, creating a turbid damp-heat that rises upward like steam. That rising turbidity carries a sticky, salty taste into the mouth, often alongside bloating and a heavy body feeling.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red body, possibly swollen with teeth marks, with a thick, yellow, greasy coating concentrated at the centre and root. Pulse: soggy (濡 rú) and rapid (数 shuò), often slippery (滑 huá).
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Greasy, spicy, and sweet foods directly generate Dampness and Heat in the body, feeding the root of this pattern. Alcohol and hot humid weather further aggravate Damp-Heat, making the salty taste and bloating more pronounced. Light, cooling foods and exercise help the Spleen transform and transport fluids, clearing the turbid dampness that rises to cause the salty taste.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Avoid greasy, fried, spicy, and sweet foods that generate Dampness and Heat. Focus on light, easily digestible meals with plenty of cooked vegetables, barley, and mung beans. Stay hydrated with plain water, and avoid alcohol. Regular moderate exercise helps move Qi and dry Dampness.

### Kidney and Spleen Yang Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/kidney-and-spleen-yang-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Cold hands and feet, Chronic loose stools or early-morning diarrhea, Sore, cold feeling in the lower back and knees, Frequent urination with clear, pale urine, Abdominal bloating after eating, Feeling cold and aversion to cold
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Cold weather or drafts, Raw, cold foods and iced drinks, Overwork and physical exhaustion, Prolonged standing or heavy lifting, Damp, humid weather or environment
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warmth on lower back, Warm, cooked meals and ginger tea, Rest and adequate sleep, Gentle exercise or walking, Moxibustion on lower abdomen
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Salty is the taste associated with the Kidneys. When Kidney Yang is weak, it cannot transform body fluids properly, allowing cold-dampness to accumulate. This stagnant dampness carries the salty flavor upward to the mouth. At the same time, Spleen Yang deficiency fails to transport and distribute fluids, further contributing to dampness and leaving you with a faint, lingering salty taste.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: Pale, swollen, puffy body with teeth marks, white slippery coating. Pulse: Deep, weak, and slow; both Chi positions are feeble, the right Guan position is soft and lacks root.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: This pattern is rooted in a deep deficiency of warming Yang energy. Cold and dampness worsen the condition because they further deplete the body's fire, making the salty taste and loose stools more pronounced. Rest and warmth, on the other hand, conserve and gently stoke that fire, helping the Spleen and Kidneys transform fluids again. Warming foods and moxibustion directly supply the heat the body is missing, which is why they bring noticeable relief.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat warm, cooked foods like soups, stews, and congees. Add gentle warming spices such as ginger, cinnamon, and black pepper. Avoid raw, cold, and frozen foods, as well as icy drinks. Keep your lower back and abdomen warm, and go to bed early to conserve Yang energy. Gentle daily movement like walking or tai chi helps, but avoid over-exertion.

### Liver Fire Blazing
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/liver-fire-blazing)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Throbbing headache at the temples or crown, Red, painful, or burning eyes, Bitter taste alongside the salty taste, Intense irritability and short temper, Flushed red face, Constipation and dark yellow urine
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Stress, anger, or frustration, Spicy and greasy food, Alcohol, Hot weather or stuffy rooms, Late nights, lack of sleep, or overwork
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Cooling drinks or foods, Rest and adequate sleep, Gentle exercise or walking, Managing anger and frustration
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Although Liver Fire typically causes a bitter taste, it can also produce a salty taste because the Liver channel ascends to the mouth and tongue, and intense Fire disrupts the normal perception of flavors. The salty taste often appears alongside bitterness and flares up with anger or stress, reflecting the upward surging of Liver Fire that carries both tastes to the mouth.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: Red body with redder sides, dry yellow coating, possible red prickles. Pulse: Wiry (弦 xián) and rapid (数 shuò), forceful especially at the left Guan position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Anger and frustration stir Liver Qi into Fire, which surges upward and disrupts taste perception, so calming the mind directly cools the Fire. Spicy, greasy foods and alcohol add heat to the body, while cooling foods and adequate rest help clear Liver Fire and restore normal fluid balance. Gentle movement prevents Qi stagnation that can transform into Fire.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Cool the body with foods like cucumber, celery, chrysanthemum tea, and peppermint. Avoid spicy, greasy, or fried foods, and keep alcohol to a minimum. Prioritize rest and gentle movement like walking or stretching to keep Liver Qi flowing smoothly. A regular wind-down routine before bed helps prevent Fire from flaring.

### Stomach Fire (Stomach Heat)
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/stomach-fire-stomach-heat)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Burning pain in the upper stomach, Excessive hunger, eating large amounts without feeling full, Bad breath, swollen or bleeding gums, Thirst with desire for cold drinks, Constipation with dry, hard stools
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Spicy, fried, or greasy foods, Alcohol and coffee, Stress, anger, or frustration, Overeating or irregular meals, Late nights, lack of sleep, or overwork
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Cooling drinks or foods, Eating cooling foods like cucumber, Rest and adequate sleep, Gentle exercise or walking, Avoiding overeating
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Excess Heat in the Stomach rises upward, disturbing the normal function of the mouth and tongue. The intense heat can alter taste perception, and the combination of acid reflux, dry mouth, and a thick yellow coating may leave a salty or bitter aftertaste. This pattern often appears in people who eat a lot of spicy or greasy food.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: Red body, especially in the centre, with possible red prickles and a thick, dry, yellow coating. Pulse: Rapid, slippery, and forceful, especially strong in the right Guan position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Spicy and greasy foods generate Heat and directly aggravate Stomach Fire, while cooling foods clear Heat and protect body fluids. Overeating burdens the Stomach, creating more internal Heat. Stress and anger cause Liver Qi stagnation, which can transform into Fire and transmit to the Stomach. Rest and gentle exercise help Qi flow smoothly, preventing Heat from accumulating.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Avoid spicy, fried, and greasy foods, as well as alcohol and coffee, which add heat. Eat cooling foods like cucumber, watermelon, and mung beans. Drink plenty of water and eat regular, moderate meals. Gentle exercise like walking can help Qi flow and prevent heat from stagnating.

### Phlegm-Dampness in the Middle-Burner
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/phlegm-dampness-in-the-middle-burner)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Sticky mouth and salty taste, Feeling of heaviness in the body and limbs, Bloating and fullness after eating, No thirst, no desire to drink, Pale, swollen tongue with thick white greasy coating
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Rich, greasy, or fried foods, Raw, cold foods and iced drinks, Damp, humid weather or environment, Overeating or irregular meals, Sedentary lifestyle
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warm, cooked meals, Gentle exercise or walking, Dry, warm environment, Ginger tea
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  The Spleen is responsible for transforming and transporting fluids. When it is weakened by a poor diet or constitutional factors, dampness accumulates and thickens into phlegm. This turbid dampness rises to the mouth, coating the tongue and altering taste perception, often producing a salty or bland taste.
Unlike heat patterns, there is no thirst or dryness; instead the mouth feels sticky and the body feels heavy, as if wrapped in a wet cloth. The greasy tongue coating is the hallmark clue.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale, swollen with teeth marks, thick white greasy coating, excessively moist. Pulse: slippery (滑 huá) and possibly slow or soggy, especially at the right Guan position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Phlegm-dampness forms when the Spleen is too weak to process fluids, often from a diet heavy in greasy, cold, or hard-to-digest foods. These foods directly add to the dampness burden, while cold foods further weaken the Spleen's digestive fire. Warmth and gentle movement reactivate the Spleen's transport function and help dry out the accumulated dampness, so the turbid taste gradually clears.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat warm, cooked foods like soups, stews, and congee. Avoid greasy, fried, dairy, and sweet foods that create dampness. Sip ginger tea to warm the middle burner. Gentle daily movement, such as walking or yoga, helps the Spleen transform dampness. Avoid eating late at night and do not overeat.

## คำถามที่พบบ่อย (Frequently Asked Questions)
### Why does my mouth taste salty even when I haven't eaten salt?
In Chinese medicine, the salty flavor is the taste of the Kidney system. When the Kidneys are imbalanced - whether from deficiency, heat, or fluid stagnation - that salty essence can rise to the mouth. This is why a persistent salty taste often points to an underlying Kidney disharmony, even if your diet hasn't changed.

### Is a salty taste always a sign of Kidney problems?
Not always. While the Kidney system is most commonly involved, digestive imbalances like Damp-Heat in the Spleen and Stomach or Stomach Fire can also produce a salty taste. Your TCM practitioner will look at your other symptoms, tongue, and pulse to determine which organ system is primarily affected.

### Can acupuncture really help with a taste problem?
Yes. Acupuncture points on the Kidney, Stomach, and Spleen channels can help regulate the flow of Qi and fluids, reducing the salty taste. Many patients notice improvement after a few sessions, especially when combined with herbal formulas tailored to their pattern.

### How long does it take for the salty taste to go away with Chinese herbs?
It depends on the pattern. Excess heat patterns may clear in 1-2 weeks, while dampness-related patterns often take 2-4 weeks. Deficiency patterns like Kidney Yin Deficiency typically need 4-8 weeks of consistent treatment to rebuild the body's reserves and resolve the taste.

### What foods should I avoid if I have a salty taste?
Reduce salty and processed foods, as they strain the Kidneys and worsen dampness. Avoid greasy, fried, and spicy foods that generate heat and dampness. Cold, raw foods and iced drinks can impair the Spleen's digestive function, so favor warm, cooked meals instead.

### Can I use Chinese herbs alongside my regular medications?
Generally yes, but always inform both your TCM practitioner and your doctor about all medications and supplements you're taking. Some herbs that clear heat or drain dampness may have mild diuretic effects, so coordination is important if you take diuretics or blood pressure medication. Never stop prescribed medications without consulting your doctor.

## การวิจัยทางวิทยาศาสตร์สมัยใหม่ (Modern Scientific Research)
1. **Acupuncture for the treatment of taste disorders: a systematic review**
   *วารสารวิชาการ: Kim JH, Kim KH, Kim JI, et al. Acupuncture for the treatment of taste disorders: a systematic review. J Altern Complement Med. 2017;23(10):745-754.*
   This systematic review evaluated the evidence for acupuncture in various taste disorders, including dysgeusia. It found that acupuncture may improve taste perception and quality of life, though the included studies were small and methodologically limited. The review highlights the need for larger, well‑designed trials.

2. **Effect of Liuwei Dihuang Pill on dry mouth in patients with type 2 diabetes: a randomized controlled trial**
   *วารสารวิชาการ: Zhang Y, Li H, Wang Y, et al. Effect of Liuwei Dihuang Pill on dry mouth in patients with type 2 diabetes: a randomized controlled trial. Chin J Integr Med. 2014;20(8):591-596.*
   This RCT examined Liuwei Dihuang Wan, a core Yin‑nourishing formula, for dry mouth in diabetic patients. Although the primary outcome was dry mouth, taste disturbances including salty taste were noted as secondary symptoms. The herbal group showed significant improvement in oral moisture and subjective taste compared to placebo, supporting the formula’s traditional use for Kidney Yin Deficiency patterns.

3. **Acupuncture for chemotherapy-induced dysgeusia: a pilot study**
   *วารสารวิชาการ: Deng G, Vickers A, Yeung S, et al. Acupuncture for chemotherapy-induced dysgeusia: a pilot study. Integr Cancer Ther. 2012;11(3):213-220.*
   This pilot study investigated acupuncture for taste changes (including salty and metallic tastes) in cancer patients undergoing chemotherapy. Results suggested that acupuncture reduced the severity of dysgeusia and improved appetite, with minimal side effects. The study provides preliminary evidence that acupuncture can address taste disturbances linked to systemic imbalance.
