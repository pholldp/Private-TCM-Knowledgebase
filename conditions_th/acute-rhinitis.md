## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนปัจจุบัน (Conventional Medicine View)
### คำอธิบาย (Description)
Acute rhinitis is the medical term for inflammation of the nasal mucous membranes, most often triggered by a viral upper respiratory infection - the common cold. The lining of the nose swells, producing excess mucus, leading to congestion, sneezing, and a runny or stuffy nose. Symptoms usually peak within two to three days and resolve within a week to ten days.
Diagnosis is based on symptoms alone; laboratory tests are rarely needed unless a secondary bacterial infection is suspected. Conventional treatment focuses on symptom relief while the immune system clears the virus.

### การรักษาหลัก (Treatments)
Standard care includes over-the-counter decongestant sprays or tablets, antihistamines for sneezing and runny nose, and pain relievers for headache or sinus pressure. Saline nasal rinses and steam inhalation are also recommended. Antibiotics are not effective against viral infections and are prescribed only if a bacterial sinusitis develops.

## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนจีน (Traditional Chinese Medicine View)
### ความเข้าใจเกี่ยวกับการแพทย์แผนจีน (TCM Understanding)
TCM understands acute rhinitis primarily through the Lung system. The Lungs open into the nose and govern the body's Defensive Qi, the protective energy that circulates at the surface. When an external pathogen - Wind, often carrying Cold, Heat, or Dampness - attacks, it first disturbs this Defensive Qi and then obstructs the Lung's ability to diffuse and descend. The nose, as the Lung's opening, becomes congested because Qi and fluids cannot move freely.
The key to TCM diagnosis lies in the details of the discharge and the accompanying whole-body sensations. If Wind-Cold invades, the pathogen tightens and blocks the Lung's function, producing clear, watery mucus, chills, and a floating tight pulse. Wind-Heat scorches the fluids, turning the discharge yellow and thick, with fever and a sore throat. Wind-Damp brings a heavy, sticky white mucus and a foggy head, as if wrapped in a damp cloth.
Because the same virus can create different patterns depending on a person's constitution and the environment, two people with the same cold may need completely different herbal formulas. One might require warming, dispersing herbs, while another needs cooling, heat-clearing ones. This is why TCM does not have a single “cold remedy” but instead tailors treatment to the exact presentation.

### การวินิจฉัยโดยแพทย์แผนจีน (Practitioner Diagnosis)
A TCM practitioner first asks about the quality of your nasal discharge and how your whole body feels. The color, thickness, and temperature sensations are the earliest clues that steer the diagnosis toward one of the three main acute patterns.
When Wind-Cold invades the Lungs, the mucus is clear and watery, like thin egg white. You feel distinctly chilly, may shiver, and often have no desire to sweat. A headache and body aches are common. The tongue looks pale with a thin white coat, and the pulse feels floating and tight - as if it is bobbing near the surface but held taut by cold.
If Wind-Heat is the culprit, the discharge turns yellow and thick, sometimes sticky. Fever is more prominent than chills, and a sore or scratchy throat is a telltale sign. The tongue tip is redder than usual with a thin yellow coat, and the pulse is floating and rapid, reflecting the heat stirring inside.
In a Wind-Damp pattern, the mucus is thick, white, and sticky, often leaving a heavy, foggy sensation in the head. You may feel a dull ache around the forehead or cheeks, as if wearing a tight hat. The tongue coat becomes greasy and white, and the pulse feels soft or slippery, like a finger gliding through wet clay - a direct signal that dampness is clogging the channels.

### Wind-Cold invading the Lungs
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/wind-cold-invading-the-lungs)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Clear, watery nasal discharge, Chills more prominent than fever, Absence of sweating, Headache and body aches, Itchy throat and sneezing
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Cold or windy weather, Iced drinks and raw foods, Overexertion or lack of sleep, Damp, cold environments
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warm soup or ginger tea, Rest and sleep, Warmth and covering up, Gentle steaming or warm compress on the nose
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  The Lungs open into the nose and govern the body's Defensive Qi at the surface. When Wind and Cold attack, they tighten and block the Lung's diffusing function, trapping Qi and fluids in the nasal passages. The result is a sudden stuffy nose with thin, clear discharge, a heavy head, and an unmistakable chilliness that makes you want to bundle up.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: normal or slightly pale body with a thin, white, moist coating. Pulse: floating (fú) and tight (jǐn), easily felt at the surface and tense like a rope.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Wind-Cold constricts the Lung's Defensive Qi and freezes the nasal passages, so anything that warms and relaxes the exterior - like hot soup, rest, and covering up - helps the Qi flow again. Cold and wind keep the pores shut and the pathogen trapped, while overexertion drains the very Qi that tries to push the invader out.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat warm, cooked foods like ginger-scallion soup or congee. Avoid cold drinks and raw foods that chill the interior. Rest and keep the back of the neck covered to protect the Defensive Qi from further Wind invasion.

### Wind-Heat invading the Lungs
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/wind-heat-invading-the-lungs)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Thick yellow or turbid nasal discharge, Sore, painful throat that may look red, Thirst and dry mouth, Fever more prominent than chills, Slight sweating
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Spicy, fried, or greasy food, Hot, stuffy environments, Overexertion or lack of sleep, Smoky or polluted air
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Cool drinks like mint tea, Rest in a quiet, airy room, Fresh, cool air, Light, non-greasy meals
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Wind-Heat is an external pathogen that enters through the nose and mouth and attacks the Lungs, which open into the nose. The Heat scorches the Lung's fluids and turns the normal mucus thick and yellow, while the Wind component blocks the free flow of Lung Qi, causing nasal congestion. Because Heat rises, you also get a sore throat, thirst, and a fever that feels stronger than any chills.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red tip, thin yellow coating, body slightly red. Pulse: floating (fú) and rapid (shuò).
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Wind-Heat is a hot, exterior pathogen. Spicy and greasy foods create more internal Heat, which combines with the external pathogen and worsens the sore throat and yellow discharge. Hot, stuffy rooms trap Heat and prevent the body from releasing it through the skin. Rest and cool drinks help the body's defensive Qi clear the pathogen by gently cooling and allowing the pores to open just enough to let the Wind-Heat out without exhausting the system.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Drink plenty of cool or room-temperature fluids like mint tea, chrysanthemum tea, or pear juice. Avoid spicy, fried, and greasy foods that add internal Heat. Rest in a well-ventilated room and keep your neck and chest lightly covered to avoid catching a chill while sweating.

### Wind-Damp
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/wind-damp)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Thick, sticky white nasal discharge, Head feels heavy, as if wrapped in a wet cloth, Nasal congestion worsens in damp or rainy weather, Body heaviness and muscle aches, White greasy tongue coating
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Damp or rainy weather, Cold raw foods, Dairy products, Sedentary lifestyle, Exposure to wind and damp
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warm, dry environment, Hot ginger tea, Gentle movement, Avoiding dairy and greasy foods, Steam inhalation
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When Wind and Dampness invade the body together, Wind disturbs the Lung's ability to open the nose, while Dampness is heavy and turbid, settling in the nasal passages. This creates a thick, sticky white discharge that feels stuck, along with a sensation of heaviness in the head, as if wrapped in a wet cloth. The obstruction is more persistent than in dry Wind patterns, and symptoms often worsen in damp weather because external moisture adds to the internal Dampness clogging the nose.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: normal or slightly pale body with a white greasy coating. Pulse: floating and soggy (濡, rú) or slippery.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Wind-Damp is an external pathogen that thrives in damp environments; cold and damp foods create internal dampness that worsens the thick, sticky nasal discharge. Warmth and movement help disperse dampness and restore the Lung's dispersing function, while steam and warm drinks thin the mucus and open the nose.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Keep warm and dry, and use a humidifier only if the air is dry; otherwise, avoid adding moisture. Eat light, warm foods like congee with ginger and scallions. Avoid dairy, greasy foods, and cold raw salads, which create internal Dampness. Gentle exercise like walking or tai chi helps move Qi and drain Dampness. Steam inhalation with a few drops of eucalyptus oil can temporarily clear the nasal passages.

## คำถามที่พบบ่อย (Frequently Asked Questions)
### Can acupuncture help a stuffy nose from a cold?
Yes, acupuncture can be very effective for acute nasal congestion. Specific points like Yingxiang (LI-20) beside the nose, Hegu (LI-4) on the hand, and Fengchi (GB-20) at the back of the head are used to expel Wind and open the nasal passages. Many patients feel their nose start to clear even during the session. The effect is often immediate, though it may need to be repeated over a few days for lasting relief.

### How quickly can Chinese herbs work for acute rhinitis?
When the correct formula is matched to your pattern, you may notice improvement within a few doses - often the first day. Herbs like Jing Jie and Fang Feng for Wind-Cold, or Jin Yin Hua and Lian Qiao for Wind-Heat, directly address the pathogen. Full resolution usually takes 3 to 5 days. The key is taking the herbs at the very first sign of symptoms, ideally within hours of feeling unwell.

### Can I take TCM herbs with over-the-counter cold medicine?
Generally, yes, but it's wise to space them out by at least one to two hours. Avoid combining herbs with medications that have similar actions - for example, a decongestant and a strong dispersing herbal formula might overstimulate. Always tell both your TCM practitioner and your pharmacist what you are taking. If you are on prescription blood thinners or have high blood pressure, consult your doctor before using any new herbs.

### What foods should I avoid when I have a cold with nasal congestion?
Dairy products, greasy foods, and sugar can increase phlegm and dampness, making congestion worse. Ice-cold drinks and raw foods can also trap the pathogen. Instead, favor warm soups, ginger tea for chills, or mint tea for a feverish feeling. Light, easily digested meals support the body's energy to fight off the pathogen.

### Does TCM work for sinus congestion that lingers after a cold?
Yes, TCM is particularly helpful for lingering congestion or post-viral sinusitis. When a cold isn't fully cleared, residual Dampness or Phlegm can settle in the sinuses, leading to ongoing stuffiness, facial pressure, or thick discharge. Herbal formulas and acupuncture can help drain dampness, open the sinus passages, and strengthen the Lung and Spleen to prevent recurrence.

### Is steam inhalation or essential oils safe with acupuncture?
Absolutely. Steam with eucalyptus or peppermint can complement acupuncture by moistening and opening the nasal passages. Just avoid applying strong oils directly to the skin right before a session, as they may interfere with the practitioner's ability to palpate points. A gentle steam at home is a wonderful supportive measure.

## การวิจัยทางวิทยาศาสตร์สมัยใหม่ (Modern Scientific Research)
1. **Chinese medicinal herbs for the common cold** ([ลิงก์](https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD004782.pub3))
   *วารสารวิชาการ: Wu T, Zhang J, Qiu Y, Xie L, Liu GJ. Chinese medicinal herbs for the common cold. Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews 2011, Issue 7. Art. No.: CD004782.*
   This Cochrane review assessed 17 randomized trials of Chinese herbs for the common cold. It found some evidence that herbs may reduce symptom duration, but the quality of trials was low, and no definitive conclusions could be drawn.

2. **Acupuncture for acute uncomplicated respiratory tract infection: a randomised controlled trial**
   *วารสารวิชาการ: Alraek T, Baerheim A, Birch S. Acupuncture for acute uncomplicated respiratory tract infection: a randomised controlled trial. British Journal of General Practice, 2002; 52(474): 22-26.*
   In this trial, 100 patients with acute respiratory infection were randomized to acupuncture or sham. The acupuncture group had significantly lower symptom scores and shorter illness duration, suggesting acupuncture may be an effective treatment for acute rhinitis and related symptoms.
