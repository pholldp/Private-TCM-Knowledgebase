## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนปัจจุบัน (Conventional Medicine View)
### คำอธิบาย (Description)
A gastric ulcer is an open sore in the lining of the stomach. In conventional medicine, it's most commonly caused by infection with Helicobacter pylori bacteria or long-term use of non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs (NSAIDs) like ibuprofen. The hallmark symptom is a burning or gnawing pain in the upper abdomen, often occurring between meals or at night, and sometimes relieved by eating or antacids.
Diagnosis is typically made through endoscopy, where a camera is passed down the throat to directly view the ulcer and take biopsies. While acid-suppressing medications can heal the ulcer, the underlying vulnerability of the stomach lining often remains.

### การรักษาหลัก (Treatments)
Standard treatment includes proton pump inhibitors (PPIs) like omeprazole to reduce stomach acid, along with antibiotics if H. pylori is present. Antacids or H2 blockers may provide symptom relief. Patients are advised to avoid NSAIDs, alcohol, and spicy foods. Treatment usually lasts 4-8 weeks, and repeat endoscopy may be done to confirm healing.

## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนจีน (Traditional Chinese Medicine View)
### ความเข้าใจเกี่ยวกับการแพทย์แผนจีน (TCM Understanding)
In TCM, the Stomach is seen as a pot that cooks and breaks down food, relying on a steady downward flow of Qi to move its contents through the digestive tract. When this downward movement is disrupted - by emotional stress, poor diet, or constitutional weakness - Qi stagnates, heat builds, or the lining becomes undernourished, and an ulcer can form. The location of the ulcer is in the Stomach, but the root cause often lies in the Liver or Spleen.
The Liver is responsible for the smooth flow of Qi throughout the body. When stress, frustration, or anger cause Liver Qi to stagnate, it often 'invades' the Stomach horizontally, disrupting its normal downward rhythm. This leads to distending pain, belching, and acid reflux - the classic stress-triggered ulcer pattern. This is why emotional regulation is a central part of TCM treatment for many ulcer patients.
The Spleen, on the other hand, is the digestive engine that transforms food into Qi and Blood. When Spleen Qi is weak - from overwork, irregular eating, or chronic illness - the Stomach lining doesn't receive enough nourishment to repair itself. This creates a dull, chronic ache that worsens after meals and is accompanied by fatigue and bloating. Over time, this deficiency can deepen into a cold pattern where the pain is relieved by warmth and pressure, or a dryness pattern where the stomach lacks its protective Yin fluids.
Other patterns include damp-heat from rich, greasy foods and alcohol, which creates a burning, heavy sensation, and blood stasis from long-standing ulcers, where the pain becomes fixed and stabbing. Because each pattern has a different root, TCM treatment is never one-size-fits-all. A practitioner will differentiate based on the pain quality, tongue and pulse, and accompanying symptoms to select the right combination of herbs and acupuncture.

### การวินิจฉัยโดยแพทย์แผนจีน (Practitioner Diagnosis)
A TCM practitioner starts by listening to exactly how the stomach ulcer (胃疡, wèi yáng) pain feels and what makes it better or worse. The nature of the discomfort - whether it is sharp, dull, burning, or stabbing - is the first big clue that points toward one pattern rather than another. Timing, triggers, and the symptoms that come along with the pain all help to narrow the picture.
When stress and mood swings are the main trigger and the pain feels distending or moves around, Liver Qi Stagnation invading the Stomach is likely. The person often belches frequently, feels a tightness under the ribs, and the tongue may look normal with a thin white coat while the pulse has a wiry, tight quality that reflects the stuck energy.
If the pain is dull, comes on after meals, and is accompanied by bloating and a feeling of heaviness, the pattern shifts toward Spleen and Stomach Qi Deficiency. Fatigue and a pale tongue with a thin white coating are common, and the pulse feels weak and thready. This pattern often underlies chronic ulcers in people who skip meals or overwork.
Pain that feels better with warmth, pressure, or eating, and worse on an empty stomach or after cold food, points to Stomach Yang Deficient and Cold. Cold hands and feet, a preference for warm drinks, and a slow, weak pulse support this picture. The tongue is pale and may look puffy with a slippery white coat.
A burning, gnawing pain that comes with a bitter taste in the mouth and sticky bowel movements suggests Damp-Heat in the Stomach and Spleen. The tongue coating is thick, greasy, and yellow, and the pulse is rapid and slippery. This pattern is often seen when there is an infection like Helicobacter pylori or after a period of rich, greasy eating.
When the pain is a subtle, gnawing hunger sensation but there is no real desire to eat, and the mouth feels dry, Stomach Yin Deficiency is at play. The tongue is red with little or no coating, and the pulse is thin and rapid. This pattern tends to develop after long-standing heat or a chronic illness that has drained the body’s fluids.
If the pain is fixed in one spot and feels stabbing rather than dull or burning, Stomach Blood Stagnation is suspected. The tongue may be purplish or show dark spots, and the pulse is choppy. This pattern indicates that the ulcer has been present for a long time and has led to deeper tissue damage.

### Liver Qi Stagnation invading the Stomach
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/liver-qi-stagnation-invading-the-stomach)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Distending, bloating pain in the upper abdomen, Pain radiates to the ribs or sides, Worsens with emotional stress, anger, or frustration, Frequent belching and acid reflux, Irritability, moodiness, and frequent sighing
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Emotional stress, Irregular eating habits, Fatty, greasy, or spicy foods, Alcohol
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Relaxation and stress reduction, Gentle walking or stretching, Warm, cooked meals
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When emotional stress, frustration, or anger builds up, the Liver's smooth flow of Qi becomes stagnant. This stuck Liver Qi then invades the Stomach horizontally, disrupting its normal downward movement. As a result, Stomach Qi rebels upward, causing distending pain, belching, and acid reflux. Over time, this persistent disharmony can erode the stomach lining and contribute to ulcer formation.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: normal or slightly red body with redder sides, thin white coat. Pulse: wiry (弦 xián), like a taut guitar string, especially at the left middle position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Emotional stress directly causes Liver Qi to stagnate and then invade the Stomach, which is the very mechanism of this pattern. Overeating or consuming greasy, spicy foods further burdens the Stomach and exacerbates rebellious Qi. Relaxation, gentle movement, and mindful eating help to smooth the flow of Liver Qi and restore the Stomach's normal downward movement, thereby relieving pain and preventing ulcer aggravation.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Stress management is the cornerstone of treatment. Incorporate gentle movement such as walking, yoga, or tai chi to keep Qi flowing smoothly. Eat regular, smaller meals in a relaxed environment and avoid skipping meals. Steer clear of spicy, greasy, or cold foods that can further disrupt the Stomach.

### Spleen and Stomach Qi Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/spleen-and-stomach-qi-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Dull epigastric discomfort or ache, Bloating and fullness that worsens after meals, Poor appetite or reduced desire to eat, Loose or poorly formed stools, Fatigue and physical weakness, Sallow or pale complexion
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Overeating or large meals, Raw and cold foods, Overwork or overexertion, Irregular eating habits
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warm, cooked meals, Small, frequent meals, Rest after meals, Warmth on the belly
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When the Spleen and Stomach lack the Qi they need to digest food properly, every meal becomes a burden. Food sits in the stomach, causing bloating and a dull ache, while the stomach lining is left undernourished and vulnerable. Over time, this persistent weakness and poor tissue repair allows an ulcer to form.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale, puffy, and often slightly enlarged with tooth marks on the sides; thin white coating. Pulse: weak, soft, and forceless, especially at the right middle (guan) position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: In Spleen and Stomach Qi Deficiency, the digestive fire is too weak to process heavy, raw, or cold foods, so they sit in the stomach and worsen bloating and pain. Overeating overwhelms the already depleted Qi, while fatigue and irregular meals further drain it. Warm, cooked, and easily digestible meals give the Spleen less work to do, and small portions prevent stagnation. Rest after eating conserves Qi for digestion, helping the stomach lining to heal.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat warm, cooked, and easily digestible meals - think soups, congees, and stewed vegetables. Avoid raw, cold, and greasy foods that demand extra digestive fire. Eat small, regular meals and rest briefly after eating to give your Spleen a chance to work.

### Stomach Yang Deficient and Cold
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/stomach-yang-deficient-and-cold)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Dull, aching upper belly pain, Pain improves with warmth and gentle pressure, Vomiting of clear watery fluid, Cold hands and feet, Loose stools, Tiredness and lack of energy
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Cold weather or air conditioning, Raw and cold foods, Overwork or overexertion, Emotional stress, Skipping meals
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warmth on the belly, Gentle abdominal pressure, Warm, cooked meals, Rest and lying down, Ginger tea
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Stomach Yang is the digestive fire that warms and transforms food. When it becomes deficient and cold, the stomach loses its ability to properly break down what you eat, allowing cold and dampness to accumulate. This leads to a dull, aching pain that feels better with warmth and gentle pressure, because cold constricts the flow of Qi and blood, while warmth relaxes and moves them. The weakened Yang also fails to manage fluids, so clear watery fluid may be vomited or appear in loose stools.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale, puffy, tender body with teeth marks; white slippery coating. Pulse: deep, slow, weak, especially at the right Guan (Stomach/Spleen) position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Cold further weakens the already deficient Stomach Yang and constricts Qi flow, worsening the dull ache. Warmth and gentle pressure help move Qi and blood, supporting the digestive fire and easing pain. Warm, cooked foods require less energy to digest and do not tax the weakened Yang, while ginger directly warms the middle. Rest conserves Qi that would otherwise be spent on activity, and stress depletes Spleen Qi, which is often already compromised in this pattern.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat warm, cooked, easy-to-digest foods like soups, stews, and congee. Avoid cold, raw, and iced items completely. Sip ginger or cinnamon tea between meals. Keep your belly warm with a scarf or hot water bottle, and rest when tired. Gentle daily walking supports Qi flow without draining your energy.

### Damp-Heat in Stomach and Spleen
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/damp-heat-in-stomach-and-spleen)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Burning epigastric pain, Bitter or sticky taste in the mouth, Sticky or incomplete bowel movements, Thick yellow greasy tongue coating, Heavy feeling in the body and limbs
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Greasy, fried, or rich foods, Alcohol, Spicy foods, Hot, humid weather, Overeating or large meals
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Light, bland meals, Cooling foods like cucumber or mung beans, Gentle walking after meals, Rest in a dry, cool environment
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When greasy, spicy foods or alcohol overwhelm the digestive system, they create a heavy, sticky dampness that mixes with heat. This damp-heat settles in the stomach and spleen, blocking the normal downward flow of stomach Qi and generating burning pain. Over time, the trapped heat and dampness can damage the stomach lining, leading to ulcer formation.

### Stomach Yin Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/stomach-yin-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Dull, gnawing burning pain, Dry mouth and throat, Hunger without desire to eat, Dry stools or constipation, Red tongue with little or no coating, especially dry in the center
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Spicy foods, Alcohol, Late nights and overwork, Emotional stress, Dry, hot weather
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Small, frequent meals, Moistening foods (porridge, pears, honey), Warm, not hot, drinks, Rest after meals, Gentle walking or stretching
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Stomach Yin is the cooling, moistening fluid that protects the stomach lining. When it runs low - often after a long illness, chronic stress, or too many spicy, drying foods - the stomach becomes dry and fragile. This leaves the lining vulnerable to its own acids, creating a gnawing, dull burning pain that feels like a slow fire in the pit of the stomach.
Without enough Yin, the stomach also struggles to receive food, so you may feel hungry but have no real appetite, and the mouth and throat stay dry. The ulcer forms in this hot, dry environment, like a crack in parched earth.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: Red, thin body, dry centre, little or no coating, possible cracks. Pulse: Fine (Xi) and rapid (Shu).
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Stomach Yin is easily consumed by heat and dryness. Spicy and fried foods, alcohol, and coffee act like fire in the stomach, burning up the protective fluids and making the lining even drier and more painful. Overwork and emotional stress also drain Yin, especially late at night when the body should be restoring itself. On the other hand, moistening foods and warm liquids replenish the stomach’s cooling layer, while rest and gentle movement allow the body to rebuild Yin without creating more heat.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat small, frequent meals of soft, moistening foods - congee, steamed pears, honey water, and cooked spinach. Avoid all spicy, fried, or roasted foods, and keep alcohol and coffee to an absolute minimum. Sip warm water or herbal teas (like chrysanthemum) throughout the day, but avoid gulping large amounts. A short rest after meals helps the stomach use its fluids wisely. Gentle, restorative movement like Tai Chi or walking supports fluid metabolism without overheating the body.

### Stomach Blood Stagnation
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/stomach-blood-stagnation)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Sharp stabbing pain fixed in one spot, Pain worsens with pressure on the abdomen, Pain worse at night, Vomiting dark or coffee-ground material, Black tarry stools, Dark purplish tongue with stasis spots, Dull or darkened complexion, Choppy (rough, uneven) pulse
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Greasy, fried, or rich foods, Alcohol, Raw and cold foods, Emotional stress, Overwork or overexertion
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Gentle walking or stretching, Small, frequent meals, Hawthorn berry tea, Rest in a calm environment
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When a gastric ulcer persists for a long time, the local blood flow in the stomach lining becomes sluggish and eventually congeals. This stagnant blood blocks the channels, creating a sharp, fixed pain that feels like a knife. The obstruction also damages small vessels, which can leak blood into the stomach, leading to dark vomit or black tarry stools.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: dark purple body with possible stasis spots, distended sublingual veins, thin white coat. Pulse: choppy (Se) or wiry (Xian), rough and uneven.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Blood stasis pain is caused by congealed blood that obstructs the stomach's channels. Cold and greasy foods further congeal blood and create dampness, while stress and overexertion stagnate Qi, tightening the obstruction. Gentle movement and small warm meals help Qi flow and gradually disperse the stasis. Hawthorn berry tea gently invigorates blood and aids digestion, and rest allows the body to focus on healing.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Choose small, warm, easily digestible meals like congee or well-cooked vegetables. Avoid cold, raw, greasy, and spicy foods, as well as alcohol and strong coffee, which can further congeal blood or irritate the ulcer. Gentle daily walking helps move Qi and blood without straining the stomach. A cup of hawthorn berry tea (Shan Zha) between meals can gently invigorate blood and aid digestion. Rest during acute pain flares.

## คำถามที่พบบ่อย (Frequently Asked Questions)
### Can TCM heal a stomach ulcer without antibiotics?
Yes, TCM can heal ulcers by restoring the stomach's self-repair mechanisms, even when H. pylori is present. Herbs like Huang Lian (Coptis) have natural antibacterial properties, but the primary goal is to change the environment in the stomach so the bacteria can't thrive. Many patients heal without antibiotics, though severe or complicated cases may still need conventional treatment. Always discuss with your doctor if you've been diagnosed with H. pylori.

### How long will it take to feel better?
Most people notice less pain and bloating within the first 1-2 weeks of herbs and acupuncture. Complete healing of the ulcer, confirmed by endoscopy, typically takes 6-12 weeks. The timeline depends on the pattern: stress-related ulcers often respond faster than those rooted in long-standing digestive weakness.

### Can I keep taking my PPI medication while doing TCM?
Yes, TCM can be safely combined with proton pump inhibitors. Many patients start TCM while still on their prescribed medication. As your symptoms improve, you can work with your doctor to gradually taper the PPI - never stop suddenly, as this can cause rebound acid. Inform both your TCM practitioner and your doctor about all treatments you're using.

### Will the ulcer come back after TCM treatment?
TCM aims to correct the underlying imbalance that made you susceptible to ulcers in the first place. When the root pattern - whether it's Liver Qi stagnation, Spleen deficiency, or damp-heat - is fully addressed, recurrence is much less likely than with acid suppression alone. However, long-term dietary and lifestyle habits play a big role in staying well, and occasional maintenance treatments may be recommended.

### Do I need to change my diet?
Yes, diet is a cornerstone of TCM ulcer treatment. Across all patterns, we recommend warm, cooked, easily digestible foods like congee, steamed vegetables, and soups. Avoid raw, cold, spicy, and greasy foods, as well as alcohol and coffee. Small, frequent meals are better than large ones. Your practitioner will give you specific advice based on your pattern - for example, someone with a cold-type ulcer benefits from warming spices like ginger, while someone with damp-heat should avoid them.

### What does acupuncture for stomach ulcers feel like?
Acupuncture for digestive issues uses points on the abdomen, legs, and arms. The needles are hair-thin and most people feel only a slight pinch or a dull ache. Many patients find the sessions deeply relaxing, which itself helps reduce stress-related ulcer patterns. Points like Zusanli (ST-36) on the lower leg are especially effective for strengthening the Stomach and Spleen.
