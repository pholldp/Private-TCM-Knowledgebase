## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนปัจจุบัน (Conventional Medicine View)
### คำอธิบาย (Description)
A trauma-related headache is a common consequence of mild traumatic brain injury (mTBI), including concussion. It can feel like a tension-type headache, a migraine, or a mixed pattern, and often comes with other symptoms like sensitivity to light and noise, dizziness, and difficulty concentrating. Diagnosis is based on the history of injury and the exclusion of more serious structural damage through imaging when needed.
While many post-traumatic headaches resolve within weeks, a significant number become chronic, lasting months or even years. Conventional medicine often views this as a disorder of pain processing in the brain, with triggers including stress, poor sleep, and muscle tension.

### การรักษาหลัก (Treatments)
Standard care typically includes over-the-counter pain relievers like acetaminophen or ibuprofen for acute episodes. When headaches become frequent or severe, preventive medications such as tricyclic antidepressants, beta-blockers, or anticonvulsants may be prescribed. Non-drug approaches like physical therapy, cognitive behavioral therapy, and lifestyle adjustments (stress management, regular sleep) are also recommended. In some cases, nerve blocks or Botox injections are used.

## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนจีน (Traditional Chinese Medicine View)
### ความเข้าใจเกี่ยวกับการแพทย์แผนจีน (TCM Understanding)
In TCM, a blow to the head directly injures the tiny channels and collaterals that carry Qi and Blood through the scalp and brain. This trauma creates a local stagnation - Blood and Qi stop moving smoothly, much like a bruise forms under the skin. The result is a fixed, stabbing pain that is the hallmark of Blood Stagnation. This pattern is nearly always present at the core of any post-traumatic headache, because physical injury always involves some degree of blood stasis.
But the story doesn't end there. The head is the meeting point of all the Yang channels, and it relies on a steady upward flow of clear Qi to stay clear and pain-free. After an injury, the body's defensive Qi is weakened, leaving an opening for external pathogenic factors like Wind-Cold or Wind-Heat to invade. That's why some people find their headache flares when the weather turns cold or drafty.
Internally, the shock and stress of the injury can disrupt the Liver, causing Qi to stagnate and transform into rising Yang or Fire, leading to a throbbing, distending pain. If the Spleen is weakened, fluids can accumulate into Dampness and Phlegm, which rise to cloud the head, producing a heavy, muffled sensation.
This is why TCM doesn't treat all post-traumatic headaches the same way. The nature of the pain - sharp or dull, fixed or moving, worse with cold or heat - along with the tongue and pulse, tells the practitioner which patterns are at play. A patient might have pure Blood Stagnation, or a combination of stasis with Wind-Cold or Damp-Phlegm. By identifying the exact mixture, TCM can target the root cause, not just the symptom.

### การวินิจฉัยโดยแพทย์แผนจีน (Practitioner Diagnosis)
After a head injury, a TCM practitioner first asks about the pain’s quality, location, and triggers. A fixed, stabbing, needle-like pain that stays in one spot and is worse with pressure strongly suggests Blood Stagnation. The tongue often appears dark or purple with possible spots, and the pulse feels choppy or rough - a hallmark of blood not moving smoothly after trauma.
If the headache worsens with cold drafts, wind, or damp weather, and you feel chilly or want to wrap your head, Wind-Cold is likely. The pain often runs from the neck upward, and the tongue coating is thin and white. The pulse becomes tight, like a stretched rope, reflecting the constricting effect of cold on the channels.
A distending, throbbing headache that feels severe and bursting, accompanied by a flushed face, thirst, and a dislike of heat, points to Wind-Heat. The tongue tip is red with a yellow coating, and the pulse is rapid and floating. This pattern often flares when the weather is warm or after consuming spicy foods.
When the head feels heavy and muffled, as if wrapped in a wet towel, and there is nausea, chest stuffiness, or a foggy sensation, Damp-Phlegm is the culprit. The tongue coating is thick and greasy, and the pulse feels slippery, like beads rolling under the finger. This pattern often arises in people with sluggish digestion after an injury.
A pounding or throbbing headache with dizziness, ringing in the ears, irritability, and a bitter taste in the mouth signals Liver Yang Rising. The tongue is red with a yellow coat, and the pulse is wiry and rapid. Emotional stress or anger can trigger or worsen this type of pain.
A dull, lingering ache that worsens with fatigue or overwork and improves with rest suggests Qi Deficiency. You may feel exhausted, have a poor appetite, and notice a pale tongue with a thin white coat. The pulse is weak and thready, lacking the strength to push blood upward to nourish the head.

### Blood Stagnation
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/blood-stagnation)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Fixed, stabbing pain like a knife or needle, Pain worsens at night or with pressure, Dark purple tongue with possible stasis spots, Dark, dusky complexion or dark circles under the eyes
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Cold weather, wind, or drafts, Sedentary lifestyle or lying still for too long, Cold drinks and raw foods, Emotional stress (anger, frustration)
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Gentle movement, Warm compress on the head, Rest in a cool, quiet, dark room, Warm, cooked, nourishing meals
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  A blow to the head directly damages the tiny blood vessels and collaterals, causing blood to pool and stagnate in the area. This stagnant blood blocks the smooth flow of Qi, creating a fixed, stabbing pain that feels like a knife or needle.
Because blood stasis is a physical obstruction, the pain does not move around and is often worse at night when circulation slows further. The tongue and pulse mirror this stuck, choppy quality.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: dark purple body, possibly with stasis spots, and distended dark sublingual veins. Pulse: choppy (涩, sè) like a knife scraping bamboo, or wiry (弦, xián) if Qi stagnation is also present.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Cold congeals and slows blood, making stasis worse, while warmth and gentle movement encourage blood to flow again. Rest helps the body repair, but complete inactivity allows blood to pool further. Stress and anger tighten the Liver Qi, which in turn tightens the blood vessels and worsens the fixed pain.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat warming, gently moving foods like ginger, turmeric, and small amounts of black pepper. Avoid cold, raw foods and icy drinks that congeal blood. Gentle daily movement-like walking or tai chi-keeps blood flowing. Apply a warm compress to the painful area, but never directly on a fresh injury.

### Wind-Cold
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/wind-cold)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Constricting or aching pain, often at the back of the head and neck, Worsens with cold, wind, or drafts, Aversion to cold and chills, no sweating, Clear, watery nasal discharge and sneezing, Thin white tongue coat
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Cold weather, wind, or drafts, Cold drinks and raw foods, Overwork or overexertion, Emotional stress (anger, frustration)
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warmth and covering the head, Rest in a draft-free room, Ginger tea, Gentle neck stretches once acute pain eases
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  After a head injury, the body's defensive Qi is temporarily weakened, leaving the channels of the head and neck vulnerable to invasion. When external Wind-Cold penetrates, it contracts and obstructs the flow of Qi and Blood in the superficial channels, causing a tight, constricting pain that often radiates to the neck and shoulders. The Cold pathogen also triggers the body's protective response: the pores shut, sweating stops, and chills develop as the defensive Qi struggles against the invader.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: normal or slightly pale body with a thin white moist coat. Pulse: floating and tight (浮紧, fú jǐn), like a string pulled taut.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Wind-Cold invades the surface and contracts the channels, so anything that adds cold or keeps the pores shut traps the pathogen, worsening the headache. Warmth and rest help the defensive Qi to open the pores and gently push the cold outward, while warm drinks like ginger tea warm the interior and support the release of the exterior.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Keep the head and neck warmly covered, especially in windy or cold weather. Sip hot ginger tea to help the body push out the cold, and avoid cold, raw foods and iced drinks. Gentle rest in a warm, draft-free room supports the defensive Qi while it fights off the pathogen.

### Wind-Heat
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/wind-heat)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Distending, bursting headache, Flushed face and red eyes, Thirst with desire for cold drinks, Mild fever or aversion to wind
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Hot, stuffy environments, Spicy or fried foods, alcohol, or coffee, Emotional stress (anger, frustration)
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Rest in a cool, quiet, dark room, Drinking cold water or cooling teas, Gentle rest
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  After a head injury, the body's protective barrier is weakened, making it easy for external Wind-Heat to invade. Wind-Heat is light and rising by nature, so it travels upward into the head, disturbing the clear Yang and causing a severe, distending pain that feels like the head is about to burst. The Heat component also stirs up facial flushing, red eyes, and an urgent thirst for cold drinks.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red tip and edges with a thin yellow coating. Pulse: floating and rapid.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Wind-Heat is a combination of an external pathogen and internal Heat. Heat aggravates Heat, so hot environments, spicy foods, and alcohol all intensify the headache by adding more Heat to the system. Emotional upset stirs up Liver Yang, which can combine with Wind-Heat to make the pain worse. Cooling measures-like a cold drink or a cool room-help drain the excess Heat, while rest allows the body's defensive Qi to push the pathogen out.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Rest in a cool, well-ventilated room and avoid hot, stuffy environments. Drink plenty of water and enjoy cooling herbal teas like chrysanthemum or mint. Steer clear of spicy, greasy, or fried foods, as well as alcohol, which all add Heat. A gentle walk in fresh air is fine, but avoid heavy exertion until the acute symptoms settle.

### Damp-Phlegm
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/damp-phlegm)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Head feels heavy or wrapped, not sharp, Nausea or vomiting, Brain fog or muzzy-headedness, Chest and upper abdominal stuffiness, Poor appetite and loose stools
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Humid or damp weather, Overeating or rich, greasy foods, Dairy and sweets, Sedentary lifestyle or lying still for too long, Lying down right after eating
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Dry, warm environment, Light movement after meals, Warm, cooked, nourishing meals, Ginger tea
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  After a head injury, the body's healing process can weaken the Spleen's ability to transform fluids. This leads to an accumulation of Dampness and Phlegm, which are heavy and turbid. These pathogenic factors rise to obstruct the clear Yang from reaching the head, causing a sensation of heaviness and mental cloudiness rather than sharp pain.
The Phlegm-Dampness blocks the normal upward flow of Qi, leaving the head feeling wrapped in a wet blanket. This pattern is more likely if a person was already prone to digestive weakness or consumed damp-producing foods during recovery.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale, swollen body with teeth marks and a thick, white, greasy coat. Pulse: slippery (滑 huá), like beads rolling in oil.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Damp-Phlegm is heavy and sticky, thriving in humid conditions and from foods that burden the Spleen, like dairy, sugar, and grease. These worsen the obstruction, making the head feel more wrapped and foggy. Warm, dry environments and cooked foods help the Spleen transform and transport fluids, while gentle movement activates Qi to dispel stagnation. Ginger tea warms the middle burner and transforms Phlegm, directly countering the pattern's root.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Avoid damp-producing foods: dairy, sugar, greasy or deep-fried items, and excessive raw or cold foods. Eat warm, cooked meals like congee with a little ginger to support the Spleen. Gentle daily movement such as walking helps Qi flow and prevents stagnation. Keep living spaces dry and well-ventilated.

### Liver Yang Rising
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/liver-yang-rising)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Throbbing or distending headache (especially at temples or top of head), Dizziness or feeling that the head is heavy while the feet are light, Irritability and quick temper, Flushed face and red eyes, Ringing in the ears (tinnitus)
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Emotional stress (anger, frustration), Spicy or fried foods, alcohol, or coffee, Overwork or overexertion, Hot, stuffy environments
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Rest in a cool, quiet, dark room, Cool compress on the forehead, Stress reduction and relaxation
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  After a head injury, the shock and stress can disrupt the Liver's ability to keep Qi flowing smoothly. When Liver Qi stagnates, it can transform into heat and surge upward as rising Yang, carrying that heat and pressure into the head. This is what produces the throbbing, distending pain at the temples or top of the head, often made worse by emotional stress or frustration.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red body with redder sides, thin dry yellow or scanty coat, possibly slightly trembling. Pulse: wiry (弦 xián), often rapid (数 shuò); may be fine (细 xì) if Yin deficiency is prominent.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Stress and anger are the classic triggers that make Liver Qi surge upward, which is the very mechanism of this pattern. Spicy foods, alcohol, and heat add fuel to the rising Yang, while overwork and poor sleep drain the Yin that normally anchors it. Rest, coolness, and relaxation help draw the Yang back down and allow the Liver to smooth its flow.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Steer clear of spicy, greasy, and fried foods, as well as alcohol and coffee, which all add heat and fuel the upward surge. Gentle, regular movement like walking or tai chi helps keep Liver Qi flowing smoothly. Prioritize winding down before bed and managing stress with breathwork or meditation.

### Qi Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/qi-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Dull, lingering headache, Worse with exertion or fatigue, Persistent tiredness and weakness, Shortness of breath on mild effort, Pale or sallow complexion, Poor appetite and loose stools
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Overwork or overexertion, Skipping meals, Cold drinks and raw foods, Cold weather, wind, or drafts
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Rest and lying down, Warm, cooked, nourishing meals, Gentle deep breathing, Keeping warm
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  After a head injury, the body uses a great deal of Qi to repair damaged tissues. If the injury is prolonged, or if there was significant blood loss, the Spleen and Lungs may become depleted and unable to produce enough Qi. When Qi is too weak to lift clear Yang up to the head, the brain and sensory orifices are undernourished, and a dull, lingering ache sets in.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale, puffy, tender body with teeth marks and a thin white coating. Pulse: empty (Xu) or weak (Ruo), especially at the right Guan position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Qi is already insufficient, so any activity that consumes it-like overwork or standing for long periods-makes the headache worse. Rest conserves Qi and allows the Spleen to direct its energy toward digestion and repair. Warm, cooked foods are easy to transform into Qi and blood, while cold, raw foods demand extra digestive fire, further depleting the Spleen and worsening the deficiency.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat warm, cooked foods like congees, soups, and stews with easily digestible proteins. Avoid raw, cold, or greasy foods that tax the Spleen. Prioritize rest and gentle activities such as walking or qigong rather than intense exercise, and protect yourself from drafts and cold.

## คำถามที่พบบ่อย (Frequently Asked Questions)
### Can acupuncture really help a headache that started after a concussion?
Yes. Acupuncture works by unblocking the stuck Qi and Blood in the channels of the head, which is the core problem in a trauma-related headache. Points like Baihui (DU-20) and Fengchi (GB-20) directly influence the local circulation and can reduce pain intensity and frequency. Many patients feel a sense of release during the session itself, though lasting improvement builds over a series of treatments.

### How long does it take for Chinese herbs to work on a post-traumatic headache?
Herbs work on a deeper level than painkillers, so they don't simply mask the pain for a few hours - they aim to correct the underlying pattern. You may notice a subtle shift in the first week, but a meaningful reduction in headache days usually takes 2 to 4 weeks of consistent dosing. Chronic conditions that have lasted months or years will naturally take longer, often 6 to 12 weeks, as the body gradually rebalances.

### My headache gets worse whenever the weather turns cold or damp. What does that mean in TCM?
That's a classic sign that external Wind-Cold or Dampness has invaded the weakened channels of your head after the injury. In TCM, these pathogenic factors can lodge in the superficial layers and react to environmental triggers. Treatment would focus on expelling Wind-Cold with warming, dispersing herbs like Chuan Xiong and Jing Jie, and protecting the neck and head from drafts. Acupuncture points like Fengchi (GB-20) are especially effective for weather-sensitive headaches.

### Is it safe to take Chinese herbs with my current headache medication?
In most cases, yes. Herbal formulas for headache can be used alongside conventional medications. However, if you are taking blood thinners like warfarin or aspirin, you must tell both your TCM practitioner and your doctor, because some blood-moving herbs (like Chuan Xiong, Hong Hua, Tao Ren) can have an additive effect. Never stop a prescribed medication abruptly - if the herbs are working, work with your doctor to adjust dosages gradually.

### Do I need to change my diet to help the headache go away?
Diet plays a supporting role. In general, avoid cold, raw foods and iced drinks, which can create Dampness and impede circulation. Minimize greasy, fried, or spicy foods that generate Heat and Phlegm. Instead, favor warm, cooked meals like soups and stews that are easy to digest and support the Spleen's ability to produce clear Qi. Ginger tea can be helpful for Wind-Cold patterns, while chrysanthemum tea may soothe a Wind-Heat type headache.

### What if my head injury was years ago - can TCM still help?
Absolutely. Chronic post-traumatic headaches often involve deeply entrenched Blood Stagnation or a mix of deficiency and excess patterns. While it may take longer to unwind than a recent injury, TCM can still improve pain levels and quality of life. The approach focuses on gradually reactivating circulation, dissolving old stasis, and strengthening the body's overall Qi to prevent recurrence. Many patients with headaches lasting years see meaningful improvement with consistent treatment over several months.
