## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนปัจจุบัน (Conventional Medicine View)
### คำอธิบาย (Description)
Tendinitis is the inflammation of a tendon, the tough cord of tissue that attaches muscle to bone. It typically develops from repetitive strain, overuse, or a sudden injury, causing pain, swelling, and reduced movement around the affected joint. Common sites include the shoulder, elbow, wrist, knee, and heel.
Diagnosis is usually made through a physical exam and a review of your activity history. In some cases, imaging such as ultrasound or MRI is used to confirm the inflammation or rule out a tear. The pain is often described as a dull ache that worsens with movement, and the area may feel tender to the touch.

### การรักษาหลัก (Treatments)
Standard care begins with rest, ice, and over-the-counter nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs (NSAIDs) to reduce pain and swelling. Physical therapy is often recommended to stretch and strengthen the surrounding muscles. If these measures fail, corticosteroid injections may be used for short-term relief. In severe or chronic cases, procedures like platelet-rich plasma (PRP) injections or even surgery to repair the tendon are considered.

## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนจีน (Traditional Chinese Medicine View)
### ความเข้าใจเกี่ยวกับการแพทย์แผนจีน (TCM Understanding)
TCM views tendinitis as a form of “sinew obstruction” (筋痹, jīn bì), where the flow of Qi and Blood through the sinew channels is blocked. The Liver is the organ system most closely tied to the sinews, so its health directly influences tendon strength and flexibility. When Qi stagnates from repetitive strain or emotional stress, or when external pathogens like Cold, Dampness, or Heat invade the channels, the result is pain, stiffness, and swelling.
The quality of the pain is a crucial clue. A stabbing, fixed pain that worsens with pressure points to Blood Stagnation, often from an old injury. A burning, red, swollen tendon that feels better with cold indicates Damp Heat has settled in the channels. If the pain is heavy and aching and gets worse in cold, damp weather, Wind-Cold-Damp is likely the culprit. And when the pain is dull and achy, with overall fatigue, it suggests the underlying Qi and Blood are too depleted to nourish the tendons properly.
Because each pattern arises from a different root, the same Western diagnosis of tendinitis can require completely different TCM treatments. One person may need herbs to invigorate Blood and break up stasis, while another needs to clear Damp Heat and cool inflammation. This is why a one-size-fits-all approach often falls short, and why TCM always looks beyond the label to the unique pattern presenting in your body.

### การวินิจฉัยโดยแพทย์แผนจีน (Practitioner Diagnosis)
A TCM practitioner begins by asking what the pain feels like, when it started, and what makes it better or worse. Tendinitis is viewed as a type of “sinew obstruction” (筋痹, jīn bì), and the quality of the discomfort-whether it is distending, stabbing, burning, or heavy-provides the first clue. The tongue and pulse are then checked, because they reveal deeper patterns that the pain alone cannot explain.
When the pain feels distending and tight, with noticeable stiffness and adhesions but no strong heat or cold, the picture points to Qi and Blood Stagnation. This is the classic overuse pattern. If the stagnation deepens, the pain becomes fixed and stabbing, worsens with pressure, and the tongue shows purple spots-this is Blood Stagnation. Both reflect a blockage of flow in the sinew channels, differing mainly in severity.
If the area is red, swollen, and burning, and the pain gets worse with warmth, the practitioner suspects Painful Obstruction due to Damp Heat in Channels. The tongue appears red with a greasy yellow coating, and the pulse feels rapid and slippery. This is an acute inflammatory flare, often triggered by an underlying imbalance that allows dampness and heat to settle in the injured tissues.
In contrast, a heavy, aching pain that intensifies in cold or damp weather signals Painful Obstruction with Wind-Cold-Damp. Here the tongue is pale with a white coating, and the pulse is deep and tight. In long-standing cases where the whole body feels weak, the muscles are thin, and fatigue is prominent, the root may be Qi and Blood Deficiency-a pale tongue and a thready pulse reveal that the sinews are simply not being nourished enough.

## กลุ่มอาการการแพทย์แผนจีนและการรักษา (TCM Patterns and Treatment)

### Qi And Blood Stagnation
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/qi-and-blood-stagnation)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Fixed, stabbing pain at the tendon, Pain aggravated by pressure, Local stiffness or adhesions, Dark purple tongue with stasis spots, Irritability or emotional tension
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Overuse or repetitive motion, Emotional stress, Cold and damp weather, Prolonged inactivity
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warm compress or heat, Gentle movement or stretching, Rest from repetitive tasks, Stress management
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Repetitive strain or overuse disrupts the smooth flow of Qi in the sinew channels. Over time, this Qi stagnation leads to Blood stasis, causing a fixed, stabbing pain at the affected tendon. The pain worsens with pressure because the stagnated Blood resists movement. Local adhesions and stiffness develop as the stagnant Qi and Blood fail to nourish and lubricate the sinews. Emotional stress can further aggravate the pattern by causing Liver Qi to tighten, which governs the sinews.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: dark purple body with stasis spots, distended sublingual veins, thin white coat. Pulse: wiry (弦 xián) and choppy (涩 sè).
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Qi and Blood stagnation is worsened by lack of movement and cold, which congeal Blood and tighten the channels. Emotional stress constrains Liver Qi, which governs the sinews, further blocking flow. Gentle movement and warmth promote circulation, while rest from overuse prevents further stasis. Stress reduction helps relax Liver Qi, allowing Blood to nourish the tendons.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Apply a warm compress to the area to promote circulation. Gentle stretching and movement help keep Qi flowing, but avoid overuse. Incorporate anti-inflammatory foods like turmeric and ginger. Manage stress with relaxation techniques, as emotional tension tightens the Liver Qi and worsens the condition.

### Blood Stagnation
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/blood-stagnation)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Fixed, stabbing pain that does not move, Pain worsens at night, Pain is worse with pressure, Dark purple tongue with stasis spots, Palpable hard lump or knot in the tendon
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Cold and damp weather, Prolonged inactivity, Heavy pressure or deep massage, Aggravating an old injury
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warm compress or heat, Gentle movement or stretching, Turmeric or ginger tea, Adequate rest
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When a tendon is overused or injured, the local circulation of Blood and Qi can become stuck. This stagnation creates a fixed, stabbing pain that feels like a sharp object is lodged in the tissue. The longer the blood pools without moving, the more intense the pain becomes, especially with pressure or at night when stillness allows the stasis to settle.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: purple or dusky body with purple stasis spots; distended, dark sublingual veins; thin white coat. Pulse: choppy (涩 Sè) like a knife scraping bamboo, or wiry (弦 Xián) if Qi stagnation is present.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Cold constricts the vessels and makes blood more sluggish, deepening the stasis, while warmth dilates them and encourages flow. Gentle movement acts as a pump for blood and Qi, but heavy pressure can crush already damaged tissue and trap blood, making the pain sharper and more fixed.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Apply gentle warmth to the area to encourage blood flow, but avoid heavy pressure or aggressive massage. Gentle, pain-free stretching and movement prevent further stagnation. Include turmeric, ginger, and dark leafy greens in your meals to support healthy circulation.

### Painful Obstruction due to Damp Heat in Channels
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/painful-obstruction-due-to-damp-heat-in-channels)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Red, hot, swollen tendon area, Burning pain, worse with warmth and better with cold, Heavy, aching sensation in the affected limb, Thirst with desire to drink, Irritability and restlessness, Dark or scanty urine
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Heat (hot showers, warm weather), Spicy, greasy, or fried foods, Alcohol, Overuse or repetitive motion, Damp, humid weather
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Cold compresses, Rest from repetitive tasks, Cool environment, Light, cooling foods
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Dampness and Heat invade the sinew channels, often after exposure to hot, humid environments or from an internal buildup of Damp-Heat due to diet. The Heat causes redness, burning pain, and swelling, while Dampness creates heaviness, stiffness, and a sensation of fullness. Together they obstruct the flow of Qi and Blood through the tendons, triggering acute tendinitis flare-ups that feel worse with warmth and better with cold.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red body, possibly slightly swollen, yellow greasy coating. Pulse: slippery and rapid, may feel soggy or wiry at the Guan position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Damp-Heat pattern is characterized by an excess of Heat and Dampness obstructing the channels. Heat aggravates the condition by further inflaming the tissues, while cold applications help clear Heat and soothe burning pain. Dampness is worsened by humid environments and heavy, greasy foods, so cooling, light, and damp-draining foods reduce the internal burden. Rest prevents further Qi and Blood stagnation, allowing the channels to recover.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: During acute flare-ups, avoid spicy, greasy, and fried foods, as well as alcohol, which generate Damp-Heat. Eat cooling, damp-draining foods like mung beans, cucumber, watermelon, and barley. Rest the affected tendon and apply cold compresses to reduce heat and swelling. Once the acute phase passes, gentle stretching helps restore mobility.

### Painful Obstruction with Wind-Cold-Damp
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/painful-obstruction-with-wind-cold-damp)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Aching, heavy pain that may shift location, Markedly worse in cold, damp, or rainy weather, Heaviness or numbness in the affected limb, Swelling without redness or heat, Aversion to cold or wind
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Cold, rainy weather, Cold foods and drinks, Prolonged inactivity, Exposure to wind or drafts, Overexertion in damp conditions
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warm compress or heat, Gentle movement or stretching, Dry, warm weather, Turmeric or ginger tea, Rest in a warm environment
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When Wind, Cold, and Dampness invade the body, they lodge in the sinew channels that nourish your tendons. Cold causes the channels to constrict and Dampness makes them heavy and sluggish, so Qi and Blood cannot flow freely. This blockage produces the deep, aching tendon pain, stiffness, and a heavy sensation that flare up whenever you are exposed to cold or wet weather.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale body, slightly swollen with teeth marks, white greasy coating. Pulse: wiry, tight, and slow (or soggy if Dampness predominates).
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Wind, Cold, and Dampness are the pathogenic factors that invade the sinew channels, causing obstruction and pain. Warmth and movement promote the flow of Qi and Blood, dispersing these pathogens, while cold, damp, and stillness cause them to accumulate, worsening symptoms.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Keep the affected area warm and dry. Use a heating pad or warm compress for relief. Avoid cold, raw foods and icy drinks; favor warm, cooked meals with ginger, cinnamon, and black pepper. Gentle stretching and movement help prevent stiffness, but do not overexert yourself in cold, damp environments.

### Qi and Blood Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/qi-and-blood-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Dull, lingering tendon pain, Worse with exertion, better with rest, General fatigue and weakness, Pale complexion and lips, Dizziness or lightheadedness, Numbness or tingling in limbs
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Overuse or repetitive motion, Cold and damp weather, Poor diet (raw, cold foods), Emotional stress, Lack of sleep
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Rest and gentle stretching, Warm compress or heat, Nourishing, easily digestible meals, Adequate sleep and relaxation, Gentle movement or stretching
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When Qi and Blood are deficient, the sinews (tendons) lack the nourishment they need to repair and stay strong. This leads to a dull, lingering ache that feels worse after activity and is accompanied by overall fatigue. The body simply doesn't have enough resources to maintain healthy tendons, so even minor strain can cause prolonged pain.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale body, may be thin or have teeth marks, thin white coating. Pulse: fine, weak, and thready.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Qi and Blood are the body's fundamental resources for repair. Overwork, stress, and poor diet drain these reserves, leaving tendons undernourished and painful. Cold constricts circulation, further depriving the sinews. Rest, warmth, and nutrient-dense foods help replenish Qi and Blood, allowing the body to heal. Gentle movement stimulates circulation without depleting energy, while sleep is when the body rebuilds.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat warm, cooked meals rich in Qi and Blood-nourishing foods like congee with red dates, bone broth, and dark leafy greens. Avoid overwork and prolonged repetitive activity; gentle stretching and rest are essential. Short naps and a regular sleep schedule support recovery.

## คำถามที่พบบ่อย (Frequently Asked Questions)
### How many acupuncture sessions will I need for tendinitis?
Most people notice some improvement after 3-4 weekly sessions. A full course for an acute flare-up may be 6-8 sessions, while a chronic or recurrent problem often benefits from 8-12 sessions spread over a few months. Your practitioner will reassess your progress regularly and adjust the frequency as your pain subsides.

### Can I still take my anti-inflammatory medication while using Chinese herbs?
Yes, in most cases you can. Many people begin TCM while still using NSAIDs and then gradually reduce them as the pain improves. Always tell both your TCM practitioner and your doctor exactly what you are taking. Certain blood-moving herbs may interact with anticoagulants or high-dose NSAIDs, so full disclosure is essential.

### Will the tendon pain come back after TCM treatment?
TCM aims to correct the underlying imbalance that made the tendon vulnerable, so recurrence is less likely than with treatments that only suppress symptoms. However, if you return to the same overuse patterns or dietary habits that contributed to the problem, the pain can return. Your practitioner will give you lifestyle and dietary guidance to help maintain the results.

### Is acupuncture for tendinitis painful?
Acupuncture needles are extremely thin - much finer than a typical injection needle - so most people feel only a brief pinch or a dull ache when the needle reaches the correct point. Many find the experience deeply relaxing. Your practitioner may also use moxibustion (gentle heat) or cupping on the area, which are generally comfortable and soothing.

### Can diet really make a difference for tendon health?
Yes. In TCM, certain foods create Dampness and Heat in the body, which can fuel inflammation and stiffness. Reducing greasy, spicy, and sugary foods while increasing warm, easily digestible meals can support healing. For deficiency patterns, blood-nourishing foods like dark leafy greens, bone broth, and moderate amounts of red meat are especially helpful.

### I have calcific tendinitis. Can TCM help dissolve the calcium deposits?
TCM sees calcifications as a form of congealed Phlegm and Blood Stasis. Herbs that invigorate Blood and transform Phlegm, along with acupuncture to restore local circulation, may help the body gradually reabsorb small deposits. This is a slower process, often requiring several months of consistent treatment, and is best combined with appropriate physical therapy.

## การวิจัยทางวิทยาศาสตร์สมัยใหม่ (Modern Scientific Research)
1. **Acupuncture for lateral elbow pain** ([ลิงก์](https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD003527))
   *วารสารวิชาการ: Green S, Buchbinder R, Barnsley L, Hall S, White M, Smidt N, Assendelft W. Acupuncture for lateral elbow pain. Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews 2002, Issue 1. Art. No.: CD003527.*
   A Cochrane systematic review of randomized controlled trials evaluating acupuncture for lateral epicondylitis. The review found that acupuncture was effective for short-term pain relief compared to placebo, but the evidence was limited by the small number of high-quality studies.

2. **Acupuncture for the alleviation of lateral epicondyle pain: a systematic review** ([ลิงก์](https://doi.org/10.1093/rheumatology/keh247))
   *วารสารวิชาการ: Trinh KV, Phillips SD, Ho E, Damsma K. Acupuncture for the alleviation of lateral epicondyle pain: a systematic review. Rheumatology (Oxford). 2004;43(9):1085-1090.*
   A systematic review of six RCTs assessing acupuncture for tennis elbow. The review concluded that acupuncture is superior to sham acupuncture in reducing pain, with a significant effect size, though the authors noted the need for more rigorous trials.

3. **Electroacupuncture for chronic Achilles tendinopathy: a randomized controlled trial**
   *วารสารวิชาการ: Zhang BM, Zhong LW, Xu SW, et al. Electroacupuncture for chronic Achilles tendinopathy: a randomized controlled trial. Chinese Journal of Integrative Medicine. 2013;19(12):900-904.*
   A randomized trial comparing electroacupuncture with eccentric exercise in patients with midportion Achilles tendinopathy. The study found that electroacupuncture provided similar pain reduction and functional improvement to exercise, suggesting it as an effective alternative treatment.
