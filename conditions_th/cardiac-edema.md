## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนปัจจุบัน (Conventional Medicine View)
### คำอธิบาย (Description)
Cardiac edema is fluid retention caused by the heart's reduced ability to pump blood effectively, most often due to heart failure. It typically appears as swelling in the feet, ankles, and lower legs, and may worsen throughout the day. The skin often pits when pressed, and the swelling is usually accompanied by shortness of breath, fatigue, and reduced exercise tolerance. Diagnosis is made through physical examination, echocardiography, and blood tests like BNP levels.

### การรักษาหลัก (Treatments)
Standard treatment focuses on reducing fluid with diuretics (such as furosemide), along with medications that support heart function - including ACE inhibitors, beta-blockers, and sometimes digoxin. Patients are also advised to restrict salt and fluid intake, and to monitor their weight daily. While effective for many, these approaches manage symptoms rather than addressing the underlying energetic weakness that TCM identifies.

## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนจีน (Traditional Chinese Medicine View)
### ความเข้าใจเกี่ยวกับการแพทย์แผนจีน (TCM Understanding)
In TCM, fluid metabolism is a team effort between the Heart, Kidneys, Spleen, and Lungs. The Heart governs blood and vessels, propelling circulation; the Kidneys provide the warming fire that transforms water into vapor and urine. When either of these systems weakens, fluid can no longer be moved or transformed properly, and it begins to seep into the tissues, especially in the lower body where gravity pulls it.
Cardiac edema is therefore never just a water problem - it always reflects a deeper deficiency. If the Kidney Yang is too weak, water accumulates as cold, heavy swelling with icy limbs and scanty urine. If the Heart Qi is insufficient, the swelling comes with extreme fatigue and palpitations, because the pump itself is failing. Over time, sluggish blood flow can turn into stasis, adding a purple hue to the lips and a stabbing chest pain to the picture.
And in chronic cases, the body's Yin fluids can also become depleted, causing a paradoxical mix of swelling with dryness and night sweats.
Because each pattern involves a different root, the same symptom of swollen ankles can require a completely different herbal strategy - warming and strengthening the Kidney fire, boosting Heart Qi, invigorating blood, or nourishing both Qi and Yin. A TCM practitioner differentiates these not just by the swelling itself, but by the temperature of the limbs, the quality of the pulse, the tongue's appearance, and the sensations that accompany the edema.

### การวินิจฉัยโดยแพทย์แผนจีน (Practitioner Diagnosis)
A TCM practitioner begins by asking where the swelling is most noticeable and what other sensations come with it. The location, temperature of the limbs, and the quality of the pulse give the first clues that separate one pattern from another, because cardiac edema (心源性水肿, xīn yuán xìng shuǐ zhǒng) is never just a water problem - it always reflects a deeper imbalance in the heart and kidneys.
If the edema is heaviest in the legs and ankles, accompanied by a deep coldness in the body, a fear of chill, and very scanty urine, the picture points to Kidney Yang Deficiency with Water overflowing. The tongue is pale and puffy with teeth marks, and the pulse feels deep and slow. This pattern means the body’s inner fire is too weak to transform and move fluids.
When swelling appears together with pronounced fatigue, shortness of breath, and a fluttering or uneasy heartbeat, the focus shifts to Heart Qi Deficiency. The edema may be milder and more general, but the person feels drained and may look pale. The tongue is pale with a thin white coat, and the pulse is weak and thready - signs that the heart lacks the strength to pump and circulate fluids properly.
If the swelling is accompanied by purplish lips, a stabbing or oppressive chest pain, and visible veins or a dark hue on the tongue, the practitioner suspects Heart Blood Stagnation. The tongue may show purple spots or a dusky body, and the pulse feels choppy or wiry. Here the obstruction of blood flow is the root cause of fluid buildup, so the signs of stuck blood are just as prominent as the swelling.
In long-standing or recurring edema where the person feels both exhausted and dried out - with a dry mouth, night sweats, and a red tongue with little coating - the pattern is Qi and Yin Deficiency. The pulse is thin and rapid. This picture tells the practitioner that the body’s reserves of both vital energy and cooling moisture have run low, making the edema stubborn and prone to relapse.

## กลุ่มอาการการแพทย์แผนจีนและการรักษา (TCM Patterns and Treatment)

### Kidney Yang Deficiency with Water overflowing
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/kidney-yang-deficiency-with-water-overflowing)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Swelling of legs and ankles, worse in the evening, Deep, internal coldness, especially in the lower back and legs, Scanty, difficult urination, Soreness and weakness of the lower back, Loose stools or diarrhoea
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Cold weather, Overexertion, Raw, cold foods and iced drinks, Prolonged standing or sitting, Emotional stress
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warmth on the lower back and abdomen, Elevating the legs, Warm, easily digested foods, Gentle walking, Moxibustion on the lower back
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  In this pattern, the body's internal furnace - Kidney Yang - is too weak to warm and transform fluids. Without that heat, water cannot be vaporized or excreted normally, so it accumulates, especially in the lower body where gravity pulls it. The coldness, scanty urination, and swelling all trace back to this deep deficiency of warming Yang.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale, puffy, with scalloped edges and a white, slippery coating. Pulse: deep, slow, and weak, especially at the Chi (Kidney) position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Kidney Yang is the body's furnace; cold, damp environments and raw foods drain it, while warmth and moxibustion feed it. Rest conserves Yang Qi, whereas overwork depletes it. Gentle movement helps Qi circulate and transform fluids, but standing still allows fluid to pool. Fear is the emotion of the Kidneys and consumes their Qi, making the internal cold worse.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Keep the body warm, especially the lower back and feet. Eat warm, cooked meals - soups, stews, and congees with a little ginger or cinnamon are ideal. Avoid raw, cold foods and iced drinks, which snuff out the body's fire. Rest with your legs elevated when swelling is worse, and take gentle walks to keep Qi moving. Low-salt, easy-to-digest foods like Job's tears, adzuki beans, and winter melon can gently support fluid drainage.

### Heart Qi Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/heart-qi-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Swelling starts in ankles and lower legs, worse after standing, Palpitations and uncomfortable awareness of heartbeat, Shortness of breath that worsens with any effort, Pale, tired-looking face and reluctance to speak, Mild anxiety or unease
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Overexertion, Emotional stress, Raw, cold foods and iced drinks, Cold weather, Salty foods
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Rest, Gentle walking, Warm environment, Small, frequent meals, Elevating the legs
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Your Heart Qi is the engine that pumps blood and moves fluids around your body. When that Qi is weak, the circulation slows down and fluid begins to seep out into the tissues, especially in the lower legs and ankles where gravity pulls it. This is why the swelling comes with a tired, breathless feeling and a fluttery awareness of your heartbeat - your Heart is simply struggling to keep up.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale, puffy with a thin white coat; a midline crack toward the tip may appear in chronic cases. Pulse: Empty (Xu) and weak, especially at the left Cun position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Heart Qi is easily depleted by physical strain and emotional upset, so rest and calm are essential. Cold and dampness further burden the Spleen and block fluid movement, while salt directly promotes water retention. Gentle movement encourages Qi circulation without overtaxing the Heart, and elevating the legs uses gravity to help fluid return.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Prioritize rest and avoid overexertion. Eat small, frequent, warm meals that are easy to digest. Include foods like Job's tears (yì yǐ rén), adzuki beans (chì xiǎo dòu), and astragalus (huáng qí) in soups to gently support Qi and drain dampness. Limit salt and avoid raw, cold, or greasy foods that burden the Spleen.

### Heart Blood Stagnation
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/heart-blood-stagnation)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Stabbing, fixed chest pain that may radiate to the shoulder or arm, Purple or dark lips and nail beds, Pitting edema in the lower limbs, worse after standing, Palpitations with anxiety and restlessness, Cold hands
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Emotional stress, Cold weather, Overexertion, Fatty, heavy foods
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Gentle walking, Warmth on the chest, Rest, Warm, easily digested foods
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  The Heart is the master of the blood vessels. When its Qi weakens over time, or when Yang fails to warm, the blood loses its driving force and begins to move sluggishly, eventually stagnating in the chest. This stasis not only causes the fixed, stabbing pain and the purple hue of the lips and nails, but also blocks the pathways that transport body fluids. With nowhere to go, the fluids seep into the tissues, creating a pitting edema that typically settles in the lower legs and feet.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: dark purple body with stasis spots and distended sublingual veins; thin white coat. Pulse: choppy (涩 Sè) or knotted (结 Jié), especially at the left Cun position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Stress and anger cause Qi to stagnate, which worsens blood stasis and triggers chest pain. Cold constricts blood vessels, further impeding circulation. Overexertion strains the already sluggish Heart, while fatty foods create Dampness that adds to fluid retention. Gentle movement and warmth promote the smooth flow of blood and Qi, and rest conserves Heart energy, allowing the stasis to gradually resolve.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Keep salt intake low and eat small, frequent, easily digestible meals. Include blood-moving foods like hawthorn, black fungus, and turmeric. Gentle daily movement, such as walking or tai chi, helps keep blood flowing smoothly without overtaxing the Heart. Avoid emotional stress and practice relaxation techniques.

### Qi and Yin Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/qi-and-yin-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Lingering edema, worse with exertion, Fatigue and lack of strength, Night sweats, Dry mouth and throat, Warm sensation in palms and soles, Palpitations
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Emotional stress, Spicy, greasy, or fried foods, Overexertion, Hot, dry weather
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Rest, Cool, quiet environment, Warm, moistening foods (pear, congee), Gentle walking
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  In chronic heart conditions, the heart muscle gradually loses both its driving force (Qi) and its nourishing fluids (Yin). When Qi is too weak to pump and circulate blood and fluids, water begins to pool, especially in the lower body. At the same time, Yin deficiency creates a subtle internal heat that thickens and dries fluids, so the edema tends to be less dramatic than in pure Yang deficiency but becomes stubborn and recurrent. The result is lingering swelling that comes with fatigue, a dry mouth, and night sweats - signs that the body is depleted on both fronts.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red or pale-red body, thin or absent coating, dry surface, possibly cracked. Pulse: fine (thin) and weak, often rapid.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Qi and Yin Deficiency means the body lacks both energy and fluids. Overwork and stress drain Qi further, while spicy and greasy foods consume Yin and generate heat. Excessive exertion burns through both resources. Rest, a cool environment, and moistening foods replenish Qi and Yin, while gentle movement encourages circulation without depleting reserves. This pattern requires a slow, steady rebuild rather than aggressive treatment.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat small, frequent meals of easy-to-digest, warm foods. Include yin-nourishing and gently diuretic ingredients like pear, winter melon, coix seed, and adzuki bean. Avoid spicy, greasy, and overly drying foods. Gentle exercise such as Tai Chi or slow walking helps Qi flow without depleting Yin. Keep the bedroom cool to reduce night sweats.

## คำถามที่พบบ่อย (Frequently Asked Questions)
### Can TCM really help with swelling from heart failure?
Yes - TCM has been used for centuries to manage edema from heart weakness. By addressing the underlying deficiency (whether it’s Kidney Yang, Heart Qi, or blood stasis), herbs and acupuncture can help the body move and transform fluids more effectively. Many patients find their swelling decreases and their energy improves, often allowing them to reduce reliance on diuretics under their doctor’s supervision.

### Is it safe to take Chinese herbs with my heart medications?
In most cases, yes, but it’s essential that both your TCM practitioner and cardiologist know exactly what you’re taking. Some herbs, particularly those that move blood, can interact with blood thinners such as warfarin. Others, like Fu Zi (aconite), must be processed and prescribed by a trained professional to avoid toxicity. Always bring a complete list of your medications to your TCM consultation.

### How quickly will the swelling go down?
You may notice a reduction in swelling within the first 2-4 weeks, but lasting change depends on rebuilding the underlying strength of your Heart and Kidneys. Acute water retention often responds faster than the fatigue and breathlessness, which can take several months to improve significantly. Consistency with herbs and lifestyle adjustments is key.

### Do I need acupuncture or just herbs?
Both can be effective, but they work best together. Herbal formulas are the primary tool for correcting deep internal imbalances, while acupuncture can immediately help move Qi and fluid, reduce pain, and calm the spirit. Most practitioners recommend weekly acupuncture sessions alongside daily herbs, at least in the initial phase of treatment.

### What dietary changes should I make?
Generally, you’ll want to avoid cold, raw foods and heavy, greasy meals that tax digestion. Favor warm, cooked foods like soups and stews with ginger, cinnamon, and small amounts of lean protein. Foods that gently support fluid metabolism - such as adzuki beans, Job’s tears, and winter melon - can be helpful. Salt restriction is just as important in TCM as it is in conventional advice.

### Can TCM cure my heart failure?
TCM does not claim to cure structural heart damage, but it can significantly improve quality of life by reducing edema, boosting energy, and supporting the heart’s function. Many patients find they feel stronger, have fewer hospital visits, and can manage their condition more comfortably when TCM is integrated with their conventional care.

## การวิจัยทางวิทยาศาสตร์สมัยใหม่ (Modern Scientific Research)
1. **Zhen Wu Tang for chronic heart failure: a systematic review and meta-analysis**
   *วารสารวิชาการ: Liang X, et al. Zhen Wu Tang for chronic heart failure: a systematic review and meta-analysis. Journal of Ethnopharmacology. 2015.*
   A meta-analysis of multiple Chinese RCTs found that adding Zhen Wu Tang to standard treatment significantly improved cardiac function (as measured by left ventricular ejection fraction) and reduced edema compared to standard treatment alone. The effect was consistent across studies, though the overall evidence quality was moderate due to methodological limitations.

2. **Shengmai (a traditional Chinese herbal medicine) for chronic heart failure**
   *วารสารวิชาการ: Chen J, et al. Shengmai (a traditional Chinese herbal medicine) for chronic heart failure. Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews. 2014.*
   A Cochrane systematic review of Shengmai preparations (injection and oral) for chronic heart failure. The review included several RCTs and found that Shengmai may improve symptoms and exercise tolerance when used as an adjunct to conventional therapy, but the evidence was graded as low quality due to risk of bias and small sample sizes.

3. **Clinical effect of Xue Fu Zhu Yu Tang combined with Western medicine on chronic heart failure with blood stasis syndrome**
   *วารสารวิชาการ: Wang Y, et al. Clinical effect of Xue Fu Zhu Yu Tang combined with Western medicine on chronic heart failure with blood stasis syndrome. Chinese Journal of Integrative Medicine. 2018.*
   This RCT evaluated Xue Fu Zhu Yu Tang added to standard heart failure therapy in patients with blood stasis pattern. The combination group showed greater improvement in 6-minute walk distance, reduction in edema, and lower BNP levels than the control group, suggesting a role for blood-moving formulas in cardiac edema with stasis.
