## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนปัจจุบัน (Conventional Medicine View)
### คำอธิบาย (Description)
Benign prostatic hyperplasia (BPH) is a non-cancerous enlargement of the prostate gland that commonly occurs as men age, particularly after 50. It can cause lower urinary tract symptoms such as a weak or interrupted urine stream, difficulty starting urination, frequent urination (especially at night), urgency, and a feeling that the bladder never fully empties. Diagnosis is typically based on symptom questionnaires like the International Prostate Symptom Score, a digital rectal exam, and sometimes urine flow studies or ultrasound to measure prostate size and post-void residual volume.

### การรักษาหลัก (Treatments)
Standard Western treatments include watchful waiting for mild symptoms, medications, and procedures. Alpha-blockers (e.g., tamsulosin) relax the prostate and bladder neck to improve flow, while 5-alpha-reductase inhibitors (e.g., finasteride) slowly shrink the prostate by blocking hormones. For more severe cases, minimally invasive therapies or surgery (such as transurethral resection of the prostate) are used to remove obstructing tissue.

## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนจีน (Traditional Chinese Medicine View)
### ความเข้าใจเกี่ยวกับการแพทย์แผนจีน (TCM Understanding)
TCM does not view the prostate as an isolated organ. It falls under the domain of the Kidney system and the Lower Burner, which together govern water metabolism, urination, and reproductive function. As men age, the Kidney Qi - especially Kidney Yang, the body's warming, transformative fire - naturally declines. When this fire dims, fluids are not properly transformed and can accumulate as Dampness, a heavy, sluggish pathogenic factor that settles in the lower body and creates the sensation of incomplete emptying or dribbling.
The Spleen also plays a central role. It transforms food and fluids into usable Qi and moisture. If the Spleen is weakened by poor diet, overwork, or aging, it fails to manage fluids, and Dampness builds up. Over time, that Dampness can combine with Heat (from spicy food, alcohol, or emotional stress) to create an inflamed, irritated state, or it can combine with Cold (from a deeper Yang deficiency) to create a cold, boggy obstruction. In other cases, the long-standing stagnation of Qi and fluids causes Blood to congeal, forming a hard, fixed mass that directly presses on the urinary passage.
This is why the same Western diagnosis of BPH can look so different from person to person. One man may have burning, urgent urination that flares after a rich meal (Damp-Heat), while another has a weak, cold dribble that worsens with fatigue (Spleen and Kidney Qi Deficiency), and a third has sharp, fixed pain with a dark purple tongue (Blood Stagnation). TCM treatment is therefore never one-size-fits-all - it is rebuilt around the pattern driving the enlargement.

### การวินิจฉัยโดยแพทย์แผนจีน (Practitioner Diagnosis)
A TCM practitioner begins by listening carefully to the details of your urination. How strong is the stream? Is there urgency, burning, or a feeling of incomplete emptying? These clues, along with your overall energy, temperature sensations, and the look of your tongue, help separate one pattern from another.
When the main complaint is a weak, dribbling stream that gets worse with fatigue, and you also feel tired, short of breath, or have achy lower back and knees, the root is likely Spleen and Kidney Qi Deficiency. The tongue is pale with a thin white coat, and the pulse feels weak and deep. This is the most common early picture in aging men.
If urination is urgent, painful, and burning, with dark yellow urine, the pattern shifts to Damp-Heat in the Lower Burner. You might also feel thirsty, have a bitter taste in the mouth, or notice that symptoms flare after alcohol or spicy food. The tongue is red with a yellow, greasy coating, and the pulse is rapid and slippery.
In older men who always feel cold, the problem may be Cold-Dampness in the Lower Burner. Here urine is clear and copious, especially at night, and you may have cold hands and feet, a pale puffy tongue with a white coat, and a slow, deep pulse. This pattern often sits on top of a deeper Kidney Yang Deficiency.
When the obstruction has been present for a long time, Blood Stagnation can develop. The hallmark is sharp, stabbing pain during urination, sometimes with blood or dark clots, and a hard sensation in the lower abdomen. The tongue looks purplish with dark spots, and the pulse feels wiry or rough. This is a less common but important complication.
A sensation of heaviness in the pelvis, scanty urine that is hard to pass, and a feeling of fullness rather than sharp pain points to Phlegm in the Lower Burner. The tongue is swollen with a thick, greasy coat, and the pulse is slippery. This pattern is often seen in men who are overweight and have a history of chronic dampness.

### Spleen and Kidney Qi Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/spleen-and-kidney-qi-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Weak, hesitant urine stream, Frequent urination, especially at night (nocturia), Chronic fatigue and lack of stamina, Pale, sallow complexion, Lower back soreness and weak knees
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Overwork and exhaustion, Cold, raw foods and iced drinks, Prolonged standing, Excessive sexual activity, Emotional stress and worry
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warm, cooked meals and soups, Adequate rest and short naps, Gentle walking, stretching, or tai chi, Warmth on the lower abdomen and back
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  As men age, the Kidney Qi naturally declines, and if the Spleen is also weak from poor diet or overwork, the body loses its ability to transform and transport fluids. This leads to an accumulation of dampness in the lower burner. Over time, the stagnant dampness congeals around the prostate, causing it to enlarge and press on the urethra. At the same time, the deficient Qi is too weak to hold urine properly or to push it out with force, resulting in frequent, hesitant urination and a weak stream.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale and swollen with teeth marks, white moist coating. Pulse: deep, weak, and thin (细 ruò), especially at the right Spleen position and both Kidney positions.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Overwork, prolonged standing, and excessive sexual activity drain the Kidney and Spleen Qi, directly worsening the root deficiency that causes the prostate to swell. Cold and raw foods damage the Spleen's digestive fire, allowing more dampness to accumulate. Rest, warmth, and nourishing cooked meals rebuild Qi and support the body's ability to transform fluids. Gentle movement keeps Qi circulating without exhausting it, which helps prevent stagnation in the lower burner.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat warm, cooked foods like soups, stews, and congees. Avoid raw, cold, and greasy foods that weaken the Spleen. Incorporate small amounts of warming spices such as ginger and cinnamon. Prioritize rest and avoid overwork; a short afternoon nap can be restorative. Gentle daily movement like walking or tai chi helps Qi flow without depleting it. Keep the lower back and abdomen warm, especially at night.

### Damp-Heat in the Lower Burner
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/damp-heat-in-the-lower-burner)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Burning or stinging pain during urination, Dark yellow or cloudy urine, Frequent, urgent urination with small volume, Heavy, dragging sensation in the lower abdomen and pelvis, Thirst with no desire to drink much, Worse after spicy or greasy food and alcohol
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Spicy, greasy, or fried foods, Alcohol and coffee, Hot, humid weather, Prolonged sitting or sedentary lifestyle, Holding urine
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Drinking plenty of water, Cool, light clothing and environment, Bland, cooling foods (e.g., mung beans, cucumber), Urinating as soon as the urge arises, Gentle walking, stretching, or tai chi
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Damp-Heat is a combination of heavy, sticky moisture and inflammatory heat that sinks to the lower body. When it settles in the bladder and prostate, it irritates the tissues and causes swelling, making urination feel burning, urgent, and incomplete. The thick, turbid nature of Damp-Heat also clouds the urine, giving it a dark yellow or cloudy appearance.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red body with a yellow greasy coating thickest at the root, slightly swollen. Pulse: slippery (滑 huá) and rapid (数 shuò), more pronounced at the rear (Chi) position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Spicy, greasy foods and alcohol directly generate Damp-Heat, which pours downward into the bladder and prostate, igniting burning and urgency. Cool drinks and bland foods help drain Dampness and clear Heat, while holding urine lets Damp-Heat accumulate and irritate the tissues. Urinating promptly flushes the bladder, and gentle movement prevents stagnation that traps Damp-Heat.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Avoid alcohol, coffee, spicy dishes, and greasy fried foods, which fuel Damp-Heat. Favour light, cooling foods such as mung beans, barley, cucumber, and watermelon. Drink plenty of warm water throughout the day to flush the urinary tract. Do not hold urine; respond to the urge promptly. Gentle daily walking helps keep Qi and fluids moving.

### Cold-Dampness in the Lower Burner
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/cold-dampness-in-the-lower-burner)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Profuse clear urine, worse at night, Cold feeling in the lower abdomen and legs, Heavy, dragging sensation in the lower abdomen and pelvis, Aversion to cold environments, Loose or watery stools
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Cold weather, Sitting on cold or damp surfaces, Cold, raw foods and iced drinks, Overwork and exhaustion
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warmth on the lower abdomen and back, Warm, cooked meals and soups, Gentle walking, stretching, or tai chi, Dry, warm living spaces
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  In older men, the Kidney Yang that powers the body's waterworks naturally weakens. Without that warming fire, Cold and Dampness settle into the lower burner, much like icy sludge clogging a pipe. This sludge obstructs the bladder's ability to open and close smoothly, so urine dribbles out weakly and frequently, especially at night when Yin and Cold are strongest.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale, swollen body with teeth marks, thick white greasy coating, excessively moist. Pulse: deep, slow, and soggy, especially in the Chi (rear) positions.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Cold and Dampness are the core obstacles here - they congeal Qi and block the bladder's opening. Warmth thaws that freeze, while cooked, spiced foods and gentle movement fan the body's fading internal fire. Cold foods, icy environments, and overexertion douse that fire further, allowing sludge-like Dampness to accumulate and worsen urinary obstruction.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Keep the lower back and abdomen warm - a belly band or warm compress can make a real difference. Favour cooked, warm foods like soups and stews with ginger, cinnamon, and fennel. Avoid icy drinks, raw salads, dairy, and greasy foods, which add more Dampness to an already sluggish system. A daily gentle walk helps Qi move, but avoid sitting for hours on cold or damp surfaces.

### Phlegm in the Lower Burner
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/phlegm-in-the-lower-burner)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Cloudy or turbid urine, Heavy, dragging sensation in the lower abdomen and pelvis, Thick white greasy tongue coating, especially at the root, Feeling of heaviness in the body, Loose, sticky stools, Cold feeling in the lower abdomen and legs
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Cold, damp weather, Spicy, greasy, or fried foods, Prolonged sitting or sedentary lifestyle, Overeating, Cold, raw foods and iced drinks
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warmth on the lower abdomen and back, Light, easily digestible foods, Gentle walking, stretching, or tai chi, Warm, cooked meals and soups
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Phlegm-Damp is a thick, sticky accumulation that forms when the Spleen fails to transform fluids. When this turbid substance settles in the lower burner, it physically obstructs the bladder's Qi transformation and narrows the urinary passage, leading to sluggish, difficult urination and a sensation of heaviness. The cloudy, turbid urine directly reflects the presence of Phlegm turbidity, while the cold nature of this pattern further slows the flow, making urination scanty and effortful.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale swollen body with teeth marks, thick white greasy coat especially at the root, wet surface. Pulse: slippery (滑 Huá) and deep (沉 Chén), may be slow, especially deep and slippery at the rear position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Phlegm is formed when the Spleen cannot transform fluids, a process worsened by cold and heavy foods. Warmth and gentle movement activate Qi, helping to transform and drain accumulated Dampness and Phlegm. Conversely, cold and greasy foods directly add to the burden, creating more turbid Phlegm that settles in the lower burner and obstructs urination.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Avoid cold, raw, and greasy foods, which directly generate Phlegm and Dampness. Favor warm, cooked meals with aromatic spices like ginger and leeks. Gentle daily movement-such as walking-helps Qi flow and transforms Dampness. Keeping the lower abdomen warm with a hot water bottle or warm clothing can ease the heavy sensation.

## คำถามที่พบบ่อย (Frequently Asked Questions)
### Can acupuncture and herbs actually shrink an enlarged prostate?
TCM does not aim to 'shrink' the prostate in the way a 5-alpha-reductase inhibitor does. Instead, it works to resolve the underlying Dampness, Phlegm, or Blood Stagnation that is causing the gland to swell and obstruct urine flow. As the pathogenic factors clear and Qi is restored, the prostate tissue often becomes less congested and the urethra less compressed. Many men find their symptoms improve significantly even if the anatomical size changes only modestly.

### Will I need to take herbs forever?
Not necessarily. During the active treatment phase, herbs are taken daily to correct the pattern. Once symptoms stabilize and the underlying imbalance is largely resolved, the formula is often tapered or switched to a maintenance dose, or you may only take herbs during flare-ups. Some men with deep-rooted Kidney deficiency choose to take a gentle tonic formula long-term to support healthy aging, but this is optional and discussed with your practitioner.

### Can I combine TCM with my alpha-blocker medication?
Yes, TCM is often used alongside conventional BPH medications. However, some herbs can have mild blood-pressure-lowering effects, so combining them with alpha-blockers may increase the risk of dizziness. Your TCM practitioner should know your full medication list and will select herbs accordingly. Never stop or adjust your prescribed medication without consulting your medical doctor. As your symptoms improve, you and your doctor can discuss whether a dose reduction is appropriate.

### How many acupuncture sessions will I need?
A typical course begins with weekly sessions for 6-12 weeks, after which the frequency may reduce to every two weeks or monthly as your condition stabilizes. Acute flare-ups of urgency or discomfort may respond after just a few treatments, but lasting change in urinary function usually requires the full course, especially when a deficiency pattern is present.

### Does diet really affect BPH symptoms?
Yes, and in TCM, diet is considered a first-line therapy. Cold, raw foods and iced drinks weaken the Spleen and promote Dampness, which can worsen urinary sluggishness. Spicy, greasy, and fried foods create Damp-Heat, often triggering burning and urgency. Alcohol is particularly aggravating. By shifting to warm, cooked meals and avoiding trigger foods, many men notice a clear reduction in symptoms even before herbs or acupuncture begin.

### Is TCM safe if I'm on blood thinners?
Some herbs used for Blood Stagnation (such as Tao Ren or Hong Hua) can have mild anticoagulant effects. If you take warfarin, aspirin, or other blood thinners, you must inform your TCM practitioner before starting treatment. They will avoid or substitute those herbs and work with your prescribing doctor to ensure safety. Acupuncture is generally safe but your practitioner should know about any bleeding risk.

### What if I suddenly can't urinate at all?
A complete inability to urinate (acute urinary retention) is a medical emergency. Do not wait for a TCM appointment - go to the emergency room immediately. See our Safety section for a full list of red-flag symptoms that require urgent medical attention.

## การวิจัยทางวิทยาศาสตร์สมัยใหม่ (Modern Scientific Research)
1. **The effects of a Chinese herbal medicine (VGHBPH0) on patients with benign prostatic hyperplasia: A pilot study** ([ลิงก์](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7526580))
   *วารสารวิชาการ: Chen YC, et al. The effects of a Chinese herbal medicine (VGHBPH0) on patients with benign prostatic hyperplasia: A pilot study. J Tradit Complement Med. 2020;10(5):460-467.*
   This pilot study evaluated a multi-herb formula containing Epimedium, Phellodendron, Anemarrhena, Cinnamon and other herbs in men with moderate BPH. After 12 weeks, the treatment group showed significant improvements in IPSS, quality of life scores, and maximum urinary flow rate compared to baseline, with a trend toward reduced prostate volume. The formula was well tolerated with no serious adverse events.

2. **Herbal formula (Danggui Beimu Kushen Wan) for prostate disorders: a systematic review of classical literature** ([ลิงก์](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6804470))
   *วารสารวิชาการ: Park J, et al. Herbal formula (Danggui Beimu Kushen Wan) for prostate disorders: a systematic review of classical literature. Integr Med Res. 2019;8(4):247-253.*
   This systematic review examined classical Chinese medical texts to trace the historical use of Danggui Beimu Kushen Wan for urinary difficulty and prostate disorders. The study found consistent references to the formula’s effectiveness for conditions characterized by Damp-Heat and Blood Stagnation in the lower burner, providing a textual foundation for its modern application in BPH and chronic prostatitis.

3. **Research Progress on the Treatment of Benign Prostatic Hyperplasia with Traditional Chinese Medicine** ([ลิงก์](https://doi.org/10.1055/s-0044-1782605))
   *วารสารวิชาการ: Zhang L, et al. Research Progress on the Treatment of Benign Prostatic Hyperplasia with Traditional Chinese Medicine. Chin Med Cult. 2024;7(1):45-52.*
   This review article summarizes the TCM understanding of BPH as a disorder of Kidney deficiency, Dampness, Blood Stasis and Qi stagnation. It outlines the main pattern differentiations and corresponding herbal formulas such as Ba Zheng San, Zhen Wu Tang, and Gui Zhi Fu Ling Wan, and discusses clinical studies supporting their use. The authors conclude that TCM offers a multi-target, personalized approach with a favorable safety profile.
