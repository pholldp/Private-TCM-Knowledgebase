## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนปัจจุบัน (Conventional Medicine View)
### คำอธิบาย (Description)
Atrophic gastritis is a chronic inflammatory condition in which the stomach lining thins and loses its glandular cells, reducing the production of stomach acid and digestive enzymes. It is most often caused by long-standing infection with Helicobacter pylori or by an autoimmune attack on the stomach's own cells. Over time, the normal stomach lining may be replaced by intestinal-type tissue (intestinal metaplasia), which is considered a precancerous change.
Common symptoms include upper abdominal discomfort, bloating, nausea, loss of appetite, and early satiety. Many people have no symptoms until the atrophy is advanced. Diagnosis is confirmed by upper endoscopy with biopsy, which allows the doctor to assess the degree of atrophy and metaplasia and to test for H. pylori.

### การรักษาหลัก (Treatments)
Conventional management focuses on eliminating H. pylori if present, usually with a combination of antibiotics and acid-suppressing drugs. For autoimmune atrophic gastritis, there is no specific treatment to reverse the atrophy; instead, doctors monitor for complications such as vitamin B12 deficiency and pernicious anemia, and may recommend periodic surveillance endoscopies to check for precancerous changes.
Symptom relief may be attempted with acid reducers or prokinetic agents, but there is no medication proven to regrow the stomach lining.

## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนจีน (Traditional Chinese Medicine View)
### ความเข้าใจเกี่ยวกับการแพทย์แผนจีน (TCM Understanding)
In TCM, atrophic gastritis falls under the umbrella of "stomach stuffiness" (痞满, pǐ mǎn), "stomach pain" (胃痛, wèi tòng), and "epigastric fullness." The condition is understood not as a single disease but as the end result of long-standing imbalances that have worn down the stomach's lining.
The core is always a deficiency - usually of Spleen Qi or Stomach Yin - which leaves the stomach vulnerable to attack by pathological factors like Qi stagnation, Damp-Heat, or Blood stasis. It's this combination of deficiency and excess that makes the condition both chronic and progressive.
The Spleen and Stomach are the central engine of digestion. When their Qi becomes deficient, food and fluids aren't transformed properly, leading to bloating, fatigue, poor appetite, and a pale puffy tongue. Over time, this malnourishment thins the stomach lining, much like a plant withering in poor soil. This is the most common root pattern, and it often sets the stage for other problems to develop.
Chronic inflammation can also consume the stomach's Yin - the cooling, moistening fluids that protect its lining. Without enough Yin, the stomach becomes dry and overheated, causing a persistent burning sensation, dry mouth, and a red peeled tongue.
Meanwhile, emotional stress plays a huge role: when Liver Qi stagnates, it often invades the Stomach, causing distension, belching, and pain that radiates to the ribs. If this stagnation generates heat, the burning intensifies and a bitter taste appears.
Damp-Heat creates a sticky, inflamed environment that erodes the stomach lining, often linked to H. pylori infection. And when any of these conditions drag on long enough, the Qi stagnation can deepen into Blood stasis - a critical turning point where microcirculation is damaged and sharp, fixed pain appears, along with a dark purplish tongue.
In TCM, Blood stasis is seen as a key step toward intestinal metaplasia and dysplasia, which is why moving Blood and transforming stasis is a major therapeutic goal in advanced cases.

### การวินิจฉัยโดยแพทย์แผนจีน (Practitioner Diagnosis)
A TCM practitioner begins by asking what the discomfort actually feels like and when it strikes. A dull, persistent fullness that worsens after eating points in a very different direction than a burning sensation that flares in the afternoon. The tongue and pulse are then examined to confirm the pattern, because the stomach’s internal environment leaves clear marks on both.
When the main complaint is a nagging epigastric fullness, poor appetite, and deep fatigue, and the tongue is pale with a thin white coat while the pulse feels weak or thready, the root is Spleen and Stomach Qi Deficiency. This person usually feels a little better after rest and warmth, and worse after exertion or a heavy meal.
If instead the discomfort is a burning ache with a dry mouth, a red tongue that lacks coating, and a thin rapid pulse, the diagnosis shifts to Stomach Yin Deficiency. The stomach lining has lost its moistening nourishment, so symptoms often intensify in the evening and the person may crave cold drinks, yet cold fluids rarely bring lasting relief.
Emotional stress that triggers distension, belching, and a sense of movement under the ribs points to Liver Qi Stagnation invading the Stomach.
When that stagnation generates heat, the pain turns more burning, the mouth tastes bitter, the tongue becomes red with a yellow coat, and the pulse turns wiry and rapid.
A heavy, stuffy sensation with nausea and a sticky yellow tongue coat suggests Damp-Heat, often linked to H. pylori.
In chronic cases, a fixed, stabbing pain with a dark purplish tongue and stasis spots reveals Stomach Blood Stagnation.

### Spleen and Stomach Qi Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/spleen-and-stomach-qi-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Poor appetite and early satiety, Bloating and fullness that worsens after eating, Loose, unformed stools, Persistent fatigue and lack of strength, Sallow or pale complexion
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Overwork and fatigue, Irregular eating habits, Cold or raw foods, Damp weather, Stress and worry
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Rest after eating, Warmth on the abdomen, Warm, easily digestible foods, Small, frequent meals, Gentle abdominal massage
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  The Spleen and Stomach are the body's main engine for turning food into Qi and Blood. When they become deficient, digestion slows, fluids accumulate as dampness, and the stomach lining loses its nourishment. Over time, this weakens the stomach's ability to receive and ripen food, leading to the gradual thinning and atrophy of the lining, along with the hallmark bloating, poor appetite, and fatigue.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale, puffy body with tooth marks and a thin white coating. Pulse: weak, soft, and especially empty at the right middle (Guan) position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: <p>The Spleen thrives on warmth and routine, so warm foods and regular meals support its transformative function, while cold and raw foods dampen its fire. Rest and gentle massage conserve Qi and encourage smooth flow, whereas overwork and stress drain Qi and cause stagnation. A calm, consistent lifestyle is the foundation of recovery for this pattern.</p>
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat warm, cooked foods like soups, congees, and steamed vegetables. Avoid raw, cold, greasy, and dairy-heavy foods that burden the Spleen. Eat at regular times and stop before you feel completely full. Gentle daily movement such as walking or tai chi helps circulate Qi without depleting it.

### Stomach Yin Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/stomach-yin-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Burning or dull burning epigastric pain, Dry mouth and throat, thirst for small sips, Feeling hungry but not wanting to eat, Red tongue with little or no coating, possibly cracked, Dry stools or constipation
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Spicy, fried, or dry foods, Alcohol and coffee, Late nights and overwork, Stress and emotional upset
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Cool, moist foods (pears, congee), Small sips of warm water, Adequate sleep and rest, Avoiding spicy, fried foods
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Chronic gastritis can wear down the stomach's Yin-the cooling, moistening fluids that protect its lining. When Stomach Yin becomes deficient, the stomach lacks lubrication and nourishment, leading to a persistent dry, burning sensation. The empty heat that arises from Yin deficiency further irritates the lining, causing pain and a feeling of hunger that isn't satisfied by eating because the stomach can't handle solid food well.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red, thin body, dry centre, little or no coating with possible cracks. Pulse: fine (Xi) and rapid (Shu).
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Stomach Yin is like the coolant and lubricant for the digestive tract. Spicy, fried, and drying foods, alcohol, and coffee directly consume these fluids and generate heat, worsening the burning pain and dryness. Late nights and overwork drain Yin further, as the body’s restorative processes occur during sleep. Stress and emotional tension can also generate internal heat that depletes Yin. Conversely, moistening foods and small sips of water gently replenish fluids without overwhelming the weakened stomach. Rest and sleep allow Yin to regenerate, calming the empty heat and soothing the lining.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat small, frequent meals of moistening, easily digestible foods like congee, steamed pears, tofu, and honey. Avoid spicy, fried, and dry foods, as well as alcohol and coffee, which further consume Yin and generate heat.
Drink small sips of warm water throughout the day rather than large amounts at once. Prioritize rest and avoid staying up late, as nighttime is when Yin is replenished.

### Liver Qi Stagnation invading the Stomach
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/liver-qi-stagnation-invading-the-stomach)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Distending epigastric pain, Pain radiating to the rib area, Worse with stress and frustration, Frequent belching or acid reflux, Irritability and frequent sighing, Sensation of a lump in the throat
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Stress and anger, Cold raw foods, Greasy or heavy meals, Skipping meals, Rushing while eating
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Gentle exercise, Warm meals, Stress management, Light stretching, Warm compress on abdomen
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  The Liver is responsible for keeping Qi moving smoothly throughout the body. When stress or frustration builds, Liver Qi becomes stuck and then surges sideways, invading the Stomach. This rebellious energy disrupts the Stomach's normal downward movement, causing bloating, distension, and pain that often spreads to the ribs. Over time, this chronic disharmony impairs digestion and can contribute to the thinning of the stomach lining seen in atrophic gastritis.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: normal or slightly red body, often with redder sides, and a thin white coat. Pulse: wiry (弦 xián), like a taut guitar string, most noticeable at the left middle position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Stress and anger are the main triggers because they directly cause Liver Qi to stagnate and invade the Stomach, so calming the mind and soothing emotions is essential. Cold and raw foods burden the Spleen and Stomach, making it harder for Qi to descend, while heavy meals create further stagnation. Warm meals and gentle movement encourage the smooth flow of Qi, and eating in a relaxed state prevents the Liver from lashing out at the digestive system.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat small, warm meals at regular times and avoid eating when upset or rushed. Steer clear of cold, raw, greasy, and spicy foods that can further disrupt Qi flow. Gentle daily movement like walking, tai chi, or yoga helps keep Liver Qi moving smoothly. Prioritize stress management through meditation, deep breathing, or enjoyable hobbies.

### Liver Qi Stagnation that transforms into Heat
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/liver-qi-stagnation-that-transforms-into-heat)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Burning epigastric pain or discomfort, Bitter taste in the mouth, Acid reflux or sour belching, Irritability and explosive anger, Dry mouth and thirst for cold drinks, Distending pain or discomfort in the ribcage area
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Anger and frustration, Spicy, fried, or greasy meals, Alcohol and coffee
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Cooling foods and drinks, Stress relief and relaxation, Gentle movement like walking
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When frustration and stress go unresolved, Liver Qi stagnates and eventually generates heat - like pressure building until something ignites. This heat flares upward and crosses into the Stomach, scorching the lining and disrupting its ability to descend smoothly. The result is a burning sensation in the upper abdomen, sour belching, and a bitter taste, all classic signs of heat invading the Stomach in atrophic gastritis.

### Damp-Heat in Stomach and Spleen
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/damp-heat-in-stomach-and-spleen)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Bloating and fullness in the upper abdomen, Thick, yellow, greasy tongue coating, Sticky or incomplete bowel movements, Bitter or sticky taste in the mouth
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Greasy, fried foods, Spicy and sweet foods, Alcohol and coffee, Humid weather, Stress and emotional upset
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Light, bland foods, Bitter greens and barley, Gentle walking, Dry, cool weather
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Dampness and Heat lodge in the Stomach and Spleen, gumming up the middle burner’s ability to transform food and fluids. The turbid damp-heat creates a sticky, inflamed environment that steadily erodes the stomach lining, contributing to the atrophy seen in chronic gastritis. This terrain is closely linked to H. pylori infection, which thrives in such damp-heat conditions. The result is a sensation of stuffy fullness, nausea, and the hallmark thick yellow greasy coating.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red body, possibly swollen with teeth marks, thick yellow greasy coating especially at centre and root. Pulse: soggy (Ru) and rapid (Shu), often slippery (Hua).
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Greasy, sweet, and spicy foods directly generate Dampness and Heat in the body, worsening the already congested Stomach environment. Alcohol and coffee add Heat, while humid weather introduces external Dampness, overwhelming the Spleen’s ability to transform fluids. Stress stagnates Liver Qi, which then invades the Stomach, further impairing digestion. Conversely, light, bland foods reduce the Damp-Heat load, and bitter greens and barley help clear Heat and drain Dampness. Gentle exercise stimulates Qi flow, helping the Spleen to transform and transport dampness out of the body.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Avoid greasy, fried, spicy, sweet, and dairy foods that generate Dampness and Heat. Eat light, easily digestible foods like congee, steamed vegetables, and small amounts of lean protein. Include bitter greens, barley, and mung beans to help clear Heat and drain Dampness. Avoid alcohol and coffee. Drink warm water or light herbal teas like chrysanthemum. Gentle daily exercise like walking helps move Qi and transform Dampness.

### Stomach Blood Stagnation
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/stomach-blood-stagnation)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Sharp stabbing or cutting pain, Pain fixed in one location that does not move, Pain worsens with pressure on the abdomen, Pain worse after eating, Pain worse at night, Dark or purplish tongue with stasis spots, Black tarry stools or vomiting dark material
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Heavy, greasy, or fried foods, Cold and raw foods, Emotional upset and anger, Overeating or large meals, Lying down immediately after eating
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warm compress on the abdomen, Gentle walking after meals, Small, warm, frequent meals, Stress reduction and relaxation
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When chronic gastritis drags on, the stomach's Qi flow can stall, and over time that stagnation deepens into the blood vessels. This creates microcirculatory damage and blood stasis in the stomach lining - like a traffic jam that causes sharp, fixed pain. The stabbing discomfort, dark tongue, and even black stools all point to blood that is stuck and no longer flowing smoothly.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: dark purple or dusky body with purple stasis spots, distended sublingual veins, thin white coating. Pulse: choppy (涩, sè) and often wiry (弦, xián), rough like a knife scraping bamboo.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Blood stasis is like a traffic jam in the stomach's vessels - cold and heavy foods congeal blood and make it worse, while warmth and gentle movement help it flow. Stress knots Qi, which then blocks blood, so calming the mind eases the pain. Small warm meals reduce the digestive burden, giving the stomach's circulation a chance to recover.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat warm, easily digestible foods in small, frequent meals - think soups, congees, and steamed vegetables. Avoid cold, raw, and greasy foods that can congeal blood. Gentle daily movement like walking or tai chi helps Qi and blood circulate. Manage stress, as emotional tension can worsen stagnation.

## คำถามที่พบบ่อย (Frequently Asked Questions)
### Can acupuncture and Chinese herbs really reverse atrophic gastritis?
Yes, a growing number of clinical studies show that TCM treatment can not only relieve symptoms but also reverse atrophy and even intestinal metaplasia in some patients. The key is to identify the correct pattern - for example, strengthening Spleen Qi, nourishing Stomach Yin, or clearing Damp-Heat - and then stick with treatment for several months. Reversal is most likely when the condition is caught early and when lifestyle changes are made alongside herbs and acupuncture.

### How long does it take to see results?
Symptom relief often begins within 2-4 weeks. However, actual reversal of the stomach lining changes is a slower process. Most practitioners recommend a minimum of 3 months of consistent treatment before a follow-up endoscopy to assess structural improvement. The timeline varies depending on the pattern: excess patterns like Damp-Heat may respond faster, while long-standing Qi or Yin deficiency requires more time to rebuild the foundation.

### Do I need to change my diet?
Yes, dietary adjustments are an essential part of treatment. In general, you'll want to eat warm, cooked, easily digestible foods and avoid raw, cold, greasy, spicy, and overly sweet items that burden the Spleen. Small, frequent meals are better than large ones. Your TCM practitioner will give you specific guidance based on your pattern - for example, someone with Stomach Yin deficiency benefits from moistening foods like pears and congee, while someone with Damp-Heat should avoid rich, heavy foods and alcohol.

### Can I take Chinese herbs while on antibiotics for H. pylori?
Yes, TCM herbs can be safely combined with H. pylori eradication therapy. In fact, some herbs have been shown to enhance the effectiveness of antibiotics and reduce side effects like nausea and diarrhea. However, it's important to inform both your TCM practitioner and your gastroenterologist about all medications you're taking. Space herbs and antibiotics at least one hour apart to avoid any potential interaction in absorption.

### Will the atrophy come back after I stop treatment?
If the underlying imbalances are fully corrected and you maintain a healthy diet and lifestyle, the atrophy is unlikely to return. However, if you return to old habits - chronic stress, irregular eating, excessive cold or greasy foods - the patterns can recur. Many patients continue with a maintenance herbal formula or occasional acupuncture sessions to stay balanced, especially if they have a constitutionally weak Spleen or Stomach.

### What does TCM treatment involve - just herbs, or acupuncture too?
Most practitioners use both herbal medicine and acupuncture together for the best results. Herbs are taken daily to work on the internal imbalances, while acupuncture (usually once or twice a week) helps regulate the nervous system, improve gastric motility, and reduce inflammation. Moxibustion - the warming of acupuncture points with a dried herb - is often added for cold or deficient patterns. Your practitioner will tailor the approach to your specific pattern and comfort level.
