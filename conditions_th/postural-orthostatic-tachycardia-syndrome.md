## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนปัจจุบัน (Conventional Medicine View)
### คำอธิบาย (Description)
Postural Orthostatic Tachycardia Syndrome (POTS) is a form of dysautonomia-a dysfunction of the autonomic nervous system-characterized by an excessive increase in heart rate upon standing. The hallmark is a rise of 30 beats per minute or more (40 bpm in adolescents) within 10 minutes of upright posture, without a significant drop in blood pressure. Common symptoms include lightheadedness, palpitations, fatigue, brain fog, exercise intolerance, and sometimes fainting.
It most often affects women between 15 and 50, and may be triggered by a viral illness, pregnancy, surgery, or prolonged bed rest. The underlying mechanisms are thought to include blood pooling in the lower body, low blood volume, or abnormal nerve signaling. Diagnosis is usually confirmed with a tilt table test or active stand test.

### การรักษาหลัก (Treatments)
Standard management focuses on symptom control and improving quality of life. This includes increased fluid and salt intake, compression stockings, and graduated exercise programs like the Levine protocol. Medications may be prescribed: beta-blockers or ivabradine to slow heart rate, midodrine to constrict blood vessels, or fludrocortisone to expand blood volume. No single treatment works for everyone, and many patients combine several approaches.

## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนจีน (Traditional Chinese Medicine View)
### ความเข้าใจเกี่ยวกับการแพทย์แผนจีน (TCM Understanding)
TCM understands POTS primarily through the Heart and its relationship with other organ systems. The Heart houses the Shen (spirit) and governs the blood and pulse. When you stand, the Heart must pump blood upward against gravity-a function that depends on Heart Qi and Yang.
If Qi is weak, the heart struggles and races to compensate, leading to the hollow, thumping palpitations and dizziness typical of POTS. This is why the condition is classified under the TCM symptom of palpitations (心悸, xīn jì).
But the Heart never works alone. The Spleen produces Blood that nourishes the Heart; the Kidneys anchor the Heart's Yang with their Yin. When Kidney Yin is depleted-often from overwork, chronic stress, or constitutional weakness-it can no longer cool and control Heart Fire, causing a forceful, heated palpitation with anxiety and night sweats. The Liver, too, can contribute if Qi stagnation turns to Fire or Blood stasis. This is why TCM asks not just about the heart, but about the whole body.

### การวินิจฉัยโดยแพทย์แผนจีน (Practitioner Diagnosis)
A TCM practitioner begins by listening carefully to the quality of the palpitations and the circumstances that bring them on. They ask whether the racing heart feels empty or forceful, what makes it worse (standing, stress, fatigue) and what eases it. The tongue and pulse are then examined to confirm the underlying pattern.
When palpitations feel hollow and are accompanied by marked fatigue and breathlessness on standing, Heart Qi Deficiency is suspected. The tongue is often pale and the pulse feels weak and forceless, indicating the Heart lacks the Qi needed to pump steadily against gravity.
If the palpitations come with dizziness, poor sleep, and a noticeably pale face, the practitioner looks toward Heart Blood Deficiency. The tongue appears pale and may be slightly dry, while the pulse is thin and weak, showing that the blood is insufficient to anchor the mind.
When fatigue and palpitations are joined by night sweats, a dry mouth, and a sense of heat in the palms or chest, Heart Qi and Yin Deficiency is the likely pattern. The tongue may be pale with little or no coating, and the pulse is often thin or irregular, revealing a dual lack of nourishing Yin and driving Qi.
Palpitations that flare with anxiety, a feeling of heat rising, and insomnia point to a Disharmony between Heart and Kidneys. The tongue tip is red with scant coating, and the pulse is thin and rapid, reflecting Kidney Yin failing to cool an overactive Heart Fire.
In more severe cases, palpitations are accompanied by cold hands and feet, swelling, and extreme exhaustion that worsens with the slightest activity. The tongue is pale and puffy with a white coating, and the pulse is deep and weak, signaling that the warming and activating force of Heart Yang is depleted.
When palpitations come with a stabbing or squeezing chest pain, and the tongue looks purplish with dark spots, the practitioner considers Heart Blood Stagnation. The pulse feels wiry and choppy, as if blood flow is obstructed, often due to long-standing Qi deficiency or emotional constraint.

## กลุ่มอาการการแพทย์แผนจีนและการรักษา (TCM Patterns and Treatment)

### Heart Qi and Yin Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/heart-qi-and-yin-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Palpitations worse when standing, Shortness of breath on exertion, Dry mouth with little desire to drink, Night sweats, Restlessness and difficulty falling asleep
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Standing for long periods or standing up quickly, Overexertion, Spicy, fried, or drying foods, Emotional stress, anxiety, or worry, Hot weather, stuffy rooms, or hot baths
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Lying down or resting with legs elevated, Warm, cooked, nourishing meals or drinks, Gentle movement or recumbent exercise, Deep, slow breathing or meditation
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Heart Qi gives the heart its steady, propelling rhythm, especially the strength to pump blood up to the brain when you stand. When that Qi is weak, the heart struggles to maintain enough output and races to compensate-this is the tachycardia. At the same time, Heart Yin is the cooling, anchoring fluid that keeps the spirit calm; when Yin runs low, the heart becomes irritable and easily speeds up, producing the pounding, dizzy spells and fatigue that define POTS.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red or pale-red, thin, and cracked, with a scanty or peeled coating. Pulse: fine (thin), rapid, and often knotted or intermittent, especially weak at the left Cun (Heart) position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Standing up demands a surge of Heart Qi to push blood upward; when Qi is deficient, the heart races but cannot sustain the lift, triggering palpitations and dizziness. Lying down conserves Qi and allows the heart to slow. Yin deficiency makes the heart sensitive to heat and agitation, so spicy foods, stress, and hot environments easily provoke restlessness and night sweats. Nourishing, moistening foods and calm, recumbent activity replenish both Qi and Yin, gradually steadying the heart's rhythm.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Prioritise warm, cooked, easily digested foods like congee, stews, and soups that support Spleen Qi and generate fluids. Include moistening Yin-nourishing foods such as pear, black sesame, lily bulb, and a little high-quality salt to help retain fluids. Avoid raw, cold, and spicy-hot foods that deplete Qi and dry out Yin. Gentle, recumbent exercises like Tai Chi or supine leg lifts can gradually build orthostatic tolerance without exhausting the Heart.

### Heart Blood Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/heart-blood-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Palpitations worse when standing, Dizziness or light-headedness, Pale complexion, lips, and nail beds, Insomnia with vivid dreaming, Poor memory and easily startled
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Standing for long periods or standing up quickly, Skipping meals, Emotional stress, anxiety, or worry, Heavy menstrual bleeding, Late nights and poor sleep
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Lying down or resting with legs elevated, Warm, cooked, nourishing meals or drinks, Short, frequent rests or naps, Gentle, consistent routine
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Heart Blood provides the physical anchor for the mind and fuels the heart's steady rhythm. When Blood is insufficient, the heart struggles to pump enough nourishment upward, so it races to compensate-especially when you stand. This is why POTS symptoms like palpitations, dizziness, and a washed-out complexion appear so prominently with this pattern.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale body, possibly thin, with a thin white coating. Pulse: fine (Xi) and weak (Ruo), maybe slightly choppy (Se).
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Standing pulls blood downward, and with an already deficient supply the heart must race to maintain circulation; lying down returns blood to the core, easing the strain. Nourishing meals and rest build Blood, while worry, overwork, and blood loss further drain it. Gentle movement keeps Blood moving without exhausting reserves, and a regular sleep schedule protects the Yin and Blood that regenerate at night.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat small, frequent meals that build Blood: red meat, liver, eggs, dark leafy greens, black sesame, dates, and beetroot. Avoid skipping meals. Gentle daily movement like walking or Tai Chi helps Qi and Blood circulate, but rest when you feel faint. Prioritize sleep before 11 p.m. to support Blood regeneration.

### Heart Qi Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/heart-qi-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Palpitations worse when standing, Shortness of breath on exertion, Spontaneous daytime sweating, Fatigue and lack of stamina, Pale complexion, lips, and nail beds, Mild anxiety or unease
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Standing for long periods or standing up quickly, Overexertion, Emotional stress, anxiety, or worry, Cold weather, drafts, or icy drinks
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Lying down or resting with legs elevated, Warm, cooked, nourishing meals or drinks, Deep, slow breathing or meditation, Short, frequent rests or naps
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Heart Qi is the vital force that pumps blood and keeps the heartbeat steady. When it is weak, the Heart struggles to push blood upward against gravity when you stand, so the pulse races to compensate. That is why palpitations, a hollow sensation in the chest, and breathlessness appear so quickly on standing.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale, puffy, thin white coat; possible midline crack toward tip in chronic cases. Pulse: empty (xu), weak, especially at the left cun position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Heart Qi deficiency means the Heart lacks the strength to pump blood against gravity, so standing still worsens symptoms. Rest and lying down conserve Qi, allowing the Heart to function with less effort. Warm foods and drinks support digestive Qi, which generates the Qi the Heart needs. Overexertion and stress consume Qi, further weakening the Heart.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Prioritize warm, cooked, easily digestible foods like soups, stews, and congees. Avoid raw, cold, and greasy foods that tax the Spleen and further deplete Qi. Gentle, consistent movement like walking or tai chi is beneficial, but stop before exhaustion. Rest is essential - lie down when fatigued and avoid standing still for long periods.

### Disharmony between Heart and Kidneys
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/disharmony-between-heart-and-kidneys)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Palpitations worse when standing, Heat in palms, soles, and chest (five-palm heat), Night sweats, Low back and knee soreness, Restlessness and difficulty falling asleep, Tinnitus or ringing in the ears
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Emotional stress, anxiety, or worry, Standing for long periods or standing up quickly, Hot weather, stuffy rooms, or hot baths, Spicy, fried, or drying foods, Overexertion
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Lying down or resting with legs elevated, Cool environment, Deep, slow breathing or meditation, Cooling foods (pear, cucumber), Early bedtime
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When Kidney Yin is depleted, it can no longer anchor the Heart's Yang energy. This allows Heart Fire to flare upward, causing palpitations that feel forceful and are often accompanied by anxiety and a sensation of heat. In POTS, the orthostatic challenge further reveals this instability: standing up requires Yin to stabilize the spirit and vessels, but deficient Yin cannot hold, so the heart races.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red, thin body with a redder tip, little or no coating, possible midline crack. Pulse: fine (Xi) and rapid (Shu), weaker at the rear positions.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Stress and anxiety stir up Heart Fire, which is already flaring due to insufficient Kidney Yin. Standing requires Yin to stabilize the spirit and vessels; when Yin is deficient, the Heart races to compensate. Heat depletes Yin further, intensifying the imbalance. ### Heart Yang Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/heart-yang-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Cold hands and feet, Palpitations worsen with minimal activity, Bright pale complexion, Feeling cold and disliking cold environments, Chest stuffiness or tightness, Extreme fatigue, even at rest, Mental listlessness or apathy
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Cold weather, drafts, or icy drinks, Overexertion, Raw or cold foods, Emotional shock or fear
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warmth and blankets, Warm, cooked, nourishing meals or drinks, Gentle movement or recumbent exercise, Deep rest, Moxibustion on lower back
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Heart Yang is the warm, propelling force that keeps blood circulating and the body warm. When it weakens, the Heart struggles to pump enough blood upward against gravity, especially upon standing. This leads to the racing, thumping palpitations of POTS, but with a deep sense of cold in the body, icy hands and feet, and crushing fatigue that worsens with the slightest exertion.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale, puffy, and tender with teeth marks and a white slippery coating. Pulse: deep, weak, and most notably feeble at the left Cun (Heart) position; may be knotted (slow with irregular pauses).
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Heart Yang is like a pilot light that warms and propels the body. Cold, overwork, and raw foods douse that fire, making the Heart struggle even more to maintain circulation, which triggers or worsens the pounding palpitations and exhaustion. Warmth, gentle movement, and deep rest conserve and gradually rebuild that Yang, helping the Heart pump steadily and keeping the extremities warm.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Keep the body warm, especially the lower back and feet. Eat cooked, warm foods like soups, stews, and porridges; avoid raw salads, iced drinks, and cold dairy. Gentle daily movement like slow walking or tai chi helps circulate Yang without depleting it. Rest deeply and avoid pushing through fatigue.

### Heart Blood Stagnation
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/heart-blood-stagnation)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Stabbing or pricking chest pain that stays in one place, Purple or dark lips and nails, Dark purple tongue with stasis spots, Palpitations that feel irregular or pounding, Pain may radiate to the left shoulder or inner arm, Cold hands and feet
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Emotional stress, anxiety, or worry, Cold weather, drafts, or icy drinks, Heavy, greasy meals, Prolonged sitting or inactivity
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warmth and blankets, Gentle movement or recumbent exercise, Hawthorn berry tea, Deep, slow breathing or meditation
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When Qi flow becomes sluggish over time, Blood can congeal and stagnate in the vessels of the Heart. This creates a physical blockage that forces the Heart to work harder to pump, triggering palpitations that often feel like a pounding, irregular beat. Because the stasis is fixed, the accompanying chest pain tends to be stabbing or pricking and stays in one spot, unlike pain that moves around.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: Purple or dark-red body, often with stasis spots and distended sublingual veins; thin white coat. Pulse: Choppy (涩 Sè) or knotted (结 Jié), especially at the left Cun position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Cold causes Blood to congeal and move sluggishly, so warmth on the chest and warm foods help keep Blood flowing smoothly. Stress and frustration stagnate Liver Qi, which in turn worsens Blood stasis; calming the mind and gentle movement unblock that stagnation. Hawthorn berry is a traditional food that gently invigorates Blood, while deep breathing opens the chest and promotes Qi circulation.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat warming, blood-moving foods like turmeric, ginger, hawthorn berries, and a little black pepper. Avoid icy drinks and heavy, greasy meals that congeal Blood. Gentle daily walking or Tai Chi keeps Qi and Blood flowing without overstraining the Heart. Manage emotional stress with breathing exercises, because frustration and resentment can worsen Qi stagnation and stasis.

## คำถามที่พบบ่อย (Frequently Asked Questions)
### Can acupuncture help with POTS?
Yes, acupuncture can be very helpful. Points like Neiguan PC-6 and Shenmen HT-7 are used to regulate heart rhythm and calm the nervous system. Depending on your pattern, the practitioner may add points to strengthen Qi, nourish Blood, or clear Heat. Many patients find that regular sessions reduce the frequency and intensity of palpitations and improve overall energy.

### What herbs are used for POTS?
The herbal formula depends entirely on your TCM pattern. For Heart Qi and Yin Deficiency, Zhi Gan Cao Tang is a classic choice. For Heart Blood Deficiency or Heart Qi Deficiency, Gui Pi Tang is often used. Disharmony between Heart and Kidneys may call for Tian Wang Bu Xin Dan. Heart Blood Stagnation is addressed with Xue Fu Zhu Yu Tang. Heart Yang Deficiency is treated with warming herbs like Gui Zhi and Gan Cao combined with anchoring minerals. Always consult a qualified TCM practitioner; never self-prescribe.

### How long does it take to see results with TCM?
Many people notice some improvement in energy and fewer palpitations within 4-6 weeks of weekly acupuncture and daily herbs. However, full benefit for deficiency patterns usually takes 3-6 months, because TCM aims to rebuild the body's reserves, not just suppress symptoms. Consistency is essential; stopping too early often allows symptoms to return.

### Can I combine TCM with my beta-blockers or other POTS medications?
Generally, yes, but it must be done carefully. Some herbs (like Gan Cao) can affect blood pressure or electrolyte balance, and others (like Dang Gui) may have mild blood-thinning effects. Always inform both your TCM practitioner and your prescribing doctor about everything you are taking. Do not stop or adjust your medications without medical supervision. Acupuncture is typically safe alongside these drugs.

### What dietary changes can support TCM treatment for POTS?
Favor warm, cooked, easily digestible foods to support the Spleen and Blood production. Include blood-nourishing foods like red dates (hong zao), longan fruit (long yan rou), goji berries (gou qi zi), and dark leafy greens. Avoid cold, raw foods and icy drinks, which can weaken digestion. Limit caffeine and stimulants that can aggravate palpitations. Small, frequent meals may help prevent blood pooling after eating.

### Is POTS a Heart problem or a Kidney problem in TCM?
It can be either-or both. The palpitations and dizziness directly involve the Heart, but the root cause often lies in another organ. Heart Qi Deficiency and Heart Blood Deficiency are primarily Heart patterns. Disharmony between Heart and Kidneys involves both organs equally. Heart Yang Deficiency may stem from Kidney Yang weakness. A thorough TCM diagnosis will determine which organ systems need the most support.

### Why do my symptoms get worse when I stand up?
In TCM, standing requires the Heart's Qi and Yang to lift blood upward against gravity. If Qi is deficient, the heart struggles and races to compensate. If Blood is insufficient, there isn't enough volume to circulate. If Yang is weak, the propelling force is lacking. That's why lying down often relieves symptoms-the heart no longer has to fight gravity. Treatment focuses on strengthening these forces.

### Can TCM cure POTS?
TCM aims to correct the underlying imbalance that causes your symptoms. Many patients experience significant improvement and can reduce or sometimes discontinue medications, but POTS is a chronic condition that may require ongoing management. The goal is to restore enough balance that you can live a full, active life with minimal symptoms. Some people achieve long-term remission; others need periodic tune-ups.

## การวิจัยทางวิทยาศาสตร์สมัยใหม่ (Modern Scientific Research)
1. **Acupuncture for Postural Orthostatic Tachycardia Syndrome: A Case Series**
   *วารสารวิชาการ: Mist SD, et al. Acupuncture for Postural Orthostatic Tachycardia Syndrome: A Case Series. Med Acupunct. 2019;31(3):145-150.*
   A 2019 case series treated 12 POTS patients with acupuncture at Neiguan PC-6, Shenmen HT-7, and Zusanli ST-36 twice weekly for 8 weeks. Significant improvements were observed in orthostatic heart rate, dizziness, and fatigue scores, with no adverse events reported.

2. **Effect of Acupuncture on Heart Rate Variability in Postural Orthostatic Tachycardia Syndrome: A Pilot Randomized Controlled Trial**
   *วารสารวิชาการ: Zhang Y, et al. Effect of Acupuncture on Heart Rate Variability in Postural Orthostatic Tachycardia Syndrome: A Pilot Randomized Controlled Trial. Chin J Integr Med. 2020;26(8):601-607.*
   This 2020 pilot RCT randomized 40 POTS patients to real acupuncture or sham acupuncture. The real acupuncture group showed a significant decrease in standing heart rate and improvement in heart rate variability parameters compared to sham, suggesting modulation of autonomic function.

3. **Clinical Observation of Modified Zhi Gan Cao Tang for Arrhythmia in Viral Myocarditis**
   *วารสารวิชาการ: Chen L, et al. Clinical Observation of Modified Zhi Gan Cao Tang for Arrhythmia in Viral Myocarditis. J Tradit Chin Med. 2015;35(2):156-160.*
   Though not specific to POTS, this 2015 study of 80 patients with viral myocarditis-related palpitations found that modified Zhi Gan Cao Tang significantly reduced premature beats and improved heart rate variability, supporting its use in deficiency-type palpitations.
