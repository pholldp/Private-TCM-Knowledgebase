## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนปัจจุบัน (Conventional Medicine View)
### คำอธิบาย (Description)
Rhinitis is inflammation of the nasal mucous membranes, causing symptoms like congestion, runny nose, sneezing, and post-nasal drip. It can be allergic (triggered by pollen, dust mites, or pet dander) or non-allergic (due to infections, irritants, or medications). Diagnosis is usually clinical, based on symptom history and nasal examination; allergy testing may be done for suspected allergic triggers. Acute episodes often resolve within a week, but chronic rhinitis can persist for months and significantly impact quality of life.

### การรักษาหลัก (Treatments)
Conventional management depends on the cause. Allergic rhinitis is treated with antihistamines, intranasal corticosteroids, and decongestants. Non-allergic rhinitis may respond to nasal saline irrigation, ipratropium sprays, or avoidance of triggers. Bacterial sinusitis may require antibiotics. For severe or refractory cases, immunotherapy or surgery to improve sinus drainage may be considered.

## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนจีน (Traditional Chinese Medicine View)
### ความเข้าใจเกี่ยวกับการแพทย์แผนจีน (TCM Understanding)
In TCM, the nose is the opening of the Lungs, so rhinitis is first understood as a Lung problem. The Lungs govern the diffusion of Qi and fluids throughout the body. When an external pathogen like Wind-Heat invades, it obstructs this diffusing function, causing fluids to accumulate and transform into thick, yellow nasal discharge. This is the acute pattern - a sudden attack that often comes with fever and sore throat, much like a cold that settles in the sinuses.
But chronic rhinitis often involves deeper organ systems. The Gallbladder channel runs to the head, and emotional stress or a rich diet can generate Damp-Heat there. This heat and dampness steam upward into the sinuses, producing thick, yellow, foul-smelling mucus and a bitter taste in the mouth. Similarly, the Spleen and Stomach are responsible for transforming food and fluids; when they are weakened by poor diet or fatigue, dampness and phlegm accumulate. This turbid dampness can rise and stagnate in the nose, causing persistent, sticky discharge that worsens after eating or when tired.
Long-standing rhinitis can also have a deficiency root. Spleen Qi Deficiency means the body cannot properly manage fluids, leading to chronic phlegm-damp. Kidney Yin Deficiency, often from overwork or aging, creates empty heat that rises and inflames the sinuses. Because the same symptom - nasal discharge - can arise from so many different mechanisms, TCM treatment is never one-size-fits-all. The color, consistency, and timing of the discharge, along with the patient's overall constitution, guide the practitioner to the correct pattern.

### การวินิจฉัยโดยแพทย์แผนจีน (Practitioner Diagnosis)
To figure out which pattern is at work, a TCM practitioner starts with the quality of the nasal discharge. Is it yellow and thick, or white and watery? Is it acute and accompanied by fever, or lingering and tied to fatigue? These clues immediately separate patterns driven by excess heat from those rooted in deficiency.
When the discharge is yellow, the onset is recent, and you have a fever, headache, or a sore throat, the pattern is likely Wind-Heat invading the Lungs. The tongue appears red with a thin yellow coating, and the pulse feels floating and rapid. This is an external attack that needs to be cleared and vented.
If the mucus is thick, yellow, and even purulent, along with a bitter taste in the mouth, irritability, or rib-side discomfort, Damp-Heat in the Gallbladder is the prime suspect. A wiry, rapid pulse and a red tongue with a greasy yellow coating confirm that stagnant Gallbladder heat is rising to the nose.
Copious yellow, turbid mucus that feels heavy and sticky, together with chest tightness and a heavy head, suggests Damp-Heat in the Stomach and Spleen. The tongue coating is thick and greasy, and the pulse is slippery and rapid. This pattern reflects digestive dampness steaming upward into the sinuses, often after rich or greasy meals.
For chronic rhinitis with persistently sticky white or yellowish discharge and a feeling of congestion that never fully clears, Damp-Phlegm in the Lungs is often the diagnosis. The tongue coating is thick and sticky, and the pulse is slippery. Here, long-standing phlegm-damp obstructs the Lung’s ability to open the nasal passages.
Lingering white and sticky discharge, or sometimes yellow, combined with poor appetite, fatigue, and loose stools, points to Spleen Qi Deficiency. The tongue is pale and swollen with teeth marks, and the pulse is weak and thin. The root problem is a weak digestive system that fails to transform fluids, so discharge persists even without strong heat.
In prolonged or recurrent rhinitis that never fully heals, the discharge may be scanty and yellow, and you might notice dizziness, tinnitus, or a dry throat. The tongue is red with little or no coating, and the pulse is thin and rapid. This is Kidney Yin Deficiency with empty heat, where the body’s cooling resources are depleted and mild heat flares upward.

### Wind-Heat invading the Lungs
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/wind-heat-invading-the-lungs)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Yellow or turbid nasal discharge, Fever with mild chills, Sore throat, Thirst
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Fried, greasy, or spicy foods, Alcohol, Wind and drafts, Overexertion or overwork
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Rest and relaxation, Cool drinks, Steam inhalation, Avoiding wind
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Wind-Heat is an external pathogen that enters through the nose and mouth, attacking the Lungs first. The Lungs govern the nose and are responsible for diffusing Qi and fluids. When Wind-Heat obstructs the Lung's diffusing function, fluids stagnate and turn into thick, yellow nasal discharge, while the heat causes fever, sore throat, and thirst. The body's defensive Qi fights the invader at the surface, producing chills and a floating pulse.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red tip, thin yellow coating. Pulse: floating (Fu) and rapid (Shu).
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Wind-Heat invades the Lung and obstructs the nasal passages. Spicy and greasy foods create internal heat that worsens the infection, while wind and drafts introduce more pathogen. Rest and cooling fluids help the body clear the heat and support the Lung's diffusing function, while steam inhalation directly opens the nasal orifices.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Drink plenty of warm water and soothing teas like mint or chrysanthemum. Avoid spicy, fried, and greasy foods that generate internal heat. Rest in a well-ventilated room and keep your neck and chest covered to avoid wind. Gentle steam inhalation can help open the nasal passages.

### Damp-Heat in the Gallbladder
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/damp-heat-in-the-gallbladder)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Thick, yellow or green purulent nasal discharge, Bitter taste in the mouth, Irritability and a short temper, Dark, scanty urine, Fullness or discomfort under the ribs
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Fried, greasy, or spicy foods, Emotional stress and anger, Hot, humid weather
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Cooling, bitter foods, Stress reduction, Avoiding alcohol
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When stress, frustration, or a rich diet causes Qi to stagnate in the Gallbladder, it can generate Damp-Heat. The Gallbladder channel runs up to the head, and this hot, damp energy can steam upward into the sinuses. The result is thick, yellow, often foul-smelling nasal discharge that feels stuck and hard to clear.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red body, may be slightly swollen, redder on the sides; yellow greasy coating. Pulse: wiry, slippery, and rapid.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Damp-Heat in the Gallbladder is a condition of excess heat and moisture. Rich, greasy, and spicy foods add more dampness and heat to the body, while emotional stress stagnates Liver Qi, which turns into fire. Cooling, bitter foods and calm habits help drain the excess, allowing the sinuses to clear.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Avoid greasy, fried, spicy, and overly rich foods that generate dampness and heat. Embrace cooling, bitter vegetables like bitter melon and dandelion greens. Manage stress with gentle exercise like walking or tai chi, as emotional tension directly fuels this pattern.

### Damp-Heat in Stomach and Spleen
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/damp-heat-in-stomach-and-spleen)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Thick, yellow, turbid nasal discharge, Bloating and fullness in the upper abdomen after eating, Sticky or incomplete bowel movements, Bitter or sticky taste in the mouth, Heavy, foggy sensation in the head, Bad breath
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Fried, greasy, or spicy foods, Dairy, sugar, and greasy foods, Alcohol, Overeating or eating late at night, Damp or humid weather, Prolonged sitting or inactivity
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Light, easily digested meals, Cooling, bitter foods, Moderate daily exercise, Dry, airy environment, Acupuncture and cupping
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Your Spleen and Stomach are responsible for transforming food and fluids into clear energy. When rich, greasy, or sweet foods overwhelm them, dampness and heat build up like a steamy compost heap. This turbid damp-heat has nowhere to go but upward, where it settles in the sinuses and produces thick, yellow, gluey mucus - the hallmark of damp-heat rhinitis.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red body, possibly swollen with teeth marks, covered by a thick, yellow, greasy coating (especially in the centre and back). Pulse: soggy (Ru) and rapid (Shu), often also slippery (Hua), most pronounced at the right Guan position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Greasy, sweet, and heavy foods directly create dampness and heat in the Stomach and Spleen, much like pouring fuel on a fire. This turbid mixture then rises to the nose as thick mucus. Light, bland foods and bitter greens drain dampness and cool heat, while gentle movement encourages the Spleen to transport and transform fluids instead of letting them stagnate. A dry environment prevents external dampness from adding to the internal burden.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat light, warm, cooked foods like congee, steamed greens, and small amounts of lean protein. Avoid dairy, sugar, fried foods, alcohol, and rich sauces - they are the raw materials for dampness and heat. Gentle daily movement, like walking after meals, helps the Spleen keep Qi moving upward and prevents stagnation.

### Damp-Phlegm in the Lungs
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/damp-phlegm-in-the-lungs)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Copious white sticky nasal discharge, Chronic nasal congestion and post-nasal drip, Cough with easy-to-expectorate white phlegm, Chest tightness and heavy sensation, Fatigue and heavy limbs, Poor appetite and abdominal bloating
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Damp, cold environments, Dairy, sugar, and greasy foods, Overeating or eating late at night, Prolonged sitting or inactivity
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warm, dry weather, Light spicy foods (ginger, pepper), Gentle exercise or walking, Steam inhalation
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  The Lungs open into the nose and are responsible for spreading Qi and fluids. When the Spleen is weak and fails to transform fluids, Dampness accumulates and condenses into Phlegm. This sticky Phlegm-Damp then settles in the Lungs, obstructing the nasal passages and producing the persistent, white, gluey discharge and congestion typical of chronic rhinitis.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale, swollen body with teeth marks and a thick, white, greasy coating that looks moist. Pulse: slippery (滑 huá), like beads rolling under the finger, and often soft or soggy.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Damp-Phlegm is a heavy, sticky pathogen that thrives in cold, damp conditions and is worsened by foods that burden the Spleen. Warmth and movement help the Spleen transform fluids and keep Qi flowing, while spicy and aromatic foods disperse the thick Phlegm that clogs the Lungs and nose. Avoiding dairy and grease starves the Dampness at its dietary source.

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Sticky, lingering nasal discharge (white or pale yellow), Poor appetite and abdominal bloating, Fatigue, heavy limbs, and loose stools, Pale, puffy tongue with tooth marks, Symptoms worsen after eating or when tired
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Overeating or eating late at night, Cold, raw, or greasy foods, Overexertion or overwork, Damp or humid weather, Stress and worry

- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:

Fatigue and stress also deplete Spleen Qi. Rest, warm meals, and gentle movement conserve and gently support the Spleen's ability to transform fluids, reducing the underlying dampness that fuels the nasal discharge.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat warm, cooked, and easily digestible foods like soups, stews, and congee. Avoid cold, raw, and greasy foods that burden the Spleen. Eat smaller, regular meals and avoid overeating. Gentle daily movement like walking supports Qi flow without depleting energy.

### Kidney Yin Deficiency

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Scanty, sticky yellow nasal discharge, Dizziness or lightheadedness, Tinnitus (ringing in the ears), Dry mouth and throat, especially at night, Night sweats, Soreness and weakness of the lower back and knees
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Overwork and late nights, Spicy, fried, or heating foods, Stress and worry, Hot, dry environments
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Adequate sleep and early nights, Cool, humidified air, Eating moistening foods (pears, tofu), Gentle exercise or walking
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  The Kidneys are the root of Yin and water in the body. When Kidney Yin is depleted over time - often from chronic illness, overwork, or aging - the cooling, moistening function fails, and empty heat rises upward. This virtual fire can flare into the nose, causing a lingering, low-grade inflammation with scanty yellow discharge, dryness, and a sense of heat. Because the Kidneys also anchor Qi, their weakness often leaves the nose vulnerable to repeated mild flare-ups that never fully resolve.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red, thin body with little or no coating, dry surface, possible cracks. Pulse: fine (xi) and rapid (shu), especially weak at the left Chi (rear) position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Kidney Yin is like the body's cooling reserve. Rest and sleep allow the Kidneys to restore Yin, while moistening foods directly replenish fluids and calm the empty heat that rises to the nose. Overwork, late nights, and emotional strain burn through Yin, and heating foods add fuel to the fire, making the nasal symptoms flare. A cool, calm environment helps settle the upward disturbance.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Prioritize rest and aim for a regular, early bedtime - late nights drain Kidney Yin. Eat moistening, cooling foods such as pears, tofu, black sesame, seaweed, and barley. Avoid spicy, fried, or heavily roasted foods that create more heat. Gentle, restorative movement like Tai Chi or slow walking supports the Kidneys without depleting them.

## คำถามที่พบบ่อย (Frequently Asked Questions)
### Can acupuncture really help with sinus congestion?
Yes. Acupuncture points like Yingxiang (LI-20) on the face and Hegu (LI-4) on the hand directly influence the nasal passages and can provide rapid relief of congestion. Many patients notice their nose opening during or shortly after a session. Regular treatments help reduce the frequency and severity of episodes by addressing the underlying pattern.

### How long does it take for Chinese herbs to work for rhinitis?
For acute rhinitis with yellow discharge, you may feel improvement within a few days. Chronic conditions take longer - typically 4 to 8 weeks for damp-heat patterns, and 3 to 6 months for deficiency patterns. Consistency with daily herbal doses is key, and your formula may be adjusted as your symptoms change.

### Is it safe to combine TCM with my allergy medications?
In most cases yes, but always inform both your TCM practitioner and your prescribing doctor. Antihistamines and nasal steroids can generally be used alongside herbs and acupuncture. Your TCM practitioner may eventually help you reduce reliance on medications, but never stop prescribed drugs abruptly without medical supervision.

### What foods should I avoid if I have chronic rhinitis?
Dairy products, greasy and fried foods, and excessive sweets tend to create dampness and phlegm in the body, which can worsen nasal discharge and congestion. Cold and raw foods can weaken the Spleen's digestive function. Alcohol and spicy foods may aggravate heat patterns. Favor warm, cooked meals with moderate amounts of pungent foods like ginger and scallion to open the nasal passages.

### Can TCM cure chronic sinusitis permanently?
TCM aims to correct the underlying imbalance that allowed the condition to develop. When the pattern is fully resolved - for example, Spleen Qi is strengthened or Damp-Heat is cleared - many patients experience long-term remission. However, if lifestyle or dietary habits that contributed to the pattern return, symptoms may recur. TCM empowers you to understand your constitutional tendencies and manage them proactively.

### Does the treatment involve needles in my face?
Yes, local points like Yingxiang (LI-20) beside the nostrils and Yintang (EX-HN-3) between the eyebrows are commonly used. These are small, superficial needles that most people tolerate well. The sensation is usually a brief pinch or a mild distending feeling, not sharp pain. Your practitioner will ensure you are comfortable.

### I'm pregnant and have bad nasal congestion. Is TCM safe?
Acupuncture is generally safe during pregnancy when performed by a qualified practitioner who avoids certain points. Herbal treatment must be carefully managed - many herbs are contraindicated in pregnancy. Always tell your practitioner if you are pregnant or trying to conceive, so they can tailor the treatment accordingly. For severe symptoms, consult your obstetrician as well.

1. **Acupuncture in patients with seasonal allergic rhinitis: a randomized trial**
   *วารสารวิชาการ: Brinkhaus B, Ortiz M, Witt CM, et al. Acupuncture in patients with seasonal allergic rhinitis: a randomized trial. Ann Intern Med. 2013;158(4):225-234.*
