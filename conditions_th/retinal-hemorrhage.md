## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนปัจจุบัน (Conventional Medicine View)
### คำอธิบาย (Description)
Retinal hemorrhage means bleeding within the light-sensitive tissue at the back of the eye. It can cause sudden vision changes - floaters, blurred vision, dark patches, or even complete vision loss if the macula is involved. Common causes include diabetes, high blood pressure, retinal vein occlusion, trauma, and age-related macular degeneration. Diagnosis is made through a dilated eye exam and imaging like optical coherence tomography or fluorescein angiography, which show the location, size, and likely source of the bleed.

### การรักษาหลัก (Treatments)
Treatment depends on the cause and severity. Small hemorrhages may be watched as the body reabsorbs them over weeks. More serious cases - especially those threatening central vision - are treated with anti-VEGF injections to stop abnormal vessel growth, laser photocoagulation to seal leaking vessels, or vitrectomy surgery to remove blood from the vitreous. Managing the underlying condition, such as controlling blood sugar or blood pressure, is a cornerstone of prevention.

## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนจีน (Traditional Chinese Medicine View)
### ความเข้าใจเกี่ยวกับการแพทย์แผนจีน (TCM Understanding)
TCM sees the eye as intimately connected to the Liver, Kidneys, and Spleen. The Liver opens into the eyes and stores Blood; if Liver Yin is too weak to anchor its Yang, that unmoored Yang can surge upward with heat and force blood out of the retinal vessels. This is why sudden bleeds often follow anger or stress - the Liver's energy rises abruptly and overflows.
The Kidneys store the essence that nourishes all tissues, including the delicate eye vessels. When Kidney Yin runs low, a dry, false heat rises upward, making vessels brittle and prone to slow, oozing bleeds. At the same time, the Spleen is responsible for holding blood inside the vessels. If Spleen Qi is weak - often from poor diet, overwork, or chronic illness - blood can seep out wherever vessels are fragile, causing chronic low-grade bleeding.
Blood stagnation is another key mechanism. When Qi becomes stuck, often from emotional frustration, it can't push blood smoothly through the tiny eye collaterals. The blood congeals, pressure builds, and a vessel ruptures. Each of these mechanisms - heat, deficiency, stagnation - produces a different kind of bleed and a different constellation of body-wide symptoms, which is why TCM practitioners ask about everything from thirst and sleep to bowel habits and menstrual cycles when assessing a retinal hemorrhage.

### การวินิจฉัยโดยแพทย์แผนจีน (Practitioner Diagnosis)
A TCM practitioner starts by asking how the vision change felt when it began - was it sudden or gradual, painless or sharp? The timing, quality, and any accompanying bodily sensations are the first clues that point toward one pattern rather than another.
If the bleeding is accompanied by persistent fatigue, dry eyes, thirst, and a sense of general depletion, the picture often matches Qi and Yin Deficiency. The tongue may appear pale and slightly dry, and the pulse feels weak and thready, reflecting the body’s inability to hold blood and nourish the vessels.
When the onset is abrupt and linked to anger, stress, or a throbbing headache - along with dizziness, a flushed face, and irritability - Liver Yang Rising is likely. The tongue is red with a thin yellow coat, and the pulse is wiry and forceful, showing that rising heat is pushing blood out of the retinal vessels.
A slow, gradual blurring with dry eyes, soreness in the low back and knees, and ringing in the ears points toward Kidney and Liver Yin Deficiency. Here the tongue is red with a scanty coat, and the pulse is thready and rapid. The fragile eye collaterals are not being nourished, making them prone to leak.
If those deficiency signs are joined by a feeling of heat in the palms and soles, night sweats, and a malar flush, the diagnosis shifts to Kidney Yin Deficiency with Empty-Heat Blazing. The tongue is red with little or no coating, and the pulse is rapid and thready, indicating that empty fire is scorching the vessels.
Sharp, fixed eye pain or a history of trauma suggests Qi and Blood Stagnation. The tongue may show dark purple spots, and the pulse feels wiry or choppy. In this pattern, physical blockage rather than weakness is the main culprit, causing vessel rupture from stasis.
Chronic, recurrent bleeding with a pale complexion, poor appetite, and loose stools points to Spleen not controlling Blood. The tongue is pale and puffy, and the pulse is weak. The Spleen’s Qi is too depleted to keep blood inside the vessels, so it leaks into the retina.

### Qi and Yin Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/qi-and-yin-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Blurred vision, Dry eyes, Fatigue and weakness, Shortness of breath, Night sweats, Dry mouth and throat
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Overwork and lack of sleep, Spicy, fried, or greasy foods, Alcohol and coffee, Prolonged screen time, Emotional stress
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Adequate sleep and rest, Warm, nourishing foods like congee, Cool, dark environment, Gentle eye exercises and breaks, Acupuncture and herbal treatment
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When Qi is deficient, the Spleen loses its ability to hold blood within the vessels. At the same time, Yin deficiency generates empty heat that makes the tiny retinal vessels fragile and prone to rupture. The combination of weak containment and heat-damaged vessels allows blood to leak into the retina, causing hemorrhage. The lack of Qi and Yin nourishment also leads to blurred vision, dry eyes, and fatigue.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red or pale-red body, thin or absent coating, possibly cracked. Pulse: fine (thin) and weak, may be rapid.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Overwork and stress deplete Qi, while spicy and drying foods consume Yin fluids, both worsening the underlying deficiency. Rest and nourishing foods help rebuild Qi and Yin, allowing the body to contain blood and heal the fragile vessels. Cooling the environment and avoiding eye strain reduce the demand on depleted resources, giving the eyes a chance to recover.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat warm, easy-to-digest foods like rice congee, steamed vegetables, and small amounts of lean protein. Include goji berries, Chinese yam, and black sesame to nourish Yin. Avoid spicy, fried, and overly hot foods that consume Yin. Prioritize rest and avoid straining the eyes with screens for long periods.

### Liver Yang Rising
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/liver-yang-rising)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Throbbing or distending headache at the temples or top of the head, Dizziness and vertigo, feeling of heavy head and light feet, Irritability and quick temper, Flushed face and red eyes, Sudden blurring or loss of vision with retinal bleeding, Ringing in the ears (tinnitus), Feeling of heat rushing up to the head
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Anger and frustration, Spicy, fried, or greasy foods, Alcohol and coffee, Overwork and lack of sleep, Hot weather or saunas
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Rest in a dark quiet room, Cool environment, Chrysanthemum tea, Avoiding anger and stress
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  The Liver stores Blood and opens into the eyes. When Liver Yin is too weak to anchor its Yang, the unmoored Yang surges upward like a wave, carrying heat and blood toward the head. This sudden upward pressure can rupture the tiny vessels in the retina, causing bleeding that clouds vision.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red body with redder sides, thin dry yellow or scanty coat. Pulse: wiry (弦 xián), often forceful and rapid.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Stress and anger surge Liver Yang upward, which is the very mechanism that forces blood out of the retinal vessels. Cooling, calming habits anchor Yang and reduce pressure on the eyes, while rest and a dark room allow the nervous system to settle, giving the vessels a chance to heal.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Avoid spicy, greasy, and fried foods, as well as alcohol and coffee, which can fan the flames of rising Yang. Favor cooling, calming foods like celery, chrysanthemum tea, goji berries, and cucumber. Prioritize stress management-anger and frustration are direct triggers. Gentle daily movement like tai chi or walking helps smooth Liver Qi without overheating.

### Kidney and Liver Yin Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/kidney-and-liver-yin-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Dry, gritty eyes with blurred vision, Dizziness and ringing in the ears, Soreness and weakness in the lower back and knees, Night sweats and heat in the palms, soles, and chest, Insomnia with vivid dreams, Flushed cheeks, especially in the afternoon
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Overwork and lack of sleep, Prolonged screen time, Spicy, fried, or greasy foods, Emotional stress, Dry, windy environments
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Adequate sleep and rest, Cool, dark environment, Moistening foods (pears, goji berries), Gentle eye exercises and breaks, Avoiding overwork
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  The Liver opens into the eyes, and the Kidneys store essence that nourishes all tissues. When Kidney and Liver Yin are deficient, the eyes lack proper moisture and nourishment. The tiny blood vessels in the retina become dry, brittle, and fragile. Without sufficient Yin fluids to cool and anchor the blood, empty heat can stir, causing blood to seep out of weakened vessels and pool in the retina. This pattern typically develops gradually, with recurrent small bleeds that slowly impair vision.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red body, thin with cracks, little or no coating, dry surface. Pulse: fine, rapid, and wiry; weak at the left Chi (Kidney) position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: <p>Kidney and Liver Yin are easily consumed by overwork, late nights, and excessive screen use, which strain the eyes and the Liver blood. Spicy and heating foods generate internal heat that further dries up Yin fluids, making the retinal vessels more brittle. Emotional stress stirs Liver Yang, which can rise and disturb the fragile vessels. Rest and sleep are the most direct ways to rebuild Yin, while moistening foods replenish the fluids that cool and nourish the eyes. A calm, dark environment reduces sensory stimulation, allowing the body to restore its Yin reserves.</p>
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Prioritize rest and avoid late nights, as sleep is when the body replenishes Yin. Eat moistening, cooling foods like pears, black sesame, goji berries, and bone broth. Reduce screen time and take frequent eye breaks. Gentle exercises such as Tai Chi or walking support Yin without overstimulating Yang. Avoid spicy, fried, and heating foods, as well as alcohol and coffee, which can further deplete Yin fluids.

### Kidney Yin Deficiency With Empty-Heat Blazing
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/kidney-yin-deficiency-with-empty-heat-blazing)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Night sweats, Five-palm heat (hot palms, soles, and chest), Red, dry tongue with little or no coating, Tinnitus and lower back soreness, Mental restlessness and irritability, Dry throat at night, thirst for small sips
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Spicy food and alcohol, Overwork and lack of sleep, Hot weather or saunas, Emotional stress
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Adequate sleep and rest, Cool environment, Cooling, moist foods (cucumber, watermelon), Gentle movement (stretching, walking)
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Kidney Yin is the body's deep cooling reserve. When it runs low, a false, dry heat rises upward, much like a kettle boiling dry. This empty heat scorches the delicate vessels in the retina, making them fragile and prone to oozing, slow bleeds rather than sudden gushes.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red, thin, dry body with little or no coating, possible cracks. Pulse: fine (细 xì) and rapid (数 shuò).
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: <p>Empty heat arises because there isn't enough Yin to anchor the body's warm, active energy. Anything that further depletes Yin or adds heat - like spicy food, alcohol, late nights, or emotional stress - fans the flames and pushes heat upward, worsening retinal bleeding. Cooling foods, proper rest, and a calm mind help rebuild Yin and gently cool the body, reducing the upward pressure that damages the eye vessels.</p>
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Prioritize deep rest and avoid late nights, which burn Yin. Eat cooling, Yin-nourishing foods like black beans, goji berries, cucumber, watermelon, and mulberries. Strictly limit spicy foods, alcohol, and coffee. Gentle movement like tai chi or walking is better than intense, sweaty exercise.

### Qi And Blood Stagnation
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/qi-and-blood-stagnation)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Stabbing or fixed pain in or behind the eye, Feeling of distension or tightness in the chest and ribs, Dark purplish tongue with stasis spots, Irritability and mood swings, Pain that worsens with pressure on the eye
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Anger and frustration, Prolonged inactivity or sitting, Cold, raw foods and iced drinks, Prolonged screen time
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Gentle movement (stretching, walking), Warm compress over the eye, Emotional calm and stress relief, Warm, cooked meals
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When Qi becomes stagnant - often from emotional stress or frustration - it can no longer push blood smoothly through the tiny vessels of the retina. The blood then congeals and blocks the collaterals, causing pressure to build until a vessel ruptures. The result is a retinal bleed that tends to be dark, fixed, and slow to resolve, often accompanied by a stabbing sensation in or behind the eye.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: dark purple body with stasis spots and distended sublingual veins, thin white coat. Pulse: wiry (弦 xián) and choppy (涩 sè), like a taut guitar string scraping bamboo.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Anger and emotional tension knot the Liver Qi, which is the very engine that drives blood through the eye's vessels - when Qi stops, blood congeals and bleeds. Cold and inactivity further slow circulation, making stasis worse. Warmth, gentle movement, and relaxation all encourage Qi to flow smoothly, which pushes stagnant blood out and allows fresh, nourishing blood to reach the retina.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat small amounts of warming, blood-moving spices like turmeric, black pepper, and ginger. Avoid cold, raw, and greasy foods that can congeal blood. Gentle daily movement like walking or tai chi is essential to keep Qi flowing. Protect your eyes from prolonged screen time and practice stress-reducing activities like meditation or deep breathing.

### Spleen not controlling Blood
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/spleen-not-controlling-blood)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Slow, persistent retinal bleeding or floaters, Pale or sallow complexion, Chronic fatigue and weakness, Poor appetite and loose stools, Easy bruising or other bleeding tendencies, Dizziness and shortness of breath
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Overwork and lack of sleep, Cold, raw foods and iced drinks, Emotional stress, Irregular eating habits
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warm, cooked meals, Adequate sleep and rest, Gentle movement (stretching, walking)
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  The Spleen is responsible for keeping blood safely inside the vessels. When Spleen Qi is weak, this holding function fails, and blood can seep out wherever vessels are fragile. In the retina, this shows up as slow, chronic oozing of blood rather than a sudden burst, often accompanied by overall fatigue and a pale complexion.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale, puffy, and tender with teeth marks; thin white coating. Pulse: fine and weak, especially the right middle (Spleen) position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Spleen Qi thrives on regularity and warmth. Overwork, stress, and cold foods further weaken the Spleen's ability to hold blood, worsening the leak. Rest and warm, easy-to-digest meals restore Spleen Qi, strengthening its grip on the blood and allowing the retinal hemorrhage to gradually resolve.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat warm, easily digested meals like congee, soups, and stewed root vegetables. Avoid raw, cold, and greasy foods that burden the Spleen. Gentle walking or tai chi can support Qi without depleting it. Prioritize rest and avoid overwork, especially mental strain.

## คำถามที่พบบ่อย (Frequently Asked Questions)
### Can acupuncture and herbs help absorb the blood faster?
Yes, many TCM formulas and acupuncture points are specifically chosen to move stagnant blood and promote its absorption. Herbs like San Qi (notoginseng) and Dan Shen (salvia) are famous for dissolving blood stasis without causing further bleeding. Acupuncture around the eye and on distal points like Sanyinjiao can improve local circulation and speed up the clearance of blood from the retina. Patients often notice their vision starting to clear within a few weeks of consistent treatment.

### Is it safe to use Chinese herbs while taking blood pressure medication?
In most cases, yes - but you must tell both your TCM practitioner and your prescribing doctor about all medications and supplements you are taking. Some Blood-moving herbs, such as Dan Shen and Chuan Xiong, can have mild antiplatelet effects, which may add to the effects of anticoagulant or antiplatelet drugs like warfarin, aspirin, or clopidogrel. Your TCM practitioner can adjust the formula to avoid interactions and will monitor you closely. Never stop or adjust your blood pressure medication without consulting your doctor.

### How long before I see improvement in my vision?
Most patients begin to notice a gradual lightening of the dark spot or improvement in clarity within 4-6 weeks of starting herbs and acupuncture. The blood absorption happens slowly, and the speed depends on the size and location of the bleed, as well as your overall health. Acute, smaller bleeds tend to clear faster. Chronic, recurrent bleeds with underlying deficiency patterns take longer - often 3-6 months - because the treatment is also working to rebuild the vessel integrity and prevent future episodes.

### Will the bleeding come back after TCM treatment?
TCM's goal is not just to clear this bleed but to correct the underlying imbalance that made your retinal vessels vulnerable in the first place. By strengthening Qi, nourishing Yin, and removing stasis, the treatment aims to reduce the likelihood of recurrence. Many patients find that once their constitution is rebuilt through a full course of herbs and acupuncture, their eyes feel more resilient and they experience fewer vascular events. Maintenance with periodic check-ups and seasonal herbal adjustments can help sustain this.

### What can I eat to support my recovery?
Favor foods that nourish the Liver and Kidneys and help move blood gently: goji berries, chrysanthemum tea, dark leafy greens, black sesame, mulberries, and small amounts of cooked liver. Avoid spicy, greasy, and fried foods, as well as alcohol and smoking, which generate heat and damage Yin. Warm, easily digested meals like congee support the Spleen's ability to hold blood in the vessels. Your TCM practitioner will give you more specific guidance based on your pattern, but these general principles apply to most retinal hemorrhage patients.

### Does acupuncture for retinal hemorrhage involve needles in the eye?
Acupuncture points around the eye, such as Jingming (BL-1) and Qiuhou (EX-HN-7), are needled very gently by trained practitioners. The needles are extremely fine and are inserted just under the skin or into the orbital tissue with great care - it is not painful and does not touch the eyeball itself. Most patients feel a mild sensation of pressure or distention, and many find the experience deeply relaxing. The majority of needles are placed on the body - on the legs, arms, and back - to address the internal pattern causing the bleed.
