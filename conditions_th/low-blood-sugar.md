## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนปัจจุบัน (Conventional Medicine View)
### คำอธิบาย (Description)
Hypoglycemia, or low blood sugar, occurs when blood glucose drops below 70 mg/dL. It's most common in people with diabetes who take insulin or certain diabetes medications, but it can also happen in non-diabetics due to prolonged fasting, excessive alcohol, certain tumors, or hormonal deficiencies.
Symptoms include shakiness, sweating, confusion, rapid heartbeat, and in severe cases, loss of consciousness. Diagnosis is confirmed by measuring blood glucose during symptoms and seeing them resolve after eating.

### การรักษาหลัก (Treatments)
Immediate treatment is to consume fast-acting carbohydrates (glucose tablets, juice, regular soda) to raise blood sugar quickly. For people with diabetes, adjusting insulin or medication doses and meal timing helps prevent episodes. In severe cases where the person cannot swallow, glucagon injection or intravenous dextrose is given. Long-term management focuses on identifying and avoiding triggers, and for non-diabetic hypoglycemia, treating any underlying condition.

## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนจีน (Traditional Chinese Medicine View)
### ความเข้าใจเกี่ยวกับการแพทย์แผนจีน (TCM Understanding)
TCM sees low blood sugar primarily through the Spleen and Stomach, which together form the body's kitchen - they transform food into Qi and Blood, the fuel that powers every cell. When these organs are weak, that kitchen runs slowly, so between meals or when you skip a meal, your body runs out of fuel.
This is why you feel shaky, weak, and lightheaded, often with a poor appetite and bloating. The tongue is often pale and puffy with tooth marks, and the pulse feels weak - signs that the digestive engine isn't producing enough.
But the Spleen isn't the only player. The Heart depends on a steady supply of Blood to stay calm and clear; when the Spleen can't make enough, palpitations, anxiety, and foggy thinking appear. The Liver stores Blood and smooths the flow of Qi; if Blood is deficient, internal Wind stirs, causing the tremors, numbness, and blurred vision that often accompany a crash. So the same low blood sugar can feel very different depending on which organ systems are most affected.
Long-standing blood sugar instability often depletes Yin, the body's cooling, moistening fluids. This creates a dry, overheated state that further exhausts Qi, making blood sugar unstable after exertion or in the evening.
And when rich food, alcohol, or irregular eating weaken the Spleen, it can generate turbid Phlegm - a heavy, clouding substance that rises to the head, causing the mental confusion and heavy-headedness of a severe hypoglycemic episode. That's why one Western diagnosis can have many TCM causes, each with its own tongue and pulse signs.

### การวินิจฉัยโดยแพทย์แผนจีน (Practitioner Diagnosis)
The practitioner first looks for signs of weak digestion-chronic fatigue, poor appetite, bloating, and loose stools. The tongue is often pale with tooth marks and a thin white coat, and the pulse feels weak or deep and thready. This pattern underlies many cases of low blood sugar because the body simply cannot produce enough Qi and blood from food.
Here fatigue is accompanied by signs of dryness: a dry mouth, thirst, and spontaneous sweating. The tongue may be red or slightly swollen with little coating or a thin yellow coat, and the pulse is thin and weak or thin and rapid. This pattern often appears in people with long-standing blood sugar instability where both energy and fluids have been depleted.
When the spleen fails to make enough blood, the heart becomes undernourished. The person complains of palpitations, anxiety, poor memory, and dizziness alongside digestive weakness. The tongue is pale with a thin white coat, and the pulse is thin and weak. Sleep disturbances and a tendency to startle easily are additional clues.
Deep Yin deficiency creates false heat, so besides fatigue there is marked dryness, night sweats, a feeling of heat in the palms and soles, and irritability. The tongue is red with little or no coating, and the pulse is thin and rapid. This pattern can cause sudden blood sugar dips because the body’s cooling and nourishing reserves are exhausted.
When liver blood is too weak to anchor Qi, internal wind stirs. The person may experience tremors, blurred vision, numbness, or even convulsions during a hypoglycemic episode. The tongue is pale red with a thin coat, and the pulse is thin and wiry. This pattern signals a more severe stage where the nervous system is affected.
This pattern is usually triggered by alcohol or overeating. Phlegm-heat clouds the mind, leading to drowsiness, confusion, or loss of consciousness. The tongue has a greasy yellow coating, and the pulse is slippery and rapid. It is a rare but serious pattern that requires immediate attention to clear the phlegm and open the orifices.

### Spleen and Stomach Qi Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/spleen-and-stomach-qi-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Poor appetite, bloating after meals, Loose or soft stools, Shakiness and weakness when hungry, Pale, sallow face, Fatigue that improves with rest
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Skipping meals or prolonged fasting, Overeating or heavy, greasy foods, Raw, cold, or greasy foods/drinks, Emotional stress, worry, or overthinking, Overwork, exhaustion, or lack of sleep
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Small, frequent meals, Warm, easily digested foods (soups, stews, congees), Rest after meals, Gentle exercise (walking, tai chi), Warmth on the abdomen
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Your Spleen and Stomach are the body's kitchen-they turn food into usable energy (Qi and blood). When they are weak, that kitchen runs slowly, so between meals or when you skip a meal, your body runs out of fuel. This is why you feel shaky, weak, and lightheaded, often with a poor appetite and bloating after eating.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: Pale, puffy body with tooth marks and a thin white coating. Pulse: Weak, soft, and empty at the right Guan (Spleen/Stomach) position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: The Spleen needs a steady supply of food to produce Qi and blood. Skipping meals or eating cold, heavy foods drains its already weak fire, causing blood sugar to crash. Warm, easily digested meals conserve Spleen Qi, while rest and gentle movement allow the body to use energy efficiently. Stress and overwork further deplete Qi, which is why calm habits and regular meals are the foundation of recovery.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat small, frequent meals that include complex carbohydrates and protein-never skip a meal. Favor warm, cooked foods like soups, stews, and congees, which are easier for a weak Spleen to digest. Limit raw, cold, and greasy foods that tax digestion. Gentle daily movement like walking after meals supports Qi flow without draining energy.

### Qi and Yin Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/qi-and-yin-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Persistent fatigue and weakness, Dry mouth with little desire to drink, Spontaneous daytime sweating, Night sweats, Heart palpitations, Warm sensation in palms, soles, and chest, Dizziness, Poor appetite
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Skipping meals or prolonged fasting, Overwork, exhaustion, or lack of sleep, Excessive sweating (sauna, heat), Spicy, fried, or greasy foods, Emotional stress, worry, or overthinking
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Small, frequent meals, Warm, easily digested foods (soups, stews, congees), Rest, sleep, and relaxation, Gentle exercise (walking, tai chi), Reducing stress
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When Qi and Yin are both depleted, the body lacks the energy (Qi) to transform food into usable fuel and the cooling fluids (Yin) that keep internal processes stable. The Spleen cannot generate enough Qi and blood to maintain steady blood sugar, so levels crash easily. The underlying Yin deficiency also creates a subtle internal heat that slowly burns through whatever reserves remain, making dips more frequent and recovery harder.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red or pale-red body with a thin or absent coating, dry surface, possibly cracked. Pulse: fine (thin) and weak, often rapid.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: This pattern is a deep depletion of both energy and fluids. Skipping meals starves the Spleen of the raw material it needs to produce Qi and blood, while overwork and sweating drain Qi and Yin directly. Spicy foods create internal heat that further consumes Yin. Rest, gentle movement, and nourishing, easy-to-digest foods help rebuild Qi and Yin, while small frequent meals prevent the blood sugar crashes that occur when reserves are already empty.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat small, frequent meals built around easily digestible foods like congee, steamed vegetables, and moderate amounts of lean protein. Avoid spicy, fried, or drying foods that deplete Yin. Sip warm water or herbal teas such as licorice and chrysanthemum. Gentle daily movement like walking or tai chi helps Qi flow without exhausting reserves. Prioritize rest and avoid overwork.

### Heart and Spleen Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/heart-and-spleen-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Palpitations or racing heart when hungry, Anxiety or feeling easily startled, Poor memory and foggy thinking, Poor appetite with bloating after meals, Loose or unformed stools, Fatigue that is worse after skipping a meal
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Skipping meals or prolonged fasting, Emotional stress, worry, or overthinking, Raw, cold, or greasy foods/drinks, Overwork, exhaustion, or lack of sleep
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Small, frequent meals, Rest, sleep, and relaxation, Warm, easily digested foods (soups, stews, congees), Regular meal times
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When the Spleen is weak, it cannot transform food into enough Qi and Blood to fuel the body. The Heart, which depends on a steady supply of Blood to stay calm and clear, becomes undernourished and unsettled. This dual deficiency means the body lacks a stable energy reserve, so during fasting or stress, blood sugar can crash, triggering dizziness, palpitations, and foggy thinking.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale, tender, and puffy with teeth marks on the edges; thin white coating. Pulse: fine (thin) and weak, especially weak at the left cun (Heart) and right guan (Spleen) positions.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: The Spleen needs a steady supply of warm, cooked nourishment to produce Qi and Blood. Skipping meals or eating cold, hard-to-digest foods depletes Spleen Qi, causing blood sugar to drop. Overthinking and worry damage both the Spleen and the Heart, intensifying palpitations, anxiety, and mental fog. Rest and emotional calm conserve Heart Blood, while small, regular meals keep the Spleen's energy production stable, preventing the sudden crashes.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat warm, cooked meals at regular intervals and never skip breakfast. Small, frequent meals help keep blood sugar steady without overwhelming a weak Spleen. Include gently nourishing foods like rice congee, stewed root vegetables, and a few red dates or longan fruit. Avoid raw, cold, and greasy foods that further weaken digestion. Manage worry and overthinking through gentle walking, meditation, or journaling, because mental strain directly consumes Heart and Spleen energy.

### Empty-Heat caused by Yin Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/empty-heat-caused-by-yin-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Night sweats and afternoon heat, Dry mouth and throat with desire for small sips, Heat in palms, soles, and chest (five-palm heat), Red, thin, dry tongue with cracks and little coating, Irritability and difficulty sleeping, Flushed cheekbones (malar flush)
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Spicy, fried, or greasy foods, Overwork, exhaustion, or lack of sleep, Alcohol and coffee, Emotional stress and anger, Hot, dry environments
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Rest, sleep, and relaxation, Cool, moistening foods, Small, frequent meals, Gentle exercise (walking, tai chi)
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When the body's cooling, nourishing Yin fluids are depleted over time, the digestive system loses its moisture and ability to transform food into stable blood sugar. The resulting emptiness creates a false heat that flares up, particularly in the afternoon and evening. This heat further consumes Qi and fluids, so the body burns through its energy reserves quickly and becomes prone to sudden blood sugar dips, especially after overwork, late nights, or when meals are skipped.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red, thin, and dry, often with cracks and little or no coating. Pulse: thready (fine) and rapid.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Yin is the body's cooling, moistening energy, and it is easily depleted by heat, overexertion, and insufficient rest. Spicy foods, alcohol, and emotional stress all generate internal heat, which further burns through Yin. Late nights prevent the body from rebuilding its Yin reserves. Rest, cool-natured foods, and gentle movement allow Yin to be replenished and keep the empty heat in check, so the body can maintain a steadier supply of blood sugar.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Prioritize cooling, moistening foods like pear, cucumber, watermelon, tofu, and mung beans. Avoid spicy, fried, or roasted foods, alcohol, and coffee, which all consume Yin. Keep a regular meal schedule and never skip meals. Go to bed before 11 p.m., as the hours before midnight are the most restorative for Yin. Gentle practices like Tai Chi or restorative yoga help calm the mind without overheating the body.

### Liver Wind agitating Internally due to Liver Blood Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/liver-wind-agitating-internally-due-to-liver-blood-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Fine trembling of hands and feet, Numbness or tingling in the limbs, Dizziness and blurred vision, Pale or sallow complexion, Symptoms worsen when hungry or after skipping meals
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Skipping meals or prolonged fasting, Emotional stress or frustration, Overwork, exhaustion, or lack of sleep, Excessive sweating or blood loss
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warm, easily digested foods (soups, stews, congees), Rest, sleep, and relaxation, Reducing stress, Regular meal times
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When the Liver lacks sufficient Blood, it cannot anchor the body's Qi. That unanchored Qi can surge upward as internal Wind, producing the trembling, numbness, and dizziness that often strike during a low blood sugar episode. The underlying Blood deficiency also means the brain and muscles are poorly nourished, so even a mild drop in sugar triggers these shaky, light-headed symptoms.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale, thin, and may tremble when extended, with a thin white coating. Pulse: fine (细 xì) and wiry (弦 xián), especially at the left Guan position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Skipping meals depletes the Qi and Blood that the Liver needs to anchor Qi, so internal Wind rises. Emotional stress stirs Liver Qi, further destabilizing it. Rest and nourishing food allow Blood to be replenished, calming the Wind and steadying the body.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat small, frequent meals with blood-nourishing foods like dark leafy greens, red meat, eggs, black sesame, and goji berries. Avoid long gaps without food. Gentle movement like walking or tai chi helps Qi flow without depleting Blood. Prioritize sleep and manage stress to protect Liver Blood.

### Turbid Phlegm Blocks Orifices
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/turbid-phlegm-blocks-orifices)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Clouded or confused consciousness, Rattling phlegm sound in the throat, Slurred or impaired speech, Heavy-headedness or feeling the head is wrapped, Greasy white tongue coating
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Alcohol or overeating, Raw, cold, or greasy foods/drinks, Damp weather, Emotional stress, worry, or overthinking, Skipping meals or prolonged fasting
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warm, easily digested foods (soups, stews, congees), Rest, sleep, and relaxation, Gentle exercise (walking, tai chi), Warm and dry environment
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When the Spleen is weakened by rich food, alcohol, or irregular eating, it fails to transform fluids properly. Dampness accumulates and thickens into turbid phlegm, which can rise to the head and cloud the mind’s orifices. This causes the mental fog, slurred speech, and even loss of consciousness that can accompany a severe hypoglycemic episode.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale, swollen body with teeth marks, thick white greasy-slippery coating. Pulse: slippery (滑 huá), wiry (弦 xián), especially slippery at the right Guan position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Phlegm arises from Spleen weakness and dampness. Greasy, cold, or raw foods create more dampness, while alcohol and overeating overwhelm the Spleen, directly producing turbid phlegm. Warm, light meals and gentle movement support the Spleen’s ability to transform fluids, and rest calms the mind, preventing phlegm from clouding consciousness. Emotional stress stagnates Qi, which further hinders fluid metabolism and thickens phlegm.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Avoid heavy, greasy, sweet, and cold raw foods that generate dampness. Eat warm, cooked meals in small frequent portions to avoid overloading the Spleen. Stay away from alcohol, which directly creates phlegm and heat. Gentle daily movement like walking helps move Qi and clear phlegm from the head.

## คำถามที่พบบ่อย (Frequently Asked Questions)
### Can TCM help if I'm not diabetic but still get low blood sugar?
Yes, this is actually one of TCM's strengths. Non-diabetic hypoglycemia often reflects an underlying Spleen Qi or Qi and Yin deficiency - the digestive system simply isn't producing enough stable energy from food. TCM treatment focuses on strengthening the Spleen and Stomach so your body can maintain blood sugar between meals without crashing. Many non-diabetic patients see fewer episodes and better energy within a few weeks of herbs and acupuncture.

### How does acupuncture raise blood sugar?
Acupuncture doesn't directly inject glucose - instead, it regulates the nervous and digestive systems to improve the body's own blood sugar control. Points like Zusanli ST-36 and Sanyinjiao SP-6 strengthen the Spleen and Stomach's ability to extract energy from food, while points like Guanyuan REN-4 build the body's reserves. Research suggests acupuncture can modulate insulin sensitivity and counter-regulatory hormones, helping to stabilize blood sugar naturally.

### Can I stop using glucose tablets if I'm doing TCM?
No - always keep fast-acting sugar on hand for acute episodes. TCM herbs and acupuncture are not a substitute for emergency treatment. Over time, as your body's ability to maintain blood sugar improves, you may find you need glucose tablets less often, but never stop carrying them without your doctor's approval. Severe hypoglycemia requires immediate sugar, not acupuncture.

### Is TCM safe to combine with my diabetes medications?
Yes, but coordination is essential. Some herbs used in TCM (such as Ren Shen and Huang Qi) can lower blood sugar, which may amplify the effects of insulin or sulfonylureas and increase the risk of hypoglycemia. Always tell both your TCM practitioner and your prescribing doctor about everything you're taking. Your medication doses may need adjustment as your blood sugar stabilizes - but only your doctor should make those changes, with careful monitoring.

### How soon will I feel better?
Most patients notice fewer energy dips and less intense shakiness within 2-4 weeks of starting weekly acupuncture and daily herbs. Over 4-8 weeks, the frequency of hypoglycemic episodes typically drops. For deep, long-standing deficiency patterns, full rebuilding may take 3-6 months, but improvement is usually steady and cumulative.

### Do I have to change my diet?
Diet is a cornerstone of TCM treatment for low blood sugar. You don't need a radical overhaul, but eating warm, cooked meals at regular times makes a big difference. Favor soups, stews, and congees; avoid cold, raw foods and icy drinks that weaken the Spleen. Small, frequent meals (every 3-4 hours) help maintain steady fuel. Your practitioner will give you specific guidance based on your pattern.

### What about severe episodes with confusion or passing out?
Severe hypoglycemia is a medical emergency - use glucagon or call emergency services immediately. TCM can help reduce the frequency of severe episodes over time by strengthening the underlying deficiency, but it does not replace emergency care. For patterns like Turbid Phlegm that cause confusion, specific herbs and points may help clear the mind, but this is preventive, not a rescue treatment.

### Is TCM safe during pregnancy if I have low blood sugar?
Many TCM treatments are safe and even beneficial during pregnancy, as they can support the Spleen and Blood that are crucial for both mother and baby. However, certain herbs and acupuncture points are contraindicated in pregnancy. Always inform your practitioner if you are pregnant or trying to conceive, and work with someone experienced in prenatal TCM care.
