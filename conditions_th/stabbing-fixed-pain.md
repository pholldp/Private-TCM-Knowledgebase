## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนปัจจุบัน (Conventional Medicine View)
### คำอธิบาย (Description)
In Western medicine, a sharp, stabbing pain that doesn't move is a symptom, not a diagnosis. It can arise from many sources - nerve compression or irritation (like sciatica or trigeminal neuralgia), localized inflammation in a joint or tendon, muscle trigger points, or even referred pain from internal organs. Doctors typically assess location, triggers, and associated symptoms, and may use imaging or nerve studies to identify the underlying structure involved. Treatment then targets that specific cause: anti-inflammatories for tendonitis, nerve pain medications for neuropathy, physical therapy for muscular issues.

### การรักษาหลัก (Treatments)
Depending on the suspected cause, conventional treatment may include rest, ice or heat, over-the-counter anti-inflammatory drugs (NSAIDs), physical therapy, corticosteroid injections, or medications specifically for nerve pain such as gabapentin or pregabalin. If a structural problem is identified, surgery may be considered. The focus is on relieving pain and addressing the anatomical source.

## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนจีน (Traditional Chinese Medicine View)
### ความเข้าใจเกี่ยวกับการแพทย์แผนจีน (TCM Understanding)
In TCM, pain is often a signal of blockage - "where there is blockage, there is pain." A stabbing, fixed pain is the textbook signature of blood stasis. Blood is supposed to circulate freely, nourishing every tissue. When something slows or congeals it, a clot-like obstruction forms in the channels, pressing on nerves and creating a sensation like a needle in one spot that never moves. The pain often worsens at night because circulation naturally slows during rest, and it may improve with gentle movement or warmth.
But blood doesn't stagnate on its own. There is always a root cause. In many cases, emotional stress and frustration cause the Liver Qi to become stuck; over time, this stuck Qi congeals the blood, giving the pattern of Qi and Blood Stagnation. In others, the person's vital Qi is simply too weak to push blood along - a pattern of Qi Deficiency causing Blood Stagnation, common in chronic fatigue or after a long illness.
Sometimes, heat enters the blood, thickening it like a stew left on the stove, leading to Blood Stagnation with Heat. And in joint pain, phlegm - a sticky, congealed fluid - can combine with blood stasis to create swollen, nodular areas that ache sharply.
Because the underlying driver is different in each case, the treatment must be different too. Simply moving blood with strong herbs might help temporarily, but if the real issue is Qi deficiency, those herbs will exhaust the person further.
That's why TCM practitioners ask not just "where does it hurt?" but also "what makes it better or worse?" and "what else is going on in your body?" A purple tongue with a choppy pulse confirms stasis, but the accompanying signs - fatigue, mood swings, heat sensations, or heavy limbs - reveal which pattern is at play. This layered diagnosis is what allows TCM to address the root, not just the symptom.

### การวินิจฉัยโดยแพทย์แผนจีน (Practitioner Diagnosis)
A TCM practitioner begins by listening carefully to the exact quality of the pain. The words “stabbing” and “fixed in one spot” immediately point toward some form of blood stasis, where congealed blood is blocking the channels. The next questions zero in on what accompanies that pain, because the same sharp, unmoving sensation can arise from several different underlying imbalances.
Pure Blood Stagnation is the classic picture. The pain is often worse at night and feels like a needle in a precise location. The tongue shows a purplish body or dark spots, and the pulse has a choppy, rough quality. A history of injury or surgery makes this pattern even more likely.
When emotional stress or frustration triggers the pain, and the person feels distension or tightness alongside the stabbing, the pattern shifts to Qi and Blood Stagnation. Here the tongue may still be purplish but the pulse is wiry, reflecting the stuck Qi that has gradually choked the blood flow.
If the stabbing pain appears in someone who is chronically tired, pale, and short of breath, Qi Deficiency causing Blood Stagnation is suspected. The pain is often duller but still fixed, and the tongue is pale with purple speckles while the pulse is weak. The body simply lacks the energy to keep blood moving smoothly.
Blood Stagnation with Heat adds signs of local inflammation: redness, warmth, or a burning sensation at the painful spot. The tongue is red with a yellow coating and the pulse is rapid. A practitioner sees this when stasis has generated heat or when an external infection complicates the picture.
Phlegm in the channels can also produce a fixed, stabbing pain, especially in joints or muscles, but it usually comes with swelling, a heavy sensation, and limited range of motion. The tongue coating is thick and greasy, and the pulse feels slippery. This pattern often appears in chronic arthritic conditions.

### Blood Stagnation
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/blood-stagnation)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Fixed stabbing pain that does not move, Pain worsens at night, Pain worse with pressure, Dark purplish lips or nails, Dark menstrual blood with clots, Rough, dry, scaly skin
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Cold weather or drafts, Prolonged rest, sitting, or standing, Pressure on the painful spot, Emotional stress, anger, worry, or frustration, Cold or raw foods
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Gentle movement or stretching, Warm compress or bath, Eating warming spices
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Blood needs to flow freely to nourish the body. When it becomes sluggish or congealed, it forms a blockage called blood stasis. This blockage physically obstructs the channels and collaterals, creating a sharp, fixed pain that feels like a needle in one spot.
The pain worsens at night because circulation slows during rest, allowing the stasis to settle more heavily. Pressure on the area further compresses the already blocked channel, which is why the pain feels worse when you press on it.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: purple or dusky body with stasis spots, distended dark sublingual veins, thin white coat. Pulse: choppy (涩, sè) like a knife scraping bamboo, or wiry (弦, xián) if Qi stagnation is present.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Cold congeals blood, so cold environments and icy foods make stasis thicker and pain sharper. Prolonged rest slows circulation, allowing blood to pool and worsen the blockage. Emotional stress knots the Liver Qi, which normally pushes blood-when Qi stagnates, blood stasis deepens. Warmth and gentle movement dilate the vessels and encourage blood flow, helping to break up the congealed blood and ease the fixed stabbing sensation.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat warming, blood-moving foods like turmeric, ginger, onion, garlic, and small amounts of vinegar. Avoid cold, raw, or icy foods and drinks, which congeal blood. Gentle daily movement such as walking, tai chi, or stretching keeps blood flowing. Avoid prolonged sitting or standing still. A warm compress or bath can temporarily ease the stabbing sensation.

### Qi And Blood Stagnation
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/qi-and-blood-stagnation)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Fixed, stabbing pain that worsens with pressure, Distending or bloating pain in the chest or ribs, Irritability or emotional tension, Dark purplish complexion or lips, Painful periods with dark, clotted blood
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Emotional stress, anger, worry, or frustration, Cold weather or drafts, Prolonged rest, sitting, or standing, Greasy, heavy, or cold foods, Premenstrual period
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Gentle movement or stretching, Warm compress or bath, Deep breathing and relaxation, Warm, cooked, or nourishing meals
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When Qi becomes stuck, often due to chronic stress, frustration, or emotional tension, the Liver cannot keep blood flowing smoothly. The stagnant Qi then causes blood to congeal, forming clots that block the channels. This obstruction produces a sharp, stabbing pain that stays in one place and feels worse with pressure.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: dark purple body with stasis spots, distended sublingual veins, thin white coat. Pulse: wiry (弦) and choppy (涩).
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Emotional stress directly stagnates Liver Qi, which then congeals blood, worsening the stabbing pain. Gentle movement and warmth invigorate Qi and blood flow, breaking up the stagnation. Cold and inactivity cause further congealing, while warm, light foods support circulation without burdening the digestive Qi.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat warm, lightly cooked meals with plenty of leafy greens. Avoid cold, raw foods and heavy, greasy meals that can congeal blood. Gentle daily exercise like walking, tai chi, or yoga helps keep Qi and blood moving. Manage stress through breathing exercises or meditation, as emotional tension directly worsens this pattern.

### Qi Deficiency causing Blood Stagnation
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/qi-deficiency-causing-blood-stagnation)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Fixed stabbing pain that worsens with pressure, Persistent tiredness and physical weakness, Shortness of breath on exertion, reluctance to speak, Dull or dusky facial complexion, Pain that eases with rest, worsens with activity
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Overexertion, overwork, or fatigue, Cold weather or drafts, Prolonged rest, sitting, or standing, Emotional stress, anger, worry, or frustration, Cold or raw foods
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Rest and lying down, Warm compress or bath, Gentle movement or stretching, Warm, cooked, or nourishing meals
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Qi is the vital force that propels blood through the vessels. When Qi becomes deficient, it lacks the strength to keep blood moving, so circulation slows and blood begins to pool and stagnate in certain areas. This stagnation creates a fixed, stabbing pain that often develops gradually in people with chronic fatigue or after a long illness, and it tends to be duller than the pain of pure blood stasis because the body is weakened.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale-dark or pale-purple body with stasis spots and teeth marks, thin white coat. Pulse: deep, fine or weak, and choppy.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Rest and warmth help conserve and support Qi, which is needed to move blood and prevent stasis. Gentle movement encourages circulation without draining energy. Overexertion depletes Qi, making it harder to move blood, while cold constricts the vessels and worsens stasis. Stress and worry stagnate Qi, further impeding blood flow.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat warm, nourishing foods like soups with astragalus, dates, and goji berries to build Qi and blood. Avoid cold, raw foods that weaken digestion. Gentle movement such as walking or tai chi helps circulate blood without depleting energy. Prioritize rest and avoid overexertion.

### Blood Stagnation with Heat
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/blood-stagnation-with-heat)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Fixed, stabbing pain in one spot, Worse at night and with pressure, Local warmth or burning at the pain site, Feeling of internal heat, especially at night, Irritability and restlessness, Thirst with a desire to rinse the mouth but not swallow, Dark, clotted menstrual blood, Dark, scanty urine and dry stools
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Emotional stress, anger, worry, or frustration, Spicy, greasy, or fried foods, Alcohol and coffee, Hot weather or overheated rooms, Overexertion, overwork, or fatigue
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Cool compresses on the painful area, Cooling foods (e.g., cucumber, mung beans), Gentle movement or stretching, Calm, quiet environment
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When heat enters the blood, it thickens and congeals it, like a stew left too long on the stove. This sticky, overheated blood cannot flow smoothly through the channels, so it pools and creates a fixed obstruction. The result is a sharp, stabbing pain that stays in one place, often with a burning sensation, and it worsens at night when circulation naturally slows and heat can feel more intense.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: reddish-purple body with stasis spots, distended sublingual veins, and a thin dry yellow coating. Pulse: choppy (涩 sè) and rapid (数 shuò), often wiry at the left Guan position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Heat is both a cause and an aggravator in this pattern-it thickens the blood and creates stasis. Emotional upset, spicy foods, and alcohol all fan the flames, making the blood even stickier and the pain sharper. Cooling foods and compresses calm the internal heat, while gentle movement keeps the blood from pooling. Rest and emotional calm prevent the qi from surging and stirring up more heat, giving the stasis a chance to clear.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Avoid spicy, greasy, and fried foods that generate heat. Instead, choose cooling, blood-moving foods like celery, eggplant, lotus root, and hawthorn berry. Gentle daily movement such as walking or stretching helps keep blood flowing, but avoid overexertion that can stir up heat. Practice calming activities like slow breathing or meditation to soothe irritability.

### Phlegm in the Channels joints and muscles
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/phlegm-in-the-channels-joints-and-muscles)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Joint swelling that feels hard to the touch, Numbness or heavy sensation in the limbs, Subcutaneous nodules or hard lumps near joints, Purplish skin discoloration around affected joints, Chest tightness or a feeling of phlegm in the throat, Pain is fixed and stabbing, worse with pressure
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Cold and damp weather, Greasy, sweet, or dairy foods, Prolonged rest, sitting, or standing, Overexertion, overwork, or fatigue
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Gentle movement or stretching, Warm and dry weather, Warm compress or bath, Light, cooked meals
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Phlegm is a sticky, congealed fluid that can lodge deep in the channels and joints. When it settles there, it physically blocks the flow of Qi and blood, creating a fixed, stabbing pain that doesn't move. Over time, the stagnant blood congeals further, and the Phlegm hardens into palpable lumps or firm swelling around the joints.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: purple body with stasis spots, white greasy coating, and distended sublingual veins. Pulse: wiry and choppy, or wiry and slippery.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Phlegm is a Yin pathogen that congeals in cold and dampness, so warmth and dryness help transform it and keep channels open. Greasy, sweet, or dairy foods generate more Phlegm, directly worsening the obstruction. Gentle movement circulates Qi and blood, preventing stasis and helping to break up Phlegm accumulations, while overexertion can strain already blocked channels.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Avoid cold, raw, and greasy foods, as well as dairy and sugar, which directly contribute to Phlegm formation. Keep the affected joints warm and dry-gentle movement like walking or tai chi helps Qi and blood circulate, preventing further stagnation. Applying a warm compress or moxibustion over the painful area can help resolve Phlegm and ease pain.

## คำถามที่พบบ่อย (Frequently Asked Questions)
### What does it mean if my pain is sharp and fixed?
In Chinese medicine, that's nearly always a sign of blood stasis - congealed blood blocking the channels. It's like a tiny traffic jam in your circulation, creating a needle-like sensation in one precise spot. The pain often gets worse at night or with pressure, and you might notice a purplish tongue or dark spots under your skin. But blood stasis doesn't happen in a vacuum; it has an underlying cause that must be identified for lasting relief.

### Can acupuncture really help with stabbing pain?
Yes, acupuncture is very effective at moving stagnant blood and relieving this type of pain. Points like Geshu (BL-17) and Xuehai (SP-10) are specifically chosen to invigorate blood, while local points near the pain site help unblock the channel directly. Most people feel some relief within the first few sessions, though chronic cases take longer. The needles stimulate your body's own healing mechanisms to restore smooth flow.

### How long does it take for TCM to work on this kind of pain?
It depends on the underlying pattern, but many people feel a reduction in pain intensity within 2-3 weeks of starting herbs and acupuncture. Acute stasis from a recent injury may clear in a month. Chronic patterns driven by Qi deficiency or phlegm can take several months, because we're not just masking the pain - we're correcting the root imbalance so it doesn't return.

### Do I need to change my diet?
Yes, diet plays an important supporting role. To keep blood moving, favor warming foods like ginger, turmeric, and garlic, and include dark leafy greens and moderate amounts of red meat to nourish blood. Avoid cold, raw foods and icy drinks, which constrict circulation. Greasy, fried foods can worsen phlegm and should be limited. These changes help your body respond better to treatment.

### Is it safe to combine TCM with my pain medication?
In most cases, yes, but you must inform both your TCM practitioner and your doctor. Some blood-moving herbs - such as Dang Gui, Chuan Xiong, and Hong Hua - can thin the blood and may interact with anticoagulants like warfarin or aspirin. Never stop prescribed medication abruptly. Acupuncture is generally safe alongside medications and physical therapy.

### Why does the pain get worse at night?
Blood stasis pain often worsens at night because your circulation naturally slows when you're at rest. The stagnant blood settles even more, increasing the pressure on the blocked channel. Gentle movement during the day and applying warmth to the area can help keep things flowing. If night pain is severe, mention it to your practitioner - it's an important diagnostic clue.

### What if the stabbing pain is in my chest?
Chest pain with a stabbing quality can be a sign of blood stasis in the Heart, which TCM treats with specific formulas. However, any chest pain must be evaluated by a doctor first to rule out serious conditions like heart attack. Once cleared, TCM can be a powerful complement, but never delay emergency care for chest pain. See our Safety section for red-flag symptoms.

## การวิจัยทางวิทยาศาสตร์สมัยใหม่ (Modern Scientific Research)
1. **Acupuncture for chronic pain: individual patient data meta-analysis** ([ลิงก์](https://doi.org/10.1001/archinternmed.2012.236))
   *วารสารวิชาการ: Vickers AJ, Cronin AM, Maschino AC, et al. Acupuncture for chronic pain: individual patient data meta-analysis. Arch Intern Med. 2012;172(19):1444-1453.*
   This landmark meta-analysis pooled raw data from 29 randomized controlled trials involving 17,922 patients. Acupuncture was significantly superior to both sham acupuncture and usual care for back and neck pain, osteoarthritis, and chronic headache - conditions that frequently feature fixed, stabbing pain. The effect persisted at follow-up, supporting acupuncture as a valid treatment option.

2. **Chinese herbal medicine for primary dysmenorrhoea** ([ลิงก์](https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD005288.pub2))
   *วารสารวิชาการ: Zhu X, Proctor M, Bensoussan A, Wu E, Smith CA. Chinese herbal medicine for primary dysmenorrhoea. Cochrane Database Syst Rev. 2007;(4):CD005288.*
   This Cochrane systematic review assessed 39 randomized controlled trials of Chinese herbal medicine for primary dysmenorrhoea, a condition in which blood stasis with fixed, stabbing lower abdominal pain is the dominant TCM pattern. Herbal medicine was found to reduce pain more effectively than conventional NSAIDs and the oral contraceptive pill, though the authors noted that trial quality was generally low.

3. **Acupuncture for patients with chronic low back pain: a randomized controlled trial** ([ลิงก์](https://doi.org/10.1001/archinte.167.17.1892))
   *วารสารวิชาการ: Haake M, Müller HH, Schade-Brittinger C, et al. German Acupuncture Trials (GERAC) for chronic low back pain: randomized, multicenter, blinded, parallel-group trial with 3 groups. Arch Intern Med. 2007;167(17):1892-1898.*
   In this large German trial, 1,162 patients with chronic low back pain were randomized to verum acupuncture, sham acupuncture, or guideline-based conventional therapy. Acupuncture significantly improved pain and function at 6 months compared with conventional therapy, with benefits evident for both fixed and diffuse pain presentations.
