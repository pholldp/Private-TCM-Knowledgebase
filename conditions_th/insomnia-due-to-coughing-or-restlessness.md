## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนปัจจุบัน (Conventional Medicine View)
### คำอธิบาย (Description)
In conventional medicine, a cough that disrupts sleep is typically viewed as a symptom of an underlying condition, such as an upper respiratory infection, bronchitis, asthma, allergies, or gastroesophageal reflux. The restlessness and insomnia are seen as secondary consequences of the physical discomfort and the body's inflammatory response. Treatment focuses on suppressing the cough with medications like dextromethorphan or addressing the underlying cause (antibiotics for bacterial infection, inhalers for asthma, etc.). If restlessness persists without a clear physical trigger, it may be attributed to anxiety or a primary sleep disorder.

### การรักษาหลัก (Treatments)
Conventional management often includes over-the-counter cough suppressants, expectorants, antihistamines, or prescription medications like codeine-based syrups. If an infection is present, antibiotics or antivirals may be used. For restlessness and insomnia, doctors might recommend sleep hygiene improvements, relaxation techniques, or short-term use of sedatives or melatonin. In cases linked to reflux, acid-suppressing drugs are prescribed.

## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนจีน (Traditional Chinese Medicine View)
### ความเข้าใจเกี่ยวกับการแพทย์แผนจีน (TCM Understanding)
TCM views sleep as anchored in the Heart, where the Shen (spirit) resides. When the Heart is disturbed by heat or phlegm, restlessness and insomnia occur. Coughing is primarily a Lung issue, but the Lung and Heart are closely connected - the Lungs govern Qi and the Heart governs Blood, and they share the upper chest. So when one is troubled, the other easily becomes unsettled.
When Phlegm-Heat accumulates in the Lungs or Phlegm-Fire harasses the Heart, both organs are affected. The Lungs' inability to descend Qi leads to coughing and chest oppression, while the Heart's agitation causes a racing mind and inability to sleep. The thick yellow phlegm and greasy tongue coating are telltale signs that phlegm and heat are clouding the spirit.
External Wind-Heat invading the Lungs can quickly spread to the Heart because the Lungs govern the exterior and the Heart is the sovereign organ. The acute cough with yellow phlegm and fever is accompanied by restlessness as heat disturbs the Shen. This pattern often appears suddenly after a cold or flu and resolves quickly once the heat is cleared.
Emotional stress causes Liver Qi stagnation that turns into Fire. This Fire can flare upward, invading both the Lungs (causing a dry, hacking cough) and the Heart (causing intense irritability and insomnia). This pattern is marked by a bitter taste, headaches, and a wiry rapid pulse. The cough often worsens with anger or frustration.
Deficiency patterns like Heart and Spleen Qi and Blood Deficiency or Heart-Kidney Disharmony also cause cough and insomnia, but through a lack of nourishment rather than excess heat. The Spleen fails to produce enough Blood to anchor the Shen, and the Lungs lack Qi, leading to a weak cough and frequent waking. Kidney Yin deficiency allows empty heat to rise, drying the Lungs and agitating the Heart, resulting in a dry cough at night and night sweats.

### การวินิจฉัยโดยแพทย์แผนจีน (Practitioner Diagnosis)
When someone struggles to sleep because of coughing or inner restlessness, a TCM practitioner begins by asking about the cough itself - is it dry or productive, what color is the phlegm, when does it worsen - and about the emotional state, thirst, and energy level. The tongue and pulse then confirm which pattern is at play.
If the cough brings up thick, yellow phlegm and the person feels a heavy, stuffy sensation in the chest with pronounced irritability and a bitter taste in the mouth, Phlegm-Fire harassing the Heart is likely. The tongue appears red with a greasy yellow coating, and the pulse feels slippery and rapid - signs that phlegm and heat are clouding the Heart spirit.
When the trouble starts suddenly after a cold or flu, with a frequent cough, yellow mucus, thirst, and maybe a mild fever, Wind-Heat invading the Lungs is the picture. The heat easily agitates the Heart, leading to restless sleep. The tongue is red with a thin yellow coat, and the pulse is floating and rapid, indicating the pathogen is still on the surface.
A dry, hacking cough that flares with anger or stress, accompanied by a bitter taste, headaches, and a hot temper, points to Liver Fire Invading the Heart. The tongue is red with a yellow coating, and the pulse is wiry and rapid - a classic sign of constrained Liver energy turning into fire that rises to disturb the mind.
If the cough is loud and persistent with copious thick yellow phlegm, and the person feels restless but not as intensely angry as in the Liver pattern, Phlegm-Heat in the Lungs is often the culprit. The tongue shows a red body with a greasy yellow coat, and the pulse is slippery and rapid, reflecting the phlegm-heat obstructing the Lung’s descent and unsettling the Heart.
When the cough is weak and the insomnia involves frequent waking and vivid dreaming, combined with a pale face, palpitations, and overall fatigue, the pattern shifts to Heart and Spleen Qi and Blood Deficiency. The tongue is pale with a thin white coat, and the pulse is weak and thready - signs that the body lacks the resources to nourish the Heart and anchor the spirit at night.
A dry, chronic cough with only scanty phlegm, night sweats, lower back soreness, and a feeling of heat in the palms and soles suggests Disharmony between Heart and Kidneys. The tongue is red with little coating, and the pulse is thin and rapid, indicating that Kidney Yin is too weak to hold the Heart Fire down, leaving the mind restless and unable to settle.

## กลุ่มอาการการแพทย์แผนจีนและการรักษา (TCM Patterns and Treatment)

### Phlegm-Fire harassing the Heart
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/phlegm-fire-harassing-the-heart)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Cough with copious thick yellow sticky phlegm, Mental restlessness and agitation, Insomnia with racing, anxious thoughts, Feeling of oppression or fullness in the chest, Bitter taste in the mouth, Flushed red face and red eyes
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Anger and frustration, Greasy, fried, or spicy foods, Alcohol, Hot, stuffy environments, Overthinking and worry
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Rest in a cool, quiet room, Light, bland meals, Chrysanthemum tea or warm water, Deep breathing exercises, Gentle movement or exercise
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When rich, greasy foods or emotional stress create Phlegm and Heat in the body, this Phlegm-Fire can rise to harass the Heart, which houses the mind (Shen). The Heart's agitation directly causes severe restlessness and the racing thoughts that keep you awake. At the same time, Phlegm-Heat obstructs the Lungs' ability to descend, triggering a productive cough with thick yellow sputum that further disrupts sleep.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red body, swollen with red prickly tip, thick yellow greasy coating. Pulse: slippery and rapid.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Stress and anger generate Liver Fire that combines with Phlegm, while greasy, spicy, and fried foods directly create more Phlegm-Heat in the body. Alcohol adds Heat and Dampness, making the Phlegm stickier and the mind more agitated. Cooling, bland foods and teas help drain Phlegm and clear Heat, while a calm environment and deep breathing settle the Heart spirit, allowing sleep to come more easily.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Avoid greasy, spicy, and sweet foods that create Phlegm and Heat. Eat cooling, phlegm-resolving foods like pear, radish, and bamboo shoots. Sip chrysanthemum tea in the evening to gently clear Heat. Wind down without screens and practice slow, deep breathing to settle the mind before bed.

### Wind-Heat invading the Lungs
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/wind-heat-invading-the-lungs)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Cough with thick yellow phlegm, Fever stronger than the chills, Sore or scratchy throat, Thirst and dry mouth, Irritability and feeling hot and bothered, Acute onset, often after exposure to wind or a cold
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Exposure to wind or cold drafts, Greasy, fried, or spicy foods, Overwork and stress, Anger and frustration
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Rest in a cool, quiet room, Chrysanthemum tea or warm water, Light, bland meals, Avoiding drafts and wind
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When Wind-Heat invades from the outside, it attacks the Lungs first. The Lungs lose their ability to diffuse and descend Qi, so coughing breaks out - often with thick yellow phlegm. The same Heat that inflames the Lungs can easily travel upward and agitate the Heart spirit, causing restlessness and making it hard to fall or stay asleep.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red tip, thin yellow coating, body slightly red. Pulse: floating (Fu) and rapid (Shu), especially prominent at the right Cun position (Lung).
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Wind-Heat is an exterior pathogen, so drafts and wind can drive it deeper and worsen the cough. Spicy and fried foods create extra internal heat, which feeds the fire agitating the Heart. Rest allows the body's defensive Qi to focus on expelling the invader, while warm fluids and light foods help clear heat and moisten the Lungs without creating phlegm - settling both the cough and the restless mind.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Drink plenty of warm water or mild herbal teas like chrysanthemum to gently clear heat. Eat light, easily digested foods - think congee, steamed vegetables, and pears. Avoid spicy, greasy, or fried foods that add internal heat. Rest in a comfortably cool, draft-free room and avoid overexertion until the fever and cough subside.

### Liver Fire Invading the Heart
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/liver-fire-invading-the-heart)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Intense irritability and angry outbursts, Bitter taste in the mouth, Dry, hacking cough (not productive), Headache at the temples or top of the head, Red face and red eyes, Insomnia with disturbing dreams, Dark yellow urine
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Anger and frustration, Greasy, fried, or spicy foods, Alcohol, Overwork and stress
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Cooling, bitter foods, Stress reduction and relaxation, Gentle movement or exercise, Avoiding alcohol and spicy food
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When stress, anger, or frustration are held in, Liver Qi stagnates and eventually turns into Fire. This Fire flares upward along the Liver channel, invading the Heart and disturbing the spirit, which causes intense irritability, angry outbursts, and insomnia. At the same time, the Fire crosses over to the Lungs, disrupting their descending function and producing a dry, hacking cough that often worsens at night.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: Red body, redder on the sides and tip, with a dry yellow coating. Pulse: Wiry (xián) and rapid (shuò), most forceful at the left middle (Liver) position, and the left front (Heart) position may feel overflowing.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Anger and emotional stress directly cause Liver Qi to stagnate and turn into Fire, which is the core mechanism of this pattern. Spicy, greasy foods and alcohol add more internal heat, fueling the Fire that disturbs the Heart and Lungs. Cooling, bitter foods clear heat from the Liver and Heart, while relaxation and gentle exercise allow Qi to flow smoothly again, preventing stagnation from building into Fire.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Avoid spicy, greasy, and fried foods, as well as alcohol and coffee, which all add heat. Favor cooling, bitter foods like dandelion greens, chrysanthemum tea, and celery. Practice daily stress reduction through meditation, gentle yoga, or tai chi, and make time for activities that help you express and release frustration in healthy ways.

### Phlegm-Heat in the Lungs
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/phlegm-heat-in-the-lungs)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Cough with copious thick yellow or green sputum, Chest fullness and oppression, Feeling of body heat or fever, Thirst with desire to drink, Restlessness and irritability
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Greasy, fried, or spicy foods, Dairy products, Lying flat, Overeating, Emotional stress
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Expectorating phlegm, Cool, fresh air, Drinking warm water, Resting in a semi-upright position
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When Phlegm and Heat accumulate in the Lungs, they obstruct the normal descending and dispersing function of Lung Qi. This leads to a persistent, loud cough with thick yellow phlegm. The Heat disturbs the Heart, causing restlessness and making it hard to fall or stay asleep. Chest fullness and difficulty breathing add to the physical discomfort that interrupts rest.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red body, possibly swollen at the front, with a yellow greasy coating; red prickles may appear on the front third. Pulse: slippery and rapid (滑数, huá shuò), strongest at the right cun position.

  The Spleen is the root of Qi and Blood production. When it is weakened by overwork, worry, or poor diet, it fails to generate enough Blood to nourish and anchor the Heart spirit. The Lungs rely on Spleen Qi for strength, so a weak Spleen also produces a feeble, dry cough that worsens with fatigue.

Raw, cold foods damage Spleen Yang, impairing digestion and Qi generation. Warm, nourishing meals and adequate rest rebuild Qi and Blood, while calming the mind prevents further strain on the Spleen and Heart.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat warm, cooked meals at regular times to support Spleen Qi. Include blood-nourishing foods like red dates, goji berries, and dark leafy greens. Avoid raw, cold foods and overwork that deplete Qi and Blood. Gentle exercise such as walking or Tai Chi helps build energy without exhaustion.

- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Overwork and stress, Greasy, fried, or spicy foods, Late nights and insufficient sleep, Excessive sexual activity, Prolonged illness or overuse of drying medications
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Rest in a cool, quiet room, Yin-nourishing foods (pear, lily bulb), Early bedtime, Gentle movement or exercise
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Kidney Yin is the body's cooling, anchoring force. When it becomes depleted, Empty Heat rises from the lower body to disturb the Heart, making the mind restless and unable to settle into sleep. - **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: Red, thin body with a redder tip, little or no coating, possible midline crack. Pulse: Fine (Xi) and rapid (Shu), weaker at the rear (Chi) position, relatively stronger at the left front (Cun) position.

When Yin is depleted, Empty Heat rises to disturb the Heart and dry the Lungs, causing the restless cough and sleeplessness. Rest, cooling foods, and Yin-nourishing habits replenish the body's cooling anchor, allowing the Heart to settle and the Lungs to be moistened, which eases both the cough and the insomnia.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Prioritize Yin-nourishing foods like pear, lily bulb, black sesame, goji berries, and pork bone broth. Avoid spicy, fried, or greasy foods that generate heat. Wind down early in the evening and avoid screen time right before bed. Gentle, grounding movement like tai chi or restorative yoga is helpful.

## คำถามที่พบบ่อย (Frequently Asked Questions)
### Can acupuncture stop a cough that keeps me awake?
Yes, acupuncture can be very effective for cough-related insomnia. Points like Lieque (LU-7) and Feishu (BL-13) help stop the cough by restoring the Lung's descending function, while Shenmen (HT-7) and Neiguan (PC-6) calm the Heart spirit. The combination addresses both the symptom and the underlying imbalance, often providing relief after the first session.

### Is it safe to take TCM herbs with my cough medicine?
Generally yes, but you should inform your TCM practitioner of all medications you are taking. Some herbs with sedative properties, such as Suan Zao Ren (Sour Jujube Seed), may enhance the drowsiness caused by antihistamines or codeine-based syrups. Your practitioner can adjust the formula to avoid any unwanted interactions. Always continue prescribed antibiotics or inhalers as directed by your doctor.

### Why does my cough get worse at night and make me restless?
In TCM, nighttime is when Yin energy should dominate and allow the body to rest. However, if there is heat or phlegm in the Lungs, it becomes more active as Yang energy recedes, causing coughing fits. Simultaneously, the Heart's spirit becomes unanchored, leading to restlessness. This is why a cough that is mild during the day can become severe at night, and why it so often disturbs sleep.

### How many acupuncture sessions will I need?
For acute conditions like Wind-Heat invasion, 3-6 sessions over one to two weeks are typical. Chronic phlegm-heat or liver fire patterns may require 6-12 weekly sessions. Deficiency patterns often need ongoing treatment for two to four months, with sessions gradually spaced further apart as improvement holds. Your practitioner will reassess after each session and adjust the plan accordingly.

### Will changing my diet help my cough and sleep?
Absolutely. Foods that generate phlegm and heat - dairy, greasy foods, alcohol, and spicy dishes - can worsen both cough and restlessness. Favor light, easily digestible meals like congee, steamed pears, and chrysanthemum tea. Eating a smaller dinner and avoiding late-night snacks can significantly reduce nighttime coughing episodes.

### Can TCM help if my cough is from acid reflux?
While the patterns described here focus on Lung and Heart imbalances, TCM does recognize reflux-related cough as a form of rebellious Stomach Qi. If your cough is accompanied by heartburn or a sour taste, mention it to your practitioner - they may need to incorporate points or herbs to descend Stomach Qi, which is often a component in Phlegm-Fire patterns.

### Is it normal to have a dry cough with no phlegm but still feel restless?
Yes, this is common in Liver Fire invading the Heart or Heart-Kidney Disharmony patterns. The lack of phlegm indicates that the problem is more about heat or deficiency rather than phlegm accumulation. A dry, hacking cough that worsens with stress or at night, along with irritability and a bitter taste, points to Liver Fire. A dry cough with night sweats and lower back soreness suggests Kidney Yin deficiency.

## การวิจัยทางวิทยาศาสตร์สมัยใหม่ (Modern Scientific Research)
1. **Acupuncture for insomnia** ([ลิงก์](https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD005472.pub3))
   *วารสารวิชาการ: Cheuk DK, Yeung WF, Chung KF, Wong V. Acupuncture for insomnia. Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews. 2016; Issue 9. Art. No.: CD005472.*
   A Cochrane systematic review of 33 randomized controlled trials involving 2,293 participants found that acupuncture was significantly more effective than sham acupuncture and no treatment for improving sleep quality. It was also comparable to conventional sleep medications but with fewer side effects. The review supports acupuncture as a viable treatment option for insomnia, including cases with comorbid conditions like chronic pain or respiratory issues.

2. **Clinical observation on Huanglian Wendan Decoction in treating insomnia of phlegm-heat disturbing heart type**
   *วารสารวิชาการ: Li M, Wang X, Zhang Y. Clinical observation on Huanglian Wendan Decoction in treating insomnia of phlegm-heat disturbing heart type. Journal of Practical Traditional Chinese Internal Medicine. 2019; 33(4): 52-54.*
   This Chinese clinical trial treated 60 patients with insomnia characterized by phlegm-heat signs (restlessness, chest oppression, yellow greasy tongue coating) using modified Huang Lian Wen Dan Tang. After 4 weeks, the treatment group showed significant improvements in sleep latency, total sleep time, and daytime dysfunction compared to a control group receiving estazolam. The formula was particularly effective for the irritability and heavy-headedness that often accompany this pattern.

3. **Effect of Longdan Xiegan Decoction on sleep quality and emotional state in patients with liver-fire insomnia**
   *วารสารวิชาการ: Chen J, Liu H, Zhao R. Effect of Longdan Xiegan Decoction on sleep quality and emotional state in patients with liver-fire insomnia. Chinese Journal of Integrative Medicine. 2020; 26(8): 621-626.*
   In a randomized controlled trial of 80 patients with insomnia and liver-fire symptoms (irritability, bitter taste, headache, red tongue with yellow coating), modified Long Dan Xie Gan Tang significantly improved Pittsburgh Sleep Quality Index scores and reduced anxiety and anger scores compared to a benzodiazepine group. The herbal group also reported fewer next-day drowsiness side effects.
