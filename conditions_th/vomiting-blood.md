## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนปัจจุบัน (Conventional Medicine View)
### คำอธิบาย (Description)
In Western medicine, vomiting blood (hematemesis) is a sign of bleeding in the upper gastrointestinal tract, most often from a peptic ulcer, erosive gastritis, esophageal varices, or a tear in the esophagus. The blood may be bright red if bleeding is active and profuse, or dark like coffee grounds if it has been partially digested by stomach acid. Diagnosis is typically made by endoscopy, which can locate the bleeding site, and treatment focuses on stopping the acute bleed and preventing recurrence with acid-suppressing drugs, antibiotics for H. pylori, or procedures to ligate varices.

### การรักษาหลัก (Treatments)
Standard acute management includes intravenous fluids, blood transfusions if needed, and endoscopic techniques such as thermal coagulation, clipping, or injection of epinephrine. Medications like proton pump inhibitors (omeprazole, pantoprazole) are given to reduce stomach acid and allow ulcers to heal. If varices are the cause, beta-blockers or repeated banding may be used. Surgery is reserved for cases that do not respond to endoscopic treatment.

## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนจีน (Traditional Chinese Medicine View)
### ความเข้าใจเกี่ยวกับการแพทย์แผนจีน (TCM Understanding)
TCM understands vomiting blood as a failure of the body to keep blood inside its vessels and flowing in the right direction. Normally, Stomach Qi descends, carrying food and fluids downward. When something forces blood to rebel and rush upward instead, it spills out through the mouth. The nature of that force - whether it is a blaze of heat, a surge of Liver Fire, a weakness in the Qi that should be containing the blood, or a stagnant mass blocking normal flow - determines which organ systems are involved and what the treatment must be.
Two excess-heat patterns produce sudden, forceful bleeding with bright red blood. Stomach Fire (胃火, wèi huǒ) builds from a diet rich in spicy, greasy, or alcoholic foods, creating a scorching internal environment that agitates blood and pushes it recklessly upward. Liver Fire Blazing (肝火, gān huǒ), often ignited by anger or emotional repression, can invade the Stomach, causing a projectile vomiting of blood that tastes bitter and is accompanied by a pounding headache. In both, the tongue is red with a thick yellow coat and the pulse is rapid and forceful.
When the bleeding is chronic and the blood appears dark or pale, the mechanism is different. Qi not controlling Blood (气不摄血, qì bù shè xuè) arises when the Spleen is too weak to hold blood within the vessels; small amounts leak out and are carried up by stomach Qi, often alongside fatigue, poor appetite, and a pale, puffy tongue. Stomach Blood Stagnation (胃血瘀, wèi xuè yū) is a less common but important pattern where old, congealed blood obstructs the stomach vessels, eventually rupturing and producing dark, clotted blood with a fixed, stabbing pain. Each pattern points to a distinct treatment strategy, which is why a one-size-fits-all approach rarely prevents recurrence.

### การวินิจฉัยโดยแพทย์แผนจีน (Practitioner Diagnosis)
A TCM practitioner begins by asking about the color, volume, and speed of the vomiting. Bright red blood that comes suddenly in large amounts points toward an acute heat pattern, while dark or pale blood that trickles out over time suggests a chronic deficiency or stagnation pattern. The accompanying sensations in the stomach and the person’s overall energy also help narrow the diagnosis.
If the vomiting is forceful, the blood is bright red or purplish-dark, and the person feels intense thirst, a burning sensation in the stomach, and constipation, this matches Stomach Fire. The tongue is typically red with a thick yellow coating, and the pulse is rapid and slippery or forceful. This pattern is all about excess heat in the stomach pushing blood upward.
When the blood is vomited in a sudden, projectile way and the person is irritable, with a bitter taste in the mouth and maybe a headache, Liver Fire invading the Stomach is likely. The tongue is red with a yellow coating, and the pulse feels wiry and rapid. The key difference is the emotional tension and the bitter taste, reflecting the liver’s involvement.
Chronic, small amounts of dark or pale blood in the vomit, along with fatigue, poor appetite, loose stools, and a pale complexion, point to Qi not controlling Blood. Here the Spleen Qi is too weak to hold blood in the vessels. The tongue is pale with a thin white coating, and the pulse is weak or thready. This pattern often appears in people with a long history of digestive weakness.
Vomiting of dark, clotted blood that comes with a stabbing, fixed pain in the stomach suggests Stomach Blood Stagnation. The tongue may show purple spots or a dark color, and the pulse feels choppy or uneven. This pattern can follow an old injury or chronic stomach illness where blood flow has become blocked.

### Stomach Fire (Stomach Heat)
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/stomach-fire-stomach-heat)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Vomiting large amounts of bright red blood, Burning pain in the upper stomach area, Intense thirst for cold drinks, Foul breath, Constipation with dry stools
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Spicy, greasy, or fried foods, Alcohol and coffee, Anger, stress, or frustration, Hot weather or environments, Overeating or large heavy meals
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Cooling foods and drinks, Rest in a quiet, cool place, Avoiding spicy and hot foods, Drinking plenty of water, Applying a cool compress to the stomach
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Stomach Fire is an excess heat condition. The intense heat agitates the blood and forces it to move recklessly upward, causing sudden vomiting of bright red blood. The heat also damages fluids, leading to intense thirst, and dries the intestines, causing constipation.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red body, especially in the centre, with a thick, dry, yellow coating. Pulse: rapid, slippery, and forceful, especially strong at the right Guan position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: <p>Stomach Fire is an excess heat pattern. Spicy and hot foods, alcohol, and emotional stress all add heat to the body, making the fire blaze higher and pushing blood upward. Cooling foods and rest help drain that heat, allowing the Stomach to settle and the blood to return to its proper channels.</p>
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Avoid spicy, greasy, and fried foods, as well as alcohol and coffee, which add heat. Eat cooling, easily digested foods like congee, cucumber, and pear. Drink plenty of water, and rest in a cool, calm environment.

### Liver Fire Blazing
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/liver-fire-blazing)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Projectile vomiting of bright red blood, Bitter taste in the mouth, Intense irritability and short temper, Throbbing headache at the temples, Flushed red face, Red, painful or burning eyes
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Anger, stress, or frustration, Alcohol, Spicy, greasy, or fried foods, Overwork or exhaustion, Hot weather or environments
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Cooling foods and drinks, Stress reduction, Bitter greens, Peppermint or chrysanthemum tea, Gentle exercise like Tai Chi
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When Liver Fire blazes upward, it can invade the Stomach, scorching its delicate lining and forcing blood to surge upward and out through the mouth. This produces a sudden, often projectile vomiting of bright red blood, typically triggered by a burst of anger or intense frustration. The bitter taste, throbbing headache, and irritability are classic signs that the root of the problem lies in the Liver, not just the Stomach.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red body with redder sides, dry yellow coating, possible red prickles. Pulse: wiry (弦 xián) and rapid (数 shuò), full and forceful, especially at the left Guan position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Liver Fire is like a flame that flares up with emotional stress, alcohol, and heating foods. These triggers cause Qi to surge upward, carrying blood with it. Cooling, bitter foods and calming practices directly douse that flame, while points like Taichong and Xingjian open the valve to release pressure, allowing blood to settle back into the vessels.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Avoid alcohol, coffee, spicy and greasy foods that add fuel to the fire. Embrace cooling, bitter greens like dandelion or chicory, and sip peppermint or chrysanthemum tea. Gentle daily movement such as walking or Tai Chi helps smooth Liver Qi, but intense, competitive exercise can aggravate heat. Prioritize winding down before bed and find healthy outlets for frustration.

### Qi not controlling Blood
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/qi-not-controlling-blood)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Small amounts of dark or pale blood, Chronic, intermittent bleeding, Fatigue and exhaustion, Poor appetite and loose stools, Pale face and lips
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Overwork or exhaustion, Cold, raw, or icy foods and drinks, Excessive talking or worry
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Rest and adequate sleep, Warm, cooked foods, Gentle walking
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When Spleen Qi is too weak to hold blood inside the vessels, blood can leak out and travel upward with stomach Qi, causing small amounts of dark or pale blood to appear in vomit. This is not a sudden, forceful bleed - it is a slow, chronic seepage that often accompanies long-standing fatigue and poor digestion.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale, puffy body with teeth marks and a thin white coating. Pulse: fine and weak, especially at the right Guan position (Spleen/Stomach).
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Overwork and worry consume Spleen Qi, making it harder for the body to hold blood. Raw and cold foods directly damage the Spleen’s digestive fire, worsening Qi deficiency. Rest, warm nourishment, and gentle movement rebuild Spleen Qi, restoring its ability to govern blood and stop the slow seepage.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat warm, easily digestible meals like congee, soups, and stewed vegetables. Avoid raw, cold, and greasy foods that burden the Spleen. Prioritize rest and gentle movement; avoid overwork and long periods of standing, which drain Qi further.

### Stomach Blood Stagnation
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/stomach-blood-stagnation)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Sharp stabbing epigastric pain, Pain fixed in one spot, worse with pressure and after eating, Vomiting dark, coffee-ground or clotted blood, Black tarry stools, Dark or purplish lips and complexion
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Cold, raw, or icy foods and drinks, Anger, stress, or frustration, Pressure on the abdomen, Overeating or large heavy meals
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warm compress on abdomen, Gentle walking, Small, frequent meals
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When blood flow in the Stomach becomes sluggish or blocked over time, it can form a fixed, painful mass of stagnant blood. This stasis weakens the vessel walls, and eventually they rupture, causing blood to leak into the Stomach. Because the blood is old and stagnant rather than fresh, it appears dark, clotted, or coffee-ground when vomited.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: dark purple body with possible purple stasis spots, thin white coat. Pulse: choppy (涩 sè) or wiry (弦 xián), rough like a knife scraping bamboo.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Warmth encourages blood to move and prevents it from congealing, while cold has the opposite effect, worsening stasis. Stress and frustration cause Qi to stagnate, which then leads to further blood stasis and pain. Pressure directly aggravates the fixed, stagnant mass, while gentle movement helps Qi and blood circulate without overburdening the Stomach.

## คำถามที่พบบ่อย (Frequently Asked Questions)
### Can TCM stop vomiting blood during an acute episode?
No. Vomiting blood is a medical emergency. If you are actively vomiting bright red blood or large amounts of coffee‑ground material, go to the emergency room immediately. TCM is not a substitute for urgent endoscopic or medical care. Once the acute bleed is stabilized and your doctor confirms it is safe, TCM can be used alongside conventional treatment to promote healing and prevent recurrence.

### How long does it take for herbs to stop the bleeding?
In heat‑driven patterns like Stomach Fire or Liver Fire invading the Stomach, cooling and blood‑stanching herbs often bring visible improvement within 2-3 days, and complete cessation of bleeding within 1-2 weeks when combined with dietary changes. For chronic, deficiency‑type bleeding where the blood is dark and oozing, it may take 4-8 weeks of consistent herbal therapy to strengthen the Spleen and stop the seepage. Every case is different, and your practitioner will adjust the formula as your tongue and pulse change.

### Can I take TCM herbs with my ulcer medication?
Generally yes, but it must be coordinated. Herbs like Da Huang (rhubarb root) can interact with blood‑thinning medications, so always give your TCM practitioner a complete list of your drugs, including proton pump inhibitors, antacids, and any anticoagulants. Take herbs and pharmaceuticals at least one hour apart. Never stop your prescribed medication without consulting your doctor.

### Is acupuncture safe for stomach bleeding?
Acupuncture is safe and beneficial once active, large‑volume bleeding has been ruled out or stabilized. Points like Zusanli ST‑36 and Zhongwan REN‑12 can calm the stomach, reduce inflammation, and support Qi. However, during an acute, heavy bleed, acupuncture is not appropriate; emergency medical care is the priority. After the acute phase, acupuncture can be a valuable part of recovery, especially for pain and nausea.

### Will the bleeding come back after TCM treatment?
The goal of TCM treatment is not just to stop the bleed but to correct the underlying pattern that caused it. When treatment is complete - meaning your tongue, pulse, and digestion have normalized - recurrence rates are significantly lower than with medication alone. However, lifestyle factors like diet, alcohol, and emotional stress can re‑trigger the pattern, so ongoing self‑care is important.

### Do I need to change my diet during TCM treatment?
Yes. Diet plays a central role in both causing and healing vomiting blood. During treatment, you will need to avoid spicy, fried, and greasy foods, alcohol, coffee, and very hot liquids. Instead, eat small, frequent meals of soft, cooling, easy‑to‑digest foods like congee, steamed vegetables, and lotus root. Your practitioner will give you specific guidance based on your pattern.
