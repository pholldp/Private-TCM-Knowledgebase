## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนปัจจุบัน (Conventional Medicine View)
### คำอธิบาย (Description)
Liver cancer, most commonly hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC), is a malignant tumor that starts in the liver's main cells. It often develops in a liver already damaged by chronic hepatitis B or C, cirrhosis, or fatty liver disease.
Typical symptoms include pain or a feeling of fullness in the upper right abdomen, unexplained weight loss, jaundice, and fluid buildup in the belly. Diagnosis usually involves imaging such as ultrasound, CT, or MRI, along with blood tests for tumor markers like alpha-fetoprotein (AFP) and sometimes a biopsy.

### การรักษาหลัก (Treatments)
Conventional treatment depends on the tumor's stage and the liver's overall function. Options include surgical removal, liver transplant, ablation, chemoembolization (TACE), and radiation therapy. For advanced disease, systemic therapies such as targeted drugs (sorafenib, lenvatinib) and immunotherapy are used. Supportive care also manages symptoms like pain, ascites, and jaundice.

## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนจีน (Traditional Chinese Medicine View)
### ความเข้าใจเกี่ยวกับการแพทย์แผนจีน (TCM Understanding)
In TCM, liver cancer is understood as a manifestation of both a root deficiency and a branch excess. The root deficiency refers to a weakened constitution (Zheng Qi), often from chronic illness, emotional stress, or poor diet, which allows pathogenic factors to accumulate. The branch excess includes Qi stagnation, Blood stasis, Damp-Heat, and toxic Fire-all of which can congeal over time into a mass.
The Liver organ system is central: it ensures the smooth flow of Qi and stores Blood. When Liver Qi becomes stuck, usually from long-standing frustration or anger, it can generate Heat and eventually cause Blood to stagnate, forming a hard, painful lump. Meanwhile, the Spleen, which transforms food into Qi, often becomes weak, leading to Dampness accumulation. This Dampness can combine with Heat to create the toxic Damp-Heat pattern, characterized by jaundice and a greasy tongue.
As the disease progresses, it drains the body's deepest reserves-the Yin of the Liver and Kidneys. This leads to deficiency heat, night sweats, and a thin body with no tongue coating. So TCM recognizes that liver cancer is not one fixed condition; it's a moving picture, with patterns shifting from early Qi stagnation to later Yin deficiency. That's why two patients with the same diagnosis may have completely different symptoms and require different herbal formulas.
This perspective allows TCM to offer truly personalized care: by identifying the dominant pattern, a practitioner can choose herbs and acupuncture points that not only address the tumor but also correct the underlying imbalances, potentially improving digestion, reducing pain, and supporting the body's ability to withstand conventional treatments.

### การวินิจฉัยโดยแพทย์แผนจีน (Practitioner Diagnosis)
A practitioner begins by asking about your energy, appetite, and any feelings of fullness under the ribs. Persistent bloating in the upper right abdomen, poor appetite, and fatigue point toward Liver Qi Stagnation with Spleen Qi Deficiency that is starting to generate heat.
The tongue is often red with redder sides, teeth marks, and a thin yellow coating, while the pulse feels wiry and rapid. Mild irritability and a dry mouth confirm that stagnation is transforming into heat, a common early change in liver cancer.
When heat becomes more pronounced, jaundice, bitter taste, and burning pain under the ribs suggest Liver and Gallbladder Damp-Heat. The tongue is red with a thick, yellow greasy coating, and the pulse is slippery and rapid.
If instead you feel intense irritability, a flushed face, and a very dry mouth without jaundice, Liver Fire Blazing may be the driver. Here the tongue is red but less greasy, and the pulse is wiry and rapid.
A fixed, hard lump in the upper abdomen that is painful to touch points to Liver Blood Stagnation. The pain is typically stabbing and refuses pressure. The tongue may be dark red or purple with possible stasis spots, and the pulse is choppy or wiry. This pattern often coexists with heat, but the presence of a palpable mass and the quality of the pain help distinguish it from purely heat-based patterns.
In later stages, deficiency patterns emerge. Spleen Deficiency with Dampness causes abdominal distension, heavy limbs, and edema, with a pale tongue that has tooth marks and a thick greasy coating. Kidney and Liver Yin Deficiency, on the other hand, brings soreness in the lower back and knees, dizziness, tinnitus, insomnia, and a red tongue with little or no coating. The contrasting tongue signs-greasy versus dry and peeled-are key to telling these two apart.

### Liver Qi Stagnation with Spleen Qi Deficiency that transforms into Heat
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/liver-qi-stagnation-with-spleen-qi-deficiency-that-transforms-into-heat)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Distending or wandering pain along the ribs, worse with stress, Bloating and loose stools, especially after eating, Irritability, quick temper, and frequent sighing, Bitter taste in the mouth, Fatigue despite feeling restless or irritable
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Anger, frustration, and stress, Greasy or fried foods, Alcohol, Overwork or insufficient rest
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Stress reduction and relaxation, Warm, cooked, easily digestible meals, Gentle movement (walking, tai chi), Acupuncture and herbal therapy
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Long-standing emotional stress and frustration cause Liver Qi to stagnate, like a traffic jam in the body's energy flow. Over time, the stuck Qi generates Heat, much like friction creates warmth, while a weakened Spleen (from poor diet or overwork) fails to produce enough Qi and allows Dampness to accumulate. In liver cancer, this combination of stagnant Qi, internal Heat, and Spleen deficiency creates an environment where the tumor can grow, and the digestive weakness worsens, leading to bloating, fatigue, and irritability.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red body with redder sides, teeth marks, and a thin to moderately thick yellow, slightly greasy coating. Pulse: wiry (xian) and rapid, particularly wiry at the left Guan (Liver) position, while the right Guan (Spleen) may feel weaker.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Stress directly aggravates Liver Qi stagnation, which then generates more Heat, so calming the mind and relaxing the body are essential. Greasy and spicy foods overburden the Spleen and create Damp-Heat, fueling the Heat transformation. Gentle movement and warm, cooked foods support the Spleen's digestive function and encourage smooth Qi flow, reducing both the stagnation and the Heat.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat small, frequent meals of warm, cooked foods like congee, steamed vegetables, and lean proteins. Avoid raw, cold, greasy, or spicy foods that burden the Spleen and generate Heat. Gentle exercise like Tai Chi or walking helps Qi flow. Practice stress management through meditation or deep breathing to prevent Qi stagnation from worsening.

### Liver and Gallbladder Damp-Heat
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/liver-and-gallbladder-damp-heat)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Yellowing of the skin and whites of the eyes (jaundice), Bitter taste in the mouth, worse in the morning, Burning or distending pain under the right ribs, Dark yellow, scanty urine like strong tea, Heavy, sluggish sensation in the body and limbs, Nausea and aversion to greasy or rich foods, Red tongue with a thick, yellow, greasy coating
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Alcohol, Greasy or fried foods, Hot, spicy dishes like chili and curry, Hot, humid weather or stuffy rooms, Overeating or eating late at night, Anger, frustration, and stress
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Light, cooling foods like mung bean soup and steamed greens, Bitter foods such as bitter melon and dandelion greens, Gentle movement (walking, tai chi), Cool, well-ventilated environment, Barley water or corn silk tea to promote urination
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Toxic Dampness and Heat accumulate in the Liver and Gallbladder, brewing together like steam trapped in a closed vessel. This obstructs the free flow of Qi and Blood through the hypochondrium, causing the characteristic burning pain and distension under the ribs.
Over time, this stagnant, toxic environment contributes to the formation and growth of masses in the liver. The intense Heat forces bile to overflow into the bloodstream, which is why the skin and eyes turn yellow, the urine becomes dark, and a bitter taste coats the mouth.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red body with redder sides, covered by a thick, yellow, greasy coating. Pulse: wiry (弦 xián), slippery (滑 huá), and rapid (数 shuò) - a taut, rolling, fast pulse that feels like beads racing under the fingers.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Damp-Heat thrives on rich, greasy, and spicy foods, which add more 'fuel' to the fire and more 'sludge' to the system. Alcohol is especially damaging because it enters the Liver channel directly, carrying Heat deep into the organ. Cooling, bitter, and lightly prepared foods do the opposite - they drain Heat, dry Dampness, and give the overburdened Liver and Gallbladder a chance to clear the toxic accumulation. Gentle movement keeps Qi and fluids circulating so they do not stagnate further, while anger and frustration cause Liver Qi to knot up, trapping the Damp-Heat inside and intensifying the burning pain and distension.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Avoid alcohol completely - it is the most direct fuel for Damp-Heat in the liver. Eliminate greasy, fried, and rich foods, as well as spicy-hot dishes, which add more Heat and Dampness to an already overloaded system.
Eat light, cooling, and easily digested foods: steamed leafy greens, mung bean soup, barley water, cucumber, and bitter melon. Small, frequent meals are easier on the digestion than large heavy ones. Gentle daily movement like walking or qigong helps Qi circulate and prevents stagnation, but rest when fatigued.

### Liver Blood Stagnation
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/liver-blood-stagnation)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Fixed stabbing pain in the upper right abdomen, Palpable hard mass or lump in the liver area, Dark purple tongue with stasis spots, Wiry and choppy pulse, Dark complexion or purplish lips and nails, Vomiting blood or nosebleeds
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Anger, frustration, and stress, Alcohol, Greasy or fried foods, Overwork or insufficient rest, Pressure on the abdomen
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Gentle movement (walking, tai chi), Warm compress on the abdomen, Stress reduction and relaxation, Light, easily digestible meals
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When Liver Qi remains stagnant for a long time, it obstructs the flow of Blood, causing it to congeal and form fixed masses. In liver cancer, this blood stasis creates hard tumors in the upper right abdomen, leading to intense stabbing pain that worsens with pressure. The stasis also disrupts the Liver's role in storing and regulating blood, leading to dark complexion, purple tongue, and bleeding tendencies.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: dark red or purple body with stasis spots, distended sublingual veins, white thick coat. Pulse: wiry and choppy, may be rapid.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Anger and frustration cause Liver Qi to stagnate, which directly leads to blood stasis and worsens pain. Alcohol and greasy foods create Damp-Heat that further congeals the blood. Gentle movement promotes the smooth flow of Qi and Blood, helping to dissolve stasis. Warmth relaxes the Liver channel and improves circulation, while rest allows the body to recover and reduces the demand on the Liver's blood-storing function.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat cooling, blood-nourishing foods like dark leafy greens, beets, and goji berries. Avoid alcohol, spicy, and greasy foods that generate damp-heat and worsen stasis. Gentle exercise such as walking or tai chi helps keep Qi and Blood moving. Manage emotional stress through meditation or counseling, as anger and frustration directly aggravate Liver stagnation.

- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Anger, frustration, and stress, Spicy, greasy, or fried foods, Alcohol and coffee, Overwork or insufficient rest, Hot weather
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Cooling foods and drinks, Stress reduction and relaxation, Gentle movement (walking, tai chi), Avoiding alcohol and spicy food
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When Liver Qi stagnation goes unresolved, the pent-up energy can transform into Fire. This blazing Fire scorches the Liver organ, creating a toxic heat that fuels the growth of malignant masses. - **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Liver Fire is fueled by anger, stress, and heating foods, which add more heat to an already overheated system. Cooling foods and calming practices directly counteract this by clearing heat and soothing the Liver, preventing the fire from rising and intensifying the disease.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat cooling, bitter foods like cucumber, celery, and dandelion greens. Avoid spicy, greasy, and fried foods, as well as alcohol and coffee, which add heat. Practice stress management techniques like meditation or gentle yoga to prevent anger from flaring up. Ensure adequate rest and avoid overwork, which can deplete yin and worsen fire.

### Spleen Deficiency with Dampness
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/spleen-deficiency-with-dampness)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Abdominal bloating and distension, worse after eating, Heaviness of the body and limbs, Loose or unformed stools, Edema or ascites (fluid accumulation in the abdomen), Poor appetite with lack of taste, Pale, swollen tongue with teeth marks and a thick white greasy coating
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Cold, raw, or greasy foods, Damp or humid weather, Overeating or eating late at night, Mental overwork or worry, Prolonged sitting or inactivity
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warm, cooked, easily digestible meals, Gentle movement (walking, tai chi), Dry, warm environment, Small, frequent meals, Abdominal warmth (hot water bottle)
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  The Spleen is responsible for transforming food into Qi and managing body fluids. When Spleen Qi is weak, it fails to transport and transform fluids, allowing dampness to accumulate internally. This dampness settles in the abdomen and limbs, causing bloating, heaviness, and edema, and over time can congeal into phlegm that contributes to mass formation in the liver.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale, swollen body with teeth marks, white greasy coating, moist surface. Pulse: soggy (Ru) and slowed-down (Huan), soft and weak at the right Guan position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Spleen Qi is easily overwhelmed by cold and dampness, which directly worsen fluid accumulation and bloating. Warm, cooked foods and gentle movement provide the gentle warmth and activity the Spleen needs to transform and transport fluids, while dryness and warmth in the environment prevent external dampness from adding to the internal burden. Overthinking and worry further weaken the Spleen, so mental calm is protective.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat warm, cooked, easily digestible foods like congee, soups, and steamed vegetables. Avoid raw, cold, greasy, or sugary foods that burden the Spleen. Gentle daily movement such as walking helps Qi circulate and transforms dampness. Keep the abdomen warm with a hot water bottle to support digestion.

- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Overwork or insufficient rest, Spicy, fried, or drying foods, Anger, frustration, and stress
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Cool, quiet environment, Moistening foods (pear, tofu, black sesame), Gentle movement like tai chi
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Liver cancer and its treatments slowly drain the body's deepest cooling and nourishing reserves - the Yin of the Kidneys and Liver. When this Yin is exhausted, the Liver loses its supple, calm nature and becomes dry and overheated, which allows the disease to progress. - **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red, thin body with cracks, little or no coating, dry surface. Pulse: fine, rapid, and wiry; weak at the left Chi (Kidney) position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Yin is the body's cooling, nourishing substance, and in this pattern it is severely depleted. Cooling, moistening foods and gentle activity restore a small measure of Yin and calm the deficiency fire that drives restlessness and pain.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat small, frequent meals of moistening and cooling foods like pear, black sesame, goji berries, and congee with lily bulb. Avoid spicy, fried, or drying foods and limit alcohol. Prioritize rest and gentle movement such as tai chi or slow walking to preserve Yin.

## คำถามที่พบบ่อย (Frequently Asked Questions)
### Can TCM cure liver cancer?
TCM is not a standalone cure for liver cancer and should never replace conventional oncology care. However, it can play a valuable supportive role. By addressing the body's underlying imbalances, TCM may help reduce tumor-related symptoms, improve appetite and energy, and lessen the side effects of chemotherapy or radiation. Some studies suggest that integrated TCM can improve quality of life and even prolong survival, but it is always used alongside standard medical treatment.

### Is it safe to combine Chinese herbs with chemotherapy or targeted therapy?
In many cases, yes, but it's crucial that your oncologist knows everything you're taking. Some herbs can affect liver enzymes that metabolize drugs, potentially altering drug levels. Others may have mild blood-thinning effects. A qualified TCM practitioner with experience in oncology will choose formulas that are safe and avoid known interactions. Always bring a complete list of your herbs to your medical appointments.

### How often will I need acupuncture?
Typically, acupuncture is given once or twice a week, especially in the beginning. As symptoms improve, the frequency may be reduced to once every two weeks or monthly for maintenance. Acupuncture can help with pain, nausea, fatigue, and stress, and it is generally well tolerated even during active cancer treatment.

### What dietary changes should I make?
Diet is a cornerstone of TCM support. In general, you'll want to avoid alcohol, greasy or fried foods, and excessive sugar, which can create Dampness and Heat. Focus on warm, cooked, easily digestible meals like congee, steamed vegetables, and soups. Small, frequent meals are better than large ones. Your practitioner will tailor recommendations to your specific pattern-for example, cooling foods for Damp-Heat or nourishing foods for Yin deficiency.

### What does my tongue tell the practitioner?
In TCM, the tongue is a mirror of the body's internal state. A red tongue with a yellow, greasy coating often points to Damp-Heat, while a purple tongue with dark spots suggests Blood Stasis. A pale, puffy tongue with teeth marks signals Spleen deficiency. These signs help confirm the pattern and guide treatment choices. Your practitioner will examine your tongue at each visit to track changes.

### Can TCM help with ascites (fluid buildup)?
Yes, depending on the pattern. Ascites in TCM is often due to Spleen deficiency with Dampness accumulation or Liver Blood Stagnation blocking fluid metabolism. Herbal formulas that strengthen the Spleen, promote urination, and move Qi can help reduce fluid. Acupuncture and moxibustion may also support fluid regulation. However, severe ascites may still require paracentesis; TCM works alongside such procedures.

## การวิจัยทางวิทยาศาสตร์สมัยใหม่ (Modern Scientific Research)
1. **Traditional Chinese medicine for hepatocellular carcinoma: A systematic review and meta-analysis**
   *วารสารวิชาการ: Zhong L, et al. Journal of Ethnopharmacology. 2019; 240: 111934.*
   This meta-analysis of 18 RCTs involving over 1,500 patients found that combining TCM with TACE significantly improved one-year survival rates and quality of life compared to TACE alone, with fewer adverse events.

2. **Acupuncture for cancer pain: a systematic review of randomized clinical trials** ([ลิงก์](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamaoncol.2019.5233))
   *วารสารวิชาการ: He Y, et al. JAMA Oncology. 2020; 6(2): 271-278.*
   A review of 20 RCTs concluded that acupuncture significantly reduces cancer-related pain intensity and opioid use, with benefits particularly noted for liver cancer patients experiencing abdominal pain.
