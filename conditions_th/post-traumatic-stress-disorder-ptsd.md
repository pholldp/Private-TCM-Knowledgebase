## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนปัจจุบัน (Conventional Medicine View)
### คำอธิบาย (Description)
Post-traumatic stress disorder develops after a person experiences or witnesses a terrifying event. Its core symptoms fall into four clusters: intrusive memories (flashbacks, nightmares), avoidance of reminders, negative changes in thinking and mood, and heightened arousal (hypervigilance, exaggerated startle).
Diagnosis follows criteria from the DSM-5, and symptoms must persist for more than a month and cause significant distress or impairment. PTSD can affect anyone, but the risk is higher after interpersonal violence, combat, or childhood trauma.

### การรักษาหลัก (Treatments)
Standard care usually combines psychotherapy and medication. Trauma-focused therapies like cognitive behavioral therapy (CBT), prolonged exposure, and EMDR are first-line approaches. Antidepressants, particularly SSRIs (sertraline, paroxetine), are commonly prescribed to reduce anxiety and improve mood.

## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนจีน (Traditional Chinese Medicine View)
### ความเข้าใจเกี่ยวกับการแพทย์แผนจีน (TCM Understanding)
TCM understands PTSD as a profound disruption of the body’s Qi - the vital substance that flows through channels and connects mind and body. A traumatic shock acts like a sudden force that scatters the Qi and disturbs the Shen (the spirit that resides in the Heart). When the Shen is unsettled, a person can’t find calm: they feel fearful, jumpy, and unable to sleep. This is the starting point for many PTSD symptoms.
The Liver is responsible for keeping Qi moving smoothly and for processing emotions. After trauma, Qi often becomes stuck - a pattern called Liver Qi Stagnation. This stuckness creates the irritability, chest tightness, and emotional numbness that many people describe. Over time, that stagnation can impair the Spleen’s ability to transform fluids, leading to Phlegm. Phlegm is heavy and turbid; it can rise and cloud the mind, causing mental fog, intrusive thoughts, and a sense of being weighed down.
Trauma also drains the body’s deeper resources. The Kidneys store our essence and govern fear; a severe fright can deplete Kidney Qi and Yin, leaving a person feeling hollow, exhausted, and unable to feel safe. The Gallbladder, the organ of courage and decision-making, can become deficient, producing the timidity and exaggerated startle that are so common in PTSD.
And when the Heart and Spleen are weakened by prolonged worry and overthinking, they fail to produce enough Blood to anchor the Shen, leading to insomnia, vivid dreaming, and palpitations. Each pattern is a different expression of the same trauma, and TCM treatment is tailored to the one that’s dominant in you.

### การวินิจฉัยโดยแพทย์แผนจีน (Practitioner Diagnosis)
A TCM practitioner begins by listening to the emotional texture of the trauma response. They ask whether the person feels more stuck and irritable, foggy and heavy, or drained and fearful - because those qualities point toward different patterns. The timing of symptoms, what makes them better or worse, and the appearance of the tongue and pulse then help to confirm the diagnosis.
If the dominant experience is emotional depression, chest and rib-side distension, sighing, and irritability that flares with stress, the practitioner suspects Liver Qi Stagnation (肝气郁结, gān qì yù jié). The tongue coating is usually thin and white, and the pulse feels wiry, like a taut guitar string.
When the person complains of mental fog, a heavy sensation in the chest, and a greasy taste in the mouth, the pattern has likely progressed to Phlegm (痰, tán) obstructing the mind. The tongue coating appears thick and greasy, and the pulse becomes slippery, as if a pearl is rolling under the finger.
A person whose main struggle is insomnia with vivid dreams, palpitations, poor appetite, and a washed‑out complexion is often showing Heart and Spleen Qi and Blood Deficiency. The tongue looks pale and may be slightly swollen, and the pulse is weak and thready.
If severe fright has left the person timid, easily startled, and hypervigilant - jumping at small sounds - the practitioner looks for Gallbladder Deficiency. Here the courage‑governing function of the Gallbladder is depleted, and the pulse may feel thin and hesitant.
In longer‑standing or more severe trauma, palpitations, poor memory, tinnitus, and a sense of emptiness point to Heart and Kidney Yin Deficiency. The tongue appears red with little or no coating, and the pulse is thin and rapid.
When that Yin deficiency deepens and empty‑heat rises, the person becomes more irritable, feels hot in the palms and chest, has a dry mouth, and may sweat at night. The tongue is red with a scant coating, and the pulse is thin and rapid - a sign that the cooling, moistening resources of the body are running low.

### Liver Qi Stagnation
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/liver-qi-stagnation)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Distension or bloating along the ribs and flanks, Frequent sighing, Irritability or short temper, Feeling of a lump in the throat (plum pit sensation), Chest tightness, Mood swings between depression and anger
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Stress and frustration, Greasy or heavy foods, Prolonged sitting or inactivity, Cold environments, Suppressing feelings
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Gentle exercise, Deep breathing, Warmth on the ribs, Light, easily digestible meals, Expressing emotions
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Trauma acts like a sudden shock that freezes the smooth flow of Qi. The Liver is responsible for keeping Qi moving throughout the body and for processing emotions. When Qi becomes stuck, especially after a distressing event, the Liver's function is impaired, leading to a buildup of emotional tension. This manifests as the irritability, chest tightness, and emotional numbness seen in PTSD, as the constrained Qi struggles to find release.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: normal or slightly dark body with red or purplish sides and a thin white coat. Pulse: wiry (弦 xián), taut like a guitar string.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Stress and frustration directly cause Qi to stagnate further, locking in the emotional tension that defines this pattern. Gentle movement and deep breathing physically stimulate the flow of Qi, while warmth relaxes the Liver channel. Heavy, greasy foods burden the Spleen and create dampness, which can further obstruct Qi, whereas light meals support smooth digestion. Expressing emotions, rather than holding them in, allows the Liver's natural function of processing and releasing feelings to resume.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Prioritize gentle, rhythmic movement like walking, tai chi, or yoga to keep Qi flowing. Avoid greasy, heavy, or processed foods that can worsen stagnation. Practice deep breathing or mindfulness to consciously release held tension. Create a daily routine that includes time for emotional expression, whether through journaling or talking with a trusted friend.

### Phlegm
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/phlegm)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Mental fog and difficulty concentrating, Heavy sensation in the head or chest, Greasy white tongue coating, Sensation of a lump in the throat, Excessive sleepiness or lethargy, Loose sticky stools
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Greasy, fried, or dairy foods, Cold and raw foods, Overthinking and worry, Sedentary lifestyle, Damp, humid weather
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warm, cooked meals, Gentle exercise, Acupuncture or acupressure, Dry, sunny weather, Spicy herbs like ginger
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  After a traumatic shock, Liver Qi easily stagnates. If this stagnation persists, it disrupts the Spleen's ability to transform and transport fluids, allowing Dampness to accumulate and condense into Phlegm.
Phlegm is heavy and turbid. It can rise and cloud the mind (Shen), causing mental fog, intrusive memories, and a heavy, stuck sensation in the chest and head that mirrors the feeling of being trapped by the trauma.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: Swollen pale body with teeth marks, thick white greasy coating, moist. Pulse: Slippery (Hua), like beads rolling on a thread.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Phlegm arises from Dampness that the Spleen fails to transform. Cold, damp, and greasy foods overload the Spleen and create more Dampness, worsening Phlegm. Overthinking knots Qi and directly weakens the Spleen. Warm foods and gentle movement support the Spleen's function, helping to transform and transport fluids, while acupuncture points like Fenglong specifically drain Phlegm from the body.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat warm, cooked foods and avoid cold, raw, greasy, and dairy products that generate Dampness. Gentle daily exercise like walking or qigong helps move Qi and disperse Phlegm. Practice mindfulness to reduce overthinking, which can weaken the Spleen.

### Heart and Spleen Qi and Blood Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/heart-and-spleen-qi-and-blood-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Palpitations or a fluttering sensation in the chest, Insomnia with excessive dreaming, Poor appetite with bloating after eating, Forgetfulness and poor concentration, Pale complexion and pale lips, Loose stools
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Worry and overthinking, Raw or cold foods, Overwork and exhaustion, Irregular eating habits
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warm, cooked meals, Gentle daily movement, Regular sleep routine, Quiet rest after eating
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  After trauma, persistent worry and overthinking drain the Spleen and Heart. The Spleen fails to produce enough Qi and Blood, leaving the Heart without the nourishment it needs to anchor the mind. This leads to the insomnia, vivid dreaming, palpitations, and forgetfulness so common in PTSD. The weakened Spleen also causes poor appetite, bloating, loose stools, and deep fatigue, making it very hard for the body and mind to recover from traumatic stress.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale, puffy, teeth-marked body with a thin white coating. Pulse: fine and weak, especially empty at the left Cun (Heart) position and soft at the right Guan (Spleen) position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: The Spleen and Heart are easily damaged by excessive mental strain and poor dietary habits. Worry and overthinking directly consume Heart and Spleen Qi and Blood, while raw and cold foods weaken the Spleen's ability to transform food into energy. Warm, nourishing meals and rest allow the Spleen to rebuild Qi and Blood, which the Heart then uses to anchor the mind. Gentle movement keeps Qi flowing without draining it further, and a regular sleep rhythm helps the Heart store the mind at night.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat warm, cooked, easily digestible meals at regular times. Soups, stews, and congees are ideal. Avoid raw, cold, or greasy foods that weaken the Spleen. Gentle daily movement like walking or tai chi helps build Qi without depleting it. Prioritize a consistent sleep routine and allow yourself quiet rest after eating.

### Gallbladder Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/gallbladder-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Timidity and lack of courage, Easily startled by small noises or movements, Indecisiveness, Restless sleep with vivid or frightening dreams, Heart palpitations, Dizziness
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Sudden loud noises, Watching scary content, Fatigue and overwork, Cold, raw foods
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Calm, quiet environment, Warm, nourishing foods, Gentle exercise, Reassuring company
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  In TCM, the Gallbladder is the organ of courage and decision-making. A severe fright or traumatic shock can drain its Qi, leaving a person feeling timid, indecisive, and unable to judge safety from danger. This deficiency directly produces the hypervigilance, easy startle, and fearful dreams that are so common in PTSD.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale, thin body with a thin white coating. Pulse: fine (Xi), weak (Ruo), and often wiry (Xian), especially weak at the left Guan (middle) position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: The Gallbladder governs courage and decision-making; after a traumatic shock its Qi is easily drained. Sudden frights or exhausting activities further scatter what little Qi remains, worsening timidity and startle. Quiet, warm, and supportive conditions allow the Qi to consolidate, while nourishing foods and gentle movement gradually rebuild the Gallbladder's strength, restoring a sense of safety and resolve.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Prioritize warm, cooked meals and avoid cold or raw foods that weaken Qi. Gentle daily movement like walking or tai chi can help rebuild internal strength without overwhelming the nervous system. Create a calm evening routine and avoid violent or frightening media.

### Heart and Kidney Yin Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/heart-and-kidney-yin-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Palpitations or pounding heartbeat, Insomnia with vivid, disturbing dreams, Night sweats, Low back soreness or weakness, Tinnitus or ringing in the ears, Poor memory and mental restlessness
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Overthinking and emotional strain, Spicy, fried, or heating foods, Overwork and staying up late, Hot, dry environments
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Rest and early nights, Cool, dark bedroom, Nourishing, moistening foods (pears, black beans, congee), Gentle walking or tai chi
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Severe or prolonged trauma can exhaust the deepest reserves of the body. The initial shock and fear injure Kidney essence, while ongoing emotional turmoil and overthinking gradually deplete Heart Yin. In a healthy state, the Heart's fire and Kidney's water balance each other to keep the mind calm and the body cool. When both become deficient, the Heart loses its anchor and the Kidney cannot nourish the brain. This disconnect leads to the palpitations, restless sleep, poor memory, and tinnitus that are so common in this pattern. The deficiency also allows empty heat to rise, producing night sweats, warm palms and soles, and a dry mouth.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red, especially at the tip, thin body, little or no coating, dry surface, possible cracks. Pulse: fine (thin) and rapid; left Kidney position (Chi) weak, left Heart position (Cun) may feel relatively more prominent and slightly floating.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: This pattern is a deficiency of cooling, nourishing Yin fluids in the Heart and Kidney. Heat, dryness, and overstimulation further consume Yin and stir up empty heat, making palpitations, restlessness, and night sweats worse. Rest, cooling foods, and a calm environment replenish Yin, anchor the Heart, and allow the Kidney essence to recover, which gradually soothes the mind and body.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Prioritize rest and avoid overwork or late nights, which further consume Yin. Eat moistening, cooling foods like pears, black beans, millet congee, lily bulbs, and sesame seeds. Steer clear of spicy, fried, or overly heating foods, as well as alcohol and coffee. A cool, dark bedroom supports deeper sleep. Gentle practices like tai chi or restorative yoga can help settle the mind without exhausting the body.

- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Cool, quiet environment, Going to bed early, Pears, black beans, and other cooling, moist foods, Gentle, restorative movement like yin yoga, Deep, slow breathing

  When trauma depletes the body's Yin-its cooling, nourishing substance-over time, a state of emptiness arises. The heat rises to the chest, palms, and head, creating a sensation of constant inner turmoil that mimics the hypervigilance and irritability of post-traumatic stress. The mind cannot settle because the body's foundation is overheated and undernourished.

- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: This pattern is rooted in a deep depletion of Yin, the body's cooling and nourishing foundation. Overwork, late nights, and emotional stress further consume Yin, while spicy foods and hot environments add pathological heat. Rest and cooling, moistening foods help replenish Yin and calm the empty fire, allowing the agitated mind to settle. Gentle movement moves Qi without creating more heat, and deep breathing anchors the spirit downward.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat cooling, moistening foods like pears, black beans, sesame seeds, and millet. Avoid spicy, fried, or overly stimulating foods that add heat. Go to bed early and prioritise deep rest; late nights are especially draining. Gentle practices like tai chi or yin yoga can help restore the body's reserves without overheating.

## คำถามที่พบบ่อย (Frequently Asked Questions)
### Can TCM help with PTSD flashbacks and nightmares?
Yes. Flashbacks and nightmares are often linked to an unsettled Shen (spirit) or to Phlegm clouding the mind. Acupuncture points like Shenmen (HT-7) and Baihui (DU-20) help calm the spirit, while herbs in formulas like Gui Pi Tang or Ding Zhi Wan can anchor the mind and reduce intrusive memories. Many people report that their sleep deepens and nightmares become less frequent within the first month of treatment.

### Is it safe to combine TCM with my antidepressant or therapy?
Generally yes, and many patients use TCM alongside conventional care. However, some herbs have mild sedative effects, so you should always tell both your TCM practitioner and your prescribing doctor about everything you’re taking. Never stop a psychiatric medication abruptly - if you and your doctor decide to taper, TCM can support you through the process. Bring a full list of your medications to your first TCM consultation so the practitioner can check for any potential interactions.

### How does acupuncture work for PTSD?
Acupuncture helps by moving stuck Qi, calming the nervous system, and directly settling the Shen. Points on the Heart, Pericardium, and Gallbladder channels are often used to reduce hypervigilance and startle. Many people feel a deep sense of relaxation during and after treatment, which can be especially valuable when the body has been stuck in a fight-or-flight state for a long time.

### How long until I see results?
Most people notice better sleep and a reduction in anxiety within 2-4 weeks of starting herbs and acupuncture. Deeper emotional healing and a lasting sense of safety take longer - often 3-6 months. Your practitioner will track your tongue and pulse to see how the pattern is shifting, even before you consciously feel the changes.

### Will I need to change my diet?
Basic dietary adjustments can make a big difference. Avoiding stimulants like caffeine and alcohol is important because they can worsen anxiety and disrupt sleep. Eating warm, cooked, easily digestible foods supports the Spleen and helps prevent Phlegm buildup. Your practitioner may give more specific advice depending on your pattern - for example, adding nourishing foods if you’re deficient, or avoiding greasy foods if you have Phlegm.

### Do I need to talk about the trauma during TCM treatment?
No. TCM diagnosis is based on your current symptoms, tongue, and pulse - not on recounting the traumatic event. You are never required to share details of the trauma. The focus is on how your body and mind are responding now, so treatment can be gentle and non-retraumatizing.

### Can TCM help with the physical symptoms of PTSD, like fatigue and palpitations?
Absolutely. TCM sees these physical symptoms as part of the same pattern as the emotional ones. For example, palpitations and fatigue often point to Heart and Spleen deficiency or Heart and Kidney Yin deficiency. Herbal formulas like Gui Pi Tang or Tian Wang Bu Xin Dan directly address these by nourishing Blood and Yin, which in turn calms the heart and restores energy.

   A pilot RCT comparing acupuncture, cognitive behavioral therapy (CBT), and a wait-list control for PTSD. Acupuncture significantly reduced PTSD symptoms and was comparable to CBT, with effects maintained at 3-month follow-up.

2. **Acupuncture for post-traumatic stress disorder: a systematic review of randomized controlled trials and prospective clinical trials** ([ลิงก์](https://doi.org/10.1155/2013/615857))
   *วารสารวิชาการ: Kim YD, Heo I, Shin BC, Crawford C, Kang HW, Lim JH. Acupuncture for post-traumatic stress disorder: a systematic review of randomized controlled trials and prospective clinical trials. Evid Based Complement Alternat Med. 2013;2013:615857.*
   A systematic review of acupuncture for PTSD, concluding that acupuncture is effective in reducing PTSD symptoms but that larger, high-quality trials are needed to confirm the findings.
