## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนปัจจุบัน (Conventional Medicine View)
### คำอธิบาย (Description)
Adenomyosis occurs when the tissue that normally lines the uterus (the endometrium) grows into the muscular wall of the uterus. This causes the uterus to become enlarged, tender, and boggy, leading to heavy, prolonged, and intensely painful periods. The pain often feels like a deep, cramping ache in the lower abdomen and can radiate to the lower back or thighs.
Diagnosis is typically made through transvaginal ultrasound or MRI, which can detect the thickened uterine wall and the characteristic 'Swiss cheese' appearance of the myometrium. Adenomyosis is most common in women in their 40s and 50s who have had children, though it can affect younger women as well.

### การรักษาหลัก (Treatments)
Conventional management usually begins with NSAIDs for pain relief and hormonal therapies to control bleeding. Birth control pills, the levonorgestrel-releasing IUD (Mirena), or GnRH agonists may be used to suppress menstruation and reduce symptoms. For severe cases that don't respond to these measures, more invasive options like uterine artery embolization or hysterectomy are considered. While these treatments can manage symptoms, they often come with side effects and do not address the root cause - and symptoms frequently return when treatment stops.

## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนจีน (Traditional Chinese Medicine View)
### ความเข้าใจเกี่ยวกับการแพทย์แผนจีน (TCM Understanding)
In TCM, adenomyosis falls under the category of 'painful menstruation' (痛经, tòng jīng) and 'abdominal masses' (癥瘕, zhēng jiǎ). The central problem is Blood stasis in the uterus - blood that is not flowing freely but is stuck, forming clots, creating fixed pain, and enlarging the organ. But the real question is: what caused the blood to stagnate? That's where TCM looks deeper, to the organ systems and external factors that govern circulation.
Cold is one of the most common culprits. When Cold invades the lower abdomen - from exposure to cold weather, swimming during menstruation, or a diet rich in cold foods - it constricts the vessels and congeals the blood, much like water turning to ice. This creates sharp, stabbing, fixed pain that feels better with warmth. The tongue may appear pale-purple, and the pulse feels deep and tight.
Another major cause is Qi stagnation, often driven by chronic stress, frustration, or emotional tension. The Liver is responsible for the smooth flow of Qi throughout the body, and when it gets stuck, Qi can no longer push blood through the pelvic vessels. The result is distending, cramping pain that worsens before the period and eases once clots pass. The tongue is purple-dark with a wiry pulse.
Deficiency patterns also play a role. When Spleen Qi is weak - from overwork, poor diet, or chronic illness - there isn't enough vital energy to move the blood, leading to stagnation. This causes profound fatigue, heavy periods, and a dull, dragging pain. In other cases, Kidney Yang deficiency fails to warm the Chong and Ren vessels, the extraordinary channels that govern the uterus, allowing Cold and stasis to settle in. A TCM practitioner listens carefully to your pain story, examines your tongue and pulse, and asks about accompanying signs like lower back soreness or mood changes to pinpoint which pattern - or combination of patterns - is driving your adenomyosis.

### การวินิจฉัยโดยแพทย์แผนจีน (Practitioner Diagnosis)
A practitioner first listens carefully to your pain story. The timing, quality, and what relieves the pain matter most. Sharp, fixed, stabbing pain that improves with warmth points toward Cold congealing the Blood. Distending, wandering pain that worsens with stress suggests Qi stagnation. Dull, persistent ache with exhaustion and heavy bleeding leans toward Qi deficiency. A sensation of heaviness and a feeling of masses inside point to Phlegm-Dampness combining with stasis.
Next, the practitioner examines your tongue and pulse. A pale, dark, or purplish tongue with stasis spots and a deep, tight pulse are hallmarks of Cold-induced stasis. A purple-dark tongue with a wiry pulse indicates Qi and Blood stagnation. A pale, swollen tongue with a weak pulse points to Qi deficiency. A thick, greasy tongue coating with a slippery pulse suggests Phlegm-Dampness. A red tongue with a yellow, greasy coating and a rapid pulse reveals Heat and Dampness scorching the Blood.
They will also ask about accompanying signs that reveal the root. Lower back soreness, knee weakness, and dizziness alongside clots point to the Kidney system failing to warm the Directing and Penetrating Vessels. Fatigue so severe it feels like the body cannot hold the blood inside, with a pale face, points to the Spleen Qi being too weak to manage the Blood. A sensation of heat, yellow-tinged discharge, and burning pain suggest Damp-Heat has brewed in the pelvis and damaged the Blood network.
Finally, the practitioner palpates the lower abdomen to feel for fixed, painful masses and checks the menstrual history for dark, clotted flow versus fresh bleeding. They weave together all these threads - the pain quality, tongue, pulse, and systemic signs - to pinpoint whether the root is Cold, Qi stagnation, Qi deficiency, Phlegm, Heat, or Kidney weakness, because the Blood stasis itself is the branch, not the root.

### Blood Stagnation in the Lower Burner  caused by Cold
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/blood-stagnation-in-the-lower-burner-caused-by-cold)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Fixed, stabbing lower abdominal pain, Pain relieved by warmth, Dark purple menstrual blood with clots, Aversion to cold, cold hands and feet, Pain worsens with cold exposure
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Cold weather or exposure to cold, Eating cold or raw foods, Emotional stress, anger, or worry, Standing for long periods, Washing with cold water
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Applying warmth on the lower abdomen (e.g., hot water bottle, warm compress), Eating warm, cooked meals or drinks (e.g., soups, ginger tea), Rest, sleep, and emotional calm, Gentle movement or exercise (e.g., walking, yoga)
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Cold is a constricting force in the body. When it invades the lower abdomen, it causes the vessels to tighten and the Blood to congeal, much like water turning to ice. This creates a fixed, stabbing pain and dark, clotted menstrual blood that is characteristic of adenomyosis. The uterus becomes a site of chronic stasis, where Blood cannot flow freely, leading to the severe cramping and tissue changes seen in this condition.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale-purple or bluish-purple body, possible stasis spots, white slippery coat. Pulse: deep (Chen), tight (Jin), and choppy (Se).
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Cold constricts and congeals, directly worsening the Blood stasis that causes the pain, so any cold exposure-whether from weather, food, or water-intensifies the symptoms. Warmth, on the other hand, dilates the vessels, melts the 'ice,' and gets Blood moving again, which is why heat is so instantly effective. Stress causes Qi to stagnate, which further blocks Blood flow, so emotional calm is also protective.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Keep your lower abdomen warm at all times, especially during your period. Use a hot water bottle or warm compress, and avoid sitting on cold surfaces. Eat warming, cooked foods like soups and stews with ginger and cinnamon; avoid raw, cold, or frozen foods and drinks. Gentle walking or yoga can help keep Qi and Blood moving without overstraining.

### Qi And Blood Stagnation
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/qi-and-blood-stagnation)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Distending lower abdominal pain before period, Dark purple menstrual blood with clots, Irritability, mood swings, or breast distension, Purple tongue body with stasis spots
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Emotional stress, anger, or worry, Sedentary lifestyle, Eating cold or raw foods, Premenstrual phase
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Gentle movement or exercise (e.g., walking, yoga), Applying warmth on the lower abdomen (e.g., hot water bottle, warm compress), Rest, sleep, and emotional calm, Eating warm, cooked meals or drinks (e.g., soups, ginger tea)
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When stress, frustration, or emotional tension build up over time, the Liver’s ability to keep Qi flowing smoothly stalls. This stagnant Qi can no longer push Blood through the vessels of the lower abdomen, so Blood begins to pool and form stasis in the uterus. The result is the hallmark distending, fixed pain before and during periods, along with dark, clotted menstrual flow.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: purple or dark body with stasis spots, distended sublingual veins, thin white coat. Pulse: wiry and choppy (弦涩 xián sè).
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Stress and emotional upset cause Liver Qi to stagnate, directly obstructing the flow of Blood in the uterus and worsening pain and clots. Gentle movement and warmth help Qi circulate, while cold and inactivity congeal both Qi and Blood, intensifying stasis.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Avoid cold and raw foods that constrict Qi flow. Eat lightly cooked vegetables and add warming spices like ginger and turmeric. Regular gentle exercise such as walking or yoga helps keep Qi moving. Make time for stress management with meditation or deep breathing.

### Qi Deficiency causing Blood Stagnation
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/qi-deficiency-causing-blood-stagnation)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Profound fatigue and weakness, Heavy, prolonged menstrual periods with dark clots, Fixed, stabbing lower abdominal pain, Pale, greyish complexion, Shortness of breath on mild exertion, Dizziness
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Overwork or physical exhaustion, Standing for long periods, Eating cold or raw foods, Emotional stress, anger, or worry, Cold weather or exposure to cold
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Rest, sleep, and emotional calm, Applying warmth on the lower abdomen (e.g., hot water bottle, warm compress), Gentle movement or exercise (e.g., walking, yoga), Eating warm, cooked meals or drinks (e.g., soups, ginger tea)
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Your body's vital energy (Qi) is the force that keeps blood moving smoothly through your vessels. When Spleen Qi becomes weak, it can no longer propel blood effectively, so the flow slows and pools in the uterus, forming dark clots and causing heavy, prolonged periods. The stasis creates a fixed, stabbing pain in the lower abdomen, while the underlying Qi deficiency leaves you profoundly tired, pale, and short of breath.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale body with a dark or purplish hue, scattered purple spots, teeth marks, thin white coating. Pulse: deep, fine, and rough (choppy), like a blade scraping bamboo.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Qi is the energy that moves blood. Rest and gentle movement build Qi, while overwork depletes it, making stasis worse. Warmth helps Qi and blood flow, whereas cold congeals blood and stiffens the channels. Stress and worry stagnate Liver Qi, which further impairs blood circulation, so calming activities ease the stasis. Eating warm, cooked foods supports Spleen Qi production, while cold raw foods weaken the digestive fire and fail to generate enough Qi to move blood.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat warm, easily digested foods like congee, soups, and stews to support Spleen Qi. Avoid raw, cold, and greasy foods that weaken digestion. Gentle movement such as walking or Qi Gong helps Qi flow without depleting it. Prioritize rest and avoid overwork.

### Blood Stagnation and Phlegm in the Uterus
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/blood-stagnation-and-phlegm-in-the-uterus)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Palpable mass or hardness in the lower abdomen, Fixed, stabbing lower abdominal pain, Copious thick, sticky vaginal discharge, Sensation of heaviness and fullness in the lower abdomen, Pain not relieved by warmth alone
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Cold weather or exposure to cold, Sedentary lifestyle, Dairy, greasy, or raw foods, Emotional stress, anger, or worry
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Gentle movement or exercise (e.g., walking, yoga), Warm, dry environment, Eating warm, cooked meals or drinks (e.g., soups, ginger tea), Avoiding damp, greasy foods
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When the Spleen is weakened by diet or lifestyle, it fails to transform fluids, allowing Phlegm-Dampness to accumulate in the body. This sticky, heavy substance then settles in the uterus and entangles with Blood stasis, creating firm, palpable masses and a dragging, heavy pain that feels fixed and stabbing. ### Heat and Blood Stagnation in the Lower Burner
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/heat-and-blood-stagnation-in-the-lower-burner)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Fixed, stabbing lower abdominal pain, Palpable mass or hardness in the lower abdomen, Dark purple menstrual blood with clots, Irritability, mood swings, or breast distension, Nighttime fever, Dry mouth and throat
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Spicy, greasy, or fried foods, Emotional stress, anger, or worry, Hot weather or overheating, Strenuous or heating exercise
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Cool compress on lower abdomen, Gentle movement or exercise (e.g., walking, yoga), Cooling foods (e.g., cucumber, celery), Rest, sleep, and emotional calm
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  In this pattern, Heat has built up in the pelvis and lower abdomen, often from long-standing emotional stress, a diet rich in spicy or greasy foods, or a lingering infection. That Heat then "scorches" the local Blood, making it thick and sticky so it cannot flow freely. The result is a painful, hardened stagnation in the uterus that shows up as sharp, fixed pain and dark, clotted menstrual bleeding.
Because Heat can also disturb the mind, restlessness, irritability, and a feeling of warmth at night are common companions to the physical pain.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: Reddish-purple body with stasis spots, distended sublingual veins, and a dry yellow coating. Pulse: Deep, choppy, and rapid.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Heat and emotional stress are the key drivers of this pattern, so spicy foods and anger literally add fuel to the fire, intensifying the stasis and pain. Cooling foods, gentle movement, and emotional calm directly counter the Heat, while cool compresses soothe the local stagnation and ease the fixed pain.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Avoid spicy, fried, and greasy foods that fan internal Heat, and cut back on coffee and alcohol. Eat more cooling, blood-nourishing foods like cucumber, celery, and small amounts of bitter greens. Gentle daily movement such as walking or restorative yoga helps keep Qi and Blood circulating without overheating the body.

### Blood Stagnation in the Directing and Penetrating Vessels
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/blood-stagnation-in-the-directing-and-penetrating-vessels)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Fixed, stabbing lower abdominal pain, Lower back soreness, Dark purple menstrual blood with clots, Pain eases after passing menstrual clots, Irregular menstrual cycles
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Cold weather or exposure to cold, Overwork or physical exhaustion, Eating cold or raw foods, Standing for long periods, Emotional stress, anger, or worry
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Applying warmth on the lower abdomen (e.g., hot water bottle, warm compress), Rest, sleep, and emotional calm, Gentle movement or exercise (e.g., walking, yoga), Passing menstrual clots, Eating warm, cooked meals or drinks (e.g., soups, ginger tea)
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  The Kidneys are the root of the Chong (Penetrating) and Ren (Directing) vessels, the two extraordinary channels that govern the uterus and menstrual blood. When Kidney Yang is too weak to warm and nourish these vessels, Blood congeals and stagnates - much like a stream freezing in winter. This stagnation produces sharp, fixed lower abdominal pain and dark, clotted menstrual blood, while the underlying Kidney deficiency causes lower back soreness and dizziness.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: purple or dusky body with possible stasis spots and engorged sublingual veins; thin white coat. Pulse: choppy (涩 sè), wiry at the left Guan position, and deep at the Chi positions.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Cold and exhaustion drain Kidney Yang, which is the very fire that keeps the Chong and Ren vessels warm and Blood flowing. When that fire dims, Blood congeals and pain intensifies. Warmth - whether from a hot water bottle, warm food, or moxibustion - rekindles the Kidney fire and thaws the stagnation. Rest and gentle movement work together: rest preserves Qi, while gentle movement prevents Blood from pooling without overtaxing the Kidneys. Passing clots relieves the physical obstruction, which is why pain often eases immediately afterward.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Keep the lower abdomen and lower back warm - a hot water bottle or gentle moxibustion can make a real difference. Eat warming, cooked foods like lamb stew, ginger, cinnamon, walnuts, and black beans; avoid raw, cold foods and icy drinks that chill the womb. Gentle daily movement such as walking or restorative yoga helps Blood circulate without depleting Kidney energy. Prioritize rest and avoid overwork, especially during your period.

## คำถามที่พบบ่อย (Frequently Asked Questions)
### Can acupuncture and herbs really help with adenomyosis pain?
Yes. Many women find significant relief from the heavy, cramping pain of adenomyosis through TCM. Acupuncture helps relax the uterine muscles, improve local blood flow, and regulate the nervous system's pain response. Herbal formulas are tailored to your specific pattern - warming the uterus to dissolve Cold-induced stasis, moving Qi to relieve stagnation, or tonifying Qi and Blood to strengthen the body's ability to circulate. The combination often reduces both the intensity and duration of menstrual pain within a few cycles.

### Can I use TCM alongside my hormonal birth control or Mirena IUD?
Yes, TCM is generally safe to combine with hormonal therapies. Acupuncture does not interfere with the hormones or the IUD, and herbal formulas are chosen with your medications in mind. However, some blood-moving herbs like Dang Gui or Chuan Xiong can have mild anticoagulant effects, so if you are also on blood thinners, it's essential to inform both your TCM practitioner and your doctor. Never stop prescribed hormones abruptly - if your symptoms improve with TCM, work with your prescribing doctor to adjust dosages gradually.

### How long before I see results?
Most patients notice some improvement in pain or bleeding within the first or second menstrual cycle after starting treatment. For significant, lasting change, plan on at least 2-3 months of consistent herbs and acupuncture. If your adenomyosis is driven by a deficiency pattern - like Qi or Kidney deficiency - the rebuilding process takes longer, often 4-6 months, because we are nourishing the body's reserves, not just moving stagnation. Your practitioner will track progress cycle by cycle and adjust the formula as your pattern shifts.

### What foods should I avoid if I have adenomyosis?
Cold and raw foods are the biggest culprit, especially for Cold-induced stasis patterns. Ice-cold drinks, smoothies, salads, and raw fruits straight from the fridge can worsen uterine cramping and clotting. Greasy, fried, and heavily processed foods promote Phlegm-Dampness, which can combine with stasis to form masses. Spicy, heating foods may aggravate Heat patterns. Instead, emphasize warm, cooked meals - soups, stews, steamed vegetables, and gentle spices like ginger and cinnamon. A TCM practitioner can give you more specific guidance based on your pattern.

### Will TCM shrink adenomyosis nodules or an enlarged uterus?
TCM can sometimes reduce uterine size and soften the texture of the myometrium by breaking up Blood stasis and resolving Phlegm, but this is a gradual process. The primary goal is to relieve pain, regulate bleeding, and prevent progression. Over months of treatment, many women see their uterus become less boggy on follow-up imaging, but complete resolution is not guaranteed. The real win is a dramatic improvement in quality of life - less pain, lighter periods, and more energy.

### Is adenomyosis curable with Chinese medicine?
TCM views adenomyosis as a chronic, manageable condition rather than something that is simply 'cured' with a single course of treatment. The goal is to bring the body back into balance so that symptoms subside and the condition stops progressing. Many women achieve long-term relief and can avoid surgery, but they may need periodic 'tune-up' treatments, especially during times of stress or seasonal changes. For some, the condition goes into complete remission; for others, it becomes a background issue that no longer controls their life.

## การวิจัยทางวิทยาศาสตร์สมัยใหม่ (Modern Scientific Research)
1. **Acupuncture for adenomyosis: A systematic review and meta-analysis**
   *วารสารวิชาการ: Liu Z, Liu Y, Xu H, et al. Acupuncture for adenomyosis: A systematic review and meta-analysis. Medicine (Baltimore). 2019;98(49):e18258.*
   This systematic review included 12 RCTs with 1,024 participants and found that acupuncture significantly reduced dysmenorrhea pain scores and improved quality of life compared to conventional medication. The effect was more pronounced with longer treatment courses, and adverse events were minimal.

2. **Efficacy and safety of Chinese herbal medicine for adenomyosis: A systematic review**
   *วารสารวิชาการ: Zhang Y, Wang C, Guo Z, et al. Efficacy and safety of Chinese herbal medicine for adenomyosis: a systematic review. Front Pharmacol. 2020;11:588.*
   This review analyzed 18 RCTs involving 1,632 women and concluded that Chinese herbal medicine was superior to conventional drugs in reducing pain and uterine volume, with a lower recurrence rate. However, the authors cautioned that the overall evidence quality was limited by methodological flaws in the primary studies.
