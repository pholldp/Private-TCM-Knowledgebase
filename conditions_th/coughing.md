## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนปัจจุบัน (Conventional Medicine View)
### คำอธิบาย (Description)
In Western medicine, a cough is a protective reflex to clear the airways of irritants, mucus, or foreign particles. It can be acute (lasting less than three weeks), subacute, or chronic, and is often classified by its sound and whether it's productive (wet) or non-productive (dry). Common causes include viral upper respiratory infections, allergies, asthma, postnasal drip, gastroesophageal reflux, and chronic bronchitis. Diagnosis typically involves listening to the lungs, sometimes chest X-rays, and tests for underlying conditions like asthma or infection.
Treatment focuses on addressing the underlying cause - antibiotics for bacterial infections, antihistamines for allergies, inhalers for asthma - along with symptomatic relief using cough suppressants or expectorants. While this approach effectively manages many acute coughs, chronic or recurrent coughs can remain a challenge when no clear cause is identified.

### การรักษาหลัก (Treatments)
Standard treatments include over-the-counter cough suppressants (dextromethorphan) for dry coughs, expectorants (guaifenesin) to thin mucus, decongestants for postnasal drip, and antihistamines for allergies. For chronic cough, doctors may prescribe inhaled corticosteroids for asthma, proton pump inhibitors for acid reflux, or neuropathic agents for a hypersensitive cough reflex. In cases of bacterial infection, antibiotics are used. Lifestyle measures like humidifiers and avoiding irritants are also recommended.

## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนจีน (Traditional Chinese Medicine View)
### ความเข้าใจเกี่ยวกับการแพทย์แผนจีน (TCM Understanding)
In TCM, the Lungs are responsible for the descent and dispersion of Qi. When this function is disrupted, Qi rebels upward, triggering a cough. The Lungs are also the most delicate organ, directly connected to the outside through the nose and throat, making them the first line of defense against external pathogens like Wind, Cold, and Heat. That's why a cough is so often the first sign of a cold or flu - the external pathogen is battling with the Lung's defensive Qi.
But the Lungs don't work alone. The Spleen transforms fluids; if it's weak, it produces phlegm, which can rise and obstruct the Lungs. The Liver ensures the smooth flow of Qi; if it stagnates, it can create Heat that flares upward and irritates the Lungs. Even the Kidneys play a role - they grasp the Qi and help the Lungs inhale deeply. A cough can therefore arise from a simple external invasion or from a deeper internal imbalance, and the character of the cough - the sound, the phlegm, the timing - reveals which organ is the root.
This is why a single Western diagnosis like 'bronchitis' or 'post-viral cough' can have several different TCM patterns. A cough with thin white phlegm, chills, and a floating pulse is Wind-Cold. A cough with thick yellow phlegm, fever, and thirst is Wind-Heat or Phlegm-Heat. A weak, breathless cough that worsens with exertion is Lung Qi Deficiency. Each pattern requires a different herbal formula and acupuncture strategy. By identifying the pattern, TCM treats not just the cough but the underlying terrain that allowed it to take hold.

### การวินิจฉัยโดยแพทย์แผนจีน (Practitioner Diagnosis)
A TCM practitioner starts by asking about the cough’s character and the sputum. The color, thickness, and ease of expectoration point toward different patterns. Accompanying sensations like chills, fever, chest tightness, and energy levels are just as important as the cough itself. The tongue coating and pulse provide the final confirmation.
If the cough brings up thin, white sputum and the person feels chilled, with an aversion to cold and a floating tight pulse, it suggests Wind-Cold invading the Lungs. The cough often sounds heavy, and the tongue coating is thin and white. This pattern is common in the early stages of a cold.
When the sputum is yellow and the cough is frequent, with a sore throat, thirst, and a floating rapid pulse, the pattern is Wind-Heat invading the Lungs. The tongue tip is red with a thin yellow coating. A person may feel feverish and prefer cool drinks.
Phlegm-Heat in the Lungs produces a productive cough with thick, yellow, sticky sputum that can be hard to bring up. The chest feels oppressed, and the tongue is red with a greasy yellow coating. The pulse is slippery and rapid. A foul taste or breath may accompany this pattern.
Damp-Phlegm in the Lungs is marked by profuse, white, sticky sputum that is easier to expectorate. The person feels a heavy sensation in the chest, and the tongue has a thick, white, greasy coating. The pulse is slippery. This cough is often worse after eating or in damp weather.
Rebellious Lung Qi is diagnosed when the cough feels like a forceful upward rush, sometimes spasmodic, and is not primarily driven by phlegm or heat. Chest distention and a wiry pulse are common. This pattern may flare with stress or talking, and it often coexists with other imbalances.
Lung Qi Deficiency causes a chronic, weak cough with clear, thin sputum and noticeable shortness of breath. The cough worsens with exertion, and the person feels tired. The tongue is pale with a thin coating, and the pulse is weak. This pattern develops over time and lacks acute signs of infection.

### Wind-Cold invading the Lungs
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/wind-cold-invading-the-lungs)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Cough with thin white watery phlegm, Chills more prominent than fever, Nasal congestion with clear watery discharge, Absence of sweating, Body aches and headache, Heavy or muffled cough sound
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Cold, windy, or drafty weather, Cold or raw foods, Dairy and phlegm-producing foods, Overexertion or heavy exercise, Air conditioning
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warmth and covering up, Hot ginger tea or warm spices, Rest, sleep, or lying down, Steam inhalation, Keeping the neck and chest covered
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Wind-Cold enters through the skin and nose, trapping the Lung’s ability to disperse and descend. The Lung Qi rebels upward, forcing a cough, while the Cold congeals fluids into thin white watery phlegm. The exterior blockage leaves you feeling chilled, achy, and unable to sweat.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: normal or slightly pale body with a thin, white, moist coating. Pulse: floating (fu) and tight (jin).
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Wind-Cold constricts the Lung’s Qi and freezes fluids into phlegm. Warmth and warm drinks relax the exterior and encourage sweating, which releases the trapped pathogen. Rest conserves the body’s defensive Qi so it can fight the invasion, while cold foods and drafts tighten the blockage and worsen the cough.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Keep your neck and chest warmly covered, and avoid cold or raw foods. Sip warm ginger tea with scallion to help sweat out the pathogen. Rest well and consider gentle steam inhalation to soothe the throat and loosen phlegm.

### Wind-Heat invading the Lungs
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/wind-heat-invading-the-lungs)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Cough with thick yellow phlegm, Sore or painful throat, Fever stronger than the chills, Thirst and dry mouth
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Spicy, fried, or greasy foods, Hot, stuffy rooms, Exposure to wind or drafts, Overwork, exhaustion, or lack of rest
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Rest and calm environment, Cool, fresh air or well-ventilated room, Warm drinks, soups, or herbal tea, Light, non-greasy, or easily digestible meals
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Wind and Heat are external pathogens that enter through the nose and mouth, first attacking the body's exterior. The Lungs are the most delicate organ and are easily injured by Heat. When Wind-Heat invades, it disrupts the Lungs' natural rhythm of dispersing and descending Qi, causing Lung Qi to rebel upward as a cough. The Heat dries up fluids, leading to thick yellow phlegm, a sore throat, and thirst.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red tip, thin yellow coating, body slightly red. Pulse: floating (Fu) and rapid (Shu).
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Wind-Heat is an external attack that traps heat in the Lungs and skin. Spicy, fried, or greasy foods create more internal heat, while hot, stuffy rooms prevent the heat from clearing. Wind and drafts can push the pathogen deeper. Rest and cooling drinks help the body release the exterior and clear heat, allowing Lung Qi to descend again. Light, moistening foods support the Lungs without adding fuel to the fire.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat light, cooling foods like pear, loquat, and steamed vegetables. Sip warm water or mint tea throughout the day. Avoid spicy, fried, and greasy foods, which add internal heat. Rest in a well-ventilated but not drafty room. Protect your throat from dry or cold air with a scarf.

### Rebellious Lung Qi
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/rebellious-lung-qi)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Forceful, persistent cough, Sensation of Qi rushing upward in the chest, Difficulty breathing when lying flat, Wheezing or labored breathing, Raised shoulders or nasal flaring during breathing
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Lying flat, Emotional stress, anger, or frustration, Overeating or rich foods, Exposure to cold wind
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Sitting upright, Slow, deep breathing exercises, Warm, light meals, Emotional calm
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  The Lungs are meant to send Qi downward with each breath. When that downward movement fails, Qi rebels and rushes upward into the throat and chest. This counterflow directly triggers a forceful, hacking cough and a tight, full sensation in the chest.
Any number of things can provoke this rebellion - emotional stress that stirs up the Liver, phlegm blocking the airways, or even a heavy meal that disturbs the Stomach. But the common thread is always Lung Qi moving the wrong way, which is why the cough feels so urgent and hard to suppress.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: often normal or light red body with a thin white coat; may become greasy if Phlegm is present or yellow if Heat is involved. Pulse: wiry (弦 xián) and particularly full in the right cun position; may be slippery (滑 huá) if Phlegm contributes.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Lying flat physically impedes the descent of Lung Qi, so sitting up lets gravity help Qi move downward. Emotional stress, especially anger, makes Liver Qi surge upward and invade the Lungs, directly provoking rebellious cough. Heavy meals burden the Stomach and can cause Stomach Qi to rebel, which then disrupts the Lungs. Cold constricts and stagnates Qi, making it harder for the Lungs to open and descend. Gentle breathing and calm emotions retrain the Qi to follow its natural downward path.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Avoid heavy, greasy, or dairy-rich meals that create phlegm and clog the chest. Eat smaller portions and chew well so the Stomach doesn't rebel upward and disturb the Lungs. Gentle, slow breathing exercises can retrain the Qi to move downward. Keep emotional stress in check, as frustration and anger easily stir Liver Qi that can invade the Lungs.

### Phlegm-Heat in the Lungs
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/phlegm-heat-in-the-lungs)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Thick yellow or green sticky sputum, Chest oppression and fullness, Fever or sensation of body heat, Thirst with desire to drink, Restlessness and irritability, Sputum may have a foul or fishy smell
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Spicy, fried, or greasy foods, Dairy and phlegm-producing foods, Emotional stress, anger, or frustration, Overwork, exhaustion, or lack of rest, Hot and humid weather
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Light, cooling foods (pear, radish), Staying hydrated, Rest and calm environment, Cool, fresh air or well-ventilated room
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:

Emotional stress stirs up internal Heat, while overwork depletes Qi and allows Phlegm to accumulate. ### Damp-Phlegm in the Lungs
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/damp-phlegm-in-the-lungs)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Copious white sticky phlegm that is easy to expectorate, Heavy, turbid cough sound, Feeling of fullness or heaviness in the chest, Poor appetite, bloating, and loose stools, Thick white greasy tongue coating, Fatigue and body heaviness
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Damp or rainy weather, Spicy, fried, or greasy foods, Dairy and phlegm-producing foods, Cold or raw foods, Sedentary lifestyle
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warm, dry weather, Hot ginger tea or warm spices, Gentle exercise, Avoiding dairy and sweets, Light, non-greasy, or easily digestible meals
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When the Spleen is weak, it fails to transform fluids properly, and internal Dampness builds up. Over time this Dampness thickens into Phlegm, which rises and settles in the Lungs. The Phlegm blocks the normal downward flow of Lung Qi, forcing the Qi to rebel upward as a cough that brings up lots of white sticky phlegm. The chest feels heavy and congested because Phlegm obstructs the free movement of Qi in the chest.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale, swollen, with teeth marks and a thick white greasy coating. Pulse: slippery (滑 huá) like beads rolling; may also feel soggy (濡 rú).
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Damp-Phlegm is essentially a buildup of fluid and turbidity due to a weak Spleen. Damp weather and heavy, greasy foods add more Dampness to the body, thickening the Phlegm and making the cough worse. Warm, dry conditions and light, cooked foods support the Spleen and help dry out excess moisture. Ginger and gentle exercise move Qi, which is essential for transforming and draining Dampness.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Avoid damp-producing foods like dairy, greasy or fried dishes, sweets, and raw cold foods. Eat warm, cooked meals such as congee with ginger and lightly spiced vegetables. Gentle daily movement like walking or tai chi helps move Qi and dry internal Dampness. Keep your living space dry and well-ventilated.

### Lung Qi Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/lung-qi-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Weak, low-force cough, Shortness of breath worsened by exertion, Spontaneous daytime sweating, Clear, thin, watery phlegm, Sensitivity to wind and cold, catching colds easily, Fatigue and a low, reluctant voice, Pale or bright white complexion
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Overexertion or heavy exercise, Cold, windy, or drafty weather, Cold or raw foods, Prolonged talking or singing, Emotional stress, anger, or frustration
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Rest, sleep, or lying down, Warm drinks, soups, or herbal tea, Slow, deep breathing exercises, Keeping the chest and neck covered
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  The Lungs govern Qi and are responsible for descending it. When Lung Qi is chronically weak, it lacks the strength to push downward properly, so Qi rebels upward and causes a feeble, lingering cough. At the same time, the weakened Lung Qi fails to consolidate the body's outer defenses, leaving you vulnerable to drafts, frequent colds, and spontaneous sweating. Because the Qi is too weak to transform and expel fluids efficiently, any phlegm tends to be thin, clear, and watery.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale and tender, possibly with teeth marks, with a thin white coating. Pulse: empty and weak, especially in the right Cun (Lung) position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Lung Qi is already depleted, so any exertion-physical or vocal-further drains it, making the cough worse. Cold and raw foods injure the Spleen, which is the source of Lung Qi, while drafts directly invade the weakened exterior. Rest, warmth, and gentle breathing conserve and rebuild Qi, allowing the Lungs to regain their descending rhythm.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat warm, cooked foods like congee with a little ginger and rice, and avoid raw, cold, or icy items that drain digestive fire. Gentle movement such as walking or Tai Chi is helpful, but stop before you feel breathless. Practice slow, deep breathing to train the Lungs to descend Qi. Keep your neck and upper back covered in windy weather, and prioritize enough rest to rebuild Qi.

## คำถามที่พบบ่อย (Frequently Asked Questions)
### Can TCM help with a cough that won't go away after a cold?
Yes, this is a common situation where TCM excels. A post-viral cough often lingers because the pathogen wasn't fully expelled, leaving behind phlegm or heat that irritates the Lungs. TCM identifies the remaining pattern - often Phlegm-Heat or Damp-Phlegm - and uses herbs and acupuncture to clear it. Many patients find their stubborn cough clears within 1-2 weeks of targeted treatment.

### Is it safe to take Chinese herbs with my cough medicine?
Generally yes, but with precautions. Over-the-counter cough syrups and expectorants are usually compatible if spaced at least 1-2 hours apart from herbal doses. If you're on prescription medications like inhalers or reflux drugs, never stop them abruptly; work with your doctor to adjust as you improve. Always bring a full list of your medications to your TCM consultation so your practitioner can check for interactions.

### How does acupuncture stop a cough?
Acupuncture doesn't just numb the throat; it regulates the Lung's Qi flow and calms the cough reflex by balancing the nervous system. Points like Lieque LU-7 and Feishu BL-13 directly restore the Lung's descending function, while points like Fenglong ST-40 clear phlegm. Many patients feel their throat relax and chest loosen during or right after the first session, with cumulative effects reducing cough frequency over subsequent treatments.

### What foods should I avoid when I have a cough?
Dairy products, cold and raw foods, and greasy or fried items are the top offenders - they tend to generate phlegm and dampness. Spicy foods may temporarily thin mucus but can also irritate the throat if there's heat. Instead, favor warm, cooked foods like soups, congees, and steamed vegetables. Pears, radishes, and ginger tea are particularly helpful for soothing the Lungs.

### My cough is worse at night - what does that mean in TCM?
A cough that worsens when you lie down often indicates phlegm or fluid accumulation in the Lungs, or a failure of the Kidney to grasp the Qi. It could also be a sign of Rebellious Lung Qi. The specific pattern depends on other symptoms, but TCM can address it with herbs that descend Qi and dry phlegm. Acupuncture and postural advice (like sleeping slightly elevated) can bring immediate relief.

### Can TCM treat a chronic cough without drugs?
Yes, many chronic coughs respond well to herbs and acupuncture alone, especially when the root is a pattern like Lung Qi Deficiency or Damp-Phlegm that conventional medicine may not target. The goal is to rebuild the body's own ability to keep the airways clear, rather than just suppressing the cough. Some patients are able to reduce or discontinue their conventional medications under medical supervision after a few months of TCM treatment.

## การวิจัยทางวิทยาศาสตร์สมัยใหม่ (Modern Scientific Research)
1. **Chinese medicinal herbs for acute bronchitis** ([ลิงก์](https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD004560.pub4))
   *วารสารวิชาการ: Wu T, Yang X, Zeng X, Poole P. Chinese medicinal herbs for acute bronchitis. Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews 2012, Issue 2. Art. No.: CD004560.*
   A Cochrane systematic review evaluating the efficacy and safety of Chinese herbal medicine for acute bronchitis. It included 17 trials and found that some herbal preparations may improve symptoms and shorten the course of illness, but the evidence was limited by poor methodological quality.

2. **Acupuncture for chronic cough: a systematic review and meta-analysis of randomized controlled trials** ([ลิงก์](https://doi.org/10.1155/2021/6622729))
   *วารสารวิชาการ: Zhang Y, et al. Acupuncture for chronic cough: a systematic review and meta-analysis of randomized controlled trials. Evidence-Based Complementary and Alternative Medicine. 2021; 2021:6622729.*
   This meta-analysis of 16 RCTs concluded that acupuncture significantly reduced cough severity scores and improved quality of life compared to control interventions, with a favorable safety profile. It supports acupuncture as a viable option for chronic cough management.

3. **Chinese herbal medicine for post-infectious cough: a systematic review of randomized controlled trials** ([ลิงก์](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0254-6272(16)30072-1))
   *วารสารวิชาการ: Liu W, et al. Chinese herbal medicine for post-infectious cough: a systematic review of randomized controlled trials. Journal of Traditional Chinese Medicine. 2016; 36(5):547-556.*
   A review of 23 RCTs found that Chinese herbal medicine, often combined with conventional treatment, improved cough resolution rates and reduced symptom scores compared to conventional treatment alone. The study highlighted the need for higher-quality trials.
