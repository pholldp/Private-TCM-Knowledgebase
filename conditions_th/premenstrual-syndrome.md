## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนปัจจุบัน (Conventional Medicine View)
### คำอธิบาย (Description)
Premenstrual syndrome (PMS) is a collection of physical and emotional symptoms that occur during the luteal phase of the menstrual cycle - typically 5-10 days before menstruation - and resolve shortly after bleeding begins. Common symptoms include bloating, breast tenderness, mood swings, irritability, fatigue, food cravings, and difficulty sleeping. Up to 80% of menstruating women experience some degree of PMS, though severity varies widely.
Diagnosis is clinical, based on symptom tracking over at least two menstrual cycles. In about 3-8% of women, symptoms are severe enough to be classified as premenstrual dysphoric disorder (PMDD), a condition that significantly impairs daily functioning. Conventional treatment focuses on lifestyle modifications, pain relief, hormonal regulation, and, in severe cases, antidepressant medication.

### การรักษาหลัก (Treatments)
Standard Western treatments for PMS include over-the-counter NSAIDs like ibuprofen for physical pain, oral contraceptives to suppress ovulation and stabilize hormonal fluctuations, and selective serotonin reuptake inhibitors (SSRIs) for severe mood symptoms. Diuretics may be used for fluid retention, and dietary supplements like calcium, magnesium, and vitamin B6 are often recommended. Cognitive behavioral therapy (CBT) and stress management techniques are also employed, particularly for PMDD.

## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนจีน (Traditional Chinese Medicine View)
### ความเข้าใจเกี่ยวกับการแพทย์แผนจีน (TCM Understanding)
TCM understands the menstrual cycle as a dynamic wave of Qi and Blood. In the week before your period, all of this energy surges downward toward the uterus to prepare for the flow. If your organ systems are balanced, this shift happens smoothly. But if there's an underlying weakness or blockage - especially in the Liver, Kidneys, or Spleen - the surge can get stuck or create chaos, and premenstrual symptoms erupt.
The Liver is often at the center of PMS because it's responsible for keeping Qi moving freely. Emotional stress, frustration, or unexpressed anger can jam up this flow, leading to the classic bloating, breast tenderness, and mood swings of Liver Qi Stagnation. If that stuck energy persists, it can generate Heat, making irritability explosive and adding headaches or a bitter taste. That's why two women with PMS can feel completely different: one might feel weepy and bloated, while another feels angry and overheated - even though both are rooted in the Liver.
But the Liver doesn't act alone. The Kidneys store the body's deepest Yin and Yang reserves. When Kidney Yin is depleted - often from overwork, late nights, or chronic stress - the Liver isn't properly moistened, and its energy can rise restlessly as anxiety, dizziness, and night sweats. The Spleen, which transforms food into Qi and manages fluids, can also be weak, causing fatigue, water retention, and loose stools. Even the Heart can be involved, leading to premenstrual palpitations and insomnia when Kidney Yin fails to anchor the mind. TCM doesn't see these as separate problems - they are all interconnected imbalances that surface when the body's resources are stretched thin before the period.

### การวินิจฉัยโดยแพทย์แผนจีน (Practitioner Diagnosis)
When premenstrual symptoms are driven by Liver Qi Stagnation, the hallmark is emotional frustration and physical distension. A person feels moody, irritable, and tearful, with pronounced breast tenderness and a bloated sensation in the chest or sides. The tongue is typically pale red with a thin white coating, and the pulse feels wiry and thin - signs that Qi is stuck and not flowing smoothly.
If that stagnation persists and generates Heat, the picture intensifies. Irritability becomes sharper, often accompanied by a flushed face, thirst, and a throbbing headache. The tongue turns red with a thin yellow coat, and the pulse quickens to a wiry, rapid quality. These heat signs distinguish this pattern from simple Liver Qi Stagnation and signal that the body’s internal climate has become overheated.
Kidney and Liver Yin Deficiency presents a very different story. Here the body lacks the cooling, moistening resources to anchor the mind. Premenstrual symptoms include dizziness, night sweats, a dry mouth, scanty menstrual flow, and insomnia. The tongue appears red with little or no coating, and the pulse is thin and rapid - clues that Yin is depleted and cannot restrain the natural pre-period surge of Qi.
Spleen and Kidney Qi Deficiency shifts the focus to water metabolism and energy. Bloating, puffiness, fatigue, and loose stools dominate the days before menstruation. The tongue looks pale and swollen, often with teeth marks on the sides, while the pulse is deep and weak. These signs point to a digestive and vital-energy deficit that leaves the body unable to manage fluids and maintain stamina.

### Liver Qi Stagnation
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/liver-qi-stagnation)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Breast tenderness and swelling, Distension along the ribs, Irritability and mood swings, Frequent sighing
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Stress and frustration, Sedentary lifestyle, Alcohol and spicy foods, Bottling up emotions
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Gentle exercise (walking, yoga), Deep breathing and relaxation, Sour foods (lemon, vinegar), Expressing feelings
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  The Liver is responsible for keeping Qi moving smoothly throughout the body, especially before your period when Qi and blood surge downward to the uterus. Emotional stress, frustration, or unexpressed anger can jam up this flow, causing Qi to stagnate in the chest and abdomen. This stuck Qi creates the classic premenstrual bloating, breast tenderness, and mood swings. Because the Liver channel runs through the breasts and ribs, that's where you feel the pressure most.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: normal or slightly dark body with red or slightly purple sides, thin white coat. Pulse: wiry (弦 xián), especially taut at the left middle position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Stress, frustration, and emotional suppression directly cause Liver Qi to stagnate, so releasing emotions and practicing relaxation are key. Gentle movement encourages the smooth flow of Qi, while a sedentary lifestyle allows it to congeal. Sour foods help the Liver maintain its proper function, while alcohol and spicy foods can generate heat and worsen stagnation.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Move your body gently with walking, yoga, or tai chi to keep Liver Qi flowing. Avoid alcohol, coffee, and spicy foods which can aggravate stagnation. Practice deep breathing and express emotions rather than bottling them up. Sour foods like lemon and vinegar can help the Liver function.

### Liver Qi Stagnation that transforms into Heat
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/liver-qi-stagnation-that-transforms-into-heat)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Explosive anger and irritability, Bitter taste in the mouth, Throbbing or distending pain at the temples, Red face and eyes, Dry mouth and throat, Acid reflux or sour belching, Constipation with dry stools
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Stress and suppressed anger, Spicy, fried, or greasy foods, Alcohol and caffeine, Overwork and lack of sleep, Hot, stuffy environments
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Cooling foods (cucumber, chrysanthemum tea), Gentle exercise (walking, yoga), Deep breathing and relaxation, Avoiding alcohol and spicy meals, Warm compress on lower abdomen
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Normally the Liver keeps Qi flowing smoothly, but ongoing stress and frustration can cause that flow to jam up. When Qi remains stuck for too long, it generates internal Heat-like friction starting a fire. That Heat surges upward, making the emotional tension of PMS much more intense and adding physical signs like a bitter taste, thirst, and a pounding head.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red body, especially red along the sides, with a yellow coating. Pulse: wiry (弦 xián) and rapid (数 shuò), forceful at the left middle position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Emotional stress directly knots Liver Qi, and when that knot persists it ignites heat-so anger and frustration are the core trigger. Heating foods and alcohol add fuel to the fire, making the bitter taste, thirst, and headaches worse. Cooling foods and calming activities soothe the Liver, clear heat, and help Qi flow smoothly again, which is why they bring relief.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Dial down heating foods and drinks-cut back on alcohol, coffee, fried dishes, and spicy curries. Favour cooling choices like cucumber, chrysanthemum tea, mung beans, and lightly steamed greens. A daily walk or gentle yoga helps Qi move and stops it from smouldering into heat. Make space to let feelings out-journaling or talking with a friend prevents the pressure cooker effect.

### Kidney and Liver Yin Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/kidney-and-liver-yin-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Dizziness and ringing in the ears, Night sweats and heat in the palms, soles, and chest, Soreness and weakness of the lower back and knees, Scanty menstrual flow or light periods, Insomnia with vivid dreams, Dry eyes, mouth, and throat, Irritability and mood swings
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Overwork and lack of sleep, Spicy, fried, or greasy foods, Alcohol and caffeine, Emotional stress and anger, Excessive sweating or sauna
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Early bedtime, Cooling, moistening foods, Gentle exercise (walking, yoga), Hydration with water or herbal tea, Stress management
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Kidney Yin is the body's cooling and nourishing foundation. When it runs low, the Liver is not properly moistened, causing its energy to become restless and rise upward. In the premenstrual phase, the body's Yin and blood concentrate in the uterus, leaving the rest of the body even more depleted. This triggers dizziness, night sweats, a dry mouth, and a feeling of heat in the palms and chest, while the Liver's restlessness leads to irritability and poor sleep.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red, thin body with cracks, little or no coating, dry surface. Pulse: fine, rapid, and wiry; weak in the left Chi (Kidney) position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Yin is the body's cooling, moistening substance. Overwork, late nights, and spicy foods consume Yin, worsening the deficiency and allowing heat to rise. Rest, early sleep, and nourishing foods rebuild Yin, while gentle movement prevents stagnation without depleting reserves.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Prioritize cooling, moistening foods like pear, black sesame, goji berries, and spinach. Avoid spicy, fried, and drying foods that consume Yin. Go to bed early and avoid overwork, especially in the second half of the menstrual cycle. Gentle exercise like yoga or walking helps Qi flow without depleting Yin.

### Spleen and Kidney Qi Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/spleen-and-kidney-qi-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Premenstrual bloating and water retention, Loose stools before period, Soreness and weakness of the lower back and knees, Persistent fatigue and low energy, Cold hands and feet
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Overwork and stress, Cold, raw foods, Standing for long periods, Cold damp weather
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warmth and rest, Warm cooked meals, Gentle exercise (walking, yoga), Abdominal warmth
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  In the week before menstruation, the body's Qi and blood shift downward to prepare for the period. When the Spleen and Kidney Qi are already weak, they struggle to manage this shift. The Spleen fails to transform and transport fluids, leading to premenstrual bloating, water retention, and loose stools. At the same time, the Kidneys cannot anchor Qi or govern water, so fatigue, lower back soreness, and frequent urination become pronounced.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale, swollen with teeth marks and a white moist coating. Pulse: deep, weak, and fine, especially weak at the right Guan and both Chi positions.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Spleen and Kidney Qi are easily depleted by overwork, stress, and cold. Raw and cold foods further weaken the Spleen's digestive fire, leading to more bloating and loose stools. Warmth, rest, and gentle movement conserve and nourish Qi, allowing the body to better manage the premenstrual shift in fluids and energy.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat warm, cooked foods like soups, stews, and congees. Avoid raw, cold, and greasy foods that tax the Spleen. Include warming spices like ginger and cinnamon. Gentle daily movement such as walking or yoga helps Qi flow without depleting energy. Prioritize rest and avoid overwork, especially in the week before your period.

### Blood Stagnation
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/blood-stagnation)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Fixed, stabbing pain in lower abdomen or breasts, Dark menstrual blood with large clots, Pain that worsens with pressure, Dark circles under eyes or dusky lips, Sharp, fixed premenstrual headache
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Cold weather or air conditioning, Sedentary lifestyle, Stress and frustration, Cold, raw foods, Overwork and lack of sleep
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warm compress on lower abdomen, Gentle exercise (walking, yoga), Warm cooked meals, Rest, Stress management
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Blood Stagnation in PMS often develops when emotional stress knots Liver Qi, which then fails to push blood through the vessels. Over time, the sluggish blood congeals, obstructing the uterus and channels. This blockage creates fixed, stabbing pain that worsens with pressure and produces dark, clotted menstrual flow.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: purple or dusky body, stasis spots, distended dark sublingual veins, thin white coat. Pulse: choppy (涩 Se) or wiry (弦 Xian).
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Cold causes blood to congeal and stagnate, making pain worse. Warmth and movement invigorate blood circulation, easing stasis. Emotional stress constrains Liver Qi, which then fails to move blood, so calming the mind helps. Rest allows the body to redirect energy toward healing.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Keep warm, especially the lower abdomen and feet. Gentle exercise like walking or yoga helps blood move. Avoid cold drinks and raw foods; instead, enjoy warm, cooked meals with spices like ginger and turmeric. Manage stress with deep breathing or meditation.

### Disharmony between Heart and Kidneys
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/disharmony-between-heart-and-kidneys)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Palpitations or a racing heart before period, Insomnia with difficulty falling asleep, Vivid, disturbing dreams, Mental restlessness and irritability, Heat in the palms, soles, and chest (five-palm heat), Night sweats, Soreness and weakness of the lower back and knees, Dry mouth and throat
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Overwork and lack of sleep, Alcohol and spicy foods, Emotional stress and worry, Excessive talking or mental strain
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Early bedtime, Cooling, moistening foods, Gentle meditation or yin yoga, Quiet, dim environment
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  In the premenstrual phase, the body naturally directs Yin, blood, and essence toward the uterus. When Kidney Yin is already depleted, this shift leaves the Heart without the cooling, grounding support it needs. The result is a flare of empty Heat in the Heart, much like a pot boiling dry on a stove.
This Heart Fire rising produces the palpitations, anxiety, and restless sleep that feel so distinct in the days before menstruation. Meanwhile, the underlying Kidney deficiency shows up as a deep, dragging soreness in the lower back and knees.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red body, thin with a redder tip, little or no coating, sometimes a midline crack. Pulse: fine (thin) and rapid, weaker at the rear (Kidney) position and relatively more perceptible at the front (Heart) position on the left wrist.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Overwork, late nights, and emotional strain consume Kidney Yin and stir up empty Heat in the Heart, which is exactly the mechanism behind this pattern. Rest, cooling foods, and calming practices replenish Yin and gently draw the Heat back down, restoring the balance between the Kidneys and Heart.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Prioritize rest and early nights in the week before your period to protect Yin. Eat cooling, moistening foods like pear, black sesame, and goji berries, and avoid spicy dishes, alcohol, and excessive caffeine, which fan the flames of Heart Fire. Gentle practices like yin yoga or meditation help settle the mind without exhausting the body.

## คำถามที่พบบ่อย (Frequently Asked Questions)
### How does TCM treat PMS differently from Western medicine?
Instead of treating PMS as a single condition with a one-size-fits-all medication, TCM identifies the specific pattern of imbalance behind your symptoms - whether it's stagnant Liver Qi, depleted Kidney Yin, or something else - and tailors treatment to correct that root cause. Acupuncture and herbs work to restore your body's natural rhythm, so symptoms resolve from the inside out rather than being temporarily suppressed.

### How long does it take to see results with TCM for PMS?
Many women notice a meaningful difference in their first 1-2 menstrual cycles of consistent treatment. Excess patterns like Liver Qi Stagnation often respond quickly, sometimes within a few weeks. Deficiency patterns that require rebuilding Yin or Qi may take 3-6 months to show lasting change. The goal is long-term balance, so treatment typically continues for several months even after symptoms improve to solidify the results.

### Can TCM help with severe PMS or PMDD?
Yes, TCM can be very effective for severe premenstrual symptoms, including PMDD. The intense mood swings, anger, and depression of PMDD often correspond to patterns like Liver Qi Stagnation transforming into Heat or Disharmony between Heart and Kidneys. Herbal formulas and acupuncture can calm the mind, clear Heat, and anchor the Shen, often reducing the need for psychiatric medication. However, severe mood symptoms should always be monitored by a healthcare professional, and TCM should be integrated with appropriate mental health support.

### Will I need to change my diet?
Yes, diet plays an important role in TCM treatment for PMS. Generally, you'll be advised to reduce cold, raw foods, excessive sugar, alcohol, and caffeine, all of which can strain the Spleen and Liver. Instead, emphasize warm, cooked meals, whole grains, leafy greens, and adequate hydration. Your practitioner may give you more specific guidance based on your pattern - for example, cooling foods if you have Heat signs, or nourishing stews if you're deficient.

### Can I continue taking my birth control pills or antidepressants?
Yes, you can usually continue these medications while receiving TCM treatment. It's crucial to inform both your TCM practitioner and your prescribing doctor about all treatments you're using. Some herbs may interact with SSRIs or hormonal contraceptives, so your herbal formula will be carefully selected to avoid conflict. Never stop prescribed medications abruptly; if your symptoms improve, work with your doctor to taper them safely.

### Does TCM treatment last, or will my symptoms come back?
Because TCM aims to correct the underlying imbalance rather than just mask symptoms, many women experience long-term relief and don't need ongoing treatment. However, PMS can recur if lifestyle factors - like chronic stress, poor diet, or overwork - recreate the same pattern. Periodic maintenance sessions or seasonal herbal adjustments can help keep you balanced, especially during particularly demanding phases of life.

### Is acupuncture painful?
Acupuncture needles are extremely thin - much finer than a typical injection needle - and most people feel only a brief, mild sensation upon insertion, often described as a dull ache or tingling. Once the needles are in place, many patients feel deeply relaxed, and some even fall asleep during treatment. Any discomfort is typically minimal and short-lived.

## การวิจัยทางวิทยาศาสตร์สมัยใหม่ (Modern Scientific Research)
1. **Effects and treatment methods of acupuncture and herbal medicine for premenstrual syndrome/premenstrual dysphoric disorder: systematic review** ([ลิงก์](https://doi.org/10.1186/1472-6882-14-11))
   *วารสารวิชาการ: Jang SH, Kim DI, Choi MS. Effects and treatment methods of acupuncture and herbal medicine for premenstrual syndrome/premenstrual dysphoric disorder: systematic review. BMC Complement Altern Med. 2014;14:11.*
   This systematic review evaluated 19 studies (RCTs and quasi-RCTs) on acupuncture and herbal medicine for PMS/PMDD. The majority of herbal medicine studies used Xiao Yao San or its variants, showing significant improvement in symptom scores. Acupuncture studies also reported positive effects, but the overall evidence was limited by small sample sizes and risk of bias. The authors concluded that TCM interventions appear effective and safe, but more rigorous trials are needed.

2. **A controlled trial of Chinese herbal medicine for premenstrual syndrome** ([ลิงก์](https://doi.org/10.1080/01674820801948658))
   *วารสารวิชาการ: Chou PB, Morse CA, Xu H. A controlled trial of Chinese herbal medicine for premenstrual syndrome. J Psychosom Obstet Gynaecol. 2008;29(3):185-92.*
   In this double-blind, placebo-controlled trial, 78 women with PMS were randomised to receive a standardised Chinese herbal formula (based on Xiao Yao San) or placebo for three menstrual cycles. The herbal group showed significantly greater reductions in total PMS symptom scores, particularly for emotional symptoms like irritability and depression. The study provided early rigorous evidence supporting Chinese herbal medicine for PMS.
