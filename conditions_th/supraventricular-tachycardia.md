## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนปัจจุบัน (Conventional Medicine View)
### คำอธิบาย (Description)
Supraventricular tachycardia is an abnormally fast heart rhythm originating above the ventricles. It often feels like a sudden, pounding heartbeat that can start and stop without warning, sometimes accompanied by dizziness, lightheadedness, shortness of breath, or chest discomfort. Episodes may last seconds to hours.
Diagnosis is usually confirmed with an electrocardiogram (ECG) during an episode, or with a Holter monitor for intermittent events. In many cases, the heart is structurally normal, but an extra electrical pathway or a trigger can set off the rapid rhythm.

### การรักษาหลัก (Treatments)
Acute episodes may be stopped with vagal maneuvers (like bearing down) or medications such as adenosine. Long-term management often includes beta-blockers or calcium channel blockers to prevent recurrences. For frequent or disruptive SVT, catheter ablation - a procedure that destroys the tiny area of heart tissue causing the abnormal circuit - can offer a more permanent solution.

## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนจีน (Traditional Chinese Medicine View)
### ความเข้าใจเกี่ยวกับการแพทย์แผนจีน (TCM Understanding)
In TCM, the Heart is the emperor of the body, housing the Shen - the spirit that governs consciousness, sleep, and emotional calm. A racing, pounding heartbeat is a sign that the Shen is unsettled. This can happen when the Heart lacks proper nourishment, when heat disturbs it, or when something physically obstructs its function.
Deficiency patterns are common. Heart Yin is the cooling, moistening substance that keeps the mind settled. When Yin runs low from overwork, stress, or late nights, a subtle internal heat stirs, like a pot simmering dry. This empty heat rises, making the heart race, especially at rest or at night. Similarly, when the Spleen fails to produce enough Blood, the Heart is undernourished - like a person going hungry - and may flutter or pound with any effort, accompanied by fatigue and pale complexion.

### การวินิจฉัยโดยแพทย์แผนจีน (Practitioner Diagnosis)
A practitioner begins by asking what else you feel besides the racing heart. When palpitations flare with anxiety, restless sleep, night sweats, or a dry mouth, Heart Yin Deficiency is the likely culprit. The tongue appears red with little or no coating, and the pulse is thin and rapid. The practitioner will ask about stress and whether you feel a wave of heat, because Yin deficiency allows an empty fire to rise and disturb the mind.
If the palpitations are accompanied by fatigue, poor appetite, and a washed‑out complexion, the picture points toward Heart and Spleen Deficiency. The tongue is often pale with a thin white coat, and the pulse feels weak or thready, signaling that the body is not making enough Qi and Blood to anchor the Heart.
If the racing heart comes with cold hands and feet, a pale face, and a sense of tightness in the chest, Heart Yang Deficiency is suspected. Here the pulse is deep, slow, and weak, and the tongue is pale with a white coat. The key clue is that warmth and rest bring relief, while cold exposure makes the pounding worse.
When palpitations are joined by swelling in the ankles, a heavy sensation in the chest, and scanty urination, the pattern shifts to Water Qi intimidating the Heart. The tongue looks puffy and pale with a slippery coat, and the pulse feels deep and slippery. The practitioner will check for edema and ask about fluid intake, because retained fluids are oppressing the heart.
Palpitations that arrive with a sharp, stabbing chest pain that stays in one spot suggest Heart Blood Stagnation. The lips and tongue may have a purplish hue or dark spots, and the pulse is choppy or wiry. The practitioner will ask whether the discomfort feels fixed and piercing, because blood stasis blocks the heart vessels.
In older adults, palpitations accompanied by dizziness, tinnitus, and a sore lower back often point to Kidney and Liver Yin Deficiency. The tongue is red with scant coating, and the pulse is thin and rapid. The practitioner will inquire about hearing changes and backaches, since the root of nourishment for the Heart has dried up from the deeper organ systems.

## กลุ่มอาการการแพทย์แผนจีนและการรักษา (TCM Patterns and Treatment)

### Heart Yin Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/heart-yin-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Palpitations worse at rest or at night, Night sweats and five-palm heat (heat in palms, soles, chest), Restless sleep with vivid or disturbing dreams, Dry mouth and throat, especially at night, Anxiety and a restless, easily startled feeling
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Late nights and insufficient sleep, Emotional stress and anxiety, Spicy, fried, or heating foods, Excessive talking or mental strain, Hot weather or heat exposure
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Rest and adequate sleep, Cooling, moistening foods, Deep breathing or meditation, Cool, quiet environment
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Heart Yin is the cooling, moistening substance that keeps the mind settled and the heartbeat steady. When it runs low from overwork, stress, or late nights, a subtle internal heat stirs, like a pot simmering dry. This empty heat rises to disturb the mind, making the heart race and pound, especially when you are trying to rest. Because Yin should naturally anchor Yang at night, the palpitations often feel worse when you lie down, and sleep becomes shallow and filled with vivid dreams.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red body, especially at the tip, with little or no coating, a dry surface, and often a midline crack. Pulse: fine (thready) and rapid, weak at the deep level, particularly at the left cun (Heart) position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Heart Yin is like the cooling water that keeps the heart's fire steady. Overwork, late nights, and emotional stress drain that water, allowing empty heat to rise and disturb the mind - hence the palpitations and restlessness. Rest, sleep, and cooling foods replenish Yin, while meditation stills the mind, reducing the internal friction that generates heat. Spicy and stimulating things add fuel to the fire, so avoiding them helps the heart settle back into a calm, steady rhythm.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Go to bed by 10:30 PM whenever possible - the hours before midnight are when Yin restores itself. Eat cooling, moistening foods like pear, lily bulb, black sesame, and congee with goji berries. Avoid coffee, alcohol, spicy foods, and late-night screen time, all of which fan empty heat. Gentle practices like restorative yoga, meditation, or slow walks help settle the mind without depleting Yin.

### Heart and Spleen Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/heart-and-spleen-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Palpitations with a sense of emptiness or fluttering, Fatigue that worsens with mental or physical effort, Poor appetite, bloating, and loose stools, Insomnia with vivid dreams and difficulty falling asleep, Pale, sallow complexion, Anxiety and feeling easily startled
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Worry and overthinking, Skipping meals or irregular eating, Raw, cold, or greasy foods, Late nights and insufficient sleep
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Rest and adequate sleep, Warm, cooked meals, Gentle movement, Stress reduction and calming activities
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Your Spleen is responsible for turning food into Qi and Blood, which then nourish the Heart and keep its rhythm steady. When digestion is weak and the Spleen is depleted, Blood production falls short, leaving the Heart undernourished and restless. This lack of solid anchoring causes the fluttering, empty-feeling palpitations of SVT, often accompanied by fatigue and poor appetite.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale, tender body with teeth marks and a thin white coating. Pulse: fine (thin) and weak, especially in the left cun (Heart) and right guan (Spleen) positions.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Worry and mental strain directly damage Spleen Qi, weakening its ability to produce the Blood that nourishes the Heart. Cold and raw foods douse the digestive fire, further depleting Qi and Blood, while irregular eating starves the Heart of its steady supply. Rest and warm, nourishing meals rebuild Spleen strength, allowing Blood to gradually fill and anchor the Heart, which quiets the palpitations.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Prioritize warm, cooked, easily digestible foods like congee, soups, and stews. Add blood-nourishing ingredients such as red dates, goji berries, and dark leafy greens. Avoid cold, raw, and greasy foods that tax the Spleen. Protect your energy by resting when tired and keeping a regular meal schedule; gentle movement like walking or tai chi helps Qi flow without draining it.

### Heart Yang Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/heart-yang-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Palpitations worse with exertion, Cold hands and feet, Bright pale or white face, Chest stuffiness or mild chest pain, Fatigue and low energy, Spontaneous sweating, Mental listlessness
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Cold weather or exposure, Overexertion, Emotional stress and fear, Raw, cold, or iced foods
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warmth, Rest, Warm, cooked meals, Gentle movement
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Heart Yang is the body's warming fire that pushes blood through the vessels. When that fire dims, the Heart lacks the strength to pump steadily, so it races or pounds especially with any effort. The cold hands, pale face, and deep fatigue all flow from the same lack of warmth and drive.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale, puffy, and tender with teeth marks and a white slippery coating. Pulse: deep, weak, especially at the left Cun position; may be knotted and slow.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Heart Yang is like a small fire that must be conserved. Cold and overexertion demand more warmth and energy than the body can supply, so the Heart flutters and struggles. Warmth, rest, and warm nourishment protect and gently rebuild that fire, steadying the rhythm.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat warm, cooked foods like soups and stews; avoid raw, cold, and iced items that chill the body. Gentle daily movement such as walking or tai chi helps keep Yang flowing without draining it. Keep the chest and feet warm, and protect yourself from cold drafts.

### Water Qi intimidating the Heart
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/water-qi-intimidating-the-heart)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Palpitations worse at night or when lying down, Sensation of something rushing upward from the stomach to the chest, Feeling of fullness and stuffiness in the chest and upper abdomen, Cold hands and feet, Swelling of the lower legs or whole body, Reduced urination, Nausea or vomiting of thin, watery saliva, Shortness of breath, inability to lie flat
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Cold or damp weather, Lying flat, Excessive fluid intake, Raw, cold, or greasy foods, Overexertion
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warmth, Sitting or standing upright, Urination, Gentle movement, Rest
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When the body's Yang energy, especially in the Spleen and Kidneys, is too weak to transform and move fluids, water accumulates internally. This stagnant water can surge upward as a pathological "Water Qi" that oppresses the Heart, much like a rising flood pressing against the chest. The Heart struggles against this pressure, leading to the pounding, rapid beats of supraventricular tachycardia, which often worsens at night or when lying down because the fluid shifts and presses more directly on the organ.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale, swollen, tender body with teeth marks and a white, slippery, water-slick coating. Pulse: wiry and slippery (xian hua) or deep and fine (chen xi).
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Cold and dampness further weaken the body's Yang, making it harder to transform fluids and allowing water to accumulate. Lying flat lets this pathological water shift upward, directly pressing on the Heart and triggering palpitations. Warmth, upright posture, and gentle movement help Yang circulate and keep fluids moving downward. Urination is the most direct route to drain the excess water, relieving the oppressive pressure on the Heart.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Keep the body warm, especially the lower back and feet, to support waning Yang. Avoid raw, cold, and heavy foods, and limit excessive fluid intake that can overwhelm a weak digestive fire. Gentle, regular movement like walking helps circulate Qi and fluids, but avoid lying flat if it triggers breathlessness; prop yourself up with pillows instead.

### Kidney and Liver Yin Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/kidney-and-liver-yin-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Palpitations with dizziness and tinnitus, Night sweats and heat in palms, soles, and chest, Soreness and weakness of the lower back and knees, Dry eyes and blurred vision, Insomnia with vivid dreams
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Overwork and stress, Spicy, fried, or heating foods, Alcohol and caffeine, Late nights and insufficient sleep, Hot weather or heat exposure
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Rest and adequate sleep, Cool, quiet environment, Cooling, moistening foods, Gentle movement
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When the Kidney and Liver Yin reserves run low, the body's cooling, nourishing fluids become depleted. This allows deficiency heat to rise and disturb the Heart, which relies on Yin to anchor the spirit (Shen). The result is palpitations that often come with dizziness, tinnitus, dry eyes, and night sweats - all signs that deep Yin is struggling to contain the fire.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red body, thin with cracks, little or no coating, dry surface. Pulse: fine, rapid, and wiry; left Chi position weak.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Overwork, stress, and late nights consume Yin and generate deficiency heat, which further disturbs the Heart. Spicy, fried, and drying foods, as well as alcohol and caffeine, add internal heat that depletes fluids. Rest, a cool environment, and moistening foods help replenish Yin and calm the spirit, allowing the Heart to settle.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Prioritize rest and avoid overwork or late nights, which deplete Yin further. Eat moistening, cooling foods such as pears, black sesame, walnuts, tofu, and goji berries. Avoid spicy, fried, or drying foods, as well as alcohol and caffeine. Gentle practices like tai chi or restorative yoga help preserve Yin without overheating.

## คำถามที่พบบ่อย (Frequently Asked Questions)
### How does TCM explain why my heart suddenly races?
TCM views SVT as a disturbance of the Heart's Shen (spirit) and the normal flow of Qi and Blood. The specific cause depends on your pattern - it could be a lack of cooling Yin allowing heat to agitate the Heart, a deficiency of Qi and Blood failing to anchor the heartbeat, or stagnant fluids or Blood obstructing the Heart's rhythm. Unlike the conventional view of a random electrical short-circuit, TCM sees the racing heart as a meaningful symptom of an underlying imbalance that can be corrected.

### Can acupuncture stop an SVT episode while it's happening?
Acupuncture can be very effective at calming an acute episode by sedating the Heart and regulating the nervous system. Points like Neiguan PC-6 and Shenmen HT-7 are often used to slow the heart rate and soothe palpitations. However, if you have a severe or prolonged episode, especially with chest pain or fainting, you should seek emergency medical care rather than relying solely on acupuncture. For non-emergency episodes, many patients learn to use acupressure on these same points to manage symptoms at home.

### Will I need to take Chinese herbs forever?
No. Herbal treatment for SVT is typically time-limited. In excess patterns where the goal is to clear stagnation or resolve fluids, herbs are taken for a few months until the obstruction is gone. In deficiency patterns, herbs may be needed for 3-6 months to rebuild Yin or Yang, after which many patients can stop or switch to a maintenance formula taken only occasionally. The aim is always to restore the body's own balance so that the heart rhythm remains stable without ongoing treatment.

### Is it safe to combine TCM with my beta-blocker or other heart medication?
In most cases, yes - TCM and conventional heart medications can work together safely. However, certain herbs can influence heart rate or blood pressure, so it's essential that both your cardiologist and TCM practitioner know exactly what you are taking. Never stop or adjust your prescribed medication without medical guidance. A qualified TCM practitioner will choose herbs and acupuncture points that complement your medication, often helping to reduce side effects and improve overall well-being.

### Can TCM help if my SVT is triggered by anxiety?
Absolutely. TCM sees a strong connection between the Heart and the mind (Shen). Anxiety, stress, and emotional turmoil are major triggers for SVT, especially in patterns like Heart Yin Deficiency or Heart and Spleen Deficiency. Treatment focuses on calming the Shen, nourishing the Heart, and regulating the Liver Qi that often underlies stress. Many patients find that as their anxiety lessens, their palpitations become less frequent and less intense.

### What foods should I avoid to help calm my heart?
Generally, avoid stimulants like caffeine, alcohol, and spicy foods, which can aggravate internal heat and stir the Heart. Cold, raw foods can weaken the Spleen and deplete Qi and Blood, making palpitations worse in deficiency patterns. Heavy, greasy, or sweet foods can create dampness and phlegm, which may obstruct the Heart. Focus on warm, cooked meals and include calming foods like jujube dates, longan fruit, and lily bulb. For specific dietary advice tailored to your pattern, consult your TCM practitioner.

### How soon will I feel a difference with acupuncture and herbs?
Many patients notice a reduction in the frequency and severity of palpitations within the first 2-4 weeks of treatment. Acupuncture can sometimes provide immediate calming during a session. However, lasting change requires addressing the root pattern, which can take several months. Consistency with herbs and regular sessions yields the best results.

### Is TCM safe for SVT during pregnancy?
TCM can be a gentle option during pregnancy, but it must be managed by a practitioner experienced in prenatal care. Many herbs are contraindicated in pregnancy, and acupuncture points must be carefully chosen. Always inform your TCM practitioner if you are pregnant or trying to conceive.
