## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนปัจจุบัน (Conventional Medicine View)
### คำอธิบาย (Description)
In Western medicine, intestinal adhesions are fibrous bands of scar tissue that form between loops of bowel or between the bowel and the abdominal wall, typically after abdominal or pelvic surgery. They can also result from infection, endometriosis, or radiation therapy.
Many adhesions cause no symptoms, but when they do, patients may experience chronic abdominal pain, bloating, nausea, or changes in bowel habits. In severe cases, adhesions can twist or pull on the intestine, leading to a bowel obstruction - a medical emergency. Diagnosis is often challenging because adhesions don't show up on standard X-rays or CT scans; they are usually identified during surgery or inferred from symptoms and history.

### การรักษาหลัก (Treatments)
Conventional treatment for symptomatic intestinal adhesions is limited. For mild symptoms, dietary modifications, pain medications, and anti-nausea drugs may be recommended. When adhesions cause recurrent obstruction, surgery (adhesiolysis) to cut the scar tissue is the main option, but this carries the risk of creating new adhesions. There are no medications that dissolve existing adhesions, and prevention strategies like adhesion barriers during surgery are not always effective.

## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนจีน (Traditional Chinese Medicine View)
### ความเข้าใจเกี่ยวกับการแพทย์แผนจีน (TCM Understanding)
In TCM, abdominal surgery is seen as a major trauma that disrupts the flow of Qi and Blood in the abdomen. The immediate result is often Qi and Blood stagnation - a kind of internal bruising where the body’s energy and blood become stuck, causing fixed, stabbing pain and bloating. Over time, this stagnation can prevent the intestines from moving properly, leading to the symptoms of adhesions.
But the story doesn’t end there. The body’s digestive energy, governed by the Spleen, is frequently weakened by surgery and prolonged illness. When the Spleen is weak, it fails to transform fluids, and Dampness accumulates - a heavy, sluggish obstruction that adds to the discomfort. Emotional stress or frustration can further complicate the picture by causing the Liver to attack the Spleen, creating a mix of bloating, loose stools, and mood-related flare-ups.
That’s why a single Western diagnosis of intestinal adhesions can correspond to several different TCM patterns, each requiring a unique treatment strategy. A patient whose pain feels cold and eases with warmth is treated very differently from one with a bitter taste and yellow tongue coating. By identifying the dominant pattern - whether it’s Blood stasis, Spleen deficiency, Damp-Heat, or Cold - a TCM practitioner can target the root cause, not just the scar tissue.

### การวินิจฉัยโดยแพทย์แผนจีน (Practitioner Diagnosis)
A TCM practitioner begins by asking about the nature of the abdominal pain - is it sharp and fixed, or dull and shifting? They also explore what makes it better or worse, your appetite, bowel habits, and emotional state. Since adhesions often follow surgery, they will ask about your surgical history and recovery. The tongue and pulse are then examined to confirm which pattern is dominant.
Two patterns that share fixed, stabbing pain are Qi And Blood Stagnation and Blood Stagnation in the Lower Burner caused by Cold. The key difference is temperature.
In the Cold pattern, the pain feels cold and is noticeably relieved by warmth, like a hot water bottle. The tongue is pale and the pulse may feel deep and tight.
In Qi And Blood Stagnation, the pain is less affected by temperature and more associated with bloating and distension; the tongue often shows purplish spots and the pulse feels wiry or choppy.
When digestive sluggishness is the main complaint, the practitioner distinguishes between Spleen Deficiency with Dampness and Damp-Heat in the Large Intestine. Both involve loose or sticky stools and a feeling of heaviness.
In Spleen Deficiency with Dampness, fatigue and poor appetite are prominent, the tongue is pale with a white greasy coating, and the pulse is weak.
If Damp-Heat is present, there will be heat signs: a bitter taste in the mouth, stools that are sticky with a sense of incomplete emptying, a red tongue with a yellow greasy coating, and a rapid, slippery pulse.
If emotional stress plays a big role - for instance, abdominal pain that flares with worry or frustration, accompanied by flank distension and alternating bowel habits - the practitioner suspects Obstruction Of the Spleen By Dampness with Liver Qi Stagnation. This pattern arises when constrained Liver Qi attacks the Spleen, worsening Dampness. The pulse is often wiry and thin, and the tongue may be slightly purplish. Asking about stress triggers and mood helps pinpoint this pattern.

## กลุ่มอาการการแพทย์แผนจีนและการรักษา (TCM Patterns and Treatment)

### Qi And Blood Stagnation
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/qi-and-blood-stagnation)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Fixed, stabbing pain in the abdomen, Pain worsens with pressure, Abdominal distension and bloating, Dark purplish complexion or lips, Hard, immovable mass felt in the abdomen, Irritability or mood swings
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Stress and frustration, Cold, greasy, or heavy foods, Prolonged sitting or inactivity, Cold weather or drafts
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Gentle walking or movement, Warm compress on the abdomen, Warm, easily digestible meals, Stress reduction and relaxation
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Surgical trauma or injury damages the local Qi and Blood, causing them to stagnate and obstruct the flow through the intestines. This stasis creates a fixed, stabbing pain and bloating that worsens with pressure, as the blocked Qi and Blood cannot move freely. When Qi and Blood stagnate, the bowels lose their ability to descend and transport, leading to distension, pain, and sometimes palpable masses.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: dark purple body with stasis spots, distended sublingual veins. Pulse: wiry (弦 xián) and choppy (涩 sè), like a taut string scraping bamboo.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Stagnation of Qi and Blood is the core pathology, so anything that promotes movement and warmth-like gentle exercise, heat, and easily digestible foods-helps to invigorate circulation and break up stasis. Conversely, emotional stress, cold, and heavy foods cause further constriction and stagnation, making the pain and bloating worse.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat small, frequent meals that are warm and easy to digest, such as soups and congees, and avoid cold, raw, and greasy foods. Gentle daily movement like walking helps Qi and Blood circulate and prevents further stasis. Manage stress with relaxation techniques, as emotional tension can worsen stagnation.

### Spleen Deficiency with Dampness
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/spleen-deficiency-with-dampness)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Dull, heavy abdominal bloating, Worse after eating, Loose or unformed stools, Heaviness of the body and limbs, Poor appetite, Fatigue and drowsiness, especially after meals
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Overeating or large meals, Cold, greasy, or heavy foods, Damp or humid weather, Excessive fatigue or overwork
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Rest and warmth on the abdomen, Warm, easily digestible meals, Gentle walking after eating, Dry, warm weather
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Surgery and prolonged illness can deplete the Spleen's ability to transform food and fluids. When the Spleen is weak, Dampness accumulates internally, creating a heavy, sluggish obstruction in the intestines. This leads to the persistent bloating, loose stools, and fatigue that characterize this pattern of intestinal adhesions.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale, swollen body with teeth marks, white greasy coating, moist surface. Pulse: soggy (Ru) and slow (Huan), soft and lacking force, especially at the right Guan position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Spleen Qi is easily overwhelmed by cold, damp, and heavy foods, which create more Dampness and worsen bloating and loose stools. Warm, cooked meals and gentle movement support the Spleen's transformative function, helping to dry Dampness and restore energy. Rest conserves Qi, while abdominal warmth directly aids digestion and relieves the heavy, sluggish feeling.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat warm, cooked meals like rice congee, soups, and steamed vegetables. Avoid cold, raw, and greasy foods that tax the Spleen. Eat small, frequent meals and avoid overeating. Gentle daily movement, such as walking, helps keep Qi and Dampness moving without exhausting the body.

### Obstruction Of the Spleen By Dampness with Liver Qi Stagnation
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/obstruction-of-the-spleen-by-dampness-with-liver-qi-stagnation)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Abdominal bloating and fullness after eating, Sticky or incomplete bowel movements, Heaviness of the body and limbs, Low mood, emotional frustration, Poor appetite and fatigue, Distending discomfort along the ribs, Dizziness with a heavy-headed feeling
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Stress and frustration, Cold or raw foods and drinks, Greasy, heavy meals, Damp or humid weather, Overthinking or worry
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warm, easily digestible meals, Gentle walking after eating, Stress reduction and relaxation, Warm compress on the abdomen
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  After abdominal surgery, the body's Qi is often disrupted, and the Spleen's ability to transform and transport fluids is weakened. When emotional stress or frustration is added, the Liver Qi stagnates and “invades” the Spleen, further impairing its function. This dual attack causes Dampness to accumulate in the intestines, creating a sticky, heavy obstruction that leads to bloating, pain, and sluggish bowel movements - the hallmarks of intestinal adhesions.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale and swollen with teeth marks; white, greasy coating that is thicker in the centre and root, and an excessively moist surface. Pulse: slippery (滑 huá) and wiry (弦 xián); the right Guan position (Spleen) is soggy or soft, while the left Guan position (Liver) is wiry.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Stress and emotional frustration directly cause Liver Qi to stagnate and attack the Spleen, which is the root mechanism of this pattern. Cold and greasy foods create more Dampness, further clogging the Spleen’s function. Warm meals and gentle movement help Qi flow and dry out excess Dampness, while calming activities reduce the Liver’s overbearing influence on digestion.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat small, warm, cooked meals and avoid cold, raw, or greasy foods that burden the Spleen. Gentle walking after meals helps Qi move, and managing stress through light exercise or breathing practices prevents Liver Qi from stagnating further.

### Damp-Heat in the Large Intestine
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/damp-heat-in-the-large-intestine)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Sticky or loose stools with foul odor, Urgent, painful straining with burning sensation at the anus, Mucus or blood in stools, Thick yellow greasy tongue coating, Feeling of heaviness and abdominal distension, Thirst with desire for cold drinks
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Greasy, fried, or spicy foods, Alcohol, Hot, humid weather, Stress and frustration, Overeating or large meals
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Light, bland diet, Cooling foods (e.g., cucumber, mung beans), Warm water hydration, Stress reduction and relaxation, Cool environment
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  After abdominal surgery, Qi and Blood stagnation can linger in the lower burner. Over time this stagnation may smolder into Heat, and if the body is prone to Dampness - from diet, constitution, or lingering Spleen weakness - Damp-Heat forms and settles in the Large Intestine. That Damp-Heat further obstructs the bowels, producing cramping pain, sticky diarrhoea with burning urgency, and a sensation of incomplete evacuation.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red body with a thick yellow greasy coating, thickest at the centre and root. Pulse: slippery (Hua) and rapid (Shu).

### Blood Stagnation in the Lower Burner  caused by Cold
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/blood-stagnation-in-the-lower-burner-caused-by-cold)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Fixed, stabbing lower abdominal pain that feels better with warmth, Pain worsens in cold weather or after consuming cold foods, Cold sensation in the lower belly or cold hands and feet, Dark or purplish menstrual blood with clots (in women), Pale or dusky complexion
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Cold weather or drafts, Cold or raw foods and drinks, Prolonged sitting in a cold room
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warm compress on the abdomen, Drinking warm ginger or cinnamon tea, Gentle walking or movement, Rest in a warm environment
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  After surgery or injury, the body's Qi and Blood can be damaged. If a person has a naturally Cold constitution or is exposed to Cold during recovery, the Cold causes Blood to thicken and stagnate, much like water turning to ice. This congealed Blood blocks the channels in the lower abdomen, producing a fixed, stabbing pain that feels better with warmth.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale-purple or bluish-purple body with possible stasis spots, white slippery coat, and engorged sublingual veins. Pulse: deep (Chen), tight (Jin), and choppy (Se).
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: In this pattern, Cold is the root cause of pain, because it makes Blood thicken and stop moving. Warmth acts like melting ice, restoring flow and easing the fixed, stabbing pain. Cold exposure and cold foods do the opposite, freezing Blood further and intensifying the stasis. Gentle movement helps Qi and Blood circulate, preventing stagnation from settling in.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Keep your abdomen warm with a belly band or hot water bottle, especially during cold months. Favor warm, cooked foods like soups and stews, and use warming spices such as ginger, cinnamon, and fennel. Avoid raw, cold, or frozen foods and drinks, which can further congeal Blood.

## คำถามที่พบบ่อย (Frequently Asked Questions)
### Can TCM dissolve existing intestinal adhesions?
TCM cannot physically dissolve scar tissue, but it can significantly improve blood flow, reduce inflammation, and restore normal bowel function so that adhesions cause fewer symptoms. Many patients find that pain and bloating decrease even though the adhesion itself remains.

### Is acupuncture safe after abdominal surgery?
Yes, acupuncture is safe once the surgical incisions have healed. Your practitioner will avoid needling directly into fresh scars and will select points away from the abdomen if needed. Always inform your acupuncturist about your full surgical history.

### How long does it take to see results with Chinese herbs?
Pain reduction often begins within 2-4 weeks of starting herbs. Improvements in bloating, bowel regularity, and energy levels may take longer, especially for deficiency patterns. Consistency is key - taking your formula daily as prescribed gives the best results.

### Can I use TCM alongside my pain medications?
Yes, TCM is often used alongside conventional medications. However, certain blood-moving herbs can interact with blood thinners, so you must inform both your TCM practitioner and your doctor about everything you are taking. Never stop prescribed medications without medical advice.

### Will I need to change my diet?
Diet plays a big role in TCM treatment for adhesions. A warm, easily digestible diet helps reduce Dampness and supports the Spleen. Avoiding cold, raw, greasy, and sugary foods can make a noticeable difference in your symptoms.

### Can TCM prevent adhesions from recurring after surgery?
Starting TCM soon after surgery may help prevent new adhesions by strengthening the Spleen, moving Qi and Blood, and reducing inflammation. Many patients use herbs and acupuncture during recovery to support healing and lower the risk of recurrence.

### What if I have a bowel obstruction?
A bowel obstruction is a medical emergency. TCM is not appropriate during an acute obstruction. If you experience severe cramping pain, vomiting, or an inability to pass gas or stool, seek immediate medical care. TCM can be used after the acute episode resolves to prevent future problems.

### Does acupuncture hurt?
Most people feel only a brief pinch or a dull ache when the needle is inserted, followed by a sense of relaxation. The needles are hair-thin and much smaller than those used for injections. Many patients look forward to their sessions as a time of deep rest.
