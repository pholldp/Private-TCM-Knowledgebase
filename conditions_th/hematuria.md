## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนปัจจุบัน (Conventional Medicine View)
### คำอธิบาย (Description)
In Western medicine, hematuria is classified as either gross (visible) or microscopic (detected only on a urine test). It can originate anywhere along the urinary tract - from the kidneys to the urethra. Common causes include infections, kidney stones, glomerulonephritis, trauma, and, less commonly, tumors. Diagnosis typically involves a urinalysis, imaging such as ultrasound or CT, and sometimes cystoscopy to visualize the bladder lining.

### การรักษาหลัก (Treatments)
Treatment depends entirely on the underlying cause. Antibiotics are prescribed for urinary tract infections, while kidney stones may be managed with pain relief, increased fluid intake, or procedures like lithotripsy. For glomerular diseases, medications such as corticosteroids or immunosuppressants may be used. When no structural cause is found, the hematuria is often monitored without specific treatment, which can be frustrating for patients.

## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนจีน (Traditional Chinese Medicine View)
### ความเข้าใจเกี่ยวกับการแพทย์แผนจีน (TCM Understanding)
In TCM, blood belongs inside the vessels, held there by the Spleen’s holding function and the Kidneys’ securing of the lower orifices. When blood appears in the urine, it means something has disrupted this containment - either a weakness in the organs that hold blood in, or a pathogenic factor like Heat that agitates the blood and forces it out. The Kidneys govern water and the bladder, so they are always central to urinary bleeding, but the Spleen and even the Heart can be involved in certain patterns.
Heat is the most common aggressor. Whether it’s excess Heat from a bladder infection (Damp-Heat) or empty Heat from a long-term Yin deficiency, the result is similar: the Heat ‘scorches’ the delicate blood vessels of the lower burner, making them fragile and leaky. Dampness adds to the trouble by creating a sticky, turbid environment that obstructs normal urine flow and makes bleeding more likely. This is why burning, urgency, and cloudy urine often accompany hematuria in acute cases.
Deficiency patterns, on the other hand, involve a lack of the substances that normally anchor blood. Qi deficiency means the body’s holding power is too weak to keep blood inside the vessels. Yin deficiency leads to a subtle heat that slowly erodes the vessel walls over time. Kidney Qi not Firm is another deficiency pattern where the Kidneys lose their ability to secure the bladder, leading to chronic, recurrent bleeding that worsens with fatigue. These patterns are more common in chronic, low-grade hematuria that comes and goes.
Because the same symptom - blood in the urine - can arise from such different roots, TCM treatment is never one-size-fits-all. A person with burning, dark, cloudy urine and a greasy yellow tongue coating needs a completely different strategy than someone with pale urine, fatigue, and a weak pulse. The tongue, pulse, and accompanying symptoms are the map that guides the practitioner to the correct pattern.

### การวินิจฉัยโดยแพทย์แผนจีน (Practitioner Diagnosis)
A TCM practitioner begins by asking about the appearance of the blood, any pain or burning during urination, and what makes the hematuria better or worse. The timing and triggers-such as exercise, stress, or diet-offer the first clues about which pattern is at play.
If the hematuria is mild, often microscopic, and worsens after exertion or fatigue, accompanied by shortness of breath, dry mouth, and warm palms, this points to Qi and Yin Deficiency (气阴两虚, qì yīn liǎng xū). The tongue is red with a thin white coat, and the pulse is deep and thready or slightly rapid.
When the urine is dark red and scanty, with lower back soreness, night sweats, and a feeling of heat in the palms and soles, the pattern is Kidney Yin Deficiency with Empty-Heat Blazing (阴虚火旺, yīn xū huǒ wàng). The tongue is red with little coating, and the pulse is thready and rapid, reflecting the inner heat.
Chronic, recurrent hematuria with pronounced lower back weakness, frequent urination, and a deep, weak pulse suggests Kidney Qi not Firm (肾气不固, shèn qì bù gù). The tongue is pale with a thin white coat, and the person often feels cold and easily fatigued.
If the blood in the urine is accompanied by general fatigue, a pale complexion, poor appetite, and maybe other easy bruising or bleeding, the practitioner suspects Spleen not controlling Blood (脾不统血, pí bù tǒng xuè). The tongue is pale and swollen with teeth marks, and the pulse is deep, thready, and weak.
A sudden onset of bright red or dark urine with burning, urgency, and a heavy sensation in the lower abdomen points to Damp-Heat in the Bladder (膀胱湿热, páng guāng shī rè). The tongue is red with a yellow, greasy coating, and the pulse is slippery and rapid-classic signs of heat and dampness.

## กลุ่มอาการการแพทย์แผนจีนและการรักษา (TCM Patterns and Treatment)

### Qi and Yin Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/qi-and-yin-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Fatigue and weakness, Shortness of breath on exertion, Dry mouth or thirst with little desire to drink, Blood in urine worsens with fatigue, Warm palms and soles
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Overwork and exhaustion, Spicy, fried, or greasy foods, Late nights, Emotional stress
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Rest and adequate sleep, Warm, cooked meals, Gentle walking
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Qi holds blood inside the vessels, while Yin cools and nourishes the blood. When both are deficient, Qi becomes too weak to contain the blood, and the mild heat from Yin deficiency agitates it. This combination allows blood to leak into the urine, especially after exertion or during chronic illness.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red or pale-red body with thin or absent coating, possibly cracked, dry surface. Pulse: fine (thin) and weak, possibly rapid.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Overexertion and stress drain Qi further, leaving it unable to hold blood, while spicy foods and late nights deepen Yin deficiency and stir up empty heat. Rest and gentle movement conserve Qi, and warm, moistening foods rebuild Yin fluids, helping the body naturally contain blood within the vessels.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Prioritize rest and avoid overexertion, as pushing through fatigue deepens the deficiency. Eat warm, easy-to-digest foods like congee, steamed vegetables, and soups with mild herbs. Avoid spicy, fried, or drying foods that consume Yin fluids. Gentle daily movement like walking or taiji helps Qi flow without draining it.

### Kidney Yin Deficiency With Empty-Heat Blazing
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/kidney-yin-deficiency-with-empty-heat-blazing)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Dark, scanty urine with blood, Night sweats, Five-palm heat (hot palms, soles, chest), Lower back soreness and weakness, Dry throat, especially at night, Irritability and mental restlessness
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Overwork and late nights, Spicy, fried, or greasy foods, Alcohol and coffee, Emotional stress, Hot weather or saunas
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Cool environment, Rest and adequate sleep, Cooling, moistening foods, Gentle walking, Drinking plenty of water
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When Kidney Yin is depleted, the cooling, moistening aspect of the body weakens, allowing empty heat to flare upward and disturb the blood. This heat scorches the delicate blood vessels in the lower burner, making them fragile and prone to leaking. Blood then seeps into the urine, often appearing as dark, scanty, or tea-colored urine, especially worse at night or after exertion.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red, thin, and dry with little or no coating, possible cracks. Pulse: fine (xi) and rapid (shu), especially weak at the rear (chi) position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Empty heat is fueled by anything that dries or heats the body: spicy food, alcohol, and overwork deplete Yin and fan the flames. Late nights directly consume Kidney Yin, making heat worse. Cooling foods and rest replenish Yin and calm the heat, while gentle movement supports circulation without straining the body's reserves.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Focus on cooling, moistening foods like pear, cucumber, watermelon, black beans, and millet. Avoid spicy, greasy, or fried foods, alcohol, and coffee, which aggravate empty heat. Prioritize rest and avoid late nights, as staying up depletes Yin further. Gentle exercise like walking or tai chi can help without overtaxing the body.

### Kidney Qi not Firm
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/kidney-qi-not-firm)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Blood in urine that worsens with fatigue or prolonged standing, Sore and weak lower back and knees, Frequent, clear urination, especially at night, Fatigue and weakness, Pale, puffy tongue with thin white coat
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Overwork and prolonged standing, Exposure to cold, Excessive sexual activity, Emotional stress, Raw, cold foods and iced drinks
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Rest and adequate sleep, Warmth on the lower back, Warm, cooked meals, Gentle walking
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  The Kidneys govern the lower orifices and are responsible for securing the bladder. When Kidney Qi is weak, it cannot hold urine or blood inside the vessels, so blood leaks out with the urine. This type of hematuria is typically chronic or comes and goes, worsening whenever you feel tired or overworked.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: Pale, puffy, moist body with a thin white coat, and possible tooth marks on the edges. Pulse: Deep (Chen), weak (Ruo), and fine (Xi), especially feeble at both rear (Chi) positions.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Overwork, standing for long periods, and cold exposure drain Kidney Qi, making it even harder for the body to hold blood and urine inside. Rest and warmth conserve that Qi, allowing the Kidneys to regain their securing function. Gentle movement keeps Qi flowing without exhausting it, while warm, nourishing foods directly support the Kidney's ability to govern the lower orifices.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Prioritize rest and avoid pushing through fatigue. Keep your lower back and abdomen warm, and choose cooked, easily digestible foods like congee, stews, and soups. Include kidney-nourishing ingredients such as walnuts, black beans, lamb, and goji berries, while avoiding raw, cold, and icy foods that can further weaken Kidney Qi.

### Spleen not controlling Blood
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/spleen-not-controlling-blood)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Blood in the urine (often microscopic or mild), Fatigue and weakness, Pale or sallow complexion, Poor appetite and loose stools, Easy bruising or prolonged bleeding from minor cuts
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Overwork and exhaustion, Raw, cold foods and iced drinks, Emotional stress
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Rest and adequate sleep, Warm, cooked meals, Small, frequent eating
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  The Spleen holds blood inside the vessels. When Spleen Qi is weakened by chronic illness, overwork, or poor diet, it can no longer perform this holding function. Blood then seeps out of the vessels and escapes into the urine, producing hematuria that is often worse after exertion.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale, puffy, and tender with teeth marks on the edges; thin white coating. Pulse: fine (thin) and weak, especially at the right middle (guan) position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Overwork and chronic fatigue directly drain Spleen Qi, weakening its grip on blood and triggering hematuria. Raw and cold foods extinguish the Spleen's digestive fire, further impairing its ability to generate Qi. Rest and warm, cooked nourishment rebuild Spleen Qi, restoring its blood-holding function, while small meals prevent overloading the weakened organ.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Prioritize regular, small, warm meals to avoid overwhelming a weak Spleen. Focus on easily digestible, cooked foods like congee, soups, and stews. Avoid raw, cold, and greasy foods that dampen Spleen fire. Gentle movement like walking or Tai Chi can support Qi, but rest is paramount when fatigue is prominent.

### Damp-Heat in the Bladder
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/damp-heat-in-the-bladder)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Burning or stinging pain during urination, Dark, cloudy urine with visible or microscopic blood, Lower abdominal fullness or pressure, Dry mouth or thirst with little desire to drink, Sudden onset or acute flare-ups
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Spicy, fried, or greasy foods, Alcohol and coffee, Hot and humid weather, Holding urine, Overwork and exhaustion
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Drinking plenty of water, Cool environment, Light, bland diet, Rest and adequate sleep, Frequent urination (not holding)
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Dampness and Heat combine and sink downward into the Lower Burner, lodging in the Bladder. The Heat agitates the blood and damages the delicate blood vessels (collaterals) lining the urinary tract, causing blood to leak into the urine. The Dampness makes the urine cloudy and difficult to pass, while the Heat creates the burning sensation and urgency.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red body with a yellow, greasy coating thickest at the root. Pulse: slippery (滑 huá) and rapid (数 shuò).
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Spicy, greasy foods and alcohol generate Damp-Heat internally, directly fueling the pattern. Hot and humid weather adds external Damp-Heat that can invade the Bladder. Holding urine allows Damp-Heat to concentrate and damage the vessels. Conversely, drinking water flushes out the Bladder, a cool environment and bland diet reduce internal Heat and Dampness, and rest prevents Qi from becoming agitated, allowing the Bladder to heal.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Avoid spicy, greasy, and fried foods, as well as alcohol and coffee, which add Heat and Dampness. Drink plenty of water and eat cooling, diuretic foods like watermelon, cucumber, mung beans, and celery. Do not hold urine; empty the bladder when you feel the urge. Keep the genital area clean and dry.

## คำถามที่พบบ่อย (Frequently Asked Questions)
### Can Chinese herbs stop blood in the urine?
Yes, many TCM formulas contain herbs that specifically cool the blood, stop bleeding, and strengthen the vessels. The key is matching the formula to the pattern - for example, a formula that clears Damp-Heat will not help someone whose bleeding is due to Qi deficiency. When the correct pattern is treated, the bleeding often diminishes within weeks.

### How long does it take for TCM to work on hematuria?
It depends on the underlying pattern. Acute infections with burning urination may improve in days. Chronic, low-grade bleeding from a deficiency pattern can take 3 to 6 months to resolve completely, because the body needs time to rebuild Qi and Yin. Most patients see some improvement within the first month if the pattern is correctly identified.

### Can I use TCM alongside my conventional treatment?
In most cases, yes. TCM can complement antibiotics, immunosuppressants, or monitoring. However, some herbs have blood-moving properties and could interact with anticoagulant medications like warfarin. Always tell both your doctor and your TCM practitioner about all medications and supplements you are taking to avoid interactions.

### Are there foods I should avoid if I have blood in my urine?
Yes. In general, TCM recommends avoiding spicy, greasy, and fried foods, as well as alcohol and coffee, because these can generate Heat and Dampness that worsen bleeding. Instead, favor light, cooling foods like cucumber, watermelon, mung beans, and plenty of plain water. Your practitioner may give more specific advice based on your pattern.

### Is acupuncture safe for hematuria?
Yes, acupuncture is very safe when performed by a licensed practitioner. It can help regulate the organ systems and reduce inflammation. Points on the lower back and legs are commonly used to strengthen the Kidneys and Spleen. It is not a substitute for urgent medical evaluation if you have heavy bleeding, pain, or fever.

### What if I see a lot of blood in my urine suddenly?
If you see a large amount of blood, especially with clots or pain, seek urgent medical care. This could indicate a serious condition like a kidney stone, infection, or tumor. Once acute issues are ruled out, TCM can be a valuable part of your recovery and prevention plan.
