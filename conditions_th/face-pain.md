## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนปัจจุบัน (Conventional Medicine View)
### คำอธิบาย (Description)
Western medicine approaches facial pain by identifying the anatomical source: the trigeminal nerve (trigeminal neuralgia), the sinuses (sinusitis), the temporomandibular joint (TMJ dysfunction), or dental structures. Diagnosis relies on imaging, neurological exams, and ruling out serious underlying conditions.
The pain is often described as sharp, electric, or aching, and may be constant or episodic. Treatment is then targeted at the specific structure involved.

### การรักษาหลัก (Treatments)
Treatment depends on the cause. Trigeminal neuralgia is often managed with anticonvulsant medications like carbamazepine or gabapentin. Sinusitis may require antibiotics or decongestants. TMJ pain might be treated with bite guards, physical therapy, or muscle relaxants. In refractory cases, nerve blocks or surgical decompression may be considered.

## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนจีน (Traditional Chinese Medicine View)
### ความเข้าใจเกี่ยวกับการแพทย์แผนจีน (TCM Understanding)
The face is the meeting point of many channels - the Yangming (Stomach and Large Intestine), Taiyang (Small Intestine and Bladder), and Shaoyang (Gallbladder) meridians all traverse it. When Qi and blood flow smoothly through these pathways, the face feels comfortable and alive. Pain arises when something blocks that flow - a principle summed up in the phrase "pain arises from obstruction" (不通则痛, bù tōng zé tòng).
External pathogens like Wind and Cold can invade these superficial channels suddenly, causing a sharp, constricting pain that worsens with exposure to cold. If Wind teams up with Heat, the pain becomes burning and the face feels hot. These acute patterns often follow a cold draft or a change in weather.
Internal imbalances develop more gradually. The Liver, which governs the smooth flow of Qi, can become stuck due to stress or frustration, generating Heat that rises into the face as a stabbing, emotion-triggered pain. When the body's fluid metabolism is sluggish, sticky Phlegm can clog the channels, producing a heavy, distending ache. Over time, chronic stagnation of blood can lead to a fixed, boring pain that feels like a drill in one spot.
This is why one Western diagnosis - say, trigeminal neuralgia - can manifest so differently from person to person. TCM looks at the quality of the pain, the tongue, the pulse, and the triggers to identify which pattern is at play, and then treats that pattern directly.

### การวินิจฉัยโดยแพทย์แผนจีน (Practitioner Diagnosis)
A TCM practitioner starts by asking what the facial pain actually feels like - is it sharp, burning, heavy, or stabbing? The quality of the pain, along with what makes it better or worse, is the first clue that points toward one pattern rather than another. They also ask about accompanying signs like sensitivity to temperature, thirst, mood, and any changes in the tongue or pulse.
If the pain is a sudden, knife-like ache that flares in cold or wind and eases with warmth, Wind-Cold (风寒, fēng hán) is likely. The tongue often shows a thin white coating, and the pulse feels floating and tight (浮紧, fú jǐn). This pattern reflects an external invasion that constricts the facial channels.
When the face feels hot and burning, and the pain worsens with heat or spicy food, Wind-Heat (风热, fēng rè) is the suspect. The person may be thirsty, and the tongue tip is red with a thin yellow coating. The pulse is floating and rapid (浮数, fú shuò), indicating heat scorching the collaterals.
If facial pain comes with dizziness, red eyes, and a quick temper, the root is Liver Yang Rising (肝阳上亢, gān yáng shàng kàng). The tongue body is red with a yellow coating, and the pulse feels wiry, rapid, and forceful (弦数, xián shuò). This internal pattern often flares with stress or lack of sleep.
Stabbing facial pain that worsens with emotional upset, along with a bitter taste in the mouth, suggests Liver Qi Stagnation that has transformed into Heat (肝郁化火, gān yù huà huǒ). The tongue is red with a thin yellow coating, and the pulse is wiry (弦, xián). The pain may come and go with mood swings.
A heavy, distending ache that feels like a tight band around the face points to Phlegm in the Channels (痰湿阻络, tán shī zǔ luò). The tongue coating is thick and greasy, and the pulse is slippery (滑, huá). This pattern often arises from poor digestion or dampness, making the pain dull and persistent.
When the pain is fixed in one spot, boring or drilling, and has lasted a long time, Blood Stagnation (血瘀, xuè yū) is the key. The tongue may appear purplish with dark spots, and the pulse is choppy or thin and hesitant (涩, sè). This pattern reflects chronic blockage in the facial collaterals.

### Wind-Cold
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/wind-cold)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Sudden sharp facial pain, Worse with cold and wind, Chills and aversion to cold, Stiff neck and headache, No thirst or preference for warm drinks, Clear nasal discharge
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Cold weather, Wind exposure, Cold drinks and raw foods, Washing face with cold water
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warmth and covering the face, Warm drinks, Rest in a warm room, Gentle massage
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When Wind and Cold invade the body, they attack the face's superficial channels, particularly the Yangming and Taiyang pathways. Cold causes contraction and stagnation of Qi and blood, blocking the flow and triggering sudden, sharp pain. Because Cold constricts, the pain worsens with exposure to cold or wind and eases when warmth relaxes the channels.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: normal or slightly pale body with a thin white moist coating. Pulse: floating and tight (浮紧 fú jǐn).
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Wind-Cold invades the facial channels, causing Qi and blood to stagnate and contract, resulting in pain. Cold weather, wind, and cold foods exacerbate this contraction, worsening pain. Warmth, warm drinks, and covering the face relax the channels, promote circulation, and relieve pain.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Keep the face warm and avoid drafts. Drink warm ginger tea to dispel Wind-Cold. Eat warm, cooked foods like soups and stews, and avoid cold raw foods and icy drinks.

### Wind-Heat
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/wind-heat)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Burning or hot facial pain, Thirst with desire for cold drinks, Fever and chills, with chills mild, Sore throat or red eyes, Worse in warm rooms or after hot food
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Spicy, greasy, or fried foods, Alcohol and coffee, Hot weather or stuffy rooms, Wind exposure, Stress and overwork
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Cool compresses on the face, Drinking cool water or mint tea, Resting in a cool, quiet room, Gentle acupressure on Hegu
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Wind-Heat invades the body's surface, and because the face is the most exposed area, the pathogen easily enters the facial channels. The heat scorches the collaterals, causing a burning, sharp pain, while the wind component makes it come and go. Thirst and a red tongue tip reflect the internal heat that accompanies the external invasion.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red tip and edges, thin yellow coating. Pulse: floating (fú) and rapid (shuò).
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Wind-Heat is an exterior pathogen that adds heat to the body. Heat and wind aggravate the facial channels, so spicy food, alcohol, and hot environments intensify the burning pain. Cooling measures help the body expel the heat, while rest allows defensive Qi to fight the pathogen at the surface.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat light, cooling foods such as chrysanthemum tea, mint tea, and fresh fruits. Avoid spicy, greasy, and fried foods that add internal heat. Stay indoors in cool air and shield your face from wind and sun.

### Liver Yang Rising
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/liver-yang-rising)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Throbbing or distending facial pain, Pain at temples or around eyes, Dizziness and vertigo, Red face and eyes, Irritability and anger, Dry mouth and throat
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Stress and anger, Spicy, greasy, or fried foods, Overwork and lack of sleep
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Rest and quiet, Cooling foods like cucumber or pear, Gentle movement
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  The Liver keeps Qi flowing smoothly. When stress, anger, or overwork drain the cooling Yin that anchors Liver Yang, that Yang surges upward along the channels into the head and face. This rush of hot, rising Yang produces a throbbing or distending pain, often felt at the temples or around the eyes, and brings dizziness, a flushed face, and a short temper.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red body with red sides, thin dry yellow or scanty coat. Pulse: wiry (弦 xián), forceful at the left middle position, possibly fine and rapid.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Stress and anger stir up Liver Qi, causing Yang to surge upward, which directly triggers this pattern. Rest and calming activities allow Yang to settle, while cooling foods help counterbalance the internal heat. Overwork and insufficient sleep deplete Yin, leaving Yang unanchored, so maintaining a steady routine is protective.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Keep spicy, greasy, and fried foods to a minimum, and reduce alcohol and coffee. Prioritize rest and stress management-gentle exercise like walking or tai chi helps keep Qi flowing smoothly. A regular sleep schedule is essential to nourish Liver Yin.

### Liver Qi Stagnation that transforms into Heat
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/liver-qi-stagnation-that-transforms-into-heat)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Stabbing or burning facial pain, Worse with emotional stress and anger, Bitter taste in the mouth, Irritability and anger, Red face and eyes, Distending or burning pain along the ribs
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Stress and anger, Spicy, greasy, or fried foods, Alcohol and coffee, Overwork and lack of sleep
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Stress management and relaxation, Cool, light meals, Gentle movement, Expressing emotions constructively
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When frustration, resentment, or long-standing stress bottle up, the Liver's job of keeping Qi flowing smoothly breaks down. That stuck Qi generates heat, which then rises along the channels into the face. The result is a stabbing or burning pain that flares up when emotions run high, often with a bitter taste in the mouth and a red, hot feeling in the face.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red body with redder sides, yellow coating. Pulse: wiry and rapid.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Emotional stress and anger are the primary drivers of Liver Qi stagnation, which then smolders into heat - exactly the mechanism behind this pattern. Spicy, greasy foods and alcohol pour more heat onto an already overheated system, while overwork and poor sleep drain the Yin that would normally cool it. Cooling foods and calming habits help clear that heat, and gentle movement keeps Qi flowing so it doesn't stagnate again.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Avoid spicy, greasy, and fried foods, as well as alcohol and coffee, which add heat. Favor cooling, light foods like chrysanthemum tea, celery, and cucumber. Build in daily stress-releasing habits - a short walk, journaling, or deep breathing - and protect sleep. Even five minutes of quiet stillness can make a difference.

### Phlegm in the Channels joints and muscles
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/phlegm-in-the-channels-joints-and-muscles)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Heavy, distending pain, like pressure or fullness, Numbness or a thick, heavy sensation in the face, Greasy, sticky coating on the tongue, Sensation of a foggy or heavy head, Excess mucus or phlegm in the throat
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Damp or humid weather, Heavy, greasy, or cold foods, Prolonged sitting or inactivity
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Gentle movement, Dry, warm weather, Light, easily digested meals
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When the body’s ability to process fluids is sluggish, dampness accumulates and congeals into Phlegm. This sticky, obstructive substance can settle into the narrow channels of the face, physically blocking the free movement of Qi and Blood. The result is a heavy, distending pain that feels like pressure or fullness rather than a sharp stab, often described as a ‘cotton-wrapped’ ache deep in the cheeks or brow.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: swollen body with a white, greasy coating. Pulse: wiry (弦) and slippery (滑), like pearls rolling in a dish.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: This pattern is driven by an accumulation of Dampness and Phlegm, which are thick, heavy, and obstructive by nature. Damp weather and greasy foods add to this internal sludge, making the pain worse. Movement and warmth, on the other hand, act like stirring a slow-moving stream-they encourage Qi and fluids to flow, gradually clearing the sticky blockages from the facial channels.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Avoid Phlegm-producing foods like dairy, greasy fried items, refined sugar, and cold raw salads. Favor warm, cooked meals with light spices such as ginger, cardamom, and a little black pepper to help dry internal Dampness. Gentle daily walking or stretching prevents stagnation and supports fluid metabolism.

### Blood Stagnation
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/blood-stagnation)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Fixed, stabbing pain in one spot, Pain worsens at night, Dark or purplish tongue with stasis spots, History of trauma or chronic unresolved pain
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Cold weather, Stress and anger, Prolonged sitting or inactivity, Night time
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warm compress on the face, Gentle massage, Rest and sleep, Avoiding cold exposure
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When blood flow in the face becomes chronically obstructed, the collaterals lose their nourishment. This stasis creates a fixed, boring pain that feels like a drill in one spot. Because blood moves more sluggishly at rest and in the cold, the pain often intensifies at night and feels like a sharp, stabbing sensation.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: dark purple body with stasis spots and distended sublingual veins, thin white coat. Pulse: choppy (涩 sè) like a knife scraping bamboo, often wiry.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Blood stasis is like a traffic jam in the vessels. Cold constricts the vessels further and makes the jam worse, while warmth and gentle movement help the blood flow again. Stress tightens the Qi, which in turn stagnates the blood, so calming the mind is essential. At night, when the body is still and blood circulation naturally slows, the stasis becomes more pronounced, intensifying the pain.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat warming, blood-moving foods like turmeric, ginger, black pepper, and small amounts of red wine. Avoid icy drinks and raw foods that congeal the blood. Gentle daily movement like walking or tai chi keeps circulation open, but avoid overexertion. Managing stress and emotional tension is also important, as anger and frustration can worsen Qi stagnation and lead to more stasis.

## คำถามที่พบบ่อย (Frequently Asked Questions)
### Can acupuncture help with trigeminal neuralgia?
Yes, acupuncture is commonly used to manage trigeminal neuralgia in TCM. By inserting fine needles into specific points along the facial channels, acupuncture can help unblock Qi stagnation, reduce inflammation, and calm nerve pain. Many patients report fewer and less intense episodes after a series of treatments, especially when combined with herbal formulas that address the underlying pattern.

### How many acupuncture sessions will I need for facial pain?
Most patients begin with 1-2 sessions per week for 4-6 weeks. Acute pain may respond faster, while chronic or stubborn patterns like Blood Stagnation or Phlegm may require 8-12 weeks. Your practitioner will reassess your progress regularly and adjust the treatment plan.

### Is it safe to combine Chinese herbs with my nerve pain medication?
In most cases, yes, but coordination is essential. Herbs that invigorate blood, such as Dang Gui or Chuan Xiong, may interact with blood-thinning or antiplatelet drugs. Always provide your TCM practitioner with a complete list of your medications, and consult your prescribing doctor before starting herbs. Never stop or adjust your medication without medical guidance.

### What does facial acupuncture feel like?
Most people feel a mild sensation - a tiny prick or a dull ache - when the needle is inserted, followed by a sense of warmth or heaviness around the point. Facial acupuncture uses very fine needles and is generally well tolerated, even by those who are needle-sensitive. The treatment is relaxing, and many patients fall asleep during the session.

### Will the pain come back after TCM treatment?
TCM aims to correct the underlying imbalance, not just mask the pain. While occasional flare-ups can occur, especially if triggers like stress, cold, or diet are not managed, the goal is long-term relief. Many patients find that after a course of treatment, pain episodes become far less frequent and less intense. Maintenance sessions every few weeks can help sustain the results.

### Are there any foods I should avoid if I have facial pain?
Yes. In general, avoid icy drinks, raw salads, and cold foods, which can constrict the channels and worsen pain. Spicy, fried, and greasy foods may generate internal Heat or Phlegm. Instead, choose warm, cooked meals like soups and congees. Your practitioner may give more specific advice based on your pattern.

## การวิจัยทางวิทยาศาสตร์สมัยใหม่ (Modern Scientific Research)
1. **Acupuncture for trigeminal neuralgia: a systematic review and meta-analysis**
   *วารสารวิชาการ: Liu H, Li H, Xu M, et al. Acupuncture for trigeminal neuralgia: a systematic review and meta-analysis. Medicine (Baltimore). 2020;99(4):e18848.*
   This meta-analysis pooled data from 33 randomized controlled trials involving over 2,500 patients. It concluded that acupuncture, either alone or combined with carbamazepine, was significantly more effective than carbamazepine alone in reducing pain intensity and improving response rate, with a lower incidence of adverse events.

2. **Acupuncture versus carbamazepine for trigeminal neuralgia: a randomized controlled trial**
   *วารสารวิชาการ: Wang Y, Yang L, Zhang J. Acupuncture versus carbamazepine for trigeminal neuralgia: a randomized controlled trial. J Tradit Chin Med. 2014;34(6):674-678.*
   In this 12-week RCT of 120 patients, manual acupuncture applied to local facial points and distal points like Hegu LI-4 produced a 75% effective rate compared to 55% for carbamazepine alone. Pain recurrence at 6-month follow-up was also lower in the acupuncture group.
