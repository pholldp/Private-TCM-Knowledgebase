## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนปัจจุบัน (Conventional Medicine View)
### คำอธิบาย (Description)
In Western medicine, sinus congestion refers to swelling of the nasal passages and sinuses, often triggered by viral infections, allergies, or irritants. The lining becomes inflamed, producing excess mucus that can become trapped, leading to a feeling of stuffiness, pressure around the nose and eyes, and sometimes thick discharge. Diagnosis is typically based on symptoms and a physical exam, with imaging reserved for chronic or complicated cases.

### การรักษาหลัก (Treatments)
Standard treatments include decongestant sprays or pills, antihistamines for allergies, nasal corticosteroid sprays to reduce inflammation, and saline rinses. For bacterial sinusitis, antibiotics may be prescribed. Chronic congestion may be managed with immunotherapy or, in some cases, surgery to improve sinus drainage.

## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนจีน (Traditional Chinese Medicine View)
### ความเข้าใจเกี่ยวกับการแพทย์แผนจีน (TCM Understanding)
In TCM, the nose is the opening of the Lungs, so any blockage here is primarily a sign that Lung Qi is not flowing smoothly. The Lungs are responsible for descending and dispersing Qi and fluids, and when this function is disrupted - whether by an external pathogen like Wind-Cold or by internal weakness - congestion results.
But the story doesn't end with the Lungs. The Spleen is in charge of transforming and transporting fluids; if it's weak, dampness and phlegm accumulate and rise to clog the sinuses. The Kidneys, especially Kidney Yang, provide the body's foundational warmth that powers this fluid metabolism. So a chronic, cold-type congestion often traces back to Kidney Yang deficiency.
This is why a single Western diagnosis of sinus congestion can have many different TCM patterns. An acute, cold-triggered stuffy nose with clear mucus points to Wind-Cold invading the Lungs - an external attack.
If the mucus turns thick and yellow and you feel hot and thirsty, it's Wind-Heat. When congestion is chronic, comes and goes with fatigue, and you catch every cold that goes around, the root is usually Spleen and Lung Qi Deficiency - your defensive shield is down.
If the congestion feels heavy and constant, with thick white mucus and a bloated feeling, Spleen Deficiency with Dampness is the culprit. And if your nose stuffs up especially at night and you always have cold hands and feet, Kidney Yang Deficiency is likely the deeper cause.
Each pattern requires a different strategy: expelling the invader for acute patterns, strengthening the body's energy for deficiency patterns, or warming the internal fire for Kidney Yang weakness. The beauty of TCM is that it doesn't just unblock the nose temporarily - it corrects the imbalance so that your sinuses can stay clear on their own.

### การวินิจฉัยโดยแพทย์แผนจีน (Practitioner Diagnosis)
A TCM practitioner begins by asking what the discharge looks like and when the congestion feels worst. The color, thickness, and timing of mucus, along with other clues like body temperature and energy levels, help separate the five common patterns behind sinus congestion.
If the congestion came on suddenly after exposure to cold and comes with clear, watery mucus, chills, and a scratchy throat, that points to Wind-Cold invading the Lungs. The tongue often looks pale with a thin white coating, and the pulse feels floating and tight, like a drum skin.
When the mucus turns thick and yellow or green, and the person feels feverish with a sore throat and thirst, the picture shifts to Wind-Heat invading the Lungs. Here the tongue tip is redder, with a thin yellow coat, and the pulse is floating and rapid - signs of heat rather than cold.
Chronic congestion that comes and goes, with clear or white thin mucus, fatigue, and a tendency to catch colds easily, suggests Spleen and Lung Qi Deficiency. The tongue is pale and slightly swollen, and the pulse feels weak. The body simply lacks the defensive Qi to keep the nose clear.
If the congestion feels heavy and constant, with thick, sticky white mucus, bloating, and loose stools, the root is Spleen Deficiency with Dampness. The tongue appears puffy with a greasy white coating, and the pulse is often slippery or weak, reflecting accumulated moisture that the Spleen cannot manage.
When the nose blocks severely at night and the person always feels cold, with a sore lower back and frequent urination, Kidney Yang Deficiency is likely. The tongue is pale and swollen with a wet, white coating, and the pulse is deep and weak - the internal fire that should warm and transform fluids has dimmed.

## กลุ่มอาการการแพทย์แผนจีนและการรักษา (TCM Patterns and Treatment)

### Wind-Cold invading the Lungs
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/wind-cold-invading-the-lungs)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Clear watery nasal discharge, Chills more prominent than fever, Absence of sweating, Thin white watery phlegm, Headache and body aches
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Cold air, Drafts, Icy drinks, Dairy products, Overwork
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warmth, Steam inhalation, Ginger tea, Rest, Warm compress on face
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Wind-Cold invades the body's surface and blocks the Lung channel, which governs the nose and the outward flow of defensive Qi. The cold constricts the nasal passages and the Lung's ability to diffuse and descend Qi, trapping fluids in the nose. This produces sudden congestion with clear, watery discharge, accompanied by chills and body aches as the external pathogen fights with the body's protective layer.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: normal or slightly pale body, thin white moist coating. Pulse: floating (fu) and tight (jin).
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: In TCM, Cold has a constricting nature - it tightens the nasal passages and blocks the Lung's ability to diffuse Qi, so cold air, drafts, and icy drinks directly worsen congestion. Dairy and raw foods generate dampness and phlegm, adding to the fluid buildup in the nose. Warmth and steam relax the contracted channels and help the Lung's defensive Qi push the pathogen out, while rest conserves the body's resources to fight the invasion. Ginger tea warms the interior and assists the exterior-releasing action of the herbs, making it a simple but effective home remedy.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Keep warm and avoid drafts. Sip warm ginger or scallion tea to help the body push the pathogen out. Steam inhalation with a few drops of peppermint or eucalyptus oil can moisten and open the nasal passages. Avoid cold drinks, raw foods, and dairy products, which can create more phlegm and dampness. Rest is essential - overexertion depletes the defensive Qi that is fighting the cold.

### Wind-Heat invading the Lungs
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/wind-heat-invading-the-lungs)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Thick yellow or turbid nasal discharge, Sore or painful throat, Fever stronger than any chills, Thirst or dry mouth, Red tip of the tongue
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Spicy or fried foods, Alcohol, Dry, hot environments
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Cool peppermint tea, Steam inhalation, Rest in a well-ventilated room
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Wind-Heat is an external pathogen that enters through the nose and mouth, striking the Lung first. The Heat component inflames the nasal passages and thickens the mucus, while the Wind obstructs the smooth flow of Lung Qi. This combination traps heat and fluid in the nose, producing the classic yellow, sticky congestion and a sore, scratchy throat.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red tip, thin yellow coating, body slightly red. Pulse: floating and rapid (Fu Shu).
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Wind-Heat congests the nose by trapping heat and fluid in the upper body. Cooling foods and herbs like mint disperse that heat, while steam moistens and opens the passages. Spicy and fried foods add more heat, aggravating the inflammation and thickening the mucus further.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Drink plenty of cooling fluids like peppermint or chrysanthemum tea. Steam inhalation with a few drops of eucalyptus or peppermint oil can help open the nose. Avoid spicy, fried, or greasy foods that add internal heat, and rest to allow your defensive Qi to push the pathogen out.

### Spleen and Lung Qi Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/spleen-and-lung-qi-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Nasal congestion that comes and goes, worse when tired, Clear or white watery nasal discharge, Fatigue and general weakness, Poor appetite and loose stools, Pale face and low, weak voice
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Fatigue and overwork, Eating cold or raw foods, Exposure to wind and cold, Stress and excessive worry
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Rest and avoiding overexertion, Warm, cooked, easily digestible foods, Gentle exercise like walking, Keeping the nose and neck warm
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Your Lungs open into the nose and your Spleen produces the defensive Qi that shields you from allergens and weather changes. When both organs are underpowered, that protective shield weakens, and the nose loses its ability to warm and filter the air. Fluids that should be circulated build up and stagnate, creating a chronic, fluctuating congestion with clear or white discharge that worsens whenever you are tired or run down.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale, puffy, tender with teeth marks and a thin white slippery coating. Pulse: weak and lacks force, especially at the right cun (Lung) and guan (Spleen) positions.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Overwork and fatigue deplete Qi further, weakening the Lung and Spleen, so rest and gentle exercise conserve Qi. Cold and raw foods damage Spleen Yang, promoting dampness and congestion, while warm cooked foods support Spleen function. Wind and cold invade the Lungs when defensive Qi is weak, so keeping warm protects the nose. Stress and worry weaken the Spleen's ability to transform fluids, so calmness helps prevent phlegm and congestion.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat warm, cooked foods like soups, stews and congee; avoid raw, cold or iced items that weaken the Spleen. Moderate daily exercise such as walking or tai chi helps Qi flow without draining reserves. Keep your nose and neck protected from wind and cold, and prioritize rest when you feel run down.

### Spleen Deficiency with Dampness
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/spleen-deficiency-with-dampness)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Thick, sticky white nasal mucus, Heavy, full sensation in the head and sinuses, Abdominal bloating, worse after eating, Loose or unformed stools, Fatigue and heaviness in the limbs, Poor appetite, feeling of fullness
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Damp, rainy weather, Overeating or heavy meals, Cold, raw foods and iced drinks, Dairy products and sweets, Sedentary lifestyle
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warm, dry environment, Eating small, frequent meals, Gentle walking after meals, Warm ginger tea with meals, Light, easily digested foods
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Your Spleen is like a pump that manages fluids in the body. When it's weak, fluids build up into a heavy, sticky dampness. That dampness drifts upward and clogs your sinuses, causing a persistent, heavy feeling of congestion with thick white mucus. The same weakness also shows up in your digestion as bloating and loose stools.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale, swollen body with teeth marks, and a white greasy coating that looks moist. Pulse: soggy (Ru) and slow (Huan), soft and lacking force.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Dampness is the root cause here. Damp weather and damp-producing foods (dairy, sweets, raw cold items) add more dampness to an already overloaded system, worsening congestion. Warm, dry conditions and foods that drain dampness (like ginger) help the Spleen function better, reducing mucus and heaviness. Small meals prevent the Spleen from being overwhelmed, and gentle movement stimulates Qi flow to transform dampness.

### Kidney Yang Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/kidney-yang-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Nasal congestion that worsens at night, Clear, watery nasal discharge, Cold lower back and knees, Frequent urination, especially at night, Feeling cold all over, especially hands and feet, Fatigue and low energy
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Cold weather or drafts, Raw and cold foods, Overwork and fatigue, Excessive sexual activity, Standing for long periods
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warmth (heating pad on lower back), Warm drinks and soups, Rest and sleep, Moxibustion, Light exercise (walking, tai chi)
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  The Kidneys anchor the body's fundamental warming fire. When Kidney Yang is weak, that fire burns too low to warm and transform fluids properly. Cold, stagnant fluids then rise and collect in the upper body, settling into the nasal passages and causing congestion.
Because Yang energy naturally ebbs at night, the congestion becomes noticeably worse when you lie down to sleep. The body's overall chill, cold limbs, and lower back soreness all point to the same root: a Kidney furnace that needs stoking.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale, puffy, and tender with tooth marks and a wet white coating. Pulse: deep, slow, and weak, especially at the Chi (third) position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Kidney Yang is like the pilot light of the body's furnace. Cold, overwork, and raw foods further deplete that fire, allowing fluids to stagnate and congestion to worsen. Warmth, rest, and warming foods and herbs help stoke the Kidney fire, enabling it to transform and move fluids, which clears the nasal passages.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat warming foods like lamb, ginger, cinnamon, and walnuts. Avoid raw, cold foods and iced drinks. Keep your lower back and feet warm, especially at night. Gentle exercise like walking or tai chi helps move Qi, but avoid overexertion. Moxibustion on the lower back and lower abdomen can be very helpful.

## คำถามที่พบบ่อย (Frequently Asked Questions)
### Can acupuncture help clear my stuffy nose immediately?
Many people experience some relief right after a session, especially when local points like Yingxiang (LI-20) on either side of the nose are stimulated. This can temporarily open the nasal passages. However, lasting improvement comes from treating the underlying pattern over several sessions, so the congestion doesn't just return the next day.

### How long does it take for Chinese herbs to work for sinus congestion?
For an acute cold or flu, herbs can start reducing congestion within a day or two, and the full formula may be needed for only 3-5 days. For chronic congestion tied to Spleen Qi deficiency or Kidney Yang weakness, you might notice some improvement in 2-4 weeks, but deeper, lasting change often requires 8-12 weeks of consistent herbal therapy to strengthen the body's constitution.

### Can I take TCM herbs with my allergy medications or decongestants?
Generally yes, but you should always tell both your TCM practitioner and your doctor about everything you're taking. Most antihistamines and nasal sprays do not interact negatively with common sinus herbs. However, if you are on blood thinners, some herbs can have mild antiplatelet effects - always inform your practitioner so they can choose safe herbs and adjust dosages if needed.

### Is steam inhalation part of TCM, and does it help?
Absolutely. TCM often recommends steaming with aromatic herbs like mint (Bo He) or ginger to help open the nasal passages and expel pathogens. It's a simple, safe home remedy that complements acupuncture and herbs, especially for Wind-Cold patterns. Just be careful not to burn yourself with hot water.

### Why does my congestion get worse at night?
In TCM, nighttime is when Yin energy dominates, and the body's Yang (warming) energy naturally recedes. If you have Kidney Yang Deficiency, your internal fire is already low, so at night the cold fluids stagnate more, causing increased nasal congestion. This pattern often responds well to warming therapies like moxibustion and kidney-tonifying herbs, and you may notice improvement within a few weeks.

### Will I need to change my diet to get rid of sinus congestion?
Diet plays a key role, especially in chronic congestion. TCM recommends avoiding foods that create dampness and phlegm, such as dairy products, cold drinks, and greasy or fried foods. Instead, favor warm, cooked foods like soups, stews, and ginger tea. These support the Spleen and help keep mucus thin and mobile. For acute Wind-Cold, a bowl of hot chicken soup with scallions and ginger can be as medicinal as it is comforting.

## การวิจัยทางวิทยาศาสตร์สมัยใหม่ (Modern Scientific Research)
1. **Acupuncture in patients with seasonal allergic rhinitis: a randomized trial** ([ลิงก์](https://doi.org/10.7326/0003-4819-158-4-201302190-00002))
   *วารสารวิชาการ: Brinkhaus B, Ortiz M, Witt CM, et al. Acupuncture in patients with seasonal allergic rhinitis: a randomized trial. Ann Intern Med. 2013;158(4):225-34.*
   A multicenter RCT involving 422 patients found that acupuncture added to rescue medication significantly improved nasal symptoms, including congestion, and quality of life compared to sham acupuncture plus rescue medication or rescue medication alone.

2. **Acupuncture for allergic rhinitis** ([ลิงก์](https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD009543.pub2))
   *วารสารวิชาการ: McDonald JL, Cripps AW, Smith PK, et al. Acupuncture for allergic rhinitis. Cochrane Database Syst Rev. 2015;(5):CD009543.*
   This Cochrane systematic review of 13 randomized trials concluded that acupuncture is effective and safe for reducing nasal symptoms, including obstruction, in allergic rhinitis, with benefits lasting up to three months after treatment.

3. **Chinese herbal medicine for allergic rhinitis: a systematic review and meta-analysis** ([ลิงก์](https://doi.org/10.1034/j.1398-9995.2003.00246.x))
   *วารสารวิชาการ: Xue CC, Li CG, Hügel HM, Story DF. Chinese herbal medicine for allergic rhinitis: a systematic review and meta-analysis. Allergy. 2003;58(12):1260-9.*
   A meta-analysis of randomized controlled trials indicated that Chinese herbal medicine improved global symptom scores, including nasal congestion, compared to placebo, though the authors noted that the quality of included studies was variable.
