## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนปัจจุบัน (Conventional Medicine View)
### คำอธิบาย (Description)
Preeclampsia is a pregnancy complication that usually begins after 20 weeks of gestation. It is defined by new-onset high blood pressure and signs of damage to other organ systems - most often the kidneys, which leak protein into the urine. Typical symptoms include severe headaches, vision changes like flashing lights or blurred sight, sudden swelling of the hands and face, and pain in the upper belly.
Western medicine diagnoses preeclampsia through repeated blood pressure readings and lab tests that check urine protein levels, liver enzymes, and platelet counts. Because it can progress rapidly and threaten both mother and baby, it is managed with close monitoring, blood pressure medications, and sometimes early delivery.

### การรักษาหลัก (Treatments)
Standard management depends on how far along the pregnancy is and how severe the condition is. For mild cases, frequent monitoring of blood pressure and fetal well-being may be enough. When blood pressure rises, medications like labetalol or nifedipine are used to keep it in a safer range. If the disease worsens, magnesium sulfate is given to prevent seizures (eclampsia), and delivery of the baby and placenta is the only definitive cure - even if it means a preterm birth.

## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนจีน (Traditional Chinese Medicine View)
### ความเข้าใจเกี่ยวกับการแพทย์แผนจีน (TCM Understanding)
In TCM, preeclampsia is understood as a crisis of the Liver, Kidney, and Spleen systems - the very organs that sustain a healthy pregnancy. During gestation, the body's Yin and Blood are heavily drawn upon to nourish the growing baby. When the reserves of Kidney and Liver Yin are already low, this extra demand depletes them further. Without enough cooling, moistening Yin, a dry, unanchored heat rises. This is the root of the high blood pressure, dizziness, and headaches seen in patterns like Kidney and Liver Yin Deficiency and Liver Yang Rising.
The Spleen is equally central. It transforms food and fluids into the energy and blood that feed the fetus. When Spleen Qi is weak, it cannot manage the body's water. Fluids accumulate and overflow, causing the widespread swelling, bloating, and scanty urine of Spleen Deficiency with Dampness. If this dampness congeals further, it forms turbid Phlegm that clouds the head and chest, adding a heavy, foggy sensation.
Another pathway involves Blood Stagnation. The uterus and placenta need a generous, unobstructed flow of blood. If that flow becomes sluggish or blocked - often due to emotional stress or cold - the vessels resist, pressure rises, and the baby's nourishment is compromised. This pattern explains the sharp, fixed pains and the dusky tongue that often accompany severe preeclampsia.
Because these root imbalances can coexist, a single pregnant woman may show signs of both Yin deficiency and rising Yang, or Spleen weakness with secondary Phlegm, which is why TCM treatment is always personalized.

### การวินิจฉัยโดยแพทย์แผนจีน (Practitioner Diagnosis)
A TCM practitioner begins by asking about the nature of your dizziness, swelling, and blood pressure changes. They want to know if the headache is dull and empty-feeling or sharp and pounding, whether swelling is puffy all over or just in the ankles, and if you feel irritable, heavy, or breathless. The answers point toward an underlying pattern of imbalance.
If dizziness and a flushed face come with night sweats, a dry mouth, and a feeling of heat in the palms and soles, the root is often Kidney and Liver Yin Deficiency. The tongue appears red with little or no coating, and the pulse feels thin and rapid. This pattern is the foundation for many preeclampsia cases, as insufficient Yin fails to anchor rising Yang.
When the Yin-deficient foundation allows Liver Yang to surge upward, the headache becomes severe and throbbing, accompanied by irritability, ringing in the ears, and a red face. The tongue is red with a yellow coating, and the pulse is wiry and forceful. This Liver Yang Rising pattern explains the high blood pressure and agitation that often appear suddenly.
If the main complaint is whole-body puffiness, abdominal bloating, poor appetite, and scanty urine, the problem lies in the Spleen’s inability to transform fluids. The tongue looks pale and swollen with teeth marks and a greasy white coating, while the pulse is soft or slippery. This Spleen Deficiency with Dampness pattern accounts for the pronounced edema and digestive sluggishness.
When the blood flow in the uterine vessels is obstructed, you may notice stabbing pains in the lower abdomen or chest, a dark or purplish complexion, and possibly restricted fetal growth. The tongue is dark red or purple with stasis spots, and the pulse feels choppy or wiry. This Blood Stagnation pattern directly affects placental perfusion and requires careful differentiation.
In more acute flare-ups, intense Liver Fire erupts, causing a dramatic spike in blood pressure, severe agitation, a bitter taste in the mouth, and bloodshot eyes. The tongue is red with a thick yellow coating, and the pulse is wiry and rapid. This Liver Fire Blazing pattern is a fiery escalation of Liver Yang and demands prompt attention to cool the Fire and calm the spirit.
When Dampness congeals into turbid Phlegm, you may feel chest oppression, a heavy sensation in the head, and cough up sticky sputum. The tongue coating is thick and greasy, and the pulse is slippery. This less common Phlegm pattern can complicate other imbalances and is often seen in those with a tendency toward fluid retention and sluggish digestion.

### Kidney and Liver Yin Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/kidney-and-liver-yin-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Soreness and weakness of the lower back and knees, Dizziness, dry eyes, and ringing in the ears, Night sweats and heat in palms, soles, and chest, Red tongue with cracks and scanty coating
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Stress, anger, or frustration, Spicy or greasy foods, Overwork and lack of sleep, Hot weather or overheating
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Rest and quiet, Cool, quiet environment, Cooling foods and drinks, Gentle walking or movement
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  During pregnancy, the body's Yin and Blood are drawn upon to nourish the growing baby. When the reserves of the Kidney and Liver are already low, this extra demand depletes Yin further. Without enough cooling, moistening Yin, a dry, unanchored heat rises, causing dizziness, dry mouth, and the sensation of warmth in the palms and soles. This deficiency is often the root that allows Liver Yang to flare upward, triggering the hypertension of preeclampsia.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red, thin body with cracks, little or no coating, dry surface. Pulse: fine, rapid, and wiry; left Chi (Kidney) position weak.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Yin is the body's cooling, moistening substance. Heat, whether from hot food, weather, or emotional fire, consumes Yin further, making the dryness and heat symptoms worse. Rest, quiet, and cooling foods allow the body to conserve and slowly rebuild its Yin reserves, calming the deficiency heat that underlies this pattern.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat cooling, moistening foods like pears, black sesame, goji berries, and cucumber. Avoid spicy, fried, and overly heating foods. Prioritize rest and gentle movement such as prenatal yoga, and avoid overwork and late nights to preserve Yin.

### Liver Yang Rising
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/liver-yang-rising)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Throbbing or distending headache, especially at temples, Dizziness and feeling of heaviness in the head, Irritability and quick temper, Flushed face and red eyes, Ringing in the ears (tinnitus), Dry mouth and throat
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Stress, anger, or frustration, Spicy or greasy foods, Hot weather or overheating, Overwork and lack of sleep, Caffeine or alcohol
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Rest and quiet, Cooling foods and drinks, Cold compresses on forehead, Gentle walking or movement
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  During pregnancy, the body's Yin and Blood naturally concentrate to nourish the fetus. If Kidney and Liver Yin are already insufficient, they fail to anchor the Liver Yang, allowing it to surge upward like steam rising from a pot with too little water. This upward rush of Yang brings heat and pressure to the head, causing the severe hypertension, throbbing headache, and dizziness that define preeclampsia.
The rising Yang disturbs the mind, leading to irritability and insomnia, while the underlying Yin deficiency contributes to lower back and knee soreness. If unchecked, this internal wind and heat can threaten both mother and baby.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red body with redder sides, thin dry yellow or scanty coat. Pulse: wiry (弦 xián), possibly fine and rapid.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Stress and anger directly surge Liver Yang upward, worsening hypertension and headache. Heat from spicy foods or hot weather adds to the internal heat, further agitating Yang. Rest, cooling foods, and a calm environment help anchor the Yang and nourish Yin, reducing upward flare.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Prioritize rest and avoid stressful situations. Eat cooling, light foods like celery, cucumber, and mung beans. Avoid spicy, greasy, and fried foods, as well as alcohol and caffeine. Gentle activities like prenatal yoga or walking can help, but avoid overheating.

### Spleen Deficiency with Dampness
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/spleen-deficiency-with-dampness)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Marked swelling of the ankles, feet, and hands, Abdominal bloating that worsens after eating, Feeling of heaviness in the body and limbs, Poor appetite or lack of taste, Loose or unformed stools, Fatigue and lack of energy, Scanty urination
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Cold or raw foods and drinks, Greasy, heavy meals, Damp environments or weather, Overwork and lack of sleep
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warm, cooked meals, Rest with legs elevated, Gentle walking or movement, Ginger tea
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  During pregnancy, the Spleen works hard to provide nourishment for the growing baby. When the Spleen is weak, it cannot properly transform and transport fluids. This causes water to accumulate, leading to the widespread swelling, abdominal bloating, and scanty urine typical of this pattern. The retained Dampness creates a sensation of heaviness and can obstruct the normal upward and downward movement of Qi, contributing to rising blood pressure.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale, swollen body with teeth marks and a white, greasy coating; the surface is moist. Pulse: soggy (Ru) and slowed-down (Huan), often soft and lacking force, especially at the right Guan position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Cold and raw foods further weaken the Spleen's digestive fire, making it harder to transform fluids and worsening Dampness. Warm, cooked foods and ginger tea provide the gentle warmth the Spleen needs to function. Rest and elevation help drain accumulated fluids downward, while gentle movement prevents Qi from stagnating, which otherwise allows Dampness to settle.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat small, frequent meals of warm, cooked foods like congee, soups, and steamed vegetables. Avoid cold, raw, and greasy foods, as well as dairy, which can worsen Dampness. Gentle daily walking can help move Qi and fluids. Rest with your legs elevated when possible to ease swelling.

### Blood Stagnation
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/blood-stagnation)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Fixed, stabbing pain in the lower back or abdomen, Dark purplish tongue with stasis spots, Dark or dusky lips, Pain that worsens at night or with pressure, Dark circles under the eyes
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Cold weather or drafts, Cold or raw foods and drinks, Prolonged sitting, Stress, anger, or frustration
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warm compress on lower back, Gentle walking or movement, Warm, cooked meals, Rest with legs elevated
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  During pregnancy, the body's demand for blood increases dramatically. If the flow becomes sluggish or obstructed, especially in the vessels that nourish the uterus, the placenta doesn't receive enough nourishment.
This stagnation creates resistance in the blood vessels, raising blood pressure, and can lead to proteinuria and poor fetal growth.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: dark purple or dusky body with possible stasis spots; distended, dark sublingual veins. Pulse: choppy (涩, sè) or wiry (弦, xián).
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Cold constricts the vessels, making blood flow even more sluggish, so warmth and gentle movement help open the channels. Emotional stress tightens the Liver Qi, which further impedes blood circulation, so calming practices are essential. Rest with feet elevated helps venous return and reduces stasis in the lower body.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat warm, cooked foods like stews and soups with mild spices to encourage blood circulation. Avoid cold, raw foods and icy drinks, which can congeal blood. Gentle walking or prenatal yoga helps keep Qi and blood moving smoothly. Avoid prolonged sitting or standing.

### Phlegm
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/phlegm)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Heavy, foggy sensation in the head, Chest oppression and fullness, Thick white greasy tongue coating, Slippery pulse, Nausea or vomiting of sticky fluids
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Damp environments or weather, Greasy, heavy meals, Dairy products, Cold or raw foods and drinks, Lack of movement
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warm, cooked meals, Ginger tea, Gentle walking or movement, Warm, dry environment
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When the Spleen is weakened during pregnancy, it struggles to transform and transport fluids. These fluids accumulate, congeal, and form turbid Phlegm that settles in the chest and middle burner. This Phlegm obstructs the smooth flow of Qi, contributing to a heavy, foggy head, chest oppression, and a rise in blood pressure as the body's normal upward-downward circulation is blocked.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: swollen pale body with teeth marks, thick white greasy coating, moist. Pulse: slippery (Hua), like beads rolling under the fingers.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Phlegm forms when the Spleen is too weak to process fluids, and Dampness accumulates. Cold, raw, and greasy foods further burden the Spleen and add more Dampness, thickening Phlegm. Warm, cooked foods and ginger tea gently support Spleen Yang, helping it transform and transport fluids. Gentle movement stimulates Qi circulation, preventing stagnation that allows Phlegm to settle. A damp environment introduces external Dampness, worsening the internal condition.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat warm, cooked foods like congee, soups, and steamed vegetables. Avoid cold, raw, greasy, and dairy-rich foods that create Dampness. Sip ginger tea to support Spleen Yang. Gentle daily walking helps keep Qi and fluids moving, and staying in a dry, warm environment is ideal.

## คำถามที่พบบ่อย (Frequently Asked Questions)
### Is TCM safe during pregnancy?
Yes, when prescribed by a qualified TCM practitioner who specializes in pregnancy care. Many herbs and acupuncture points are entirely safe and have been used for centuries to support maternal health. However, certain herbs that strongly move blood or drain downward are avoided because they could stimulate contractions. Always inform your practitioner that you are pregnant, and never self-prescribe herbs. Your TCM treatment should run alongside - not in place of - your regular prenatal checkups.

### Can acupuncture help lower my blood pressure in preeclampsia?
Acupuncture can be a helpful supportive tool, especially for patterns like Liver Yang Rising where calming the Liver and subduing Yang is key. Points such as Taichong (LR-3) and Fengchi (GB-20) are used to settle upward-rushing Qi and ease tension. While acupuncture alone is not a replacement for antihypertensive medication, many women find it reduces headache intensity, irritability, and overall stress, which can contribute to better blood pressure control. Always keep your medical team informed about any complementary treatments you are receiving.

### What herbs are used for preeclampsia, and are they safe for the baby?
Herbal formulas are chosen strictly according to your TCM pattern. For Liver Yang Rising, a classic formula like Tian Ma Gou Teng Yin uses herbs such as Gastrodia (Tian Ma) and Uncaria (Gou Teng) to calm the Liver and anchor Yang. For Spleen Deficiency with Dampness, formulas like Si Jun Zi Tang with added diuretic herbs like Fu Ling and Yi Yi Ren gently strengthen the Spleen and drain excess fluid. These herbs are considered safe when prescribed in pregnancy-appropriate doses by an experienced practitioner. Formulas that strongly invigorate blood, like Xue Fu Zhu Yu Tang, are used only with extreme caution and close monitoring, as they can affect placental blood flow. Your practitioner will select and adjust the formula with the baby's safety as the top priority.

### How quickly can I expect results from TCM treatment?
Many women notice a reduction in swelling and headaches within the first two weeks. Blood pressure improvements can take a bit longer, often 2-4 weeks for excess patterns and 6-12 weeks for deeper deficiency patterns. Progress is gradual and cumulative, and it depends on how consistently you take your herbs and attend acupuncture sessions. Because preeclampsia can change rapidly, your TCM practitioner will work closely with you and your obstetrician to monitor your response and adjust the plan as needed.

### Can I combine TCM with my prescribed blood pressure medication?
Yes, TCM is often used alongside conventional medications. Herbs and acupuncture are generally complementary and do not interfere with drugs like labetalol or nifedipine. However, some herbs have mild diuretic or blood-moving effects, so your blood pressure should be tracked carefully. Always give your TCM practitioner a full list of your medications, and tell your obstetrician about any herbs or supplements you are taking. Never stop or adjust your prescribed medication without consulting your doctor.

### Does TCM treat the root cause or just the symptoms of preeclampsia?
TCM aims to treat the root imbalance that is driving your symptoms. For example, if your preeclampsia stems from Kidney and Liver Yin Deficiency, the herbs and acupuncture points are chosen to deeply nourish Yin and anchor Yang - not just to mask the headache. This means treatment continues even after blood pressure stabilizes, to rebuild your body's reserves and reduce the chance of recurrence. The goal is to create a healthier internal environment for the remainder of your pregnancy.
