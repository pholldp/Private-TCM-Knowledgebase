## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนปัจจุบัน (Conventional Medicine View)
### คำอธิบาย (Description)
In Western medicine, facial itching (pruritus) is often a symptom of dermatological conditions such as eczema, contact dermatitis, psoriasis, seborrheic dermatitis, or allergies. It can also be caused by dry skin (xerosis), sun exposure, or systemic conditions like liver or kidney disease. Diagnosis typically involves a physical exam, medical history, and sometimes patch testing or blood work to identify underlying causes.

### การรักษาหลัก (Treatments)
Topical treatments include corticosteroid creams, calcineurin inhibitors, and emollients. Oral antihistamines are often used for allergic itching, and in some cases, systemic medications like immunosuppressants or biologics may be prescribed. Phototherapy is another option for chronic conditions.

## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนจีน (Traditional Chinese Medicine View)
### ความเข้าใจเกี่ยวกับการแพทย์แผนจีน (TCM Understanding)
TCM sees the face as a mirror of the body's internal environment, directly connected to the Lungs (which govern the skin), the Stomach, and the Liver channel. The face is also the most exposed part of the body, making it vulnerable to external pathogens like Wind, Heat, Cold, and Dryness. When these invaders get trapped in the skin, they disrupt the flow of Qi and Blood, causing itching that can be sudden, red, and sensitive to temperature changes.
But not all facial itching comes from the outside. Internal imbalances - such as Damp-Heat brewing in the Liver and Gallbladder or a Spleen too weak to manage fluids - can send heat, dampness, or dryness rising to the face. The type of itch tells a story: a burning, red itch that worsens with heat points to Wind-Heat; a sticky, oozing itch with a bitter taste in the mouth suggests Damp-Heat in the Liver channel; a dry, flaking itch that's worse at night often signals Blood Deficiency, where the skin lacks nourishment.
This is why the same Western diagnosis of "facial itching" can have completely different TCM treatments. A corticosteroid cream might calm the skin temporarily, but it doesn't clear dampness, nourish blood, or expel wind. TCM aims to match the treatment to the root pattern, using herbs and acupuncture to restore balance so the skin can heal from within.

### การวินิจฉัยโดยแพทย์แผนจีน (Practitioner Diagnosis)
A TCM practitioner starts by asking what the itching feels like-dry, sticky, burning, or crawling-and when it flares. The quality of the sensation and the time of day, season, or triggers are the first big clues. They also ask about thirst, digestion, and energy levels, because the face reflects deeper organ balances.
If the itching is sudden, with a red face and a burning sensation that worsens with heat or spicy food, the practitioner suspects Wind-Heat invading the skin. The tongue is red with a thin yellow coating, and the pulse feels floating and rapid-classic signs of external heat stirring the blood.
When the itch is persistent, with oozing or crusting after scratching and a bitter taste in the mouth, Damp-Heat in the Liver Channel is the likely pattern. The tongue coating is thick, greasy, and yellow, while the pulse is slippery and rapid, reflecting dampness and heat trapped inside.
A dry, flaking itch that worsens at night and often appears in older adults points to Blood Deficiency with External Wind. The skin lacks moisture because blood is insufficient to nourish it. The tongue looks pale or slightly red with little coating, and the pulse is thin and rapid.
If the face feels sticky, with a heavy sensation and a dull itch, Spleen Deficiency with Dampness may be the root. A pale, swollen tongue with a greasy white coating and a weak pulse show that the Spleen’s digestive power (Spleen Qi) isn’t managing fluids, allowing dampness to surface on the face.
Itching that flares in cold weather and improves with warmth suggests Wind-Cold. The complexion is pale, and the tongue body is pale with a thin white coating. The pulse feels floating and tight, reflecting cold constraining the surface and blocking the normal flow.
When the face is dry and scaly, with a fine powdery rash and a dry mouth and nose, Dry-Wind is the cause. This often appears in autumn or after long illness. The tongue may be red with little coating, and the pulse is floating and rapid, showing that wind-dryness has invaded the surface and depleted body fluids.

## กลุ่มอาการการแพทย์แผนจีนและการรักษา (TCM Patterns and Treatment)

### Wind-Heat
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/wind-heat)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Redness and heat sensation on the face, Itching worse with heat, sun, or spicy food, Thirst and dry mouth, Sore throat or yellow nasal discharge
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Sun exposure, Spicy, greasy, or fried foods, Alcohol, Hot, windy weather, Stress and overwork
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Cool compresses, Chrysanthemum tea, Rest in a cool room, Avoiding spicy food
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Wind-Heat is an external pathogen that invades the body's surface, and the face is often the first place it strikes because it is the most exposed area. The Heat irritates the skin and blood vessels, causing redness and a hot, itchy sensation that worsens with warmth. The Wind component makes the itch come and go, moving around the face. This acute reaction often follows exposure to hot, windy weather or a diet rich in spicy, greasy foods.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red tip and edges with a thin yellow coating. Pulse: floating and rapid.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Wind-Heat is an external pathogen that thrives on warmth and wind. Heat expands blood vessels and irritates the skin, while Wind carries the pathogen to the surface. Cooling measures and wind-dispelling herbs neutralize these factors, calming the itch. Spicy and greasy foods generate internal Heat, feeding the condition, so avoiding them helps the body clear the invasion.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Stay cool and avoid prolonged sun exposure. Drink plenty of water and cooling teas like chrysanthemum or mint. Avoid spicy, greasy, and fried foods, as well as alcohol, which add Heat. Use a gentle, fragrance-free cleanser and keep your face shielded from wind.

### Damp-Heat in the Liver Channel
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/damp-heat-in-the-liver-channel)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Itch is sticky and persistent, with oozing or crusting after scratching, Bitter taste in the mouth, Redness and warmth on the face, Pain or fullness along the ribs, Dark scanty urine
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Alcohol, Spicy, greasy, or fried foods, Hot, humid weather, Anger or frustration
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Cooling, light diet, Cool environment, Stress reduction
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Damp-Heat brews in the Liver and Gallbladder, then rises along the channel to the face. The dampness makes the itch sticky and persistent, often with weeping or crusting after scratching. The heat adds redness and a burning sensation, while the bitter taste and rib fullness reflect the underlying Liver disharmony.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red body with redder sides, yellow greasy coating thicker at center and root. Pulse: wiry, slippery, and rapid.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Damp-Heat thrives on rich, greasy, and hot foods, as well as emotional stress that stagnates Liver Qi and generates heat. Cooling, light foods and a calm environment help drain dampness and clear heat, directly counteracting the pattern. Alcohol is especially inflammatory because it is both heating and damp-producing.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Avoid greasy, fried, and spicy foods, as well as alcohol, which all generate damp-heat. Eat cooling, damp-draining foods like mung beans, bitter gourd, celery, and cucumber. Light exercise and stress management help prevent Liver Qi stagnation.

### Blood Deficiency with External Wind
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/blood-deficiency-with-external-wind)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Dry, flaky, or rough skin, Itching worse at night, Pale or sallow complexion, Aversion to wind or drafts, Dizziness or light-headedness
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Windy weather, Dry air or dry, windy weather, Nighttime, Spicy, greasy, or fried foods, Stress and overwork
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Applying moisturizer, Rest and good sleep, Warmth and wind protection, Gentle facial massage
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When the body's blood is chronically deficient, the skin loses its nourishment and moisture. The face becomes dry and flaky, and the dryness generates internal wind, which produces itching that comes and goes or shifts location.
Because the skin's protective layer is weak, external wind easily invades and triggers sudden itching spells. The itching often worsens at night, when blood circulation naturally slows and yin energy is dominant, further depriving the skin of moisture.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale, thin body with a thin white and slightly dry coating. Pulse: fine (thin), wiry on the left middle position, floating on the right front position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Blood deficiency creates internal dryness and wind, so wind exposure and dry environments worsen itching. Nighttime is yin, when blood is relatively more deficient, so itching intensifies. Spicy foods dry out the blood further. Rest and sleep help the body rebuild blood, while moisturizing and protecting from wind compensate for the skin's weakened defenses. Gentle massage moves blood without overstimulating wind.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat blood-nourishing foods like black sesame, red dates, spinach, and liver. Avoid spicy, drying foods and alcohol. Protect your face from wind and cold by wearing a scarf. Use a gentle moisturizer to keep skin hydrated, and try to get enough sleep to help rebuild blood.

### Spleen Deficiency with Dampness
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/spleen-deficiency-with-dampness)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Sticky, heavy itch on the face, Puffy or sallow complexion, Poor appetite and loose stools, Feeling of heaviness in body and limbs, Worse after eating greasy or cold foods
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Spicy, greasy, or fried foods, Cold foods and drinks, Damp, humid weather, Overeating, Sedentary lifestyle
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warm, light meals, Dry weather, Gentle walking, Rest after meals
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When Spleen Qi is weak, it fails to transform and transport body fluids. Unprocessed fluids accumulate into dampness, which is heavy, sticky, and tends to rise. This dampness surfaces on the face, causing a lingering, dull itch that feels worse in humid weather or after eating greasy foods. The face may appear slightly puffy and sallow, and the itch is not red or hot, but heavy and sticky.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale and swollen with teeth marks, white greasy coating, moist. Pulse: soggy (Ru) and slowed-down (Huan), possibly slippery.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Cold and greasy foods, overeating, and damp environments further weaken Spleen Qi and create more dampness, which rises to the face and worsens the itch. Warm, dry, and moderate habits strengthen the Spleen’s ability to transform fluids, reducing dampness and calming the skin.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat warm, cooked foods like congee, soups, and steamed vegetables. Avoid cold, raw, greasy, and sweet foods. Small, frequent meals are easier on the Spleen. Gentle daily movement like walking helps Qi flow and dries dampness.

### Wind-Cold
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/wind-cold)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Itching worsens with cold exposure, Itching improves with warmth, Pale or slightly red face, no true redness, Chills and aversion to cold, Clear runny nose, sneezing
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Cold wind or air conditioning, Cold foods and drinks, Sudden temperature drops
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warmth, like a warm washcloth, Staying indoors away from wind, Warm drinks, like ginger tea
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Wind-Cold is an external pathogen that attacks the body's surface, especially the exposed face. It blocks the flow of defensive Qi (Wei Qi) in the skin, trapping it and creating an itch that feels like tiny pinpricks. Because Cold contracts and tightens the pores, the itch flares up in chilly or windy conditions and eases when warmth relaxes the skin and restores circulation.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: normal or slightly pale body with a thin, white, moist coating. Pulse: floating and tight.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Wind-Cold tightens the skin and blocks the flow of defensive Qi, which triggers itching. Warmth relaxes the pores and disperses the pathogen, while cold wind and cold foods reinforce the contraction and worsen the itch.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Keep your face protected from cold wind with a scarf. Sip warm ginger tea daily to gently push out Wind-Cold. Avoid raw, cold foods and icy drinks that can trap the pathogen inside.

### Dry-Wind
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/dry-wind)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Dry, scaly, or flaky facial skin, Itching worse in dry weather or heated rooms, Dry mouth, nose, and throat, Mild aversion to wind or cold, Low-grade fever or slight sweating
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Dry air or dry, windy weather, Heated indoor air, Spicy, greasy, or fried foods, Smoking or alcohol, Overwork and late nights
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Applying moisturizer, Using a humidifier, Drinking warm water or pear juice, Cool, moist weather, Gentle rest
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When external Dryness combines with Wind, it invades the skin of the face, consuming its natural moisture. This leaves the skin parched, scaly, and unable to protect itself, so even mild air movement triggers a dry, flaky itch. The pattern often appears in autumn or in heated indoor environments, especially in people who already tend toward dryness.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: slightly red body with a dry surface and a thin, white, dry coating, possibly with fine cracks. Pulse: floating and rapid, with the right cun position relatively stronger.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Dry-Wind depletes the fluids that keep skin supple and protected. Moisturizers and humidifiers directly replenish that lost moisture, while hydrating foods and rest help the body generate its own fluids. Dry air, wind, and heating pull more moisture from the skin, and spicy or fried foods generate internal heat that further consumes fluids, making the itch worse.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Use a humidifier in dry rooms and apply a gentle, fragrance-free moisturizer to your face daily. Eat hydrating foods like pear, snow fungus soup, and cucumber, while avoiding spicy, fried, or overly dry foods. Keep alcohol and coffee modest, as they can further dry you out. Gentle breathing exercises or walks in fresh, moist air help the Lungs spread fluids to the skin.

## คำถามที่พบบ่อย (Frequently Asked Questions)
### What is causing my face to itch according to TCM?
It depends on the specific pattern. Common causes include an invasion of Wind-Heat (often from sun, wind, or spicy food), Damp-Heat rising from the Liver (linked to stress or rich foods), or Blood Deficiency that leaves the skin dry and undernourished. A TCM practitioner will examine your tongue, pulse, and the quality of the itch to determine the root cause.

### Can acupuncture really help with facial itching?
Yes. Acupuncture can calm the nervous system, reduce inflammation, and regulate the immune response that drives itching. Points on the face and body are chosen to clear heat, dispel wind, or tonify blood depending on your pattern. Many patients feel a reduction in itch intensity after just a few sessions.

### How long will it take for the herbs to stop the itching?
For acute itching caused by Wind-Heat, you may notice relief within a few days. Chronic patterns like Blood Deficiency or Damp-Heat may take 3-6 weeks to show significant improvement. Herbal formulas are taken daily, and it's important to follow the full course even if symptoms improve, to address the underlying imbalance.

### Can I use TCM alongside my steroid cream?
Generally, yes. TCM herbs and acupuncture can be safely combined with topical moisturizers and mild steroid creams. However, always inform both your dermatologist and TCM practitioner about all treatments you're using, as some herbs may interact with systemic medications. Do not stop prescribed creams abruptly without consulting your doctor.

### Will I need to change my diet?
Diet plays a big role in TCM. Across all patterns, it's wise to avoid spicy, greasy, and overly processed foods, which can generate heat and dampness. Alcohol and caffeine can aggravate itching. Your practitioner may also give specific advice - for example, reducing dairy and sugar if Dampness is involved, or eating more moistening foods like pear and spinach if Dryness is the issue.

### Is facial itching related to my emotions or stress?
Absolutely. In TCM, stress and frustration can stagnate Liver Qi, which can transform into Heat or Fire that rises to the face, causing redness and itching. Emotional upset is a common trigger for Damp-Heat in the Liver pattern. Acupuncture and herbs can help smooth the Liver Qi and calm the mind, which often reduces skin symptoms.

### Can TCM treat itching from allergies?
Yes. Allergic reactions that cause facial itching often fall under Wind-Heat or Wind-Cold patterns in TCM. Herbs like Fang Feng and Chan Tui are specifically used to dispel wind and stop itching. By strengthening the body's defensive Qi, TCM may also reduce the frequency of allergic flare-ups over time.

## การวิจัยทางวิทยาศาสตร์สมัยใหม่ (Modern Scientific Research)
1. **Acupuncture for the treatment of itch in atopic dermatitis: a randomized controlled trial** ([ลิงก์](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1398-9995.2011.02779.x))
   *วารสารวิชาการ: Pfab F, et al. Allergy. 2012;67(4):566-73.*
   This RCT compared acupuncture, antihistamine, and placebo in patients with atopic dermatitis. Acupuncture significantly reduced itch intensity and improved quality of life, with effects lasting after treatment ended.

2. **Chinese herbal medicine for atopic eczema: a systematic review** ([ลิงก์](https://doi.org/10.1111/bjd.12142))
   *วารสารวิชาการ: Tan HY, et al. Br J Dermatol. 2013;168(4):724-31.*
   A systematic review of randomized trials evaluating Chinese herbal medicine for atopic eczema. Several formulas, including Xiao Feng San, showed significant improvement in symptoms and reduced steroid use, though trial quality varied.

3. **Acupuncture for chronic urticaria: a systematic review**
   *วารสารวิชาการ: Zhang W, et al. Evid Based Complement Alternat Med. 2016;2016:5194230.*
   This review analyzed multiple RCTs on acupuncture for chronic urticaria. The majority reported that acupuncture reduced itching and wheal formation compared to controls, but the overall evidence was limited by small sample sizes and risk of bias.
