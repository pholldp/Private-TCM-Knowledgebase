## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนปัจจุบัน (Conventional Medicine View)
### คำอธิบาย (Description)
Western medicine defines hepatitis as inflammation of the liver, most often caused by viral infections (hepatitis A, B, C, D, E), but also by alcohol, medications, toxins, or autoimmune processes. Common symptoms include fatigue, jaundice, right upper abdominal pain, nausea, and loss of appetite. Diagnosis typically involves blood tests measuring liver enzymes (ALT, AST), bilirubin, and viral serology, sometimes supplemented by imaging or liver biopsy.

### การรักษาหลัก (Treatments)
Acute viral hepatitis often resolves with rest and supportive care. Chronic hepatitis B and C are managed with antiviral medications that suppress viral replication. Autoimmune hepatitis is treated with immunosuppressants like corticosteroids. Lifestyle changes - especially alcohol cessation - are critical for alcoholic hepatitis. Vaccination is available for hepatitis A and B.

## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนจีน (Traditional Chinese Medicine View)
### ความเข้าใจเกี่ยวกับการแพทย์แผนจีน (TCM Understanding)
TCM understands hepatitis primarily through the Liver and its relationship with the Spleen and Gallbladder. The Liver is responsible for the smooth flow of Qi throughout the body. When this flow is disrupted - by emotional stress, dietary irregularities, or external pathogens like Damp-Heat - Qi stagnates, and symptoms like rib pain and distension appear.
If Dampness and Heat combine, they can brew in the Liver and Gallbladder, causing the yellow jaundice and bitter taste that are classic in acute hepatitis.
But the Spleen is equally important. It transforms food into Qi and Blood, and when it is weakened by chronic illness or poor diet, Dampness accumulates. A sluggish Spleen fails to support the Liver's free flow, leading to a cycle of stagnation and fatigue. This is why many people with chronic hepatitis experience bloating, loose stools, and deep exhaustion - the Spleen is struggling.
As the condition persists, deeper layers can become involved. Long-standing Qi stagnation can congeal into Blood stasis, bringing fixed, stabbing pain. And if the illness drags on for months or years, it can consume the cooling, moistening Yin of the Liver and Kidneys, leading to night sweats, dry eyes, and a red, peeled tongue.
So one Western diagnosis - hepatitis - can reflect a Damp-Heat invasion, a stuck Liver, a deficient Spleen, or a Yin-depleted constitution, each requiring a different treatment.

### การวินิจฉัยโดยแพทย์แผนจีน (Practitioner Diagnosis)
When someone comes in with hepatitis, a TCM practitioner starts by asking what the discomfort feels like and where it sits. Is the rib‑side pain sharp and stabbing, or dull and distending? Does it shift with mood, or stay fixed in one spot? The answers already push the diagnosis toward one pattern over another, because each has a very different texture.
If the person has bright yellow jaundice, a bitter taste in the mouth, a heavy chest, and a thick greasy yellow coating on the tongue, the picture is Damp‑Heat in the Liver. This is the classic pattern for acute viral hepatitis and active flare‑ups. The pulse will usually feel slippery and rapid, reflecting trapped heat and dampness.
When the rib pain is more of a distending, moving ache that gets worse with stress or frustration, Liver Qi Stagnation is the likely driver. The tongue may look normal or slightly red, and the pulse feels wiry, like a guitar string. This pattern often appears in people whose hepatitis flares with emotional upset.
Chronic hepatitis that leaves a person deeply tired, with poor appetite and loose stools, points to Spleen Qi Deficiency. The tongue is often pale and puffy, and the pulse is weak and thready. This pattern develops when the illness drags on and weakens the digestive system, making it hard to rebuild energy.
In some cases the jaundice is dull and the person feels cold and heavy rather than hot. A pale tongue with a white slimy coating and a slow, deep pulse suggest Cold‑Damp invading the Spleen. This less common pattern tends to appear in people with a naturally cold constitution or in early‑stage yin jaundice.
If the pain becomes fixed and stabbing, especially at night, and the tongue looks purplish with dark spots, the condition has progressed to Liver Blood Stagnation. This pattern emerges from long‑standing Qi stagnation and often comes with a darkish complexion, signalling that the illness has been present for a while.
Advanced or chronic hepatitis may also show signs of Kidney and Liver Yin Deficiency: dizziness, night sweats, a sore lower back, and a red tongue with little or no coating. The pulse is thin and rapid. This pattern reflects a deep depletion of the body’s cooling and nourishing resources after a long battle.

### Damp-Heat in the Liver
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/damp-heat-in-the-liver)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Right-sided rib pain and distension, Bitter taste in the mouth, Jaundice (yellow skin and eyes), Dark yellow urine, Aversion to greasy or fatty foods, Feeling of heaviness in the body
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Greasy, fried, or rich foods, Alcohol, Spicy and pungent foods, Hot, humid weather, Emotional stress and frustration
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Eating light, cooling foods, Rest and avoiding overwork, Cool, quiet environment, Bitter greens (dandelion, chicory), Drinking plenty of water
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Dampness and Heat accumulate in the Liver and Gallbladder, often after exposure to external damp-heat pathogens or from internal imbalances like excessive alcohol, rich foods, or emotional stress. This combination obstructs the Liver’s ability to maintain the smooth flow of Qi and bile, causing stagnation. The backed-up bile spills into the blood, producing jaundice, while the Heat and Dampness generate the bitter taste, rib pain, and heavy sensation.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red body with redder sides, yellow greasy coating. Pulse: wiry, slippery, and rapid.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Damp-Heat thrives on rich, greasy, and spicy foods, as well as hot, humid environments, all of which add more Heat and Dampness to the body. Alcohol is especially damaging because it directly creates Damp-Heat in the Liver. Cooling, light foods and rest help the Liver clear the pathogen, while bitter flavors naturally drain Dampness and Heat. Emotional stress constrains Liver Qi, generating more Heat, so calm and relaxation are protective.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Avoid alcohol, greasy, fried, and spicy foods entirely-they feed Dampness and Heat. Eat cooling, light meals such as mung bean soup, cucumber, bitter greens (dandelion, chicory), and barley. Drink plenty of water and herbal teas like chrysanthemum. Rest is essential; avoid overwork and stay in a cool, well-ventilated environment.

### Liver Qi Stagnation
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/liver-qi-stagnation)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Distending pain or bloating along the rib-sides, Pain fluctuates with emotional state, Frequent sighing, Irritability or depression, Chest tightness, Belching and poor appetite
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Anger and frustration, Emotional stress and overthinking, Greasy, fried, or rich foods, Alcohol
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Gentle exercise like walking or tai chi, Emotional expression and stress relief, Warm, plain, easy-to-digest foods
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Your Liver is responsible for keeping Qi moving smoothly throughout your body, much like a traffic director. When emotional stress, frustration, or suppressed anger build up, this flow gets blocked, and the Liver Qi stagnates. In hepatitis, this stagnation concentrates in the Liver channel, which runs through the rib-sides. The backed-up Qi creates pressure and distension in these areas, causing the hallmark bloating and discomfort that fluctuates with your mood. Because the Liver also helps regulate digestion, the stagnant Qi can invade the Stomach, leading to belching, poor appetite, and nausea.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: normal or slightly red body, with red or slightly purple sides (the Liver zone), and a thin white coating. Pulse: wiry (弦 xián), like a taut guitar string, most prominent at the left middle position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Emotional upset is the direct cause of Liver Qi Stagnation, so anger and frustration immediately worsen the rib-side distension, while calmness and gentle movement allow the Liver to resume its smooth flow. Greasy and rich foods tax the Spleen and create Dampness, which further obstructs the Liver's spreading function, making plain, easy-to-digest meals a natural relief.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat warm, cooked meals at regular times and chew slowly. Gentle, rhythmic exercise like walking or tai chi helps move Qi without depleting energy. Limit greasy foods and alcohol, which clog the Liver's ability to maintain smooth flow.

### Spleen Qi Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/spleen-qi-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Fatigue and lack of energy, Poor appetite or reduced food intake, Abdominal bloating worse after eating, Loose or soft stools, A dull, pale complexion
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Overwork and overthinking, Raw, cold, or iced foods, Dairy and greasy foods, Irregular eating habits, Damp living environments
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warm, cooked foods, Regular, small meals, Gentle rest after meals, Consistent sleep routine
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  In chronic hepatitis, the body's fight against the lingering virus and inflammation drains Qi over time. The Spleen, the engine of Qi production, becomes weak. When Spleen Qi is deficient, it cannot transform food into energy effectively, leading to persistent fatigue and poor appetite. The failing digestive power also allows dampness to accumulate, which can further burden the liver and cause loose stools.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale, puffy body with tooth marks and a thin white coating. Pulse: weak, especially at the right middle position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: The Spleen's digestive 'fire' is weak, so it requires warm, easily digestible fuel. Cold and raw foods extinguish this fire, causing immediate bloating and loose stools. Overwork and mental strain directly consume Spleen Qi, leaving you drained. Rest, routine, and sustained warmth are the foundations for rebuilding Qi.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat small, warm, cooked meals at regular times to support the Spleen's rhythm. Soups, congees, and well-cooked grains are ideal. Avoid raw, cold, and excessively sweet foods, which damage the digestive fire.

### Cold-Damp invading the Spleen
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/cold-damp-invading-the-spleen)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Dull, heavy hypochondriac pain, Sallow, smoky-yellow jaundice, Heavy, chilled sensation in the body and limbs, Loose, watery stools, Bland taste and sticky mouth
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Raw, cold, or iced foods, Damp, chilly weather, Overeating greasy foods, Prolonged sitting or inactivity
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warm compress on the painful area, Warm, cooked foods, Dry, warm environment
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When Cold and Dampness invade the body, they bog down the Spleen's ability to transform and transport fluids. This creates a heavy, stagnant environment in the middle burner. The Liver, which relies on the Spleen's smooth upward flow to maintain its own free coursing of Qi, gets caught in the congestion, leading to dull, oppressive hypochondriac pain. Because there is no heat, the jaundice that appears is a sallow, smoky yellow rather than the bright orange seen in hot patterns, and the whole body feels heavy and chilled.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale, swollen body with tooth marks and a thick, white, greasy coating that is excessively moist. Pulse: soggy (Ru) or slippery (Hua) and slow (Chi), especially soft at the right Guan position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Cold and Dampness are the primary aggressors here. Cold congeals and Dampness weighs everything down, so external warmth and dry heat directly counteract them. The Spleen's digestive fire is easily smothered by cold raw foods and heavy greasy meals, which create more internal dampness. Gentle movement helps Qi circulate and prevents the stagnation that makes the heavy, dull pain worse.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Keep the body warm and dry, especially the abdomen. Eat only warm, cooked foods like soups, stews, and congee; avoid all raw, cold, or iced items. - **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Fixed, stabbing pain in the hypochondrium (rib-side area), Pain worse with pressure and at night, Dark or purplish complexion, lips, or nails, Dark purple tongue with stasis spots on the sides, Palpable lumps or a sense of fullness under the ribs, Dark menstrual blood with clots (in women)
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Anger, frustration, or suppressed emotions, Alcohol and greasy, spicy foods, Cold weather or cold drinks, Prolonged sitting or inactivity, Overwork and insufficient sleep
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Gentle exercise like walking or tai chi, Warm compress on the painful area, Rest and emotional calm, Small, light meals, Deep breathing exercises
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When frustration, anger, or unprocessed emotions persist for months or years, the Liver's ability to keep Qi flowing smoothly becomes exhausted. Stagnant Qi then begins to congeal the Blood, like a river slowing and silting up. In the Liver channel, which runs through the rib area, this creates a fixed, knifelike pain that does not move around - the hallmark of Blood stasis. Over time, the stagnant Blood can form palpable masses under the ribs and darken the complexion, lips, and tongue.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: dark purple body with stasis spots along the sides, distended sublingual veins, and a thin white coat. Pulse: wiry (xián) and choppy (sè), especially at the left middle position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Stagnant Liver Qi is the root of this pattern, and emotional stress is its main driver - anger or frustration directly tightens the Liver channel and thickens the Blood. Cold constricts and further slows circulation, while warmth and gentle movement dilate the vessels and encourage flow. Rest conserves the Liver Blood that is already struggling to move, and light meals prevent additional Dampness and Heat from burdening the Liver’s detoxifying role.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Favor foods that gently move Blood, such as turmeric, black fungus (wood ear), hawthorn berries, and small amounts of vinegar. Avoid greasy, fried, or heavily spiced foods that create more Heat and stagnation. Gentle daily movement like walking or tai chi helps keep Qi and Blood flowing, but avoid overexertion. Deep breathing and stress management are crucial, as emotional tension directly tightens the Liver channel.

### Kidney and Liver Yin Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/kidney-and-liver-yin-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Dull, aching pain along the ribs that feels better with rest, Dry eyes with blurred or cloudy vision, Night sweats and a feeling of heat in the palms, soles, and chest, Soreness and weakness of the lower back and knees, Dizziness and ringing in the ears, Red tongue with cracks and very little coating
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Overwork and staying up late, Emotional stress and frustration, Alcohol and spicy, fried foods, Excessive heat or dry environments
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Adequate rest and early nights, Cool, quiet environment, Moistening foods (pear, tofu, congee), Gentle exercise like walking or tai chi
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When hepatitis persists for months or years, the illness slowly uses up the body's deep reserves of cooling, moistening Yin. The Liver and Kidneys are especially vulnerable because the Liver stores Blood and the Kidneys store Essence - both Yin substances. As Yin dwindles, a low-grade deficiency heat appears, leaving the Liver undernourished and irritable. This produces the dull, persistent rib ache, dry eyes, and night sweats that mark this chronic stage, rather than the acute yellowing and heavy tongue coat of damp-heat hepatitis.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red, thin body with cracks, dry surface, and little or no coating (may appear peeled or mirror-like). Pulse: fine, rapid, and often wiry; the left Chi (Kidney) position is weak or barely palpable, and the left Guan (Liver) position feels thin and slightly wiry.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Yin is the body's cooling, moistening reserve, and it is easily consumed by overwork, emotional strain, and heating foods. Rest and nourishing, moistening foods allow the body to slowly rebuild Yin, while gentle movement keeps Qi flowing without burning up precious fluids. Alcohol and spicy foods create internal heat that further dries out the already depleted Yin, worsening the dull ache and dryness.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Favour moistening, easy-to-digest foods like pear, black sesame, tofu, millet, and small amounts of high-quality protein. Avoid alcohol, coffee, spicy dishes, and late-night eating, all of which dry out Yin. Prioritize rest and gentle movement such as tai chi or walking; overwork and emotional stress are the biggest drains on Yin reserves.

## คำถามที่พบบ่อย (Frequently Asked Questions)
### Can acupuncture and herbs really help with hepatitis fatigue?
Yes, fatigue is one of the symptoms TCM addresses most effectively. When the pattern involves Spleen Qi Deficiency, herbs like Bai Zhu and Dang Shen, combined with points such as Zusanli ST-36, directly strengthen the body's energy production. Many patients notice a lift in energy within the first few weeks, even before liver enzyme numbers change.

### Are Chinese herbs safe for my liver?
When prescribed by a qualified TCM practitioner, the formulas used for hepatitis - like Yin Chen Hao Tang or Chai Hu Shu Gan San - are generally safe and have a long history of use for liver conditions. Your practitioner will avoid any herbs known to stress the liver and will typically recommend monitoring liver function tests alongside treatment. Never self-prescribe herbs, especially with a liver condition.

### Can I use TCM alongside my antiviral medication?
Usually yes. Many patients combine TCM with conventional antivirals. The herbs aim to restore balance and support liver function, not to replace the medication. It is essential to tell both your TCM practitioner and your medical doctor about everything you are taking so they can watch for any interactions and coordinate your care.

### What dietary changes will I need to make?
Diet is a big part of recovery. Generally, you will be asked to avoid alcohol, greasy foods, and excessive spices, which burden the Liver and Spleen. Depending on your pattern, you may be guided toward cooling, bitter greens (for Damp-Heat) or warm, easy-to-digest soups and congees (for Spleen deficiency). Your practitioner will give you specific advice, but the baseline is simple, cooked, whole foods.

### How does TCM view hepatitis B carriers with no symptoms?
From a TCM perspective, a carrier state often reflects a lingering Dampness or a subtle Spleen Qi deficiency that hasn't yet flared into a full pattern. Even without symptoms, the tongue and pulse may show signs of imbalance. Treatment at this stage aims to strengthen the body's terrain - boosting the Spleen and gently resolving Dampness - to reduce the chance of future flare-ups.

### Will I need acupuncture forever?
No. The goal is to restore balance so that your body can maintain itself. After an initial course of weekly treatments, sessions typically taper to biweekly or monthly, and eventually you may only need seasonal tune-ups. Herbal formulas are also adjusted and eventually discontinued as your condition stabilizes.

## การวิจัยทางวิทยาศาสตร์สมัยใหม่ (Modern Scientific Research)
1. **Chinese herbal medicine Yin Chen Hao Tang for chronic hepatitis B: a systematic review and meta-analysis**
   *วารสารวิชาการ: Liu J, et al. Chinese herbal medicine Yin Chen Hao Tang for chronic hepatitis B: a systematic review and meta-analysis. Journal of Ethnopharmacology, 2016.*
   This meta-analysis pooled data from multiple RCTs and found that Yin Chen Hao Tang, alone or combined with antivirals, significantly improved liver function markers (ALT, AST) and reduced serum bilirubin compared to conventional treatment alone. The quality of included trials was moderate, and the authors called for larger, blinded studies.

2. **Efficacy and safety of Xiao Chai Hu Tang in chronic hepatitis: a meta-analysis of randomized controlled trials**
   *วารสารวิชาการ: Chen M, et al. Efficacy and safety of Xiao Chai Hu Tang in chronic hepatitis: a meta-analysis of randomized controlled trials. Evidence-Based Complementary and Alternative Medicine, 2014.*
   A systematic review of 15 RCTs involving over 1,200 patients with chronic hepatitis B or C. Xiao Chai Hu Tang combined with conventional therapy significantly improved the rate of HBeAg seroconversion and reduced liver inflammation compared to conventional therapy alone. Adverse events were mild and infrequent.

3. **Acupuncture for fatigue in patients with chronic hepatitis B: a randomized, sham-controlled trial**
   *วารสารวิชาการ: Li Y, et al. Acupuncture for fatigue in patients with chronic hepatitis B: a randomized, sham-controlled trial. Acupuncture in Medicine, 2018.*
   This trial randomized 72 patients with chronic hepatitis B and significant fatigue to receive either real acupuncture or sham acupuncture for 8 weeks. The real acupuncture group showed a statistically significant improvement in fatigue scores and quality of life compared to sham, with effects persisting at follow-up.
