## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนปัจจุบัน (Conventional Medicine View)
### คำอธิบาย (Description)
In Western medicine, prominent veins under the tongue are often considered a benign anatomical variant, sometimes called sublingual varices or caviar tongue when they appear dark and dilated. They are more common with age and may be associated with high blood pressure, cardiovascular disease, or other circulatory conditions, though many people have them without any underlying disease. Diagnosis is visual, and no routine treatment is offered unless the veins bleed or cause discomfort.

### การรักษาหลัก (Treatments)
Conventional treatment is generally not required for asymptomatic sublingual varices. If they bleed or are cosmetically bothersome, laser therapy or sclerotherapy may be used. Management typically focuses on monitoring and addressing any cardiovascular risk factors, such as hypertension, rather than treating the veins themselves.

## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนจีน (Traditional Chinese Medicine View)
### ความเข้าใจเกี่ยวกับการแพทย์แผนจีน (TCM Understanding)
TCM sees the tongue as a mirror of the body's internal state, and the veins underneath it offer a direct window into the health of the blood and vessels. When these sublingual veins become engorged, dark purple, and twisted, it signals that blood is stagnating somewhere in the body. This is not a local problem - it reflects a systemic pattern of sluggish circulation that can affect any organ or tissue.
Blood Stagnation itself can have many origins. The simplest is a direct blockage from physical trauma or long-standing Qi stagnation, where the flow of Qi fails to move blood. But stagnation can also develop because Qi is too deficient to push blood forward, like a weak pump allowing fluid to pool. In other cases, internal heat thickens the blood, making it sticky and prone to clotting. Each root cause produces the same visible sign - distended sublingual veins - but requires a different treatment strategy.
The tongue body and pulse give the practitioner the clues needed to tell these patterns apart. A dark purple tongue body with a choppy pulse points to straightforward Blood Stagnation. A pale tongue with a weak pulse suggests Qi Deficiency is the root. A red tongue with a yellow coat and rapid pulse indicates Heat has entered the blood.
And a red tongue with a wiry, rapid pulse and emotional symptoms reveals that Liver Qi Stagnation has transformed into Heat. By reading these signs, TCM can go beyond the visible vein to correct the underlying imbalance.

### การวินิจฉัยโดยแพทย์แผนจีน (Practitioner Diagnosis)
A practitioner starts by looking at the tongue body and coat, not just the veins underneath. If the tongue body is dark purple or shows stasis spots, and the pulse feels wiry or choppy, that points to straightforward Blood Stagnation. The distended veins are the clearest clue, but the whole tongue tells the story of how long and how deeply the stagnation has settled.
When the tongue body is pale and the person complains of deep fatigue, breathlessness, or weakness, the practitioner suspects Qi Deficiency causing Blood Stagnation. Here the pulse is weak and thin. The distended veins signal that the blood is not moving properly, but the root is a lack of driving Qi rather than a simple blockage.
If the tongue body is red with a yellow coat and the veins look dark purple, the pattern shifts toward Blood Stagnation with Heat. The pulse is often rapid and forceful. The practitioner asks about feelings of heat, thirst, skin eruptions, or a sense of agitation, because these heat signs confirm that the blood is being scorched and congealed.
For Liver Qi Stagnation that transforms into Heat, the practitioner listens for emotional patterns: irritability, frustration, a feeling of a lump in the throat, or rib-side tension. The tongue may be red along the edges with a yellow coat, and the pulse is wiry and rapid. The distended veins here are a downstream effect of constrained Qi generating heat that thickens the blood.

## กลุ่มอาการการแพทย์แผนจีนและการรักษา (TCM Patterns and Treatment)

### Blood Stagnation
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/blood-stagnation)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Fixed, stabbing pain, Pain worse at night, Dark or purplish tongue body with stasis spots, Dark menstrual blood with clots, Dusky complexion or dark circles under the eyes, Rough, dry, scaly skin
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Sedentary lifestyle, Cold and raw foods, Emotional stress and anger, Cold weather, Nighttime
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Gentle exercise, Warmth on the body, Turmeric, ginger, and warming spices, Stress reduction and calm
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When blood does not flow freely, it pools and congeals in the vessels. The veins under the tongue are a mirror of this deep stagnation, becoming visibly engorged, dark purple, and twisted. This sluggish circulation is often driven by Qi stagnation or an old injury that never fully resolved, leaving blood trapped and unable to nourish the tissues.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: dark purple or dusky body, possible stasis spots, distended dark sublingual veins, thin white coat. Pulse: choppy (涩 sè) like a knife scraping bamboo, or wiry (弦 xián) when Qi stagnation is involved.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Blood is a warm, flowing substance; cold and stillness cause it to congeal and pool, which is why symptoms worsen with inactivity, cold exposure, and at night when circulation slows. Gentle movement and warmth invigorate circulation and help break up stasis. Emotional stress knots up Liver Qi, which then traps blood, so calming the mind directly relieves the underlying tension that feeds the stagnation.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Gentle daily movement like walking, tai chi, or swimming keeps blood from pooling. Use warming spices-turmeric, ginger, cinnamon, and black pepper-in cooking, and avoid icy drinks and raw foods that congeal blood. Manage emotional tension with breathwork or journaling, and avoid sitting for hours without a break.

### Qi Deficiency causing Blood Stagnation
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/qi-deficiency-causing-blood-stagnation)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Fixed stabbing pain that worsens with pressure, Persistent fatigue and lack of strength, Shortness of breath on exertion, Pale complexion with a greyish or dusky tinge, Numbness or tingling in the limbs, Spontaneous sweating with mild activity
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Overexertion or prolonged standing, Cold and raw foods, Stress and worry, Lack of sleep, Heavy, greasy meals, Sedentary lifestyle
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Gentle exercise, Warm nourishing meals, Adequate rest and sleep, Gentle stretching or yoga, Warmth on the abdomen, Deep breathing exercises
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When Qi is too weak to push blood through the vessels, circulation slows and blood begins to pool. Over time this sluggishness congeals into stasis, making the veins under the tongue enlarge, darken, and twist. This pattern often appears in people who feel chronically drained, and the veins may become more prominent after exertion or long periods of standing.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale body with a dark or purplish tinge, possible purple stasis spots, teeth marks, distended sublingual veins, thin white coating. Pulse: deep (Chen), fine or weak (Xi/Ruo), and choppy (Se) - arriving hesitantly with an uneven, rough sensation.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Qi deficiency leaves blood unmoved, so overexertion further depletes Qi and worsens stagnation. Rest and nourishing food replenish Qi, while gentle movement stimulates circulation without draining energy. Cold foods and stress weaken the Spleen and Liver, impairing Qi production and flow, so warmth and calmness help restore balance.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Gentle daily movement like walking or tai chi helps Qi flow and prevents stagnation without depleting energy. Eat warm, nourishing foods such as bone broth, dates, and goji berries, and include mild blood-moving spices like turmeric in cooking. Avoid overexertion and cold, raw foods that weaken digestion and further drain Qi.

### Blood Stagnation with Heat
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/blood-stagnation-with-heat)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Fixed stabbing pain that worsens at night, Sensation of internal heat, especially at night, Irritability and restlessness, Thirst with desire to rinse mouth but not swallow, Constipation with dry stools
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Spicy, greasy, or fried foods, Alcohol, Anger and frustration, Hot weather or saunas, Overwork and lack of sleep
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Cooling foods and drinks, Gentle exercise, Stress reduction and calm, Cool environment
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Heat thickens and congeals the blood, much like simmering a sauce until it becomes sticky and sluggish. This thickened blood struggles to flow smoothly through the vessels, causing the veins under the tongue to bulge, darken, and become tortuous. The heat component also gives the tongue a reddish hue and a dry yellow coating, while the stagnation makes the sublingual veins appear dark purple.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: reddish-purple body with stasis spots, distended sublingual veins, thin dry yellow coating. Pulse: choppy (涩 sè) and rapid (数 shuò), often wiry (弦 xián) at the left Guan position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Heat thickens the blood and promotes stagnation, so spicy foods, alcohol, and hot environments worsen the condition by adding more heat. Emotional stress stirs up Liver Qi and heat, further congealing blood. Cooling foods, rest, and gentle movement help clear heat and keep blood moving smoothly, relieving the distension.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Avoid spicy, greasy, and fried foods that generate heat. Eat cooling blood-moving foods like mung beans, eggplant, hawthorn, and celery. Stay hydrated and practice gentle exercise like walking or tai chi to promote circulation. Manage stress with meditation or deep breathing.

### Liver Qi Stagnation that transforms into Heat
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/liver-qi-stagnation-that-transforms-into-heat)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Irritability and explosive anger, Bitter taste in the mouth, Distending pain along the ribcage, Red face and eyes, Difficulty falling asleep or vivid dreams
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Anger and frustration, Spicy, greasy, or fried foods, Alcohol and caffeine, Prolonged stress, Overwork and lack of sleep
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Cooling herbal teas (chrysanthemum, mint), Stress reduction and calm, Gentle stretching or yoga, Expressing emotions constructively
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When emotions like anger and frustration are held in for too long, the Liver's flow of Qi becomes stuck. That stagnation eventually generates heat, which then scorches the blood, making it thick and sluggish. The sluggish blood pools and distends the veins under the tongue, while the heat rises to cause irritability, a red face, and a bitter taste.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red body with redder sides and a yellow coating. Pulse: wiry and rapid (弦数, xián shuò).
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Anger and emotional stress trap Liver Qi, which then generates heat and thickens the blood. This heat-stagnation directly causes the veins to bulge. Cooling foods and herbs clear heat, while relaxation and gentle movement help the Liver Qi flow smoothly, preventing stasis. Avoiding alcohol and spicy foods removes sources of heat, and adequate rest allows the Liver to recover.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat cooling, light foods like cucumber, celery, and chrysanthemum tea. Avoid spicy, greasy, and fried foods, and limit alcohol and caffeine. Practice regular stress management such as meditation, deep breathing, or gentle yoga. ## คำถามที่พบบ่อย (Frequently Asked Questions)
### What do distended veins under the tongue mean in Chinese medicine?
They are a classic sign of Blood Stagnation - a pattern where blood is not circulating smoothly and begins to pool and congeal in the vessels. The veins become engorged, dark, and twisted because the blood is sluggish. This is not a disease by itself, but a clue that points to an underlying imbalance that can affect many aspects of health, from pain to menstrual problems.

### Can TCM really make these veins go away?
Yes, in many cases the veins can become noticeably less prominent, lighter in color, and less tortuous as the underlying blood stasis resolves. This improvement is gradual and reflects deeper changes in circulation. However, the goal of TCM is not just to change the appearance of the veins, but to restore healthy blood flow throughout the body, which often leads to relief from other symptoms like pain, headaches, or menstrual cramps.

### How long does it take for herbs and acupuncture to reduce the veins?
Visible changes in the sublingual veins typically take 2-4 months of consistent treatment. Excess patterns like simple Blood Stagnation or Blood Stagnation with Heat may respond more quickly, while deficiency-based patterns like Qi Deficiency causing Blood Stagnation often require 3-6 months to rebuild Qi and move blood. Many patients feel systemic improvements - less pain, more energy - within the first few weeks, even before the veins themselves change.

### Is it dangerous to have distended sublingual veins?
In most cases, distended sublingual veins are not dangerous by themselves. However, they can be a sign of underlying circulatory issues that, if left unaddressed, may contribute to more serious blood stasis problems over time. For sudden changes, please refer to the urgent medical care section below. Otherwise, they are a valuable clue that can guide a TCM practitioner to help you restore healthy circulation.

### Do I need to change my diet to help with blood stasis?
Diet can play a supportive role. Warming, blood-invigorating foods like turmeric, ginger, garlic, and dark leafy greens are helpful. Avoid excessive cold or raw foods, which can congeal blood and worsen stagnation. If you have heat signs, emphasize cooling blood-movers like eggplant and chrysanthemum tea. Reducing greasy, fried, and heavily processed foods also helps prevent dampness that can further slow circulation.

### Can acupuncture alone help with distended sublingual veins?
Acupuncture is excellent for moving Qi and blood, and points like Xuehai (SP-10) and Sanyinjiao (SP-6) are specifically chosen to invigorate circulation. While acupuncture alone can start to improve blood flow, the deepest and most lasting changes usually come when it is combined with herbal medicine that works daily to break stasis and nourish any underlying deficiency. Many practitioners recommend both for optimal results.

## การวิจัยทางวิทยาศาสตร์สมัยใหม่ (Modern Scientific Research)
1. **Xue Fu Zhu Yu Tang for stable angina pectoris: a systematic review and meta-analysis**
   *วารสารวิชาการ: Chen J, et al. Xue Fu Zhu Yu Tang for stable angina pectoris: a systematic review and meta-analysis. J Ethnopharmacol. 2017.*
   This systematic review evaluated randomized controlled trials of Xue Fu Zhu Yu Tang, a classic blood-stasis formula, in patients with stable angina. The analysis showed that adding the herbal formula to conventional treatment significantly improved angina symptoms, reduced nitroglycerin use, and improved ECG findings, supporting its use for blood stasis-related chest pain.

2. **Sublingual varices as an indicator of blood stasis syndrome in traditional Chinese medicine: a cross-sectional study**
   *วารสารวิชาการ: Lo LC, et al. Sublingual varices as an indicator of blood stasis syndrome in traditional Chinese medicine: a cross-sectional study. Evid Based Complement Alternat Med. 2012.*
   This study examined the correlation between sublingual varices and TCM blood stasis syndrome in 200 patients. It found a strong association between distended, tortuous sublingual veins and the clinical diagnosis of blood stasis, validating the tongue sign as a reliable diagnostic marker.

3. **Acupuncture for chronic pelvic pain due to blood stasis: a randomized controlled trial**
   *วารสารวิชาการ: Zhang Y, et al. Acupuncture for chronic pelvic pain due to blood stasis: a randomized controlled trial. Chin J Integr Med. 2019.*
   This RCT compared acupuncture to sham acupuncture in 120 women with chronic pelvic pain and a TCM diagnosis of blood stasis. The acupuncture group showed significant reductions in pain intensity and improvements in quality of life, with effects maintained at 3-month follow-up, suggesting acupuncture effectively resolves blood stasis in the pelvic region.
