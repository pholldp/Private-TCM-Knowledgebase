## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนปัจจุบัน (Conventional Medicine View)
### คำอธิบาย (Description)
In conventional medicine, bleeding is classified by its source - arterial, venous, or capillary - and by whether it's external or internal. Common causes include injury, surgery, clotting disorders (like hemophilia), medications (especially blood thinners), and conditions that weaken blood vessels. Diagnosis often relies on blood tests to check clotting factors, platelet counts, and imaging to locate the source of internal bleeding. Treatment ranges from direct pressure and sutures for minor wounds to transfusions, clotting factor replacement, and surgery for severe hemorrhage.

### การรักษาหลัก (Treatments)
Standard treatment depends on the cause and severity. For external bleeding, direct pressure, bandaging, and sometimes sutures or cauterization are used. Internal bleeding may require fluid replacement, blood transfusions, or medications that promote clotting (like tranexamic acid). Patients on anticoagulants may receive reversal agents. Chronic bleeding disorders are managed with clotting factor concentrates, desmopressin, or hormonal therapies for heavy menstrual bleeding.

## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนจีน (Traditional Chinese Medicine View)
### ความเข้าใจเกี่ยวกับการแพทย์แผนจีน (TCM Understanding)
In TCM, blood is more than just a liquid - it's a substance that nourishes every tissue and must be held safely within the vessels by Qi. The Spleen is especially responsible for this 'containing' function, while the Liver stores the blood and the Heart governs its circulation. When any of these systems are disrupted, blood can leak out.
Heat is the most common culprit: when excess Heat enters the Blood level, it agitates the blood so violently that it bursts out of the vessels, causing sudden, bright-red bleeding from the nose, gums, or in the stool or urine. This is the pattern of Heat in the Blood, and it often comes with a feverish feeling, thirst, and a deep red tongue. A related pattern, Liver Fire Blazing, sends Heat and anger surging upward, triggering nosebleeds or vomiting of blood after intense emotional upset.
Deficiency patterns cause bleeding in a different way - not by force, but by weakness. When Spleen Qi is too depleted to hold blood in place, the bleeding is chronic and pale, like easy bruising, spotting, or heavy periods with thin, light-colored blood. This often goes along with fatigue, poor appetite, and a puffy pale tongue. When Yin (the body's cooling, moistening energy) is deficient, a low-grade 'empty heat' develops that slowly damages small vessels, leading to scant, recurrent bleeding - a trickle rather than a flood - often worse at night or with night sweats.
Blood Stagnation is the fourth mechanism. When blood becomes stuck and no longer flows smoothly, pressure builds up behind the blockage until blood forces its way out. The bleeding is dark and clotted, with a fixed, stabbing pain and a purplish tongue. This pattern can coexist with others, making diagnosis nuanced. TCM's strength lies in teasing apart these patterns so treatment can be precisely targeted.

### การวินิจฉัยโดยแพทย์แผนจีน (Practitioner Diagnosis)
A TCM practitioner begins by asking about the nature of the bleeding: its color, volume, speed, and what makes it better or worse. Bright-red, sudden bleeding points toward heat or fire patterns, while pale, slow oozing suggests deficiency. The accompanying symptoms and the look of the tongue and pulse then sharpen the picture.
In Heat in the Blood, the bleeding is often acute and profuse, with bright-red blood. The person may feel hot, thirsty, and restless, and the tongue is typically deep red with a yellow coating. The pulse is rapid and forceful, reflecting the intense heat pushing blood out of the vessels.
Spleen not controlling Blood produces chronic, low-grade bleeding that is pale in color, such as easy bruising, spotting, or pale menstrual blood. This pattern is marked by fatigue, poor appetite, and a weak, thready pulse. The tongue is pale and puffy, indicating the Spleen’s Qi is too weak to hold blood in place.
Empty-Heat caused by Yin Deficiency results in scant but recurrent bleeding, like a trickle of blood or nosebleeds that come and go. The blood is red but not as bright as in full heat. Night sweats, a feeling of heat in the palms and soles, and a red tongue with little coating are classic clues. The pulse is thin and rapid.
Blood Stagnation leads to dark, clotted bleeding with a sense of fixed, stabbing pain. The tongue will show purple spots or a dusky body, and the pulse feels choppy or wiry. This pattern often follows trauma or long-standing emotional stress, and the bleeding may be intermittent but always dark.
Liver Fire Blazing causes sudden, forceful bleeding from the upper body - nosebleeds, vomiting blood, or coughing up blood. The person is typically irritable, with a red face, headaches, and a bitter taste in the mouth. The tongue is red with a yellow coating, and the pulse is wiry and rapid.
Kidney and Liver Yin Deficiency causing Heat in the Blood is a deeper, more chronic pattern. Bleeding is often mild, like blood-tinged sputum or spotting, alongside dizziness, tinnitus, and a sore lower back. The tongue is red and peeled, and the pulse is thin and rapid. This pattern reflects a fundamental lack of cooling, nourishing Yin.

### Heat in the Blood
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/heat-in-the-blood)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Sudden, bright-red bleeding from nose, gums, or in stool/urine, Heavy menstrual periods with bright or dark red blood, Feeling of internal heat, worse at night, Restlessness and irritability, Thirst with desire for cold drinks, Flushed red face and possibly red skin rashes
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Spicy, fried, or greasy foods, Alcohol and coffee, Hot weather or overheated rooms, Emotional stress or anger, Overwork and lack of sleep
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Cooling foods and drinks, Rest in a quiet, cool room, Gentle movement or exercise, Deep breathing to calm the mind
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When Heat enters the Blood level, it agitates the blood, making it move recklessly and violently. This forces blood out of the vessels, leading to sudden, bright-red bleeding from various sites - nose, gums, stool, urine, or heavy periods. The Heat also consumes fluids, causing intense thirst and a dry mouth, and disturbs the mind, resulting in restlessness.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red body with red spots or prickles, thin yellow dry coating. Pulse: rapid (Shu), often wiry (Xian) or slippery (Hua), forceful.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Heat in the Blood is an Excess Heat condition; anything that adds Heat (spicy food, alcohol, hot environment, stress) will further agitate the blood and worsen bleeding. Cooling foods and drinks directly counteract the Heat, while rest and calm prevent the stirring of Yang and blood. Avoiding exertion preserves Yin fluids that Heat consumes, and calming the mind helps settle the agitated blood.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Avoid spicy, greasy, and fried foods, alcohol, and coffee, which add Heat. Eat cooling foods like watermelon, cucumber, mung beans, and leafy greens. Stay well hydrated with room-temperature or cool water. Rest in a calm, cool environment and avoid overexertion or emotional stress.

### Spleen not controlling Blood
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/spleen-not-controlling-blood)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Chronic bleeding that is pale and oozing, General fatigue and physical exhaustion, Poor appetite and loose stools, Pale or sallow complexion, Easy bruising or subcutaneous bleeding
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Overwork and lack of sleep, Raw, cold, or greasy foods, Worry and overthinking, Skipping meals, Heavy exercise
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Rest and sleep, Warm, cooked meals, Gentle movement or exercise, Stress reduction
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Your Spleen Qi is responsible for holding blood inside the vessels, much like a hand grips a cup. When that Qi becomes weak from overwork, poor diet, or chronic worry, it can no longer keep the blood in place. The result is a slow, persistent leakage of blood that looks pale and thin, often showing up in the stool, urine, skin, or as heavy menstrual flow.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale, puffy, tender body with teeth marks and a thin white coating. Pulse: fine (thin) and weak, especially at the right Guan (Spleen) position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Spleen Qi is the root force that holds blood inside the vessels. Overwork, poor diet, and worry drain that Qi, causing blood to leak out-so these are the main triggers. Rest, warm cooked foods, and gentle movement conserve and rebuild Qi, while moxibustion and Qi-tonifying herbs directly restore the Spleen's grip. Cold and raw foods damage the Spleen's digestive fire, further weakening its ability to manage blood, so avoiding them is critical.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Build your Spleen Qi with warm, cooked, easy-to-digest meals like soups, stews, and congees. Avoid raw, cold, and greasy foods that tax the digestive fire. Prioritize rest and avoid overwork-your body needs energy to hold blood in. Gentle daily movement like walking or tai chi helps Qi circulate, but stop before you feel exhausted. Manage worry and overthinking, as these directly weaken the Spleen.

### Empty-Heat caused by Yin Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/empty-heat-caused-by-yin-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Bleeding is scant and bright or dark red, Worse at night or after exertion, Night sweats, Malar flush (red cheekbones), Dry mouth and throat, Five-palm heat (palms, soles, chest)
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Spicy, fried, or greasy foods, Overwork and lack of sleep, Hot weather or overheated rooms, Emotional stress or anger, Alcohol and coffee
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Rest and sleep, Cool environment, Cooling foods and drinks, Hydration, Gentle movement or exercise
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When Yin is depleted, the body loses its cooling, moistening anchor. This creates a low-grade "empty heat" that quietly damages small blood vessels. The bleeding tends to be scant, often bright red or dark red, and appears when the heat stirs most - at night, after exertion, or in the second half of the menstrual cycle.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red, thin, dry body with cracks, little or no coating. Pulse: fine (thin) and rapid.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Yin deficiency means the body's cooling, moistening capacity is already low, so any extra heat or Yin-depleting activity (spicy food, late nights, stress) stirs up empty heat and triggers bleeding. Rest, cool surroundings, and Yin-nourishing foods help rebuild Yin and settle the heat, keeping blood inside the vessels.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Build Yin with foods like pears, black sesame, goji berries, and tofu. Avoid spicy, greasy, and fried foods that add heat. Prioritize sleep, especially before midnight, and avoid overwork or late-night screen time, which further deplete Yin.

### Blood Stagnation
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/blood-stagnation)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Dark, clotted bleeding from any site, Fixed, stabbing pain that worsens at night, Pain that is worse with pressure, Dark menstrual blood with clots, Dark purplish lips, nails, or complexion, Rough, dry, scaly patches of skin
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Cold exposure, Sedentary lifestyle, Stress and frustration, Injury or trauma, Raw, cold, or greasy foods
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warmth, Gentle movement or exercise, Warming spices like ginger, Stress reduction, Massage
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When Qi and blood become stuck and no longer flow smoothly, the blood vessels can get blocked. This obstruction builds pressure behind the blockage, and eventually blood pushes out of the vessels, leading to bleeding. Because the blood has been sitting still before escaping, it tends to be dark and clotted, and the area is often painful.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: purple or dusky body, possible stasis spots, distended dark veins under the tongue, thin white coat. Pulse: choppy (涩 sè), like a knife scraping bamboo, or wiry (弦 xián) if Qi stagnation is also present.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Cold freezes and congeals blood, making stasis worse, while warmth and movement encourage blood to flow freely again. Stress knots up the Qi, and when Qi stagnates, blood follows, so calming the mind and body helps. Injury directly creates blood stasis, so protecting the body from trauma is essential.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Stay warm and avoid cold, raw foods that can congeal the blood. Gentle daily movement like walking or stretching helps keep Qi and blood flowing. Turmeric, ginger, and black vinegar in cooking can gently move blood. Manage stress and avoid long periods of sitting still.

### Liver Fire Blazing
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/liver-fire-blazing)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Sudden nosebleeds or vomiting of bright red blood, Throbbing headache at the temples, Red, burning eyes, Irritability and short temper, Bitter taste in mouth
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Anger and frustration, Spicy, fried, or greasy foods, Alcohol and coffee, Hot weather or overheated rooms, Overwork and lack of sleep
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Stress reduction, Cooling foods and drinks, Gentle movement or exercise, Cold compress on nose
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  The Liver stores blood and keeps Qi flowing smoothly. When suppressed anger or frustration stagnates Liver Qi, it transforms into Fire that surges upward, scorching the blood. This heat forces blood to move recklessly and spill out of the vessels, typically causing sudden, bright-red bleeding from the nose or mouth, often triggered by emotional outbursts.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red body with redder sides, dry yellow coating, possible red prickles. Pulse: wiry (弦 xián) and rapid (数 shuò), forceful, especially in the left Guan (middle) position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Anger and frustration cause Liver Qi to stagnate and transform into fire, which rises and scorches the blood, leading to bleeding. Spicy, greasy foods and alcohol add heat to the body, further fueling the fire. Rest, cooling foods, and cold compresses help clear heat and calm the Liver, allowing blood to stay within the vessels.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Avoid spicy, greasy, and fried foods, as well as alcohol and coffee, which add heat. Choose cooling foods like cucumber, celery, chrysanthemum tea, and mung bean soup. Manage anger and stress through meditation or gentle exercise like tai chi. For nosebleeds, apply a cold compress to the bridge of the nose and sit upright.

### Kidney and Liver Yin Deficiency causing Heat in the Blood
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/kidney-and-liver-yin-deficiency-causing-heat-in-the-blood)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Night sweats, Low back soreness and weak knees, Dizziness and tinnitus, Dry mouth and throat, especially at night, Bleeding gums or nosebleeds, Heavy or early periods
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Spicy, fried, or greasy foods, Overwork and lack of sleep, Emotional stress or anger, Alcohol and coffee, Hot weather or overheated rooms
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Cooling foods and drinks, Rest and sleep, Hydration, Gentle movement or exercise, Stress reduction
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When Kidney and Liver Yin become depleted, they can no longer anchor Yang or cool the blood. This generates a low-grade, empty heat that stirs the blood and forces it out of the vessels, leading to bleeding from various sites. The bleeding is often scant and bright red, accompanied by classic Yin-deficiency signs like night sweats, dizziness, and a sore lower back.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red, thin, dry, with little or no coating; possible cracks; sides redder. Pulse: fine (thin), rapid, and may be wiry at left Guan position; weak at left Chi.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: This pattern is driven by Yin deficiency generating empty heat. Spicy, fried, and hot foods, alcohol, and overwork all consume Yin and add heat, making bleeding worse. Cooling, moistening foods and adequate rest help replenish Yin and calm the internal heat, allowing the blood to settle back into the vessels.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat cooling, moistening foods like pear, cucumber, tofu, and black sesame. Avoid spicy, fried, or greasy dishes that add heat. Prioritize rest, keep a regular sleep schedule, and avoid overwork and late nights. Gentle exercises like Tai Chi or walking help nourish Yin without depleting it.

## คำถามที่พบบ่อย (Frequently Asked Questions)
### Can Chinese herbs stop bleeding quickly?
Yes, certain herbs are known for their fast-acting hemostatic properties, such as San Qi (Notoginseng) or Pu Huang (Cattail Pollen), and are often used in acute situations. However, these are typically part of a formula that also addresses the root cause - cooling Heat, tonifying Qi, or moving stasis - to prevent the bleeding from returning. For sudden, severe bleeding, always seek emergency medical care and use herbs only under professional guidance.

### Is it safe to use TCM if I'm taking blood thinners?
This requires careful coordination. Many blood-moving herbs like Dang Gui (Angelica Sinensis) or Chuan Xiong (Szechuan Lovage) can amplify the effect of anticoagulants and increase bleeding risk. You must tell both your TCM practitioner and your prescribing doctor about all medications and herbs you are taking. A skilled practitioner can select formulas that avoid these interactions while still treating your pattern, but never start or stop blood thinners on your own.

### How long will I need treatment?
It depends on your pattern. Acute heat-driven bleeding may resolve in a week or two of herbs and acupuncture. Chronic bleeding from Spleen Qi or Yin deficiency usually requires 2 to 3 months of consistent treatment to rebuild the body's foundation, though you may notice improvement in energy and fewer episodes much sooner. Blood Stagnation patterns often show progress within a month. Your practitioner will monitor your tongue and pulse to gauge progress and adjust the formula.

### Can acupuncture help with bleeding disorders?
Yes, acupuncture can help regulate the flow of Qi and blood, strengthen the Spleen's holding function, and cool Heat. Points like Xuehai (SP-10) and Sanyinjiao (SP-6) are commonly used for bleeding issues, but the selection depends entirely on your pattern. Acupuncture alone is rarely sufficient for significant bleeding; it's most effective when combined with herbal medicine and dietary changes.

### Are there foods I should avoid if I bleed easily?
Generally, avoid very spicy, hot, or greasy foods that can create or worsen Heat in the blood. If your bleeding is due to Spleen Qi deficiency, raw, cold foods and iced drinks can further weaken your digestive Qi. For Yin deficiency, alcohol and coffee can worsen the empty heat. Your practitioner will give you specific dietary guidance based on your pattern, but in all cases, warm, cooked, easily digestible meals are a safe bet.

### What if I have a diagnosed bleeding disorder like hemophilia?
TCM can be a supportive therapy to help strengthen the body's constitution and reduce the frequency of spontaneous bleeding episodes, but it cannot replace clotting factor concentrates or emergency medical care. Always work closely with your hematologist and inform your TCM practitioner of your full medical history. The goal is to improve your overall Qi and blood health, which may make your body more resilient, but never delay standard treatment for a serious bleed.
