## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนปัจจุบัน (Conventional Medicine View)
### คำอธิบาย (Description)
Attention Deficit Hyperactivity Disorder (ADHD) is a neurodevelopmental condition characterized by persistent inattention, hyperactivity, and impulsivity that interferes with daily functioning. It is typically diagnosed in childhood, though symptoms often continue into adulthood. Diagnosis is based on clinical criteria from the DSM-5, which requires multiple symptoms to be present for at least six months across two or more settings.
Conventional medicine views ADHD as a brain-based disorder involving differences in neurotransmitter systems, particularly dopamine and norepinephrine. The exact cause is unknown, but genetics, prenatal exposures, and environmental factors are thought to play a role.

### การรักษาหลัก (Treatments)
Standard treatment usually combines behavioral therapy with medication. Stimulant medications such as methylphenidate (Ritalin) and amphetamines (Adderall) are the most widely prescribed, as they increase dopamine and norepinephrine activity to improve focus and reduce impulsivity. Non-stimulant options like atomoxetine (Strattera) or guanfacine (Intuniv) are also used. Parent training, school accommodations, and cognitive behavioral therapy are key non-pharmacological supports.

## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนจีน (Traditional Chinese Medicine View)
### ความเข้าใจเกี่ยวกับการแพทย์แผนจีน (TCM Understanding)
TCM understands ADHD primarily through the lens of the Heart, Liver, Spleen, and Kidney systems. The Heart houses the Shen - the mind and spirit - which governs clear thinking, memory, and calm presence. When the Shen is disturbed, the mind becomes scattered, restless, or foggy. This disturbance can come from excess factors, like Phlegm and Fire clouding the Heart's orifice, or from deficiency, when the Heart lacks enough Qi and Blood to anchor the Shen securely.
The Liver plays an equally central role. It ensures the smooth flow of Qi and Blood, and it anchors the body's Yang energy. When Liver Qi stagnates, it can transform into Fire or stir up internal Wind, producing the classic ADHD symptoms of fidgeting, impulsivity, and explosive anger. Often, Kidney Yin - the body's deep cooling reserve - is too weak to hold the Liver's Yang in check, allowing heat and restlessness to rise upward.
The Spleen is the root of digestion and the source of Qi and Blood. A weak Spleen fails to transform food into clear nourishment, leading instead to the production of Phlegm and Dampness. This sticky, turbid substance can clog the channels and veil the Heart, creating a heavy-headed, foggy inability to focus. At the same time, insufficient Blood production leaves the Shen undernourished, resulting in the daydreamy, forgetful, and easily fatigued presentation of ADHD.
Because these organ systems are interconnected, the same Western diagnosis of ADHD can arise from very different TCM patterns - Phlegm-Fire harassing the Heart, Liver Yang Rising, Liver Fire Blazing, Heart and Spleen Deficiency, or Kidney and Liver Yin Deficiency. Each pattern has its own characteristic tongue, pulse, and emotional profile, and each requires a different treatment strategy. This is why a TCM practitioner spends so much time observing the tongue, feeling the pulse, and asking about specific behaviors, sleep, and digestion before forming a diagnosis.

### การวินิจฉัยโดยแพทย์แผนจีน (Practitioner Diagnosis)
A practitioner starts by asking about the quality of restlessness and what seems to trigger it. In Phlegm‑Fire harassing the Heart, the person often feels a hot, agitated sensation in the chest, with a heavy-headed fogginess and a tendency to act impulsively without thinking. The tongue is red with a thick yellow greasy coat, and the pulse is slippery and rapid, pointing to phlegm and heat clouding the Shen.
When Liver Wind agitates internally due to Liver Yang Rising, the restlessness shows up as constant fidgeting, tics, or sudden jerky movements. There is often a background of Kidney Yin deficiency, so the tongue is red with little coating and the pulse is wiry and rapid. Dizziness, a dry mouth, and trouble settling down in the evening are common clues that Wind is stirring because Yin cannot anchor Yang.
In simple Liver Yang Rising, the person is irritable and excessively active, but without the phlegm signs of the first pattern or the tics of Wind. The tongue body is red with a thin yellow or dry coating, and the pulse feels wiry. Practitioners often find that weak Spleen Qi is failing to keep the Liver in check, so there may be poor appetite or loose stools alongside the hyperactivity.
With Liver Fire Blazing, the heat is more intense and directed upward. The person is quick‑tempered, easily angered, and may have a red face, bitter taste in the mouth, or bloodshot eyes. The tongue is red with a yellow coating, and the pulse is rapid and wiry. This pattern points to an excess fire that directly stirs up the Heart Shen, making focus nearly impossible.
Heart and Spleen Deficiency presents very differently. Here the main struggle is mental fatigue, forgetfulness, and a dreamy, spaced‑out inattention rather than explosive energy. The person may be pale, have a poor appetite, and tire easily. The tongue is pale with a thin white coat, and the pulse is weak and thready, reflecting the lack of Qi and Blood to nourish the Shen.
Kidney and Liver Yin Deficiency tends to appear in older children or adults with a longer history of ADHD. The restlessness is accompanied by poor memory, a feeling of heat in the palms and soles, and night sweats. The tongue is red with little or no coating, and the pulse is thin and rapid. Here the brain is under‑nourished, so concentration is poor even when the person tries hard to focus.
Heart and Spleen Qi and Blood Deficiency looks similar to the Heart‑Spleen deficiency pattern but with more pronounced signs of Blood deficiency, such as a dull pale complexion, palpitations, and a tendency to startle easily. The tongue is pale and may be slightly dry, and the pulse is thready and weak. The person’s attention drifts because the Shen simply lacks the nourishment to stay anchored.

## กลุ่มอาการการแพทย์แผนจีนและการรักษา (TCM Patterns and Treatment)

### Phlegm-Fire harassing the Heart
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/phlegm-fire-harassing-the-heart)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Mental fog with inner restlessness - can&#x27;t focus yet can&#x27;t sit still, Quick temper, impulsive outbursts, easily frustrated, Feeling of phlegm in the throat, chest oppression, or heavy head, Red face, bitter taste in the mouth, feeling hot, Tossing and turning at night, disturbed sleep with vivid dreams
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Greasy, fried, or spicy foods, Stress, anger, and frustration, Overstimulation (screens, loud noise), Lack of sleep or late nights, Hot weather
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Cooling foods, Regular physical activity, Quiet, calm environment, Deep breathing, Cool, dry weather
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  In this pattern, two forces work together to disrupt focus and self-control. Phlegm is a thick, sticky byproduct of poor digestion that clouds the Heart - in TCM, the Heart houses the Shen (mind and spirit), so when phlegm veils it, thinking becomes foggy, scattered, and unclear.
At the same time, Fire - excess heat - agitates the Heart Shen, creating inner restlessness, impulsivity, and a drive to move constantly. The result is the classic ADHD picture: a mind that cannot settle, a body that cannot stay still, and emotions that flare up quickly.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red body, swollen with a red prickly tip, and a thick yellow greasy coating. Pulse: slippery and rapid (滑数, huá shuò), forceful and full.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Greasy, sweet, and dairy-heavy foods create dampness and phlegm - the very substance clouding the Heart in this pattern. Spicy foods and stimulants add heat, fanning the fire that agitates the mind. Cooling, light foods help clear heat and reduce phlegm production. Exercise moves stagnant Qi and helps the body process phlegm, while a calm, structured environment soothes the overstimulated Shen and prevents fire from flaring up. Hot, humid weather and late nights both add to the internal heat and dampness that drive this pattern.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Avoid greasy, fried, and heavily processed foods, as well as excessive sugar and dairy - these all generate phlegm. Spicy, hot foods and stimulants like coffee add fire and should also be limited.
Favor light, cooling foods like leafy greens, cucumber, pear, and mung beans. Regular moderate exercise - especially outdoors - helps move Qi and clear phlegm. A structured daily routine with consistent meal times and a calm, screen-free wind-down before bed helps settle the agitated Shen.

### Liver Wind agitating Internally due to Liver Yang Rising
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/liver-wind-agitating-internally-due-to-liver-yang-rising)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Constant fidgeting, squirming, or tapping, Irritability and explosive temper, Trembling hands or head (may be subtle), Dizziness or unsteady feeling, Stiff neck and tension headaches, Red tongue that trembles when extended
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Stress, anger, and frustration, Caffeine and energy drinks, Greasy, fried, or spicy foods, Overstimulation (screens, loud noise), Lack of sleep or late nights
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Quiet, calm environment, Gentle exercise (walking, yoga), Cooling foods, Deep breathing, Adequate sleep and rest
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When Kidney Yin is too weak to anchor the Liver's Yang energy, that Yang rises upward like a flame, stirring up internal Wind. This inner Wind creates a sensation of restlessness and uncontrollable movement - the constant fidgeting, squirming, and inability to sit still that are hallmark ADHD behaviors. The agitated mind, prone to irritability and distraction, is like a flag flapping in a strong wind, unable to settle.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red body, stiff or trembling, possibly deviated, with thin yellow or scanty coating. Pulse: wiry (弦 xián) and fine (细 xì), may be rapid (数 shuò).
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Stress and anger cause Liver Qi to surge upward, directly feeding the rising Yang and stirring Wind. Overstimulating environments and stimulating substances like caffeine act like wind on a flame, making restlessness worse. Cooling foods and calming activities help anchor the Yang and nourish the deficient Kidney Yin, while quiet and rest allow the inner Wind to settle.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Keep a consistent daily routine with quiet time before bed. Limit screen time and stimulating activities. Include cooling, Yin-nourishing foods like pears, cucumber, and tofu. Avoid spicy, fried, and sugary foods that stir up Wind and Heat.

### Liver Yang Rising
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/liver-yang-rising)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Throbbing or distending headache at the temples or top of the head, Irritability, quick temper, and emotional outbursts, Flushed face and red eyes, Dizziness or feeling of heat rising to the head, Difficulty sitting still, constant fidgeting, Lower back and knee soreness or weakness
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Stress, anger, and frustration, Greasy, fried, or spicy foods, Lack of sleep or late nights, Overstimulation (screens, loud noise), Hot weather
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Quiet, calm environment, Adequate sleep and rest, Cooling foods, Gentle exercise (walking, yoga)
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  In this pattern, Kidney or Liver Yin is too weak to anchor Liver Yang, so the Yang energy floats upward like a kite with a broken string. This rising Yang creates a state of internal restlessness and heat that shows up as constant fidgeting, impulsive outbursts, and an inability to settle the mind. The head and senses become overstimulated, making it hard to filter out distractions and stay focused.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red body with red sides, thin dry yellow or scanty coating, possibly trembling. Pulse: wiry (弦 xián), often fine and rapid at the left middle position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Stress and anger directly stir up Liver Qi, making Yang surge upward even more. Spicy and fried foods add internal heat, while lack of sleep depletes Yin, removing the anchor for Yang. On the other hand, cooling foods and a calm environment help sedate the rising Yang, and gentle movement channels excess energy without further agitating the system.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Prioritize a regular sleep schedule and a calm, screen-free wind-down before bed. Eat plenty of cooling, Yin-nourishing foods like pears, cucumber, spinach, and millet, and avoid spicy, fried, or overly stimulating foods. Gentle daily movement like walking or swimming helps settle rising Yang without overexciting the system.

### Liver Fire Blazing
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/liver-fire-blazing)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Intense irritability and short temper, Throbbing headache at the temples or crown, Red, painful, or burning eyes, Bitter taste in the mouth, Flushed red face, Restless agitation and difficulty sleeping
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Stress, anger, and frustration, Greasy, fried, or spicy foods, Alcohol, Lack of sleep or late nights, Hot weather
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Cooling foods, Gentle exercise (walking, yoga), Quiet, calm environment, Deep breathing, Avoiding alcohol and caffeine
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When Liver Fire blazes, it surges upward and disturbs the Heart Shen - the spirit that governs clear thinking and calm. This internal heat makes the mind feel restless and agitated, so it is almost impossible to settle down and focus. Stress and overthinking consume Heart Blood and weaken Spleen Qi, making symptoms worse. Cold, raw foods and irregular eating directly damage the Spleen's ability to produce Qi and Blood. Rest, warm nourishment, and a calm environment allow the body to rebuild these vital substances, restoring mental clarity and emotional stability.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat warm, cooked, easily digestible foods like soups, stews, and congees to support the Spleen. Include blood-nourishing foods such as red dates, longan fruit, and dark leafy greens. Avoid cold, raw foods and irregular meal times. Maintain a consistent sleep schedule to replenish Heart Blood. Gentle daily movement like walking helps Qi flow without depleting energy. Limit screen time and mental overwork to conserve Heart Blood.

### Kidney and Liver Yin Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/kidney-and-liver-yin-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Forgetfulness and poor concentration, Dry eyes with blurred vision, Night sweats with heat in palms and soles, Soreness and weakness of the lower back and knees, Dizziness and ringing in the ears, Insomnia or restless sleep with vivid dreams
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Stress, anger, and frustration, Greasy, fried, or spicy foods, Lack of sleep or late nights, Hot weather, Excessive exercise and sweating, Alcohol
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Adequate sleep and rest, Quiet, calm environment, Cooling foods, Gentle exercise (walking, yoga), Consistent daily routine
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Kidney Yin is the body's deep reservoir of cooling, nourishing fluid. When it runs low, the Liver Yin it feeds also dries up. The brain-called the Sea of Marrow in Chinese medicine-relies on Kidney essence for fuel, so this deficiency leaves it undernourished, causing poor concentration, forgetfulness, and mental fog. At the same time, insufficient Yin fails to anchor the mind's Yang spark, allowing a low-grade restlessness and insomnia that looks like the inattentive, fidgety side of ADHD.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red, thin body with cracks, little or no coating, and a dry surface. Pulse: fine (thin), rapid, and wiry; the left Chi (Kidney) position is weak.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Yin is the body's cooling, restorative substance. Activities that burn the candle at both ends-late nights, overwork, emotional stress-consume Yin directly, making the mind more restless and the brain more foggy. Heat, whether from the weather, spicy food, or alcohol, acts like a stove that boils away the last of the body's fluids, flaring up the deficiency heat that disturbs sleep and focus. Gentle, cooling habits and moistening foods replenish that deep reservoir, allowing the mind to settle and the brain to be nourished again.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Prioritise deep rest and a regular sleep schedule-nothing rebuilds Yin like consistent, quality sleep. Eat moistening, building foods: pears, black sesame seeds, walnuts, goji berries, bone broths, and dark leafy greens. Avoid spicy, fried, and overly roasted foods, as well as alcohol and coffee, which all dry up precious fluids. Gentle movement like tai chi or restorative yoga can circulate Qi without sweating out Yin.

## คำถามที่พบบ่อย (Frequently Asked Questions)
### Can TCM really help my child's ADHD?
Yes, many families find significant improvement in focus, emotional regulation, and overall calmness with TCM. The key is matching the right treatment to your child's specific pattern. A child with a hot, impulsive, Phlegm-Fire pattern will respond to different herbs and points than a child with a quiet, forgetful, Spleen-deficient pattern. When the pattern is correctly identified, results are often felt within a couple of months.

### Are Chinese herbs safe for children with ADHD?
When prescribed by a qualified TCM practitioner, Chinese herbal formulas are generally very safe for children. Practitioners use lower dosages and select herbs specifically suited to a child's developing constitution. Formulas are typically given as granules dissolved in water, making them easy to take. Always inform your practitioner of any medications your child is taking, and never self-prescribe herbs.

### Can my child continue taking ADHD medication while doing TCM?
Yes, TCM can be safely combined with conventional stimulant or non-stimulant medications. In fact, many families use TCM to help manage side effects like poor appetite or insomnia, and some are eventually able to reduce medication under their doctor's supervision. It is essential that both your TCM practitioner and prescribing physician are aware of all treatments your child is receiving.

### How does acupuncture work for ADHD?
Acupuncture helps regulate the nervous system and rebalance the organ systems involved in ADHD. Points on the head, like Baihui (DU-20) and Sishencong, calm the mind and improve focus. Points on the body, such as Taichong (LR-3) to subdue Liver Yang or Zusanli (ST-36) to strengthen the Spleen, address the root pattern. Most children find the treatments relaxing, and very fine, shallow needles are used for young patients.

### What dietary changes does TCM recommend for ADHD?
TCM generally advises avoiding foods that create Phlegm and Heat - such as excessive sugar, artificial additives, greasy foods, and very cold or raw foods that weaken the Spleen. Warm, cooked meals with plenty of vegetables, whole grains, and moderate protein help build stable Qi and Blood. Specific calming foods like walnuts, longan fruit, and jujube seeds (Suan Zao Ren) are often recommended.

### How long until we see results?
For excess patterns, many parents notice their child is calmer and less impulsive within 4-8 weeks. Deficiency patterns take longer - often 3-6 months - because the body needs time to rebuild Qi and Blood. The first signs of progress are usually better sleep, less irritability, and a longer attention span for quiet activities. Consistency with herbs and weekly acupuncture sessions is key.

### Does TCM treat adult ADHD too?
Absolutely. The same patterns apply to adults, though they often present as chronic restlessness, difficulty completing tasks, or emotional volatility rather than the obvious hyperactivity seen in children. Treatment follows the same principles of pattern differentiation, and many adults find TCM helps them feel more grounded and focused without the side effects they may have experienced with medication.
