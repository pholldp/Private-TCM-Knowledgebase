## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนปัจจุบัน (Conventional Medicine View)
### คำอธิบาย (Description)
Melasma is a common, chronic skin condition characterized by symmetrical, brown to gray-brown patches on sun-exposed areas of the face - most often the cheeks, forehead, upper lip, and chin. It affects women far more frequently than men, especially during pregnancy or when taking hormonal contraceptives, though men can develop it too. Diagnosis is usually made by a dermatologist simply by looking at the skin; a special lamp (Wood's lamp) may be used to see how deep the pigment goes.
The exact cause isn't fully understood, but sun exposure, hormonal changes, and genetics play major roles. Melanocytes, the pigment-producing cells, become overactive, depositing excess melanin in the skin. Conventional treatment focuses on blocking melanin production and protecting the skin from UV light.

### การรักษาหลัก (Treatments)
Standard first-line treatment is strict sun protection combined with topical creams that inhibit melanin synthesis, such as hydroquinone (often in combination with tretinoin and a mild steroid). Other options include azelaic acid, kojic acid, and vitamin C serums. For stubborn cases, chemical peels, microdermabrasion, or laser and light therapies may be used, though these carry a risk of post-inflammatory hyperpigmentation, especially in darker skin tones.

## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนจีน (Traditional Chinese Medicine View)
### ความเข้าใจเกี่ยวกับการแพทย์แผนจีน (TCM Understanding)
TCM sees melasma as a sign that Qi, Blood, and body fluids are not flowing smoothly, and that the face isn't receiving proper nourishment. The condition is almost always linked to three organ systems: the Liver, the Spleen, and the Kidneys. When any of these are out of balance, stagnation, dampness, or deficiency heat rise upward and settle in the skin as brown patches.
The Liver is responsible for the smooth movement of Qi and Blood. Emotional stress, frustration, or long-held anger can cause Liver Qi to stagnate, like a traffic jam on a highway. That stagnation prevents Blood from reaching the face properly, and over time, the backed-up energy darkens into brownish patches. If the stagnation deepens to involve Blood stasis, the patches become darker and more sharply defined.
The Spleen transforms food into Qi and Blood and manages fluid metabolism. When it's weakened - often by poor diet, overwork, or worry - it fails to process fluids, which turn into a heavy, turbid dampness. This dampness rises to the face and creates dull, ill-defined patches, often accompanied by fatigue, bloating, and a sallow complexion.
The Kidneys store the body's fundamental Yin and Essence. As we age, or after prolonged illness or overwork, Kidney and Liver Yin can become depleted. This creates a state of deficiency heat that rises and scorches the skin, leaving dark brown, irregular patches. This pattern is deeper and slower to correct, but it responds well to consistent nourishment.

### การวินิจฉัยโดยแพทย์แผนจีน (Practitioner Diagnosis)
A practitioner begins by examining the patches themselves - their color and how sharply they are defined. Deep or light brown patches that flare up with stress, alongside chest tightness and irritability, suggest the Liver is not moving Qi smoothly. The tongue often looks red with a thin coating, and the pulse feels wiry, like a guitar string.
When stagnation persists, the blood also becomes sluggish, and the patches turn darker with clear, well-defined edges. This Qi and Blood Stagnation pattern often brings sharper discomfort in the chest or flanks, and the tongue may appear darker, sometimes with a purple hue. The pulse feels deep or choppy, like a stream hitting stones.
If the patches are a dull brown or gray-brown with blurry borders, and the person feels chronically tired, bloated, and has loose stools, the root is likely a weak Spleen struggling with Dampness. The complexion looks sallow, the tongue is pale with a greasy coating, and the pulse is soft and fine - signs of digestive weakness rather than emotional tension.
Dark brown, irregularly shaped patches accompanied by dizziness, tinnitus, and aching lower back and knees point to a deeper depletion of Kidney and Liver Yin. The tongue is often red with little or no coating, and the pulse is deep and thread-like. This pattern reflects a long-term wear-and-tear rather than acute stress or dietary issues.

## กลุ่มอาการการแพทย์แผนจีนและการรักษา (TCM Patterns and Treatment)

### Liver Qi Stagnation
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/liver-qi-stagnation)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Deep or light brown patches on cheeks or forehead, Worsened by emotional stress, Distension or bloating along the ribs and flanks, Irritability, mood swings, or frequent sighing, Breast tenderness or menstrual irregularities
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Emotional stress, frustration, or worry, Spicy, greasy, or fried food, Alcohol, Irregular sleep
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Emotional calm or stress management, Gentle exercise (e.g., walking, Tai Chi), Chrysanthemum or rose tea, Warm compress on rib area
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  The Liver is responsible for keeping Qi flowing smoothly throughout the body. When emotional stress, frustration, or long-held resentment blocks this flow, Qi stagnates. That stagnation prevents blood and fluids from moving freely in the face, leading to the gradual buildup of brownish patches on the cheeks and forehead.
Because the Liver channel runs through the rib cage, the stagnation also causes the classic chest and rib-side distension. The more the Qi backs up, the darker and more persistent the pigmentation becomes, especially whenever new stress hits.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: normal or slightly dark body, red or slightly purple sides, thin white coat. Pulse: wiry (弦 xián), taut like a guitar string, especially at the left middle position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Stress surges Liver Qi upward and sideways, directly feeding the stagnation that causes pigmentation to darken. Calming the mind and moving the body gently unblocks that stuck Qi. Warming the rib area relaxes the Liver channel, while cooling, green foods and floral teas clear any heat that stagnation may generate. Spicy, greasy foods and alcohol add heat and dampness, making the stagnation stickier and harder to resolve.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Make stress management a daily priority - gentle movement like Tai Chi, yoga, or a short walk helps keep Liver Qi moving. Sip chrysanthemum or rosebud tea to soothe the Liver. Eat plenty of leafy greens, cucumber, and celery, and avoid greasy, fried, or spicy foods that can turn stagnation into heat.

### Qi And Blood Stagnation
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/qi-and-blood-stagnation)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Deep brown to purplish patches with clear, well-defined edges, Stabbing or fixed pain in the chest or rib area, worse with pressure, Painful periods with dark, clotted blood, Irritability and a feeling of tightness in the chest
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Emotional stress, frustration, or worry, Cold, greasy, or raw foods, Sedentary lifestyle, Suppressing anger or resentment
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Gentle exercise (e.g., walking, Tai Chi), Warm, easily digestible meals, Emotional calm or stress management, Warm compress on the lower abdomen
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When emotional stress or frustration persists, Liver Qi becomes stuck and eventually slows the flow of Blood. Over time, this stagnation creates deeper, more fixed patches because the Blood cannot properly nourish the face. The stagnant Blood darkens and forms well-defined, purplish-brown marks that do not fade easily.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: purple or dark body with stasis spots, thin white coat. Pulse: wiry (弦 xián) and choppy (涩 sè).
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Emotional stress knots the Liver Qi, which directly leads to Blood stasis-the core of this pattern. Cold and greasy foods burden the Spleen and can congeal Blood, making patches darker. A sedentary lifestyle allows stagnation to settle. Movement and warmth, on the other hand, encourage free flow of Qi and Blood, gradually lightening the patches. Calming the mind prevents the Qi from stagnating in the first place, breaking the cycle that creates the dark spots.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat warm, lightly cooked meals with blood-nourishing foods like dark leafy greens, beets, and goji berries. Avoid cold, raw, and greasy foods that congeal Blood. A daily walk or gentle stretching keeps Qi and Blood moving. Practice deep breathing or journaling to release emotional tension before it turns into physical stagnation.

### Spleen Deficiency with Dampness
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/spleen-deficiency-with-dampness)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Dull-brown or gray-brown patches with ill-defined borders, Sallow, lackluster complexion, Feeling of heaviness in the body and limbs, Fatigue and lack of energy, Abdominal bloating, worse after eating, Loose stools or diarrhoea, Poor appetite
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Cold, greasy, or raw foods, Damp, humid weather, Overeating or irregular meals, Sedentary lifestyle, Emotional stress, frustration, or worry
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warm, easily digestible meals, Gentle exercise (e.g., walking, Tai Chi), Rest and avoiding overwork, Dry, warm climate, Regular meal times
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When the Spleen is weak, it fails to transform food and fluids properly. This leads to an accumulation of internal Dampness, a heavy, turbid substance that rises to the face and settles in the skin, producing dull, ill-defined brown patches. Because the Spleen also generates Qi and blood, its deficiency leaves the complexion sallow and lacking luster.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale, swollen body with teeth marks, white greasy coating, moist surface. Pulse: soggy (Ru) and slow (Huan), soft and weak, especially at the right Guan position; may be slippery (Hua) if Dampness is pronounced.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: The Spleen is easily overwhelmed by cold, dampness, and irregular eating, which cause dampness to accumulate and rise to the face as dull patches. Warm, cooked foods and dry environments lighten the burden on the Spleen, while gentle movement and rest help Qi flow and drain dampness. Stress knots Liver Qi, which can invade the Spleen, so emotional calm protects digestive function and keeps the complexion clear.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Favor warm, cooked, easily digestible foods like congee, soups, and steamed vegetables. Avoid cold, raw, greasy, and dairy-heavy meals that create dampness. Incorporate spleen-strengthening foods such as yam, millet, ginger, and Job’s tears. Eat regular meals and avoid overeating. Gentle daily exercise like walking or tai chi helps move Qi and drain dampness. Manage stress and get adequate rest to support Spleen function.

### Kidney and Liver Yin Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/kidney-and-liver-yin-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Dark brown, irregularly shaped patches, Dizziness and ringing in the ears, Soreness and weakness of the lower back and knees, Night sweats and heat in the palms, soles, and chest, Dry eyes and blurred vision, Insomnia with vivid dreams
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Late nights and overwork, Spicy, greasy, or fried food, Emotional stress, frustration, or worry, Hot, dry weather
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Cooling, moistening foods, Going to bed early, Gentle exercise (e.g., walking, Tai Chi)
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  The Liver stores Blood and the Kidneys store Essence. When both are depleted, the face loses its nourishment and luster. Deficiency heat rises upward, scorching the skin and leaving dark brown, irregularly shaped patches on the cheeks and forehead.
This is a deeper, more chronic pattern where the body's cooling and moistening reserves have run low, so the pigmentation tends to be stubborn and accompanied by signs of aging and dryness throughout the body.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red, thin body with cracks, little or no coating, dry surface. Pulse: fine, rapid, and wiry; the left Chi (Kidney) position is weak.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Kidney and Liver Yin are like the body's cooling reservoir. Late nights, overwork, and stress drain that reservoir, allowing deficiency heat to rise and darken the skin. Cooling, moistening foods and deep rest replenish Yin fluids, calming the heat and gradually fading the patches.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat yin-nourishing foods like black sesame, walnuts, goji berries, mulberries, and pears. Avoid spicy, fried, and drying foods that consume fluids. Prioritize deep rest, go to bed before 11 p.m., and protect your skin from the sun daily.

## คำถามที่พบบ่อย (Frequently Asked Questions)
### Can TCM really lighten melasma?
Yes, many patients see significant lightening when the correct pattern is treated. Rather than bleaching the skin, TCM works internally to resolve the stagnation, dampness, or deficiency that is causing pigment to form. The patches fade gradually as the underlying imbalance is corrected, and the surrounding skin often looks healthier and more radiant as a side benefit.

### How long does it take to see results?
Most people notice some improvement within 4 to 8 weeks, but patience is key. Excess patterns like stress-related Liver stagnation can lighten faster, while deeper deficiency patterns may need 3 to 6 months. Consistent treatment and strict sun protection are essential - even one day of unprotected sun exposure can set progress back.

### Do I need to change my diet?
Diet plays a supporting role. In general, avoid greasy, spicy, and very cold foods, which can worsen dampness and stagnation. Favor warm, cooked meals, leafy greens, and foods that gently support the Liver and Spleen, like chrysanthemum tea, rose tea, and lightly cooked vegetables. Your practitioner will give you specific guidance based on your pattern.

### Can I use my regular skincare products with TCM treatment?
Yes, gentle cleansers, moisturizers, and mineral sunscreens are fine. However, avoid harsh chemical peels or strong bleaching creams while taking herbal formulas, as some herbs can make skin more sensitive. Always tell your TCM practitioner about any topical prescriptions you're using, and let your dermatologist know you're taking herbs.

### Will the patches come back after treatment?
TCM aims to correct the internal tendency that caused the melasma, so recurrence is less likely than with topical treatments alone. However, if the original triggers return - high stress, poor diet, hormonal shifts - the patches can reappear. Many people find that periodic tune-up acupuncture sessions and lifestyle maintenance keep their skin clear long-term.
