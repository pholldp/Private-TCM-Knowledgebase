## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนปัจจุบัน (Conventional Medicine View)
### คำอธิบาย (Description)
Hyperaldosteronism occurs when the adrenal glands produce too much aldosterone, a hormone that regulates salt and water balance. The excess hormone causes the body to retain sodium and lose potassium, leading to high blood pressure, muscle weakness, fatigue, and sometimes excessive thirst or urination. It is most often caused by a benign adrenal tumor or by overactivity of both adrenal glands. Diagnosis is made through blood tests measuring aldosterone, renin, and potassium, often followed by imaging studies.

### การรักษาหลัก (Treatments)
Conventional treatment of hyperaldosteronism focuses on blocking aldosterone's effects and controlling blood pressure. Mineralocorticoid receptor antagonists such as spironolactone and eplerenone are the mainstay, often combined with other antihypertensives. Potassium supplements may be given to correct low potassium levels. When a benign adrenal tumor (adenoma) is identified, surgical removal can be curative. Lifestyle measures like sodium restriction are also recommended.

## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนจีน (Traditional Chinese Medicine View)
### ความเข้าใจเกี่ยวกับการแพทย์แผนจีน (TCM Understanding)
In TCM, hyperaldosteronism is understood primarily as a disorder of water metabolism rooted in the Kidney, Liver, and Spleen systems. The Kidneys govern water and store the body's essential Yin and Yang. When Kidney Yin becomes depleted - through overwork, aging, or constitutional weakness - it fails to anchor Kidney Yang. This allows Yang to flare upward, driving up blood pressure and causing dizziness, tinnitus, and a flushed face. At the same time, the Kidney's ability to regulate fluids falters, contributing to fluid retention and potassium loss.
The Liver plays a central role because it ensures the smooth flow of Qi and Blood. Emotional stress, frustration, and anger cause Liver Qi to stagnate and transform into heat or fire, which rises to the head. This is the classic Liver Yang Rising pattern - pounding headaches, irritability, and a wiry pulse. Over time, this rising heat consumes Yin and Blood, creating a vicious cycle where the body's cooling, anchoring resources are burned up, making the Yang even more unstable.
The Spleen is responsible for transforming food and fluids into usable energy. When the Spleen is weakened by poor diet, worry, or fatigue, it fails to manage dampness. Dampness accumulates and thickens into phlegm, which clogs the middle of the body and leads to the heavy, bloated, sluggish sensations seen in Phlegm-Dampness patterns. This phlegm can further obstruct the normal movement of Qi and fluids, worsening edema and hypertension.
Finally, when the condition persists, Qi stagnation and heat can congeal the Blood, leading to Blood Stagnation. This pattern is marked by fixed, stabbing pain, a dark purple tongue, and a choppy pulse. So, while Western medicine sees one disease with a single hormonal driver, TCM sees several distinct landscapes - each requiring a different map for healing.

### การวินิจฉัยโดยแพทย์แผนจีน (Practitioner Diagnosis)
A practitioner starts by asking what the dizziness and headache feel like. A pounding, distending headache with a spinning sensation that worsens with stress or anger points toward Liver Yang Rising. The tongue is often red with a yellow coating, and the pulse feels wiry and rapid. This pattern is very common in the early stages of hyperaldosteronism.
When the same rising Yang symptoms appear alongside dryness and restlessness-such as a dry mouth, thirst, insomnia, or a sensation of heat in the palms and soles-the picture shifts to Liver Yang Rising with Blood and Yin Deficiency. The tongue remains red but the coating is thinner, and the pulse is thin and rapid, reflecting depleted Yin fluids.
If the dizziness is dull and persistent, accompanied by ringing in the ears (tinnitus) and a weak, aching lower back and knees, the root is likely Kidney and Liver Yin Deficiency. Night sweats and a dry throat are common. The tongue appears red with little or no coating, and the pulse is thready and rapid. This pattern often develops slowly over years.
Chest oppression, a heavy sensation in the head or body, and fatigue that feels like wearing a wet blanket suggest Phlegm-Dampness obstructing the Middle-Burner. The tongue coating is thick and greasy, and the pulse feels slippery. This pattern arises when the Spleen fails to transform fluids, creating turbid dampness that clouds the senses.
In chronic cases, a practitioner will look for signs of Blood Stagnation: stabbing or fixed pain, a dark or purplish complexion, and a tongue that is purple with possible stasis spots. The pulse is choppy or wiry and hesitant. Although less common, this pattern signals that blood flow has become sluggish and may complicate other patterns.

## กลุ่มอาการการแพทย์แผนจีนและการรักษา (TCM Patterns and Treatment)

### Liver Yang Rising
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/liver-yang-rising)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Dizziness or vertigo, Distending headache at temples or top of head, Flushed face and red eyes, Irritability and quick temper, Insomnia with vivid dreams, Dry mouth and throat, Lower back and knee soreness, Feeling of heat rising to the face
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Stress and anger, Spicy, greasy, or fried foods, Alcohol and coffee, Overwork and lack of sleep, Hot weather or overheated rooms, Intense exercise
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Rest and adequate sleep, Cooling, moistening foods (cucumber, pear, tofu), Gentle exercise (tai chi, walking), Deep breathing and meditation, Quiet, dark environment, Avoiding alcohol and caffeine
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  The Liver is responsible for the smooth flow of Qi throughout the body. When chronic stress, frustration, or overwork deplete Liver Yin, the cooling, anchoring aspect of the Liver weakens, and Liver Yang flares upward like a flame without water. This surging Yang rushes to the head, causing the severe dizziness, throbbing headache, and flushed face that are so common in hyperaldosteronism. The underlying Yin deficiency also leads to dry mouth, restless sleep, and irritability, while the upward pressure contributes to the high blood pressure that defines the condition.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red body with redder sides, thin dry yellow or scanty coat. Pulse: wiry (弦 xián) and often forceful at the left middle position; may be fine and rapid if Yin deficiency is prominent.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Stress and anger cause Liver Qi to stagnate and then surge upward, directly aggravating Liver Yang rising. Spicy, greasy, and heating foods add heat to the body, further fueling the upward flame. Overwork and insufficient rest deplete Liver Yin, weakening the anchor that holds Yang down. Cooling, Yin-nourishing foods and adequate rest replenish the Yin and help subdue the Yang. Gentle movement smooths the flow of Qi without stirring it up, and calming practices settle the mind, preventing emotional triggers from flaring the pattern.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat plenty of cooling, Yin-nourishing foods like celery, spinach, cucumber, and pear. Avoid spicy, greasy, and fried foods, as well as alcohol and coffee, which add heat and stir up Yang. Gentle exercise such as walking, tai chi, or yoga helps smooth Liver Qi without overexertion. Prioritize rest and a regular sleep schedule to protect Liver Yin. Practice stress-reduction techniques like meditation or deep breathing to prevent emotional surges from flaring Liver Yang.

### Liver Yang Rising with Blood and Yin Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/liver-yang-rising-with-blood-and-yin-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Throbbing or distending headache, often at the temples, Dizziness and vertigo with a heavy head and weak legs, Irritability and quickness to anger, Weak and aching lower back and knees, Dry eyes and blurred vision, Insomnia with vivid dreams
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Stress and anger, Spicy, greasy, or fried foods, Alcohol and coffee, Overwork and lack of sleep
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Rest and adequate sleep, Cooling, moistening foods (cucumber, pear, tofu), Gentle exercise (tai chi, walking), Quiet, dark environment
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  In hyperaldosteronism, the early surge of Liver Yang drives blood pressure up and creates throbbing headaches and dizziness. Over time, that rising Yang heat burns up Yin and Blood, which are meant to anchor and cool the body. The depleted Yin and Blood can no longer hold the Yang down, so the cycle of hypertension, irritability and dryness continues-this is the mixed excess-and-deficiency picture you see in chronic hyperaldosteronism.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red body, thin, with red sides and a dry surface; the coating is scanty or peeled. Pulse: wiry (xian) and fine (xi), especially forceful at the left guan (middle) position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Emotional stress and anger directly stir Liver Yang upward, which is the very engine of this pattern. Spicy, fried and hot foods add heat that further consumes Yin and makes the Yang rise more. Overwork and lost sleep drain Yin and Blood, weakening the anchor that keeps Yang in check. Rest, cooling foods and a calm environment do the opposite: they replenish Yin and Blood, so the body can hold the Yang down and quiet the head.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Favor cooling, moistening foods like pears, tofu, celery, spinach, cucumber and millet. Avoid alcohol, coffee, spicy dishes and fried or greasy food-they add heat and dry out Yin. Gentle daily movement such as walking or tai chi helps Qi flow without overheating. Protect sleep: going to bed by 10:30 PM is one of the most effective ways to rebuild Yin.

### Kidney and Liver Yin Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/kidney-and-liver-yin-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Soreness and weakness of the lower back and knees, Dizziness and ringing in the ears (tinnitus), Dry eyes with blurred vision, Night sweats and heat in the palms, soles, and chest, Dry mouth and throat, worse at night, Insomnia with vivid dreams and forgetfulness
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Overwork and lack of sleep, Spicy, greasy, or fried foods, Stress and anger, Hot, dry weather
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Rest and adequate sleep, Cooling, moistening foods (cucumber, pear, tofu), Gentle exercise (tai chi, walking), Quiet, dark environment
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  The Kidneys store the body's Yin essence and govern water metabolism, while the Liver ensures smooth Qi flow and stores Blood. When Kidney Yin runs low, it fails to nourish Liver Yin, allowing Liver Yang to rise unchecked. This rising Yang creates the high blood pressure and dizziness typical of hyperaldosteronism. At the same time, Yin deficiency generates internal heat, which disturbs fluid balance and contributes to the dry mouth, night sweats, and electrolyte imbalances seen in this condition.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: Red, thin body with cracks, little or no coating, dry surface. Pulse: Fine, rapid, and often slightly wiry; weak at the left Chi (Kidney) position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Kidney and Liver Yin are like the body's cooling and nourishing reserves. Overwork, late nights, and emotional stress burn through these reserves, allowing deficiency heat and rising Yang to dominate - which worsens hypertension, dizziness, and dryness. Rest, cooling foods, and gentle exercise replenish Yin and anchor Yang, calming the internal heat and restoring fluid balance.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Prioritize rest and avoid late nights, as sleep is when Yin rebuilds. Eat moistening, cooling foods like pear, black sesame, goji berries, and spinach; minimize spicy, fried, and drying foods. Gentle movement such as Tai Chi or walking supports Qi flow without depleting Yin.

### Phlegm-Dampness in the Middle-Burner
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/phlegm-dampness-in-the-middle-burner)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Bloating and distension after eating, Heavy sensation in the head, as if wrapped in a cloth, Thick, white, greasy tongue coating, Nausea or feeling of queasiness, Loose or sticky stools, Drowsiness and excessive sleepiness
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Greasy, heavy meals, Dairy and sweets, Damp or humid weather, Sitting for long periods, Overeating
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warm, cooked foods, Ginger tea, Gentle exercise (tai chi, walking), Dry, warm weather
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When the Spleen's ability to transform and transport fluids weakens, dampness builds up and condenses into phlegm. This phlegm-dampness clogs the middle burner, creating the bloating, nausea, and heavy sluggishness that are so typical of this pattern.
In hyperaldosteronism, the body's excess fluid retention and rising blood pressure mirror this internal dampness overload. As phlegm rises to the head, it clouds the clear senses, causing the characteristic dizziness and foggy-headed feeling.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale, swollen body with scalloped edges and a thick, white, greasy coating that is excessively moist. Pulse: slippery (滑 huá), often soggy (濡 rú) and moderate.

Greasy, heavy, sweet, and dairy foods further overwhelm the Spleen, generating more dampness, while damp weather and inactivity cause Qi to stagnate. Warm, cooked foods and ginger strengthen Spleen Yang and transform dampness, and gentle movement disperses stagnation and dries dampness, lightening the body and clearing the head.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat warm, cooked foods like congee with a little ginger, and avoid raw, cold, greasy, and sweet items that create dampness. Gentle daily movement, such as walking after meals, helps Qi circulate and dries dampness. Keep living spaces dry and well-ventilated.

### Blood Stagnation
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/blood-stagnation)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Fixed, stabbing pain (often head or chest), Pain worsens at night, Dark or dusky facial complexion, Purplish lips or nail beds, Rough, dry, scaly skin
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Cold weather, Cold foods and drinks, Prolonged inactivity, Emotional stress
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Gentle exercise (tai chi, walking), Applying warmth, Warm, cooked meals with spices, Massage or acupressure
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  In hyperaldosteronism, prolonged Liver Yang rising and Qi stagnation can eventually impair blood circulation, leading to Blood Stagnation. The stagnant blood obstructs the vessels, causing fixed stabbing pain, especially in the head or chest, and contributes to stubborn hypertension. The dark purple tongue and choppy pulse are classic signs of this deep-seated stasis.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: dark purple body with stasis spots, distended sublingual veins, thin white coat. Pulse: choppy (涩 Sè) like a knife scraping bamboo, or wiry (弦 Xián) if Qi stagnation is prominent.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Cold causes blood vessels to constrict and congeals blood, worsening stasis; warmth and gentle movement dilate vessels and promote flow. Emotional stress causes Qi stagnation, which further impedes blood circulation, so calming the mind and moving the body are essential.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat warm, cooked foods with blood-moving spices like turmeric, ginger, and garlic. Avoid cold, raw foods and icy drinks, which congeal blood. Gentle daily exercise such as walking or tai chi keeps circulation moving. Avoid prolonged sitting.

## คำถามที่พบบ่อย (Frequently Asked Questions)
### Can TCM help me reduce my blood pressure medication?
Many patients find that TCM treatment - especially when the correct pattern is addressed - can lower blood pressure naturally over weeks to months. However, you must never stop or adjust your conventional medication on your own. Work with both your prescribing doctor and your TCM practitioner to monitor your blood pressure and potassium levels, and taper medications only under medical supervision.

### How soon can I expect my potassium levels to improve?
Herbs that nourish Yin and clear deficiency heat can help the body conserve potassium, but improvement depends on the underlying pattern. In Liver Yang Rising or Yin deficiency patterns, some patients see a gradual rise in potassium within 3 to 6 weeks of consistent herbal therapy. Regular blood tests are essential to track progress and ensure safety.

### Is acupuncture safe if I have an adrenal tumor?
Acupuncture is generally safe, but you should inform your practitioner about any diagnosed tumor. Acupuncture does not shrink tumors, but it can help manage symptoms like headache, dizziness, and anxiety. Avoid direct needling over the adrenal area. Always keep your endocrinologist informed of any complementary therapies you are using.

### Will I need to change my diet for TCM treatment to work?
Dietary adjustments are an important part of TCM therapy. You will likely be advised to reduce salt, processed foods, alcohol, and caffeine. Specific recommendations depend on your pattern - for example, more cooling, moistening foods if you have Yin deficiency, or lighter, less greasy foods if you have Phlegm-Dampness. Your practitioner will give you a tailored plan.

### Can TCM help with the fatigue and muscle weakness?
Yes. In TCM, fatigue and weakness often stem from Spleen Qi deficiency or Kidney deficiency that underlies the water metabolism problem. Acupuncture points such as Sanyinjiao SP-6 and Shenshu BL-23 can strengthen the body's foundation, and herbs like Bai Zhu (Atractylodes) can support energy. Many patients report improved stamina within a few weeks of starting treatment.

### Do any Chinese herbs interact with spironolactone?
Some TCM herbs have mild diuretic effects (e.g., Fu Ling), but in balanced formulas they are unlikely to cause dangerous potassium shifts. However, because spironolactone is a potassium-sparing diuretic, it is critical that your TCM practitioner and your prescribing doctor both know exactly what you are taking. Never add single herbs or over-the-counter supplements without professional guidance.

## การวิจัยทางวิทยาศาสตร์สมัยใหม่ (Modern Scientific Research)
1. **Tianma Gouteng Yin for essential hypertension: a systematic review and meta-analysis of randomized controlled trials** ([ลิงก์](https://doi.org/10.1155/2013/751309))
   *วารสารวิชาการ: Xiong XJ, Yang XC, Liu YM, et al. Evidence-Based Complementary and Alternative Medicine. 2013;2013:751309.*
   This meta-analysis included 22 RCTs and found that Tian Ma Gou Teng Yin significantly reduced systolic and diastolic blood pressure compared to placebo, and was comparable to antihypertensive medications. The review highlighted the formula's potential, but noted high risk of bias in most trials.

2. **Chinese herbal medicine for hypertension: a systematic review of randomized controlled trials** ([ลิงก์](https://doi.org/10.1097/HJH.0000000000000361))
   *วารสารวิชาการ: Wang J, Xiong X, Liu W. Journal of Hypertension. 2014;32(12):2394-2404.*
   This systematic review evaluated multiple Chinese herbal formulas for hypertension and found that several, including Tian Ma Gou Teng Yin, showed significant blood pressure-lowering effects. However, the overall evidence was limited by small sample sizes and methodological flaws, and no study specifically targeted hyperaldosteronism.

3. **Acupuncture for essential hypertension: a meta-analysis of randomized sham-controlled trials** ([ลิงก์](https://doi.org/10.1155/2014/279478))
   *วารสารวิชาการ: Li DZ, Zhou Y, Yang J, et al. Evidence-Based Complementary and Alternative Medicine. 2014;2014:279478.*
   This meta-analysis of 12 sham-controlled trials concluded that acupuncture significantly reduced blood pressure, with effects lasting beyond the treatment period. While not specific to hyperaldosteronism, it supports the use of acupuncture for hypertension, a key feature of the condition.
