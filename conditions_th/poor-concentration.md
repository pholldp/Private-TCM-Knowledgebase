## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนปัจจุบัน (Conventional Medicine View)
### คำอธิบาย (Description)
From a Western medical perspective, poor concentration is a symptom rather than a diagnosis. It can be a feature of attention deficit hyperactivity disorder (ADHD), anxiety, depression, sleep disorders, thyroid dysfunction, or simply chronic stress and fatigue. Diagnosis typically involves a clinical interview, rating scales, and sometimes cognitive testing. The focus is on identifying any underlying condition and measuring the severity of the attention difficulty.

### การรักษาหลัก (Treatments)
Treatment depends on the underlying cause. For ADHD, stimulant medications like methylphenidate or non-stimulants are common. Cognitive behavioral therapy (CBT) and executive function coaching help build coping skills. If poor concentration stems from anxiety or depression, antidepressants or anti-anxiety medications may be prescribed. General advice often includes improving sleep hygiene, regular exercise, and minimizing distractions.

## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนจีน (Traditional Chinese Medicine View)
### ความเข้าใจเกี่ยวกับการแพทย์แผนจีน (TCM Understanding)
In TCM, clear thinking depends on the Heart, Spleen, Kidney, and Liver working in harmony. The Heart houses the Shén, the spirit or consciousness that must be calm and well-rooted for focus. The Spleen produces the Qi and Blood that nourish the Heart and the brain. The Kidney stores essence, which generates marrow to fill the brain and support memory. The Liver ensures the smooth flow of Qi and Blood so the mind is not disturbed by internal wind or heat.
When the Spleen is weak and cannot produce enough Blood, the Heart becomes undernourished and the Shén floats restlessly. This is the foggy, forgetful, easily-fatigued type of poor concentration - the mind simply runs out of fuel. When Kidney and Liver Yin are depleted, the cooling, moistening forces of the body run low, allowing a restless Yang energy to rise and agitate the mind. This creates a 'wired but tired' state where focus is scattered by internal heat and irritability.
A less common but important pattern is Phlegm-Fire harassing the Heart. Here, a heavy, sticky phlegm combines with heat to cloud the mind's clarity and agitate the Shén. Concentration becomes impossible not because the mind is empty, but because it is filled with a restless, murky sensation, often accompanied by a feeling of phlegm in the chest and a thick yellow tongue coating.
Each of these three patterns - deficiency of Qi and Blood, deficiency of Yin, and excess of Phlegm-Fire - demands its own treatment logic, which is why TCM never uses one formula for all cases of poor concentration.

### การวินิจฉัยโดยแพทย์แผนจีน (Practitioner Diagnosis)
A TCM practitioner begins by asking what "poor concentration" actually feels like for you. Is it a foggy, forgetful blankness that gets worse when you are tired? Or a restless, revved-up mind that cannot settle on one thing? The quality of the distraction is the first big clue that points toward one pattern rather than another.
If the main picture is mental fatigue, fuzzy thinking, and a tendency to space out, especially after meals or when overworked, the practitioner suspects Heart and Spleen Deficiency. They will look for a pale tongue with a thin white coating and feel for a weak, thready pulse. These signs confirm that the body is not making enough Qi and Blood to nourish the mind.
When poor concentration comes with irritability, a short fuse, and a feeling of being wired but tired, Kidney and Liver Yin Deficiency moves to the top of the list. The tongue is often red with little or no coating, and the pulse feels thin and rapid. This pattern reflects a deep cooling and nourishing Yin that has run low, allowing a restless Yang energy to float upward and disturb focus.
A less common but very distinct picture is Phlegm-Fire harassing the Heart. Here the inability to focus is paired with agitation, impulsive reactions, and a heavy, foggy sensation in the head. The tongue has a greasy yellow coating and the pulse is rapid and slippery. The practitioner recognizes this as a combination of sticky phlegm and heat clouding the mind's clarity, like a smoky, cluttered room.

## กลุ่มอาการการแพทย์แผนจีนและการรักษา (TCM Patterns and Treatment)

### Heart and Spleen Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/heart-and-spleen-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Mental fog and forgetfulness, Fatigue and physical exhaustion, Poor appetite with bloating, Pale or sallow complexion, Insomnia with vivid dreams, Palpitations or awareness of heartbeat
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Overwork or lack of sleep, Excessive worry, Cold, raw foods, Skipping meals
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Rest and relaxation, Warm, nourishing soups, Gentle walking, Early bedtime
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  The Spleen is the engine that transforms food into Qi and Blood. When it weakens, production slows down, leaving the Heart without enough Blood to anchor the mind (Shen). The result is a foggy, unfocused mind that tires easily, much like a lamp running low on oil.
Without sufficient nourishment, the Heart struggles to sustain clear thinking and steady attention. The fatigue and mental drift you feel are direct signs that both the Spleen's energy and the Heart's blood are running on empty.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale, tender body with teeth marks and a thin white coating. Pulse: fine, weak, and possibly choppy; the left cun (Heart) and right guan (Spleen) positions are especially weak.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Overwork, worry, and irregular eating all drain Spleen Qi, which is already weak in this pattern. When the Spleen can't produce enough Qi and Blood, the Heart loses its nourishment and the mind becomes foggy. Rest, warm foods, and gentle movement conserve and rebuild that energy, while calming the mind prevents further depletion. Together they restore the steady supply of Blood the Heart needs to keep attention clear and steady.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat warm, cooked meals at regular times and avoid cold, raw, or greasy foods that burden the Spleen. Include blood-nourishing foods like red dates, goji berries, dark leafy greens, and small amounts of well-cooked meat or bone broth.
Prioritize restful sleep and gentle daily movement such as walking or tai chi. Protect your mind from excessive worry and overthinking, as these directly drain both the Spleen and the Heart.

### Kidney and Liver Yin Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/kidney-and-liver-yin-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Restless, irritable mind that can&#x27;t settle, Dry eyes and blurred vision, Night sweats and hot palms/soles, Dizziness and tinnitus, Lower back soreness, Insomnia with vivid dreams
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Stress and anger, Overwork or lack of sleep, Greasy, spicy, or rich foods, Alcohol and coffee, Excessive screen time
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Early bedtime, Cool, quiet environment, Eating pears and black sesame, Gentle stretching or yoga, Hydration with water or herbal tea
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  The Kidney stores essence, which produces marrow to fill the brain. When Kidney Yin is depleted, the Sea of Marrow is undernourished, leading to poor memory and foggy thinking.
The Liver relies on Kidney Yin to stay moist and flexible; without it, Liver Yin becomes deficient and Liver Yang can rise, creating a restless, irritable mental state that makes it hard to settle and focus. The resulting deficiency heat further disturbs the Heart Shen, causing insomnia and a scattered mind.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: Red, thin body with cracks, little or no coating, dry surface. Pulse: Fine, rapid, possibly wiry; weak at the Kidney (left Chi) and Liver (left Guan) positions.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Yin is the body's cooling, nourishing substance. Late nights, overwork, and stress consume Yin and generate heat, making the mind even more restless and unfocused. Spicy, fried foods and alcohol are heating and drying, further depleting Yin fluids. Rest, hydration, and moistening foods replenish Yin and calm deficiency heat, allowing the mind to settle. A calm environment and gentle movement prevent Liver Yang from flaring up.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Prioritize rest and avoid staying up late, as sleep is the body's time to replenish Yin. Eat moistening, cooling foods like pears, black sesame, goji berries, and bone broths. Avoid spicy, fried, or overly stimulating foods and limit alcohol and caffeine. Gentle exercise such as walking, yoga, or tai chi helps move Qi without depleting Yin.

### Phlegm-Fire harassing the Heart
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/phlegm-fire-harassing-the-heart)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Mental restlessness and agitation, Thick yellow sticky phlegm in throat, Chest oppression and fullness, Flushed face and bitter taste, Insomnia with disturbing dreams
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Greasy, spicy, or rich foods, Stress and anger, Hot, stuffy environment, Overwork or lack of sleep
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Light, cooling foods, Cool, quiet environment, Gentle walking, Deep breathing exercises
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When Phlegm and Fire combine, they harass the Heart and cloud the Shen (mind). The Heart houses the Shen, and its clarity is essential for focus. Fire agitates the mind, making it restless and easily distracted, while Phlegm creates a foggy, heavy sensation that obstructs clear thinking. This is why concentration feels impossible-the mind is both racing and clouded, often accompanied by chest tightness and a sensation of being 'wired but stuck.'
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red body, swollen with a red prickly tip, and a thick, yellow, greasy coating. Pulse: slippery and rapid, especially forceful at the left Cun (Heart) position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Phlegm and Fire are fueled by rich, heating foods and emotional stress. A cooling diet and calm environment reduce the internal heat and prevent further phlegm formation, allowing the Heart's Shen to settle. Gentle movement helps Qi flow without overheating, while rest restores the clarity needed for concentration.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Avoid greasy, fried, and spicy foods that generate Phlegm and Fire. Favor light, cooling foods like pear, radish, bamboo shoots, and mung beans. Keep a regular sleep schedule and practice calming activities like gentle walking or meditation to prevent the mind from overheating.

## คำถามที่พบบ่อย (Frequently Asked Questions)
### Can acupuncture and herbs really help me focus better?
Yes, many people find that TCM treatment improves mental clarity by addressing the underlying imbalance that clouds the mind. The goal is not to force focus like a stimulant, but to nourish the organs that produce clear thinking and calm the agitation that scatters attention. Results are often gradual but lasting.

### Is poor concentration always a sign of ADHD in TCM?
No. TCM sees poor concentration as a symptom that can arise from several different patterns, only some of which correspond to the Western concept of ADHD. For example, a person with Heart and Spleen Deficiency may simply be depleted and foggy, not hyperactive at all. A proper TCM diagnosis looks at the whole picture, not just the attention difficulty.

### How long until I see results?
Many patients notice subtle improvements in mental clarity and a calmer mind within the first 2 to 3 weeks of herbs and acupuncture. For deeper deficiency patterns, full rebuilding may take 3 to 4 months. The Phlegm-Fire pattern often clears more quickly, sometimes in 4 to 6 weeks. Consistency with herbs and lifestyle changes is key.

### Can I take TCM herbs with my ADHD medication?
In most cases, yes, but it is essential to inform both your prescribing doctor and your TCM practitioner. Some herbs, such as Suan Zao Ren (Sour Jujube Seed), have mild sedative properties. Your TCM practitioner will choose a formula that supports your focus without interfering with your medication. Never stop or adjust your prescription without medical guidance.

### What foods help with concentration?
Warm, cooked foods that are easy to digest support the Spleen and help produce clear Qi and Blood. Think soups, stews, and congees. For Kidney and Liver Yin deficiency, black sesame, walnuts, and pears are nourishing. Avoid greasy, heavy, and very spicy foods, which can create Phlegm and Heat. A stable blood sugar from regular meals also helps prevent mental fog.

### Is TCM safe for children with poor concentration?
Yes, TCM is generally very safe for children when administered by a qualified practitioner. Pediatric acupuncture often uses very gentle needling or non-needle techniques like acupressure and pediatric tuina massage. Herbal formulas can be given in reduced dosages. Many parents seek TCM for their children to avoid or reduce stimulant medications.

### Why does my concentration get worse after eating?
This is a classic sign of Spleen Qi deficiency. After a meal, the Spleen directs its energy to digestion, temporarily leaving less Qi to ascend to the head and nourish the mind. The result is a post-meal fog or sleepiness. Strengthening the Spleen with herbs and diet is the primary solution.
