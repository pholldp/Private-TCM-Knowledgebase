## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนปัจจุบัน (Conventional Medicine View)
### คำอธิบาย (Description)
In conventional medicine, feeling overwhelmed is understood as a psychological symptom that often accompanies stress, anxiety, burnout, or depression. It is not a formal diagnosis itself but a signal that demands are exceeding a person's perceived ability to cope. Physiologically, chronic overwhelm is linked to elevated cortisol, an overactive sympathetic nervous system, and cognitive fatigue.
Diagnosis typically involves a clinical interview and screening tools for anxiety or mood disorders. Treatment focuses on stress management, psychotherapy, and sometimes medication, but the underlying constitutional factors that make one person more prone to feeling overwhelmed than another are rarely addressed in the same targeted way.

### การรักษาหลัก (Treatments)
Standard approaches include cognitive behavioral therapy (CBT), mindfulness-based stress reduction, lifestyle adjustments, and social support. When an underlying anxiety or depressive disorder is diagnosed, selective serotonin reuptake inhibitors (SSRIs) or other medications may be prescribed. The goal is to reduce symptom severity and improve coping skills.

## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนจีน (Traditional Chinese Medicine View)
### ความเข้าใจเกี่ยวกับการแพทย์แผนจีน (TCM Understanding)
TCM understands feeling overwhelmed primarily as a disturbance of the Heart-Spirit (Shén). The Heart houses the mind, and when the Shén is unsettled - whether by heat, deficiency, or phlegm - the mind loses its calm anchor. But the Heart is rarely the sole culprit. The Liver, Spleen, and Kidneys all play essential roles in maintaining emotional equilibrium.
The Liver is responsible for the smooth flow of Qi. When stress or frustration blocks this flow, Qi stagnates, creating a sense of tension and irritability that makes even small demands feel insurmountable. If the stagnation persists, it can generate heat that rises to agitate the Heart, turning the overwhelm into a more explosive, hot-tempered state. This is why some people feel like they might snap, while others just feel stuck and sigh a lot.
The Spleen makes Qi and Blood from food, and the Heart relies on that nourishment to house the Shén securely. Overthinking and worry weaken the Spleen, reducing its ability to produce Blood. When the Heart is undernourished, the mind becomes fragile - easily startled, anxious, and overwhelmed by tasks that used to be routine. If the Spleen's weakness also allows dampness to accumulate, that heavy, turbid dampness can rise and cloud the Heart orifice, producing a thick mental fog and a sense of being weighed down.
Chronic stress, overwork, and late nights can also burn through the body's Yin reserves - the cooling, nourishing fluids that anchor the mind. When Heart and Kidney Yin are both depleted, empty heat rises, leaving you feeling restless, dry, and overwhelmed by a deep internal unease that is hard to put into words. Finally, when Liver Qi stagnation and phlegm combine, Qi-Phlegm lodges in the chest and throat, creating a physical lump sensation and a mental stuckness that makes overwhelm feel both emotional and somatic.

### การวินิจฉัยโดยแพทย์แผนจีน (Practitioner Diagnosis)
A practitioner starts by asking what the overwhelm actually feels like and when it strikes. If it comes with chest tightness, frequent sighing, and irritability that eases after a good cry or a walk, Liver Qi Stagnation is likely. The tongue may look normal or slightly dusky, and the pulse feels wiry - like a taut guitar string. This pattern is often the earliest stage before heat or phlegm develops.
When the overwhelm turns into outright agitation, a short temper, a bitter taste in the mouth, and a flushed face, the Liver stagnation has generated heat. The tongue becomes red with a thin yellow coating, and the pulse is rapid and wiry. The person feels hot and restless, and the emotional overwhelm is more explosive than the dull pressure of simple stagnation.
If the person reports mental fatigue, a tendency to overthink, poor appetite, loose stools, and palpitations that worsen with worry, the pattern is Heart and Spleen Deficiency. The tongue is pale with a thin white coat, and the pulse is weak and thready. Here the overwhelm stems from a lack of nourishment to the Heart-Spirit, leaving the mind fragile and easily unsettled by small demands.
When overwhelm is accompanied by a heavy, foggy sensation in the head, chest oppression, nausea, and a feeling of being weighed down, Spleen Deficiency with Dampness is present. The tongue has a thick, greasy coating, and the pulse is slippery. Dampness and phlegm cloud the Heart orifice, making thinking sluggish and the world feel heavy and confusing.
Chronic stress that burns up the body’s yin can lead to Heart and Kidney Yin Deficiency. The person feels persistent unease, palpitations, night sweats, dry mouth, and a fluttering heat in the chest. The tongue is red with little or no coating, and the pulse is thin and rapid. The overwhelm is a constant background hum, worse at night and not relieved by rest alone.
A distinct pattern is Qi-Phlegm, where stagnant Qi and congealed phlegm create a sensation of a lump in the throat, chest tightness, and mental depression. The person feels stuck and overwhelmed, unable to move forward. The tongue coating is sticky, and the pulse is slippery or wiry. This pattern often follows prolonged Liver stagnation with poor digestion.

### Liver Qi Stagnation
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/liver-qi-stagnation)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Frequent sighing, Chest or rib-side distension, Feeling of a lump in the throat (plum pit sensation), Irritability that comes and goes, Worse before periods (if applicable)
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Stress, Anger, Greasy foods, Alcohol, Sedentary lifestyle, Premenstrual phase
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Gentle exercise, Deep breathing, Expressing emotions, Warm compress on chest
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  The Liver is responsible for the smooth flow of Qi throughout the body. When stress, frustration, or emotional pressure build up, that flow stalls and gets stuck. This stagnation creates a sense of tension, tightness, and irritability that makes everyday demands feel impossible to handle.
As Qi backs up in the chest and rib area, you may feel a physical weight or distension, and the constant sighing is your body's attempt to release the trapped energy. Without free movement, even small stressors can tip you into feeling completely overwhelmed.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: normal or slightly dark body, red or slightly purple sides, thin white coat. Pulse: wiry (弦 xián), taut like a guitar string, especially at the left middle position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Stress and anger directly cause Liver Qi to stagnate, which is the root of this pattern, so calming the mind and expressing emotions relieves the pressure. Movement and deep breathing physically encourage the smooth flow of Qi in the chest and ribs. Greasy foods and alcohol burden the Liver's detoxification function, making stagnation worse, while warmth relaxes the Liver channel and eases the tight sensation.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Move your body gently every day-walking, yoga, or tai chi helps Qi flow. Avoid heavy, greasy foods and alcohol, which bog down the Liver. Add aromatic herbs and spices like peppermint, fennel, and turmeric to your meals. Make space to express how you feel, whether through talking, journaling, or creative outlets.

### Liver Qi Stagnation that transforms into Heat
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/liver-qi-stagnation-that-transforms-into-heat)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Explosive anger and irritability, Bitter taste in the mouth, Distending or burning pain along the ribs, Red face and eyes, Throbbing headache at the temples
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Stress and frustration, Spicy, greasy, or fried foods, Alcohol and coffee, Hot weather or stuffy rooms, Suppressing anger
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Cooling foods (cucumber, mint), Gentle exercise, Expressing emotions, Deep breathing, Cool environment
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When stress and frustration are held in for too long, Liver Qi gets stuck. That stuck energy eventually generates heat, which rises up and disturbs the Heart-Spirit (Shen). This is why you feel constantly on edge, quick to snap, and overwhelmed by even small demands - the inner fire keeps your mind agitated.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red body with redder sides and a yellow coating. Pulse: wiry and rapid (弦数, xián shuò), especially at the left middle position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Stress and suppressed emotions are the root cause of Liver Qi stagnation; they directly feed the fire. Spicy or greasy foods and alcohol add more heat to an already overheated system, while cooling foods and calm environments help clear it. Gentle movement unblocks stagnant Qi, and safe emotional expression releases the pressure that would otherwise rise to disturb the Heart.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat cooling, light foods like cucumber, celery, and peppermint tea, and steer clear of spicy, greasy, or fried dishes and alcohol. Daily movement - a brisk walk or gentle stretching - helps keep Qi moving. Make space to vent frustrations safely, whether through journaling, talking with a friend, or deep breathing.

### Heart and Spleen Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/heart-and-spleen-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Palpitations or awareness of heartbeat, Poor appetite with bloating, Fatigue and lack of energy, Difficulty falling asleep, Sallow or pale complexion, Anxiety and feeling easily startled
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Overthinking and worry, Irregular eating habits, Raw and cold foods, Overwork and late nights
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Rest and quiet time, Warm, easy-to-digest foods, Gentle exercise, Consistent sleep schedule
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  The Spleen makes Qi and Blood from the food you eat, and the Heart houses the mind. When worry and overthinking weaken the Spleen, it can no longer produce enough nourishing Blood. The Heart then lacks proper nourishment, so the mind loses its anchor, leaving you feeling emotionally fragile, anxious, and easily overwhelmed by everyday demands.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale, tender body with teeth marks and a thin white coating. Pulse: fine (thin) and weak, especially at the left cun (Heart) and right guan (Spleen) positions.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Overthinking and worry directly weaken Spleen Qi, impairing its ability to produce Blood. Without enough Blood, the Heart-Spirit lacks a stable anchor, making you feel fragile and overwhelmed. Raw and cold foods further damage the Spleen’s digestive fire, while rest, warm meals, and gentle movement conserve and rebuild Spleen Qi, gradually restoring enough Heart Blood to calm the mind.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat regular, warm, cooked meals like soups and stews to support Spleen Qi. Avoid raw, cold, or greasy foods that tax digestion. Include blood-nourishing foods like dark leafy greens, bone broth, and dates. Gentle daily movement such as walking helps Qi and Blood flow. Most importantly, create quiet time to rest the mind and reduce overthinking.

### Spleen Deficiency with Dampness
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/spleen-deficiency-with-dampness)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Heavy, muzzy head as if wrapped, Loose stools and abdominal bloating, Sticky or greasy taste in the mouth, Drowsiness, especially after meals, Feeling of heaviness in the limbs, Chest oppression
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Greasy, heavy foods, Raw and cold foods, Overthinking and worry, Damp, humid weather, Lying down after eating
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warm, light meals, Gentle exercise, Rest after meals, Dry, sunny weather, Avoiding cold drinks
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When the Spleen is weak, it cannot transform fluids properly, so dampness and phlegm build up inside the body. This thick, turbid dampness rises to cloud the Heart orifice, making your thinking feel heavy and muddled. The sensation of physical heaviness and chest oppression adds to the mental fog, leaving you feeling weighed down and easily overwhelmed by even small tasks.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale, swollen body with teeth marks, white greasy coating, moist surface. Pulse: soggy (Ru) and slow (Huan), soft and lacking force, possibly slippery.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Dampness is a heavy, sticky pathogen that thrives on cold, raw, and greasy foods, as well as humid environments. Warm, dry conditions and light, cooked meals support the Spleen's ability to transform and transport fluids, preventing phlegm-dampness from accumulating. Overthinking directly injures Spleen Qi, so calming the mind is essential; gentle movement disperses stagnation and lifts the heavy sensation, while rest after meals allows the Spleen to focus on digestion.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat warm, cooked foods like congee, soups, and lightly steamed vegetables; avoid cold, raw, and greasy dishes that worsen dampness. Gentle daily movement such as walking or stretching helps Qi flow and lifts the heavy feeling. Avoid heavy meals before bed and try not to overthink or worry late at night, as mental strain further weakens the Spleen.

### Heart and Kidney Yin Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/heart-and-kidney-yin-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Night sweats, Palpitations or pounding heartbeat, Low back soreness or weakness, Tinnitus or dizziness, Dry mouth and throat, Warm palms, soles, and chest, Poor memory and mental restlessness
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Chronic stress and overthinking, Staying up late or shift work, Spicy, greasy, or fried foods, Hot, dry weather or overheated rooms, Overwork without rest
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Quiet, dark room, Cool environment, Warm water and herbal teas, Gentle meditation or breathwork, Consistent, early bedtime
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Chronic stress, overwork, and late nights burn through your body's cooling Yin reserves, much like a kettle boiling dry. When Heart and Kidney Yin both become depleted, there is not enough nourishing fluid to anchor the mind. This allows empty heat to rise and agitate the Heart-Spirit, leaving you feeling persistently on edge, mentally restless, and easily overwhelmed by everyday demands.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red, especially at the tip, with a thin body, little or no coating, and a dry surface; cracks may appear. Pulse: fine and rapid, with the left Kidney position especially weak.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: When Yin is depleted, the body lacks the cooling, grounding substance needed to keep the mind settled. Stress, late nights, and heating foods further consume Yin and fan the flames of empty heat, directly agitating the Heart spirit. Rest, a cool environment, and nourishing foods and herbs replenish Yin and gently clear heat, allowing the mind to feel anchored and calm again.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Protect your Yin by going to bed before 11 p.m. and avoiding late-night screen time. Eat cooling, moistening foods like pear, black sesame, millet congee, and goji berries; reduce spicy, fried, and stimulating foods. Gentle, restorative practices like tai chi, yin yoga, or meditation help conserve Yin rather than deplete it.

### Qi-Phlegm
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/qi-phlegm)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Sensation of a lump in the throat that cannot be swallowed, Chest stuffiness and upper abdominal oppression, Emotional depression, moodiness, and frequent sighing, Symptoms worsen with emotional stress, Irritability and a feeling of being stuck
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Stress and frustration, Greasy, fried, or dairy-rich foods, Sedentary lifestyle, Damp, cold weather
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Gentle exercise, Deep breathing, Warm, lightly spiced foods, Expressing emotions
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When stress and frustration build up, Liver Qi gets stuck and stops moving smoothly. Over time, that stagnant Qi disrupts fluid metabolism, causing phlegm to form. The stuck Qi and congealed phlegm rise together and lodge in the chest and throat, creating a physical sensation of a lump and a mental feeling of being trapped. This obstruction clouds the Heart orifice, leaving a person feeling foggy, depressed, and easily overwhelmed by situations they used to handle.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: normal-coloured body, possibly slightly swollen, with a white greasy or slippery coating. Pulse: wiry (Xian) and slippery (Hua).
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Stress and emotional upset are the primary drivers of Liver Qi stagnation, which directly worsens the stuck feeling and phlegm formation in this pattern. Greasy and dairy-heavy foods generate dampness that thickens into phlegm, adding to the obstruction. Gentle movement and deep breathing actively circulate Qi, helping to break through the stagnation and transform phlegm, while warm, aromatic foods support the Spleen and dry dampness, preventing further accumulation.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Avoid greasy, fried, and dairy-heavy foods that create dampness and phlegm. Instead, eat lightly cooked warm meals with aromatic herbs like ginger and cardamom. Incorporate gentle, regular movement such as walking or yoga to keep Qi flowing. Practice slow, deep breathing to release chest tension and reduce the urge to sigh.

## คำถามที่พบบ่อย (Frequently Asked Questions)
### Can acupuncture really help me feel less overwhelmed?
Yes. Acupuncture works by calming the sympathetic nervous system and regulating the flow of Qi. Many patients feel a deep sense of relaxation during and after a session, and with regular treatment that calm begins to carry over into daily life. The specific points chosen depend on your pattern - for example, points like Neiguan (PC-6) and Shenmen (HT-7) are used to settle the Heart-Spirit, while Taichong (LR-3) helps move stuck Liver Qi.

### How quickly will I notice a difference?
Some people feel more grounded and less reactive after just one or two sessions, but lasting change takes time. For excess patterns, you might notice a reduction in tension, irritability, or chest tightness within 2-4 weeks. If your overwhelm stems from long-standing deficiency, expect a more gradual improvement - often a subtle but steady increase in resilience over 1-2 months, with full benefit after several months of herbs and acupuncture.

### Can I combine TCM with my anxiety or depression medication?
Generally, yes. Many patients use TCM alongside prescribed medications. However, it is essential to tell both your TCM practitioner and your prescribing doctor about everything you are taking. Some calming herbs like Suan Zao Ren (sour jujube seed) have mild sedative properties, so they should be used carefully with medications that cause drowsiness. Never stop or adjust your medication without your doctor's guidance.

### What if I'm pregnant or breastfeeding?
Acupuncture is safe during pregnancy when performed by a qualified practitioner, and there are specific protocols for emotional wellbeing. However, many herbs are contraindicated during pregnancy. Always inform your practitioner if you are pregnant or breastfeeding so they can tailor your treatment safely. For more urgent concerns, follow the red-flag guidance in the Safety section.

### Will the results last, or will I need treatment forever?
The goal of TCM is to correct the underlying imbalance, not just suppress symptoms. Once your pattern is resolved, many patients find that their baseline resilience is stronger and that they no longer get overwhelmed as easily. Some people choose to return for seasonal tune-ups or during particularly stressful periods, but ongoing treatment is not usually necessary once the root imbalance has been addressed.

### Do I need to change my diet?
Dietary adjustments can support your recovery, but they don't have to be extreme. In general, TCM recommends eating warm, cooked meals, avoiding excessive cold or raw foods that tax the Spleen, and minimizing stimulants like caffeine and alcohol. Specific foods that calm the Shén - such as jujube dates, longan fruit, and millet - are often encouraged. Your practitioner will give you more tailored advice based on your pattern.

### Is this just stress, or is there something deeper going on?
From a TCM perspective, feeling overwhelmed is always more than just stress - it is a signal that one or more organ systems are out of balance. The patterns described on this page show how the same external stressor can produce very different internal responses depending on your constitution. By treating the underlying pattern, TCM addresses the reason you became overwhelmed in the first place, not just the stressful event.
