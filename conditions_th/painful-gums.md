## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนปัจจุบัน (Conventional Medicine View)
### คำอธิบาย (Description)
In conventional medicine, painful gums are most often caused by gingivitis or periodontitis - inflammation of the gum tissue usually triggered by plaque buildup along the gumline. The gums become red, swollen, and may bleed when brushing or flossing. If untreated, the inflammation can extend deeper, damaging the bone that supports the teeth and leading to tooth loosening or loss. Other causes include abscesses, viral infections, hormonal changes, or nutritional deficiencies. Diagnosis typically involves a dental exam, probing of gum pockets, and sometimes X-rays.

### การรักษาหลัก (Treatments)
Standard treatment focuses on removing plaque and tartar through professional cleaning (scaling and root planing) and improving home oral hygiene. Antiseptic mouthwashes, antibiotic gels, or oral antibiotics may be prescribed for infection. Pain is managed with over-the-counter anti-inflammatories like ibuprofen. For advanced periodontitis, surgical interventions such as flap surgery or bone grafts may be recommended. The emphasis is on local control of bacteria and inflammation.

## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนจีน (Traditional Chinese Medicine View)
### ความเข้าใจเกี่ยวกับการแพทย์แผนจีน (TCM Understanding)
In TCM, the gums are considered an extension of the flesh and are closely tied to the Stomach and Spleen. The Stomach channel runs directly through both the upper and lower gums, so when excessive Heat builds up in the Stomach - often from a diet rich in spicy, greasy foods or alcohol - it can flare upward along this pathway, causing bright red, swollen, and painfully bleeding gums. This is the most common acute presentation.
The Kidneys, meanwhile, govern the bones and teeth. Chronic Kidney Yin or Yang deficiency fails to nourish the gums and tooth roots, leading to a very different picture: dull, lingering pain, receding gums, and loose teeth without the fiery redness. This is why the same symptom of gum pain can stem from either an excess Heat condition or a deficiency condition, and the treatment must match the root.
External factors like Wind-Heat can also invade suddenly, causing acute gum pain with feverish sensations. And when the Spleen fails to produce enough Qi and Blood, the gums become pale, thin, and fragile, aching dully and bleeding only scantily. A TCM practitioner differentiates these patterns through the nature of the pain, the appearance of the gums, accompanying symptoms like thirst or fatigue, and the tongue and pulse picture.

### การวินิจฉัยโดยแพทย์แผนจีน (Practitioner Diagnosis)
A TCM practitioner first looks at whether the gum pain is acute and fiery or chronic and dull. Bright Yang Stomach Heat is the classic acute picture: the gums are bright red, swollen, and painful, bleed easily, and the breath is foul. The person feels thirsty and may have a red tongue with a thick yellow coat. The pulse is rapid or slippery. A history of spicy, greasy food or emotional stress often confirms this pattern.
If the same heat signs are present but the gums also develop small pus points and the tongue coating is sticky and yellow, the pattern shifts to Damp-Heat in the Stomach. The pulse feels slippery and rapid. The person may complain of a heavy sensation in the head or stomach. The practitioner distinguishes this from pure Stomach Heat by the damp, sticky quality of the coating and the presence of pus rather than just redness and swelling.
Wind-Heat invasion causes a sudden, severe flare-up of gum pain, redness, and swelling, often after exposure to a draft or a cold. The tongue may be red with a thin yellow coat, and the pulse is floating and rapid. The practitioner asks about recent chills, mild fever, or a sore throat. This pattern appears quickly and resolves once the external pathogen is cleared, unlike the deeper, more persistent Stomach Heat.
When gum problems are chronic and the teeth feel loose, Kidney Yin Deficiency is often behind the scenes. The pain is dull, worsens at night, and the gums may recede without bright redness. The tongue is red with little or no coat, and the pulse is thin and rapid. The person may also have low-back soreness, night sweats, or tinnitus. This empty-heat picture is very different from the full-heat patterns above.
Qi and Blood Deficiency produces pale, atrophied gums that bleed scantily. The pain is mild, and the person looks tired and pale, with a pale tongue and a weak, thin pulse. Dizziness and poor appetite are common. There are no heat signs, which helps the practitioner separate this from inflammatory patterns. It is often seen after prolonged illness or in people with poor nutrition.
In older or debilitated individuals, Kidney Yang Deficiency can cause a persistent, mild gum ache without any redness or swelling. The pain is often worse at night and is accompanied by cold limbs, frequent urination, and a pale, puffy tongue with a deep, weak pulse. This rare pattern lacks the heat and inflammation of other types, and the practitioner looks for these cold, deficient signs to identify it.

### Bright Yang Stomach Heat
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/bright-yang-stomach-heat)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Bright red, swollen gums that bleed easily, Foul breath and dry mouth, Intense thirst with craving for cold drinks, Pain worsens with hot food or drink, Irritability and restlessness
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Spicy, greasy, or fried foods, Alcohol and coffee, Emotional stress, Hot weather, Overwork and lack of sleep
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Cold or cool drinks and foods, Cool environment, Salt water rinses, Rest and sleep
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  The Stomach channel runs directly through the gums. When excessive Heat accumulates in the Stomach-often from a diet rich in spicy, greasy, or fried foods, alcohol, or strong emotions-it rises like a flame along this channel. The Heat inflames the gums, making them bright red, swollen, and acutely painful, and it can force the blood to move recklessly, causing easy bleeding.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red body with a yellow, dry coating. Pulse: surging (Hong) and rapid (Shu), especially forceful at the right middle (Stomach) position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Stomach Heat is an excess fiery condition; spicy, greasy foods and alcohol add fuel to the fire, while stress and overwork stir up internal heat. Cooling foods, rest, and a calm environment help bring the fire down, allowing the gums to heal.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Avoid spicy, greasy, and deep-fried foods, as well as alcohol and coffee, which add heat. Eat cooling foods like watermelon, cucumber, pear, and mint tea. Drink plenty of water to help cool the body. Use a soft-bristled toothbrush and rinse with salt water to soothe the gums.

### Damp-Heat in the Stomach
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/damp-heat-in-the-stomach)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Swollen gums with possible pus points, Sticky or greasy taste in the mouth, Feeling of heaviness in the body, Nausea or poor appetite, Thick yellow greasy tongue coating
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Greasy or fried food, Alcohol, Hot, humid weather
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Light, bland meals, Cool, dry environment, Gentle exercise (walking, tai chi)
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Dampness and Heat can brew together in the Stomach when digestion is sluggish and overheated, often from rich, greasy foods or alcohol. This turbid mixture rises along the Stomach channel, which passes directly through the gums, causing them to become swollen, sore, and heavy-feeling. The Dampness creates puffiness and a sticky coating in the mouth, while the Heat brings redness, pain, and sometimes small pus points.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red body with a thick, yellow, greasy coating that is difficult to scrape off. Pulse: slippery (滑, huá) and rapid (数, shuò), especially at the right middle position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Greasy, rich foods and alcohol directly feed Dampness and Heat in the Stomach, making the gums more swollen and painful. A hot, humid environment adds external Dampness and Heat, worsening the internal imbalance. Cooling, bland foods and gentle movement help the Stomach clear the turbidity, while a dry, cool atmosphere prevents further accumulation.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Avoid greasy, fried, and sweet foods, as well as alcohol, which all generate Dampness and Heat. Eat light, easily digested meals like congee with barley or mung beans. Gentle daily walking helps move Qi and dry internal Dampness.

### Wind-Heat
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/wind-heat)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Sudden onset of gum pain and swelling, Red, swollen gums that may bleed easily, Mild fever, chills, or aversion to wind, Sore throat or scratchy throat
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Windy or drafty environments, Spicy, greasy, or fried foods, Overwork and lack of sleep
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Cold or cool drinks and foods, Rest and sleep, Salt water rinses, Applying a cool compress to the cheek
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  External Wind-Heat invades through the mouth and nose, often lodging in the upper body first. The Stomach channel runs through the gums, so the pathogenic Heat can settle there, causing sudden redness, swelling, and pain. Wind’s nature is to rise and spread quickly, which is why the gum pain appears abruptly and can be intense.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red at the tip and edges with a thin yellow coating (may be thin white in early stages). Pulse: floating and rapid, easily felt at the surface.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Wind-Heat is an exterior pathogen that attacks the upper body. Rest and avoiding drafts allow the body’s defensive Qi to fight it off. Cooling foods and drinks counteract the Heat nature, while spicy and fried foods add internal Heat, worsening inflammation. Gargling with salt water helps clear local Heat and Toxicity from the gums.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat light, cooling foods like pears, watermelon, and mung bean soup. Drink plenty of water and chrysanthemum tea. Avoid spicy, fried, and overly hot foods that can aggravate Heat. Get extra rest and protect yourself from drafts and wind.

### Kidney Yin Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/kidney-yin-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Dull, lingering gum pain, Worse at night, Loose teeth, Receding gums, Dry mouth and throat, Night sweats, Sore lower back and knees
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Overwork and lack of sleep, Spicy, fried, or heating foods, Excessive sweating (intense exercise), Emotional stress, Hot, dry weather
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Rest and sleep, Cooling, moist foods (pears, cucumber), Gentle hydration, Gentle exercise (walking, tai chi)
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When Kidney Yin is chronically depleted, there isn't enough Yin to anchor the body's Yang, allowing empty-heat to flare upward along the channels to the gums. This produces a dull, lingering pain that is worse at night, along with loose teeth and receding gums. Unlike acute Stomach Fire, the gums may not be bright red or swollen, but they feel dry and uncomfortable.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red, thin, dry, with little or no coating; possible cracks. Pulse: fine (xi) and rapid (shu), especially weak at the left rear (Kidney) position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Overwork, late nights, and intense sweating consume Yin and fluids, making the empty-heat worse. Spicy and fried foods generate internal heat that flares upward. Rest, cooling foods, and hydration help replenish Yin and calm the deficiency fire, which reduces the upward flare to the gums.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat plenty of Yin-nourishing foods like pears, black sesame seeds, goji berries, and spinach. Avoid spicy, fried, or overly heating foods that can further deplete Yin. Go to bed early and avoid overwork or staying up late, as these burn Yin. Gentle exercise like walking or tai chi is better than intense workouts that cause sweating and fluid loss.

### Qi and Blood Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/qi-and-blood-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Pale, atrophied gums, Scant, pale bleeding when brushing, Dull ache rather than sharp pain, Fatigue and dizziness, Poor appetite
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Overwork and exhaustion, Skipping meals or irregular eating, Cold or raw foods and drinks, Emotional stress
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Rest and sleep, Warm, cooked meals, Iron-rich foods (spinach, red dates), Gentle exercise (walking, tai chi)
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Your gums rely on a steady supply of Qi and Blood to stay healthy and firm. When your Spleen and Stomach aren't producing enough of these resources, the gums become pale, thin, and fragile. They may ache dully and bleed just a little, because there isn't enough nourishment to hold the tissue together.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale body, may be thin or have teeth marks, thin white coating. Pulse: fine (thin) and weak, especially at the right Guan (Spleen/Stomach) position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Qi and Blood are produced by the Spleen from food and rest. Overwork, poor diet, and emotional strain deplete them, leaving gums undernourished and fragile. Nourishing foods and adequate rest rebuild the resources the body needs to heal the gums, while gentle movement supports circulation without draining energy.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat warm, cooked meals with moderate amounts of iron-rich foods like spinach, red dates, and lean red meat. Bone broth and congee are especially easy to digest and help build Qi and Blood. Avoid raw, cold foods and iced drinks that chill the Spleen. Prioritize rest and a regular sleep schedule, and try gentle movement like walking or tai chi to build energy without depleting it.

### Kidney Yang Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/kidney-yang-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Dull, lingering ache, not sharp, Gums are pale, not red or swollen, Worse at night and with cold, Cold sensation in the lower back and knees, Fatigue and low spirits
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Cold weather, Cold or raw foods and drinks, Overwork and fatigue, Overwork and lack of sleep
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warmth on the jaw, Warm foods and drinks, Rest and sleep, Moxibustion on lower back
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Kidney Yang is the body's warming fire. When it weakens, cold settles in and the gums lose their gentle warmth and nourishment. This produces a dull, lingering ache that feels deep in the tooth sockets, without the redness or swelling of a heat condition.
The pain is typically worse at night because Yang is at its lowest ebb then, and it improves with warmth. This pattern is most often seen in older adults or people who are constitutionally cold and depleted.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale, puffy, tender body with teeth marks and a wet white coating. Pulse: deep, slow, and weak, especially at the Chi (rear) positions.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Kidney Yang is the body's warming fire. Cold, raw foods, and overwork drain this fire further, allowing cold to settle in the gums and making the dull ache worse. Warmth, rest, and moxibustion gently support and restore Kidney Yang, bringing relief by warming the channels and nourishing the gums.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Keep the lower back and feet warm, and apply a warm (not hot) compress to the jaw. Eat warming, cooked foods like soups, stews, ginger, and cinnamon, and avoid raw, cold, or icy items. Gentle movement such as walking or tai chi helps, but rest when tired. Moxibustion on the lower back can be especially supportive.

## คำถามที่พบบ่อย (Frequently Asked Questions)
### Can acupuncture really help with gum pain?
Yes. Acupuncture can reduce inflammation and pain by clearing Heat from the Stomach channel and calming the local area. Points like Hegu (LI-4) and Jiache (ST-6) are commonly used to move Qi and Blood in the jaw. Many patients feel a noticeable easing of discomfort during or shortly after a session, especially in acute cases.

### Is it safe to take Chinese herbs for gum pain while I'm on antibiotics?
Generally yes, but you should always inform both your dentist and TCM practitioner about all medications you are taking. The herbal formulas used for gum pain, such as Qing Wei San, are typically well-tolerated and have a low risk of interaction. However, if your antibiotic regimen includes metronidazole, avoid alcohol-based herbal tinctures. A qualified practitioner will adjust the formula if needed.

### How long does it take for herbal mouth rinses to work?
Herbal rinses made from cooling herbs like Huang Lian can soothe hot, swollen gums within a day or two of regular use. They work best as part of a full treatment plan that also addresses the internal imbalance with teas or powders. For acute Stomach Heat, you may feel relief after just a few rinses, but deeper patterns like Kidney deficiency won't respond to rinses alone.

### Why does my gum pain get worse at night?
Nighttime worsening often points to a Yin deficiency pattern, especially Kidney Yin Deficiency. Yin energy is naturally quieter at night, and when it's already depleted, the relative excess of Yang (empty heat) can flare upward, causing dull, throbbing gum pain. This is different from Stomach Heat, which tends to be constant or aggravated by hot foods. Your practitioner will check for other signs like night sweats or a dry mouth.

### Can TCM treat receding gums?
TCM can help slow or halt further recession by nourishing the underlying deficiencies that weaken the gums. If the recession is due to Kidney Yin Deficiency, herbs like Shu Di Huang and Gu Sui Bu are used to strengthen the bones and gums. However, lost gum tissue will not grow back. The goal is to preserve what remains and eliminate pain and bleeding. Consistent treatment over several months yields the best results.

### Do I still need to see a dentist if I use TCM?
Absolutely. TCM and conventional dentistry are complementary. A dentist can remove tartar, treat abscesses, and assess structural damage that herbs cannot reverse. TCM addresses the internal imbalances that make you prone to gum problems. We recommend maintaining regular dental check-ups and cleanings alongside TCM treatment.

## การวิจัยทางวิทยาศาสตร์สมัยใหม่ (Modern Scientific Research)
1. **Clinical observation on Qingwei San in the treatment of chronic periodontitis**
   *วารสารวิชาการ: Wang J, Li M, Zhang H. Clinical observation on Qingwei San in the treatment of chronic periodontitis. Chinese Journal of Integrated Traditional and Western Medicine. 2015;35(8):945-948.*
   This study evaluated Qing Wei San as an adjunct to scaling and root planing for chronic periodontitis. Patients receiving the herbal formula showed significantly greater reductions in gingival bleeding and probing depth compared to the control group after 3 months.

2. **Acupuncture for acute dental pain: a systematic review of randomized controlled trials**
   *วารสารวิชาการ: Liu Y, Zhang X. Acupuncture for acute dental pain: a systematic review of randomized controlled trials. Evidence-Based Complementary and Alternative Medicine. 2018;2018:6071983.*
   This systematic review analysed 16 RCTs on acupuncture for dental pain, including gum pain. It concluded that acupuncture, especially at LI-4 and ST-6, provides effective short-term pain relief and can reduce the need for analgesics, though the quality of evidence was limited by heterogeneity.
