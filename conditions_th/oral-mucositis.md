## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนปัจจุบัน (Conventional Medicine View)
### คำอธิบาย (Description)
Oral mucositis is the painful inflammation and ulceration of the mucous membranes lining the mouth. It is most often a side effect of chemotherapy or radiation therapy to the head and neck, but can also arise from infections, autoimmune conditions, or nutritional deficiencies. The sores can make eating, drinking, and even talking difficult, and they increase the risk of local and systemic infection. Diagnosis is usually made by a doctor based on the appearance of the ulcers and the patient's medical history.

### การรักษาหลัก (Treatments)
Conventional management focuses on pain relief, oral hygiene, and protecting the mucosa. This often includes topical anesthetics (like lidocaine), coating agents, antimicrobial mouth rinses, and systemic painkillers. For severe chemotherapy-related mucositis, palifermin (a growth factor) may be used to stimulate mucosal cell growth. Treatment may also involve dose reduction or temporary breaks in cancer therapy.

## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนจีน (Traditional Chinese Medicine View)
### ความเข้าใจเกี่ยวกับการแพทย์แผนจีน (TCM Understanding)
TCM understands oral mucositis as a visible sign of internal disharmony, most often involving the Heart, Spleen, Stomach, and Kidney systems. The Heart opens into the tongue, the Spleen opens into the mouth, and the Stomach channel runs through the gums and cheeks. So when any of these organs are out of balance, the mouth is one of the first places to show it.
Excess heat is the most common culprit. Emotional stress or anxiety can stir up Heart Fire, which blazes upward along the Heart channel to the tip of the tongue, causing acute, burning ulcers. Overeating spicy, greasy, or fried foods can generate Stomach Fire, which rises to cause multiple painful sores with foul breath and constipation. In both cases, the problem is too much heat - the treatment is to clear that heat and guide it downward.
But not all mouth ulcers are from real heat. When Kidney Yin is depleted by overwork, chronic illness, or aging, it can no longer anchor the body's warming Yang. This unattached Yang floats upward as empty-heat, producing recurrent, shallow ulcers that burn more at night and come with a dry mouth and a red, peeled tongue. Conversely, when Kidney and Spleen Yang are weak, deep internal cold can push the remaining warmth upward as a 'floating' fire, causing pale, stubborn ulcers that heal slowly and are accompanied by cold limbs and fatigue. TCM treats these by nourishing Yin or warming Yang, not by clearing heat.
Damp-Heat and Blood Stagnation add further layers. A weak Spleen can fail to manage fluids, leading to Dampness that turns into Heat and steams upward, causing ulcers with a sticky taste and greasy tongue coating. Long-standing ulcers can damage local blood vessels, leading to stasis - dark, fixed, stabbing sores that worsen at night. Each pattern requires a fundamentally different herbal formula and acupuncture strategy.

### การวินิจฉัยโดยแพทย์แผนจีน (Practitioner Diagnosis)
A TCM practitioner begins by asking where the ulcer is and what it feels like. Heart Fire blazing typically produces a burning ulcer right on the tip of the tongue, with a bright red tongue tip and a rapid pulse. The person often feels restless, irritable, and may have trouble sleeping, pointing to emotional stress as a trigger.
When Stomach Fire is the culprit, ulcers appear in multiple spots on the cheeks, gums, or lips. The pain is sharp and burning, with a hot sensation in the mouth. Bad breath, intense thirst, craving for cold drinks, and constipation are common. The tongue is red with a thick yellow coating, and the pulse is rapid and forceful.
Recurrent, shallow ulcers that are mildly painful and worsen at night suggest Kidney Yin Deficiency with Empty-Heat. The mouth feels dry, the throat is sore, and there may be night sweats or a low-grade fever. The tongue is red with little or no coating, and the pulse is thin and rapid. This often follows prolonged illness or overwork.
If the ulcers are pale, few in number, and stubbornly slow to heal, the practitioner suspects Kidney and Spleen Yang Deficiency. The person feels cold, fatigued, and may have loose stools. The tongue is pale and swollen, and the pulse is deep and weak. This pattern reflects a deeper exhaustion where the body’s warming fire has faded.
Damp-Heat invading the Spleen shows recurrent, shallow ulcers with a greasy yellow coating on the tongue and a heavy, sluggish feeling. The person often feels bloated, has a poor appetite, and a sticky taste in the mouth. The pulse feels slippery or soggy. This arises from a weak Spleen that fails to manage fluids, creating dampness that heats up.
In chronic, long-standing cases where ulcers look dark or purplish and cause a fixed, stabbing pain, Blood Stagnation is considered. The tongue may be purplish or have dark spots, and the pulse is choppy. This pattern often appears when other treatments have not resolved the issue, and the pain is more intense and localized.

## กลุ่มอาการการแพทย์แผนจีนและการรักษา (TCM Patterns and Treatment)

### Heart Fire blazing
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/heart-fire-blazing)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Burning, very painful ulcers on the tip or edges of the tongue, Red, swollen edges around the sores, Red tongue tip, often with swollen red prickles, Mental restlessness, anxiety, and insomnia, Thirst with a desire for cold drinks, Bitter taste in the mouth, especially after poor sleep
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Spicy, fried, or greasy foods, Emotional stress, anger, or frustration, Alcohol and coffee, Overwork and late nights, Hot weather or environment
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Cooling foods and drinks, Rest and adequate sleep, Gentle exercise or movement
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  The Heart opens into the tongue, and its channel runs up to the mouth. When emotional stress, anxiety, or excessive joy stirs up Heart Fire, the heat blazes upward along this pathway. This scorches the tongue and lining of the mouth, producing acute, burning ulcers with red, swollen edges, especially on the tongue tip.
The same fire disturbs the mind, leading to restlessness, insomnia, and palpitations that often accompany the sores.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red body with a distinctly redder tip, often showing swollen red prickles at the tip, and a dry yellow coating. A midline crack may extend toward the tip in chronic cases. Pulse: rapid, forceful, and overflowing, especially at the left front (Heart) position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Heart Fire is an excess heat condition that flares upward when the mind is agitated or the body is overheated. Spicy food, alcohol, and emotional stress directly add fire to the Heart, making the tongue ulcers worse. Cooling foods and drinks, rest, and stress reduction help to clear heat and calm the mind, allowing the fire to subside and the ulcers to heal.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Avoid spicy, greasy, and deep-fried foods, as well as alcohol and coffee, which add heat. Eat cooling foods like mung beans, cucumber, watermelon, and bitter gourd. Drink plenty of room-temperature water. Practice stress-reducing activities such as meditation, gentle yoga, or walking in nature to calm the mind. Ensure adequate sleep and avoid staying up late, which can deplete Yin and allow fire to flare.

### Stomach Fire (Stomach Heat)
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/stomach-fire-stomach-heat)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Multiple painful ulcers with red, swollen edges, Burning pain in the mouth and gums, Foul breath (halitosis), Intense thirst with desire for cold drinks, Constipation with dry, hard stools, Excessive hunger or constant appetite, Swollen, painful, or bleeding gums
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Spicy, fried, or greasy foods, Alcohol and coffee, Emotional stress, anger, or frustration, Overeating or irregular meals, Constipation
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Cooling foods and drinks, Regular bowel movements, Rest and adequate sleep
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  The Stomach is the furnace of digestion. Overeating spicy, greasy, or fried foods, drinking too much alcohol, or holding in strong emotions like frustration can generate excess heat in the Stomach. This fire has nowhere to go but up, following the Stomach channel directly into the gums, tongue, and lining of the mouth, where it scorches the tissues and creates multiple painful ulcers with red, swollen edges. The heat also burns off fluids, leaving you intensely thirsty and constipated.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red body, often with red prickles in the center, thick dry yellow coating. Pulse: rapid, slippery, and forceful, especially strong at the right Guan (Stomach) position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Spicy and greasy foods directly generate heat in the Stomach, while alcohol and coffee add damp-heat that flares upward. Emotional stress stagnates Liver Qi, which invades the Stomach and transforms into fire. Cooling foods and drinks counteract this heat, and regular bowel movements help the body eliminate it downward. Rest and calm prevent Qi stagnation, breaking the cycle of heat production.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Avoid spicy, fried, and greasy foods, as well as alcohol and coffee, which add fuel to the Stomach fire. Eat plenty of cooling, moistening foods like cucumber, watermelon, pear, and mung bean soup. Drink warm or room-temperature water throughout the day, and keep your bowel movements regular with fiber-rich vegetables. Gentle exercise like walking can help move Qi and prevent stagnation that turns into heat.

### Kidney Yin Deficiency With Empty-Heat Blazing
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/kidney-yin-deficiency-with-empty-heat-blazing)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Shallow, recurrent ulcers, often on the tongue or inner cheeks, Burning pain that worsens in the evening and at night, Dry mouth and throat, desire for small sips of water, Five-palm heat, night sweats, malar flush, Lower back soreness and weakness, tinnitus, Red tongue with little or no coating, possible cracks
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Overwork and late nights, Spicy, fried, or greasy foods, Alcohol and coffee, Emotional stress, anger, or frustration, Hot weather or environment
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Rest and adequate sleep, Cooling foods and drinks, Gentle exercise or movement
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Kidney Yin is the body's cooling, moistening foundation. When it becomes depleted-often through overwork, chronic illness, or aging-it can no longer anchor the body's warming Yang energy. This unattached Yang rises as empty-heat, flaring upward along the Kidney channel to the mouth and tongue. The result is recurrent, shallow ulcers that feel dry and burn, especially at night when Yin is naturally lowest.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red, thin, dry body with little or no coating, possible cracks. Pulse: fine (xi) and rapid (shu), possibly floating empty, with a weak chi (rear) position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Late nights and overwork drain Kidney Yin, which is the root of this pattern, so the empty-heat flares more strongly. Spicy foods and alcohol add external heat to a system already struggling with internal heat. Rest and cooling, moistening foods nourish Yin and help the body's natural cooling function regain control, calming the fire. Gentle movement keeps Qi and fluids flowing without further depletion, while stress reduction prevents the emotional heat that can rise and aggravate the mouth.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Avoid spicy, fried, and overly heating foods, as well as alcohol and coffee, which further deplete Yin. Emphasize moistening, cooling foods like pear, lily bulb, black sesame, and tofu. Sip warm water throughout the day and avoid eating late at night. Prioritize adequate sleep and gentle exercise like walking or tai chi, and protect time for rest to rebuild Yin reserves.

### Kidney and Spleen Yang Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/kidney-and-spleen-yang-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Pale, few, slow-healing ulcers, Cold hands and feet, Chronic fatigue and lack of energy, Loose stools or early-morning diarrhea, Sore and cold lower back, Poor appetite with abdominal bloating
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Cold, raw, or dairy foods, Overwork and late nights, Exposure to cold weather, Emotional stress, anger, or frustration
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warm foods and drinks, Rest and adequate sleep, Keeping the body warm, Moxibustion on the lower abdomen
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When the warming fire of the Kidney and the digestive fire of the Spleen are both weak, the body's internal climate turns cold. This deep coldness pushes the body's remaining warmth upward, creating a 'floating' deficient fire that settles in the mouth. The ulcers that appear are not from real heat but from a lack of yang to anchor the yin, so they look pale, are few in number, and heal very slowly because the body's repair energy is low.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale, swollen, puffy with teeth marks and a white slippery coating. Pulse: deep, weak, and slow; both Chi positions are especially feeble.

## คำถามที่พบบ่อย (Frequently Asked Questions)
### Can TCM help with mouth ulcers caused by chemotherapy or radiation?
Yes. TCM is often used alongside cancer treatment to reduce the severity of oral mucositis and speed healing. Herbal formulas are chosen to clear heat, nourish Yin, and promote tissue repair without interfering with the cancer therapy. However, it is critical to inform both your oncologist and your TCM practitioner of all treatments you are receiving, as some herbs can affect liver enzymes or interact with chemotherapy agents.

### How quickly does acupuncture relieve mouth ulcer pain?
Many people notice a reduction in pain within a few hours of the first acupuncture session, especially when points like Hegu (LI-4) are used. For acute, burning ulcers, regular acupuncture (1-2 times per week) combined with herbs can bring significant relief within a few days. Chronic, recurrent ulcers may take a few weeks to respond fully.

### What foods should I avoid if I get frequent mouth ulcers?
In general, it's best to avoid spicy, greasy, deep-fried, or very sweet foods, as these can generate internal heat and dampness. Alcohol and coffee can also aggravate heat patterns. Instead, favor cooling, moistening foods like cucumber, watermelon, pear, and lightly cooked green vegetables. If your ulcers are pale and you feel cold, your practitioner may recommend warming foods instead - so the diet is always tailored to your specific pattern.

### Does the location of the ulcer really matter in TCM?
Yes. The tip of the tongue is closely linked to the Heart, so ulcers there often point to Heart Fire, especially if accompanied by restlessness or insomnia. Ulcers on the gums or inner cheeks are more often related to the Stomach or Spleen channels. The specific location is one of the clues your practitioner uses to identify the underlying pattern.

### Can children use Chinese herbs for mouth ulcers?
Yes, Chinese herbal medicine can be safely used for children when prescribed by a qualified practitioner who adjusts the dosage to the child's age and weight. Pediatric mouth ulcers are often due to digestive heat or food stagnation, and gentle, targeted formulas can clear the heat and promote healing quickly.

### Will my mouth ulcers come back after TCM treatment?
The goal of TCM treatment is not just to heal the current ulcer but to correct the underlying imbalance that causes them. While no treatment can guarantee that you'll never have another ulcer, many people find that the frequency and severity of recurrences drop significantly after a course of herbs and acupuncture, especially when dietary and lifestyle advice is followed.

### Is it safe to use Chinese herbs while I'm on painkillers or other medications?
In most cases, yes, but you must always inform your TCM practitioner of all medications you are taking. Some herbs used for mouth ulcers, such as those that move blood or clear heat, may interact with anticoagulants or certain cancer drugs. Your practitioner will choose a formula that is safe for your specific situation and may advise spacing out the timing of herbs and medications.

1. **Acupuncture for the prevention of chemotherapy-induced oral mucositis in patients with cancer**
   *วารสารวิชาการ: Zhang HW, Lin ZX, Cheung F, Cho WC, Tang JL. Acupuncture for the prevention of chemotherapy-induced oral mucositis in patients with cancer. Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews 2014, Issue 2. Art. No.: CD009556.*

   *วารสารวิชาการ: Li Y, Zhang J, Wang L, et al. Efficacy and safety of Chinese herbal medicine for recurrent aphthous stomatitis: a systematic review and meta-analysis. Journal of Ethnopharmacology. 2020; 256: 112785.*
