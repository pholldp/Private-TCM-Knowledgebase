## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนปัจจุบัน (Conventional Medicine View)
### คำอธิบาย (Description)
The abducens nerve is the sixth cranial nerve, and it controls the lateral rectus muscle that pulls the eye outward. When this nerve is damaged or compressed, the eye cannot move fully toward the temple, causing horizontal double vision that worsens when looking toward the affected side. The condition can be idiopathic, or it can result from diabetes, high blood pressure, head trauma, or microvascular ischemia.
Diagnosis is usually made through a clinical eye exam and may be confirmed with MRI or CT scans to rule out more serious causes. Many cases resolve on their own within weeks to months, but some patients are left with persistent double vision or require intervention.

### การรักษาหลัก (Treatments)
Standard care often starts with watchful waiting, as many sixth nerve palsies recover spontaneously. Prism glasses or an eye patch may be used temporarily to manage double vision. If an underlying condition like diabetes or hypertension is present, controlling it is key. In cases that don't improve after several months, surgery to realign the eyes may be considered.

## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนจีน (Traditional Chinese Medicine View)
### ความเข้าใจเกี่ยวกับการแพทย์แผนจีน (TCM Understanding)
In TCM, the eyes are not just optical instruments - they are extensions of the Liver and its partner, the Gallbladder. The Liver opens into the eyes, and the Gallbladder channel runs directly through the temple and around the eye socket. When the smooth flow of Qi and blood through these channels is disrupted, the eye muscles lose their coordination and the gaze drifts.
The most common disruptors are Wind and Phlegm. External Wind can invade suddenly - think of sitting in a draft or being exposed to cold wind - and lodge in the eye's channels, causing acute deviation. When the Spleen is weak and fails to transform fluids, dampness thickens into Phlegm; this sticky substance can combine with internal Wind and clog the tiny collaterals that feed the eye muscles, leading to gradual onset with a heavy sensation.
Less often, Liver Yang rises because the Yin that anchors it is depleted. The unrestrained Yang surges upward like a storm, carrying internal Wind that yanks the eye muscles off balance. This pattern often comes with a hot temper, throbbing headache, and dizziness. Because each root cause - external Wind, Phlegm, or rising Yang - produces a different set of accompanying signs and a different tongue and pulse picture, TCM practitioners can pinpoint the pattern and treat it precisely.

### การวินิจฉัยโดยแพทย์แผนจีน (Practitioner Diagnosis)
A TCM practitioner starts by asking how suddenly the eye deviation appeared and what other sensations accompany it. The speed of onset, associated symptoms, and the appearance of the tongue and pulse are the main clues that distinguish the three common patterns behind abducens nerve paralysis.
If the eye deviation came on gradually and is accompanied by a heavy sensation in the head, chest tightness, and nausea, the pattern is likely Wind-Phlegm (风痰阻络, fēng tán zǔ luò) obstructing the eye collaterals. The tongue often looks puffy with a white greasy coating, and the pulse feels wiry and slippery. This is the most common presentation, where dampness and phlegm combine with internal wind to block the eye muscles.
When the strabismus appears suddenly, often after exposure to wind or a cold, and is accompanied by dizziness and a mild aversion to cold, the picture points to external Wind invading the channels (风邪中络, fēng xié zhōng luò). The tongue may be pale with a thin coating, and the pulse is typically floating and rapid. This acute pattern suggests that a wind pathogen directly struck the eye area before deeper phlegm or heat developed.
If the eye deviation is paired with marked irritability, a red face, a bitter taste in the mouth, and a throbbing headache or dizziness, the underlying driver is likely Liver Yang Rising (肝阳上亢, gān yáng shàng kàng). The tongue appears red and dry, sometimes with a yellow coating, and the pulse is wiry and rapid. Here, internal wind generated by rising Liver Yang disturbs the eye system, usually in people with chronic stress or hypertension.

## กลุ่มอาการการแพทย์แผนจีนและการรักษา (TCM Patterns and Treatment)

### Wind-Phlegm
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/wind-phlegm)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Eye deviation (strabismus), Double vision, Chest stuffiness or tightness, Nausea or vomiting of phlegm, Heavy sensation in the head or body, Copious white, frothy sputum
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Exposure to wind or drafts, Raw or cold foods and drinks, Overeating rich or fried meals, Damp, humid weather, Stress and frustration
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Avoiding dairy and greasy foods, Warm ginger tea, Keeping the head and neck covered, Gentle walking or stretching, Dry, warm environment
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When the Spleen is weak, it fails to transform fluids, allowing dampness to build into phlegm. This phlegm then combines with internal Wind and rises to obstruct the tiny channels that control eye movement. The muscles lose their coordination, so the eye drifts out of alignment and you see double.
The chest tightness, nausea, and heavy-headed feeling happen because phlegm is also clogging the chest and head, making everything feel sluggish and stuck.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: normal or slightly pale and swollen with teeth marks, white greasy coating. Pulse: wiry and slippery (Xian Hua).
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Phlegm forms when the Spleen cannot handle dampness, so heavy, greasy, and cold foods make the condition worse by adding more dampness and phlegm. Warm, dry foods and ginger tea help the Spleen transform fluids and cut through phlegm. Wind exposure directly stirs up internal Wind, which then carries phlegm upward to the eyes, so staying covered and warm keeps things settled. Gentle movement encourages Qi to flow and stops phlegm from stagnating, while stress can aggravate Liver Qi and stir Wind, making the eye deviation more pronounced.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Cut out greasy, fried, and dairy-rich foods that create dampness and phlegm. Eat warm, cooked meals like rice congee with a little ginger. Avoid cold raw salads and icy drinks. Keep your neck and head covered in windy weather, and do gentle daily movement like walking to keep Qi flowing and phlegm from settling.

### Wind invading the Channels joints and muscles
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/wind-invading-the-channels-joints-and-muscles)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Sudden eye deviation (strabismus), Double vision (diplopia), Limited eye movement, Dizziness, Mild chills or aversion to wind
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Exposure to wind or drafts, Cold weather, Air conditioning blowing directly on the face, Raw or cold foods and drinks
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Keeping the head and neck covered, Resting in a draft-free room, Warm compresses over the eye
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  External Wind is a pathogen that can enter the body through the surface and travel along the channels. When it lodges in the delicate channels that supply the eye muscles, it obstructs the normal flow of Qi and blood. This disruption prevents the eye muscles from coordinating properly, leading to sudden eye deviation and double vision. The acute onset, often following exposure to a draft or cold wind, is a hallmark of this pattern.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: normal or pale body with a thin white coating. Pulse: floating (Fú) and rapid, may be slightly tight.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: External Wind entered the channels through the surface, so any further wind or cold exposure pushes it deeper and worsens the blockage. Keeping the face and neck warm helps the body expel the pathogen, while warm foods support the defensive Qi and prevent Cold from lodging in the muscles. Rest allows Qi to focus on clearing the channel obstruction and restoring normal eye alignment.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Avoid drafts, fans, and air conditioning directly on the face and neck. Wear a scarf or high collar in windy weather. Eat warm, cooked foods like soups and stews, and avoid raw, cold, or icy items that can invite Wind and Cold deeper into the channels.

### Liver Yang Rising
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/liver-yang-rising)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Sudden eye deviation with dizziness and vertigo, Irritability and quick temper, Flushed face and red eyes, Throbbing or distending headache at the temples, Dry mouth and throat, Lower back and knee soreness and weakness
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Anger and frustration, Spicy and greasy foods, Alcohol and coffee, Lack of sleep, Hot weather
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Rest and closing the eyes, Cooling foods (cucumber, pear), Stress reduction and relaxation, Dark, quiet environment
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When Liver Yin is depleted, the anchor that holds Liver Yang down weakens. The unrestrained Yang surges upward as internal Wind, disturbing the delicate channels that control eye movement. This internal storm can suddenly pull the eye muscles off balance, causing the eye to deviate, while the heat and wind in the upper body produce irritability, a throbbing headache, and dizziness.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red body, especially red at the sides, with a thin dry yellow or scanty coat, possibly trembling. Pulse: wiry (弦 xián), rapid (数 shuò), and possibly fine (细 xì).
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Anger and emotional stress directly stir Liver Yang upward, triggering the internal Wind that pulls the eye off center. Spicy, greasy foods and alcohol generate excess heat and further damage Liver Yin, making the Yang even more unanchored. Rest, cooling foods, and a calm environment help nourish Yin and settle the rising Yang, allowing the eye muscles to relax back into balance.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat cooling, Yin-nourishing foods like spinach, cucumber, pear, and celery. Avoid spicy, greasy, and fried foods, as well as alcohol and coffee, which add heat and deplete Yin. Prioritize stress management and adequate sleep; even short periods of rest with the eyes closed can help. Gentle exercise like walking or tai chi supports smooth Liver Qi flow without overstraining.

## คำถามที่พบบ่อย (Frequently Asked Questions)
### How quickly can acupuncture improve double vision?
Many patients notice a subtle improvement in eye movement or a reduction in double vision after 4-6 sessions. Full realignment typically takes several weeks of consistent treatment. Acute cases from a recent wind invasion tend to respond faster than chronic, phlegm-heavy patterns.

### Can I combine TCM herbs with my blood pressure or diabetes medication?
Yes, in most cases TCM treatment can safely complement your existing medications. However, some herbs used for Wind-Phlegm or Liver Yang patterns, such as Tian Ma (Gastrodia) or Gou Teng (Uncaria), can influence blood pressure. Always inform both your TCM practitioner and your prescribing doctor about everything you are taking so they can coordinate care and monitor for any interactions.

### Will I need to change my diet?
Diet plays a supportive role. If your pattern involves Phlegm, you'll be advised to avoid dairy, greasy foods, and cold drinks, which can create more dampness and phlegm. For Liver Yang rising, cutting back on spicy, fried, and stimulating foods helps calm internal wind. Simple, warm, cooked meals like congee and ginger tea are universally beneficial during recovery.

### Is acupuncture around the eye safe?
Absolutely, when performed by a trained professional. The points around the eye are needled very superficially with fine, single-use needles. You may feel a mild sensation or brief pressure, but it is generally not painful. Occasionally a tiny bruise may appear, which fades quickly. The technique is gentle and specifically designed to avoid injury to the eyeball.

### What if my double vision comes back after treatment?
Recurrence is uncommon if the underlying pattern has been fully resolved, but it can happen if the original trigger - such as stress, dietary habits, or exposure to wind - returns. In such cases, a short course of booster acupuncture and herbs usually restores stability. Your practitioner will also give you lifestyle tips to prevent relapse.

### Does TCM work if the nerve has been paralyzed for months?
Yes, but the timeline may be longer. Even chronic cases can benefit from acupuncture's ability to reawaken dormant channels and herbs that dissolve stubborn phlegm or nourish deficient Liver Yin. The key is consistent treatment over 8-12 weeks, and patience - the body's repair mechanisms can be re-engaged even after a long delay.

   *วารสารวิชาการ: Zhang XX, Li XX, et al. Zhongguo Zhen Jiu. 2024;44(9):1021-1024.*
   This case series treated 13 patients with acquired extraocular muscle paralysis using acupuncture directly at the attachment points of the affected muscles. After a course of treatment, most patients showed marked improvement in eye alignment and reduction of diplopia. The study suggests that local needling can help release muscle tension and restore coordinated eye movement.

2. **Qian-Zheng-San promotes regeneration after sciatic nerve crush injury in rats** ([ลิงก์](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6352607/))
   *วารสารวิชาการ: Li M, et al. Neural Regen Res. 2019;14(3):470-479.*
   This animal study investigated the neuroregenerative effects of the classic TCM formula Qian Zheng San. Rats with sciatic nerve crush injury treated with the formula showed faster functional recovery and increased expression of nerve growth factors. Although not directly on abducens nerve, it supports the traditional use of wind-dispelling, channel-opening formulas for nerve damage.
