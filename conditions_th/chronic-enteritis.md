## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนปัจจุบัน (Conventional Medicine View)
### คำอธิบาย (Description)
Chronic diarrhea is defined as loose or watery stools occurring three or more times per day for at least four weeks. It can stem from many causes, including infections, inflammatory bowel diseases like Crohn's or ulcerative colitis, malabsorption disorders such as celiac disease, irritable bowel syndrome, and medication side effects. Diagnosis typically involves stool tests, blood work, and sometimes endoscopy or imaging to identify the underlying condition.

### การรักษาหลัก (Treatments)
Treatment depends on the cause. For infections, antibiotics or antiparasitics may be used. Inflammatory conditions may be managed with anti-inflammatory drugs, immunosuppressants, or biologics. Functional disorders like IBS are often addressed with dietary changes, fiber supplements, antidiarrheal medications such as loperamide, and stress management. Probiotics are sometimes recommended to restore gut flora.

## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนจีน (Traditional Chinese Medicine View)
### ความเข้าใจเกี่ยวกับการแพทย์แผนจีน (TCM Understanding)
In TCM, the Spleen is the central organ of digestion - it transforms food into Qi and separates the pure from the impure. When Spleen Qi is strong, stools are well-formed and regular. But if the Spleen becomes weak from poor diet, overwork, or illness, it fails to manage fluids. Dampness accumulates and pours down into the intestines, resulting in chronic loose stools. This is the most common root of persistent diarrhea, often accompanied by bloating, fatigue, and a pale, puffy tongue with tooth marks.
The Kidneys provide the body's foundational warmth, stoking the digestive fire. Over time, if Kidney Yang declines - due to aging, chronic stress, or constitutional weakness - the Spleen loses its heat source. This leads to a specific type of diarrhea that strikes in the early morning (around 5 a.m.), often with undigested food in the stool, cold limbs, and a sore lower back. The tongue is pale and wet, and the pulse feels deep and weak.
The Liver also plays a key role. It ensures the smooth flow of Qi throughout the body. When emotional stress, frustration, or anger block this flow, the Liver Qi can 'rebel' sideways and attack the Spleen. The result is abdominal pain that suddenly appears, followed by an urgent need to defecate, with the pain easing after the bowel movement. This pattern is tightly linked to stress and often comes with irritability or rib-side discomfort.
Because these three organ systems can be involved alone or in combination, chronic diarrhea is never a one-size-fits-all condition in TCM. A practitioner will examine your stool consistency, timing, accompanying symptoms, tongue, and pulse to determine which pattern is dominant - and then design a treatment that strengthens the Spleen, warms the Kidneys, or harmonizes the Liver as needed.

### การวินิจฉัยโดยแพทย์แผนจีน (Practitioner Diagnosis)
To tell which pattern is driving chronic diarrhea, a practitioner first looks at the stool itself and the timing. When the main problem is simply loose, unformed stools that come and go without a fixed hour, and the person feels easily tired after eating, Spleen Qi Deficiency is likely. The tongue is pale with a thin white coat, and the pulse feels weak - signs that the Spleen’s transforming power has simply run low, without a heavy dampness or cold complication.
If the diarrhea is watery, leaves a heavy or sticky sensation in the abdomen, and the tongue shows a noticeably greasy white coating, the picture shifts to Spleen Deficiency with Dampness. Here the weak Spleen has allowed dampness to accumulate, so the stools are wetter and the body feels heavy. The pulse is often soft and weak, telling the practitioner that dampness is now a distinct layer that needs its own attention alongside tonifying the Spleen.
When the diarrhea strikes predictably in the very early morning, often around 5 a.m., and is accompanied by cold hands and feet, a sore lower back and weak knees, the root has moved deeper to Kidney and Spleen Yang Deficiency. The tongue is pale and puffy with a wet, slippery coat, and the pulse is deep and slow - a clear signal that the body’s warming fire has dimmed and the Spleen can no longer hold fluids through the night.
If abdominal cramps and an urgent need to pass stool flare up during or right after emotional stress, and the diarrhea brings relief, the pattern is Rebellious Liver Qi invading the Spleen. The tongue may look normal or slightly dusky, often with a thin white coat, while the pulse feels wiry - especially on the left side. The practitioner asks about stress, irritability, and whether the pain moves around the rib area, because the Liver is overacting and dragging the Spleen down with it.

## กลุ่มอาการการแพทย์แผนจีนและการรักษา (TCM Patterns and Treatment)

### Spleen Qi Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/spleen-qi-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Loose or soft stools, sometimes with undigested food, Abdominal bloating that gets worse after eating, Constant fatigue and lack of energy, Poor appetite or reduced food intake, Pale, puffy tongue with tooth marks on the edges
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Raw, cold, or iced foods, Overeating or large meals, Dairy and greasy foods, Overwork and exhaustion, Damp or humid weather
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warm, cooked meals, Small, frequent meals, Gentle exercise like walking, Rest and avoiding overwork, Warmth on the abdomen
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Your Spleen is the engine that transforms food and fluids into usable energy and separates the clear from the turbid. When Spleen Qi becomes weak, this engine sputters: it can no longer properly absorb nutrients or manage water. Fluids accumulate and turn into dampness, which then sinks downward into the intestines, producing the loose, persistent stools that define chronic enteritis. Because the root is a lack of Qi, every meal can feel like a burden, leaving you bloated and exhausted afterward.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale, puffy, and tender body with tooth marks along the edges, thin white coating. Pulse: weak and soft overall, especially noticeable at the right middle position (Spleen).
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Spleen Qi is like a warm fire that cooks food into energy. Cold and raw foods, overeating, and exhaustion douse that fire, allowing dampness to pool in the gut and cause diarrhea. Warm, cooked foods and regular rest feed the fire gently, helping the Spleen regain its strength to transform and transport properly. Gentle movement keeps Qi flowing without depleting it, while warmth directly comforts the Spleen and dries dampness.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Make warm, cooked, and easy-to-digest foods the backbone of your diet-think soups, congees, steamed vegetables, and well-cooked grains. Avoid raw, cold, and iced foods and drinks, as they chill the Spleen and worsen dampness. Eat small, regular meals rather than large plates, and chew thoroughly. Gentle daily movement like walking or tai chi helps Qi circulate without draining your reserves. Prioritize rest and avoid pushing through exhaustion.

### Spleen Deficiency with Dampness
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/spleen-deficiency-with-dampness)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Loose, watery stools, Abdominal bloating worse after eating, Heaviness in body and limbs, Sticky, greasy sensation in the mouth, Drowsiness after meals
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Raw, cold, or iced foods, Dairy and greasy foods, Overeating or large meals, Damp or humid weather, Sedentary lifestyle, Excessive worry
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warm, cooked meals, Ginger tea, Gentle exercise like walking, Rest after meals, Small, frequent meals
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  The Spleen is responsible for transforming food into Qi and transporting fluids. When Spleen Qi is deficient, it fails to manage water, allowing dampness to accumulate. This dampness then pours down into the intestines, causing watery, loose stools that persist over time. The heavy, bloated feeling and sticky mouth are direct signs of this internal dampness.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale, swollen body with teeth marks, white greasy coating, moist. Pulse: soggy (Ru) and slow (Huan), soft and weak at the right Guan position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Cold, raw, and greasy foods are difficult to digest and directly generate dampness, overwhelming the already weak Spleen. Overeating taxes the Spleen's capacity, while damp weather adds external dampness to the internal condition. Worry knots Spleen Qi, further impairing its function. Conversely, warm, cooked foods are easy on digestion and support Spleen Yang. Ginger warms the middle and transforms dampness. Light exercise moves Qi and helps the body process fluids, while rest conserves the Spleen's limited energy. Small meals avoid overburdening the digestive system, allowing the Spleen to recover.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Focus on warm, cooked, easily digestible foods like congee, steamed vegetables, and soups. Avoid cold, raw, dairy, and greasy foods that create dampness. Eat small, frequent meals and sip ginger tea to warm the digestive fire. Gentle daily movement like walking helps Qi move and transforms dampness.

### Kidney and Spleen Yang Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/kidney-and-spleen-yang-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Early-morning diarrhea (around 5 AM), Undigested food in stools, Cold lower back and knees, Cold hands and feet, Aversion to cold
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Cold weather, Raw, cold, or iced foods, Overwork and exhaustion, Late nights
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warm, cooked meals, Warmth on the abdomen, Rest and early nights, Light, consistent movement
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Your body's warming fire, rooted in the Kidneys, is meant to support the Spleen's digestive work. When that fire dims over time, the Spleen loses its heat and can no longer cook food or manage fluids properly. The result is chronic loose stools that often strike in the early morning - the coldest, lowest-Yang part of the day - with undigested food passing straight through.
Because Yang is the body's warmth, its decline also leaves you feeling cold right to the core, with icy hands and feet and a sore, aching lower back. This isn't just a digestive upset; it's a deep cold that needs rekindling at the root.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale, swollen, and puffy with teeth marks along the edges, covered by a white slippery coating. Pulse: deep, weak, and slow; especially feeble at both rear (Kidney) positions and the right middle (Spleen) position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: In Kidney and Spleen Yang Deficiency, the body's inner fire is already low. Cold weather and cold foods pour water on that fire, while overwork and late nights further drain the Kidneys' reserves. Warmth, rest, and gentle movement all help to conserve and slowly rebuild that deep Yang, which is exactly what the Spleen needs to start digesting properly again.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat only warm, cooked, easy-to-digest foods like congee, soups, and stews. Avoid all raw, cold, and icy items. Keep your abdomen and lower back warm, especially at dawn. Gentle daily movement like walking supports Yang, but avoid exhaustion. Prioritize deep rest and go to bed early to protect your Kidney reserves.

### Rebellious Liver Qi invading the Spleen
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/rebellious-liver-qi-invading-the-spleen)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Cramping abdominal pain before diarrhea, Pain eases after bowel movement, Symptoms triggered by stress or anger, Distension or discomfort along the ribs, Irritability or moodiness
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Stress and anger, Skipping meals, Raw, cold, or iced foods, Overwork and exhaustion
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Emotional calm, Warm, cooked meals, Gentle exercise like walking, Regular eating schedule
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  The Liver is responsible for the smooth flow of Qi throughout the body. When emotional stress, frustration, or anger build up, Liver Qi stagnates and then 'rebels' horizontally, invading the Spleen. The Spleen's ability to transform and transport food and fluids is disrupted, leading to undigested food and dampness accumulating in the intestines, which triggers abdominal pain and urgent diarrhea. The pain often eases after the bowel movement because the stagnation is temporarily released.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: Normal or slightly pale body, possible teeth marks, thin white coating. Pulse: Wiry (弦 xián), especially at the left middle position; the right middle position is softer.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Emotional upset causes Liver Qi to stagnate and invade the Spleen, directly triggering pain and diarrhea; calm emotions allow Qi to flow smoothly. Irregular eating and cold foods weaken the Spleen and encourage dampness, while warm, regular meals strengthen the Spleen and prevent the Liver from overacting. Gentle movement helps Qi circulate, but overwork depletes Spleen Qi, making the pattern worse.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat warm, cooked, easily digestible meals at regular times to support the Spleen. Avoid raw, cold, greasy, or spicy foods that can aggravate dampness and Liver Qi. Manage stress through meditation, gentle walks, or tai chi, and allow time for relaxation after meals.

## คำถามที่พบบ่อย (Frequently Asked Questions)
### Can acupuncture and herbs really stop chronic diarrhea?
Yes. TCM has a long track record of treating chronic diarrhea by addressing its root cause rather than just suppressing symptoms. Acupuncture helps regulate the nervous system and gut motility, while herbal formulas strengthen the Spleen, drain dampness, warm the Kidneys, or harmonize the Liver. Many patients see a reduction in frequency and urgency within a few weeks, and with consistent treatment, stools can return to normal consistency.

### How long does it take to see results?
Most people notice some improvement within 2-4 weeks. If the pattern is primarily Spleen Qi Deficiency or Liver invading the Spleen, stools often become firmer and abdominal pain decreases quickly. Dampness-heavy patterns may take 8-12 weeks because the excess moisture must be gradually drained. Kidney Yang Deficiency, which involves a deeper constitutional cold, may require 3-6 months of treatment to fully rebuild warmth and stop early-morning diarrhea.

### Do I have to change my diet completely?
You don't have to overhaul everything overnight, but diet plays a crucial role in recovery. The most important shift is to eat warm, cooked foods and avoid raw, cold, and greasy items that burden the Spleen. Even simple changes - like starting the day with congee instead of cold cereal, or sipping ginger tea - can make a noticeable difference. Your TCM practitioner will guide you step by step, and many patients find that as their digestion strengthens, they can gradually tolerate a wider variety of foods.

### Is it safe to combine TCM with my current medications?
Generally, yes. Herbal formulas and acupuncture do not interfere with common antidiarrheals, probiotics, or anti-inflammatory drugs. If you are taking immunosuppressants, biologics, or corticosteroids, inform both your TCM practitioner and your prescribing doctor so they can coordinate care. Never stop prescribed medications abruptly. Always bring a complete list of your medications to your TCM consultation.

### Will the diarrhea come back after treatment stops?
Relapse is less likely when the underlying imbalance has been corrected. TCM aims to rebuild the Spleen's strength, clear dampness, or warm the Kidneys so that the digestive system can function on its own. Many practitioners recommend a tapering-off period and may suggest dietary habits or occasional herbal boosts to maintain results. However, if the original triggers - such as chronic stress, poor diet, or overwork - return, symptoms may reappear, so long-term lifestyle adjustments are important.

### Can TCM help if my diarrhea is caused by IBS or IBD?
Yes. TCM doesn't treat the Western diagnosis label but the pattern of imbalance. Whether your chronic diarrhea is labeled IBS, Crohn's, or ulcerative colitis, a TCM practitioner will identify the underlying pattern - such as Spleen Qi Deficiency, Dampness, or Liver invading the Spleen - and treat accordingly. Many patients with these conditions find significant relief from acupuncture and herbs, often alongside their conventional care.

### Does acupuncture hurt for abdominal issues?
Most people find acupuncture very comfortable. The needles are hair-thin and are inserted just below the skin. Points on the abdomen, legs, and back are commonly used for chronic diarrhea. You may feel a mild ache, warmth, or tingling - signs that Qi is responding. Many patients actually find the sessions deeply relaxing, which can further help with stress-related digestive patterns.

### Are there any side effects from Chinese herbs?
When prescribed by a qualified practitioner, Chinese herbal formulas are generally safe and well-tolerated. Some warming or astringent herbs may cause mild constipation if the dosage is too high, but your practitioner will adjust the formula if that happens. Always source herbs from a reputable supplier, and inform your practitioner of any new symptoms. Serious side effects are rare when herbs are prescribed correctly.
