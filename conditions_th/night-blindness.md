## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนปัจจุบัน (Conventional Medicine View)
### คำอธิบาย (Description)
Night blindness, or nyctalopia, is difficulty seeing in low light or darkness. It's not a disease itself but a symptom of an underlying issue with the retina's rod cells, which are responsible for vision in dim conditions. Common causes include vitamin A deficiency, retinitis pigmentosa, cataracts, glaucoma, and certain medications.
Diagnosis typically involves a detailed eye exam, visual field testing, and sometimes electroretinography to measure retinal function. Treatment depends on the cause - vitamin A supplements for deficiency, surgery for cataracts, or low-vision aids for degenerative conditions. For many inherited retinal diseases, conventional options remain limited.

### การรักษาหลัก (Treatments)
Conventional treatment targets the underlying cause. If vitamin A deficiency is identified, supplementation is the primary approach and can reverse the symptom. Cataract surgery restores clarity when the lens has clouded. For progressive conditions like retinitis pigmentosa, management focuses on low-vision aids, protective eyewear, and sometimes vitamin A palmitate to slow progression, though results vary. Genetic therapies are emerging but not yet widely available.

## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนจีน (Traditional Chinese Medicine View)
### ความเข้าใจเกี่ยวกับการแพทย์แผนจีน (TCM Understanding)
In TCM, the ability to see in darkness depends on the eyes being richly supplied with Blood and Essence. The Liver stores Blood, and its channel opens directly into the eyes. When Liver Blood is plentiful, the eyes are moist, bright, and adapt easily to changing light. When it's deficient, the eyes become dry and struggle in dim conditions. The Kidneys store Essence, the deepest source of yin and yang in the body, and this Essence rises to nourish the pupils. If Kidney Yin or Yang is depleted, the foundational fuel for vision runs low.
The Spleen plays a quieter but equally vital role. It transforms food into Qi and Blood - the raw materials the Liver and Kidneys need. A weak Spleen means not enough Blood is made to reach the eyes, no matter how well the other organs are functioning. That's why digestive symptoms like bloating and fatigue often accompany night blindness in Spleen-deficient patterns.
Because night vision relies on this chain of supply - Spleen makes Blood, Liver stores it, Kidneys provide Essence - a breakdown at any point can cause the same symptom. That's why one person's night blindness might come with dry eyes and pale nails (Liver Blood Deficiency), while another's comes with low back pain and feeling cold (Kidney Yang Deficiency). TCM treatment traces the symptom back to its origin and rebuilds from there.

### การวินิจฉัยโดยแพทย์แผนจีน (Practitioner Diagnosis)
To understand why night vision is failing, a TCM practitioner looks at the whole person, not just the eyes. The first clues come from the quality of the vision loss and the accompanying body signs. Is the eye dry and gritty, or does it ache? Is the person tired and pale, or restless and warm? These details point toward different underlying patterns of disharmony.
When the main complaint is dry, tired eyes with blurred vision and perhaps pale nails, the practitioner suspects Liver Blood Deficiency. The Liver stores Blood and its channel opens to the eyes; without enough nourishing Blood, the eyes become malnourished. The tongue is often pale and the pulse thin, confirming that Blood is not reaching the sense organs.
If poor night vision comes with poor appetite, bloating, loose stools, and a sallow complexion, the root is likely Spleen Qi Deficiency. The Spleen transforms food into Qi and Blood; when it is weak, not enough Blood is produced to feed the eyes. A pale tongue with a thin coat and a weak pulse support this diagnosis.
In older adults or those with long-term strain, night blindness may be accompanied by dry eyes, floaters, tinnitus, dizziness, and soreness of the lower back and knees. This points to Kidney and Liver Yin Deficiency. The tongue is red with little coating, and the pulse is thin and rapid, indicating a deep depletion of the body’s cooling, moistening fluids.
When night blindness appears alongside marked fatigue, pale face and lips, dizziness, and heart palpitations, the pattern is likely a broader Qi and Blood Deficiency. Here both the energy and the material Blood are insufficient to raise clear Yang to the head and eyes. The tongue is pale and may be slightly swollen, and the pulse is thin and weak.
If the person feels constantly cold, has a low and aching back, and lacks vitality, the deeper issue may be Kidney Yang Deficiency. The warming Ming Men Fire is failing to transform essence into the bright, nourishing fluids the eyes need. The tongue is pale and puffy with a white coat, and the pulse is deep and weak.
When night blindness comes with a dull, fixed aching around the eyes, and the complexion is dark or the tongue shows purple spots, the practitioner considers Blood Stagnation. Often this develops after long-standing deficiency; the blood flow becomes sluggish and fails to irrigate the eye collaterals. The pulse feels choppy or wiry.

### Liver Blood Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/liver-blood-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Dry, tired eyes, Pale complexion and brittle nails, Scanty, pale menstruation, Muscle cramps or numbness, Vivid dreaming or restless sleep, Dull headache
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Stress and worry, Excessive raw or cold foods, Overwork and insufficient sleep, Excessive screen time, Heavy menstrual bleeding, Spicy, greasy foods
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Quality sleep, Blood-nourishing foods (liver, spinach, goji berries), Warm, cooked meals, Gentle eye exercises, Adequate hydration
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  The Liver stores Blood and its channel opens into the eyes. When Liver Blood is insufficient, the eyes lose their nourishment, especially at night when vision relies on Blood to anchor the spirit. This leads to difficulty seeing in dim light, often with dry, tired eyes and a pale face.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale body, especially at the sides, thin and slightly dry with a thin white coating. Pulse: fine (细 xì) and choppy (涩 sè), often wiry (弦 xián) at the left middle position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Liver Blood is easily depleted by overwork, emotional strain, and insufficient sleep, all of which consume Yin and Blood. Raw and cold foods weaken the Spleen, the source of Blood production. Nourishing foods, rest, and eye exercises help rebuild Blood and direct it to the eyes, while avoiding screen strain preserves precious Blood.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Include blood-building foods like dark leafy greens, beets, small amounts of liver, goji berries, and black sesame. Rest your eyes regularly and prioritize deep sleep, as Blood is regenerated during the night.

### Kidney and Liver Yin Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/kidney-and-liver-yin-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Dry, gritty eyes, Blurred vision or floaters, Night sweats, Heat in the palms, soles, and chest, Dizziness and tinnitus, Sore, weak lower back and knees
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Stress and frustration, Spicy, fried, or greasy foods, Alcohol and coffee, Staying up late, Prolonged screen time
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Rest and adequate sleep, Cool, dark environment, Moistening foods (pears, black sesame), Goji berry tea, Gentle eye exercises
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  The Liver opens into the eyes, and the Kidneys store essence that nourishes them. When both Liver and Kidney Yin run low, the eyes lose their moisture and brightening nourishment. This leaves them dry, prone to floaters, and unable to adapt to dim light-so night vision gradually fades.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red, thin body with cracks, little or no coating, dry surface. Pulse: fine, rapid, and wiry, especially weak at the left Chi (Kidney) position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Kidney and Liver Yin are cooling, moistening forces. Stress, spicy foods, and alcohol generate heat that burns up Yin even more. Late nights and screen time drain the very essence and blood the eyes rely on. Rest, cooling foods, and eye breaks replenish Yin, soothe deficiency heat, and help the eyes recover their natural lubrication and night adaptation.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat moistening, Yin-nourishing foods like pears, black sesame, goji berries, and spinach. Avoid spicy, fried, or greasy foods and cut back on alcohol and coffee. Go to bed early, as staying up late further drains Yin. Rest your eyes regularly from screens and try gentle eye exercises.

### Spleen Qi Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/spleen-qi-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Poor appetite or reduced food intake, Abdominal bloating, worse after eating, Loose stools, Fatigue and lack of energy, Dull yellowish or pale complexion
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Overthinking and mental strain, Cold or raw foods, Irregular eating habits, Physical overexertion
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Eating small, warm meals, Consistent meal times, Rest and adequate sleep
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  The Spleen is your body's engine for turning food into Qi and Blood. When Spleen Qi is weak, this engine sputters, and you simply cannot produce enough Blood to nourish your eyes. Without sufficient Blood reaching the retinas, your night vision fades, as the eyes are starved of what they need to adapt to darkness.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale, puffy body with tooth marks and a thin white coating. Pulse: weak and soft, especially at the right middle (Spleen) position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: The Spleen is the factory for your Qi and Blood. Eating warm, regular meals provides a steady stream of fuel for this factory, while rest conserves its energy. Cold raw foods and mental strain directly damage Spleen Qi, shutting down production and starving the eyes of the nourishment they need to see clearly at night.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat regular, warm, cooked meals at consistent times; your Spleen thrives on routine. Focus on easily digestible foods like congee, soups, and stews, and avoid raw, cold, or iced foods and drinks that douse your digestive fire.

### Qi and Blood Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/qi-and-blood-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Poor night vision with overall fatigue, Pale or sallow complexion, Dizziness or lightheadedness, Heart palpitations, Poor appetite, Pale lips and nail beds
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Overwork and exhaustion, Skipping meals or eating cold raw foods, Excessive screen time straining eyes, Stress and worry, Late nights and poor sleep
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Adequate sleep and daytime rest, Eating warm, cooked meals, Gentle daily movement, Iron&#x2011;rich foods like liver or spinach
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  The eyes need a steady supply of Qi and Blood to see clearly, especially in dim light when the body's Yang energy must lift nourishment upward. When both Qi and Blood are deficient, the clear Yang fails to rise to the head and the retinas are starved of the nutrients they need to regenerate light‑sensitive pigments. This leaves the eyes unable to adapt to darkness, producing the hallmark night blindness. The root is often a weak Spleen that cannot transform food into enough Qi and Blood.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale body, may be thin or have teeth marks, thin white coating. Pulse: fine, weak, and may feel empty.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Qi and Blood are manufactured by the Spleen from the food we eat and the rest we take. Overwork, poor diet, and emotional strain directly consume these resources, leaving too little to send to the eyes. Rest, warm nourishing meals, and gentle movement conserve and rebuild Qi and Blood, allowing the clear Yang to rise and restore night vision.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Build meals around warm, easily digested foods like congee, bone broth, and well‑cooked root vegetables. Include small amounts of high‑quality red meat or liver a few times a week, along with cooked spinach, goji berries, and black sesame to gently nourish Blood. Avoid raw, cold, and greasy foods that weaken the Spleen. Prioritize a regular sleep schedule and short daily walks rather than intense exercise, because overexertion drains the Qi you are trying to rebuild.

### Kidney Yang Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/kidney-yang-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Night blindness that feels deep and constant, not just occasional, Feeling cold all over, especially lower back and knees, Low energy, desire to sleep, mental dullness, Frequent urination at night, Pale or dark complexion with a lack of warmth
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Cold weather or drafts, Raw, cold foods and drinks, Overwork and exhaustion, Excessive sexual activity
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warmth on the lower back, Eating warm, cooked foods, Moxibustion therapy, Adequate rest and sleep
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  The Kidneys store the body's fundamental Yang, the warming fire that drives all physiological processes. When Kidney Yang is depleted, the Gate of Life Fire weakens and cannot warm the body or transform essence. In the eyes, this means the warming Yang Qi fails to ascend and illuminate the pupils, leaving them dim and unable to adjust in darkness. The result is night blindness that feels deep-rooted and is accompanied by profound coldness and low vitality.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale, puffy and tender body with teeth marks and a wet white coating. Pulse: deep, slow and weak, especially at the Chi (third) position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Kidney Yang is the body's fundamental warming fire. Cold and raw foods directly extinguish this fire, while overwork and excessive sexual activity drain the essence that supports Yang. Warmth applied to the lower back and eating warm, cooked foods help to preserve and gently stoke the Gate of Life Fire. Moxibustion is particularly powerful as it adds external Yang Qi directly into the channels to warm the Kidneys from the outside in, helping the eyes receive the warmth they need to function in darkness.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Keep the lower back and feet warm at all times. Eat cooked, warm foods like soups, stews, and root vegetables; avoid raw, cold, and frozen foods. Go to bed early and avoid overwork, which depletes Kidney Yang.

### Blood Stagnation
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/blood-stagnation)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Dull, fixed ache behind the eyes, Vision worse at night and with fatigue, Dark or purplish lips and nail beds, Dark menstrual blood with clots (if applicable)
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Prolonged screen time, Cold weather or icy drinks, Sedentary lifestyle, Emotional stress
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warm compress on eyes, Gentle exercise, Warm cooked meals
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Blood must flow freely through the tiny vessels around the eyes to nourish the retina. When blood stagnates, often after chronic deficiency or injury, these collaterals become blocked. The eyes are starved of fresh blood, so night vision fades and a dull, fixed ache often settles behind the eyes.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: purple or dusky body with stasis spots, distended dark sublingual veins. Pulse: choppy (涩 Sè) or wiry (弦 Xián).
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Blood stasis thickens and congeals with cold and inactivity, while warmth and movement encourage circulation. Stress knots Liver Qi, which can drive blood stasis further, so calming the mind and staying active directly counter the root of this pattern.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Gentle daily movement like walking or tai chi helps keep blood moving. Avoid cold, raw foods and icy drinks, which can congeal blood. A warm compress over closed eyes in the evening can soothe local stagnation.

## คำถามที่พบบ่อย (Frequently Asked Questions)
### Can acupuncture actually improve night vision?
Yes, many patients report clearer vision in dim light after a course of acupuncture, especially when points around the eyes and on the liver and kidney channels are used. Acupuncture works by increasing local circulation and guiding the body's resources to the eyes. While it doesn't cure structural damage, it can optimize the nourishment reaching the retina and often reduces accompanying dryness and strain.

### What foods help night blindness according to TCM?
Foods that build blood and nourish the eyes are central. Goji berries, dark leafy greens, liver (if tolerated), black sesame seeds, eggs, and bone broths are excellent. Warming, cooked meals support the spleen, which is key to producing blood. Avoid too many cold, raw foods or icy drinks, which can weaken digestion and make it harder to generate the nourishment your eyes need.

### How long does TCM take to work for night blindness?
You might notice less eye fatigue and a subtle improvement in dim-light comfort within 4 to 6 weeks. Fuller restoration of night vision often takes 3 to 6 months, especially when the root is a long-standing deficiency. Consistency with herbs, acupuncture, and diet makes the biggest difference.

### Can I take vitamin A supplements along with Chinese herbs?
In most cases, yes. Many Chinese herbs that benefit night vision, like goji berries, naturally contain carotenoids. If you're taking high-dose vitamin A, it's wise to inform both your TCM practitioner and your eye doctor, as excessive vitamin A can be toxic. They can coordinate a safe, complementary approach.

### Does TCM help with night blindness from retinitis pigmentosa?
While TCM cannot reverse the genetic changes in retinitis pigmentosa, many patients find that herbs and acupuncture slow the progression of vision loss and improve quality of life. Treatment focuses on nourishing kidney essence and liver blood to preserve remaining retinal function for as long as possible. It's often used alongside conventional monitoring.

### Is poor night vision always a sign of liver blood deficiency?
Not always. Liver blood deficiency is a very common cause, but night blindness can also arise from spleen qi weakness, kidney yin or yang deficiency, or even blood stagnation. The accompanying signs - like digestive problems, coldness, or fixed eye pain - help distinguish the pattern. That's why a full TCM assessment looks at the whole person, not just the eyes.

## การวิจัยทางวิทยาศาสตร์สมัยใหม่ (Modern Scientific Research)
1. **Acupuncture for retinitis pigmentosa: a systematic review and meta-analysis**
   *วารสารวิชาการ: Liu Z, et al. Acupuncture for retinitis pigmentosa: a systematic review and meta-analysis. Medicine (Baltimore). 2019;98(18):e15447.*
   A 2019 systematic review of 12 RCTs involving 968 patients found that acupuncture significantly improved visual field mean sensitivity and electroretinogram b-wave amplitude compared to Western medical therapy alone. The quality of evidence was graded low to moderate due to risk of bias in included studies.

2. **Effect of Qi Ju Di Huang Wan on visual function in patients with age-related macular degeneration**
   *วารสารวิชาการ: Chen M, et al. Clinical observation on Qi Ju Di Huang Wan for age-related macular degeneration. Chinese Journal of Experimental Traditional Medical Formulae. 2017;23(5):178-182.*
   In a 12-week RCT of 60 patients with dry AMD, those receiving Qi Ju Di Huang Wan plus routine care showed greater improvement in contrast sensitivity and low-luminance visual acuity compared to routine care alone (P<0.05). Night vision complaints were not a primary endpoint but improved in the herbal group.
