## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนปัจจุบัน (Conventional Medicine View)
### คำอธิบาย (Description)
Premature ventricular contractions are extra heartbeats that begin in the ventricles, the heart's lower chambers. They cause a sensation of a skipped beat or a fluttering in the chest, and are very common - many people experience them occasionally without any underlying heart disease. They are diagnosed through an electrocardiogram (ECG) or a Holter monitor, and are often classified by their frequency and whether they occur during exercise or rest.

### การรักษาหลัก (Treatments)
For most people with a low burden of PVCs and no structural heart disease, treatment is reassurance and observation. When symptoms are bothersome, beta-blockers or calcium channel blockers may be prescribed to reduce the frequency. In cases of very high burden or when medication fails, catheter ablation - a procedure that destroys the small area of heart tissue triggering the extra beats - may be considered.

## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนจีน (Traditional Chinese Medicine View)
### ความเข้าใจเกี่ยวกับการแพทย์แผนจีน (TCM Understanding)
In TCM, the heart is not just a pump - it houses the Shen, the mind-spirit that needs a calm, well-nourished environment to keep the heartbeat steady. When the heart's own Qi, Blood, Yin, or Yang become depleted, the rhythm falters. But the heart doesn't work in isolation. The Liver ensures smooth flow of Qi through the chest; when stress knots that flow, palpitations can flare. The Spleen transforms food into clear energy - if it's weak, dampness and phlegm accumulate and cloud the heart's rhythm. The Kidneys provide the foundational warmth that powers the heart's beat; when that fire dims, the pulse becomes slow and irregular.
This is why one Western diagnosis of PVCs can map to several TCM patterns. A person whose palpitations come with deep exhaustion, a dry mouth, and a thin red tongue likely has Qi and Yin Deficiency - the heart lacks both energy and cooling moisture. Someone with palpitations that worsen with frustration, a tight chest, and frequent sighing is dealing with Liver Qi Stagnation. If the chest feels heavy, the tongue is greasy, and palpitations are worse after eating, Phlegm-Dampness is the culprit. Each pattern points to a different root, and therefore requires a different treatment.
Even the nature of the skipped beat gives clues. A forceful, pounding PVC often suggests excess - perhaps Phlegm-Fire agitating the heart or stagnant Blood irritating the vessels. A weak, faint, missed beat that leaves you drained points to deficiency - the heart simply doesn't have enough Qi or Blood to sustain a regular rhythm. By listening to these details, a TCM practitioner can trace the disturbance back to its source, whether it's in the heart itself or in another organ that has lost its balance.

### การวินิจฉัยโดยแพทย์แผนจีน (Practitioner Diagnosis)
Practitioners start by asking about the nature of the palpitations and what makes them better or worse. A thorough inquiry into energy levels, emotional state, chest sensations, and temperature tolerance quickly narrows the field of possible patterns.
If the palpitations come with pronounced fatigue, a thin body, a dry mouth, and a tongue that looks red with little coating, the diagnosis leans toward Qi and Yin Deficiency. The pulse is often thin and weak, reflecting the heart’s lack of both vital energy and cooling nourishment.
When palpitations feel forceful and are accompanied by stabbing chest pain that is fixed in location, Heart Blood Stagnation is likely. The tongue appears dark purple with stasis spots, and the pulse has a choppy or wiry quality, indicating obstructed blood flow in the vessels.
Palpitations that flare up or worsen with emotional stress, frustration, or mood swings point to Liver Qi Stagnation. A sensation of distension under the ribcage, frequent sighing, and a tight, wiry pulse are telltale signs that the smooth flow of Qi in the chest has been disrupted.
A heavy, suffocating sensation in the chest along with palpitations suggests Phlegm-Dampness in the Middle-Burner. The tongue coating is thick and greasy, and the pulse feels slippery, as if rolling under the fingers. This pattern often coexists with digestive sluggishness and a feeling of bodily heaviness.
If the heavy chest sensation is joined by irritability, a bitter taste in the mouth, insomnia, and a red tongue with a yellow greasy coating, the pattern has shifted to Phlegm-Fire harassing the Heart. The pulse is rapid and slippery, signaling that heat has combined with phlegm to agitate the heart spirit.
Palpitations paired with a deep, slow pulse, cold hands and feet, and a pale, puffy tongue point to a Disharmony between Heart and Kidneys due to Kidney Yang Deficiency. The person often feels chilly and may have low back soreness, indicating that the fire of the Kidneys is too weak to warm the Heart.

## กลุ่มอาการการแพทย์แผนจีนและการรักษา (TCM Patterns and Treatment)

### Qi and Yin Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/qi-and-yin-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Palpitations with a sensation of skipped or missed beats, Fatigue that worsens with even mild exertion, Dry mouth and throat with little desire to drink, Warm sensation in palms, soles, and chest, Night sweats, Tongue red with thin or absent coating
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Overwork and exhaustion, Late nights and insufficient sleep, Spicy, fried, or drying foods, Emotional stress and worry, Excessive talking or singing
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Rest and adequate sleep, Cooling, moistening foods (pear, millet), Gentle exercise like walking, Stress reduction, Avoiding spicy foods
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  The heart relies on both Qi (vital energy) and Yin (cooling, moistening nourishment) to beat steadily. When Qi is deficient, the heart muscle lacks the strength to pump rhythmically, leading to a sensation of missed or weak beats. At the same time, Yin deficiency deprives the heart of moisture and generates a subtle empty heat that agitates the heart spirit, making the palpitations more noticeable and often accompanied by a dry mouth, warm hands and feet, and night sweats.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red or pale-red body with a thin or absent coating, possibly cracked, dry surface. Pulse: fine (thin) and weak, often rapid.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Overwork and late nights drain both Qi and Yin directly, making the heart even more unsteady. Spicy and drying foods generate internal heat and consume Yin fluids, which aggravates the empty heat that disturbs the heart. Rest, nourishing foods, and gentle movement rebuild the body's energy and moisture, allowing the heart to beat more calmly and steadily.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Focus on gently nourishing, moistening foods like millet congee, steamed pears, and small amounts of lean protein. Avoid spicy, fried, and overly stimulating foods that dry up Yin. Prioritize rest and avoid late nights, which consume Yin. Gentle movement like walking or Tai Chi can support Qi without depleting it.

### Disharmony between Heart and Kidneys caused by Kidney Yang Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/disharmony-between-heart-and-kidneys-caused-by-kidney-yang-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Palpitations with a feeling of fright, Cold lower limbs or feet, Sensation of heat or flushing in the chest, Cold and aching low back and knees, Fatigue and lack of energy, Insomnia or restless sleep
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Cold weather or drafts, Raw or cold foods, Overwork and exhaustion, Stress and anxiety, Excessive sexual activity
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Applying warmth (heating pad, moxa), Rest and avoiding overwork, Eating warm, cooked foods, Keeping feet warm, Gentle movement like walking
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Kidney Yang is the root of all Yang Qi in the body. When it weakens, it can no longer warm the Heart, and Heart Yang becomes insufficient. The heart then lacks the strength to beat regularly, producing palpitations and skipped beats. At the same time, the cold from the deficient Yang drives the body's remaining warmth upward, creating a false sensation of heat or agitation in the chest while the lower body remains cold.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale, puffy, teeth-marked body with moist white coat; tongue tip may be slightly redder. Pulse: deep (沉 chén), slow (迟 chí), and weak (弱 ruò), especially at the Chi (proximal) position; the Cun (distal) position may feel slightly more perceptible or wiry (弦 xián).
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Kidney Yang is the body's warming fire. Cold weather, cold foods, and exhaustion drain that fire, making the heart beat irregularly. Warmth, rest, and warming foods conserve and replenish Kidney Yang, while moxa directly adds heat to the body. Stress and overwork stir up the false heat that rises from the deficiency, so calming the mind and avoiding strain help keep the heart rhythm steady.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Keep the lower back and feet warm, and avoid exposure to cold. Eat warming, cooked foods like lamb stew, ginger, and cinnamon; avoid raw, cold, or iced items. Gentle daily movement such as walking helps circulate Yang without exhausting it.

### Heart Blood Stagnation
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/heart-blood-stagnation)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Stabbing or pricking chest pain in a fixed location, Purple or dark lips and nails, Dark purple tongue with stasis spots, Palpitations that feel forceful and irregular, Pain radiating to the left shoulder or inner arm, Anxiety or restlessness during episodes
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Emotional stress and frustration, Cold weather or cold drinks, Overexertion, Fatty, greasy foods
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Gentle movement (walking, tai chi), Warm compress on the chest, Rest during acute episodes, Light, warm meals
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When the heart's blood vessels become obstructed - often after years of emotional strain, injury, or prolonged illness - blood flow grows sluggish and congeals. This stasis directly irritates the heart muscle, triggering the forceful, irregular beats of premature ventricular contractions. The chest pain that accompanies these palpitations is typically sharp and fixed in one spot, reflecting the stagnant blood pressing on the vessels.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: purple or dark-red body with stasis spots, distended sublingual veins, thin white coat. Pulse: choppy (涩 Sè) or knotted (结 Jié), often felt most clearly at the left front position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Blood stasis worsens when Qi stagnates or when cold congeals the vessels. Emotional stress knots the Qi that moves blood, while cold physically slows circulation, so both increase the stasis that triggers palpitations. Gentle movement, warmth, and rest do the opposite - they encourage blood to flow smoothly, easing the pressure on the heart and quieting the irregular beats.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Keep the chest warm and avoid cold drinks, which can congeal blood. Gentle daily movement like walking or tai chi helps keep blood moving without straining the heart. Include small amounts of warming spices like ginger and turmeric in cooking, and steer clear of heavy, greasy foods that burden circulation.

### Liver Qi Stagnation
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/liver-qi-stagnation)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Palpitations triggered or worsened by emotional stress, Rib-side distension or bloating, Frequent sighing, Irritability or short temper, Chest tightness
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Emotional stress or anger, Frustration or resentment, Alcohol or greasy food, Irregular eating habits
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Stress relief and relaxation, Gentle exercise like walking, Deep breathing or sighing, Warm herbal teas (peppermint, chamomile)
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  The Liver is responsible for keeping Qi flowing smoothly throughout the body, especially in the chest. When emotional stress, frustration, or worry build up, Liver Qi stagnates and creates pressure and distension around the ribs. This blocked Qi disrupts the heart's natural rhythm, leading to palpitations that come and go with mood swings. The heart skips beats because the Qi is dammed up and then surges, much like a river hitting a blockage.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: normal or slightly dark body, red or slightly purple sides, thin white coat. Pulse: wiry (弦 xián), taut like a guitar string.

### Phlegm-Fire harassing the Heart
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/phlegm-fire-harassing-the-heart)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Palpitations with irritability and restlessness, Chest oppression with a feeling of heat, Copious thick yellow or sticky phlegm, Bitter taste in the mouth and thirst, Red tongue with a thick, yellow, greasy coating, Slippery and rapid pulse
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Anger and frustration, Greasy, spicy, or fried foods, Alcohol and coffee, Hot, stuffy rooms, Staying up late
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Cool, calm environment, Light, bland meals, Deep breathing and meditation, Herbal teas like chrysanthemum
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When phlegm-dampness lingers in the body-often from a rich diet or weak digestion-it can combine with internal heat to form phlegm-fire. This fiery phlegm rises and agitates the Heart, disturbing the Shen (the mind/spirit that resides in the Heart). The result is a chaotic, unsettled heartbeat that feels like skipped beats or a fluttering chest, often accompanied by intense restlessness and insomnia.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red body, often swollen with a red prickly tip, and a thick yellow greasy coating. Pulse: slippery (滑 huá) and rapid (数 shuò), full and overflowing at the left Cun (Heart) position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Greasy, spicy, and heating foods and drinks directly create dampness and heat, the raw materials of phlegm-fire. Emotional stress, especially anger and frustration, stirs up Liver Fire which can combine with phlegm to harass the Heart. Cooling, bland foods, a calm environment, and relaxation techniques all help to clear heat and settle the Shen, cutting off the fire that agitates the heartbeat. Gentle movement also disperses stagnation, preventing phlegm from accumulating.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Avoid greasy, fried, spicy, and sweet foods, as well as alcohol and coffee-these all generate dampness and heat. Favor light, cooling foods like cucumber, pear, mung beans, and leafy greens. Gentle daily movement such as walking or tai chi helps move Qi and reduce phlegm stagnation, while a regular sleep schedule and stress-reducing practices like meditation prevent emotional heat from rising.

## คำถามที่พบบ่อย (Frequently Asked Questions)
### Can acupuncture stop a PVC episode while it's happening?
Yes, in many cases. Acupuncture can help calm the heart's rhythm during an acute episode by settling the Shen and redirecting chaotic Qi. Points like Neiguan (PC-6) and Shenmen (HT-7) are particularly effective at restoring a steady beat. However, the real goal is regular treatment to reduce the frequency and intensity of episodes over time.

### How long does it take for Chinese herbs to reduce skipped beats?
Most patients begin to notice fewer palpitations within 2 to 4 weeks of starting a tailored herbal formula. The full effect builds gradually as the underlying imbalance is corrected. For deficiency patterns, it may take 2 to 3 months to see a consistent, lasting reduction, because the body needs time to rebuild Qi, Blood, or Yang.

### Is it safe to take Chinese herbs with my beta-blocker or other heart medication?
Generally yes, but it must be managed carefully. Some herbs can influence heart rate and blood pressure, so your TCM practitioner and cardiologist should both be informed of everything you are taking. Never stop or adjust your heart medication without your doctor's guidance. If you are on blood thinners like warfarin, certain blood-moving herbs (such as Dan Shen or Chuan Xiong) may require dose adjustments - always disclose your full medication list at your TCM consultation.

### Will my PVCs come back after I stop TCM treatment?
When the underlying pattern is fully corrected, the improvement is usually lasting. However, if the lifestyle or emotional triggers that caused the imbalance return - chronic overwork, prolonged stress, or a poor diet - the PVCs may gradually reappear. Your TCM practitioner will often recommend a maintenance plan with occasional treatments and dietary guidance to prevent recurrence.

### Do I need to change my diet to get better?
Diet plays a significant role, especially if your PVCs are linked to Phlegm-Dampness or Phlegm-Fire. Avoiding greasy, fried, and overly rich foods while emphasizing light, warm, cooked meals can make a big difference. Even for deficiency patterns, eating regular, nourishing meals supports the body's ability to rebuild. Your practitioner will give you specific recommendations based on your pattern.

### What does a TCM diagnosis for PVCs involve?
Your practitioner will ask detailed questions about the sensation of your palpitations, what triggers them, your energy levels, sleep, emotions, and digestion. They will examine your tongue for color, coating, and shape, and take your pulse on both wrists to feel its quality - whether it is thin, wiry, slippery, or choppy. This information, combined with your medical history, reveals which pattern is causing your PVCs and guides the treatment plan.
