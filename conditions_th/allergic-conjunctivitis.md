## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนปัจจุบัน (Conventional Medicine View)
### คำอธิบาย (Description)
Allergic conjunctivitis is an inflammation of the clear membrane covering the white part of the eye and the inside of the eyelids, triggered by an allergic reaction. When the eye encounters an allergen like pollen, dust mites, or pet dander, immune cells release histamine and other chemicals, causing blood vessels to swell and nerves to fire - leading to intense itching, redness, watering, and sometimes eyelid swelling. It can be seasonal or year-round, and is often accompanied by other allergic symptoms like sneezing or a runny nose.
Diagnosis is usually straightforward based on the pattern of symptoms and an eye exam. In some cases, an allergist may perform skin or blood tests to identify specific triggers. While it’s rarely sight-threatening, the relentless itch and discomfort can significantly affect quality of life, sleep, and concentration.

### การรักษาหลัก (Treatments)
Standard care begins with allergen avoidance and cool compresses. Over-the-counter or prescription antihistamine eye drops provide quick relief by blocking histamine, while mast cell stabilizers prevent the release of histamine if used regularly. For more stubborn cases, dual-action drops (antihistamine plus mast cell stabilizer) or short courses of corticosteroid eye drops may be prescribed.
Oral antihistamines can help when eye symptoms are part of wider hay fever. In severe, chronic cases, allergists may consider immunotherapy (allergy shots or tablets) to desensitize the immune system.

## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนจีน (Traditional Chinese Medicine View)
### ความเข้าใจเกี่ยวกับการแพทย์แผนจีน (TCM Understanding)
In TCM, the eyes are the upper openings most sensitive to Wind - an external pathogen that can carry Heat, Cold, or Dampness into the body. When your defensive Qi (Wei Qi) is strong, Wind passes by without harm. But if your surface defenses are weak, or if internal imbalances like Dampness or Blood Deficiency are already present, Wind latches on and stirs up trouble in the eyes. This is why the same allergy trigger can cause completely different symptoms in different people.
The Liver is the organ that opens into the eyes, and it stores Blood that nourishes and moistens them. When Blood is insufficient - often from overwork, poor diet, or chronic illness - the eyes lose their lubrication and internal Wind stirs, producing a mild, recurrent itch that comes and goes. This is the Blood Deficiency with External Wind pattern, where dryness, light sensitivity, and a pale tongue are key clues.
The Spleen, meanwhile, governs the transformation of fluids. If it’s weakened by dietary habits (think greasy, sweet, or cold foods), Dampness accumulates in the body. When an external Wind invades, it scoops up this Dampness and carries it to the eyes, creating the heavy, sticky, weeping itch of Damp-Wind. The tongue becomes coated and greasy, and symptoms worsen in humid weather.
When the pathogen is purely Wind-Heat, the eyes burn and redden, and relief comes with cold. Wind-Cold produces an itch with a strong aversion to drafts and cold air. In every case, the treatment must address both the external invader (Wind) and the internal terrain that let it settle in - which is why TCM doesn’t just suppress the itch but works to change the body’s reactivity over time.

### การวินิจฉัยโดยแพทย์แผนจีน (Practitioner Diagnosis)
A practitioner begins by asking when the itching started and what makes it feel better or worse. In Wind-Heat, the itching is sharp and comes with a burning sensation; the eyes are clearly red, and symptoms ease with a cool compress. The tongue tip is red with a thin yellow coating, and the pulse feels floating and rapid - like a breeze that carries heat.
If the eyelids are puffy, red, and dotted with tiny bumps or even weeping fluid, the picture shifts toward Damp-Wind. Here the itch feels heavy and sticky rather than purely hot. The tongue coating is thick, yellow, and greasy, and the pulse is slippery, pointing to dampness trapped with wind in the eye area.
When the itching is mild, comes and goes, and is accompanied by dryness, light sensitivity, and blurred vision, a practitioner suspects Blood Deficiency with External Wind. The eyelid margins may look thickened and chronically pink. The tongue is pale with a thin white coat, and the pulse is moderate or fine - signs that the body’s nourishing blood is too weak to anchor the wind.
In Wind-Cold, the itching is paired with a strong dislike of wind and cold air, and the eyes may feel gritty or achy. The tongue looks pale with a thin white coat, and the pulse is deep and tight, like a winter chill. This pattern often appears in people who feel cold easily and whose symptoms worsen in chilly weather.

## กลุ่มอาการการแพทย์แผนจีนและการรักษา (TCM Patterns and Treatment)

### Wind-Heat
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/wind-heat)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Sudden, intense itching with burning sensation, Red, swollen eyes, sometimes with sticky yellow discharge, Aversion to wind and warmth, slight fever or chills, Thirst with desire to drink cool fluids
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Hot, spicy foods, Windy, dusty environments, Excessive screen time, Emotional frustration
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Cool compresses on the eyes, Chrysanthemum or mint tea, Rest in a dark, quiet room, Avoiding wind and sun
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Wind-Heat is an external pathogen that enters through the body's surface and naturally rises toward the head. The eyes are the upper openings most sensitive to Wind, so when Wind-Heat invades, it stirs up Qi and Blood in the eyes, causing sudden intense itching, burning, and redness. The pathogen is still at the exterior level, so symptoms often come on quickly and can be relieved by cooling measures that help disperse the Heat.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red tip and edges with a thin yellow coating. Pulse: floating (fú) and rapid (shuò).
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Wind-Heat is a warm, rising pathogen that flares up with anything that adds heat or stirs the exterior. Hot, spicy foods and hot environments feed the Heat, while wind and dust carry more external pathogens into the eyes. Emotional frustration can stir up Liver Qi, which also tends to rise and aggravate the eyes. Cooling measures like cold compresses and chrysanthemum tea directly counter the Heat, and rest helps the body's defensive Qi push the pathogen out.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Drink chrysanthemum or mint tea to gently cool the body and clear the eyes. Avoid spicy, greasy, and fried foods that add internal Heat. Use cool compresses on closed eyes and wear sunglasses to protect from wind and bright sunlight. Rest your eyes frequently and keep the room air clean and slightly cool.

### Damp-Wind
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/damp-wind)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Eyelid redness and swelling, Small papules or vesicles along the lid margin, Sticky, sometimes weeping discharge, Itching that worsens in damp, humid weather, Sensation of heaviness in the eyes or head
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Damp, humid weather, Windy days, Greasy or dairy-heavy meals, Prolonged sitting in damp places
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Dry, warm weather, Warm compress on eyes, Light, cooked meals
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When Dampness accumulates in the body, often due to weak Spleen Qi and dietary habits, it creates a heavy, sticky internal environment. An external Wind invasion then carries this Dampness upward to the eyes, where the two pathogens become entangled. The result is not just simple itching, but a sensation of grittiness and heaviness, along with the visible swelling, papules, and weeping discharge that characterize this pattern.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: normal or slightly swollen body with a white, greasy coating and a moist surface. Pulse: floating and slippery (Fu Hua) or soggy (Ru).
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: External Dampness and Wind directly aggravate this pattern because they are the very pathogens lodged in the eyes. Humid, windy weather adds more of the same, while greasy food creates internal Dampness that rises upward. Dry warmth helps evaporate Dampness, and warm compresses move Qi and fluids, relieving the heavy, stuck sensation.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Avoid cold, raw, and greasy foods that create Dampness, such as dairy, fried items, and sugary snacks. Opt for lightly cooked vegetables, moderate amounts of warming spices like ginger, and easily digested grains like rice. Keep the living space dry and well-ventilated, and avoid sitting in damp, windy places.

### Blood Deficiency with External Wind
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/blood-deficiency-with-external-wind)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Mild, intermittent itching, Dry, gritty sensation, Photophobia, Blurred vision, Worse at night, Pale complexion
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Exposure to drafts, Cold, dry weather, Overwork and fatigue, Night time, Spicy or greasy foods
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warmth, Rest and calm, Gentle eye massage, Blood-nourishing foods
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When the body’s Blood is insufficient, the eyes lose their nourishment and become dry, blurry, and sensitive to light. This deficiency also allows internal Wind to stir, producing a mild, recurrent itching that comes and goes. Because Blood supports the body’s protective Qi, a deficiency leaves you vulnerable to external Wind, which can invade the eyes and trigger or worsen the itch, especially in drafts or cold weather.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale, thin body with a thin white and slightly dry coating. Pulse: fine (thin) overall, wiry on the left guan position, and may be floating at the superficial level.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Blood Deficiency creates an internal environment where Wind easily arises. External Wind and cold invade because the body's defenses are weak, triggering itching. Overwork and night time (when Blood returns to the Liver) can worsen symptoms if Blood is already insufficient. Warmth and rest support Blood circulation and production, while blood-nourishing foods directly replenish the deficiency, calming the Wind.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat blood-nourishing foods such as dark leafy greens, beets, goji berries, and red dates. Avoid raw, cold, and greasy foods that weaken digestion. Protect eyes from wind and dust with sunglasses. Prioritize rest and avoid overworking to preserve Blood.

### Wind-Cold
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/wind-cold)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Itching worse in cold and wind, Aversion to cold, mild fever, no sweating, Clear watery nasal discharge, Pale or normal conjunctiva, Stiff neck and upper back
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Cold windy weather, Cold foods and drinks, Exposure to drafts
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warm compress on eyes, Staying indoors away from wind, Warm drinks
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Wind-Cold is an exterior pathogen that attacks the surface of the body, including the eyes. The Wind carries the Cold into the eye area, where it disrupts the normal flow of Qi and Blood. This produces an itchy sensation, while the Cold causes a marked aversion to wind and a preference for warmth.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: normal or slightly pale body with a thin white moist coat. Pulse: floating and tight.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Cold constricts the body's channels and slows Qi and Blood flow, which intensifies itching and aversion to cold. Wind carries the pathogen into the eyes, so avoiding drafts and cold air is essential. Warmth relaxes the channels and helps the body's defensive Qi push the pathogen out, relieving symptoms.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat warm, cooked foods like soups and stews with ginger and green onion. Avoid cold drinks and raw foods. Protect your eyes from wind and cold with sunglasses or a scarf.

## คำถามที่พบบ่อย (Frequently Asked Questions)
### Will acupuncture for eye itching hurt?
Acupuncture around the eyes uses very fine needles and is generally gentle. You may feel a slight pinch or a dull sensation when the needle reaches the right depth, but most people find it surprisingly comfortable. Points on the hands, feet, and back of the head are also used to address the root pattern, and those feel like a brief ache or pressure.

### Can I use my antihistamine eye drops while taking Chinese herbs?
Yes, in most cases you can use them together. Herbs work internally to rebalance the body, while drops provide immediate local relief. Always tell your TCM practitioner about every medication you use, and let your eye doctor know you are starting herbs. Over time, as the herbs take effect, many people find they need the drops less often - but never stop a prescribed medication without consulting the doctor who prescribed it.

### How long does it take to see results?
Acute Wind-Heat or Wind-Cold attacks often improve within 3 to 7 days of herbs and acupuncture. Damp-Wind patterns, with their sticky discharge, may need 2 to 4 weeks to clear. Blood Deficiency-related itching, which tends to be chronic and mild, typically takes 1 to 3 months of consistent treatment to rebuild Blood and calm internal Wind. For seasonal allergies, starting treatment a few weeks before your trigger season can significantly reduce or even prevent symptoms.

### Is TCM safe for children with allergic conjunctivitis?
Yes, TCM can be adapted for children. Acupuncture may be replaced with gentle acupressure or pediatric tui na massage on the same points. Herbal formulas are adjusted in dosage and often given as granules or syrups. Always work with a practitioner experienced in pediatric care, and inform both your child’s pediatrician and eye doctor.

### Does TCM cure allergies permanently?
TCM doesn’t promise a permanent cure, but it often changes how your body responds to allergens. By strengthening the defensive Qi and clearing underlying imbalances like Dampness or Blood Deficiency, many people experience fewer, shorter, and milder episodes. Some find that after a course of treatment, they no longer react to triggers that used to set them off - but this depends on the pattern and how long it has been present.

### What foods should I avoid to help my eyes stop itching?
Generally, steer clear of spicy, greasy, and deep-fried foods that create Heat and Dampness. Cold, raw foods and excessive dairy can weaken the Spleen and generate more Dampness. Sugar and alcohol also tend to flare inflammation. Instead, favor lightly cooked vegetables, chrysanthemum tea, goji berries, and foods rich in natural beta-carotene - but the exact diet depends on your pattern, so ask your practitioner for personalized guidance.
