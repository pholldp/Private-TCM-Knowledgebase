## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนปัจจุบัน (Conventional Medicine View)
### คำอธิบาย (Description)
In Western medicine, nosebleeds that occur only around the menstrual period are uncommon and may be called catamenial epistaxis. The exact cause isn’t always clear, but it’s thought to be linked to hormonal changes that affect the blood vessels in the nose, or rarely to tiny deposits of endometrial-like tissue in the nasal passages (nasal endometriosis). Diagnosis usually involves ruling out other causes of nosebleeds, such as clotting disorders or structural problems, and noting the timing with the menstrual cycle.

### การรักษาหลัก (Treatments)
If the nosebleeds are mild and self-limiting, no treatment may be needed. For more bothersome episodes, doctors may recommend nasal cauterization to seal off fragile blood vessels, or short courses of hormonal therapy to suppress ovulation and stabilize the nasal lining. These approaches can reduce bleeding but do not address any underlying systemic tendency.

## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนจีน (Traditional Chinese Medicine View)
### ความเข้าใจเกี่ยวกับการแพทย์แผนจีน (TCM Understanding)
The core of this condition lies in the Penetrating Vessel (Chong Mai), known as the “sea of blood.” Its job is to guide menstrual blood downward to the uterus. When heat builds up inside this vessel - often from emotional stress, diet, or constitutional factors - the Qi rebels and rushes upward, carrying blood with it. This counterflow forces blood out through the nose instead of the uterus, which is why the nosebleed and the period often coincide and the menstrual flow itself may be scanty.
Heat is the most common driver, but its source can vary. Liver Fire, often triggered by anger or frustration, surges up along the Liver channel to the head and nose, bringing dark-red bleeding with irritability and a bitter taste. Stomach Fire follows the Stomach meridian, which runs right through the nose, producing heavy, bright-red bleeds with thirst and constipation. Heart Fire agitates the blood and disturbs the mind, leading to restless sleep and a fiery-red tongue tip. In all these excess patterns, the body is too hot, and blood is pushed where it shouldn’t go.
Not every nosebleed during menstruation is caused by heat. When the body is too weak to hold blood inside the vessels, a different kind of bleeding occurs. Spleen Qi deficiency fails to contain blood, leading to pale, oozing nosebleeds with fatigue and poor appetite. Deep Yang deficiency leaves the body cold and unable to anchor blood, causing scanty, pale bleeding with cold limbs. These deficiency patterns are less dramatic but just as real - and require a completely different treatment approach focused on strengthening and warming, not cooling.

### การวินิจฉัยโดยแพทย์แผนจีน (Practitioner Diagnosis)
A TCM practitioner first asks about the timing, color and volume of the nosebleed. The fact that it appears around the menstrual period is the crucial clue, because it points to a disruption in the normal downward flow of blood. They then check the tongue and pulse to distinguish excess heat patterns from deficiency patterns that cannot hold blood in the vessels.
If the bleeding coincides exactly with the menses and the period flow may be scanty, the classic “reverse menstruation” (倒经, dào jīng) of Chong Mai Qi Rebellion is suspected. The blood is often bright or dark red, and the tongue is red with little coating, while the pulse feels rapid and wiry - signs of heat in the Penetrating Vessel pushing blood upward.
When the nosebleed is accompanied by a bitter taste, irritability, headache and rib-side distension, Liver Fire Blazing is likely. The blood tends to be dark red, the tongue is red with a yellow coat, and the pulse is wiry and rapid. Stress or anger often makes it worse, revealing the Liver’s role in driving heat upward.
Stomach Fire presents with a profuse, bright-red nosebleed and a sensation of heat in the face. The person also notices intense thirst, dry mouth, constipation and bad breath. The tongue is red with a thick yellow coating, and the pulse is flooding and rapid, indicating deep heat in the Yangming channel that passes through the nose.
Heart Fire blazing leads to a bright-red nosebleed with restlessness, insomnia, a red face and mouth ulcers. The tip of the tongue is especially red, and the pulse is rapid. This pattern reflects emotional turmoil stirring fire that agitates the blood and sends it upward.
In contrast, Spleen not controlling Blood produces a pale, oozing nosebleed with fatigue, a pale complexion and poor appetite. The tongue is pale with a thin white coat and the pulse is weak. Here the problem is not excess heat but a lack of Spleen Qi to hold blood inside the vessels.
Yang Deficiency is rare and shows a similar oozing bleed but with cold hands and feet, clear urine and lower back soreness. The tongue is pale and puffy, and the pulse is deep and weak. This deep cold fails to anchor the blood, allowing it to leak during the menstrual phase.

## กลุ่มอาการการแพทย์แผนจีนและการรักษา (TCM Patterns and Treatment)

### Chong Mai Qi Rebellion
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/chong-mai-qi-rebellion)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Nosebleeds that occur just before or during menstruation, Sensation of Qi or heat rushing upward from the lower abdomen, Feeling of heat in the face with cold feet, Fullness or distension in the lower abdomen, chest, or breasts, Anxiety or internal restlessness
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Emotional stress or anger, Spicy, greasy, or fried foods, Alcohol and coffee, Hot weather or overheated environments, Standing for long periods
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Lying down with head elevated, Keeping feet warm, Cooling foods and drinks, Gentle movement or walking, Deep breathing and relaxation
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  The Penetrating Vessel (Chong Mai) is the sea of blood that normally guides menstrual blood downward to the uterus. When heat builds up inside this vessel, its Qi rebels and rushes upward, carrying blood with it. This counterflow forces blood out through the nose instead of the uterus, which is why the nosebleeds arrive right on schedule with your period.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: often unremarkable with a thin white coat; may be slightly pale if Blood deficiency is present. Pulse: Firm (Lao) and wiry, especially at the middle (Guan) positions.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Stress and anger stir up Liver Qi, which fuels the upward rebellion of the Chong Mai and makes nosebleeds more likely. Spicy, greasy foods and alcohol add heat to the blood, while coffee and hot environments further agitate the Qi. Cooling foods and drinks help clear that heat, and keeping the feet warm draws Qi downward, counteracting the upward surge. Gentle movement promotes smooth Qi flow without overheating the body, and relaxation keeps the nervous system calm, preventing the internal restlessness that triggers bleeding.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Avoid spicy, greasy, and fried foods that create heat. Sip peppermint or chrysanthemum tea to cool and descend Qi. Keep your feet warm with socks and warm soaks, which helps anchor Qi downward. Gentle walking and deep breathing are excellent, but avoid vigorous exercise during your period. Lie down with your head slightly elevated if you feel a nosebleed coming on.

### Liver Fire Blazing
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/liver-fire-blazing)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Nosebleed blood is dark red, Throbbing headache at temples, Bitter taste in mouth, Red, painful eyes, Intense irritability and short temper
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Emotional stress or anger, Spicy, greasy, or fried foods, Alcohol and coffee, Hot weather or overheated environments, Overwork and fatigue
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Cooling foods and drinks, Rest and relaxation, Cold compress on nose, Gentle movement or walking
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  The Liver is responsible for the smooth flow of Qi and blood. When anger or frustration builds, Liver Qi stagnates and turns into Fire. During menstruation, the body's Qi and blood are already in motion, but this Liver Fire surges upward along the Liver channel, which connects to the head and nose. The heat scorches the delicate nasal blood vessels, causing nosebleeds that often appear just before or during the period.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red body with redder sides, dry yellow coating. Pulse: wiry, rapid, and forceful, especially at the left Guan position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Anger and frustration cause Liver Qi to stagnate and then surge upward as Fire, directly triggering nosebleeds. Spicy and greasy foods, alcohol, and hot environments add heat to the body, further fueling the fire. Cooling foods, stress reduction, and cold compresses help drain excess heat and calm the Liver, guiding blood back downward.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Avoid spicy, greasy, and fried foods, as well as alcohol and coffee, which add heat and fuel Liver Fire. Instead, eat cooling foods like cucumber, celery, chrysanthemum tea, and mung beans. Practice stress-reducing activities such as meditation, gentle yoga, or walking to prevent anger from building up. Apply a cold compress to the nose during a nosebleed to constrict blood vessels.

### Stomach Fire (Stomach Heat)
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/stomach-fire-stomach-heat)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Heavy bleeding with bright red blood, Foul breath (bad breath), Intense thirst for cold drinks, Constipation with dry stools, Burning sensation in the stomach area, Swollen or bleeding gums
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Spicy, greasy, or fried foods, Alcohol and coffee, Emotional stress or anger, Hot weather or overheated environments
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Cooling foods and drinks, Rest and relaxation
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  The Stomach channel (the Yangming meridian) runs right through the nose. When excess heat builds up in the Stomach, it naturally rises upward along this pathway. During your period, your body's Qi and blood are already in motion, and that rising fire can push blood out through the delicate vessels in your nose. The result is a heavy, bright-red nosebleed that feels hot and forceful.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red body, especially in the center, with a thick dry yellow coating. Pulse: rapid, slippery, and forceful, particularly strong at the right middle position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Stomach Fire is a pattern of excess heat. Spicy, greasy, or hot foods and alcohol directly add more heat, making the fire flare up and push blood out of the nose. Stress and anger can also generate heat and stir Qi upward. Cooling foods and drinks, on the other hand, help clear that excess heat, while rest prevents further agitation of Qi and blood.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Avoid spicy, fried, and greasy foods, alcohol, and coffee - they add heat. Eat cooling foods like cucumber, watermelon, and peppermint tea. Stay well hydrated. Gentle exercise like walking can help Qi flow without overheating.

### Heart Fire blazing
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/heart-fire-blazing)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Bright red nosebleeds during period, Palpitations and chest heat sensation, Mental restlessness and agitation, Insomnia with vivid dreaming, Mouth or tongue ulcers with red edges
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Spicy, greasy, or fried foods, Alcohol and coffee, Emotional stress or anger, Overwork and fatigue, Hot weather or overheated environments
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Cooling foods and drinks, Rest and relaxation, Cold compress on nose
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  In Heart Fire blazing, intense heat in the Heart agitates the blood and pushes it recklessly upward. During menstruation, when blood is already moving downward, this upward surge can redirect blood to the nose, causing bright red nosebleeds. The mind is also disturbed, leading to restlessness, insomnia, and palpitations.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: Red body with a distinctly redder tip, red prickles, and a yellow dry coating. Pulse: Rapid, forceful, and overflowing (Hong), especially at the left front position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Heart Fire blazing is an excess heat condition. Anything that adds heat-spicy food, alcohol, emotional stress, or overexertion-will fan the flames and provoke nosebleeds and restlessness. Conversely, cooling foods, rest, and stress reduction help drain the fire and calm the mind, allowing blood to settle and flow downward normally during menstruation.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Avoid spicy, greasy, and heating foods such as chili, alcohol, coffee, and lamb. Instead, eat cooling foods like cucumber, watermelon, celery, and mung beans. Prioritize rest and calming activities like gentle yoga or meditation to soothe the mind. Keep a regular sleep schedule to prevent the Heart fire from flaring up at night.

### Spleen not controlling Blood
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/spleen-not-controlling-blood)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Pale, oozing nosebleed (not bright red), General fatigue and weakness, Poor appetite and loose stools, Pale or sallow complexion, Heavy or prolonged menstrual bleeding
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Raw, cold foods and drinks, Overwork and fatigue, Worry and overthinking, Spicy, greasy, or fried foods
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Rest and relaxation, Warm, cooked foods, Gentle movement or walking, Warmth on the abdomen
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  The Spleen is responsible for holding blood within the vessels. When Spleen Qi is weak, this containing function fails, and blood can leak out from the nose, especially around the menstrual period when the body's blood is already in motion. The nosebleed tends to be pale and oozing rather than bright red and forceful, and is accompanied by signs of Qi deficiency such as fatigue and poor digestion.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale, puffy, tender body with teeth marks, thin white coating. Pulse: fine, weak, especially at the right Guan position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Spleen Qi is easily damaged by cold, raw foods, and overwork. Worry and mental strain directly weaken the Spleen's ability to transform and transport, and to hold blood. Rest, warmth, and easily digested foods conserve and strengthen Spleen Qi, helping it regain its blood-holding function.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat warm, cooked, easily digestible foods like congee, soups, and stewed vegetables. Avoid raw, cold, and greasy foods that burden the Spleen. Prioritize rest and avoid overworking; gentle exercise like walking or tai chi can help build Qi without depleting it. Manage worry and overthinking, which further weaken the Spleen.

- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Cold weather or cold exposure, Overwork and fatigue, Raw, cold foods and drinks, Emotional stress or anger, Standing for long periods
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warmth and warm drinks, Rest and relaxation, Hot compress on lower abdomen, Moxibustion on lower back, Warm, cooked foods
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Yang is the body's warming and holding force. When Yang is deeply deficient, it fails to anchor and contain the blood within the vessels. During menstruation, when blood is already in motion, this weak holding causes blood to ooze out from the nose. The bleeding is scanty and pale, reflecting the cold, depleted state.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale, swollen, moist with teeth marks, white slippery coating. Pulse: deep, slow, weak.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Yang Deficiency cold fails to anchor blood; cold exposure and exhaustion further deplete Yang, making bleeding more likely. Warmth, rest, and warm foods help restore Yang and its holding function, while moxibustion directly warms the channels and stops bleeding.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat warm, cooked foods like stews and soups with ginger and cinnamon. Avoid raw, cold, and icy foods. Keep the body warm, especially the lower back and abdomen; moxibustion at home can be very helpful. Gentle exercise like walking or tai chi can support Yang, but avoid exhaustion.

## คำถามที่พบบ่อย (Frequently Asked Questions)
### Is it normal to have nosebleeds with my period?
In Western medicine, it’s considered rare and not “normal,” but in TCM it is a recognized condition called reverse menstruation. It signals that your body’s flow of Qi and blood is out of balance - usually from heat pushing upward or weakness failing to hold blood in. While it may not be dangerous, it’s a sign that something deeper needs attention, and TCM treatment can often resolve it completely.

### How does TCM treat nosebleeds during menstruation?
Treatment depends on the underlying pattern. For heat patterns (Liver Fire, Stomach Fire, Heart Fire), we use cooling herbs and acupuncture points to clear fire and guide blood downward. For deficiency patterns (Spleen Qi weakness or Yang deficiency), we strengthen the body’s ability to hold blood with tonifying herbs and warming therapies like moxibustion. The goal is to stop the nosebleeds and also to make your periods more regular and comfortable.

### Can acupuncture help with this condition?
Yes, acupuncture is very effective for redirecting the flow of Qi and blood. Points on the legs (like Sanyinjiao SP-6 and Taichong LR-3) are often used to draw energy downward and away from the head. Treatment is usually done weekly, and many women notice their nosebleeds become lighter and less frequent after just a few sessions.

### How long does it take to see results?
For excess heat patterns, you may see improvement within 1-2 menstrual cycles of consistent treatment (weekly acupuncture plus daily herbs). Deficiency patterns, where the body needs to rebuild its strength, can take 3-6 months. The key is that once the underlying imbalance is corrected, the nosebleeds tend to stop permanently rather than recurring each month.

### Will I need to change my diet?
Diet plays a supporting role. If your pattern is heat-related, you’ll be advised to avoid spicy, greasy, and heating foods (like chili, alcohol, and lamb) and to favor cooling foods such as cucumber, watermelon, and mung beans. For deficiency patterns, nourishing, warm foods like bone broth, eggs, and dates are helpful. Your practitioner will give you specific guidance based on your diagnosis.

### Is it safe to combine TCM with my usual medications?
In most cases, yes. However, always tell your TCM practitioner about all medications you take, especially blood thinners or hormonal treatments. Some herbs may have mild blood-moving effects, so coordination with your doctor is important. Never stop prescribed medications without medical advice - TCM can work alongside them to help your body regain balance.

### What if the nosebleed is very heavy?
If you experience a nosebleed that is heavy, doesn’t stop with pressure, or makes you feel faint, seek urgent medical care immediately. TCM is excellent for preventing these episodes, but an acute, severe bleed needs conventional first aid. See the Safety section for a full list of red-flag symptoms.
