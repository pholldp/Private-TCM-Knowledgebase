## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนปัจจุบัน (Conventional Medicine View)
### คำอธิบาย (Description)
In Western medicine, knee stiffness is usually a symptom of an underlying condition such as osteoarthritis, rheumatoid arthritis, a meniscal tear, or bursitis. It is often accompanied by pain, swelling, and reduced range of motion. Diagnosis typically involves a physical exam, imaging like X-rays or MRI, and sometimes blood tests to rule out inflammatory arthritis. The stiffness may be worst in the morning or after periods of inactivity, and is generally understood as a mechanical or inflammatory problem within the joint structures.

### การรักษาหลัก (Treatments)
Conventional treatment depends on the cause but commonly includes physical therapy to strengthen the muscles around the knee, non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs (NSAIDs) to reduce pain and swelling, and corticosteroid injections for more severe inflammation. In advanced osteoarthritis, hyaluronic acid injections or knee replacement surgery may be recommended. Lifestyle modifications such as weight loss and low-impact exercise are also standard advice.

## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนจีน (Traditional Chinese Medicine View)
### ความเข้าใจเกี่ยวกับการแพทย์แผนจีน (TCM Understanding)
TCM understands knee stiffness primarily through the lens of Painful Obstruction (痹证, bì zhèng), where something is blocking the smooth flow of Qi and Blood through the joint. This obstruction can come from outside - Wind, Cold, and Dampness invading the knee channels - or from inside, when the body's own deficiencies allow stagnation to take hold. The Liver governs the sinews and the Kidneys govern the bones, so when these organ systems are weakened by aging, overwork, or chronic illness, the knee loses its natural lubrication and nourishment, becoming stiff and achy.
But not all stiffness is the same. A knee that feels heavy and stiff in cold, damp weather, and that eases with a hot pack, points to a Wind-Cold-Damp obstruction. A knee that is red, hot, and swollen, and that stiffens in humid heat, suggests Damp-Heat in the channels. Stiffness that follows an old injury - fixed, stabbing, and worse with rest - is a Qi and Blood Stagnation pattern. And a knee that feels weak and stiff after fatigue, with a pale tongue and a thin pulse, signals that Qi and Blood are simply too depleted to nourish the joint.
This is why TCM doesn't have one treatment for knee stiffness. Each pattern requires a different strategy - expelling wind-damp, clearing heat, moving blood, or nourishing deficiency. The tongue, pulse, and the quality of the stiffness itself guide the practitioner to the right approach, often combining acupuncture, herbal formulas, and moxibustion to restore free flow and rebuild the joint's resilience.

### การวินิจฉัยโดยแพทย์แผนจีน (Practitioner Diagnosis)
A practitioner starts by asking when the stiffness began and what makes it better or worse. If the knee stiffened suddenly after exposure to cold wind or damp weather, and feels heavy and painful, that points toward Wind-Cold-Damp obstruction. The tongue often looks pale with a thin white coating, and the pulse may feel wiry or tight, confirming that external pathogens have lodged in the channels.
If the knee is not only stiff but also red, warm, and swollen, especially in humid or hot conditions, Damp-Heat in the channels is likely. The practitioner checks for a red tongue with a yellow, greasy coating and a rapid, slippery pulse. These signs of heat and moisture help distinguish this pattern from cold-damp invasions, where the joint feels cold rather than hot.
When stiffness follows an old injury or years of repetitive strain, and the pain is fixed and stabbing, Qi and Blood Stagnation is the main concern. The tongue may be dusky or show purple spots, and the pulse often feels choppy or wiry. Gentle movement might briefly ease the stiffness, whereas rest makes it feel locked, which is a useful clue for the practitioner.
Gradual knee stiffness that worsens with overuse, feels worse at night, and comes with weakness or soreness in the lower back suggests Liver and Kidney Deficiency. The tongue is often red with little coating, and the pulse is thready and weak. This pattern is more common with aging, and the practitioner looks for signs that the sinews and bones are not being properly nourished.
When the knee feels weak, achy, and stiff after rest, and the person looks pale and fatigued, Qi and Blood Deficiency may be the root. The tongue is pale and the pulse is weak and thready. Unlike stagnation patterns, the stiffness here often improves with gentle activity and worsens when the person is run-down, guiding the practitioner toward a tonifying approach.

### Painful Obstruction with Wind-Cold-Damp
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/painful-obstruction-with-wind-cold-damp)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Knee stiffness worse in cold or damp weather, Pain and heaviness in the knee, Swelling without redness or heat, Pain relieved by warmth, Aversion to cold or wind
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Cold and damp weather, Exposure to wind or drafts, Prolonged sitting or standing, Eating cold or raw foods
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Applying warmth (hot pack, warm bath), Gentle movement (walking, tai chi), Dry, warm weather, Moxibustion on the knee
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When Wind, Cold, and Damp invade the knee joint, they block the flow of Qi and Blood through the channels. Cold causes the tissues to contract and stiffen, Dampness adds heaviness and swelling, and Wind makes the pain shift from spot to spot. This obstruction starves the sinews and bones of nourishment, leaving the knee stiff, achy, and hard to bend, especially in cold or wet weather.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale, slightly swollen with teeth marks, white greasy coating, moist surface. Pulse: wiry and tight (弦紧 xián jǐn), or slow (迟 chí); may feel soggy (濡 rú) if Dampness predominates.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Cold and Dampness are the main culprits in this pattern. Cold contracts the channels and slows circulation, while Dampness creates heaviness and swelling. Warmth relaxes the channels and promotes Qi and Blood flow, and gentle movement disperses stagnation. Cold, damp weather, and inactivity allow the pathogenic factors to settle deeper, making stiffness worse.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Keep your knees warm with a wrap or knee sleeve, especially in cold or damp weather. Gentle daily movement like walking or tai chi helps keep Qi flowing without straining the joint. Eat warming, cooked foods like soups with ginger and cinnamon; avoid raw, cold, or greasy foods that create internal Dampness.

### Painful Obstruction with Liver and Kidney Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/painful-obstruction-with-liver-and-kidney-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Dull, chronic knee ache, Lower back soreness and weakness, Stiffness worse after rest or at night, Fatigue and weak, heavy legs, Preference for warmth and pressure
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Cold and damp weather, Overexertion and prolonged standing, Inadequate rest and fatigue, Cold, raw foods and drinks, Aging and chronic illness
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warmth and gentle heat, Moderate, consistent activity, Nourishing warm foods, Rest after overexertion
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  The Liver governs the sinews and the Kidneys govern the bones. When these organs are weakened by aging, chronic illness, or overwork, they fail to nourish the knee joint adequately. The joint becomes under-lubricated and malnourished, leading to a gradual, deep stiffness that worsens with fatigue.
Because the body's foundation is deficient, cold and damp from the environment can more easily settle into the knee, further congealing Qi and Blood flow. This creates a chronic cycle of weakness, stiffness, and dull ache that feels worse at night and after rest.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale, thin body with a thin white coating. Pulse: fine (thin), weak, and slow, especially weak at the Chi (rear) position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Cold and damp contract the channels and congeal Qi, making the under-nourished joint even stiffer, while warmth relaxes the sinews and encourages Blood flow. Overexertion further drains the already depleted Liver and Kidney energy, so rest and moderate movement restore function without causing harm. Nourishing, warm foods directly build the Blood and Essence that are lacking, providing the raw materials needed to lubricate and strengthen the knee joint.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Keep the knees warm and protected from drafts. Gentle, daily movement like Tai Chi or walking helps circulate Qi without straining the joint. Eat warm, nourishing foods such as bone broth, walnuts, black beans, and dark leafy greens. Avoid cold, raw foods and prolonged standing.

### Painful Obstruction due to Damp Heat in Channels
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/painful-obstruction-due-to-damp-heat-in-channels)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Knee is red and hot to the touch, Heavy, swollen feeling in the knee, Stiffness worsens in warm, humid weather, Relief from cold compresses, Thirst and dark, scanty urine
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Hot, humid weather, Hot compresses or baths, Spicy, greasy food, Alcohol
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Cold packs, Rest in a cool, dry place, Cooling foods and drinks, Avoiding humid weather
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When Damp-Heat settles in the knee joint, the combination of heavy, sticky dampness and fiery heat causes the tissues to swell, redden, and feel hot to the touch. This swelling and the dampness obstructing the channels make the knee feel tight and stiff, especially when the weather is humid or hot. The heat component makes the pain worse with warmth and better with cold.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red body, possibly slightly swollen, with a yellow greasy coating. Pulse: slippery and rapid.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Damp-Heat is a combination of pathogenic dampness and heat. Cold applications and cooling foods reduce heat, while avoiding humidity prevents dampness from accumulating. Spicy, greasy foods and alcohol generate more Damp-Heat, and hot compresses add heat to an already inflamed joint, worsening stiffness and pain.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Avoid spicy, greasy, and fried foods that generate Damp-Heat. Eat cooling, damp-draining foods like celery, cucumber, mung beans, and barley. Keep the knee cool and dry; avoid hot compresses. Gentle range-of-motion exercises help prevent stiffness without overheating the joint.

### Qi And Blood Stagnation
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/qi-and-blood-stagnation)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Fixed, stabbing knee pain, Stiffness eases with gentle movement but worsens with rest, History of knee injury or chronic strain, Dark purple tongue with stasis spots, Irritability or emotional tension
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Prolonged sitting or inactivity, Cold, damp weather, Stress and frustration, Heavy, greasy meals
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Gentle movement (walking, stretching), Warm compress or heat therapy, Eating warming spices like turmeric, Light massage
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When Qi and Blood stagnate in the knee, often after an old injury or years of chronic strain, the channels become blocked. This prevents the joint from getting proper nourishment and lubrication, causing a deep, fixed stiffness and stabbing pain. The stiffness may feel slightly better after gentle movement, which temporarily gets the blood flowing, but returns with rest or overuse.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: dark purple body with stasis spots and distended sublingual veins. Pulse: wiry (弦 xián) and choppy (涩 sè), feeling rough and uneven.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Stagnation of Qi and Blood is the core issue. Gentle movement and warmth help to move Qi and dilate the vessels, breaking up stagnation. Cold and inactivity cause further contraction and stasis. Stress knots the Liver Qi, which controls smooth flow, worsening the blockage. Heavy foods create dampness that gums up the channels.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Keep the knee warm and avoid cold drafts. Gentle daily movement like walking or Tai Chi helps circulate Qi and Blood, but avoid overexertion. Include warming spices in your diet such as turmeric, ginger, and black pepper. ### Painful Obstruction with Qi and Blood Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/painful-obstruction-with-qi-and-blood-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Dull aching knee pain that worsens with exertion, Feeling of weakness and heaviness in the knees, Numbness or tingling in the limbs, Fatigue and pale complexion, Stiffness often worse after rest or inactivity, Sensitivity to cold
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Overexertion or prolonged standing, Cold and damp weather, Skipping meals or poor diet, Emotional stress and worry, Excessive sweating
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Rest and gentle warmth, Eating warm, nourishing meals, Gentle stretching or tai chi, Massage with warm oil, Adequate sleep and relaxation
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When the body's Qi and Blood are depleted, the muscles, tendons, and ligaments around the knee lose their nourishment. This leaves the joint feeling weak and poorly supported, so it stiffens easily, especially after rest or when you're tired. The deficiency also means circulation is sluggish, so the knee feels achy and heavy rather than sharp and inflamed.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale, possibly thin with teeth marks, thin white coating. Pulse: fine (细 xì) and weak (弱 ruò), may feel slow (缓 huǎn), especially weak in the rear (chi) positions and right middle (guan) position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Qi and Blood deficiency means the body has limited resources to nourish the sinews and joints. Rest and warm, nourishing food allow the body to rebuild these reserves, while overexertion, cold, and stress further deplete them, making stiffness worse. Warmth encourages gentle circulation, and light movement prevents stagnation without draining precious energy.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat warm, cooked, easily digestible foods like soups, stews, and congees to support Spleen Qi and Blood production. Include moderate amounts of lean protein, dark leafy greens, and a little red meat or bone broth. Avoid cold, raw foods and excessive standing or walking that drains Qi. Gentle daily movement like slow walking or tai chi is beneficial, but always rest before you become exhausted.

## คำถามที่พบบ่อย (Frequently Asked Questions)
### Can acupuncture really help with knee stiffness?
Yes. Acupuncture works by unblocking the channels that run through the knee, restoring the free flow of Qi and Blood. For many people, the knee feels looser and warmer even after the first session. The points chosen depend on the underlying pattern - for example, ST-35 (Dubi) and GB-34 (Yanglingquan) are almost always used locally, while distal points like SP-9 (Yinlingquan) address dampness or heat.

### How long until I see results?
Most patients notice some reduction in stiffness within 3 to 6 weeks of weekly acupuncture combined with daily herbs. Acute, recent-onset stiffness often improves faster. Chronic stiffness that has built up over years, especially when there is underlying deficiency, may take 2 to 4 months of consistent treatment to see meaningful, lasting change.

### Can I keep taking my anti-inflammatory medication while doing TCM?
In most cases, yes. TCM treatment can run alongside NSAIDs or other prescribed medications. However, some blood-moving herbs (such as Dang Gui, Chuan Xiong, or Yan Hu Suo) may interact with anticoagulant or antiplatelet drugs. Always bring a full list of your medications to your TCM practitioner, and never stop prescribed medication without consulting your doctor.

### Are there foods I should avoid to help my knee stiffness?
Generally, it's wise to avoid cold, raw foods and icy drinks, which can worsen internal dampness and cold - two big contributors to knee stiffness. Greasy, fried, and very sweet foods also tend to create dampness. Instead, favor warm, cooked meals with gentle spices like ginger, turmeric, and black pepper, which help move Qi and Blood and warm the channels.

### Is TCM safe for elderly patients with knee stiffness?
Yes, TCM is often an excellent choice for older adults because it is gentle and aims to rebuild the body's reserves rather than simply suppress symptoms. Herbal formulas and acupuncture points are adjusted to the individual's constitution. In fact, many of the classic formulas for knee stiffness, like Du Huo Ji Sheng Tang, were designed specifically to address the Liver and Kidney deficiency that comes with aging.

### Will the stiffness come back after treatment?
TCM aims to resolve the root imbalance, not just mask the symptom. When the underlying pattern is corrected - whether that means expelling lingering dampness, nourishing deficient Kidneys, or moving old blood stasis - the knee is less likely to stiffen again under normal circumstances. However, if the same lifestyle or environmental factors that caused the problem return, the stiffness can recur. Your practitioner will give you dietary and self-care guidance to help prevent that.

## การวิจัยทางวิทยาศาสตร์สมัยใหม่ (Modern Scientific Research)
1. **Acupuncture for peripheral joint osteoarthritis** ([ลิงก์](https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD001977.pub3))
   *วารสารวิชาการ: Manheimer E, Cheng K, Linde K, et al. Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews. 2010; Issue 1: CD001977.*
   A Cochrane systematic review of 16 RCTs found that acupuncture provides statistically significant improvements in pain and function for peripheral joint osteoarthritis, including the knee, compared to sham acupuncture or usual care. The benefits were modest but clinically relevant, with a low risk of serious adverse events.

2. **Acupuncture for chronic knee pain: a randomised clinical trial** ([ลิงก์](https://doi.org/10.1136/bmj.m697))
   *วารสารวิชาการ: Tu JF, Yang JW, Shi GX, et al. BMJ. 2020;368:m697.*
   This 2020 single-blind RCT published in the BMJ compared acupuncture, sham acupuncture, and usual care in 282 patients. Acupuncture significantly reduced knee pain and stiffness at 12 weeks compared to sham, and the effects were maintained at 1 year. The study supports acupuncture as a viable treatment for chronic knee stiffness.

3. **Traditional Chinese Medications for Knee Osteoarthritis Pain: A Meta-Analysis of Randomized Controlled Trials** ([ลิงก์](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27222066))
   *วารสารวิชาการ: Chen B, Zhan H, Marszalek J, et al. Am J Chin Med. 2016;44(4):677-703.*
   A meta-analysis of 20 RCTs evaluating Chinese herbal medicine for knee osteoarthritis. The analysis found that oral herbal medicine significantly reduced pain and stiffness compared to placebo or conventional analgesics, with Du Huo Ji Sheng Tang being the most studied formula. The authors noted that most trials were of low to moderate quality.
