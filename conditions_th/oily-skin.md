## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนปัจจุบัน (Conventional Medicine View)
### คำอธิบาย (Description)
Western medicine describes oily skin as the result of overactive sebaceous glands producing excess sebum. This can be influenced by hormonal fluctuations (especially androgens), genetics, stress, humidity, and certain medications. It often manifests as a shiny complexion, enlarged pores, and a predisposition to acne or blackheads. Diagnosis is based on visual examination, and treatment typically focuses on controlling sebum production and preventing clogged pores.

### การรักษาหลัก (Treatments)
Conventional management of oily skin includes gentle cleansing, oil-free moisturizers, and topical agents like salicylic acid, benzoyl peroxide, or retinoids to reduce oil and unclog pores. For more persistent cases, oral medications such as combined oral contraceptives, spironolactone, or isotretinoin may be prescribed to regulate hormones or shrink sebaceous glands.

## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนจีน (Traditional Chinese Medicine View)
### ความเข้าใจเกี่ยวกับการแพทย์แผนจีน (TCM Understanding)
In TCM, the skin is seen as an extension of the internal organs, and the quality of facial oil reflects the state of your digestive and emotional health. The Spleen and Stomach are responsible for transforming food and fluids into usable energy and moisture. When this system is weakened by poor diet, irregular eating, or stress, it fails to manage fluids properly. These stagnant fluids turn into dampness, a heavy, turbid substance that can rise to the face and make the skin greasy.
But dampness alone is only one piece of the puzzle. If the body is also hot - from spicy food, alcohol, or emotional fire - dampness combines with heat to create a more inflamed, shiny, and often acne-prone complexion. This Damp-Heat pattern is one of the most common TCM causes of oily skin. Alternatively, long-standing dampness can thicken into phlegm, producing a waxy, dull oiliness with a sallow tone.
The Liver plays a role too. When stress, frustration, or anger cause Liver Qi to stagnate and generate Fire, that Fire blazes upward along the Liver channel to the face, heating the skin and stimulating oil glands. This pattern produces a red, flushed, oily face that flares with stress. Finally, if the body's cooling Yin fluids are depleted - from overwork, chronic illness, or aging - a low-grade 'empty heat' can rise to the face, creating a surface shine over dry, undernourished skin.
Because each pattern has a different root, the same symptom of oily skin can call for completely different treatments: one person may need to clear Damp-Heat from the digestive system, while another must cool Liver Fire or nourish Yin. This is why TCM doesn't offer a one-size-fits-all solution.

### การวินิจฉัยโดยแพทย์แผนจีน (Practitioner Diagnosis)
A TCM practitioner starts by examining the quality of the oiliness and the overall complexion. Is the shine thin and watery, or thick and sticky? Is the face red and flushed, or dull and sallow? These first impressions, combined with questions about digestion, emotions, and energy, quickly narrow the possibilities.
If the skin is persistently greasy, with a bright shine and maybe acne or blackheads, and the person feels heavy, bloated, or has a sticky taste in the mouth, the picture points toward Damp-Heat in the Stomach and Spleen. The tongue often shows a thick, greasy yellow coating, and the pulse feels slippery and rapid, confirming that dampness and heat are steaming upward.
When the oil feels thicker and waxier, and the face has a dull, almost muddy undertone, Damp-Phlegm is likely. Here the tongue is swollen with a greasy white coat, and the pulse is slippery but less forceful. The person may also describe a foggy head, chest tightness, or a sensation of something stuck in the throat, signs that dampness has congealed into phlegm.
A red, flushed face with oily shine that flares with stress or anger suggests Liver Fire Blazing. The practitioner will ask about irritability, headaches, a bitter taste in the mouth, and rib-side tension. The tongue is red with a yellow coat, and the pulse feels wiry and rapid, a pattern that reveals heat rising along the Liver channel directly to the face.
If the face looks shiny yet feels tight or dry, with fine lines and a tendency to flush in the afternoon or evening, the root is Empty-Heat from Yin Deficiency. The tongue is red with little or no coating, and the pulse is thin and rapid. Other clues include night sweats, a dry throat, and a feeling of warmth in the palms and soles, indicating a lack of cooling Yin fluids.

### Damp-Heat in Stomach and Spleen
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/damp-heat-in-stomach-and-spleen)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Persistent greasy shine all over the face, Bloating and fullness in the upper abdomen, especially after eating, Sticky or bitter taste in the mouth, Loose, sticky bowel movements that feel incomplete, Heavy, foggy sensation in the head
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Greasy, fried foods, Dairy and sweets, Alcohol, Hot and humid weather, Overeating or eating late at night
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Light, bland meals, Cooling foods and drinks, Regular, complete bowel movements, Gentle exercise
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Your Spleen and Stomach are responsible for transforming food and fluids into usable energy. When they become overwhelmed by rich food, alcohol, or stress, they fail to manage moisture properly. This undigested moisture turns into dampness, which then combines with heat. The hot, heavy dampness rises like steam to your face, causing a persistently greasy, shiny complexion that just keeps coming back.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red body, possibly swollen with teeth marks, and a thick, yellow, greasy coating that is most obvious in the centre and back. Pulse: soggy, rapid, and slippery, strongest at the right guan (Spleen-Stomach) position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Dampness and heat are directly fuelled by rich, greasy, sweet, and alcoholic foods, which overwhelm the Spleen and generate internal steam. A light, cooling diet and gentle movement help the Spleen drain moisture and clear heat, cutting off the source of the oily shine. Regular bowel movements remove damp-heat from the body, while humid environments add external dampness, making symptoms worse.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Cut back on fried, greasy, and sugary foods, as well as dairy and alcohol - they all create dampness and heat. Focus on light, cooked meals with plenty of vegetables, and include barley, mung beans, or job’s tears. Gentle daily movement like walking helps your Spleen process moisture, and never skip breakfast.

### Damp-Phlegm
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/damp-phlegm)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Thick, sticky, waxy oil on the face, not just a shiny sheen, Dull, sallow complexion, Poor appetite, bloating, and a sticky taste in the mouth with no thirst, Heavy sensation in the limbs and head, as if wrapped in a wet towel, Brain fog and drowsiness
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Greasy, fried, or sweet foods, Cold, raw foods and iced drinks, Hot and humid weather, Sedentary lifestyle, Overeating or irregular meals
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warm, cooked meals, Gentle exercise, Avoiding dairy and sugar, Dry, well-ventilated environment, Gentle skin cleansing
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When the Spleen is weak, it fails to transform and transport fluids. These fluids stagnate into dampness, which over time thickens into a sticky, turbid phlegm.
This phlegm rises to the face along the Stomach channel, clogging the pores and creating a heavy, waxy oiliness that feels thick rather than just shiny. The complexion often looks dull and sallow because the phlegm obscures the normal luster of the skin.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale, swollen body with scalloped edges and a thick, white, greasy coat that looks oily. Pulse: slippery (滑, huá), like beads rolling through oil.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Greasy and cold foods weaken the Spleen and generate dampness, which congeals into phlegm and surfaces as oily skin. Warm, cooked meals and light exercise strengthen the Spleen’s ability to transform fluids, reducing the source of phlegm. Damp environments further burden the body, while gentle movement helps disperse stagnation.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat warm, cooked meals and avoid cold, raw, greasy, and sweet foods that generate dampness. Gentle daily movement like walking helps the Spleen transform fluids. Cleanse your face with a mild cleanser, but don’t strip the skin-overwashing can trigger more oil production.

### Liver Fire Blazing
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/liver-fire-blazing)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Flushed, red face with oily shine, Bitter taste in the mouth, Intense irritability or short temper, Throbbing headache at the temples
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Stress and anger, Spicy, fried, or greasy foods, Alcohol and coffee, Late nights and overwork
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Cooling foods and drinks, Stress reduction and relaxation, Adequate sleep and rest, Gentle exercise
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When the Liver is overloaded by stress, anger, or frustration, its Qi can stagnate and turn into Fire. This Fire follows the Liver channel upward to the face, heating the skin and stimulating the sebaceous glands to overproduce oil. The result is a red, flushed, and intensely shiny complexion that flares up with emotional tension.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red body with redder sides, dry yellow coating. Pulse: wiry and rapid, especially forceful at the left middle position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Stress and anger directly stagnate Liver Qi, which then transforms into Fire that surges upward to the face, making skin red and oily. Heating foods and stimulants add fuel to that Fire. Cooling foods, rest, and relaxation help clear the Liver, settle the Fire, and restore a calm, balanced complexion.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Favor cooling, bitter, and lightly cooked foods like cucumber, celery, chrysanthemum tea, and watermelon. Avoid alcohol, coffee, spicy dishes, and fried foods, which add heat. Regular, moderate exercise and a consistent sleep schedule help smooth Liver Qi flow. Practices like journaling, meditation, or gentle yoga can prevent emotional heat from building up.

### Empty-Heat caused by Yin Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/empty-heat-caused-by-yin-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Oily but dry, tight-feeling skin, Flushed cheekbones (malar flush), Night sweats, Dry mouth and throat, worse at night, Heat in palms, soles, and chest, Restlessness and insomnia
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Spicy or fried foods, Alcohol and coffee, Late nights and overwork, Emotional stress, Dry, hot weather
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Cooling foods and drinks, Adequate sleep and rest, Gentle exercise, Humid environment
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When the body's Yin fluids are depleted, they can no longer anchor the body's warmth. This creates a low-grade 'empty heat' that rises to the face. The heat stimulates oil glands, causing a surface shine, but beneath the skin remains dry and undernourished, leading to fine lines and a tight sensation.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red, thin, dry body with cracks and little or no coating. Pulse: fine (thin) and rapid.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Empty heat is a byproduct of depleted Yin, so anything that further consumes Yin or adds heat will worsen the oily shine and dryness. Spicy foods, alcohol, and late nights are particularly draining. Cooling, moistening foods and adequate rest help rebuild Yin fluids and calm the empty heat, reducing the paradoxical oily-yet-dry complexion.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Prioritize moistening, Yin-nourishing foods like pear, lily bulb, black sesame, and tofu. Sip warm water throughout the day rather than drinking large amounts at once. Avoid spicy, fried, or overly heating foods. Go to bed early and avoid overwork or late-night screen time, as these habits consume Yin. Gentle, grounding exercises like tai chi or yoga are better than intense, sweat-drenching workouts.

## คำถามที่พบบ่อย (Frequently Asked Questions)
### Why does my face get oily even though I wash it often?
Over-washing can strip your skin's natural protective oils, which may trigger your sebaceous glands to produce even more oil as a defense. In TCM, oily skin is driven by internal dampness, heat, or phlegm rising to the face, so cleaning the surface alone cannot resolve the root cause. A gentler cleansing routine combined with internal treatment often yields better, more lasting results.

### Can TCM really cure oily skin?
TCM aims to correct the internal imbalance that generates excess oil, so many people achieve long-term improvement rather than a temporary fix. While it may not permanently 'cure' oily skin if underlying dietary or lifestyle habits persist, it often restores a balanced complexion that requires minimal maintenance. The goal is to make your skin's oil production appropriate for your body's needs.

### Is oily skin in TCM always related to digestion?
Digestion is a very common root, but not the only one. Patterns like Damp-Heat and Damp-Phlegm are directly tied to a weak Spleen and Stomach, while Liver Fire comes from emotional stress and Yin Deficiency from depleted fluids. Your practitioner will look at the quality of the oiliness and your other symptoms to pinpoint the source.

### Will acupuncture help my oily skin?
Yes, acupuncture can help regulate the organ systems involved - clearing heat, draining dampness, and calming the mind - which in turn can reduce facial oiliness. Points are chosen based on your specific pattern, and many people notice their skin feels less greasy and more balanced after a series of treatments.

### Do I need to change my diet for TCM to work?
Diet is a cornerstone of TCM treatment for oily skin. Foods that create dampness and heat - like greasy, spicy, sweet, and dairy-rich items - directly feed the patterns that cause oiliness. Your practitioner will guide you toward foods that support your specific pattern, but generally a light, cooling, and easy-to-digest diet helps speed results and prevent recurrence.

### Can I combine TCM with my current skincare routine?
Absolutely. TCM works internally, so you can continue with gentle, non-stripping skincare. However, it's wise to avoid harsh drying products that may irritate your skin and work against the rebalancing process. Always let your TCM practitioner know about any medicated topicals you're using.

## การวิจัยทางวิทยาศาสตร์สมัยใหม่ (Modern Scientific Research)
1. **Complementary therapies for acne vulgaris** ([ลิงก์](https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD009436.pub2))
   *วารสารวิชาการ: Cao H, Yang G, Wang Y, et al. Complementary therapies for acne vulgaris. Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews. 2015, Issue 1. Art. No.: CD009436.*
   This Cochrane review assessed herbal medicine, acupuncture, and other complementary therapies for acne. It found moderate-quality evidence that some Chinese herbal formulas reduce lesion count and may improve skin oiliness, though the wide variety of interventions limits definitive conclusions.

2. **Acupuncture for acne vulgaris: a systematic review and meta-analysis** ([ลิงก์](https://doi.org/10.1097/MD.0000000000008826))
   *วารสารวิชาการ: Li X, Xiao Y, Wang J, et al. Acupuncture for acne vulgaris: a systematic review and meta-analysis. Medicine (Baltimore). 2017;96(48):e8826.*
   A meta-analysis of 12 randomized controlled trials involving 1,246 participants. Acupuncture significantly reduced acne severity scores and sebum excretion rates compared to conventional treatments, suggesting a direct effect on oil production.
