## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนปัจจุบัน (Conventional Medicine View)
### คำอธิบาย (Description)
In Western medicine, heart palpitations are the uncomfortable awareness of your heartbeat - often described as fluttering, pounding, racing, or skipping a beat. They can be triggered by stress, caffeine, hormonal changes, or underlying conditions like arrhythmias, thyroid disease, or anxiety. Diagnosis usually involves an electrocardiogram (ECG), a Holter monitor, or blood tests to rule out serious causes. While many palpitations are harmless, they can be unsettling and disruptive to daily life.

### การรักษาหลัก (Treatments)
Conventional treatment depends on the cause. For benign palpitations, reducing stimulants and managing stress may be enough. When an arrhythmia is present, doctors may prescribe beta-blockers, calcium channel blockers, or antiarrhythmic drugs. In some cases, procedures like cardioversion or ablation are recommended. For anxiety-related palpitations, therapy or medication may be offered.

## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนจีน (Traditional Chinese Medicine View)
### ความเข้าใจเกี่ยวกับการแพทย์แผนจีน (TCM Understanding)
In TCM, the Heart is not just a pump - it houses the Shen, the spirit that governs consciousness and emotional calm. When the Shen is well-anchored by abundant Heart Blood and Yin, the heartbeat is steady and unnoticed. Palpitations arise when the Shen is disturbed - either because the Heart isn't being nourished enough, or because a disruptive force like Heat, Phlegm, or stagnant Blood is agitating it.
This is why the same palpitation can have different roots. A fluttery, timid sensation that worsens with stress points to Heart Qi Deficiency - the Heart lacks the energy to keep the Shen settled. A pounding, anxious palpitation with night sweats suggests Heart Yin Deficiency, where cooling Yin has dried up and empty Heat is flaring. A heavy, suppressed beat with cold limbs signals Heart Yang Deficiency, the warming fire of the Heart growing dim. And when the palpitations feel hot, forceful, and come with chest tightness and a bitter taste, Phlegm-Fire is likely the culprit.
Other organs are often involved. The Spleen must produce enough Qi and Blood to supply the Heart - when digestion is weak from worry or poor diet, Heart and Spleen Deficiency develop together, leading to palpitations with fatigue and pale complexion. The Kidneys store the body's Yin and Yang reserves; when Kidney Yin is low, Heart Yin often follows, causing night-time palpitations. And emotional stagnation can generate Fire that rises to disturb the Heart. TCM practitioners read the tongue and pulse to distinguish these patterns: a pale, puffy tongue with a weak pulse points to deficiency, while a red tongue with a rapid, slippery pulse suggests heat or phlegm.

### การวินิจฉัยโดยแพทย์แผนจีน (Practitioner Diagnosis)
When someone comes in with palpitations, a TCM practitioner first asks about the sensation - is it racing, pounding, skipping, or fluttering? The character of the heartbeat, along with what makes it better or worse, provides the first clues. The tongue and pulse then confirm whether the root is a deficiency of Qi or Blood, an excess of Phlegm or Fire, or a stagnation of Blood.
If the palpitations come with marked fatigue, poor appetite, and a pale, washed-out complexion, the practitioner suspects Heart and Spleen Qi and Blood Deficiency. The tongue is pale with a thin coating, and the pulse feels weak and thready. This pattern arises when the Spleen fails to produce enough Qi and Blood to nourish the Heart, leaving the mind unsettled.
When palpitations are accompanied by anxiety, irritability, night sweats, and a feeling of heat in the palms and soles, Heart Yin Deficiency is likely. The tongue appears red with little or no coating, and the pulse is thin and rapid. The lack of Yin allows empty heat to rise, disturbing the Heart and making the person feel restless and on edge.
Palpitations that appear after a fright or during stress, with a sense of timidity and being easily startled, point to Heart Qi Deficiency with an underlying Gallbladder weakness. The tongue is pale, and the pulse is thin and wiry. Here the Heart Qi is too weak to ground the spirit, so even minor shocks trigger a flutter.
A heavy, oppressed sensation in the chest along with palpitations, cold hands and feet, and a deep, weak pulse suggest Heart Yang Deficiency. The tongue is pale and swollen. In this pattern, the warming, activating force of Yang is so depleted that blood circulation becomes sluggish, and the heartbeat feels laborious.
If the palpitations come and go with a sense of heat, chest tightness, a bitter taste in the mouth, and a thick yellow tongue coating, Phlegm-Fire is harassing the Heart. The pulse is slippery and rapid. This excess pattern often appears in people with a robust constitution and a diet rich in greasy or spicy foods, which generate Phlegm and Fire that agitate the mind.
When stabbing chest pain accompanies the palpitations, and the tongue is dark purple with a wiry, choppy pulse, the diagnosis shifts to Heart Blood Stagnation. This pattern indicates that blood is not flowing smoothly through the heart vessels, often after long-standing Qi stagnation or cold, and is more common in chronic or severe cases.

### Heart and Spleen Qi and Blood Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/heart-and-spleen-qi-and-blood-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Palpitations that worsen with exertion or after meals, Poor appetite with bloating after eating, Pale or sallow complexion, lips, and nails, Deep fatigue and lack of energy, Insomnia with excessive dreaming and forgetfulness, Loose stools
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Worry and overthinking, Raw, cold foods and drinks, Skipping meals or irregular eating, Overwork and mental exhaustion, Excessive physical exertion
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Rest and relaxation, Warm, cooked meals, Regular meal times, Gentle movement (walking, qigong), Stress management and meditation
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  The Heart relies on a steady supply of Qi and Blood to house the spirit calmly. When the Spleen is weak-often from overwork, worry, or poor diet-it fails to produce enough Qi and Blood. This leaves the Heart undernourished and unanchored, so the spirit becomes restless and you feel your heart fluttering or racing, especially when you're tired or after eating.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale, puffy, and tender with teeth marks on the edges and a thin white coating. Pulse: fine and weak; the left Cun (Heart) position feels empty, the right Guan (Spleen) position is soft and weak.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: The Spleen's ability to produce Qi and Blood is easily damaged by mental strain, irregular eating, and overexertion-all of which drain the very resources the Heart needs to stay calm. Warm, regular nourishment and adequate rest rebuild Spleen Qi, allowing Blood to be manufactured and delivered to the Heart. When the Heart is properly nourished, the spirit settles and palpitations naturally ease.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat warm, cooked foods that are easy to digest-think soups, stews, and congees with root vegetables, a little ginger, and small amounts of quality protein. Avoid raw, cold, and greasy foods that tax the Spleen. Rest is medicine: prioritize consistent sleep, short naps if needed, and gentle movement like walking or qigong rather than intense exercise. Protect your energy by setting boundaries on overthinking and overworking.

### Heart Yin Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/heart-yin-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Palpitations worse at night or when lying down, Night sweats, Five-palm heat (heat in palms, soles, chest), Anxiety and mental restlessness, Red cheekbones (malar flush)
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Emotional stress and anxiety, Spicy, fried, or heating foods, Alcohol and coffee, Hot weather or overheated rooms, Overwork and insufficient sleep, Late nights and screen time before bed
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Rest and quiet environment, Cooling, moistening foods, Hydration with water or herbal teas, Gentle exercise like tai chi or walking, Early bedtime and consistent sleep schedule
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Heart Yin is the cooling, moistening substance that anchors the mind. When it becomes depleted, the heart lacks its calming reservoir, allowing empty heat to flare upward. This heat agitates the mind, causing the heart to beat fast and irregularly - especially at night when Yin should be dominant. The result is palpitations accompanied by anxiety, night sweats, and a restless, fluttering sensation.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red body, especially at the tip, with little or no coating, dry surface, possible midline crack. Pulse: fine (thin) and rapid, weak at the deep level, especially in the left cun (Heart) position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Heart Yin is cooling and moistening. Triggers like stress, heat, and overwork consume Yin and generate empty heat, which agitates the mind and worsens palpitations. Rest, cooling foods, and nourishing activities replenish Yin and calm the spirit, allowing the heart to settle.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat cooling, moistening foods such as pear, lily bulb, black sesame, goji berries, and cucumber. Avoid spicy, fried, or heating foods, and keep alcohol and coffee to a minimum. Go to bed early and create a calm, dark sleeping environment. Gentle activities like tai chi, yoga, or walking help without depleting Yin.

### Heart Qi Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/heart-qi-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Palpitations worsened by exertion, Shortness of breath on activity, Fatigue and lack of stamina, Pale complexion, Reluctance to speak or weak voice
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Physical exertion, Fright or sudden shock, Skipping meals, Late nights and overwork
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Rest and sleep, Gentle movement like walking, Warm, nourishing soups
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Your Heart relies on Qi to maintain a steady, calm rhythm. When Heart Qi is insufficient, the Heart muscle lacks the energy to beat evenly, so you feel it flutter or pound. This is why the palpitations worsen with exertion - activity demands more Qi, which your Heart struggles to supply. The shortness of breath and fatigue are direct results of Qi being too weak to sustain normal circulation and respiration.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale, puffy, thin white coat. Pulse: empty (虚, xū) - soft at first but disappears under pressure; weakest at the left Cun (Heart) position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Heart Qi is the engine of circulation; physical exertion and overwork drain it further, worsening the palpitations. Fright and shock scatter Qi, which is why a sudden scare can make your heart pound. Rest and gentle movement help conserve and rebuild Qi. Warm, nourishing foods are easy to digest and contribute directly to the production of new Qi. Skipping meals deprives the Spleen of the fuel it needs to generate Qi, weakening the Heart over time.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat warm, easily digested foods like congee, stews, and soups to preserve digestive Qi. Gentle movement like walking or Tai Chi builds Qi without depleting it. Avoid overexertion and late nights, which drain Heart Qi.

### Heart Yang Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/heart-yang-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Palpitations feel heavy or suppressed, Worse with exertion, Cold hands and feet, Bright pale face, Chest stuffiness or mild chest pain, Mental listlessness and fatigue
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Cold weather or drafts, Overexertion or heavy exercise, Raw, cold foods and iced drinks, Emotional shock or fear
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warmth (clothing, room), Rest and lying down, Warm drinks and soups, Gentle movement in the sun
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Heart Yang is the warming, driving force that keeps blood moving and the chest comfortable. When it becomes depleted, the heart lacks the fire it needs to pump with steady strength. The result is a heavy, suppressed, or sinking palpitation, often with a sense of cold and tightness in the chest.
Because Yang is the body's warmth, its deficiency quickly shows up as cold hands and feet, a pale face, and an aversion to chill. Exertion demands even more Yang, which is why the fluttering or pounding gets noticeably worse with any activity.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale, puffy, and tender with teeth marks and a white slippery coating. Pulse: deep, weak, and most notably faint at the left cun (Heart) position; may be knotted (slow with irregular pauses).
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Heart Yang is the body's warming fire; cold and overwork smother that fire, making the heartbeat feel heavy and irregular. Rest and warmth gently restore the Yang, allowing the heart to pump more steadily. Cold foods and environments drain what little warmth remains, so protection from chill is the most direct way to ease this pattern.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Keep the chest and feet warm, and avoid cold or raw foods and iced drinks. Gentle daily walks in the sun are ideal, but stop before you feel tired. Warm, cooked meals like soups and stews with a little ginger or cinnamon help kindle internal warmth.

- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Greasy, fried or spicy food, Alcohol and coffee, Stress and frustration, Hot, humid weather
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Cool, light meals, Calm, cool environment, Gentle movement like walking
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When the Spleen fails to transform fluids properly, dampness accumulates and congeals into Phlegm. Emotional stress, frustration or a diet rich in greasy, spicy foods then generates Heat. This Heat combines with the Phlegm and rises to disturb the Heart, agitating the mind (Shen) and disrupting the steady rhythm of the heartbeat. The result is a palpitation that feels hot, forceful and often comes with chest tightness and a bitter taste in the mouth.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red body, swollen with a red prickly tip, and a thick yellow greasy coating. Pulse: slippery (滑 huá) and rapid (数 shuò), forceful, with the left Cun position feeling full and overflowing.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Greasy and spicy foods generate Phlegm and Heat, directly fueling the pattern, while alcohol and emotional stress stir up Fire and agitate the Heart. Cooling foods and a calm environment help clear Heat and settle the mind. Gentle movement prevents stagnation without overheating, which is crucial when Phlegm and Fire are already present.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Avoid greasy, fried, and spicy foods, as well as alcohol and coffee, which fan the flames of Phlegm and Heat. Eat more cooling, light foods like celery, cucumber, mung beans and pear. Gentle daily exercise helps move Qi and clear dampness, but avoid overheating. A calm evening routine and managing frustration are essential to prevent Fire from flaring up.

This obstruction creates a stabbing, fixed pain in the chest and a sensation of the heart pounding or fluttering irregularly. - **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Favor warm, cooked meals and include blood-moving spices like turmeric and ginger. Avoid icy drinks and raw foods, which can congeal blood. Gentle daily movement like walking helps keep Qi and Blood flowing.

## คำถามที่พบบ่อย (Frequently Asked Questions)
### Can acupuncture stop heart palpitations?
Acupuncture can often reduce the frequency and intensity of palpitations, but it doesn't work like an emergency stop button during an acute episode. Regular weekly sessions, combined with daily herbs, calm the nervous system and rebalance the underlying pattern. Many patients notice fewer episodes within 3 to 4 sessions, with continued improvement over 6 to 8 weeks.

### How long does it take for Chinese herbs to work for palpitations?
Most people begin to feel a difference within 2 to 3 weeks of taking a well-matched herbal formula. The herbs work gradually to nourish the Heart or clear the disturbance, rather than suppressing the sensation instantly. For deep deficiency patterns, full resolution may take several months, but the palpitations usually become milder and less frequent along the way.

### Can I take TCM herbs with my beta-blockers or other heart medication?
Yes, in most cases TCM can be used alongside conventional medications, but it's essential that both your TCM practitioner and your prescribing doctor know exactly what you're taking. Some herbs, like Dan Shen or Chuan Xiong, can thin the blood and may interact with anticoagulants. Never stop or adjust your medication dosage on your own - if your palpitations improve, work with your doctor to taper safely.

### What foods should I avoid if I have palpitations?
Avoid or limit stimulants like coffee, strong tea, energy drinks, and alcohol, as they can directly agitate the Heart. Spicy, greasy, and fried foods create heat and phlegm, which can worsen palpitations in excess patterns. Cold, raw foods and icy drinks weaken the Spleen's ability to produce Blood, potentially aggravating deficiency-type palpitations. Focus on warm, cooked meals and calming foods like longan fruit, lily bulb, and jujube seeds.

### Are heart palpitations dangerous in TCM?
TCM views palpitations as a signal that the Heart's balance is disturbed, but not necessarily a sign of structural damage. However, any palpitation accompanied by chest pain, fainting, or severe shortness of breath requires immediate medical evaluation. Once serious causes are ruled out, TCM can safely address the root pattern and help restore a calm, steady heartbeat.

### Does TCM treat anxiety-related palpitations?
Yes, and this is one of TCM's strengths. Anxiety often stems from Heart Yin Deficiency, Heart Qi Deficiency, or Phlegm-Fire harassing the Heart - all patterns that directly cause palpitations. By calming the Shen and rebalancing the organs, treatment addresses both the emotional trigger and the physical sensation. Many patients report feeling calmer overall as their palpitations fade.
