## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนปัจจุบัน (Conventional Medicine View)
### คำอธิบาย (Description)
Peritoneal tuberculosis is a form of extrapulmonary tuberculosis caused by the bacterium Mycobacterium tuberculosis. It occurs when the infection spreads to the peritoneum, the thin tissue lining the inner abdominal wall and covering the abdominal organs.
Common symptoms include abdominal pain and distension, ascites (fluid buildup), fever, night sweats, weight loss, and fatigue. Diagnosis is often made through imaging studies, analysis of ascitic fluid, and sometimes laparoscopy with biopsy.

### การรักษาหลัก (Treatments)
Standard treatment involves a prolonged course of multiple anti-tuberculosis antibiotics-typically isoniazid, rifampicin, pyrazinamide, and ethambutol-taken for at least six months. In some cases, corticosteroids may be added to reduce inflammation and prevent complications like intestinal obstruction. Adherence to the full course is critical to prevent drug resistance.

## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนจีน (Traditional Chinese Medicine View)
### ความเข้าใจเกี่ยวกับการแพทย์แผนจีน (TCM Understanding)
In TCM, peritoneal tuberculosis is classified as a "consumptive" disease (lao) that often leads to the formation of abdominal masses (jia). The illness is seen as a deep-seated infection that disrupts the flow of Qi and Blood, creating stagnation and eventually depleting the body's vital substances. Because the peritoneum is governed by the Spleen and Stomach networks, these organs are the first to suffer.
When the Spleen's ability to transform and transport fluids is impaired, dampness accumulates. Over time, this dampness can combine with heat from the chronic infection, giving rise to a pattern of Damp-Heat in the Stomach and Spleen. This explains the bloating, heavy sensation, and low-grade fever many patients experience.
As the disease drags on, it begins to consume the body's Yin, the cooling and nourishing foundation. Without enough Yin, empty heat flares up, causing the classic afternoon fevers, night sweats, and a red, dry tongue. Eventually, both Qi and Blood become deficient, leaving the person profoundly weak, pale, and unable to recover.
Because the same infection can present so differently-one person may have a fixed, stabbing mass while another is simply exhausted-TCM differentiates several distinct patterns. The treatment for a hard, painful lump (Qi and Blood Stagnation) is entirely different from the treatment for a low-grade fever with night sweats (Yin Deficiency), even though both are caused by peritoneal tuberculosis.

### การวินิจฉัยโดยแพทย์แผนจีน (Practitioner Diagnosis)
A TCM practitioner first listens to your story and gently palpates the abdomen. The hallmark of peritoneal tuberculosis is the formation of masses, which in TCM is understood as Qi and Blood Stagnation. The practitioner will ask whether the lump feels fixed or movable, whether the pain is sharp and stabbing, and whether it gets worse with pressure. A dark purple tongue with stasis spots and a wiry or choppy pulse confirm that stagnation is the core pattern.
If the infection is active, you may feel more than just a mass. A heavy, bloated sensation, a low-grade fever, and a greasy yellow tongue coating point toward Damp-Heat in the Stomach and Spleen. The practitioner will ask about nausea, loose stools, or a feeling of mugginess. Here the pulse is often slippery and rapid, and the abdominal tenderness is diffuse rather than a single fixed lump, which helps distinguish it from pure stagnation.
When the illness has dragged on, your body may show signs of consumption. Afternoon fevers, night sweats, a malar flush, and a dry mouth are classic clues of Empty-Heat from Yin Deficiency. The tongue becomes red with little or no coating, and the pulse feels thin and rapid. This pattern often appears alongside others, so the practitioner asks how long you have had these symptoms and whether they worsen late in the day.
In the late stages, the body’s resources become deeply depleted. Severe fatigue, unintentional weight loss, a sallow complexion, and breathlessness suggest Qi and Blood Deficiency. The tongue is pale and the pulse weak and thready. Even if a mass remains, the overriding picture is one of emptiness rather than excess, so the practitioner will gently prioritize building you up before aggressively moving stagnation.

## กลุ่มอาการการแพทย์แผนจีนและการรักษา (TCM Patterns and Treatment)

### Qi And Blood Stagnation
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/qi-and-blood-stagnation)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Fixed, stabbing abdominal pain, Hard, immovable masses in the abdomen, Dark purplish complexion or lips, Irritability and emotional tension, Purple tongue with stasis spots, Wiry and choppy pulse
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Emotional stress, Cold foods and drinks, Sedentary lifestyle, Overwork
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Gentle exercise like walking, Warm compress on abdomen, Stress reduction, Light, warm meals
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Peritoneal tuberculosis creates a chronic inflammatory process that, in TCM terms, reflects Qi stagnation and Blood stasis congealing in the abdomen. Emotional tension, dietary irregularities, or prolonged illness cause the Liver Qi to stagnate, which over time fails to move Blood. This leads to the formation of fixed, palpable masses (瘕 jiǎ) and a characteristic stabbing pain that is worse with pressure.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: dark purple or dusky body with stasis spots, distended sublingual veins. Pulse: wiry (弦 xián) and choppy (涩 sè), like a taut guitar string that scrapes unevenly.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Emotional stress and anger cause Liver Qi to stagnate, which directly worsens Blood stasis and hardens the masses. Cold congeals Blood, making pain sharper and masses more stubborn. Gentle movement and warmth promote Qi and Blood circulation, helping to soften masses and relieve pain. A light, warm diet supports Spleen Qi without creating Dampness or stagnation, preventing further congealing of Blood.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat warm, cooked foods and avoid cold, raw, or greasy items that can congeal Blood. Gentle movement like walking or tai chi helps keep Qi flowing. Manage stress with breathing exercises or meditation to prevent Liver Qi stagnation.

### Damp-Heat in Stomach and Spleen
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/damp-heat-in-stomach-and-spleen)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Bloating and fullness in the upper abdomen, Bitter or sticky taste in the mouth, Yellow greasy tongue coating, Sticky, incomplete bowel movements, Heavy limbs and body, Low-grade fever that persists despite sweating
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Greasy, spicy, or fried foods, Damp, humid weather, Overeating, Alcohol
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Light, bland meals, Cool, dry environment, Adequate rest and sleep, Avoiding heavy, greasy foods
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  In peritoneal tuberculosis, the chronic infection generates a pathological combination of Dampness and Heat that settles in the Spleen and Stomach. This disrupts the middle burner's ability to transform and transport fluids and food, leading to abdominal bloating, a sticky taste, and loose, incomplete bowel movements. The Damp-Heat also causes low-grade fever and a heavy sensation, and over time contributes to the formation of abdominal masses (痨瘕) as turbid fluids congeal.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red body, possibly swollen with teeth marks, with a thick, yellow, greasy coating that is most pronounced at the centre and root. Pulse: soggy (Ru) and rapid (Shu), often also slippery (Hua).
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Greasy and spicy foods directly create Dampness and Heat, which are the pathological substances behind this pattern, so avoiding them prevents aggravation. Light, bland foods and rest allow the Spleen to recover its function of transforming and transporting, gradually clearing the Dampness. A cool, dry environment helps dissipate the low-grade Heat and reduces the heavy sensation.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Focus on light, easily digestible foods like congee, steamed vegetables, and small amounts of lean protein. Strictly avoid greasy, fried, spicy, and sugary foods, as well as alcohol, which all generate Dampness and Heat. Keep the environment cool and dry, and avoid overexertion.

### Empty-Heat caused by Yin Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/empty-heat-caused-by-yin-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Low-grade afternoon or evening fever, Night sweats, Heat sensation in palms, soles, and chest, Dry mouth and throat, worse in the evening, Flushed cheekbones (malar flush), Restlessness and insomnia, Weight loss and thin body frame
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Late nights and overwork, Greasy, spicy, or fried foods, Emotional stress
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Cool, moist foods, Adequate rest and sleep, Gentle exercise like walking
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Peritoneal tuberculosis is a consumptive disease that slowly drains the body’s Yin, the cooling and nourishing foundation. As Yin becomes deficient, the relative Yang heat is no longer held in check, creating an empty heat that rises and disturbs the whole system. This internal heat manifests as low-grade afternoon fever, night sweats, and a flushed face, while the abdomen may feel warm and uncomfortable even without a palpable mass.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red, thin, dry body with cracks, little or no coating. Pulse: fine (thin) and rapid.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Yin is the body’s coolant and structural reserve; late nights, overwork, and emotional strain burn through Yin, allowing empty heat to flare. Spicy and fried foods add external heat on top of the deficiency fire. Rest, early sleep, and cool, moist foods replenish Yin and gently contain the rising heat, which is why they bring relief.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat moistening, cooling foods such as pear, honey, tofu, and congee. Avoid spicy, fried, and drying foods. Prioritise rest and go to bed early, as staying up late directly consumes Yin. Gentle breathing exercises or restorative yoga can help settle the mind and preserve Yin.

### Qi and Blood Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/qi-and-blood-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Severe, persistent fatigue and weakness, Pale or sallow yellowish complexion, Dizziness and heart palpitations, Poor appetite and weight loss, Abdominal masses that feel soft or indistinct, without sharp pain
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Overwork, Cold foods and drinks, Emotional stress, Exposure to cold environments
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Adequate rest and sleep, Warm, nourishing soups and congees, Gentle exercise like walking, Warm compress on abdomen
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Peritoneal tuberculosis is a chronic, consuming illness. Over time, the persistent infection drains the body's Qi and Blood, leaving the Spleen too weak to transform food into the energy and nourishment needed for recovery.
As Qi and Blood become depleted, the abdomen loses the strength to dissolve the tubercular masses, and any slight bleeding further deepens the Blood deficiency. The result is a downward spiral of wasting, pallor, and exhaustion.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale body, may be thin or have teeth marks on the edges, thin white coating. Pulse: fine, weak, and forceless.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Qi and Blood are the body's fundamental energy and nourishment; overwork, stress, and cold foods deplete them further, while rest and warm, easily digested meals allow the Spleen to slowly rebuild them. Gentle movement encourages Qi flow without exhausting it, and warmth helps the weakened digestive fire transform food into usable strength.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat warm, easily digestible foods like rice congee with red dates, bone broths, and gently cooked vegetables. Avoid cold, raw, and greasy foods that burden the weakened Spleen. Gentle daily walks help Qi flow, but rest as soon as you feel tired.

## คำถามที่พบบ่อย (Frequently Asked Questions)
### Can TCM replace my anti-tuberculosis medication?
No. Anti-tuberculosis drugs are essential to kill the bacteria and must be taken exactly as prescribed. TCM is used alongside them to strengthen your body, ease side effects, and help resolve masses and digestive problems.

### How long until I feel better?
Many people notice improved energy and digestion within a few weeks. Relief from abdominal pain and bloating often takes 4-6 weeks, while softening of hard masses may require several months. The timeline depends on your pattern and overall strength.

### Is acupuncture safe while I'm on TB drugs?
Yes, acupuncture is generally safe and can be very helpful for managing pain, nausea, and fatigue. Always inform your acupuncturist about all medications you are taking. Your practitioner will avoid points that might interfere with your treatment.

### Will the abdominal mass go away with TCM?
TCM can help soften and gradually reduce masses, especially when combined with conventional treatment. Results vary; some masses resolve completely, while others shrink significantly. Patience is key, as this process often takes months.

### Do I need to change my diet?
Yes, dietary adjustments support healing. Focus on warm, cooked, easily digestible foods like congee and soups. Avoid raw, cold, greasy, or spicy foods that burden the Spleen. Specific guidance depends on your pattern, but alcohol should be strictly avoided.

### What if I have drug-resistant TB?
TCM can still play a supportive role by boosting your immune system and helping your body cope with stronger second-line drugs. However, you must remain under close medical supervision, as drug-resistant TB requires specialized antibiotic regimens.

## การวิจัยทางวิทยาศาสตร์สมัยใหม่ (Modern Scientific Research)
1. **Adjunctive Chinese herbal medicine therapy improves survival in patients with tuberculosis: A nationwide population-based cohort study**
   *วารสารวิชาการ: Lin YJ, et al. Journal of Ethnopharmacology. 2015; 165: 103-110.*
   This large retrospective cohort study from Taiwan analyzed over 23,000 TB patients and found that those who used Chinese herbal medicine as an adjunct had a significantly lower mortality rate. The most commonly prescribed formulas included Bu Zhong Yi Qi Tang and Liu Jun Zi Tang, which target Qi deficiency and Dampness patterns commonly seen in chronic TB.

2. **Clinical observation on treatment of tuberculous peritonitis with integrated traditional Chinese and Western medicine**
   *วารสารวิชาการ: Zhang XH, et al. Chinese Journal of Integrated Traditional and Western Medicine. 2008; 28(6): 534-536.*
   This study reported on 30 cases of tuberculous peritonitis treated with a combination of anti-TB drugs and Chinese herbal decoctions based on pattern differentiation. The addition of TCM significantly reduced the time to ascites resolution and abdominal pain relief compared to a control group receiving only conventional therapy. Key herbs included Yi Yi Ren, Bai Jiang Cao, and Tao Ren.
