## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนปัจจุบัน (Conventional Medicine View)
### คำอธิบาย (Description)
In conventional medicine, abnormal behavior is typically assessed as a symptom of an underlying psychiatric condition, such as schizophrenia, bipolar disorder, or severe depression. Diagnosis is based on clinical criteria like the DSM-5, focusing on clusters of symptoms including delusions, hallucinations, disorganized speech, and grossly disorganized or catatonic behavior. The causes are understood to involve a combination of genetic predisposition, neurotransmitter imbalances (especially dopamine and glutamate), and environmental stressors.

### การรักษาหลัก (Treatments)
Standard treatment usually involves antipsychotic medications (such as risperidone, olanzapine, or haloperidol), mood stabilizers (like lithium or valproate), and sometimes antidepressants. Psychotherapy, including cognitive behavioral therapy, and hospitalization during acute episodes are also common. The goal is to manage symptoms, prevent relapse, and support daily functioning.

## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนจีน (Traditional Chinese Medicine View)
### ความเข้าใจเกี่ยวกับการแพทย์แผนจีน (TCM Understanding)
TCM understands abnormal behavior primarily as a disturbance of the Heart and its housing of the mind (Shen). The Heart is the residence of consciousness, and when it is agitated or clouded, thinking, emotion, and behavior become disordered. This disturbance rarely begins in the Heart itself - more often, it arises from other organs that generate pathogenic factors like Phlegm, Fire, or Stagnation, which then travel upward to disturb the Shen.
The Liver plays a central role. When emotions like anger, frustration, or resentment are held in, Liver Qi stagnates. Over time, this stagnation can generate Heat, which rises to agitate the Heart and cause manic, explosive behavior.
Alternatively, if the Spleen is weakened by poor diet or worry, it fails to transform fluids, and a thick, sticky Phlegm forms. This Phlegm can ascend to mist the Heart orifices, leading to the withdrawn, foolish behavior of depressive psychosis - muttering, staring at walls, social withdrawal.
When Phlegm combines with Fire, the result is the most intense manic picture: reckless, violent outbursts, incessant shouting, and a sense of inner heat.
In prolonged cases, chronic emotional distress or trauma can cause Blood to stagnate in the Heart vessels, unmoored the mind and causing fixed, irrational delusions. And in some, long-term worry and overwork drain the Spleen and Heart of Qi and Blood, leaving the Shen unanchored and the person confused, pale, and exhausted. Each of these patterns requires a fundamentally different treatment strategy.

### การวินิจฉัยโดยแพทย์แผนจีน (Practitioner Diagnosis)
A TCM practitioner first observes whether the person is withdrawn and quiet, or agitated and loud. This is the classic divide between depressive psychosis (癫, diān) and manic psychosis (狂, kuáng). Quiet muttering, staring at walls, and social withdrawal point toward patterns that cloud the mind with Phlegm, while shouting, violent behavior, and excessive talking suggest Fire is agitating the Heart.
If the person is restless, reckless, and shouting, Phlegm-Fire harassing the Heart is likely. The tongue will be red with a thick yellow greasy coat, and the pulse rapid and slippery. These signs confirm that Heat and Phlegm are rising to disturb the mind, creating the classic manic picture.
When the person appears dull, foolish, and mutters to themselves, Phlegm Misting the Heart is the prime suspect. Here the tongue shows a white greasy coat, and the pulse is often wiry and slippery. The Phlegm is cold and turbid, blocking the Heart orifices without the Heat that drives manic excitement.
Emotional frustration often sits behind both extremes. Liver Qi Stagnation that transforms into Heat can generate the inner pressure that feeds either depressive brooding or manic outbursts. The tongue is red with a thin yellow coat, and the pulse wiry and rapid. This pattern is especially considered when the behavior clearly worsens after emotional stress.
In chronic or traumatic cases, Heart Blood Stagnation may be at play. The person’s behavior is fixed and irrational, and the tongue appears dark purple with stasis spots. The pulse feels choppy. This pattern is less common, but when present it signals that the mind is literally obstructed by stuck Blood.
After a long illness, especially in depressive psychosis, Heart and Spleen Qi and Blood Deficiency can emerge. The person is timid, confused, and exhausted, with a pale tongue and a deep, thready pulse. The mind is undernourished rather than agitated, and the behavior is more a fading away than a flare-up.

## กลุ่มอาการการแพทย์แผนจีนและการรักษา (TCM Patterns and Treatment)

### Phlegm-Fire harassing the Heart
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/phlegm-fire-harassing-the-heart)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Manic, reckless behavior with violent outbursts, Incessant talking or shouting, Flushed red face and red eyes, Chest oppression with thick yellow sticky phlegm, Bitter taste in the mouth and thirst
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Spicy, greasy food, Alcohol, Emotional stress, Overwork, Hot weather
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Cool, light foods, Calm, quiet environment, Gentle exercise, Adequate sleep
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When emotional stress, poor diet, or internal imbalance generates thick Phlegm and Heat, they can combine into Phlegm-Fire that rushes upward to disturb the Heart. The Heart houses the mind (Shen), so this agitation leads to manic, reckless behavior, incessant talking, and violent outbursts. The Fire also rises to the head, causing a flushed red face, red eyes, and a bitter taste, while the Phlegm creates a sense of chest oppression and a rattling throat.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red body, swollen with a red prickly tip, and a thick, yellow, greasy coating. Pulse: slippery and rapid, full and overflowing at the left Cun (Heart) position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Spicy, greasy foods and alcohol directly fuel Phlegm and Fire, while emotional stress stirs Liver Qi into Fire, which then agitates the Heart. Overwork and hot environments add Heat to the body, worsening the manic state. Cooling foods, a calm setting, and rest help clear Heat, settle Phlegm, and allow the Heart-Shen to regain stability.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Avoid spicy, greasy, and fried foods that generate Phlegm and Heat, as well as alcohol and coffee. Eat cooling, light foods like cucumber, pear, and mung beans. Gentle daily movement and a calm, quiet environment help settle the mind. Keep a regular sleep schedule and avoid overstimulation from screens or loud noise.

### Phlegm Misting the Heart
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/phlegm-misting-the-heart)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Staring blankly at walls, Muttering or talking to oneself, Emotional flatness, depression, withdrawal, Rattling or gurgling sound in the throat, Thick white greasy tongue coating, Chest stuffiness and nausea
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Damp, greasy, or cold foods, Sedentary lifestyle, Overthinking and rumination, Damp, rainy weather
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warm, dry, easily digested foods, Gentle daily movement, Structured, simple mental tasks, Clear, uncluttered environment
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  In this pattern, the Spleen fails to transform fluids, and a thick, sticky phlegm accumulates. This phlegm rises to the Heart, physically clouding its orifices like a fog settling over the mind. The result is confusion, withdrawal, and the strange, foolish behaviors typical of depressive psychosis, such as staring at walls or muttering to oneself.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: Pale swollen body with a thick white greasy coating, often stiff or sluggish. Pulse: Slippery (滑 Huá), especially in the middle position, like beads rolling on a string.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Phlegm forms when the Spleen fails to transform fluids, often due to dietary dampness and inactivity. Warm, dry foods and gentle movement support the Spleen, helping it clear phlegm, while damp, cold, and sedentary habits worsen the accumulation. Mental engagement helps disperse the phlegm misting the mind, much like sunlight burning off a fog.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Avoid damp-producing foods such as dairy, sugar, and greasy or fried meals. Eat warm, light, easily digested foods like congee, steamed vegetables, and small amounts of warming spices. Gentle daily movement like walking or tai chi helps the Spleen transform dampness. Keep the mind engaged with simple, structured activities to prevent mental stagnation and lift the fog.

### Liver Qi Stagnation that transforms into Heat
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/liver-qi-stagnation-that-transforms-into-heat)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Explosive anger or sudden outbursts, Bitter taste in the mouth, Throbbing headache at the temples, Distending pain in the ribcage, Red face and eyes
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Emotional stress and frustration, Spicy, greasy, or fried foods, Alcohol and coffee, Hot weather, Sedentary lifestyle
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Gentle exercise, Cooling foods and herbal teas, Relaxation and deep breathing, Cool, quiet environment
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When feelings of frustration and anger are held in, the Liver's Qi gets stuck. Over time, this stuck energy produces heat, which rises to disturb the mind. The result is a mix of agitation, explosive outbursts, and erratic behavior that can flip between depressive withdrawal and manic irritability.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red body with redder sides, yellow coating. Pulse: wiry and rapid (弦数 xián shuò).
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Emotional stress and frustration directly cause Liver Qi to stagnate, which then generates heat. This heat rises to disturb the mind, leading to irritability and explosive behavior. Cooling foods and a calm environment help clear that heat, while gentle exercise and relaxation techniques move the Qi and soothe the Liver, addressing the root of the problem.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat plenty of cooling, green vegetables like celery and cucumber, and drink chrysanthemum or peppermint tea. Avoid spicy, greasy, or fried foods, alcohol, and coffee, which add heat. Gentle daily exercise such as walking or yoga helps move stuck Qi. Make time for relaxation and stress management, as emotional tension is the main trigger.

### Heart Blood Stagnation
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/heart-blood-stagnation)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Fixed, irrational behavior or delusions, Stabbing chest pain in a fixed location, Purple or dark lips and nails, Palpitations with anxiety, Insomnia with disturbing dreams
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Emotional stress and trauma, Cold weather or cold foods, Sedentary lifestyle, Greasy, heavy meals, Overwork
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warmth and gentle movement, Eating hawthorn or turmeric, Calm, quiet environment, Deep breathing exercises
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  The Heart houses the Mind (Shen). When chronic emotional distress or trauma causes Blood to stagnate in the Heart vessels, the Mind becomes unmoored. This leads to fixed, irrational behavior or delusions that are stubborn and difficult to reason away. Unlike the manic agitation of Phlegm-Fire, the behavior here is more rigid and may be accompanied by stabbing chest pain, palpitations, and a sense of oppression.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: dark purple body with stasis spots, distended sublingual veins, thin white coat. Pulse: choppy (涩 Se) or knotted (结 Jie), especially at the left Cun position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Blood stasis arises from Qi stagnation and congealing of Blood. Emotional stress stagnates Qi, while cold and inactivity cause Blood to congeal further. Warmth, gentle movement, and blood-moving foods promote circulation, while a calm environment helps prevent Qi stagnation from emotional upset.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat warming, blood-moving foods like turmeric, ginger, garlic, and hawthorn berry. Avoid cold, raw, and greasy foods that can congeal Blood. Gentle daily exercise such as walking or tai chi helps keep Qi and Blood moving. Manage emotional stress with meditation or counseling to prevent further stagnation.

### Heart and Spleen Qi and Blood Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/heart-and-spleen-qi-and-blood-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Extreme fatigue and lack of energy, Pale, sallow complexion with pale lips, Poor appetite and bloating after meals, Timidity, social withdrawal, and muttering, Palpitations and insomnia with excessive dreaming, Forgetfulness and mental confusion
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Overwork and exhaustion, Worry and overthinking, Cold raw foods, Irregular eating habits, Stressful social situations
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Rest and adequate sleep, Warm, cooked meals like congee, Gentle walks and tai chi, Calm, supportive environment
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  In prolonged depressive psychosis, chronic worry, overwork, or poor digestion drain the Spleen's ability to produce Qi and Blood. The Heart, which houses the mind (Shen), then lacks the nourishment it needs to keep thinking clear and emotions stable. Without enough Blood to anchor the Shen, the mind becomes confused, timid, and withdrawn, leading to muttering, staring, and other bizarre but passive behaviors. The deep fatigue, pale face, and poor appetite are signs that the body's entire engine of energy and nourishment is failing.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale, puffy, and tender with teeth marks and a thin white coating. Pulse: fine and weak, especially empty at the left Cun (Heart) and soft at the right Guan (Spleen) positions.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Overwork and worry directly consume the Spleen's Qi and the Heart's Blood, deepening the deficiency that starves the mind. Rest and nourishing warm foods rebuild that lost Qi and Blood, giving the Heart-Shen a stable anchor. Cold and raw foods injure the Spleen's digestive fire, so warm cooked meals support its function. A calm environment reduces mental strain, allowing the Shen to settle and clarity to return.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat warm, cooked, easily digestible foods such as congee, soups, and stews. Avoid raw, cold, and greasy foods that burden the Spleen. Gentle daily movement like walking or tai chi helps Qi circulate without draining it. Prioritize rest and avoid overthinking or prolonged mental strain.

## คำถามที่พบบ่อย (Frequently Asked Questions)
### Can acupuncture really help with abnormal or psychotic behavior?
Yes, acupuncture can help rebalance the flow of Qi and calm the mind. Points like Baihui (DU-20) and Shenmen (HT-7) are used to settle the Shen, while points such as Fenglong (ST-40) clear Phlegm and Taichong (LR-3) smooth Liver Qi. It is often used alongside herbal medicine and conventional care. However, it is not a substitute for emergency psychiatric intervention when someone is a danger to themselves or others.

### Can I take Chinese herbs with my psychiatric medication?
In many cases, yes, but it is essential to inform both your TCM practitioner and your prescribing doctor. Some herbs may interact with medications, particularly sedatives or those metabolized by the liver. Herbs like Chai Hu (Bupleurum) can influence drug metabolism. Never stop or adjust psychiatric medication without medical supervision. A qualified TCM practitioner can design a formula that supports your treatment while minimizing risks.

### How soon will I see changes in behavior?
Many people notice a subtle calming effect within the first few days of herbal treatment or acupuncture. For acute agitation, relief can come quickly. For long-standing patterns, especially those involving deficiency or deep Phlegm, it may take several weeks to see meaningful shifts. The goal is not just symptom relief but correcting the underlying imbalance, which requires patience and consistency.

### What if the person is hearing voices or having delusions?
In TCM, auditory hallucinations and fixed delusions are often seen as Phlegm misting the Heart orifices or Heart Blood Stagnation. Herbal formulas and acupuncture can address these root causes. However, if the voices command harm or cause extreme distress, seek urgent psychiatric care. TCM can then be used as a complementary therapy to help stabilize and reduce the intensity of such experiences over time.

### Is TCM safe for someone who becomes violent?
Safety is the first priority. If someone is actively violent or threatening harm, they need immediate medical or police intervention. TCM can play a role in preventing and managing the underlying patterns of Phlegm-Fire that drive such outbursts, but it is not a crisis-response tool. Once the person is stabilized, acupuncture and herbs can help reduce the frequency and intensity of manic episodes.

### Will I need to follow a special diet?
Diet is an important part of TCM treatment. Generally, it's best to avoid greasy, spicy, and heavily processed foods, as well as alcohol, because these generate Phlegm and Heat. Light, easily digested foods like cooked vegetables, congee, and moderate amounts of cooling fruits can help. A TCM practitioner will give you specific guidance based on your pattern - for example, someone with Phlegm Misting will need to avoid damp-producing foods like dairy and cold drinks.

## การวิจัยทางวิทยาศาสตร์สมัยใหม่ (Modern Scientific Research)
1. **Acupuncture for schizophrenia** ([ลิงก์](https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD005475.pub2))
   *วารสารวิชาการ: Shen X, Xia J, Adams CE, et al. Acupuncture for schizophrenia. Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews 2014, Issue 10. Art. No.: CD005475.*
   This Cochrane review assessed 30 RCTs and found that acupuncture as an adjunct to antipsychotics may improve global state and reduce positive symptoms, but evidence was low quality.

2. **Chinese herbal medicine for schizophrenia** ([ลิงก์](https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD003444.pub2))
   *วารสารวิชาการ: Rathbone J, Zhang L, Zhang M, et al. Chinese herbal medicine for schizophrenia. Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews 2005, Issue 4. Art. No.: CD003444.*
   This review evaluated seven RCTs of Chinese herbal medicine for schizophrenia. Some herbs showed benefit over placebo, but the studies were small and of poor quality, making conclusions uncertain.

3. **Acupuncture for bipolar disorder: a systematic review** ([ลิงก์](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jad.2010.06.032))
   *วารสารวิชาการ: Sarris J, Kavanagh DJ, Byrne G, et al. Acupuncture for bipolar disorder: a systematic review. Journal of Affective Disorders 2011; 132(1-2): 1-8.*
   This systematic review found limited evidence from case reports and small trials suggesting acupuncture may help stabilize mood in bipolar disorder, but no definitive RCTs exist.
