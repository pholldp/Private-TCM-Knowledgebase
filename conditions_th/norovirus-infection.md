## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนปัจจุบัน (Conventional Medicine View)
### คำอธิบาย (Description)
Norovirus is a highly contagious virus that causes acute gastroenteritis - inflammation of the stomach and intestines. The hallmark symptoms are sudden-onset vomiting, watery diarrhea, nausea, and stomach cramps.
Most people recover within one to three days without specific medical treatment, but the illness can be severe for young children, older adults, and those with weakened immune systems. Diagnosis is usually based on symptoms alone, though lab tests can confirm the virus during outbreaks.
The virus spreads easily through contaminated food, water, surfaces, or close contact with an infected person. Because there is no antiviral medication for norovirus, conventional care focuses on rest and replacing lost fluids to prevent dehydration.

### การรักษาหลัก (Treatments)
Standard treatment for norovirus is supportive. The priority is oral rehydration with water, electrolyte solutions, or clear broths to replace fluids and salts lost through vomiting and diarrhea. Over-the-counter antiemetics or antidiarrheals may be used cautiously in adults, but they are generally not recommended for children. In severe cases where a person cannot keep liquids down, intravenous fluids may be needed. There is currently no vaccine for norovirus.

## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนจีน (Traditional Chinese Medicine View)
### ความเข้าใจเกี่ยวกับการแพทย์แผนจีน (TCM Understanding)
In TCM, acute vomiting and diarrhea are seen as a sudden invasion of external pathogenic factors - most often Damp-Heat or Cold-Damp - that disrupt the Stomach and Spleen. The Stomach is responsible for receiving food and sending it downward; when it is attacked, its Qi rebels upward, causing vomiting. The Spleen transforms and transports fluids; when it is overwhelmed by dampness, it cannot separate the clear from the turbid, and watery diarrhea results.
This is why the same virus can produce different symptoms in different people: the nature of the pathogen and the strength of your digestive system determine whether the pattern will be hot, cold, or toxic.
Norovirus is particularly aggressive because it creates both dampness and turbidity - a kind of murky, pathogenic fog - that blocks the middle burner (the digestive organs). In severe cases, the rapid loss of fluids can drain the body's Yin (cooling, moistening substances) or even exhaust its Yang (warming, activating energy), leading to dangerous states of collapse.
This is why TCM treats not only the acute infection but also the recovery phase, carefully rebuilding Qi and Yin so that the digestive system regains its strength.
Because TCM recognizes multiple patterns within one illness, treatment is tailored. A person with burning diarrhea, thirst, and a yellow tongue coating needs herbs that clear heat and dry dampness. Someone with chills, clear watery stools, and a white tongue coating needs warming, drying herbs that dispel cold-dampness. This pattern-based approach is what allows TCM to address both the acute symptoms and the underlying vulnerability that allowed the pathogen to take hold.

### การวินิจฉัยโดยแพทย์แผนจีน (Practitioner Diagnosis)
A TCM practitioner begins by asking about the very first symptoms, because the quality of the vomiting and diarrhea is the strongest clue. Is the vomit sour-smelling and the stool yellow and foul, or is it clear and watery with little odor? These details separate the two most common acute patterns: Damp-Heat in the Stomach (湿热蕴胃, shī rè yùn wèi) and Cold-Damp invading the Spleen (寒湿困脾, hán shī kùn pí).
If the person has a fever, thirst for cold drinks, and passes yellow, foul-smelling watery stools, the picture points to Damp-Heat in the Stomach. The tongue is red with a yellow greasy coating, and the pulse feels rapid and slippery.
In contrast, when the main complaint is chills, an absence of thirst or a desire for warm drinks, and clear, non-foul watery diarrhea, Cold-Damp invading the Spleen is likely. Here the tongue is pale with a white greasy coating, and the pulse is slow and soft.
When the illness strikes with sudden high fever, severe cramping abdominal pain, and explosive vomiting and diarrhea, the practitioner considers Toxic-Heat (疫毒炽盛, yì dú chì shèng). This pattern reflects a deeper invasion of epidemic heat toxins. The tongue appears deep red with a dry yellow coating, and the pulse is rapid and forceful. The person may look pale or grey, and the whole body feels profoundly unwell.
After the acute vomiting and diarrhea subside, lingering fatigue, a dry mouth, and a thin rapid pulse suggest Qi and Yin Deficiency from the massive fluid and energy loss.
In severe cases where dehydration becomes critical, the body may tip into Collapse of Yin with sunken eyes, a very rapid thready pulse, and dry, hot skin, or into Collapse of Yang with cold limbs, a faint pulse, and a state of shock. These are medical emergencies.

### Damp-Heat in the Stomach
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/damp-heat-in-the-stomach)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Nausea and vomiting with sour or foul smell, Watery yellow, foul-smelling diarrhea, Heavy sensation in limbs and body, Thirst with sticky or greasy taste in the mouth, Fever and a sense of heat
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Spicy, fried, or greasy foods, Alcohol, Overeating
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Resting the stomach by fasting briefly, Eating plain congee or light foods, Cool environment or well-ventilated room, Hydration with electrolyte solutions
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Norovirus is an acute Damp-Heat pathogen. It invades the Stomach and Intestines, where Heat churns the fluids into turbid Dampness and Dampness obstructs the normal downward flow of Stomach Qi. The result is sudden, forceful vomiting of sour, foul-smelling contents and watery yellow diarrhea - the body's way of ejecting the pathogen.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red body with a thick, yellow, greasy coating. Pulse: slippery (滑, huá) and rapid (数, shuò).
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Damp-Heat in the Stomach is fueled by foods that are themselves hot and damp - greasy, fried, spicy, and alcoholic items directly worsen the pathogen. Resting the stomach and eating bland, easily digestible foods allows the digestive system to clear the Damp-Heat without overburdening. Hydration replenishes the fluids lost to vomiting and diarrhea, preventing Yin injury, while a cool environment helps dissipate the Heat.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Avoid all greasy, fried, spicy, and dairy foods - they add more Dampness and Heat. Stick to plain congee, steamed vegetables, and clear broths. Sip water or electrolyte drinks frequently to replace fluids lost through vomiting and diarrhea. Rest completely until the acute symptoms pass.

### Cold-Damp invading the Spleen
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/cold-damp-invading-the-spleen)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Clear watery diarrhea without foul odor, Chills and aversion to cold, no fever, Heavy sensation in limbs and body, Thick white greasy tongue coating, Dull abdominal pain that improves with warmth
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Cold or raw foods and drinks, Dairy or greasy foods, Cold, damp environment or drafts, Overeating
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warmth on the abdomen, Drinking warm ginger tea, Eating plain congee or light foods, Resting, Dry, warm environment
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When cold and dampness invade the body-often through contaminated food or exposure to a cold, damp environment-they obstruct the Spleen's ability to transform and transport fluids. The Spleen Qi becomes impaired, causing fluids to accumulate and overflow downward as watery diarrhea and upward as clear vomiting. The cold nature of the pathogen explains the absence of fever, the chills, and the dull abdominal pain that feels better with warmth.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale, swollen body with teeth marks and a thick, white, greasy coating that is excessively moist. Pulse: soggy (Ru) or slippery (Hua) and slow (Chi), especially weak and soft at the right Guan position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Cold and dampness are the primary pathogens in this pattern. Cold foods, dairy, and damp environments directly increase cold-damp, further obstructing the Spleen. Warmth, ginger, and light congee dispel cold, transform dampness, and support the Spleen's function, thus relieving symptoms.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat only warm, cooked, and easily digestible foods like rice porridge (congee) with ginger. Avoid all cold, raw, greasy, dairy, and sweet foods that create dampness. Keep the abdomen warm with a hot water bottle or moxibustion. Rest in a dry, warm environment.

### Toxic-Heat
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/toxic-heat)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: High fever (often above 39&#xB0;C), Explosive vomiting and watery diarrhea, Intense thirst with desire for cold drinks, Restlessness and irritability, Red tongue with prickly raised papillae and dry yellow coating
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Spicy, fried, or greasy foods, Overexertion or physical exertion, Emotional stress
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Sipping cool water, Complete rest in a cool room, Eating plain congee or light foods
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  This pattern occurs when a particularly virulent epidemic toxin invades the body, overwhelming the stomach and intestines. The intense heat and toxin churn upward and downward, causing sudden violent vomiting and profuse watery diarrhea. The heat also rages through the whole body, producing high fever, extreme thirst, and a red tongue with prickly thorns.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red body with prickly raised papillae (thorns), dry yellow coating. Pulse: rapid (shu) and forceful (you li), often full or overflowing.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Toxic-Heat is an intense heat pathogen that rapidly consumes body fluids. Cool liquids and rest help preserve fluids and Qi, while bland foods avoid adding more heat or dampness. Spicy and greasy foods, as well as overexertion, generate more internal heat and overwhelm the already struggling digestive system, making the vomiting and diarrhea worse.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Sip small amounts of cool water or electrolyte solutions frequently to replace lost fluids. Eat only when you can keep food down, starting with bland, easy-to-digest foods like rice congee. Avoid all spicy, greasy, or dairy foods until fully recovered, and rest completely to allow your body to fight the toxin.

### Qi and Yin Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/qi-and-yin-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Profound fatigue and weakness, Dry mouth and throat with little desire to drink, Spontaneous sweating or night sweats, Shortness of breath, worse with exertion, Warm sensation in palms, soles, and chest, Weak voice, reluctance to speak
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Overexertion or physical exertion, Spicy, fried, or greasy foods, Dehydration, Emotional stress
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Resting, Warm, moistening fluids, Small, frequent light meals, Gentle deep breathing
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  After the acute vomiting and watery diarrhea of a norovirus infection, the body loses a tremendous amount of fluids and vital energy. This dual loss drains both Qi (your functional energy) and Yin (the cooling, moistening substances), leaving you feeling profoundly tired and dried out. The Spleen and Stomach, which produce Qi and distribute fluids, are temporarily weakened, so recovery feels slow and fragile.

Prioritize rest and gentle movement like stretching; avoid rushing back to work or intense exercise until your energy returns.

### Collapse of Yin
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/collapse-of-yin)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Profuse hot, sticky sweat that beads like oil, Sunken eye sockets and wrinkled, shrunken skin, Intense thirst with desire for cold drinks, Burning hot body but warm extremities, Restlessness and irritability
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Continued vomiting and diarrhea, Hot, dry environment, Spicy, fried, or greasy foods, Overexertion or physical exertion, Emotional stress
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Intravenous fluids, Cool environment or well-ventilated room, Sipping cool water, Rest in a dark, quiet room
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Norovirus causes violent vomiting and watery diarrhea that rapidly drain the body's fluids. When fluid loss becomes extreme, the body's cooling, moistening Yin is critically depleted. Without enough Yin to anchor Yang, Empty Heat rises, producing a burning sensation, intense thirst, and a deep red, dry tongue. This is the emergency stage of Yin collapse, where the body's very substance is evaporating.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: deep red or crimson, completely dry with no coating (mirror tongue), possibly cracked and shrunken. Pulse: fine (thready) and rapid, hollow or scattered on deep pressure.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Yin collapse occurs when massive fluid loss creates a state of severe dehydration and Empty Heat. Cooling measures and fluid replenishment directly restore Yin and calm the unanchored Yang. Heat, exertion, and stress further consume the remaining fluids and aggravate the condition.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: This is a medical emergency - seek immediate hospital care for intravenous fluids. After stabilization, sip small amounts of cool water or Sheng Mai San decoction. Avoid hot, spicy, and drying foods. When recovery begins, eat plain congee with a little pear or white fungus to gently rebuild fluids. Rest completely in a cool, quiet room.

### Collapse of Yang
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/collapse-of-yang)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Ice-cold hands and feet extending past elbows and knees, Profuse cold sweating that does not stop, No thirst, or desire for warm drinks only, Ashen-white or greyish complexion, Extremely faint or absent pulse
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Cold, damp environment or drafts, Cold or raw foods and drinks, Continued vomiting and diarrhea, Overexertion or physical exertion
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warm blankets and heating pads, Drinking warm ginger tea, Resting, Moxibustion on the abdomen
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Norovirus infection triggers violent vomiting and watery diarrhea that rapidly drain the body’s fluids and Yang Qi. When the loss becomes extreme, the life-warming fire (Yang) fades, leaving the interior in a state of deep cold. This internal cold overwhelms the body, causing the limbs to turn ice-cold, a faint pulse, and profuse cold sweating - the hallmark signs of shock as the vital warmth collapses.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale, moist, flaccid body with thin white slippery coating. Pulse: minute (wei) and on the verge of disappearing, extremely faint at all positions.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Yang collapse is a state of extreme internal cold and deficiency where the body's vital fire is nearly extinguished. Warmth from blankets, ginger tea, and moxibustion helps preserve the remaining Yang and gently rekindle the digestive fire. Cold, exertion, and further fluid loss drain what little Yang remains, pushing the body deeper into shock.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: This is a medical emergency - seek hospital care immediately. While waiting, keep the person warm with blankets and offer small sips of warm ginger tea if they are conscious. Avoid all cold foods and drinks, and do not force food. Once stable, congee with a pinch of ginger can be introduced gradually.

## คำถามที่พบบ่อย (Frequently Asked Questions)
### Can TCM cure a stomach virus?
TCM does not kill the virus directly. Instead, it helps your body expel the pathogenic factor and restore normal digestive function. The right herbal formula can shorten the duration of vomiting and diarrhea and reduce post-infection fatigue. Most acute cases resolve within a day or two with treatment.

### What is the best TCM remedy for vomiting and diarrhea?
It depends on the pattern. For sudden vomiting and watery diarrhea with chills and a white tongue coating, Huo Xiang Zheng Qi San is a classic formula. For foul-smelling, burning diarrhea with thirst and a yellow tongue coating, a formula like Ge Geng Huang Lian Huang Qin Tang may be used. A TCM practitioner will choose based on your specific symptoms and tongue appearance.

### Is acupuncture safe when I have stomach flu?
Yes, acupuncture can be very helpful for acute gastroenteritis. Points on the abdomen and legs can calm the Stomach, stop vomiting, and ease cramping. Treatment is gentle and brief. However, if you are severely dehydrated or dizzy, rest and rehydration come first - see the Safety section for red flags.

### Can I take Chinese herbs with oral rehydration salts?
Absolutely. Oral rehydration solutions replace lost fluids and electrolytes, which is critical. Chinese herbs work on a different level - they help your digestive system regain its balance. There is no known interaction, but always tell your TCM practitioner what you are taking and follow rehydration guidelines from your doctor.

### How soon will I feel better with TCM?
Many people notice a reduction in vomiting and diarrhea within hours of taking the first dose of herbs, especially in Damp-Heat or Cold-Damp patterns. Full resolution of acute symptoms often takes one to three days. The lingering tiredness and lack of appetite can take a week or two to fully clear, but herbs can speed that process.

### What should I eat after a stomach bug according to TCM?
Stick to warm, cooked, easily digestible foods. Rice congee, plain steamed rice, sweet potato, and carrot soup are all gentle on the Spleen. Avoid raw, cold, greasy, spicy, and dairy foods until your digestion feels strong again. Ginger tea can help if you still feel chilled; a little mint tea may help if you feel overheated. See the Dietary Guidance section for more details.

### Can TCM prevent norovirus?
TCM cannot prevent infection entirely, but it can strengthen your digestive Qi (Spleen and Stomach) so that your body is less vulnerable to external pathogens. Regular acupuncture, a balanced diet, and avoiding cold, raw foods during flu season are all part of building resilience. If you are frequently getting stomach bugs, a TCM practitioner can help identify and correct the underlying weakness.

### Is TCM safe for children with stomach flu?
Yes, when prescribed by a qualified practitioner. Pediatric dosing of herbs is adjusted for age and weight, and acupuncture can be replaced with acupressure or gentle massage in very young children. Always inform the practitioner of the child's exact age and any medications. For severe dehydration or high fever, seek conventional medical care immediately.
