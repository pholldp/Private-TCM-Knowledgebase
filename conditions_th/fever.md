## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนปัจจุบัน (Conventional Medicine View)
### คำอธิบาย (Description)
Fever is defined as a temporary increase in body temperature, often due to an illness. It's typically a sign that the body is fighting an infection, but can also result from inflammation, autoimmune conditions, or certain medications. A normal body temperature is around 98.6°F (37°C), and fever is generally considered present above 100.4°F (38°C).

### การรักษาหลัก (Treatments)
Conventional treatment focuses on reducing the fever and addressing the underlying cause. Over-the-counter medications like acetaminophen or ibuprofen are commonly used to lower temperature and relieve discomfort. If a bacterial infection is present, antibiotics may be prescribed. Rest and hydration are universally recommended.

## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนจีน (Traditional Chinese Medicine View)
### ความเข้าใจเกี่ยวกับการแพทย์แผนจีน (TCM Understanding)
In TCM, fever is understood as a sign of disharmony between the body's Yin and Yang, or between its defensive Qi and external pathogens. The body's protective layer, called the exterior, is the first line of defense. When a pathogen like Wind-Cold or Wind-Heat invades, a battle ensues at the surface, generating fever and chills. This is the most common acute fever pattern.
When the pathogen penetrates deeper, or when internal imbalances generate excessive heat, the fever becomes interior - high and without chills. This can come from Excess-Heat blazing in the Stomach or Lungs, or from Damp-Heat where humidity and heat combine to create a smoldering, lingering fever. These patterns often involve the Spleen and Stomach's ability to transform fluids.
Not all fevers are from excess. When the body's cooling Yin fluids are depleted, a relative excess of Yang creates a low-grade, afternoon fever with night sweats. This is Empty-Heat from Yin Deficiency, often rooted in the Kidneys. Similarly, when the Spleen's Qi is too weak to lift Yang upward, a deficiency fever can arise that worsens with exertion - the body's energy is too depleted to maintain normal temperature regulation.
This is why two people with the same thermometer reading may need completely different treatments. One person's fever might be a Wind-Heat invasion needing cooling, dispersing herbs, while another's might be a Qi deficiency needing tonics to strengthen the Spleen. TCM's strength is in matching the treatment to the specific pattern, not just the number on the thermometer.

### การวินิจฉัยโดยแพทย์แผนจีน (Practitioner Diagnosis)
When someone comes in with a fever, a TCM practitioner starts by asking what the fever feels like and what other signs accompany it. The presence or absence of chills, the time of day the fever peaks, and the quality of thirst and sweat are among the first clues. The tongue and pulse then confirm which pattern is at play.
If the fever comes on quickly with mild chills, a sore throat, and a floating rapid pulse, the practitioner suspects an exterior Wind-Heat invasion. The tongue tip is often red, and the person may feel hot but still want to avoid drafts. This pattern is acute and superficial, with the battle between pathogen and defensive Qi happening at the body's surface.
When the fever is accompanied by strong chills, body aches, and no sweating, the picture points to Wind-Cold. Here the pulse is floating and tight, and the tongue coat is thin and white. The person feels cold and shivery even though a fever is present, and the absence of sweating tells the practitioner that the exterior is tightly shut.
A high fever without any chills, with intense thirst, a red face, and a rapid full pulse, signals interior Excess-Heat. The tongue is red with a dry yellow coat. This pattern means the heat is blazing deep inside the body, not at the surface, so the person feels hot all over and craves cold drinks. The practitioner looks for signs of which organ is most affected-lung, stomach, or intestines.
If the fever lingers and the person complains of a heavy, muzzy sensation, along with nausea or loose stools, Damp-Heat becomes the prime suspect. The tongue coat is thick, sticky, and yellow; the pulse is soggy and rapid. The fever often rises in the afternoon and does not feel as sharp as pure heat, because the dampness clings and slows everything down.
A low-grade fever that comes in the afternoon or evening, with night sweats, a dry mouth, and a thin rapid pulse, suggests Empty-Heat from Yin Deficiency. The tongue is red with little or no coat. Instead of true excess fire, this is a relative heat born from insufficient cooling Yin fluids, so the fever is mild and tidal, never blazing high.
When the fever is also low-grade but worse after exertion, and the person is exhausted, has a poor appetite, and a weak pulse, the practitioner thinks of Qi Deficiency Fever. The tongue is pale and puffy. Here the Spleen Qi is too weak to lift clear Yang upward, so a smoldering heat appears, especially when the body is strained.

### Wind-Heat
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/wind-heat)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Fever greater than chills, Sore, red throat, Thirst with desire to drink, Cough with yellow phlegm, Headache with a distending quality
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Spicy food, Alcohol, Windy drafts, Overexertion, Emotional stress
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Cool drinks, Rest, Light clothing, Fresh air
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When a Wind-Heat pathogen invades the body's exterior, it clashes with the defensive Qi at the surface. This battle generates heat, causing a fever that is more prominent than the accompanying mild chills. The Heat nature of the pathogen rises to the throat and head, producing a sore, red throat, thirst, and a distending headache.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red tip and edges, thin yellow or white-to-yellow coating. Pulse: floating and rapid.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Wind-Heat is an exterior heat pattern. Cooling drinks and light clothing help the body's defensive Qi push the pathogen outward without adding internal heat. Spicy food, alcohol, and emotional stress generate additional heat, while windy drafts and overexertion weaken the exterior, allowing the pathogen to penetrate deeper. Rest conserves energy so the body can fight the invasion.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Drink plenty of room-temperature or cool fluids like chrysanthemum or mint tea. Avoid spicy, greasy, and heavy foods that add internal heat. Rest and keep comfortably warm without overheating. Gentle fresh air is fine; avoid strong drafts.

### Wind-Cold
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/wind-cold)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Strong aversion to cold (chills predominate), Mild fever, No sweating, Headache and body aches, Clear, watery nasal discharge, No thirst or preference for warm drinks
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Cold drafts and air conditioning, Iced drinks and raw foods, Overwork and lack of sleep, Damp, chilly weather
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warmth, Drinking hot ginger tea, Resting in a draft-free room, Light sweating
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When Wind-Cold invades the body's surface, it constricts the skin pores and traps the defensive Yang Qi. This trapped Yang accumulates and creates a mild fever, while the Cold predominates, causing intense chills and a lack of sweating. The struggle between the pathogen and the body's protective energy produces the feverish sensation, but the chills remain more pronounced because the Cold is blocking the warming function of Yang.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: normal or slightly pale body, thin white moist coat. Pulse: floating (fú) and tight (jǐn).
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Wind-Cold invades the exterior and constricts the pores, trapping Yang Qi and causing fever and chills. Warmth and warm drinks help relax the tightened surface, open the pores, and promote sweating, which releases the trapped Cold. Rest allows the body's defensive Qi to focus on expelling the pathogen. Conversely, cold environments and cold foods further constrict the exterior and weaken the Spleen, allowing the Cold to linger or deepen.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Drink plenty of warm fluids like fresh ginger tea with scallion (Cong Bai) to help the body sweat lightly and expel the Cold. Bundle up and rest in a warm, draft-free room. Avoid cold drinks, raw foods, and dairy which can worsen phlegm. A light sweat is beneficial, but avoid heavy sweating that can deplete Qi.

### Excess-Heat
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/excess-heat)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: High fever without any chills, Intense thirst with craving for cold drinks, Red flushed face and eyes, Restlessness and irritability, Profuse sweating
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Spicy food, Alcohol, Hot weather, Stress, Overexertion
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Cool environment, Cold drinks, Rest, Light clothing
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When pathogenic Heat penetrates deep into the body, it blazes in the Interior, overwhelming the body's cooling systems. This intense internal fire drives the body temperature up, producing a high fever that is not accompanied by chills. The Heat consumes Yin fluids, causing intense thirst, dry mouth, and dark scanty urine, while the excess Yang energy agitates the Heart spirit, leading to restlessness and irritability.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red body, possibly with prickly papillae, thick dry yellow coating. Pulse: rapid (Shu) and forceful, often overflowing (Hong) and surging.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Excess-Heat is a blazing internal fire. Cooling measures directly counteract the heat, while spicy food, alcohol, and hot environments add more fuel. Rest prevents agitation of Qi, which can fan the flames, and stress stirs up Liver fire, further increasing internal heat.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Drink plenty of cooling fluids like chrysanthemum tea, mung bean soup, or watermelon juice. Avoid spicy, greasy, and fried foods that add internal heat. Rest in a cool, well-ventilated room and avoid overexertion.

### Damp-Heat
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/damp-heat)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Feeling of heaviness in the body and head, as if wrapped in a cloth, Fever that worsens in the afternoon, Sticky or greasy sensation in the mouth, with a bitter taste, Chest and upper abdominal stuffiness and fullness, Nausea or queasiness, poor appetite, Loose, sticky stools that feel incomplete, Dark, scanty urine
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Damp, humid weather, Greasy, fried, or dairy-rich foods, Overeating or eating late at night, Alcohol and sugary drinks, Sedentary lifestyle
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Light, easily digested meals, Warm, dry environment, Gentle movement (walking), Bitter or bland foods (celery, adzuki beans)
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Dampness and Heat combine like steam in a closed pot. The Dampness is heavy and sticky, so it traps the Heat inside the body instead of letting it rise and escape. This produces a lingering, low-grade fever that feels heavy and oppressive, not sharp and high like a dry Heat pattern. Because Dampness sinks and clings to the middle and lower body, it also drags down your energy, clouds your head, and upsets your digestion.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red body with a yellow, greasy coating that is thickest in the centre and root. Pulse: soggy-rapid (Ru Shu), feeling soft and fine but fast.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Damp-Heat is a sticky, heavy pathogen that thrives in humid environments and feeds on greasy, sweet foods. A warm, dry setting and light, bland meals starve the Dampness and let the Heat dissipate. Gentle movement activates the Spleen Qi to transform and transport fluids, breaking the cycle of stagnation that traps the fever. Overeating and rich foods overwhelm the Spleen, producing more Dampness and smothering the body's cooling mechanisms.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Avoid cold, raw, greasy, and sweet foods that create more Dampness. Focus on light, cooked meals with aromatic, bitter, or bland ingredients like barley, adzuki beans, celery, and lightly cooked greens. Sip warm fluids like plain congee water or barley tea. Gentle daily movement helps the Qi push out the Dampness, but avoid sweating heavily, which can exhaust the body without clearing the sticky pathogen.

### Empty-Heat caused by Yin Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/empty-heat-caused-by-yin-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Low-grade afternoon or evening fever, Night sweats, Heat sensation in palms, soles, and chest, Dry mouth and throat, worse in the evening, Flushed cheekbones (malar flush), Restlessness and difficulty sleeping
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Overwork and late nights, Spicy, fried, or drying foods, Excessive sweating, Emotional stress and worry, Hot, dry weather
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Rest and early nights, Cool, quiet environment, Warm, moistening fluids sipped slowly, Gentle, grounding movement
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When the body's cooling, nourishing Yin fluids run low, they can no longer anchor the warm Yang energy. This creates a relative excess of Yang that floats upward and outward like a pilot light that won't go out. The result is a low-grade, lingering heat that feels most pronounced in the afternoon and evening, when Yin is naturally at its weakest, often accompanied by night sweats as the unanchored heat escapes during sleep.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red, thin, dry body with cracks and little or no coating. Pulse: fine (thin) and rapid, often weak or hollow on deep pressure.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Yin deficiency means the body's cooling reserves are already low. Late nights, overwork, and emotional strain consume Yin further, while spicy foods and hot environments add more heat. Sweating depletes both fluids and Yin, making the internal heat worse. Rest, a cool setting, and moistening foods help preserve and slowly rebuild Yin so that the floating heat can settle back down.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Prioritize rest and a regular sleep schedule, avoiding late nights that drain Yin further. Eat cooling, moistening foods like pear, lily bulb, black sesame, and tofu; avoid spicy, fried, or drying foods. Sip warm water or herbal teas throughout the day rather than drinking large amounts at once. Gentle practices such as tai chi or restorative yoga help preserve Yin without overheating the body.

### Qi Deficiency Fever
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/qi-deficiency-fever)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Fever worsens after exertion, Profound fatigue and weakness, Shortness of breath, Spontaneous sweating, Pale or sallow complexion, Loose stools, Reluctance to speak
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Overexertion or long hours, Stress and worry, Raw, cold, or greasy foods, Irregular eating habits, Lack of sleep
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Rest and lying down, Warm, light meals, Gentle, consistent movement, Warm drinks
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When Spleen Qi is weak, it cannot lift the clear Yang upward to the surface. Instead, the Qi sinks downward, generating a low-grade 'deficiency heat' that feels like fever. Because any physical or mental effort uses up Qi, the fever gets worse after exertion.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale, puffy body with teeth marks and a thin white coating. Pulse: weak (Ruo) and fine (Xi), may feel flooding (Hong) but forceless, especially at the right Guan position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Exertion and stress consume Qi, deepening the sinking mechanism that generates deficiency heat, so rest and calm are essential. Cold, raw, and greasy foods further weaken Spleen Yang and dampen the middle burner, while warm, easily digested meals gently replenish Qi and allow the clear Yang to rise again. Consistent, mild movement supports Qi circulation without draining it, helping to restore the natural upward lift.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat warm, easily digestible foods like congee, soups, and well-cooked vegetables. Avoid raw, cold, and greasy foods that tax the Spleen. Rest is vital; overexertion will worsen the fever. Gentle daily movement like walking or Tai Chi helps Qi flow without draining it.

## คำถามที่พบบ่อย (Frequently Asked Questions)
### Can TCM help with a high fever or should I go to the hospital?
High fever with severe symptoms like stiff neck, confusion, or difficulty breathing requires immediate emergency care. TCM can support recovery after the acute crisis is managed, but it is not a substitute for urgent medical treatment. For moderate fevers without red-flag symptoms, TCM can be very effective when started early.

### How does TCM treat a fever without using cooling drugs?
Instead of simply suppressing the temperature, TCM herbs work by addressing the underlying pattern. For a Wind-Heat invasion, cooling, dispersing herbs like Jin Yin Hua and Lian Qiao release the exterior and clear heat. For Wind-Cold, warm pungent herbs like Ma Huang promote sweating to push the pathogen out. The goal is to restore balance, not just lower the thermometer.

### Will acupuncture help bring down a fever?
Yes, acupuncture can be very useful for fever, especially in the early stages. Points like Dazhui (DU-14) and Hegu (LI-4) are commonly used to release the exterior and clear heat. For acute fevers, treatment may be daily until the fever breaks; for chronic low-grade fevers, weekly sessions are typical.

### Can I take TCM herbs alongside ibuprofen or acetaminophen?
Generally yes, but they should be taken at least 1-2 hours apart to avoid digestive overlap. Always inform both your TCM practitioner and your doctor about all medications you are taking. Do not stop prescribed medications without medical guidance.

### Why does my fever come back every afternoon?
An afternoon rise in temperature is a classic sign in TCM of either Damp-Heat or Yin Deficiency. Damp-Heat creates a heavy, sticky fever that worsens as the day progresses, while Yin Deficiency causes a low-grade heat that flares in the afternoon and evening, often with night sweats. A TCM practitioner can differentiate these through your tongue and pulse and provide targeted treatment.

### Is it safe to use TCM for my child's fever?
Yes, TCM can be safely used for children, with dosages adjusted for age and weight. Pediatric fevers often respond quickly to gentle herbal formulas. However, children can deteriorate rapidly, so always monitor them closely and seek urgent care if warning signs appear. Consult a qualified TCM pediatric practitioner.

### How long does it take for TCM to work on a fever?
That depends on the pattern. Acute external fevers may break within 24-48 hours of starting herbs. Chronic low-grade fevers take longer - often 2-4 weeks to notice improvement, with full resolution over months. Your practitioner will give you a realistic timeline based on your specific pattern.
