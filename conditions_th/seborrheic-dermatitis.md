## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนปัจจุบัน (Conventional Medicine View)
### คำอธิบาย (Description)
Seborrheic dermatitis is a common, chronic inflammatory skin condition that appears on oily areas of the body like the scalp, face, and chest. It causes red, scaly patches, stubborn dandruff, and itching. The exact cause isn't fully understood, but it's linked to an overgrowth of a yeast called Malassezia, an overproduction of oil, and an abnormal immune response. It tends to flare with stress, cold dry weather, and hormonal changes.

### การรักษาหลัก (Treatments)
Conventional treatment often includes medicated shampoos (containing ketoconazole, selenium sulfide, or zinc pyrithione), topical corticosteroids or antifungal creams, and in severe cases, oral antifungals or low-dose isotretinoin. These treatments aim to reduce yeast, inflammation, and oil production.

## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนจีน (Traditional Chinese Medicine View)
### ความเข้าใจเกี่ยวกับการแพทย์แผนจีน (TCM Understanding)
TCM sees seborrheic dermatitis as a surface sign of a deeper internal disharmony, primarily involving the Stomach and Spleen. The Stomach channel runs directly across the face, so any internal heat or dampness easily rises along this pathway and irritates the skin. When digestion is weakened by stress, poor diet, or constitutional tendency, the body fails to transform fluids properly, creating Dampness that mixes with Heat and steams upward to the face and scalp.
The condition splits into a “wet” type and a “dry” type, which reflect completely different pathogenic factors. The wet type - oily, red patches with greasy scales - is driven by Damp-Heat in the Stomach and Spleen, often from eating too many rich, greasy, or sweet foods. The dry type can have two roots: one is Stomach Fire, where intense internal heat consumes fluids and leaves the skin dry and scaly; the other is a deficiency condition, where not enough Blood or Yin is available to nourish and moisten the skin, leading to fine, dry flakes and a wandering itch.
Because the same red, scaly patch can arise from either excess or deficiency, TCM uses the tongue, pulse, and accompanying symptoms to tell them apart. A greasy yellow tongue coating and a slippery pulse point to Damp-Heat, while a thin, dry tongue with a rapid pulse suggests heat or deficiency. This is why two people with identical-looking seborrheic dermatitis may receive opposite treatments - one needs cooling, drying herbs, the other needs nourishing, moistening herbs - and both can improve dramatically when the right root cause is addressed.

### การวินิจฉัยโดยแพทย์แผนจีน (Practitioner Diagnosis)
A practitioner begins by asking whether the rash feels oily and weepy or dry and flaky. Seborrheic dermatitis (面游风) splits into a “wet” type and a “dry” type, and that first distinction points toward very different underlying patterns. The tongue and pulse then add crucial detail, helping to confirm which specific imbalance is driving the skin trouble.
When the face and scalp are greasy, with bright red or yellowish patches and sticky scales, the picture points to Damp-Heat in Stomach and Spleen. A person with this pattern often feels heavy, bloated, and may have a sticky taste in the mouth. The tongue is red with a thick, greasy yellow coating, and the pulse feels slippery or rapid. Digestive discomfort and loose, smelly stools are common clues that dampness and heat are brewing in the middle burner and steaming upward.
If the skin is dry, with red patches and fine, bran-like scales, and the itching is intense, Stomach Fire (Stomach Heat) is often at play. Here internal heat rises from the stomach to the face, drying out the skin. A person will likely feel thirsty, prefer cold drinks, and struggle with constipation. The tongue is red with a thin yellow coating, and the pulse is rapid or wiry. The rash may flare with spicy foods, stress, or hot weather.
In chronic or milder cases where the redness is dull and the skin is dry with fine scaling and some hair thinning, Blood Deficiency with External Wind is a common root. Blood fails to nourish the skin, and a “wind” itch develops. A pale tongue and a thin, choppy pulse support this picture. When the patches are a muted red, the mouth feels dry, and symptoms worsen in the evening, Empty-Heat caused by Yin Deficiency is more likely. The tongue is red with little or no coating, and the pulse is thin and rapid.

### Damp-Heat in Stomach and Spleen
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/damp-heat-in-stomach-and-spleen)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Oily, red or yellowish patches with greasy, yellowish scales or crusts, Itching, especially when skin is oily, Bloating and fullness in the upper abdomen, Sticky, incomplete bowel movements, Bitter or sticky taste in the mouth, Heavy feeling in the body and limbs
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Greasy, fried, or spicy foods, Alcohol, Humid weather, Stress and worry, Overeating
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Avoiding greasy, sweet, and dairy foods, Eating light, bland meals, Cool, dry environment, Regular bowel movements, Gentle exercise
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When the Spleen and Stomach are overwhelmed by rich, greasy foods and stress, they fail to transform fluids, creating internal Dampness that combines with Heat. This Damp-Heat rises to the face and scalp, where it overstimulates oil glands and inflames the skin, producing the classic oily, red, and greasy-scaled patches of seborrheic dermatitis. The digestive symptoms like bloating and sticky stools are the deeper sign that the root is in the gut.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red body, possibly swollen with teeth marks, thick yellow greasy coating especially at centre and root. Pulse: soggy (Ru) and rapid (Shu), often also slippery (Hua).
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Damp-Heat in the Spleen and Stomach is fueled by rich, heavy foods that the digestive system cannot process, creating internal dampness and heat that rise to the skin. Stress and worry further weaken the Spleen, impairing its ability to transform and transport. Light, bland foods and a calm mind support the Spleen's function, allowing dampness to be cleared rather than accumulating and flaring up on the face.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Cut out greasy, fried, and sugary foods, which directly feed Dampness and Heat. Focus on lightly cooked vegetables, whole grains like barley and millet, and legumes that support the Spleen. Avoid alcohol and late-night eating. Gentle daily movement like walking helps Qi move and Dampness drain.

### Stomach Fire (Stomach Heat)
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/stomach-fire-stomach-heat)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Facial redness with dry, fine scales, Bad breath, Strong thirst for cold drinks, Constant hunger or large appetite, Constipation with dry, hard stools, Swollen, painful, or bleeding gums
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Spicy or fried foods, Alcohol and coffee, Emotional stress or anger, Hot, stuffy environments
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Cool, bland foods, Drinking plenty of water, Regular bowel movements, Cool compresses on the face
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Stomach Fire is an internal excess heat that rises upward along the Stomach channel, which travels across the face. This upward steaming of heat irritates the skin, causing facial redness, dry scales, and itching. The heat also consumes body fluids, leading to the thirst, dry stools, and dry skin that typify the dry-type pattern of seborrheic dermatitis.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red body, possibly with red prickles in the centre, and a thick, dry, yellow coating. Pulse: rapid, slippery, and forceful, especially strong at the right middle position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Spicy, fried, and heating foods stoke Stomach Fire, causing more heat to rise to the face and worsen redness and dryness. Emotional stress and anger stir up Liver Qi, which can transform into Fire and combine with Stomach Heat. Cooling foods and adequate hydration help drain the excess heat and replenish the fluids consumed by the internal Fire, while regular bowel movements provide an outlet for the heat to leave the body.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Avoid spicy, fried, and greasy foods, as well as alcohol and coffee, which directly add heat to the Stomach. Eat plenty of cooling, moistening foods like cucumber, pear, and mung beans. Drink plenty of water and keep bowel movements regular to help clear internal heat.

### Blood Deficiency with External Wind
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/blood-deficiency-with-external-wind)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Dry, fine, flaky scaling on the face or scalp, Itching that shifts location or comes and goes, Worse at night or after stress, Pale or sallow complexion, Aversion to wind or drafts, Dizziness or light-headedness
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Exposure to wind and dry air, Overwork and stress, Spicy and fried foods, Excessive alcohol or coffee, Poor sleep
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Rest and avoiding drafts, Gentle moisturizing with natural oils, Warm, nourishing soups, Stable sleep schedule, Gentle exercise like walking
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When the body's Blood is insufficient, the skin loses its nourishment and becomes dry, thin, and flaky. This dryness creates a weakness that allows external Wind to enter the skin, stirring up an itch that tends to move around and flare up without warning. On the face and scalp, this shows up as pale pink or dull patches with fine, dry scaling and a persistent, wandering itch that often feels worse at night.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale, thin body with a thin white and slightly dry coat. Pulse: fine (thin), wiry on the left Guan position, and floating at the superficial level in the right Cun position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Blood Deficiency leaves the skin undernourished and vulnerable, so external Wind easily invades and triggers the wandering itch. Rest, nourishing foods, and stable sleep help build Blood, while protecting the skin from wind and avoiding drying habits prevent further Blood loss. Gentle movement encourages Blood flow without depleting reserves, keeping the skin calmer and more resilient.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat warm, cooked, Blood-nourishing foods such as bone broths, dark leafy greens, black sesame, eggs, and small amounts of high-quality red meat. Avoid overly spicy, fried, or drying foods that can consume precious Blood and fluids. Protect your face and scalp from wind with a scarf, and keep indoor air from becoming too dry. Gentle daily movement like walking helps Qi and Blood circulate without exhausting you.

### Empty-Heat caused by Yin Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/empty-heat-caused-by-yin-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Dull-red or dark-red patches on the face, Fine, dry scaling rather than greasy flakes, Itching that worsens in the evening or at night, Dry mouth and throat, thirst for small sips of water, Heat sensation in the palms, soles, and chest (five-palm heat), Night sweats, Restlessness, irritability, or difficulty sleeping
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Spicy or fried foods, Alcohol and coffee, Late nights and overwork, Dry, heated indoor air
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Cool, humidified environments, Going to bed early, Moistening foods (pears, congee), Sipping warm water
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When the body's Yin fluids are depleted over time, the skin loses its natural moisture and nourishment. The resulting empty heat rises to the face, creating dull-red patches and fine, dry scales. Because Yin and nighttime both belong to the quiet, cooling side of the body, the itching and discomfort tend to grow worse in the evening and at night.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red, thin, and dry body with cracks; little or no coating. Pulse: fine (thin) and rapid.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Empty heat arises because cooling Yin is too weak to balance the body's normal warmth. Spicy foods, alcohol, and late nights consume Yin further, making the heat flare. Rest, moistening foods, and early nights help rebuild Yin and gently cool the skin from within.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Favour moistening, cooling foods like pear, cucumber, tofu, and a little honey. Avoid late nights and overwork, which burn Yin. Sip warm water throughout the day and keep your living space gently humidified.

## คำถามที่พบบ่อย (Frequently Asked Questions)
### What's the difference between the 'wet' and 'dry' types in TCM?
The wet type is characterized by oily, red patches with greasy, yellowish scales. It often feels itchy and may ooze or crust. This type is caused by Damp-Heat in the digestive system rising to the skin. The dry type shows up as red patches with fine, white, bran-like scales on a dry surface. It can be caused by Stomach Fire consuming fluids, or by a deficiency of Blood or Yin that leaves the skin undernourished. Telling them apart is the first step in choosing the right herbs and foods.

### Can TCM cure seborrheic dermatitis permanently?
TCM aims to correct the internal imbalance that allows the rash to appear, so many people experience long-term remission after treatment. However, because diet, stress, and lifestyle can re-trigger the pattern, occasional maintenance herbs or dietary adjustments may be needed. It's more accurate to think of it as managing the tendency rather than a one-time cure.

### Do I have to change my diet?
Yes, diet plays a central role in TCM treatment for seborrheic dermatitis. Greasy, fried, sugary, and dairy foods all tend to create Dampness and Heat, which feed the rash. Cutting back on these while eating more cooling, light foods (leafy greens, cucumber, congee) makes the herbs work better and helps prevent recurrence. Your practitioner will give you specific advice based on your pattern.

### Can I use my medicated shampoo alongside Chinese herbs?
Absolutely. Topical treatments like antifungal shampoos or creams don't interact with internal herbs. Continue using them as directed while you start TCM. If you're taking oral medications (antifungals, isotretinoin), let both your dermatologist and TCM practitioner know so they can coordinate care.

### How quickly will I see results?
Itching and redness often improve within the first two weeks of herbal treatment. Oily scales and patches may take a bit longer - around 3 to 6 weeks - to noticeably clear. Deficiency patterns (Blood or Yin deficiency) typically respond more slowly, with steady improvement over 6 to 12 weeks, because the body needs time to rebuild its reserves.

### Is seborrheic dermatitis linked to stress?
Yes. In TCM, stress directly disrupts the Liver's ability to keep Qi flowing smoothly, which can lead to heat building up and rising to the face. Stress also weakens the Spleen's digestive function, promoting Dampness. Many people notice their rash flares during stressful periods, and acupuncture can be especially helpful for calming the nervous system and reducing stress-induced outbreaks.

### Can acupuncture help with the itching?
Yes. Acupuncture can quickly relieve itching by moving stagnant Qi and Blood in the affected area and by calming the internal wind that stirs up the itch. Points on the arms and legs are used to clear heat and drain dampness, while local points on the face are avoided during active flares to prevent irritation. Many patients feel a soothing effect after the first session.

1. **中医辨证治疗脂溢性皮炎170 例**

   A clinical study of 170 patients with seborrheic dermatitis treated with pattern-based herbal formulas. Patients were divided into damp-heat, wind-heat with blood dryness, and yin deficiency with internal heat patterns. Results showed a 70.6% cure rate and 100% total effective rate after 1-2 treatment courses of 20 days each.

   An expert consensus published in 2020 providing standardized TCM diagnostic patterns, treatment principles, and herbal formulas for seborrheic dermatitis. It recognizes three main patterns: blood-heat with wind-dryness, spleen-stomach damp-heat, and blood deficiency with wind-dryness, and recommends corresponding formulas like Pi Pa Qing Fei Yin and Chu Shi Wei Ling Tang.
