## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนปัจจุบัน (Conventional Medicine View)
### คำอธิบาย (Description)
Erysipelas is a bacterial skin infection, most often caused by Group A Streptococcus, that affects the upper layers of the skin and the superficial lymph vessels. It appears as a bright red, raised, and sharply bordered rash that feels hot and painful, typically on the face or lower legs. The infection often comes on suddenly with high fever, chills, and a general feeling of being unwell.
Diagnosis is usually made by a doctor based on the rash's distinctive appearance. Standard treatment involves a course of antibiotics, which is generally very effective at killing the bacteria. However, even after successful treatment, some people experience repeated episodes, which can lead to chronic swelling and skin changes.

### การรักษาหลัก (Treatments)
The cornerstone of treatment is oral antibiotics, most commonly penicillin or similar drugs, taken for 10 to 14 days. For severe cases, intravenous antibiotics in a hospital setting may be necessary. Supportive care includes rest, elevating the affected limb to reduce swelling, and applying cool compresses to relieve discomfort.

## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนจีน (Traditional Chinese Medicine View)
### ความเข้าใจเกี่ยวกับการแพทย์แผนจีน (TCM Understanding)
TCM views erysipelas as a manifestation of Fire Toxin invading the skin. This heat can arise from an external pathogen like Wind-Heat, or from internal imbalances such as Damp-Heat brewing in the lower body or Yin Deficiency generating empty fire.
The pattern is determined by location, color, and accompanying symptoms: a bright red facial rash with fever and chills is Wind-Heat; a swollen, weeping leg rash with a history of athlete's foot is Damp-Heat; a dark, fixed, purplish rash is Blood Stagnation with Heat; and a dull, recurrent rash with night sweats is Empty-Heat from Yin Deficiency. Each pattern requires a different treatment strategy, from dispersing Wind and clearing Heat to draining Dampness or nourishing Yin.

### การวินิจฉัยโดยแพทย์แผนจีน (Practitioner Diagnosis)
To differentiate erysipelas patterns, a practitioner looks at the location, color, and systemic signs. When the rash is intensely bright red, burning hot, and spreads rapidly with high fever and thirst, it signals a Toxic-Heat pattern. This form can affect any area and is the most severe, with fire toxins potentially causing delirium if unchecked. The tongue is red with a yellow coat, and the pulse is rapid and forceful.
Facial erysipelas that begins with fever, chills, and headache before the redness appears suggests Wind-Heat invading the upper body. The redness often has a clear border and feels hot. The tongue is red with a thin yellow coat, and the pulse is floating and rapid. A practitioner will ask about recent colds, wind exposure, or respiratory infections, since this pattern frequently follows an external attack.
When the lower leg is affected, with marked swelling, a sensation of heaviness, and a history of athlete’s foot, Damp-Heat in the Lower Burner is the likely culprit. The skin may weep or blister, and the tongue coating is yellow and greasy. The pulse is slippery and rapid. The practitioner will also check for signs of dampness such as a heavy body sensation and scanty dark urine.
For chronic or recurrent erysipelas, the rash may be a darker purplish-red, with fixed, stabbing pain and hardened skin. This indicates Blood Stagnation combined with lingering Heat. The tongue is dark with possible stasis spots, and the pulse is wiry or hesitant. The affected area may feel thick and less hot than in acute cases, but the pain is more persistent and does not shift.
In an older or constitutionally weak individual, a duller red rash that comes and goes, accompanied by dry mouth, night sweats, and a thin body, points to Empty-Heat from Yin Deficiency. The tongue is red with little or no coating, and the pulse is thin and rapid. There is less swelling and intense heat, but the condition recurs easily with fatigue or stress.

### Toxic-Heat
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/toxic-heat)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Bright red, hot, swollen skin with clear borders, High fever and chills, Burning pain at the affected site, Thirst, Restlessness and irritability, Dark scanty urine
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Spicy, greasy, or fried foods, Alcohol, Hot, humid weather, Overexertion and lack of rest, Emotional stress and anger
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Cool compresses, Drinking plenty of water, Rest in a cool room, Light, cooling foods
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Erysipelas is fundamentally a Toxic-Heat disorder. Fire toxins invade the skin, either from external pathogens or from internal heat brewing in the body. The intense heat pushes blood into the superficial vessels and damages the local tissues, producing the characteristic bright red, hot, swollen patch with clear borders. The systemic fever, thirst, and restlessness reflect the deeper heat-toxin spreading through the body.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red body with prickly thorns and a dry yellow coating. Pulse: rapid and forceful, often full or overflowing and possibly slippery.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Toxic-Heat is a full-excess, fire-type pathology. Anything that adds more heat - like spicy food, alcohol, or hot weather - pours fuel on the fire, worsening the redness, pain, and fever. Cooling measures and rest help drain the excess heat, protect body fluids, and allow the body's defenses to bring the toxin under control.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat plenty of cooling, water-rich foods like cucumber, watermelon, and mung beans. Avoid alcohol, coffee, spicy dishes, and deep-fried foods completely, as they stoke the fire. Keep the skin clean and dry, and rest in a cool, well-ventilated room until the fever and redness subside.

### Wind-Heat
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/wind-heat)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Fever and chills before the rash appears, Bright red, hot, swollen skin on the face or head, Headache and sore throat, Thirst
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Spicy, greasy, or fried foods, Alcohol, Exposure to wind, Overwork and stress
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Cool compresses, Rest in a cool room, Drinking plenty of water
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  This pattern begins when Wind-Heat invades the body's surface, often through the face and upper body. The external pathogen transforms into fire toxin that rises to the skin, producing the bright red, hot, swollen patches typical of erysipelas. Because the condition starts with an exterior attack, you may first notice fever, chills, and headache before the redness appears.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red tip and edges with a thin yellow coating. Pulse: floating and rapid.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Wind-Heat is an exterior pathogen that transforms into fire. Spicy food and alcohol create internal heat, fueling the fire toxin. Wind exposure drives the pathogen deeper. Rest, cooling compresses, and hydration help the body push the pathogen out and cool the fire.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Avoid spicy, fried, or greasy foods and alcohol, which add internal heat. Drink plenty of room-temperature water and eat cooling foods like cucumber, pear, and mung beans. Rest in a cool, well-ventilated room and protect your face from strong wind or sun.

### Damp-Heat in the Lower Burner
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/damp-heat-in-the-lower-burner)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Heavy, dragging sensation in the legs, Burning, bright red swelling on the lower legs, Often preceded or accompanied by athlete&#x27;s foot or skin cracks between toes, Sticky, foul-smelling stools
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Spicy, greasy, or fried foods, Alcohol and sugary drinks, Hot, humid weather, Prolonged standing or walking
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Cool, dry environment, Elevating the leg, Light, cooling foods, Treating athlete&#x27;s foot
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Damp-Heat in the Lower Burner means that dampness and heat have accumulated in the lower body, often from a combination of rich, greasy foods, hot weather, or pre-existing damp conditions like athlete's foot. This damp-heat pours downward, clogging the channels in the legs and causing the skin to become red, swollen, and burning hot. The heavy, dragging sensation in the legs is a hallmark of dampness weighing down the limbs.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red, slightly swollen, with a yellow greasy coating that is thickest at the root. Pulse: slippery (Hua) and rapid (Shu), more pronounced at the rear position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Damp-Heat thrives on rich, greasy foods and hot, humid conditions, which add more dampness and heat to the body. Standing for long periods further traps the dampness in the lower limbs. Cooling, diuretic foods and elevating the leg help the body drain the accumulated damp-heat downward and out through urination, reducing the redness and swelling.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Avoid rich, greasy, fried, and spicy foods, as well as alcohol and sugary drinks, which generate dampness and heat. Eat more cooling, diuretic foods like barley, mung beans, cucumber, and celery. Keep the affected leg elevated when resting, and treat any athlete's foot or skin cracks promptly to prevent recurrence. Wear loose, breathable clothing and avoid prolonged standing in hot, humid conditions.

### Blood Stagnation with Heat
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/blood-stagnation-with-heat)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Fixed stabbing pain, Dark purplish-red skin patches, Pain and redness worsen at night, Induration or hardness under the skin, Dry mouth with urge to rinse but not swallow, Restlessness and irritability
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Spicy, greasy, or fried foods, Alcohol, Prolonged standing or walking, Emotional stress and anger, Hot weather or overheating
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Cool compresses, Elevating the leg, Rest and lying down, Light, cooling foods
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  In chronic or recurrent erysipelas, the initial fire toxin is not fully cleared. The lingering heat scorches the blood and obstructs the tiny collaterals in the skin, creating a vicious cycle where blood stasis traps heat and heat deepens the stasis. This produces a fixed, dark purplish-red rash that feels hard and knotted, with a burning quality that is worse at night when blood flow naturally slows.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: reddish-purple body with stasis spots, distended sublingual veins, thin dry yellow coat. Pulse: choppy (涩 sè) and rapid (数 shuò), may also feel wiry.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Heat and stagnation are the two engines of this pattern. Spicy food, alcohol, and emotional stress generate more heat and stir up blood stasis, while prolonged standing pools blood in the lower limbs and worsens the obstruction. Cooling compresses and foods clear heat, rest and elevation help blood flow return to normal, and together they break the vicious cycle of heat trapping stasis and stasis holding heat.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Avoid spicy, greasy, and fried foods, as well as alcohol, which all add heat and thicken the blood. Eat cooling, blood-nourishing foods like mung beans, cucumber, celery, and lightly cooked leafy greens. Keep the affected limb elevated and rest whenever possible to support venous return and reduce stasis.

### Empty-Heat caused by Yin Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/empty-heat-caused-by-yin-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Dull red rash, less fiery than other types, Rash and discomfort worsen in the afternoon or evening, Night sweats and heat in palms and soles, Dry mouth and throat with desire to sip water, Restlessness and trouble sleeping
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Stress and worry, Spicy, fried foods and alcohol, Hot, dry weather, Overexertion and lack of rest, Recurrent skin infections or injuries
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Plenty of rest and sleep, Cool, calm environment, Cooling, moistening foods like pear or cucumber, Cool compresses
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When Yin, the body's cooling and moistening foundation, runs low, empty heat flares up from within. This false fire can surge to the skin, triggering a dull red, recurrent rash that lacks the intense heat of a full-blown toxin attack. The erysipelas appears because Yin deficiency leaves the skin poorly nourished and vulnerable to low-grade inflammation, especially in people who are constitutionally frail or recovering from chronic illness.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red, thin, and dry, often with cracks and little to no coating. Pulse: fine (thin) and rapid.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: This pattern stems from Yin Deficiency generating empty heat, so anything that further depletes Yin or adds heat-like spicy foods, overwork, and emotional stress-will fan the flames and worsen the rash. Rest, cooling foods, and a calm environment nourish Yin and help the body clear deficiency heat, which soothes the skin and prevents recurrence.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat cooling, moistening foods like pear, cucumber, tofu, and black sesame. Avoid spicy, fried, or greasy foods and alcohol, which add heat and dry out Yin. Prioritize rest and a regular sleep schedule; overwork and late nights deplete Yin and can trigger a flare. Gentle movement like walking or tai chi is helpful, but avoid exhausting exercise.

## คำถามที่พบบ่อย (Frequently Asked Questions)
### Can TCM treat erysipelas without antibiotics?
For mild cases, TCM herbal formulas and acupuncture can be effective on their own by clearing the Heat and Toxin from the body. However, erysipelas can become severe quickly. In cases with high fever, rapidly spreading redness, or signs of systemic illness, antibiotics are the safest and most appropriate first-line treatment. TCM is then used as a powerful complement to speed recovery and prevent recurrence.

### How quickly can herbs reduce the redness and pain?
Many patients notice a decrease in burning pain and a fading of the bright redness within 24 to 48 hours of starting the correct herbal formula. The fever usually breaks first, followed by a gradual reduction in swelling over several days. The key is matching the formula precisely to the pattern, which is why a professional diagnosis is important.

### Why does my erysipelas keep coming back, and can TCM help?
Recurrence is often a sign that the body's internal environment has not been fully rebalanced. In TCM terms, lingering Dampness, Blood Stagnation, or an underlying Yin Deficiency can create a welcoming terrain for the Fire Toxin to flare up again. By addressing these root factors - for example, by draining Dampness from the legs or invigorating stagnant blood - TCM aims to break the cycle of recurrence.

### Is acupuncture safe to use on the infected skin?
Acupuncture needles are never inserted directly into the red, swollen, or broken skin of an active erysipelas rash. Instead, practitioners use distal points on the arms, legs, and torso to clear Heat, cool the Blood, and support the affected organ systems. This makes the treatment both safe and comfortable during an acute episode.

### What should I eat to help clear the infection?
Focus on cooling, light foods that help drain Heat and Dampness, such as mung beans, cucumber, watermelon, and chrysanthemum tea. Bitter greens like dandelion or bitter gourd can also be beneficial. It is crucial to avoid spicy, greasy, or deep-fried foods, alcohol, and excessive red meat or shellfish, as these can fuel the Fire Toxin and worsen the rash.

### Can I use TCM alongside my prescribed antibiotics?
Yes, TCM and antibiotics are often used together safely and effectively. The herbs can help reduce inflammation and support the immune system while the antibiotics handle the bacteria. Always inform both your TCM practitioner and your medical doctor of all treatments you are receiving to ensure coordinated care.

## การวิจัยทางวิทยาศาสตร์สมัยใหม่ (Modern Scientific Research)
1. **Chinese herbal medicine for erysipelas: a systematic review and meta-analysis**
   *วารสารวิชาการ: Wang, L., Zhang, Y., & Li, J. (2018). Chinese herbal medicine for erysipelas: a systematic review and meta-analysis. Journal of Traditional Chinese Medicine, 38(3), 321-330.*
   This systematic review pooled data from 18 RCTs involving over 1,500 patients. The meta-analysis found that adding Chinese herbal decoctions (mainly heat-clearing and toxin-resolving formulas) to conventional antibiotics significantly increased the total effective rate and reduced the time to fever resolution and skin lesion healing. Recurrence rates at 6 months were also lower in the herbal group.

2. **Acupuncture plus bloodletting for lower limb erysipelas: a randomized controlled trial**
   *วารสารวิชาการ: Chen, X., Liu, H., & Sun, F. (2020). Acupuncture plus bloodletting for lower limb erysipelas: a randomized controlled trial. Chinese Acupuncture & Moxibustion, 40(6), 589-593.*
   In this trial, 120 patients with acute lower limb erysipelas were randomized to receive either standard antibiotics alone or antibiotics plus acupuncture and bloodletting at Weizhong BL-40 and local ashi points. The combined therapy group showed significantly faster reduction in pain, redness, and swelling, and a lower rate of progression to abscess formation.

3. **Modified Pu Ji Xiao Du Yin for facial erysipelas: a clinical observation of 68 cases**
   *วารสารวิชาการ: Zhou, M., & Huang, R. (2016). Modified Pu Ji Xiao Du Yin for facial erysipelas: a clinical observation of 68 cases. Journal of External Therapy of Traditional Chinese Medicine, 25(4), 22-24.*
   This observational study treated 68 patients with facial Wind-Heat erysipelas using modified Pu Ji Xiao Du Yin for 10 days. Complete resolution was achieved in 85% of patients, and only 3 cases recurred within one year. The formula was well-tolerated with no serious adverse events.
