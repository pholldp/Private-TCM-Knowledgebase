## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนปัจจุบัน (Conventional Medicine View)
### คำอธิบาย (Description)
Chronic glomerulonephritis is a long-term condition in which the tiny filtering units of the kidneys (glomeruli) become inflamed and scarred, gradually reducing the kidneys' ability to filter waste and excess fluid from the blood. Common symptoms include swelling (edema), especially in the legs and around the eyes, foamy urine from protein loss, high blood pressure, and fatigue. Diagnosis is usually made through urine tests (protein, blood), blood tests (creatinine, eGFR), and sometimes a kidney biopsy. Over time, it can progress to chronic kidney disease and end-stage renal failure.

### การรักษาหลัก (Treatments)
Treatment typically focuses on controlling blood pressure with ACE inhibitors or ARBs, reducing proteinuria, and managing complications. Immunosuppressive medications like corticosteroids or cyclophosphamide may be used when an autoimmune cause is suspected. Dietary changes (low salt, controlled protein) are often recommended. In advanced stages, dialysis or kidney transplant may be necessary.

## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนจีน (Traditional Chinese Medicine View)
### ความเข้าใจเกี่ยวกับการแพทย์แผนจีน (TCM Understanding)
TCM views chronic glomerulonephritis as a deep-rooted weakness of the Kidney and Spleen systems, often with the Liver involved. The Kidneys govern water metabolism and store essence; when they are weakened, fluids accumulate as edema, and the body loses its foundation. The Spleen transforms and transports fluids; if it fails, dampness builds up, creating turbidity.
Yang deficiency is a common root, causing coldness and water retention. Yin deficiency can develop over time, leading to heat and internal wind (dizziness, tinnitus). Damp-heat can also lodge in the lower burner, causing cloudy urine and inflammation.
Because the same Western diagnosis can present with very different symptoms-cold and swollen vs. hot and dry-TCM distinguishes several patterns. This explains why some patients feel worse with cold and others with heat. The treatment must match the pattern, not just the disease label.

### การวินิจฉัยโดยแพทย์แผนจีน (Practitioner Diagnosis)
A TCM practitioner begins by listening carefully to your story - when the fatigue started, how the swelling behaves, and what your body temperature feels like. Chronic Glomerulonephritis (慢肾风, màn shèn fēng) is seen as a deep, progressive weakness of the Kidney and Spleen systems, often complicated by dampness, heat, or internal wind. The pattern is identified through a combination of symptoms, tongue appearance, and pulse quality.
If the dominant complaint is feeling constantly cold, with puffy ankles and a pale, puffy face, the practitioner suspects Kidney and Spleen Yang Deficiency. The tongue is pale and swollen with tooth marks, and the pulse feels deep and weak. These signs tell the practitioner that the body’s warming and water-transforming functions have become too weak to manage fluids.
When fatigue and shortness of breath mix with a dry mouth, night sweats, and a sensation of heat in the palms, the picture shifts to Qi and Yin Deficiency. The tongue may appear pale with a thin or absent coating, and the pulse is thin and weak. This combination of weakness and dryness points to a dual depletion of vital energy and cooling fluids.
Dizziness, tinnitus, and a sore lower back that feels worse when you are tired suggest Kidney and Liver Yin Deficiency. The tongue is often red with little coating, and the pulse feels wiry and thin. These upward-disturbing symptoms, together with the wiry pulse, tell the practitioner that Liver Yin is also involved and a subtle internal wind may be stirring.
If the urine is noticeably turbid or dark, with a sense of heaviness or burning, Damp-Heat in the Lower Burner is likely an added complication. The tongue coating becomes yellow and greasy, and the pulse feels slippery and rapid. Unlike the pure deficiency patterns, this one shows clear signs of heat and dampness aggravating the kidneys.
In advanced stages, a person may feel cold and swollen yet also experience hot flashes, night sweats, and a dry throat. This confusing mix signals Deficiency of both Kidney Yin and Yang. The tongue may be pale and swollen with teeth marks and cracks, and the pulse is deep and feeble. The practitioner sees this as an exhaustion of both the body’s warming and cooling reserves.

## กลุ่มอาการการแพทย์แผนจีนและการรักษา (TCM Patterns and Treatment)

### Kidney and Spleen Yang Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/kidney-and-spleen-yang-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Cold hands and feet, especially below the knees, Early-morning diarrhea (around 5 AM), Swelling of the legs and ankles, Sore and cold lower back and knees, Poor appetite and abdominal bloating after eating
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Cold, damp weather, Raw and cold foods, Overwork, Prolonged standing
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warmth and heat packs, Adequate rest and sleep, Warm, cooked meals (soups, stews), Gentle movement (e.g., tai chi, walking)
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  In chronic glomerulonephritis, the kidneys rely on warming Yang energy to filter fluids, while the spleen needs it to transform and transport moisture. When both Kidney and Spleen Yang become deficient, the body loses its ability to manage water, so fluids build up as swelling, especially in the legs. The lack of internal warmth also slows digestion, leading to loose stools and poor appetite, and fails to keep the limbs and back warm, leaving you feeling heavy, cold, and exhausted.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale, swollen, puffy body with teeth marks and a white slippery coating. Pulse: deep, weak, slow, and especially feeble in the rear (Kidney) and right middle (Spleen) positions.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Kidney and Spleen Yang are like the body's pilot light-they need warmth to function. Cold and dampness smother that fire, worsening edema and fatigue. Rest and warmth conserve Yang, while warm foods and gentle movement help the Spleen transform fluids and the Kidney to manage water.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat warm, cooked foods like soups and stews; avoid raw, cold, and iced drinks. Include gentle warming spices such as ginger and cinnamon. Keep the lower back and abdomen warm, and get plenty of rest. Gentle daily movement like walking helps Qi flow without exhausting Yang.

### Qi and Yin Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/qi-and-yin-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Persistent fatigue and lack of strength, Dry mouth with little desire to drink, Night sweats and warm palms, soles, and chest, Shortness of breath that worsens with exertion, Pale-red tongue with thin or absent coating, Fine, weak pulse
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Overwork, Greasy, spicy, or fried foods, Hot, dry weather, Emotional stress, Late nights and insufficient sleep
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Adequate rest and sleep, Moistening foods (pears, congee, tofu), Gentle movement (e.g., tai chi, walking), Cool, calm environment
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  In chronic glomerulonephritis, the long-term inflammation and damage gradually exhaust both Qi and Yin. The Spleen and Kidneys lose their ability to generate energy and manage fluids, so you feel persistently tired and weak. Yin depletion allows mild Empty Heat to rise, leading to dry mouth, warm palms, and night sweats without the heavy coldness or water retention seen in other patterns.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red or pale-red body with a thin, possibly absent coating; the surface may appear dry or cracked. Pulse: fine (thin) and weak, often with a rapid quality reflecting mild Empty Heat.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Qi and Yin are the body's fuel and cooling system. Rest and bland, moistening foods replenish these reserves. Overwork and heat further burn through Yin and scatter Qi, making fatigue and dryness worse. Stress knots Qi and generates internal heat, aggravating the empty heat from Yin deficiency.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Choose moistening, easily digested foods like pear, millet congee, and steamed vegetables. Avoid spicy, greasy, or overly heating foods that further consume Yin. Gentle daily movement such as tai chi or walking helps Qi flow without draining your reserves. Prioritize rest and avoid staying up late, which can deplete both Qi and Yin.

### Kidney and Liver Yin Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/kidney-and-liver-yin-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Dizziness and tinnitus (ringing in the ears), Dry eyes with blurred vision, Night sweats with heat in the palms, soles, and chest, Soreness and weakness of the lower back and knees, Dry mouth and throat, worse at night
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Emotional stress, Overwork, Greasy, spicy, or fried foods, Alcohol and coffee, Hot, dry weather
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Adequate rest and sleep, Cool, calm environment, Moistening foods (pears, congee, tofu), Gentle movement (e.g., tai chi, walking), Staying well hydrated
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  In chronic glomerulonephritis, the kidneys' filtering function is gradually lost, and over time the essential Yin fluids become depleted. When Kidney Yin is insufficient, it can no longer nourish the Liver, causing Liver Yin to also become deficient. This dual deficiency allows internal Wind to stir, producing dizziness, tinnitus, and a wiry pulse, while the lack of cooling Yin leads to night sweats, dry mouth, and heat in the palms, soles, and chest.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red, thin body with cracks, little or no coating, and a dry surface. Pulse: fine (thin), rapid, and often wiry; especially weak at the left Chi (Kidney) position and thin-wiry at the left Guan (Liver) position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Yin is the body's cooling and moistening substance. Activities and foods that generate heat or consume fluids - like stress, overwork, and spicy meals - further deplete Yin and aggravate deficiency heat and internal wind. Rest, a cool environment, and moistening foods help preserve and replenish Yin, calming the wind and cooling the empty heat.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Prioritize cooling, moistening foods such as pears, tofu, cucumber, black sesame, and goji berries. Avoid late nights and overwork, which consume Yin. Gentle, grounding movement like tai chi or slow walking supports fluid balance without overheating the body.

### Damp-Heat in the Lower Burner
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/damp-heat-in-the-lower-burner)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Dark, turbid, or cloudy urine, Burning or stinging during urination, Heaviness or soreness in the lower back and legs, Thirst with little desire to drink, Lower abdominal fullness and distension, Foul-smelling urine or vaginal discharge
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Hot, humid environments, Greasy, spicy, or fried foods, Alcohol and coffee, Prolonged sitting or inactivity, Suppressing the urge to urinate
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Cool, dry weather, Light, bland meals, Gentle movement (e.g., tai chi, walking), Staying well hydrated
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When Dampness and Heat combine and sink into the lower body, they can lodge in the kidneys and bladder. This toxic mixture inflames the delicate filtering structures of the kidneys, much like a slow-burning infection. The result is the turbid, dark, and sometimes burning urine that marks this pattern in chronic glomerulonephritis.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red body, slightly swollen, with a yellow greasy coating thickest at the root. Pulse: slippery (滑, huá) and rapid (数, shuò), more pronounced at the rear position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Damp-Heat is a sticky, inflammatory combination. External humidity and greasy food add more Dampness and Heat, making the urine even more turbid and irritating. Light, cooling foods and water help flush the urinary tract, while gentle movement encourages the body's Qi to process and drain the stagnant fluids, reducing the pressure and inflammation in the kidneys.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat light, cooling foods that drain dampness, like mung beans, barley, cucumber, and watermelon rind tea. Strictly avoid alcohol, fried foods, spices, and dairy, which all brew more Damp-Heat. Gentle walking or tai chi helps move Qi and prevent stagnation in the lower body.

### Deficiency of both the Kidney Yin and Yang
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/deficiency-of-both-the-kidney-yin-and-yang)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Alternating chills and hot flushes, Lower back cold and aching, Deep, unrelenting fatigue, Night sweats, Dry mouth and throat, Frequent nighttime urination, Cold hands and feet, Loose stools or early-morning diarrhea, Dizziness and ringing in the ears, Poor memory and forgetfulness
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Overwork, Exposure to cold and drafts, Raw and cold foods, Late nights and insufficient sleep, Excessive sexual activity
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warmth and heat packs, Adequate rest and sleep, Warm, cooked meals (soups, stews), Gentle movement (e.g., tai chi, walking)
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  In advanced chronic glomerulonephritis, the Kidneys become deeply depleted. When both Yin and Yang are exhausted, the body loses its ability to warm, transform, and hold fluids (Yang deficiency) and to cool, moisten, and anchor Qi (Yin deficiency). This dual failure leads to persistent water-damp retention and edema on one hand, and internal heat with dizziness and dryness on the other. The result is a chaotic mix of cold and heat signs, reflecting a profound, complex deficiency that underlies the chronic, smoldering nature of the disease.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: Pale, puffy-tender body with teeth marks and cracks, thin white coat that may be partially peeled. Pulse: Deep, fine, weak, especially in the Chi (Kidney) position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Warmth, rest, and nourishing warm foods gently support the depleted Kidney Yang, helping to transform fluids and dispel internal cold. At the same time, deep rest and moistening foods like broths and goji berries conserve and replenish Kidney Yin, easing night sweats and dryness. Overwork, cold, and raw foods further exhaust the already weak Yang, while late nights and sexual excess drain the essence that both Yin and Yang depend on. Because this pattern is a deep dual deficiency, any stressor can tip the fragile balance into a flare of symptoms.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Prioritize warm, slow-cooked foods like bone broths, congees, and stews with black beans, walnuts, and goji berries. Avoid raw, cold, and icy foods that further damage Yang. Keep the lower back and feet warm, and rest deeply. Gentle practices like Tai Chi or restorative yoga can help rebuild vitality without depleting reserves.

## คำถามที่พบบ่อย (Frequently Asked Questions)
### Can acupuncture and herbs really help chronic kidney disease?
Yes, TCM can support kidney health by addressing the underlying imbalances that contribute to inflammation and fluid retention. Many patients experience reduced swelling, better energy, and slower disease progression. However, TCM is not a replacement for dialysis or transplant when kidney function is severely compromised. It works best as a complementary therapy, especially in earlier stages.

### What diet should I follow with chronic glomerulonephritis?
Generally, a low-salt, moderate-protein diet is recommended. TCM also advises avoiding cold, raw foods that weaken the Spleen and damp-producing foods like dairy and greasy items. Warm, cooked meals and soups are beneficial. Your practitioner will tailor advice to your specific pattern - for example, those with damp-heat may need to avoid spicy, fried foods.

### How long does it take to see results?
Most patients notice improved energy and less swelling within the first 4-8 weeks of consistent treatment. Deeper changes in kidney function markers may take 3-6 months or longer. The timeline depends on the pattern and how advanced the condition is. Patience and regular follow-ups are key.

### Are there any herbs I should avoid if I have chronic kidney disease?
Yes. Some herbs can be hard on the kidneys, so self-prescribing is dangerous. Always work with a qualified TCM practitioner who understands kidney disease. Certain herbs like aristolochic acid-containing plants are strictly avoided. Your practitioner will select kidney-safe formulas and adjust dosages based on your kidney function.

### Can I take TCM herbs along with my blood pressure medications?
Yes, but you must inform both your doctor and TCM practitioner. Some herbs may have mild diuretic or blood-pressure-lowering effects, so your medication doses may need monitoring. Never stop or adjust prescription drugs without medical guidance. Regular blood pressure checks are important.

### Does acupuncture for kidney disease hurt?
Acupuncture uses very thin needles and is generally not painful. You may feel a slight pinch or a dull ache when the needle reaches the right depth, which is a sign of Qi activation. Most patients find the sessions relaxing and report feeling more balanced afterward.
