## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนปัจจุบัน (Conventional Medicine View)
### คำอธิบาย (Description)
The heart's rhythm is controlled by electrical impulses that travel from the atria to the ventricles through the atrioventricular node. In AV block, these signals are delayed or completely blocked, leading to a slow or irregular heartbeat. First-degree AV block is usually harmless; second-degree causes occasional dropped beats; third-degree (complete heart block) can cause severe bradycardia, fainting, and requires a pacemaker. Diagnosis is made by electrocardiogram (ECG).

### การรักษาหลัก (Treatments)
Mild AV block often requires no treatment beyond monitoring. For symptomatic bradycardia, a pacemaker is the definitive treatment, especially for second-degree type II and third-degree block. In emergencies, atropine or temporary pacing may be used. There are no medications that reliably restore normal conduction long-term.

## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนจีน (Traditional Chinese Medicine View)
### ความเข้าใจเกี่ยวกับการแพทย์แผนจีน (TCM Understanding)
In TCM, the Heart is not just a pump - it houses the Shen (Spirit), the consciousness that gives us a sense of peace and steady rhythm. A normal heartbeat depends on the Heart receiving sufficient Qi and Blood to anchor the Shen, and enough Yang to drive the beat. When any of these are deficient, or when pathogenic factors like phlegm, fire, water, or blood stasis obstruct the Heart, the rhythm becomes unsteady, producing palpitations (心悸).
This is why AV block, from a TCM perspective, is not a single disease but a symptom that can stem from very different root imbalances.
A TCM practitioner listens carefully to the quality of the heartbeat - is it slow and weak, rapid and thin, or forceful and irregular? They then ask about accompanying sensations: cold limbs, fatigue, night sweats, irritability, swelling. The tongue's color and coating, along with the pulse's speed, strength, and rhythm, provide the final confirmation.
For example, a pale, puffy tongue with a deep, slow pulse points to Heart Yang Deficiency, where the heart lacks warmth to drive the beat. A red tongue with little coating and a thin, rapid pulse suggests Heart Yin Deficiency, where cooling Yin is too weak to anchor the rhythm. A thick, yellow tongue coating with a slippery, rapid pulse indicates Phlegm-Fire harassing the Heart.
The Heart does not work alone. The Spleen produces the Blood that nourishes the Heart; the Kidneys provide the foundational Yin and Yang that warm or cool the Heart; the Liver ensures smooth Qi flow so Blood can circulate.
A Spleen weakened by overwork or poor diet fails to generate enough Blood, leading to palpitations with fatigue and poor appetite (Heart and Spleen Qi and Blood Deficiency). When Kidney Yang is weak, water metabolism fails, and retained fluids surge upward to oppress the Heart, causing a pounding sensation and swelling (Water Qi intimidating the Heart). Thus, AV block often reflects a deeper systemic imbalance that TCM traces back to these organ systems.
Because the same Western diagnosis can have multiple TCM causes, treatment is highly individualized. A person with cold hands and feet and a slow pulse needs warming Yang herbs, while someone with night sweats and a rapid pulse needs nourishing Yin. This is why TCM can offer relief even when conventional options are limited - by restoring the body's own ability to maintain a steady rhythm.

### การวินิจฉัยโดยแพทย์แผนจีน (Practitioner Diagnosis)
A TCM practitioner starts by listening carefully to the quality of the heartbeat-whether it feels like a flutter, a pause, a forceful pound, or a slow, heavy thud. They then ask about accompanying sensations: fatigue, cold limbs, night sweats, or irritability. The tongue’s color and coating, along with the pulse’s speed, strength, and rhythm, provide the final confirmation of which pattern is causing the AV block.
If the palpitations come with overwhelming fatigue, poor appetite, and a pale, washed-out complexion, the root is often Heart and Spleen Qi and Blood Deficiency. The tongue looks pale with a thin white coat, and the pulse feels weak and thin. This pattern reflects the Spleen’s inability to produce enough Blood to anchor the Heart, leaving the heartbeat unsteady and easily triggered by exertion or worry.
When the heartbeat is rapid and accompanied by a restless mind, dry mouth at night, and a feeling of heat in the palms and soles, Heart Yin Deficiency is likely. The tongue appears red with little or no coating, and the pulse is thin and rapid. Here, the cooling, anchoring Yin is too weak to hold the Heart’s spirit, so the rhythm speeds up and sleep becomes shallow.
A slow, deep, or intermittent heartbeat that worsens with cold and is paired with chilly hands and feet points to Heart Yang Deficiency. The tongue is pale and puffy, and the pulse is deep, slow, and weak. In this pattern, the Heart’s warming, pushing force has dimmed, much like a furnace running low, causing the electrical signal to slow and the chest to feel oppressed.
If the palpitations are forceful and paired with a bitter taste, chest fullness, and a short temper, Phlegm-Fire may be harassing the Heart. The tongue is red with a thick, yellow, greasy coat, and the pulse is slippery and rapid. This pattern is like sticky, heated phlegm clouding the Heart’s chamber, triggering erratic beats and a sense of inner agitation.

### Heart Yang Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/heart-yang-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Cold hands and feet, feeling chilly, Bright pale or white face, Chest stuffiness and mild pain, Palpitations worsen with exertion, Profound fatigue and low energy, Mental listlessness
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Cold weather or air conditioning, Overexertion or overwork, Cold foods and iced drinks, Emotional stress and fear, Late nights and sleep loss
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warmth and warm clothing, Rest and adequate sleep, Warm, nourishing meals and drinks, Gentle movement like walking, Deep breathing
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Heart Yang is the body's internal fire that powers the heartbeat. When it weakens, the heart lacks the warmth and drive to pump blood strongly, causing a slow, irregular rhythm and palpitations. The chest feels heavy and cold, and the limbs grow icy because Yang cannot reach them. This pattern is a common root of atrioventricular block in TCM.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale, puffy, tender body with teeth marks, and a white slippery coating. Pulse: deep, weak, and slow, possibly knotted (结代 jié dài) especially at the left Cun position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Heart Yang is like a small flame that needs fuel and protection. Cold and overexertion drain that flame, making the heart sluggish and causing palpitations. Warmth, rest, and nourishing foods conserve and gently stoke the Yang, allowing the heart to beat more steadily. Gentle movement encourages circulation without exhausting the heart's reserve.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat warm, easy-to-digest foods like soups, stews, and congees. Avoid raw, cold, and icy foods that chill the body. Keep the chest and limbs warm, especially in cold weather. Gentle daily movement like walking or tai chi helps circulate Yang without overtaxing the heart. Prioritize rest and avoid overwork.

### Heart and Spleen Qi and Blood Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/heart-and-spleen-qi-and-blood-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Palpitations worse with fatigue, Poor appetite and loose stools, Insomnia with excessive dreaming, Pale complexion and lips, Forgetfulness and poor concentration
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Overexertion or overwork, Worry or excessive mental strain, Skipping meals or eating irregularly, Raw, cold, or greasy foods
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Rest and adequate sleep, Warm, nourishing meals and drinks, Gentle movement like walking, Reducing mental overwork, Warmth and warm clothing
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Your Spleen transforms food into Qi and Blood, the fuel that keeps your Heart beating steadily. When the Spleen is weakened by overwork, worry, or poor diet, it cannot produce enough nourishment. The Heart, starved of Blood, loses its anchor, leading to palpitations, a fluttering sensation, or a feeling that your heart is skipping beats.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale, puffy, teeth-marked body with thin white coating. Pulse: fine and weak, especially feeble at the left cun (Heart) and right guan (Spleen) positions.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: The Spleen needs regular, warm meals and rest to produce Qi and Blood. Overthinking and worry directly weaken the Spleen's ability to transform food into nourishment. Cold and raw foods further dampen Spleen function, while warmth and gentle activity support it, allowing the Heart to be properly nourished and the rhythm to stabilize.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat warm, easily digestible meals like congee with dates and goji berries. Avoid raw, cold, and greasy foods that weaken the Spleen. Gentle exercise such as walking or tai chi helps build Qi without exhausting it. Prioritize rest and a consistent sleep routine to allow your body to rebuild.

### Heart Yin Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/heart-yin-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Palpitations often worse at rest or at night, Insomnia with vivid, disturbing dreams, Mental restlessness and agitation, Night sweats, Feeling of heat in palms, soles, and chest, Dry mouth and throat, Malar flush (redness over cheekbones)
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Stress and overwork, Spicy, greasy or fried foods, Late nights and sleep loss, Excessive talking or mental strain, Hot or humid weather
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Rest and adequate sleep, Cooling, moistening foods, Gentle movement like walking, Calm, quiet environment
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Heart Yin is the cooling, nourishing substance that anchors the mind and keeps the heartbeat steady. When it runs low, the heart loses its quiet foundation and subtle empty-heat rises, making the mind restless and the pulse unsteady. This explains why palpitations often strike when you finally sit still or try to sleep - the deficiency is unmasked in the quiet.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red body, redder at the tip, with little or no coating, dry, and possibly a midline crack. Pulse: thin (细 xì) and rapid (数 shuò), empty on deep pressure, especially at the left cun position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: <p>Heart Yin is easily consumed by overwork, mental strain, and insufficient sleep, which explains why these triggers make palpitations worse. Spicy and fried foods generate internal heat that further dries up yin fluids. Cooling, moistening foods and adequate rest rebuild yin and quiet the empty-heat that disturbs the heart. A quiet, dark environment helps settle the mind, which in turn steadies the heartbeat.</p>
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Prioritize rest and a consistent early bedtime - staying up late directly consumes yin. Favor cooling, moistening foods like pears, lily bulb, lotus seed, and black sesame; avoid spicy, fried, or overly stimulating foods and drinks. Gentle movement such as walking or tai chi supports circulation without overheating, but intense sweating workouts can further deplete yin.

### Phlegm-Fire harassing the Heart
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/phlegm-fire-harassing-the-heart)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Mental restlessness and irritability, Bitter taste in the mouth, Chest oppression with thick yellow phlegm, Red face and red eyes, Insomnia with disturbing dreams
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Spicy, greasy or fried foods, Alcohol, Emotional stress and anger, Hot or humid weather
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Cooling, moistening foods, Calm, quiet environment, Gentle exercise like walking
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When the diet is rich in greasy, spicy foods or when emotional stress lingers, the Spleen and Stomach struggle to transform fluids. This creates Phlegm, which combines with Heat to form a sticky, fiery mass that obstructs the Heart. The Heart Spirit, agitated by the Phlegm-Fire, loses its calm rhythm, producing palpitations that feel sudden and intense, often with a sense of heat and irritability.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red body, swollen with red prickly tip, thick yellow greasy coating. Pulse: slippery and rapid, full and overflowing at the left Cun (Heart) position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Phlegm-Fire is aggravated by heating and damp-producing factors. Spicy, greasy foods and alcohol generate more Phlegm and Heat, while emotional stress stirs up Fire and disrupts the Heart. Cooling foods and a calm environment help clear Heat and settle the mind, reducing the agitation that disturbs the Heart rhythm.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Avoid greasy, fried, spicy, and sweet foods that create Phlegm and Heat. Eat cooling, light meals like congee, steamed vegetables, and moderate amounts of pear or cucumber. Manage emotional stress with meditation or gentle walking. Keep a regular sleep schedule to allow the Heart to settle.

### Water Qi intimidating the Heart
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/water-qi-intimidating-the-heart)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Swelling of lower legs or whole body, Sensation of something rushing upward from stomach to chest, Cold hands and feet, feeling cold overall, Nausea or vomiting of thin watery saliva, Worse at night or when lying down
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Cold weather or cold drinks, Lying flat, Overeating or heavy, greasy meals, Damp or humid environments, Excessive fluid intake
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warmth and warm clothing, Warm, nourishing meals and drinks, Elevating the head while resting, Gentle movement like walking, Moxibustion on lower back and abdomen
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When Spleen and Kidney Yang are too weak to transform and move fluids, water accumulates inside the body instead of being excreted. This retained water can surge upward, pressing on the Heart and disrupting its rhythm. The result is a pounding or irregular heartbeat, often accompanied by a sensation of fluid rushing from the stomach toward the chest, especially when lying down.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale, swollen, tender body with teeth marks; white, slippery, water-slick coating. Pulse: wiry and slippery (xian hua) or deep and fine (chen xi), with a notably weak left cun (Heart) and right chi (Kidney) position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Cold and dampness weaken Yang Qi further, making it harder for the body to transform and excrete fluids, so water accumulates and rushes upward. Warmth, both from food and external sources, supports Yang and encourages fluid metabolism. Lying flat allows pathological water to press on the Heart more directly, while elevating the head uses gravity to reduce that upward surge. Gentle movement circulates Qi and Yang without exhausting them, helping to mobilize retained fluids.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat warm, cooked, easily digested foods like soups and stews. Avoid cold, raw, and damp-producing items such as dairy, iced drinks, and greasy foods. Gentle daily movement helps Yang Qi circulate, but rest when fatigued. Keep the body, especially the lower back and abdomen, warm. Moxibustion on the lower back and belly can be especially supportive.

### Heart Blood Stagnation
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/heart-blood-stagnation)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Stabbing, fixed chest pain, Pain radiating to shoulder or left arm, Purple or dark lips and nails, Cold hands, Anxiety or restlessness during pain, Chest tightness or a feeling of constriction
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Emotional stress and anger, Cold weather or air conditioning, Overeating or heavy, greasy meals, Prolonged sitting or inactivity
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Gentle exercise like walking, Warmth on the chest, Light, easily digestible meals, Stress reduction
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When Qi flow becomes stuck for a long time - often from stress, unresolved emotions, or chronic illness - Blood can no longer move freely through the vessels of the Heart. This stagnation creates an actual physical obstruction, like a logjam in a river, that directly disrupts the Heart's rhythm and triggers the severe, stabbing palpitations of an AV block.
The pain is fixed and piercing because the Blood is literally trapped in one place, and the purple lips, nails, and tongue are visible signs that the circulation is deeply stagnant.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: Purple or dark-red body with stasis spots; distended, dark sublingual veins; thin white coat. Pulse: Choppy (涩, Sè) or knotted (结, Jié) at the left Cun position, reflecting Blood struggling through the vessels.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Blood stasis is like a frozen river - warmth and gentle movement thaw it, while cold and stillness make it congeal further. Stress and anger knot up Qi, which in turn traps Blood, so calming the mind is just as important as moving the body. Light, warm foods support the Spleen's ability to build healthy Blood and don't create the sticky phlegm-dampness that can worsen stagnation.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat light, warm meals that are easy to digest - stews, soups, and cooked vegetables - and avoid heavy, greasy foods that bog down circulation. Gentle daily movement like walking or tai chi is essential to keep Blood flowing, but avoid exhausting exercise. Keep the chest warm, and manage emotional stress with calming practices like deep breathing or meditation.

## คำถามที่พบบ่อย (Frequently Asked Questions)
### Can TCM cure AV block?
TCM aims to improve the underlying imbalance causing palpitations; many patients experience fewer skipped beats and less fatigue, but it does not replace a pacemaker if one is medically necessary. It's best used alongside conventional monitoring.

### Will I still need a pacemaker if I try TCM?
TCM does not reverse structural electrical blocks that require pacing. If your cardiologist recommends a pacemaker, you should follow that advice. TCM can support your overall heart health and reduce symptoms like palpitations and anxiety, but it is not a substitute for life-saving devices.

### How long until I feel less palpitations?
Many patients notice a reduction in palpitations within 4-6 weeks of consistent herbal and acupuncture treatment. The full effect depends on the pattern - excess patterns respond faster, while deficiency patterns may take several months.

### Is acupuncture safe with a heart condition?
Yes, when performed by a licensed practitioner, acupuncture is generally safe for people with heart rhythm disorders. Needles are placed at specific points that regulate the Heart's rhythm, such as Neiguan (PC-6). Always inform your practitioner and cardiologist about all treatments you are receiving.

### Can I take Chinese herbs with my heart medications?
Some herbs can interact with medications like beta-blockers, digoxin, or anticoagulants. For example, licorice (Gan Cao) may affect potassium levels and blood pressure. It is essential to provide your TCM practitioner with a complete list of your medications, and to keep your cardiologist informed. Never stop prescribed medications without medical supervision.

### What dietary changes can help with palpitations?
Generally, avoid cold, raw foods and icy drinks, which can weaken the digestive fire and contribute to phlegm-dampness. For Heart Yang Deficiency, include warming foods like ginger, cinnamon, and lamb. For Heart Yin Deficiency, emphasize moistening foods like pears, lily bulb, and black sesame. Avoid greasy, spicy, and fried foods if you have Phlegm-Fire. A TCM practitioner can give you specific advice based on your pattern.
