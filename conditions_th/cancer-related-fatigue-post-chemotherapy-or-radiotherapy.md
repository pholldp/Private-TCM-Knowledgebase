## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนปัจจุบัน (Conventional Medicine View)
### คำอธิบาย (Description)
Cancer-related fatigue is one of the most common and distressing symptoms experienced by people with cancer, particularly during and after treatments like chemotherapy and radiation. It is a persistent, overwhelming tiredness that is not relieved by rest and can significantly impair daily functioning, mood, and quality of life. The exact causes are complex and may include inflammation, anemia, hormonal changes, muscle wasting, and psychological distress. Diagnosis is usually based on the patient's report and standardized fatigue scales, as there is no single lab test.

### การรักษาหลัก (Treatments)
Standard approaches focus on managing symptoms and improving function. They may include energy conservation strategies, supervised exercise programs, cognitive behavioral therapy, nutritional support, and sometimes medications such as psychostimulants (methylphenidate) or antidepressants. Treatment is often multidisciplinary, but no single intervention works for everyone, and many patients continue to struggle with fatigue even after cancer treatment ends.

## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนจีน (Traditional Chinese Medicine View)
### ความเข้าใจเกี่ยวกับการแพทย์แผนจีน (TCM Understanding)
TCM views cancer-related fatigue as a form of "consumptive disease" (虚劳, xū láo) - a state where the body's most vital substances have been drained by the cancer itself and then further injured by aggressive treatments. The Spleen, Lungs, and Kidneys are most often involved because they are the source of Qi and Blood, the distributors of energy, and the reservoir of constitutional strength. When these organs are weakened, every system in the body suffers, leaving you profoundly exhausted.
But the quality of the tiredness tells the real story. A pale, washed-out exhaustion with heart palpitations and poor appetite points to Qi and Blood Deficiency - the raw materials of energy are simply missing. A dry-mouth fatigue with night sweats and a red tongue signals Qi and Yin Deficiency, where the body's cooling, moistening fluids have been scorched away, often by radiation. A deep, cold fatigue with a sore lower back, loose stools, and a feeling of internal chill reveals Kidney and Spleen Yang Deficiency - the body's metabolic fire is barely smoldering.
Shortness of breath and a weak voice with every little effort, alongside poor appetite and bloating, point to Spleen and Lung Qi Deficiency. Here the Spleen fails to produce enough Qi from food, and the Lungs lack the strength to distribute it, leaving you winded and drained.
Sometimes the fatigue is compounded by stagnation. When Qi and Blood become too weak to flow smoothly, they can stagnate, causing fixed pains, a dark or purplish tongue, and a fatigue that feels heavy and stuck. This is Blood and Qi Deficiency with Blood Stagnation. The tongue and pulse provide a clear map: a pale, puffy tongue with teeth marks suggests deficiency, while a purple hue or dark spots indicate stagnation. TCM treatment therefore never just "gives energy" - it reads the body's signals and supplies exactly what is missing while gently removing any blockages.

### การวินิจฉัยโดยแพทย์แผนจีน (Practitioner Diagnosis)
A TCM practitioner begins by carefully listening to how the fatigue feels and what accompanies it. Because cancer treatments deplete the body in different ways, the quality of the tiredness, along with other signs, points toward a specific pattern. The tongue and pulse provide essential confirmation.
If the person looks pale or sallow, feels weak and short of breath, and has palpitations or trouble sleeping, Qi and Blood Deficiency is likely. The tongue is pale with a thin coating, and the pulse feels thin and weak. This pattern reflects a profound depletion of the body's foundational energy and nourishment.
When fatigue is accompanied by a dry mouth, night sweats, a sensation of warmth in the palms or chest, and a red tongue with little coating, Qi and Yin Deficiency is suspected. The pulse is thin and weak, sometimes a bit rapid. Radiation therapy often damages Yin fluids, creating this combination of exhaustion and dryness.
If the fatigue comes with cold limbs, a sore lower back, loose stools, and a feeling of internal cold, the practitioner thinks of Kidney and Spleen Yang Deficiency. The tongue is pale and puffy with a white, slippery coating, and the pulse is slow and deep. This pattern emerges when the body's warming, transformative functions have weakened.
Shortness of breath, a weak cough with thin sputum, and poor appetite alongside fatigue point to Spleen and Lung Qi Deficiency. The tongue is pale with a white coating, and the pulse is weak. This is common in lung cancer patients, where both the respiratory and digestive systems lack Qi.
In later stages, deficiency can lead to stasis. Fatigue pairs with fixed, stabbing pains, a dark complexion, and a tongue that is purple with stasis spots. The pulse may feel thin and rough. This pattern shows that while the body is depleted, there is also a blockage of blood flow that needs attention.

## กลุ่มอาการการแพทย์แผนจีนและการรักษา (TCM Patterns and Treatment)

### Qi and Blood Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/qi-and-blood-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Pale or sallow yellowish complexion, Heart palpitations and poor sleep, Dizziness and lightheadedness, Shortness of breath on exertion, Poor appetite and reduced food intake, Numbness or tingling in the limbs
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Overexertion or overwork, Stress, worry, or emotional upset, Raw, cold foods and iced drinks, Irregular eating or skipping meals
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Rest and adequate sleep, Warm, cooked, nourishing meals, Gentle movement (walking, qigong, stretching), A calm, low-stress environment
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Cancer itself and the harsh effects of chemotherapy and radiotherapy heavily deplete the body's fundamental substances. Qi, the vital force that powers every movement and function, and Blood, the material that nourishes tissues and anchors the mind, become profoundly insufficient. When the Spleen, the organ responsible for extracting Qi and Blood from food, is weakened, the body cannot replenish what is lost, leading to the deep, persistent exhaustion characteristic of cancer-related fatigue.
Without enough Qi, even simple tasks feel overwhelming and shortness of breath develops. The lack of Blood fails to nourish the heart and brain, causing dizziness, palpitations, and a pale, sallow complexion. This dual deficiency creates a cycle where the body lacks both the fuel (Qi) and the nourishment (Blood) to function, making recovery from treatments extremely difficult.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale body, may be slightly thin or show teeth marks on the edges, with a thin white coating. Pulse: fine (thin), weak, and may feel empty or slow, lacking force.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Qi and Blood are the body's core resources, and in this pattern they are dangerously low. Rest and sleep conserve precious Qi and allow the Spleen to channel energy into Blood production rather than movement. Overexertion and stress burn through the body's limited reserves, deepening the deficiency. Warm, cooked foods require less digestive effort, making it easier for a weakened Spleen to extract nourishment, whereas cold and raw foods act like a blow to the digestive fire, further impairing the very system needed to rebuild strength.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Focus on warm, easily digestible, and nutrient-dense foods like congees, slow-cooked stews, and bone broths. Incorporate Qi and Blood-building ingredients such as red dates (da zao), goji berries, and a small amount of high-quality animal protein if tolerated. Avoid raw, cold, and greasy foods which further weaken the Spleen's digestive fire. Gentle practices like Qigong or restorative yoga can help move Qi without depleting it. Prioritize deep rest and sleep, as this is when the body naturally regenerates Blood.

### Qi and Yin Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/qi-and-yin-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Persistent tiredness and physical weakness, Dry mouth and throat with little desire to drink, Night sweats or spontaneous daytime sweating, Warm palms, soles, and chest, Shortness of breath, worse with exertion
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Overexertion or overwork, Spicy, fried, or drying foods, Hot, dry weather
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Rest and adequate sleep, Cool, moistening foods, Gentle movement (walking, qigong, stretching)
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Cancer itself and treatments like chemotherapy and radiotherapy heavily deplete the body’s Qi (vital energy) and Yin (cooling, moistening fluids). When Qi is deficient, you lack the energy to function and feel persistently tired and short of breath. When Yin is damaged, the body loses its ability to stay cool and hydrated, leading to dry mouth, night sweats, and a sensation of internal heat. Together, these deficiencies create a deep, lingering fatigue that rest alone does not easily fix.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red or pale-red body, thin or absent coating, possibly cracked and dry. Pulse: fine, weak, and often rapid.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Qi and Yin Deficiency means the body’s energy and cooling fluids are low. Rest and moistening foods help replenish them, while overwork and heat further drain what little is left. Gentle movement circulates Qi without depleting it, and avoiding spicy or drying inputs protects precious Yin fluids.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Prioritize gentle, nourishing, and moistening foods like congee, steamed pears, and soups with Chinese yam. Avoid spicy, fried, or overly drying foods that can further consume Yin. Gentle movement such as qigong or walking helps Qi flow without exhausting you. Protect your rest and avoid overwork, especially in hot weather.

### Kidney and Spleen Yang Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/kidney-and-spleen-yang-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Cold hands and feet, especially below the knees, Chronic loose stools or early-morning diarrhea, Sore, aching, and cold lower back and knees, Fatigue that worsens with cold and improves with warmth, Pale, swollen tongue with teeth marks and a white slippery coating
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Cold weather or drafts, Raw, cold foods and iced drinks, Overexertion or overwork, Stress, worry, or emotional upset, Late nights and lack of sleep
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warmth (heating pad, warm baths, compresses), Rest and adequate sleep, Warm, cooked, nourishing meals, Gentle movement (walking, qigong, stretching), Moxibustion on the lower back and belly
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Cancer and its treatments drain the body's deepest reserves of Yang, the warming and activating force. When Kidney Yang weakens, it can no longer fuel the Spleen's digestive fire, so food is not transformed into energy, leaving you profoundly exhausted and cold. This internal chill makes the lower back and knees ache, and the Spleen's failure to manage fluids causes loose stools and morning diarrhea.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale, swollen, and puffy with teeth marks and a white slippery coating. Pulse: deep, weak, and slow, especially feeble at the rear (Kidney) and right middle (Spleen) positions.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Kidney and Spleen Yang Deficiency is a pattern of deep internal cold and low metabolic fire. Cold, whether from weather or food, directly extinguishes that fire, worsening fatigue and digestive weakness. Rest and warmth conserve and restore Yang, while warm, cooked foods are easier for a weakened Spleen to transform into usable energy. Moxibustion and gentle movement stimulate the flow of Yang Qi without draining it, gradually rebuilding the body's core warmth.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat only warm, cooked foods like soups, stews, and congees. Avoid raw vegetables, cold drinks, and iced foods entirely. Keep the lower back and abdomen warm with a heating pad or warm clothing. Gentle daily movement like walking helps Qi flow without exhausting you, but rest when tired.

### Spleen and Lung Qi Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/spleen-and-lung-qi-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Shortness of breath that worsens with exertion, Weak, low voice and reluctance to speak, Chronic cough with thin watery phlegm, Poor appetite and abdominal bloating after eating, Loose stools, Spontaneous sweating, Catching colds easily
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Overexertion or overwork, Raw, cold foods and iced drinks, Stress, worry, or emotional upset, Cold weather or drafts, Irregular eating or skipping meals
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Rest and adequate sleep, Gentle movement (walking, qigong, stretching), Warm, cooked, nourishing meals, Warmth (heating pad, warm baths, compresses)
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Chemotherapy and radiation often damage the Spleen and Lung Qi. The Spleen is the source of Qi and Blood from food; when it is weakened, you cannot produce enough energy, leading to poor appetite, bloating, and loose stools. The Lungs govern respiration and distribute Qi throughout the body; when Lung Qi is deficient, breathing becomes shallow, the voice grows weak, and even small efforts leave you winded. Together, these two systems failing create a deep, lingering fatigue that feels like you are always running on empty.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: Pale, puffy, tender body with teeth marks and a thin white slippery coating. Pulse: Weak and forceless, especially at the right-side Cun (Lung) and Guan (Spleen) positions; feels fine and soft.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Spleen and Lung Qi deficiency means the body's engine runs on low power. Overexertion, stress, and cold foods drain that precious Qi further, while rest, warmth, and easily digestible meals conserve and slowly rebuild it. Breathing exercises directly train the Lungs, and gentle movement stimulates the Spleen without causing exhaustion.

### Blood and Qi Deficiency with Blood Stagnation
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/blood-and-qi-deficiency-with-blood-stagnation)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Fixed stabbing pain in specific locations, Pale or sallow face with a dusky, dark undertone, Purple or dark lips, Dark circles under the eyes, Numbness or tingling in the limbs
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Overexertion or overwork, Raw, cold foods and iced drinks, Prolonged sitting or inactivity, Stress, worry, or emotional upset, Cold weather or drafts
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Gentle movement (walking, qigong, stretching), Warm, cooked, nourishing meals, Warmth (heating pad, warm baths, compresses), Rest and adequate sleep
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Cancer and its treatments like chemotherapy and radiotherapy deeply deplete the body's Qi and Blood, causing a profound fatigue. When this deficiency persists, the blood becomes too weak to flow smoothly, leading to Blood Stagnation-a kind of internal traffic jam. The stagnation itself blocks the delivery of fresh Qi and nutrients, turning simple tiredness into a deep, dragging exhaustion often accompanied by fixed, stabbing pains.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale-purple body with possible stasis spots, thin white coat, and distended sublingual veins. Pulse: fine (xi) and choppy (se), like a knife scraping bamboo.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: In this pattern, Qi and Blood are already depleted, so overexertion drains them further and worsens fatigue. Cold and inactivity cause Blood to congeal, intensifying stasis and stabbing pain. Warmth, gentle movement, and nourishing foods build Qi and encourage Blood to flow again, easing both the exhaustion and the pain. Rest allows the body to rebuild, while stress tightens the vessels, making stagnation worse.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat warm, easily digested foods like bone broths with dark leafy greens and a splash of black vinegar to gently move Blood. Add small amounts of turmeric, ginger, and cinnamon to your cooking. Avoid raw, cold, or greasy foods that can congeal Blood and further deplete Spleen Qi. Gentle daily movement like walking or qigong is essential-it encourages circulation without exhausting your reserves. Apply a warm compress to painful areas to soothe stabbing pain.

## คำถามที่พบบ่อย (Frequently Asked Questions)
### Can acupuncture and herbs really help with cancer-related fatigue?
Yes, many studies and clinical experience show that TCM can significantly reduce fatigue and improve quality of life in cancer patients. Acupuncture helps regulate the nervous system and reduce inflammation, while herbal formulas directly replenish Qi, Blood, Yin, or Yang. The effect is gradual but often deep, with patients reporting not just less tiredness but also better sleep, appetite, and mood.

### Is it safe to combine TCM with chemotherapy or radiation?
When managed by a qualified TCM practitioner experienced in oncology, it is generally safe and can even help mitigate side effects like nausea, low blood counts, and fatigue. However, you must inform both your oncologist and your TCM practitioner about all treatments. Some herbs may interact with chemotherapy drugs or affect blood clotting, so professional guidance is essential. Never stop or alter your conventional treatment without medical advice.

### How long until I feel less tired?
Most patients notice a subtle lift within the first month, but deep, lasting improvement usually takes 3-6 months. This is not a quick fix - it's a rebuilding process. The body needs time to replenish its reserves, and the speed depends on the pattern and how depleted you are. Consistency with herbs and regular acupuncture sessions is key.

### Will I need to change my diet?
Yes, dietary adjustments are a fundamental part of TCM treatment. You'll be encouraged to eat warm, cooked, easily digestible foods that support the Spleen and Stomach, such as soups, congees, and steamed vegetables. Avoiding raw, cold, and greasy foods helps conserve your digestive energy. Your practitioner will give you specific recommendations based on your pattern, like adding moistening foods for Yin deficiency or warming spices for Yang deficiency.

### What if my fatigue is from a specific cancer like breast cancer?
TCM treats the person, not just the disease. While the patterns of fatigue are similar across cancer types, your treatment will be tailored to your unique presentation. For example, breast cancer patients often have a Liver Qi stagnation component, which may be addressed with additional acupuncture points or herbs. The core principle remains the same: identify the deficiency or stagnation pattern and treat accordingly.

### Can TCM help with the emotional exhaustion too?
Absolutely. In TCM, the mind and body are inseparable. The Spleen and Heart are closely linked to worry and anxiety, the Liver to frustration and depression. Many fatigue patterns include emotional symptoms, and as the physical energy improves, mental clarity and emotional resilience often follow. Acupuncture points like Shenmen (HT-7) and herbs like Long Yan Rou (Longan fruit) are specifically used to calm the spirit.

### Do the herbs have side effects?
When prescribed correctly by a trained practitioner, side effects are rare and usually mild, such as slight digestive changes that resolve as the body adjusts. However, some herbs can interact with medications or be unsuitable for certain conditions. That's why a thorough consultation, including a full medication list, is vital. Always obtain herbs from a reputable source and follow the prescribed dosage.

### How do I know which pattern I have?
While this page can give you a strong indication, a definitive diagnosis requires a TCM practitioner's examination of your tongue, pulse, and detailed symptoms. Patterns can overlap, and subtle differences matter. For example, a slightly red tongue versus a pale one can shift the treatment approach significantly. A professional diagnosis ensures you get the most effective and safe formula for your specific condition.

   *วารสารวิชาการ: Zhang Y, Lin L, Li H, et al. Acupunct Med. 2023;41(2):88-98.*
