## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนปัจจุบัน (Conventional Medicine View)
### คำอธิบาย (Description)
In Western medicine, a sensation of abdominal bloating and pain is a common symptom seen in conditions like functional dyspepsia, irritable bowel syndrome (IBS), gastritis, or simply after overeating. It's typically assessed by asking about bowel habits, diet, and stress, and may be investigated with endoscopy or imaging if red flags are present. Treatment often focuses on symptom relief - antacids, prokinetics, antispasmodics, or dietary changes - but doesn't always address the underlying triggers.

### การรักษาหลัก (Treatments)
Standard treatments include antacids or acid blockers, prokinetic drugs to speed stomach emptying, antispasmodics for cramping, and dietary modifications like avoiding gas-producing foods. For IBS, low-FODMAP diets and stress management are commonly recommended. When no structural cause is found, it's often labeled 'functional dyspepsia' and managed with symptom-based medication.

## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนจีน (Traditional Chinese Medicine View)
### ความเข้าใจเกี่ยวกับการแพทย์แผนจีน (TCM Understanding)
In TCM, the Liver is responsible for the smooth flow of Qi throughout the body. When you're stressed, frustrated, or holding in emotions, Liver Qi stagnates - it gets stuck, like a traffic jam. This stuck Qi creates a sensation of pressure and bloating, especially in the rib cage and upper abdomen. This is why emotional tension so often leads to that distended, tight feeling.
The Stomach and Spleen are the digestive team: the Stomach receives food and pushes it downward, while the Spleen transforms it into energy. When you overeat, eat too quickly, or consume heavy, greasy foods, the Stomach can't keep up. Food stagnates and ferments, producing gas and distension. Dampness from improper diet or external climate can also clog the system, creating a heavy, bloated sensation.
So the same distending pain can come from very different roots: Liver Qi stagnation from stress, Food Stagnation from overindulgence, Damp-Heat from a combination of humidity and rich food, Cold-Damp from exposure to cold and damp weather, Blood Stagnation from long-term Qi blockage, or Stomach Yang deficiency from chronic digestive weakness. Each feels different - the stress-related pain moves and shifts, the food-related pain is fixed and comes with sour belching, the Cold-Damp pain feels heavy and improves with warmth. TCM treatment targets the specific mechanism, not just the symptom.

### การวินิจฉัยโดยแพทย์แผนจีน (Practitioner Diagnosis)
A TCM practitioner starts by asking what the distending pain (胀痛) feels like, where it sits, and what makes it better or worse. The quality of the pain - whether it moves around, feels heavy, or is fixed and stabbing - gives the first clues. They also check the tongue coating, pulse, and any digestive or emotional signs that accompany the pain.
If the pain comes and goes, shifts from one spot to another, and flares up with stress or frustration, Liver Qi Stagnation is the likely pattern. The tongue may look normal or have slightly red edges, and the pulse often feels wiry and tight, like a guitar string.
When the distension appears after a heavy meal, along with belching, sour regurgitation, and a feeling of fullness that won’t shift, it points to Food Stagnation in the Stomach. The tongue coating is thick and greasy, and the pulse feels slippery, like beads rolling.
A heavy, oppressive distending pain with nausea, a sticky taste, and a sensation of incomplete bowel movements suggests Damp-Heat in the Stomach. The tongue has a thick, yellow, greasy coating, and the pulse is rapid and slippery, reflecting heat and dampness obstructing the Middle Burner.
If the pain is worse with cold weather or cold drinks and improves with warmth or a hot compress, Cold-Damp invading the Spleen is often the cause. The tongue coating is white and greasy, and the pulse can feel slow or tight, showing that cold and damp are chilling the digestive system.
A fixed, stabbing pain that feels like a needle and gets worse at night or with pressure signals Blood Stagnation. The tongue may appear dark purple with stasis spots, and the pulse is often choppy or wiry. This pattern often develops after long-standing Qi stagnation.
When the distending pain is mild and chronic, feels better with warmth and gentle pressure, but worsens after eating, Stomach Yang Deficient and Cold is the underlying pattern. The tongue is pale and swollen, and the pulse is deep and weak, indicating that the digestive fire is too low to move Qi properly.

### Liver Qi Stagnation
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/liver-qi-stagnation)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Distending, moving pain, Rib-side and abdominal fullness, Worse with stress and frustration, Frequent sighing, Irritability, mood swings
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Emotional stress, Greasy, heavy, or fried foods, Lack of movement or prolonged sitting, Cold or raw foods and iced drinks, Premenstrual tension
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Deep breathing and sighing, Gentle movement or walking, Warmth on the abdomen, Emotional release or relaxation, Small, frequent meals
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  The Liver is responsible for keeping Qi flowing smoothly throughout the body. When stress, frustration, or unexpressed emotions build up, the Liver Qi gets stuck and can no longer move freely. This stagnation creates a buildup of pressure that feels like distension and pain, especially in the ribs, flanks, and abdomen, and the pain often shifts because the trapped Qi wanders.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: normal or slightly dark body with red or slightly purple sides and a thin white coat. Pulse: wiry (弦 xián), taut like a guitar string, especially at the left middle position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Stress and frustration directly aggravate Liver Qi stagnation, which is the root of this pattern, so emotional upset makes the distension and pain worse. Gentle movement, deep breathing, and sighing help to get Qi moving again, relieving the stuck sensation. Cold and heavy foods further obstruct Qi flow, while warm, light meals and external warmth help relax the Liver and promote smooth flow.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Make stress relief part of your daily routine-gentle walking, yoga, or meditation can prevent Qi from stagnating. Eat warm, light meals and avoid heavy, greasy foods that clog Qi. Add mildly pungent foods like peppermint, fennel, and citrus peel to help get things moving.

### Food Stagnation in the Stomach
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/food-stagnation-in-the-stomach)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Sour, rotten-smelling belching, Bloating and pain worse after eating, better after vomiting, Aversion to food and its smell, Foul-smelling flatulence like rotten eggs, Thick, greasy tongue coating
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Overeating, Greasy, heavy, or fried foods, Cold or raw foods and iced drinks, Eating when stressed or upset
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Vomiting or passing gas, Light, bland diet, Warm foods and drinks, Gentle abdominal massage
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When you overeat or eat too quickly, the Stomach cannot process the food efficiently. Undigested food sits and ferments, creating a physical blockage that stops Qi from moving downward. This trapped Qi inflates the upper abdomen like a balloon, causing the bloated, distending pain that defines food stagnation.
The pain is often worse with pressure or after another meal, because the already clogged Stomach is being asked to handle even more. Relief comes when the body forcefully expels the contents through belching, vomiting, or passing gas.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: normal body with a thick, greasy, curd-like coating concentrated in the center. Pulse: slippery (滑 huá) and replete, especially forceful at the right middle (Stomach) position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Overeating overwhelms the Stomach's capacity, turning food into a stagnant mass that blocks Qi and causes distending pain. Vomiting or belching physically expels this obstruction, giving immediate relief. Warm, light foods like congee are easy for the Spleen and Stomach to transform, so they nourish without adding burden. Massage and gentle movement encourage the stuck Qi to descend, helping the digestive system empty itself naturally.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Give your Stomach a break. Skip a meal or eat only light congee until the bloating subsides. Chew food thoroughly and avoid overeating in the future. Steer clear of greasy, fried, and very cold foods that are hard to digest. A short, gentle walk after meals can help Qi descend, but avoid lying down right after eating.

### Damp-Heat in the Stomach
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/damp-heat-in-the-stomach)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Distending, heavy, burning pain in the upper belly, Nausea and poor appetite, Feeling of heaviness in the body and limbs, Sticky or greasy taste in the mouth and bad breath, Thirst with little desire to drink, Loose, sticky stools that feel incomplete, Thick, yellow, greasy tongue coating
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Greasy, heavy, or fried foods, Alcohol, Hot, humid weather, Overeating, Emotional stress
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Light, bland diet, Cool, dry weather, Gentle movement or walking, Resting in a cool place
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When Dampness and Heat combine in the Stomach, they create a sticky obstruction that blocks the normal downward flow of Stomach Qi. This trapped Qi causes the upper abdomen to feel full, heavy, and distended, often with a burning discomfort. The Dampness adds a sensation of heaviness and sluggishness, while the Heat contributes the burning pain, bad breath, and thirst.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red body, possibly swollen with prickles in the centre, thick yellow greasy coating. Pulse: slippery (Hua) and rapid (Shu), especially at the right Guan position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Damp-Heat thrives on rich, greasy, and spicy foods that add more Heat and Dampness to the body, while alcohol and hot, humid weather further aggravate the condition. Light, bland foods and a cool environment help clear Heat and dry Dampness. Gentle movement encourages Qi to flow, preventing stagnation that leads to distension, and supports the Spleen in transforming fluids. Emotional stress tightens the Liver Qi, which can then invade the Stomach and worsen the obstruction.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Avoid greasy, spicy, fried, and sweet foods, as well as alcohol, which all generate Dampness and Heat. Eat light, easily digestible meals like congee, steamed vegetables, and small portions of lean protein. Keep meals small and frequent rather than large and heavy. Stay in a cool, dry environment and avoid hot, humid conditions. Gentle daily movement, like walking or stretching, helps Qi flow and transforms Dampness.

### Cold-Damp invading the Spleen
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/cold-damp-invading-the-spleen)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Dull, cold, heavy distending pain in the upper abdomen, Worse with cold weather or cold foods, better with warmth, Heavy sensation in the body and limbs, head feels wrapped, Poor appetite, loose or watery stools, Thick white greasy tongue coating, pale swollen tongue with teeth marks
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Cold or raw foods and iced drinks, Damp, chilly weather, Greasy, heavy, or fried foods, Overeating
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warmth on the abdomen, Warm foods and drinks, Gentle abdominal massage, Dry, warm weather
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When external Cold and Dampness invade the body, they obstruct the Spleen's ability to transform and move fluids and food. The Spleen Qi becomes stuck, and the Middle Burner fills with stagnant Dampness, creating a sensation of bloating and distending pain. Because Cold contracts and slows movement, the pain feels dull and heavy, and it worsens with cold while warmth brings relief.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale, swollen body with teeth marks and a thick, white, greasy coating that is excessively moist. Pulse: soggy (Ru) or slippery (Hua) and slow (Chi), especially weak in the right Guan position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Cold contracts and congeals, while Dampness is heavy and obstructive, so they easily block the Spleen's Qi and cause distending pain. External cold and damp environments or cold foods introduce more of these pathogens, making the stagnation worse. Warmth, on the other hand, stimulates Qi movement, helps the Spleen transform fluids, and disperses Cold, which is why a hot water bottle, warm meals, and warming spices bring quick relief.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat warm, cooked foods like soups, stews, and congees. Add warming spices such as fresh ginger, cinnamon, and a small amount of black pepper. Strictly avoid cold drinks, raw salads, and dairy, which all worsen Dampness. Keep the abdomen warm with a hot water bottle and avoid damp, chilly environments.

### Blood Stagnation
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/blood-stagnation)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Fixed, stabbing pain that does not move, Pain worsens at night, Dark purple tongue or stasis spots, Dark menstrual blood with clots, Pain worse with pressure
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Cold weather or drafts, Emotional stress, Lack of movement or prolonged sitting, Greasy, heavy, or fried foods
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warmth, Gentle movement or walking, Rest
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When Qi stagnation persists, the flow of blood becomes obstructed, much like a traffic jam that solidifies over time. This leads to a fixed, stabbing distending pain because the stagnant blood presses on the surrounding tissues. The pain worsens at night when circulation naturally slows, and the tongue develops a purple hue with dark spots as a visible sign of the stasis.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: Purple or dusky body with stasis spots and distended dark sublingual veins. Pulse: Choppy (Se), rough and uneven, or wiry (Xian) if Qi stagnation is present.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Cold congeals blood and worsens stasis, so warmth eases it. Movement helps circulate Qi and blood, while rest prevents further stagnation. Stress tightens the Liver Qi, which can lead to blood stasis, so calmness helps.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat warm, cooked foods and avoid cold, raw items that congeal blood. Gentle exercise like walking or tai chi helps move Qi and blood. Avoid stress, which can worsen stagnation.

### Stomach Yang Deficient and Cold
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/stomach-yang-deficient-and-cold)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Dull, cold aching distension in the upper belly, Pain worsens after eating even small amounts, Strong preference for hot food and drinks, Loose stools, Cold hands and feet, Fatigue and lack of energy, Pain relieved by warmth and pressure
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Cold or raw foods and iced drinks, Overeating, Cold weather or drafts, Eating late at night, Physical overexertion
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warm foods and drinks, Gentle pressure or heat on the belly, Rest, Small, frequent meals
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  The Stomach relies on Yang (warming energy) to digest food and push Qi downward. When Stomach Yang is weak, internal Cold develops and Qi movement slows to a crawl. Food sits heavy, creating a dull, distending ache that feels better with warmth and gentle pressure. The discomfort typically worsens after eating because the already-deficient Yang is further taxed, and the body may try to expel unprocessed fluids as clear vomit or loose stools.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale, puffy, tender body with teeth marks; white slippery coating. Pulse: deep, slow, weak, especially at the right Guan (middle) position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Stomach Yang is the digestive fire. Cold foods and overeating demand more warmth than a deficient Stomach can supply, so Qi stagnates and distension worsens. Warmth, gentle pressure, and rest conserve and support that weak fire, helping Qi move downward and relieving the dull ache. Small, warm meals give the Stomach just enough to digest without overwhelming it.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat mostly warm, cooked foods like soups, stews, and congees; avoid raw, cold, and iced items. Sip ginger tea between meals. Eat small, frequent portions rather than large meals. Keep the abdomen warm with a heat pack or warm compress, and avoid sitting on cold surfaces. Gentle daily walking helps Qi move without overtaxing the digestion.

## คำถามที่พบบ่อย (Frequently Asked Questions)
### What does distending pain feel like in TCM terms?
It's a sensation of fullness, bloating, and pressure that can be mild or severe. In TCM, it's a sign that Qi is stuck and not flowing properly. You might feel it in your upper abdomen, rib area, or lower belly, and it often comes with belching, gas, or a feeling of heaviness.

### Can acupuncture really help with bloating?
Yes. Acupuncture points like Zusanli ST-36, Zhongwan REN-12, and Taichong LR-3 are commonly used to move Qi and relieve distension. Many patients feel a release during the session, and regular treatments can reduce the frequency and severity of bloating episodes.

### Do I need to change my diet when taking Chinese herbs?
Generally, yes. While herbs work to rebalance your system, continuing to eat foods that cause stagnation (greasy, cold, raw, or overly rich) will work against the treatment. Your practitioner will give you specific advice, but eating warm, cooked, simple meals is a good baseline.

### Is distending pain always related to digestion?
Not always. While most cases involve the Stomach and Spleen, Liver Qi stagnation from stress can cause distension even without food issues. It's common to feel bloated during emotional stress. TCM looks at the whole picture.

### How long does it take to see results?
Acute bloating from a heavy meal might resolve in a day or two with herbs. Chronic distending pain that's been present for months or years can take several weeks to months of consistent treatment. The timeline depends on the pattern and your overall health.

### Can I combine TCM with my IBS medication?
In most cases, yes. Herbs and acupuncture can complement conventional treatments. However, always inform both your TCM practitioner and your doctor about all medications and supplements you're taking. Some herbs can interact with drugs, especially those affecting motility or acid production.

   This meta-analysis of 17 RCTs found that acupuncture significantly improved abdominal pain and bloating in IBS patients compared to sham acupuncture and pharmacological treatments, with a low risk of adverse events.

   Review of 28 RCTs showing that Chinese herbal formulas, particularly those moving Qi and harmonizing the Stomach, were superior to placebo in relieving postprandial fullness, bloating, and epigastric pain, with Xiao Yao San and Chai Hu Shu Gan San among the most studied.
