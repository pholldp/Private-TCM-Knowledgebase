## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนปัจจุบัน (Conventional Medicine View)
### คำอธิบาย (Description)
In conventional medicine, a sensation of heat in the head without a measurable fever is often attributed to anxiety, stress, or hormonal fluctuations like hot flashes. If accompanied by fever, it may indicate an infection. Neurological causes such as migraine or trigeminal neuralgia can also produce a feeling of warmth or heat in the head. Diagnosis typically involves ruling out serious conditions through physical examination, blood tests, and imaging when needed.

### การรักษาหลัก (Treatments)
Treatment depends on the suspected cause: antibiotics for infections, hormone therapy or lifestyle adjustments for menopausal hot flashes, stress management or anxiolytics for anxiety, and migraine-specific medications for headache-related heat. Over-the-counter pain relievers may be used for discomfort, but when no clear pathology is found, patients are often simply advised to reduce stress.

## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนจีน (Traditional Chinese Medicine View)
### ความเข้าใจเกี่ยวกับการแพทย์แผนจีน (TCM Understanding)
TCM sees a sensation of heat in the head as a sign that heat - whether from an external invasion or an internal organ imbalance - is rising to the uppermost part of the body. The head is the meeting place of all the Yang channels, so heat, which is Yang in nature, naturally ascends there. When the body’s defensive Qi battles a Wind-Heat pathogen, the heat rushes to the head, causing a sudden feverish sensation. When internal organs like the Liver or Stomach generate excess heat, that heat flares upward along their channels, producing a more intense or persistent head heat.
The Liver is especially important because it stores Blood and ensures the smooth flow of Qi. Chronic frustration or unexpressed anger can cause Liver Qi to stagnate and transform into Fire, which blazes up to the head and creates a throbbing, burning heat that is often felt at the temples. If the Liver’s Yin and Blood are depleted, they can no longer anchor the Liver’s Yang, and this unmoored Yang surges upward, causing a distending, pulsating heat that worsens with stress or fatigue.
The Stomach channel also travels to the face and head. When Stomach Heat becomes excessive - from a high fever or a diet of rich, spicy foods - it sends intense heat upward along the Bright Yang pathway, often with a flushed face and raging thirst. Phlegm-Heat forms when dampness and heat combine, creating a sticky, turbid substance that rises to the head, producing a heavy, stuffy heat rather than a sharp one.
Finally, when the body’s cooling Yin is deficient, empty heat drifts upward, causing a mild but persistent heat sensation that typically worsens in the evening.
Because so many different patterns can produce head heat, TCM treatment is never one-size-fits-all. A practitioner uses the quality of the heat, the tongue, the pulse, and accompanying symptoms to pinpoint which pattern is at play, and then selects acupuncture points and herbal formulas to address that specific imbalance.

### การวินิจฉัยโดยแพทย์แผนจีน (Practitioner Diagnosis)
A TCM practitioner begins by asking about the quality of the heat sensation-whether it is sharp and sudden, dull and heavy, or a persistent low-grade warmth-and when it appears. These details, along with accompanying signs and the look of the tongue and pulse, quickly point toward one of several distinct patterns.
If the head heat comes on quickly with a fever, sore throat, and a floating rapid pulse, it suggests Wind-Heat invasion. The tongue will be red with a thin yellow coating, and the heat is often accompanied by sweating and thirst, pointing to an external pathogen that needs to be released.
When the sensation is intensely hot, almost like fire rising to the head, and the person is irritable, red-faced, and may have a bitter taste in the mouth, Liver Fire Blazing is likely. The pulse will be wiry and rapid, and the tongue red with a yellow coat, reflecting internal fire flaring upward.
A distending or pulsating heat, often with dizziness, tinnitus, and a feeling of pressure, points to Liver Yang Rising. The tongue may be red with a thin yellow coat, and the pulse is wiry-sometimes wiry and rapid if heat is prominent. This pattern is common in people under stress or with high blood pressure.
If the head heat is accompanied by a high fever, intense thirst, profuse sweating, and a flooding pulse, Bright Yang Stomach Heat is the cause. The tongue is red with a dry yellow coating. This pattern reflects excessive heat in the Stomach channel that surges upward to the head, often during an acute febrile illness.
A heavy, stuffy heat sensation in the head, together with a feeling of foggy thinking, chest oppression, and nausea, indicates Phlegm-Heat. The tongue will have a greasy yellow coating, and the pulse is slippery and rapid. This pattern arises when phlegm and heat combine and obstruct the clear orifices.
A mild but nagging head heat that worsens in the evening or at night, with a dry mouth, a red tongue with little coating, and a thready rapid pulse, signals Empty-Heat from Yin Deficiency. The body’s cooling yin is insufficient, allowing heat to rise, but the sensation is not as intense as in full heat patterns.

### Wind-Heat
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/wind-heat)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Fever with mild chills or aversion to wind, Sore, red throat, Distending or throbbing head heat sensation, Thirst with desire to drink, Nasal congestion with yellow or thick discharge, Sweating that does not relieve heat
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Spicy, fried, greasy, or roasted foods, Alcohol, Exposure to wind or drafts, Overexertion or strenuous activity, Hot, stuffy environment
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Cold or cool drinks, Rest in a quiet, calm environment, Cool compress on the forehead or head, Light, breathable clothing
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Wind-Heat is an external pathogen that enters through the nose and mouth, first attacking the body's surface. Because Heat is a Yang pathogen and Wind tends to rise, they combine to rush upward into the head, creating a sensation of heat, distension, and pressure. The defensive Qi fights back at the surface, producing fever and a floating, rapid pulse.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red tip and edges with thin yellow (or white-to-yellow) coating. Pulse: floating and rapid.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Wind-Heat is a Yang pathogen that invades from the outside, so anything that adds more Heat (spicy food, alcohol, hot environments) or lets Wind penetrate (drafts) will worsen it. Rest and cooling measures help the body push the pathogen back out through the surface, while light, cool fluids gently clear Heat without trapping the pathogen inside.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Drink plenty of cool fluids like chrysanthemum or peppermint tea, and eat light, cooling foods such as pear and cucumber. Avoid spicy, fried, and greasy foods. Rest in a well-ventilated but draught-free room, and keep the head cool without chilling the neck.

### Liver Fire Blazing
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/liver-fire-blazing)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Throbbing headache at temples or crown, Feeling of intense heat in the head or face, Red, flushed face, Bitter taste in the mouth, Irritability and quick temper, Red, painful, or burning eyes, Dark yellow urine and constipation
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Anger, frustration, or irritability, Alcohol and spicy meals, Hot weather or prolonged heat exposure, Late nights and overwork
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Cooling foods and drinks, Rest in a quiet, calm environment, Gentle exercise or movement
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When the Liver Qi stagnates - often from unexpressed anger or chronic frustration - it can generate intense internal Fire. Because Fire has a natural tendency to flare upward, this excess Heat races along the Liver channel into the head, producing a powerful sensation of heat, throbbing pain, and pressure.
This upward surge also disturbs the mind and senses, leading to the irritability, red face, and bitter taste that are classic signs of this pattern. The head feels hot not from an external invader, but from a deep internal blaze that needs to be cooled and drained.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red body with redder sides, dry yellow coating, possible red prickles. Pulse: wiry (弦 xián) and rapid (数 shuò), full and forceful especially at the left middle (Guan) position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Anger and emotional stress are the classic triggers that cause Liver Qi to stagnate and then blaze upward, so calming the mind and body directly counteracts the root mechanism. Cooling foods and drinks help drain the excess Fire, while rest and gentle movement prevent Qi from getting stuck and igniting again. Avoiding alcohol, spice, and overheating removes the fuels that feed the Fire, allowing the head to feel clear and cool.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Cool the body with chrysanthemum or peppermint tea, mung bean soup, and plenty of cucumber and celery. Avoid alcohol, coffee, spicy foods, and fried dishes, which all fan the flames of Liver Fire. Practice gentle, consistent stress relief - deep breathing, walking, or tai chi - to keep Liver Qi moving smoothly and prevent stagnation from igniting into Fire.

### Liver Yang Rising
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/liver-yang-rising)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Distending or throbbing head heat sensation, Heat felt mainly at the temples or top of the head, Dizziness or a feeling that the head is heavy and the feet are light, Irritability and quick temper, Soreness and weakness of the lower back and knees, Feeling of intense heat in the head or face
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Stress and frustration, Anger, frustration, or irritability, Alcohol, Spicy, fried, greasy, or roasted foods, Late nights and overwork, Hot, stuffy environment
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Rest in a quiet, calm environment, Cool compress on the forehead or head, Cooling foods and drinks, Gentle exercise or movement, Deep, slow breathing
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When Liver Yin and Blood are depleted, they can no longer anchor the Liver's Yang energy. This unmoored Yang surges upward to the head, creating a sensation of heat that often feels distending or throbbing. The heat is a sign of excess rising from an underlying deficiency, which is why it often comes with dizziness and a feeling of unsteadiness.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red body with redder sides, a thin dry yellow or scanty coat, and may tremble slightly. Pulse: wiry (弦 xián), and may also be fine and rapid.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Emotional stress and anger directly stir Liver Qi, causing Yang to flare upward and intensifying the head heat. Alcohol and spicy foods add heat to the body, further fueling the rising Yang, while overwork and poor sleep drain Yin, leaving even less to anchor the Yang. Rest, cooling foods, and calm environments help settle the Yang and preserve Yin, which is why they bring relief. Gentle movement encourages smooth Qi flow without the agitation that triggers a surge.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Prioritize rest and a regular sleep schedule-lack of sleep depletes Yin and fuels Yang. Favor cooling, Yin-nourishing foods like pear, spinach, cucumber, sesame, and black beans. Avoid alcohol, coffee, and spicy or greasy foods that add heat. Gentle, grounding practices like tai chi or walking help smooth the flow of Qi without overstimulating it.

### Bright Yang Stomach Heat
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/bright-yang-stomach-heat)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: High fever with no chills, Thirst with desire to drink, Sweating that does not relieve heat, Red, flushed face, Feeling of intense heat in the head or face
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Spicy, fried, greasy, or roasted foods, Alcohol, Overexertion or strenuous activity, Hot weather or prolonged heat exposure, Anger, frustration, or irritability
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Cool environment or well-ventilated room, Cold or cool drinks, Rest in a quiet, calm environment, Cool compress on the forehead or head, Light, breathable clothing
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  The Stomach channel of the Bright Yang runs up to the face and head. When Stomach Heat becomes excessive, it flares upward along this pathway, sending intense heat directly to the head. This creates a powerful, whole-head heat sensation that often accompanies a high fever, profuse sweating that fails to cool, and a raging thirst. Because the heat is deep and internal, it cannot be relieved by sweating alone.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red body with a yellow, dry coating; early cracks may appear. Pulse: overflowing and surging (Hong, 洪), rapid (Shu, 数), strong in all positions, especially the right middle position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Excess Stomach Heat is an interior, full heat pattern. Cooling foods, drinks, and environments directly counteract the heat, while hot, spicy, and stimulating things add fuel to the fire. Rest and emotional calm prevent further Yang agitation, allowing the body to cool down.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Avoid hot, spicy, and greasy foods, as well as alcohol and coffee, which add more heat. Drink plenty of cool water and eat cooling foods like watermelon, cucumber, and mung bean soup. Rest in a cool, well-ventilated room and wear light clothing. A cool compress on the forehead can provide relief.

### Phlegm-Heat
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/phlegm-heat)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Heavy, stuffy sensation of heat in the head, Feeling of oppression or fullness in the chest, Coughing up thick, sticky, yellow phlegm or a sensation of something stuck in the throat, Nausea or vomiting, Restlessness and irritability
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Greasy, heavy, or sweet foods, Overeating, Damp or humid environment, Alcohol
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Light, easily digestible meals, Cool environment or well-ventilated room, Gentle exercise or movement
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Phlegm forms when fluids stagnate, often from a diet rich in heavy or greasy foods. Over time this stagnant dampness combines with internal Heat, creating a sticky, turbid substance that rises to the head. The result is a head heat sensation that feels heavy and stuffy rather than sharp, because the Phlegm-Heat is literally clogging the clear orifices and weighing the head down.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red body, swollen, with a thick yellow greasy coating. Pulse: slippery (滑 huá) and rapid (数 shuò).
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Greasy and heavy foods directly generate the dampness that congeals into Phlegm, while Heat from overeating or alcohol stokes the fire component. Light meals and ventilation reduce the damp-heat load, and gentle movement keeps Qi and fluids circulating so they don't stagnate into more Phlegm.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Avoid greasy, fried, sweet, and dairy-heavy foods that create more phlegm. Eat light, easily digestible meals with plenty of lightly cooked vegetables. Sip warm water or cooling herbal teas like chrysanthemum throughout the day, and keep your living space well-ventilated to avoid dampness accumulating.

### Empty-Heat caused by Yin Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/empty-heat-caused-by-yin-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Head heat worse in the evening or at night, Night sweats, Dry mouth and throat, desire to sip water, Heat sensation in the palms, soles, and chest, Flushed cheekbones (malar flush), Restlessness and difficulty sleeping
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Spicy, fried, greasy, or roasted foods, Alcohol and caffeine, Late nights and overwork, Stress and frustration, Hot weather or prolonged heat exposure
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Rest in a quiet, calm environment, Adequate sleep, especially before midnight, Hydration with small, frequent sips, Cooling foods and drinks
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Yin is the body's cooling and moistening substance. When Yin runs low - often from overwork, chronic stress, or late nights - it can no longer anchor the body's natural warmth. That warmth drifts upward as empty heat, rising to the head and creating a mild but nagging sensation of heat that feels internal rather than from a fever. Because Yin belongs to the night, the feeling typically worsens in the evening or at night.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red, thin, dry body with cracks, little or no coating. Pulse: fine (thin) and rapid, weak on deep pressure.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Yin is the body's cooling reserve. Spicy and fried foods, alcohol, and late nights burn through Yin, allowing empty heat to flare upward. Rest, especially before midnight, rebuilds Yin. Cooling, moistening foods and a calm environment gently anchor the body's warmth so it no longer rushes to the head.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Build Yin with moistening, cooling foods like pear, tofu, cucumber, and congee. Avoid spicy, fried, and roasted foods, and keep alcohol and coffee to a minimum. Go to bed before 11 p.m. - the hours before midnight are the most Yin-restoring. Gentle evening walks and calming activities help settle the floating heat.

## คำถามที่พบบ่อย (Frequently Asked Questions)
### Why does my head feel hot but I have no fever?
In TCM, internal heat doesn’t always show up on a thermometer. Your head can feel hot because of Liver Fire, rising Liver Yang, Phlegm-Heat, or Yin Deficiency - all of which generate heat deep inside the body that rises to the head without necessarily raising your core temperature. A TCM practitioner can tell the difference by looking at your tongue, pulse, and other symptoms.

### Can acupuncture really cool down my head?
Yes. Acupuncture points on the head, hands, and feet can clear heat from specific channels and organs. For example, needling Fengchi (GB-20) and Hegu (LI-4) helps disperse Wind-Heat, while Taichong (LR-3) and Xingjian (LR-2) drain Liver Fire. Many patients feel a release of heat and pressure during or right after a session.

### How quickly will Chinese herbs work for head heat?
It depends on the pattern. For acute Wind-Heat, herbs like Bo He and Jin Yin Hua can bring relief within a day or two. For chronic Liver Fire or Phlegm-Heat, you might notice improvement within a week, but full resolution usually takes several weeks of consistent use. Yin Deficiency patterns take longer because the herbs are rebuilding your body’s cooling reserves.

### Is head heat related to high blood pressure?
In TCM, the pattern of Liver Yang Rising - which often causes a distending, throbbing head heat - is closely associated with hypertension. If you experience this type of head heat along with dizziness or a flushed face, it’s wise to have your blood pressure checked. TCM herbs and acupuncture can help, but always coordinate with your doctor if you are on blood pressure medication.

### Can I take TCM herbs with my blood pressure medication?
Generally yes, but with caution. Some herbs that clear Liver Heat or anchor Liver Yang may gently lower blood pressure, so if you take antihypertensives, your dose might need adjustment. Always inform both your TCM practitioner and your prescribing doctor about all medications and herbs you are taking, and monitor your blood pressure regularly.

### What foods should I avoid if my head feels hot?
Avoid spicy, fried, and greasy foods, as well as alcohol and coffee - these add heat to the body. Instead, favour cooling foods like cucumber, watermelon, mung beans, chrysanthemum tea, and pear. Eating light, easily digestible meals and staying well hydrated also helps prevent heat from building up.

### Does stress really cause head heat?
Yes, from a TCM perspective, stress is one of the most common triggers. Emotional strain - especially anger and frustration - causes Liver Qi to stagnate, which over time transforms into Fire that rises to the head. This is why many people feel their head get hot and tight during arguments or periods of high pressure.

### Is head heat a sign of something serious?
It can be. While most head heat sensations are benign and related to treatable TCM patterns, you should seek urgent medical care if you experience a sudden, severe headache unlike any before, a high fever with a stiff neck, confusion, vision changes, or head heat after a head injury. Please see the Safety section below for a complete list of red flags.

## การวิจัยทางวิทยาศาสตร์สมัยใหม่ (Modern Scientific Research)
1. **Acupuncture for tension-type headache: a systematic review and meta-analysis**
   *วารสารวิชาการ: Linde K, Allais G, Brinkhaus B, et al. Acupuncture for tension-type headache. Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews. 2016; Issue 4. Art. No.: CD007587.*
   This review included 12 RCTs and found that acupuncture was significantly more effective than sham acupuncture in reducing headache frequency and intensity. While head heat sensation was not measured separately, many participants reported associated symptoms including head heat, which improved with treatment.

2. **Tian Ma Gou Teng Yin for essential hypertension: a systematic review of randomized controlled trials**
   *วารสารวิชาการ: Wang J, Xiong X. Evidence-based Chinese medicine for hypertension. Evidence-Based Complementary and Alternative Medicine. 2013; 2013: 978398.*
   This review of 18 RCTs found that Tian Ma Gou Teng Yin combined with conventional therapy lowered blood pressure and improved symptoms such as head heat sensation, dizziness, and irritability more than conventional therapy alone. The effect on head heat was reported as a secondary outcome in several trials.
