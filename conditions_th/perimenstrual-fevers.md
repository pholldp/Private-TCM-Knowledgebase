## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนปัจจุบัน (Conventional Medicine View)
### คำอธิบาย (Description)
In Western medicine, low-grade fevers that occur only around menstruation are not a distinct diagnosis but are often noted as part of premenstrual syndrome (PMS) or premenstrual dysphoric disorder (PMDD). The rise in body temperature after ovulation is well understood - progesterone has a thermogenic effect, raising basal body temperature by about 0.5°F until menstruation begins. However, some women experience a more pronounced or prolonged temperature elevation, sometimes with flu-like aches, that can't be fully explained by progesterone alone.
Doctors may investigate for underlying conditions like endometriosis, pelvic inflammatory disease, or autoimmune flares that coincide with the menstrual cycle, but often no specific cause is found and the symptom is managed supportively.

### การรักษาหลัก (Treatments)
When perimenstrual fevers are mild and no underlying disease is identified, conventional care typically focuses on symptom relief. Over-the-counter anti-inflammatories like ibuprofen can reduce fever and any associated achiness. If the fevers are part of a broader PMS picture, hormonal contraceptives may be prescribed to suppress ovulation and stabilize hormonal fluctuations. For women with diagnosed conditions like endometriosis or pelvic infection, treatment targets the root disease. However, for many women, these fevers remain an unexplained monthly nuisance with few targeted medical options.

## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนจีน (Traditional Chinese Medicine View)
### ความเข้าใจเกี่ยวกับการแพทย์แผนจีน (TCM Understanding)
Menstruation is a time of profound internal shift in TCM. The body directs Qi and Blood downward to the uterus to shed the lining, which temporarily leaves the body's outer defenses (Wei Qi) slightly weaker and the internal organs more vulnerable to imbalance. If there is already an underlying disharmony - whether from stress, overwork, or constitutional weakness - this monthly shift can trigger a fever as the body struggles to maintain equilibrium.
The timing of the fever within your cycle is the first and most important clue: before the period points to stagnation and heat, during the period often signals heat in the blood or an external invasion, and after the period suggests deficiency.
The Liver is central to many cases. Its job is to ensure the smooth flow of Qi and Blood, especially in the days leading up to menstruation. Emotional stress, frustration, or long-held tension can cause Liver Qi to stagnate, which then generates heat. That heat rises upward, causing a premenstrual fever accompanied by irritability, breast distension, and a bitter taste in the mouth.
This is an excess pattern - the body has too much heat that needs to be cleared and a stuck flow that needs to be soothed.
On the other side of the spectrum, deficiency patterns arise when the body's cooling, moistening resources (Yin and Blood) are already low. The Kidney system stores Yin, and if it's depleted by years of overwork, late nights, or chronic illness, the monthly blood loss can tip the balance, leaving nothing to anchor the body's heat. This produces a low-grade afternoon fever, night sweats, and a restless warmth in the palms and soles - a picture of empty heat.
Other patterns include Blood Stagnation, where sluggish pelvic circulation creates friction and heat during menstruation, and external Wind-Cold, where the temporary weakness of the defenses allows a cold pathogen to enter, producing fever with chills and body aches.

### การวินิจฉัยโดยแพทย์แผนจีน (Practitioner Diagnosis)
A TCM practitioner first asks when the fever appears - before, during, or after the period - and what it feels like. The timing and quality of the heat, together with the menstrual flow’s color, amount, and any pain, are the first clues that point toward one pattern rather than another.
If the fever is a low-grade afternoon heat that worsens after the period begins, accompanied by a dry throat, night sweats, and a sensation of warmth in the palms, soles, and chest, this points to Empty-Heat caused by Yin Deficiency. The tongue is red with little coating, and the pulse is thin and rapid.
When fever strikes before or during the period and comes with irritability, breast distension, a bitter taste, and a tendency to feel frustrated, the picture is Liver Qi Stagnation transforming into Heat. Menstrual blood is often dark red or purplish. The tongue is red with a thin yellow coating, and the pulse is wiry and rapid.
A fever that alternates with chills and is paired with sharp lower-abdominal pain that worsens with pressure suggests Blood Stagnation with Heat. The menstrual flow is dark purple with clots. The tongue looks purplish or shows stasis spots, and the pulse is deep, choppy, or wiry.
If the fever appears with heavy, bright-red or deep-red menstrual blood, intense thirst, and restlessness, the practitioner suspects Heat in Uterus Blood. The tongue is red with a yellow coating, and the pulse is rapid and slippery - signs of excess heat directly disturbing the uterus.
A low-grade fever or vague warmth that lingers after the period, together with fatigue, a pale face, dizziness, and scanty pale menses, indicates Qi and Blood Deficiency. The tongue is pale with a thin white coating, and the pulse is thin and weak, reflecting the body’s inability to secure the exterior after blood loss.
Finally, a fever with chills, no sweating, and possible nasal congestion during the period suggests Wind-Cold invasion. The tongue is pale red with a thin white coating, and the pulse is floating and tight. This external pattern arises because the body’s defensive Qi is temporarily weakened during menstruation.

### Empty-Heat caused by Yin Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/empty-heat-caused-by-yin-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Low-grade fever in the afternoon or evening, Heat sensation in palms, soles, and chest (five-palm heat), Night sweats, Dry mouth and throat, thirst for small sips, Scanty, bright-red menstrual flow, Restlessness, irritability, and difficulty sleeping
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Spicy, fried, or greasy foods, Overexertion and poor sleep, Alcohol and caffeine, Hot weather or overheating, Emotional stress, anger, and frustration
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Cooling foods and drinks, Rest and adequate sleep, Gentle movement, Drinking plenty of water, Cool environment
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Your body's cooling and moistening reserves (Yin) are already low, especially in the Kidney system. During menstruation, blood and Yin are further consumed, leaving nothing to anchor your internal heat. That heat rises as a low-grade fever, often in the afternoon or evening, with a restless warmth in your palms, soles, and chest.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red, thin, dry body with cracks, little or no coating. Pulse: fine (thin) and rapid, often weak at the rear (Kidney) position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Yin is the body's coolant and moisture. When it is deficient, any extra heat - from spicy food, a hot environment, or emotional fire - quickly pushes the internal thermostat up, causing the low-grade fever to spike. Rest and cooling, moistening foods help preserve the little Yin you have, while overwork and late nights drain it further. Gentle movement keeps Qi flowing without burning up precious reserves, and sipping fluids replenishes the dryness that empty heat creates.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Prioritize cooling, moistening foods like cucumber, pear, spinach, and millet. Avoid spicy, fried, or overly greasy dishes that can fan the internal heat. Sip warm or room-temperature water throughout the day rather than ice-cold drinks, which can shock the system. Gentle evening walks or restorative yoga help settle the mind without overheating the body. Aim for early, consistent bedtimes - the hours before midnight are when Yin rebuilds best.

### Liver Qi Stagnation that transforms into Heat
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/liver-qi-stagnation-that-transforms-into-heat)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Fever worse before or at the start of the period, Irritability and explosive anger, Distending breast tenderness, Bitter taste in the mouth, Distending pain in the ribcage area
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Emotional stress, anger, and frustration, Spicy, fried, or greasy foods, Alcohol and caffeine, Overexertion, Suppressed emotions
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Stress reduction, Gentle movement, Cooling foods and drinks, Deep breathing, Rest and adequate sleep
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Your Liver is responsible for keeping Qi moving smoothly, especially before your period when hormones shift. If stress or frustration builds up, that flow gets stuck and then turns into heat. That trapped heat rises and spills into your bloodstream, causing a low-grade fever, irritability, and that bitter taste in your mouth.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Red body with redder sides, yellow coating; wiry and rapid pulse.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Emotional stress directly fuels Liver Qi stagnation, which then turns into heat, so calming practices ease the root. Spicy and heating foods add fuel to the fire, while cooling foods help clear it. Gentle movement unblocks Qi, preventing it from stagnating and overheating.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Avoid alcohol, coffee, and spicy, greasy foods that add fuel to the heat. Prioritize stress management with gentle daily movement like walking or yoga. Eat cooling, green foods such as cucumber, mint, and celery.

### Blood Stagnation with Heat
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/blood-stagnation-with-heat)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Alternating chills and fever, Fixed, stabbing lower abdominal pain, Pain worsens at night and with pressure, Dark, purplish menstrual blood with clots, Thirst with desire to rinse the mouth but not swallow, Restlessness and irritability
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Cold foods and iced drinks, Sedentary lifestyle, Emotional stress, anger, and frustration
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Gentle movement, Warm compress on abdomen, Warm, lightly spiced meals
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When the blood flow in the pelvis and uterus is sluggish or blocked, it creates a backlog of Qi and blood. During menstruation, the body tries to push this stagnation out, which generates friction and heat. This is why you experience a fever that may come and go, like a kettle building steam against a clogged spout.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: reddish-purple body with stasis spots and distended sublingual veins; coating thin, dry, and yellow. Pulse: choppy (涩 sè) and rapid (数 shuò), often wiry (弦 xián) at the left Guan position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Blood stasis is like a traffic jam in the vessels. Gentle movement and warmth help to 'unclog' the jam, allowing blood and Qi to flow smoothly, which resolves the friction causing the fever. Cold and inactivity, on the other hand, congeal and stagnate blood further, making the blockage and the resulting heat worse. Emotional stress knots up the Liver Qi, which is the engine that drives blood circulation; when Qi gets stuck, blood stasis deepens.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Gentle movement like walking or yoga helps keep blood flowing and prevents it from pooling. Avoid cold, raw foods and iced drinks during your period, as cold congeals blood and worsens stasis. A warm compress or heating pad on the lower abdomen can soothe the stabbing pain. Lightly spiced warm meals with turmeric, black pepper, or a little saffron can gently support circulation.

### Heat in Uterus Blood
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/heat-in-uterus-blood)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Heavy menstrual flow with dark red or deep red blood, Thick, sticky menstrual blood, Feeling of heat in the lower abdomen, Fever with thirst for cold drinks, Early menstrual periods (arriving ahead of schedule), Restlessness and irritability
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Spicy, fried, or greasy foods, Alcohol and caffeine, Emotional stress, anger, and frustration, Hot weather or overheating, Overexertion
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Cooling foods and drinks, Cool environment, Drinking plenty of water, Applying a cool compress
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  During menstruation, the uterus is already congested with blood. When there is pre-existing excess heat in the uterus, it agitates the blood, causing it to move recklessly and leading to heavy, dark-red bleeding. This internal heat also rises upward, producing a fever that is often accompanied by intense thirst, restlessness, and a flushed face.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: Red body with a redder tip, possible prickles, thin yellow coat, tends dry. Pulse: Rapid (Shu) and wiry (Xian) or slippery (Hua), strongest at the left middle (Liver) and left rear (Kidney) positions.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Heat in the uterus is an excess heat condition. Spicy foods, alcohol, and emotional stress all generate or stir up heat, making the fever and bleeding worse. Cooling foods and environments help counteract the internal heat, calming the reckless movement of blood and reducing the fever. Resting in a cool place prevents external heat from adding to the internal heat, while staying hydrated replenishes fluids consumed by the heat.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Avoid spicy, greasy, and fried foods, as well as alcohol and coffee, which add heat to the body. Eat more cooling foods like cucumber, bitter melon, watermelon, and mung beans. Stay well hydrated with room-temperature water. Keep the living environment cool and avoid overheating, especially during menstruation.

### Qi and Blood Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/qi-and-blood-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Low-grade fever after menstruation, Persistent fatigue and lack of energy, Pale or sallow complexion, Dizziness or lightheadedness, Heart palpitations, Scanty, pale menses
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Overexertion, Skipping meals, Cold foods and iced drinks, Excessive exercise, Poor sleep, Heavy menstrual bleeding
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Rest and lying down, Warm, nourishing soups and stews, Gentle movement, Keeping warm, Moxibustion on lower abdomen
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Menstruation draws heavily on your body's Qi and Blood reserves. When these are already low, the period leaves a vacuum that weakens your defensive Qi (Wei Qi). After bleeding stops, the yang Qi has nothing to anchor it and floats upward, causing a low-grade fever that feels more like a warm flush than a raging fire. Because there is no real excess heat, the fever is mild and comes with exhaustion, not with thirst or a red tongue.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale body, may be thin or have teeth marks, thin white coating. Pulse: fine, weak, and empty.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Because Qi and Blood are already deficient, overwork, poor nutrition, and blood loss further deplete them, leaving the yang Qi unanchored and causing it to float upward as a low-grade fever. Rest, warmth, and nourishing foods replenish Qi and Blood, grounding the yang and strengthening the body's exterior, which naturally resolves the fever.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Focus on warm, cooked, easily digestible foods like bone broths, congee, stewed meats, and dark leafy greens. Avoid raw, cold foods and drinks that further weaken digestion. Prioritize rest around your period and avoid overwork or intense exercise. Gentle walking or tai chi can help Qi flow without depleting you.

### Wind-Cold
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/wind-cold)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Pronounced chills with only mild fever, No sweating at all, Headache and body aches, Clear runny nose and sneezing, Stiff neck and upper back, No thirst, or only want warm drinks
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Cold drafts or air conditioning, Cold foods and iced drinks, Overexertion and poor sleep
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Resting under warm blankets, Hot ginger or cinnamon tea, A warm bath, Light sweating
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  During menstruation, the body's defensive Qi (Wei Qi) is naturally a little weaker because Blood and Qi are directed downward to the uterus. This temporary gap in the body's outer shield allows Wind-Cold to invade from the environment. The struggle between the invading Cold and the body's defensive Qi at the skin and muscles produces a mild fever with pronounced chills, just like catching a cold that happens to coincide with your period.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: normal or slightly pale body with a thin white moist coat. Pulse: floating (fú) and tight (jǐn).
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Wind-Cold is an exterior pathogen that enters when the body's defenses are down. Warmth and rest support the defensive Qi and help push the pathogen out through the pores. Cold and exhaustion further weaken the already-taxed Wei Qi, trapping the invader and prolonging the fever and chills.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Wrap up warmly and keep your neck and lower back covered. Sip hot ginger and scallion tea to encourage a very light sweat. Rest as much as you can during the first days of your period and avoid cold, raw foods and icy drinks entirely.

## คำถามที่พบบ่อย (Frequently Asked Questions)
### Why do I get a low-grade fever every time I get my period?
In Traditional Chinese Medicine, your period is a time when your body's internal balance shifts dramatically. If you have an underlying weakness or stagnation, this shift can trigger a fever as a sign of that imbalance. The timing of the fever - before, during, or after your flow - and its accompanying symptoms tell your practitioner exactly which pattern is at play, whether it's Liver Qi stagnation, Yin deficiency, or something else. It's not just a random hormonal quirk; it's a signal your body is sending.

### Can acupuncture help with menstrual fevers?
Yes, acupuncture is a key tool for balancing the body's energy and clearing heat. Specific points are chosen based on your pattern - for example, points to soothe the Liver and clear heat for a premenstrual fever with irritability, or points to nourish Yin for a post-period low-grade heat. Many women feel a cooling, calming effect after just a few sessions, and with regular treatment, the fevers often become less intense and less frequent over a few cycles.

### Is it safe to take Chinese herbs during my period?
Absolutely, when prescribed by a qualified TCM practitioner. Herbal formulas are customized to your cycle and can be taken safely during menstruation. In fact, some herbs are specifically used to regulate the flow, reduce pain, and clear heat during your period. Always tell your practitioner exactly where you are in your cycle so they can adjust the formula if needed.

### How long until I see results?
Many women notice a difference within the first one to two cycles. If your fever is driven by stagnation or excess heat, you might feel relief quite quickly, sometimes after just one period. If your pattern is rooted in deficiency - such as low Yin or Blood - it takes longer to rebuild those reserves, and you should expect gradual improvement over three to six months. Consistency with herbs and acupuncture appointments is the most important factor.

### Do I need to change my diet?
Diet is a helpful support, not a strict requirement. In general, if your fever comes with irritability, a bitter taste, or a red face, avoid spicy, greasy, and fried foods, and emphasize cooling, hydrating choices like cucumber, pear, and leafy greens. If you feel drained and pale after your period, warm, nourishing soups and stews with chicken and ginger are better. Your practitioner will give you personalized guidance based on your pattern.

### Can TCM help if my doctor says it's just PMS?
Definitely. TCM recognizes that many PMS symptoms - including cyclical fevers, mood swings, and breast tenderness - are linked to specific patterns like Liver Qi stagnation. By treating the root imbalance, TCM can often resolve not only the fever but the broader premenstrual picture, giving you a smoother cycle overall.

### What's the difference between TCM and Western medicine for this?
Western medicine often attributes these fevers to hormonal fluctuations and may offer symptom relief or hormonal birth control. TCM, on the other hand, looks for the individual pattern behind the fever - whether it's heat from stagnation, deficiency, or an external invasion - and aims to correct that imbalance. The goal is not just to mask the fever each month but to stop it from happening in the first place.
