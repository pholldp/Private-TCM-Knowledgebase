## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนปัจจุบัน (Conventional Medicine View)
### คำอธิบาย (Description)
Western medicine classifies headaches into primary types like migraine, tension-type, and cluster, but a headache that moves location doesn't fit neatly into one box. It may be considered a variant of migraine or attributed to muscle tension, sinus pressure, or stress. Diagnosis relies on a detailed history, neurological exam, and sometimes imaging to rule out serious causes. Treatment is typically symptom-focused, aiming to relieve pain and reduce triggers.

### การรักษาหลัก (Treatments)
Common treatments include over-the-counter analgesics such as ibuprofen or acetaminophen, triptans for suspected migraine, and anti-nausea medications. For frequent headaches, preventive drugs like beta-blockers, antidepressants, or anticonvulsants may be prescribed. Non-drug approaches include stress management, physical therapy, and identifying dietary or environmental triggers.

## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนจีน (Traditional Chinese Medicine View)
### ความเข้าใจเกี่ยวกับการแพทย์แผนจีน (TCM Understanding)
TCM understands a migrating headache primarily through the lens of Wind, a pathogenic factor that moves and changes. Just as wind in nature shifts direction, a Wind-induced headache can move from the forehead to the temples or the back of the head. This moving quality is the hallmark that distinguishes it from a fixed, stationary pain. The head is considered the meeting point of all Yang channels, and when Wind disturbs this clear Yang, pain and discomfort arise.
External Wind invades from the environment, often entering through the back of the neck. Depending on what it combines with - Cold, Heat, or Damp - the headache feels different. Wind-Cold brings a tight, constricting pain that worsens with cold air. Wind-Heat creates a distending, throbbing ache that flares up in warm rooms. Wind-Damp causes a heavy, wrapped sensation that gets worse on rainy days. Each of these external patterns can shift location because the Wind component keeps moving.
Internal Wind arises from deeper imbalances. When the Liver's Yin is deficient, its Yang can rise and stir up internal Wind, producing a throbbing, shifting headache often accompanied by dizziness, irritability, and a stiff neck. Alternatively, if the Spleen is weak and Phlegm accumulates, this Phlegm can be churned upward by Liver Wind, creating a fuzzy, dizzy headache that feels like a wet towel wrapped around the head. Both patterns share the migratory quality because internal Wind is just as mobile as external Wind.
Because one Western diagnosis of 'migrating headache' can stem from five different TCM patterns, the treatment must be carefully matched to the individual. A person whose pain tightens in the cold needs warming herbs, while someone whose head pounds with heat needs cooling ones. A heavy, weather-sensitive headache responds to drying herbs, and a dizzy, Phlegm-driven headache requires herbs that transform Phlegm and extinguish Wind. This is why TCM asks not just 'where does it hurt?' but 'what does it feel like and what makes it better?'

### การวินิจฉัยโดยแพทย์แผนจีน (Practitioner Diagnosis)
A TCM practitioner begins by asking what the pain actually feels like and what makes it better or worse. A headache that migrates can arise from external wind invasions or internal imbalances, and the quality of the pain - whether it is tight, distending, heavy, or wrapped - is often the first clue that points toward one pattern over another.
If the pain feels tight and gripping, like a band around the head, and worsens with cold or wind while improving with warmth, the pattern is likely Wind‑Cold. The person may also feel chilly, have a stuffy nose, and dislike drafts. The tongue coating is thin and white, and the pulse feels floating and tight, like a rope bobbing on water.
When the headache is distending or throbbing, flares up in hot weather or after eating spicy food, and is accompanied by a red face, thirst, and a sore throat, Wind‑Heat is suspected. Here the tongue tip is redder with a thin yellow coating, and the pulse is floating and rapid, reflecting heat and the body’s attempt to push the pathogen out.
A heavy, dull ache that shifts location and gets worse on damp or rainy days points to Wind‑Damp. The whole body may feel heavy or sluggish, and the tongue often shows a greasy white coating with a slippery pulse. This pattern is more about moisture clinging to the head rather than the sharp pain of wind‑cold or wind‑heat.
When the headache feels as if the head is wrapped in a wet cloth, and it comes with dizziness, nausea, or a foggy sensation, Wind‑Phlegm is likely. Here internal phlegm combines with external wind. The tongue coating is thick and greasy, and the pulse is slippery. This pattern is more common in people who tend toward digestive sluggishness or phlegm formation.
In chronic or severe cases where the headache is distending and moves around, often on one side, and is accompanied by dizziness, ringing in the ears, irritability, and a flushed face, the root is Liver Wind agitating internally due to rising Liver Yang. The tongue is red with a yellow coating, and the pulse feels wiry and rapid - a sign of internal wind and heat that requires calming the liver rather than just expelling external factors.

### Wind-Cold
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/wind-cold)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Tight constricting pain that moves, Worsens with cold or wind, Aversion to cold, chills, No sweating, Nasal congestion with clear watery discharge, Stiff neck and upper back
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Cold wind or draughts, Iced drinks or cold food, Air-conditioning, Washing hair and going out wet, Overwork or lack of sleep
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warmth, Warm drinks, Rest and calm, Covering the head, Steam inhalation, Gentle neck stretches
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Wind-Cold is an external pathogen that invades the body's surface, especially the channels of the head and neck. The cold contracts and tightens the vessels, causing a constricting pain, while the wind nature makes the pain shift from place to place. The body's defensive Qi fights the invader at the surface, producing chills, aversion to cold, and a floating tight pulse.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: normal or slightly pale body with a thin white moist coat. Pulse: floating and tight (浮紧 fú jǐn).
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Wind-Cold attacks the exterior, constricting the channels and causing pain. Warmth and rest help open the pores and expel the pathogen. Cold and draughts drive it deeper, worsening pain.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Keep warm, especially the neck and head. Avoid cold and raw foods. Drink warm ginger tea. Rest and avoid draughts. Gentle steam inhalation can help open the nasal passages.

### Wind-Heat
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/wind-heat)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Distending or bursting pain that shifts location, Worse from heat, warm rooms, or spicy food, Red face and bloodshot eyes, Sore, red throat and thirst for cold drinks, Fever with mild chills or aversion to wind
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Hot or stuffy rooms, Spicy, fried, or greasy food, Alcohol and coffee, Strenuous activity in the heat, Emotional agitation
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Cool, fresh air, Cold or cool drinks, Rest in a quiet, dim room, Cool compress on the forehead
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Wind-Heat is an external invasion that strikes the uppermost part of the body first. The Heat swells the channels in the head, creating a distending, restless pain that shifts as the Wind moves. Because the pathogen is still at the surface, the body tries to push it out with fever and sweating, but the Heat keeps the pain flaring up whenever you get warm or eat spicy food.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red tip and edges with a thin yellow (or white-to-yellow) coating. Pulse: floating (fú) and rapid (shuò), easily felt at the surface.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Wind-Heat is an external Hot pathogen; anything that adds internal Heat-like spicy food, alcohol, or a hot environment-feeds the fire and makes the distending pain worse. Cooling measures (cold drinks, fresh air, rest) help the body clear the Heat and calm the Wind, easing the headache naturally.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Stay in a cool, airy room and avoid hot, stuffy spaces. Sip plenty of room-temperature water or mild chrysanthemum tea. Skip spicy, fried, or greasy foods, and go easy on coffee and alcohol until the headache clears.

### Wind-Damp
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/wind-damp)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Heavy, wrapped sensation in the head, Pain moves from place to place, Worse with damp or rainy weather, Body heaviness and limb soreness
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Damp or rainy weather, Heavy, greasy, or sweet foods, Cold, raw foods, Sedentary lifestyle, Exposure to wind and dampness
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warm, dry weather, Gentle movement, Light, warm meals, Ginger tea
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  External Wind carries Dampness into the body, and because Wind is mobile, the pain can shift from one area of the head to another. Dampness is heavy and sticky, so it settles in the head and creates a sensation of weight, like a tight wrap. When the weather turns damp or rainy, the external Dampness reinforces the internal Dampness, making the headache worse.
Dampness also obstructs the clear Yang Qi that should rise to the head, leaving you feeling foggy, heavy, and tired. The combination of moving Wind and clinging Dampness is what gives this headache its wandering, heavy character.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: normal or slightly pale body with a white greasy coating. Pulse: floating and soggy (soft), may be slippery.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Damp weather and heavy foods increase internal dampness, which obstructs the head and makes the pain feel heavier and more stuck. Warmth and movement, on the other hand, help dispel dampness by promoting the flow of Qi and the transformation of fluids, restoring the clear Yang that should rise to the head.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Keep warm and dry, especially in rainy or humid weather. Avoid heavy, greasy, and cold raw foods that create more internal dampness. Gentle daily movement like walking or stretching helps Qi flow and drains dampness. A cup of warm ginger tea can also help dispel wind and dampness.

### Wind-Phlegm
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/wind-phlegm)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Heavy or wrapped sensation in the head, Headache moves from place to place, Dizziness or vertigo, as if the room is spinning, Nausea, vomiting of white frothy phlegm, Chest stuffiness and heaviness in the limbs
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Damp or rainy weather, Heavy, greasy, or sweet foods, Dairy products, Sedentary lifestyle, Stress and emotional upset
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warm, dry weather, Light, easily digestible meals, Ginger tea, Gentle movement, Rest in a quiet, dim room
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When the Spleen is weak and fails to transform fluids, phlegm builds up inside the body. This phlegm can then be stirred upward by internal Wind, which often arises from Liver disharmony. The phlegm and wind together cloud the clear orifices of the head, causing a headache that shifts location and feels heavy or wrapped, like a wet towel.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: normal or slightly pale body, swollen with teeth marks, white greasy coating. Pulse: wiry and slippery (Xian Hua).
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Dampness and phlegm are aggravated by humid weather and greasy foods, which further burden the Spleen and thicken the phlegm that clouds the head. Stress stirs internal Wind, making the headache move. Warmth, light eating, and gentle movement help transform dampness and keep qi flowing. Ginger tea warms the middle burner and resolves phlegm, while rest calms the Wind that disturbs the clear orifices.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Avoid dairy, greasy, and sweet foods that create dampness and phlegm. Eat warm, light, and easily digested meals. Sip ginger tea to warm the middle and transform phlegm. Gentle daily movement like walking helps qi flow and prevents stagnation. Keep the neck and head protected from wind and dampness.

### Liver Wind agitating Internally due to Liver Yang Rising
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/liver-wind-agitating-internally-due-to-liver-yang-rising)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Throbbing or distending headache that moves around, Dizziness or vertigo with a sense of falling, Irritability and easy anger, Stiff neck, Tremor of hands or head, Flushed face and red eyes
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Stress and anger, Alcohol and spicy food, Overwork or lack of sleep
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Rest and calm, Cooling foods and drinks, Gentle movement
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When Liver Yin is depleted, the anchoring of Yang weakens, allowing it to surge upward and stir up internal Wind. This Wind rushes to the head, disturbing the clear orifices and causing a throbbing, distending pain that shifts location as the Wind moves. The moving quality of the headache directly mirrors the erratic, mobile nature of internal Wind.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red body, stiff or trembling, possibly deviated, with a thin yellow or scanty coating. Pulse: wiry, fine at the proximal positions, and often rapid.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Anger and stress cause Liver Qi to surge upward, fanning the Wind that drives the headache, so calming the mind and resting the Liver are essential. Cooling, Yin-rich foods help anchor the rising Yang and clear Heat, while alcohol and spices add fuel to the fire. Adequate sleep and gentle movement restore Yin and smooth Qi, preventing the erratic upward rush that makes the pain move.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Favor cooling, Yin-nourishing foods like celery, cucumber, chrysanthemum tea, and goji berries. Avoid alcohol, coffee, spicy, and greasy foods that heat the Liver. Gentle movement such as tai chi or walking helps smooth Liver Qi. Prioritize rest and protect sleep, especially before midnight.

## คำถามที่พบบ่อย (Frequently Asked Questions)
### Why does my headache move around?
In TCM, a moving headache is a classic sign of Wind, a pathogenic factor that is mobile by nature. Whether Wind invades from outside (as in Wind-Cold or Wind-Heat) or is stirred up internally (as in Liver Yang Rising), its shifting quality makes the pain travel. Identifying the accompanying symptoms - like chills, heaviness, or irritability - helps pinpoint the exact pattern.

### Can acupuncture help a headache that shifts location?
Yes, acupuncture is very effective for migrating headaches. Points are chosen to expel Wind and restore the smooth flow of Qi to the head. For external patterns, points like Fengchi (GB-20) and Hegu (LI-4) are used to release the exterior. For internal patterns, points such as Taichong (LR-3) and Fenglong (ST-40) calm the Liver and transform Phlegm. Many patients notice relief after the first few sessions, with cumulative improvement over a course of treatment.

### Will Chinese herbs interact with my painkillers?
In general, TCM herbs for Wind patterns can be safely combined with over-the-counter pain relievers like ibuprofen or acetaminophen. However, some herbs, such as Chuan Xiong, have mild blood-moving properties, so if you are taking blood thinners, consult your TCM practitioner and doctor. Always bring a list of your medications to your TCM consultation.

### How many acupuncture sessions will I need?
For acute external Wind headaches, 2-4 sessions over one or two weeks may be enough. Chronic internal patterns often require weekly sessions for 6-12 weeks. Your practitioner will adjust the frequency based on your response. The goal is not just pain relief but reducing the overall tendency for headaches to return.

### Is it safe to treat during pregnancy?
Acupuncture can be safely used during pregnancy for headaches, but certain points are avoided. Herbal formulas must be prescribed by a practitioner experienced in prenatal care, as some herbs are contraindicated. Always inform your practitioner if you are pregnant or trying to conceive.

### What if my headache gets worse with weather changes?
Weather-sensitive headaches are often a sign of Wind-Damp or Wind-Cold. In TCM, damp or cold weather can trigger an external invasion or aggravate an existing imbalance. Treatment focuses on drying Dampness or warming the channels, and many patients find that as their constitution strengthens, they become less reactive to weather shifts.

## การวิจัยทางวิทยาศาสตร์สมัยใหม่ (Modern Scientific Research)
1. **Acupuncture for tension-type headache** ([ลิงก์](https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD007587.pub2))
   *วารสารวิชาการ: Linde K, Allais G, Brinkhaus B, et al. Acupuncture for tension-type headache. Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews 2009, Issue 1. Art. No.: CD007587.*
   A Cochrane systematic review of 11 RCTs with 2317 participants found that acupuncture is effective for frequent episodic or chronic tension-type headache. Adding acupuncture to usual care halved headache frequency over three months compared to usual care alone.

2. **Acupuncture for the prevention of episodic migraine** ([ลิงก์](https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD001218.pub3))
   *วารสารวิชาการ: Linde K, Allais G, Brinkhaus B, et al. Acupuncture for the prevention of episodic migraine. Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews 2016, Issue 6. Art. No.: CD001218.*
   This 2016 Cochrane review of 22 RCTs (4985 participants) concluded that acupuncture is at least as effective as prophylactic drug treatment for reducing migraine frequency, and it has a better safety profile. The effect was sustained over six months.

3. **Efficacy of Chuanxiong Chatiao San for migraine: a systematic review and meta-analysis**
   *วารสารวิชาการ: Li J, Li Y, Zhang Y, et al. Chuanxiong Chatiao San for migraine: A systematic review of randomized controlled trials. Evidence-Based Complementary and Alternative Medicine. 2019;2019:6191536.*
   A meta-analysis of 14 RCTs involving 1200 migraine patients found that Chuanxiong Chatiao San, alone or with Western medicine, significantly improved headache frequency and intensity compared to conventional treatment. The quality of evidence was moderate due to risk of bias in the included trials.
