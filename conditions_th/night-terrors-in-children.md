## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนปัจจุบัน (Conventional Medicine View)
### คำอธิบาย (Description)
Night terrors are a type of parasomnia that occurs during deep non-REM sleep, most often in children between the ages of 3 and 12. During an episode, a child may suddenly sit up, scream, thrash, or appear intensely frightened, but they are not fully awake and will have no memory of the event in the morning. The episodes are usually brief and the child settles back to sleep on their own.
Conventional medicine views night terrors as a benign developmental phenomenon linked to an immature nervous system and deep sleep transitions. Diagnosis is clinical, based on the description of the events, and no laboratory tests are required unless another condition is suspected.

### การรักษาหลัก (Treatments)
Standard management focuses on reassurance and safety. Parents are advised to gently guide the child back to bed without waking them, ensure a safe sleep environment, and maintain a consistent bedtime routine. In frequent or severe cases, scheduled awakenings shortly before the typical terror time may be used to disrupt the sleep cycle. Medications are rarely prescribed and only considered when the terrors cause significant distress or risk of injury.

## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนจีน (Traditional Chinese Medicine View)
### ความเข้าใจเกี่ยวกับการแพทย์แผนจีน (TCM Understanding)
In TCM, peaceful sleep depends on the Shen - the spirit - resting calmly in the Heart. A child’s Shen is naturally more delicate than an adult’s, easily unsettled by heat, digestive upset, or emotional shock. Night terrors occur when something disturbs the Shen during the deepest part of the night, causing the child to cry out, thrash, or appear terrified while still asleep.
One common source of disturbance is heat. When the Heart or Liver carries excess fire - often from a mother’s spicy diet during pregnancy or breastfeeding, or from the child’s own constitutional tendency - the Shen becomes agitated. These children typically have a bright red face, a red tongue tip, and cry out loudly as if frightened.
Another common trigger is the Stomach. If a child eats too much, too late, or consumes rich, heavy foods, undigested food stagnates and generates turbid heat that rises to disturb the Heart. The tongue coating becomes thick and greasy, and the belly is distended.
Not all night terrors are hot and loud. When a child’s Spleen Yang is weak, the digestive system lacks the warmth to move food and Qi smoothly. At night, internal cold becomes more pronounced, causing a dull, crampy discomfort that makes the child whimper and curl up. These children have a pale, puffy tongue and feel better when their tummy is warmed.
Each of these patterns - Heart Fire blazing, Food Stagnation, Spleen Yang Deficiency, and the less common Liver Fire Blazing - produces a distinct picture, and TCM treatment is chosen to match that picture exactly.

### การวินิจฉัยโดยแพทย์แผนจีน (Practitioner Diagnosis)
A TCM practitioner begins by observing the child's face, tongue, and behavior during the terror. In Heart Fire blazing, the face is flushed, the eyes may appear red, and the tongue tip is strikingly red with a yellow coating. The cry is loud and the child seems afraid of light. The pulse feels rapid and may be wiry. Questions often focus on the mother’s diet during pregnancy or breastfeeding, as spicy or heating foods can transmit fire to the child.
When Food Stagnation in the Stomach is the culprit, the clues are digestive. The belly is distended, the child belches, and the tongue coating is thick and greasy. Night terrors come with restlessness and possibly constipation. A practitioner asks about feeding routines, recent overeating, or new foods. This pattern often follows a heavy meal; the stomach’s discomfort directly disturbs sleep, and the pulse tends to be slippery and rapid.
Spleen Yang Deficiency presents a very different picture. The cry is weak and whimpering, the abdomen feels cool to the touch, and the child prefers it pressed or warmed. The tongue is pale with a thin white coat, and the pulse is deep and weak. A practitioner asks about appetite, digestion, and a tendency to feel cold. This pattern often stems from a constitutional weakness or a diet that includes too many cold or raw foods.
Liver Fire Blazing, though less common, is unmistakable in its intensity. The child is extremely irritable, the eyes are red, and there may be a bitter taste in the mouth. The tongue is red with a yellow coat, and the pulse is wiry and rapid. Unlike Heart Fire, the tongue tip may not be the most prominent red area. A practitioner looks for emotional triggers, such as frustration or anger, and notes that the terrors tend to explode with little warning.

### Heart Fire blazing
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/heart-fire-blazing)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Bright red flushed face, Mouth or tongue ulcers with red edges, Thirst with desire for cold drinks, Irritability and anxiety during the day, Sudden fearful screaming at night, Tongue tip distinctly redder than the rest, with red points
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Spicy, greasy, or fried foods (including mother&#x27;s diet if breastfeeding), Overexcitement or overstimulation before bed, Hot weather or stuffy, overheated rooms, Emotional upset or anger during the day
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Cool room temperature, Gentle rocking or holding, Avoiding spicy foods (for breastfeeding mother), Calm, quiet environment and gentle routines
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  The Heart houses the Shen (spirit), and its fire must stay gently contained for peaceful sleep. When excess heat - often passed from a mother's spicy diet, emotional stress, or a child's own constitution - blazes upward, it agitates the Shen. At night, this restless fire erupts as sudden, fearful screaming, a bright red face, and an inability to settle back to sleep.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: Red body with a markedly redder tip, swollen red points or prickles at the tip, and a yellow dry coating. Pulse: Rapid and forceful, overflowing (Hong) quality strongest at the left front (Heart) position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Heart Fire is a pattern of excess internal heat. Spicy and greasy foods add more heat, and excitement or emotional upset fans that fire upward, directly agitating the Shen. Cooling the environment and the body, along with quiet, soothing routines, helps drain the excess fire and allows the spirit to settle, making sleep calm and uninterrupted.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: If breastfeeding, the mother should strictly avoid spicy, greasy, and fried foods, as well as coffee and alcohol. Keep the child's bedroom cool and quiet, and establish a predictable, soothing bedtime routine. For older children, offer small amounts of cooling liquids like pear juice or mung bean soup, and avoid stimulating play right before sleep.

### Food Stagnation in the Stomach
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/food-stagnation-in-the-stomach)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Upper abdominal bloating and distending pain, Sour, rotten-smelling belching, Thick, greasy, curd-like tongue coating, Worse after eating, especially heavy meals, Loss of appetite or aversion to food, Foul-smelling flatulence or loose stools
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Overeating or large meals, Eating late at night, Greasy, fried, or sweet foods, Cold or raw foods and drinks, Irregular meal times
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warm, light meals and drinks, Gentle abdominal massage, Burping after feeding, Skipping a meal or fasting briefly
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When a child eats too much or consumes rich, hard-to-digest foods, the Stomach and Spleen become overwhelmed. Undigested food sits and stagnates, generating turbid dampness and heat. This internal disturbance rises upward, agitating the Heart spirit and causing restless sleep and sudden night terrors.
The classic saying "when the Stomach is not at peace, sleep is not restful" (胃不和则卧不安, wèi bù hé zé wò bù ān) captures this perfectly. The bloating, sour belching, and abdominal discomfort that accompany the stagnation directly wake the child from sleep in a frightened, crying state.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: normal body, thick greasy or curd-like coating, thickest in the centre. Pulse: slippery (滑 huá), especially forceful at the right middle (Stomach) position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Overeating and heavy, greasy foods overwhelm the Stomach's digestive capacity, leading to stagnation and turbid damp-heat that rise to disturb the Heart spirit at night. Light, warm, easily digested foods and smaller portions reduce the burden on the Stomach, allowing Qi to descend and food to be properly transformed. Gentle abdominal massage and burping physically move stagnant Qi downward, easing distension and removing the internal discomfort that triggers night terrors.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Avoid heavy, greasy, or sweet foods, especially close to bedtime. Offer warm, easily digestible meals like congee, and ensure the child does not overeat. A regular feeding schedule with smaller portions helps the Stomach settle before sleep. Gentle clockwise abdominal massage after feeding can encourage downward movement of Qi.

### Spleen Yang Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/spleen-yang-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Dull abdominal pain that improves with warmth and pressure, Cold hands and feet, Poor appetite with abdominal bloating, Loose, watery stools or undigested food in stools, Tiredness and listlessness, Pale face and pale, puffy tongue
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Cold or raw foods and drinks, Exposure to cold or drafts, Overeating or large meals
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warm compress on the tummy, Warm, light meals and drinks, Being held or swaddled warmly
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When a child's Spleen Yang is weak, the digestive system lacks the warmth needed to process food and maintain a calm abdomen. At night, when the body's protective Yang Qi naturally retreats inward, the internal cold becomes more pronounced, causing dull, gnawing abdominal pain or discomfort. This physical distress wakes the child, whose spirit is already unsettled by the lingering cold, leading to crying and night terrors.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: Pale, puffy, and tender with scalloped edges and a white, slippery coating. Pulse: Deep, slow, and weak, especially at the right middle position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: In Spleen Yang Deficiency, the body's internal furnace is too weak to keep the abdomen warm, and cold congeals inside, causing pain. Warmth from foods, drinks, or external heat directly supplements the missing Yang and relaxes the cramped digestive tract. Cold and raw foods further drain that fragile fire, intensifying the discomfort that triggers night terrors.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Keep the child's abdomen warm with a soft belly band or a gentle moxa-free heat pack at bedtime. Offer easily digestible, cooked, warm foods like congee with a pinch of ginger; avoid raw fruits, cold drinks, and dairy. A warm bath before bed can help the whole body relax.

### Liver Fire Blazing
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/liver-fire-blazing)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Sudden night terrors with screaming and flushed face, Intense daytime irritability and quick temper, Red, painful, or burning eyes, Bitter taste in the mouth, Dry mouth and throat, craving cold drinks, Constipation and dark yellow urine
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Spicy, greasy, or fried foods (including mother&#x27;s diet if breastfeeding), Emotional upset or anger during the day, Hot weather or stuffy, overheated rooms, Overexcitement or overstimulation before bed, Constipation
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Cooling foods and drinks, Calm, quiet environment and gentle routines, Regular bowel movements, Cool room temperature
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When a child's Liver Qi becomes constrained-often from emotional tension, dietary heat, or overstimulation-it can transform into fire. This Liver Fire flares upward, disturbing the Heart and agitating the spirit (Shen). At night, when the body should be quiet, the rising fire causes the child to wake suddenly in terror, screaming, with a flushed face and intense irritability.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: Red body with redder sides, dry yellow coating, possible red prickles. Pulse: Wiry (弦 xián) and rapid (数 shuò), often forceful at the left Guan position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Liver Fire is an excess heat condition. Spicy foods and emotional upset add more heat and stir the Liver, making the fire flare up. Cooling foods and calm environments reduce heat and settle the spirit. Regular bowel movements help drain heat from the body, preventing it from rising to disturb sleep.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Offer cooling foods like cucumber, pear, and chrysanthemum tea. Avoid spicy, greasy, and fried foods that add heat.
Keep a calm, quiet bedtime routine without screens or exciting play. Ensure regular bowel movements to help clear heat from the body.

## คำถามที่พบบ่อย (Frequently Asked Questions)
### Can TCM really help my child’s night terrors?
Yes. TCM has been used for centuries to treat childhood sleep disturbances. By identifying the specific pattern - whether it’s heat, food stagnation, cold, or fright - a practitioner can select herbs, acupressure, and dietary changes that calm the spirit and resolve the underlying cause. Many parents notice a reduction in the frequency and intensity of night terrors within the first two weeks of treatment.

### Is herbal medicine safe for young children?
When prescribed by a qualified TCM practitioner, pediatric herbal formulas are gentle and dosed according to the child’s age and weight. The herbs used for night terrors are generally mild and well-tolerated. Always inform your practitioner of any medications or supplements your child is taking, and never give adult formulas to a child without professional guidance.

### How long will it take to see results?
Children often respond quickly. Excess patterns like Food Stagnation or Heart Fire may show improvement in a few days once the diet is corrected and the right herbs are started. Deficiency patterns, such as Spleen Yang Deficiency, can take a few weeks to build up warmth and energy. Your practitioner will monitor progress and adjust the treatment as needed.

### Do I need to change my child’s diet?
Diet plays a key role. Even before an in-depth pattern diagnosis, you can help by offering a light, warm dinner at least two hours before bed and avoiding stimulating foods like sugar, chocolate, and spicy snacks in the afternoon and evening. If your child tends to have a cold belly, also limit cold drinks and raw fruits. Your practitioner will give more specific advice based on your child’s pattern.

### Can TCM be used alongside our pediatrician’s advice?
Absolutely. TCM works well as a complement to conventional care. There are no known conflicts between gentle herbal formulas and standard management for night terrors. It is always wise to let both your pediatrician and your TCM practitioner know about any treatments your child is receiving, so everyone is on the same page.

### My child also has tummy troubles - are they connected?
Very often, yes. In TCM, the Stomach and Heart are closely linked, and digestive upset is a major trigger for night terrors. Bloating, constipation, or a thick tongue coating point to Food Stagnation, while a pale tongue and a cold abdomen suggest Spleen Yang Deficiency. Treating the digestion frequently resolves the sleep disturbance at the same time.
