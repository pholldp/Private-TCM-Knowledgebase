## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนปัจจุบัน (Conventional Medicine View)
### คำอธิบาย (Description)
Central Serous Chorioretinopathy is a condition where fluid accumulates under the central retina (the macula), causing blurred or distorted central vision. It typically affects one eye and is most common in men between 30 and 50, often triggered by high stress or corticosteroid use. Diagnosis is confirmed with optical coherence tomography (OCT), which shows the pocket of fluid, and sometimes fluorescein angiography.
Many cases resolve on their own within a few months, but the fluid can return, and some people develop chronic or recurrent episodes that leave lasting visual distortion or thinning of the retina. The conventional view is that a leak in the choroid allows fluid to seep beneath the retina, though the exact reason for the leak is not always clear.

### การรักษาหลัก (Treatments)
For acute CSC, doctors often recommend watchful waiting, as the fluid may reabsorb on its own. If it persists or recurs, treatment options include focal laser photocoagulation to seal the leak, or photodynamic therapy (PDT) to reduce abnormal choroidal vessels. In some cases, anti-VEGF injections are used off-label, though they are not FDA-approved for CSC. Oral medications like eplerenone or spironolactone, which affect mineralocorticoid receptors, are sometimes prescribed to reduce fluid, with mixed results.

## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนจีน (Traditional Chinese Medicine View)
### ความเข้าใจเกี่ยวกับการแพทย์แผนจีน (TCM Understanding)
TCM understands CSC primarily through the organ systems that nourish the eyes - especially the Liver, Kidney, and Spleen. The Liver opens into the eyes and stores Blood; the Kidneys store Essence, which rises to support clear vision. When these reserves run low, the retina can become undernourished and fluid balance falters. The Spleen transforms food into Qi and manages fluids; if it becomes weak or overwhelmed by dampness, turbid fluid can accumulate and drift upward, clouding the macula.
Fluid under the retina is seen as a form of Dampness, but the reason that Dampness forms varies widely. In one person, it may be because the Spleen is too weak to move fluids, creating a sticky, turbid dampness that fogs the vision. In another, it may be because Yin and Blood are so depleted that the eye tissues lose their integrity, allowing fluid to seep out. In still others, stagnant Blood blocks the tiny eye collaterals, causing fixed dark spots and wavy lines.
This is why two people with the same OCT scan can feel very different - one may feel heavy and sluggish, another dry and hot, and a third exhausted and washed out - and why TCM treats each differently.
The patterns also explain triggers. Stress, which is a major CSC trigger, directly disrupts Liver Qi flow, which can generate Heat or stagnation that rises to the eyes. Heavy, greasy meals can overwhelm the Spleen and generate turbid dampness. Late nights and overwork drain Yin and Qi, weakening the foundation that holds the retina steady. By tracing the fluid back to its source, TCM aims not just to clear the fluid but to correct the imbalance that allowed it to form in the first place.

### การวินิจฉัยโดยแพทย์แผนจีน (Practitioner Diagnosis)
A practitioner starts by listening to how you describe your vision. Is it a gradual fog, a sudden distortion, or fixed dark spots? The quality and timing of the blur, together with your energy level, sleep, and digestion, offer the first clues. Tongue and pulse examination then confirms which pattern is driving the fluid under the retina.
When the main complaint is slow-onset blur with dry, gritty eyes and a feeling of heat in the palms or night sweats, the picture points to Kidney and Liver Yin Deficiency. The tongue is often red with little coating, and the pulse feels thin and rapid. This pattern shows the eyes are undernourished because the body’s cooling, moistening reserves have run low.
If the vision worsens after exertion and you feel deeply tired, short of breath, and washed out, Qi and Yin Deficiency is likely. The tongue may be pale or slightly red with a thin coat, and the pulse is weak or fine. Here the body lacks both the vital lift of Qi to send clear nourishment upward and the Yin fluids to keep the retina supple.
Fixed dark spots or wavy lines that do not shift, often with a prickly or distending sensation around the eye, suggest Blood Stagnation. The tongue looks dusky or has small purple spots, and the pulse feels choppy or wiry. Stagnation rarely stands alone; it frequently complicates a deficiency pattern, adding a layer of obstruction that traps fluid.
A heavy, cloudy veil across the vision with dizziness, a foggy head, and a greasy tongue coat points to Turbid Dampness obstructing the Middle Burner. The pulse is slippery. This pattern arises when digestion is sluggish, allowing dampness and phlegm to rise and mist the eye’s clear orifices, much like steam fogging a window.
When blurred vision comes with palpitations, restless sleep, poor appetite, and a pale tongue with a weak pulse, Heart and Spleen Deficiency is the root. The Spleen fails to make enough blood, so the Heart cannot house the mind and the eyes lose their nourishment. This pattern often appears in people who overthink or worry.

### Turbid Dampness obstructing the Middle Burner

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Vision like looking through fog or a dirty window, Heavy, wrapped sensation in the head, Sticky or bland taste in the mouth, not thirsty, Poor appetite and upper belly stuffiness after eating, Loose, sticky stools and heavy limbs
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Greasy, rich, or fried foods, Dairy and cold raw foods, Damp, humid environments, Sedentary lifestyle
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warm, lightly spiced meals, Gentle movement or walking, Dry, crisp weather
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When the Spleen is overwhelmed by heavy, greasy foods or chronic dampness, it fails to transform fluids. A turbid, sticky dampness builds up in the middle burner and then drifts upward, clouding the clear orifices of the eyes. This misting of the visual pathway produces a sensation of fog or a veil over the vision, often with a heavy head and a sticky mouth taste.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale, swollen body with tooth marks; thick, white, greasy coating concentrated in the centre. Pulse: soggy (濡 ru) and moderate (缓 huan), or slippery (滑 hua).
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Dampness is a heavy, sticky pathogen that likes to settle and stagnate. Greasy, sweet, and cold foods add more dampness to the middle burner, directly feeding the pattern. Warm, aromatic foods and movement act like a gentle wind and sun, drying and dispersing the turbidity so it can no longer rise to cloud the eyes.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Warm, cooked meals with aromatic spices like ginger, cardamom, and a little pepper help the Spleen transform dampness. Reduce or avoid dairy, sugar, deep-fried foods, and raw cold salads, which all generate more turbid dampness. Gentle daily movement-like walking-keeps Qi and fluids moving so they do not stagnate.

### Kidney and Liver Yin Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/kidney-and-liver-yin-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Gradual, painless central blurring, Dry, gritty sensation in the eyes, Worse in the evening or after overwork, Lower back and knee soreness or weakness, Night sweats, heat in palms and soles, Dizziness, tinnitus, or poor memory
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Overwork and staying up late, Spicy, fried, or drying foods, Chronic stress and anger, Excessive screen time, Hot, dry weather
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Adequate sleep and rest, Cool, humid environment, Moistening foods (pear, goji, black sesame), Gentle eye rest and palming
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  The Liver opens into the eyes and stores Blood, while the Kidneys store Essence that rises to nourish vision. When Liver and Kidney Yin run low, the eyes lose their deepest layer of moisture and nourishment. This leaves the retina dry and undernourished, causing gradual blurring and a sensation of something floating in front of the eye. The deficiency heat that often accompanies Yin depletion can disturb the delicate fluids of the macula, leading to the fluid leakage and central distortion typical of this condition.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red, thin body with cracks, little or no coating, dry surface. Pulse: fine, rapid, and wiry; weak in the left Chi (Kidney) position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Yin represents the body's cooling, moistening, and restorative energy. Late nights, overwork, and stress consume Yin, while heat and dryness further evaporate it. The eyes, as the sense organ most dependent on Yin for lubrication and clarity, are among the first to suffer. Rest, cooling environments, and moistening foods help preserve and rebuild Yin, allowing the eyes to regain their natural nourishment and visual sharpness.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Go to bed by 11 p.m. to protect the Yin restorative window, and take frequent screen breaks to rest the eyes. Eat moistening, dark-colored foods like black sesame, goji berries, mulberries, and pears. Avoid spicy, fried, or drying foods, and keep alcohol and coffee modest. Gentle exercise like walking or Taiji supports Yin without overheating.

### Qi and Yin Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/qi-and-yin-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Blurred central vision that worsens with fatigue, Dry eyes, Fatigue and lack of strength, Shortness of breath, Dry mouth and throat, Spontaneous sweating during the day
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Overwork and mental strain, Spicy, fried, or drying foods, Late nights and sleep deprivation, Excessive sweating or heat
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Adequate sleep and rest, Warm, moistening foods like congee, Gentle movement or walking
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Qi and Yin are the body's vital energy and moisture. When both are depleted, the eyes lose their nourishment and the delicate fluid balance in the retina breaks down. Qi deficiency fails to lift clear Yang upward, so central vision blurs and worsens with fatigue. Yin deficiency dries the eyes and generates subtle empty heat that disturbs the retinal vessels, allowing fluid to seep under the macula and causing distortion.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red or pale-red body with a thin, dry coating, possibly cracked. Pulse: fine (thin) and weak, often rapid.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Qi and Yin are both easily depleted by overwork, mental strain, and insufficient sleep. Rest rebuilds Qi, while warm, moistening foods replenish Yin fluids without burdening digestion. Spicy or fried foods generate heat and dry up precious Yin, directly worsening dry eyes and fluid imbalance. Excessive sweating or heat further depletes both Qi and Yin, deepening the deficiency and making central vision more blurry.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat warm, moistening foods like pear, congee, and small amounts of high-quality protein. Avoid raw, cold, or spicy foods that further damage Qi and Yin. Prioritize deep rest and avoid overwork or late nights, which drain both energy and fluids.

### Blood Stagnation
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/blood-stagnation)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Fixed dark spots or blind spots, Distorted vision (metamorphopsia), Dull, fixed ache around the eye, Worse at night
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Stress and frustration, Cold exposure, Sedentary lifestyle, Fatigue and overwork
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Gentle movement or walking, Warm compress on eyes, Stress reduction, Warm foods
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Blood Stagnation obstructs the tiny collaterals that nourish the retina. When blood flow becomes sluggish and congealed, it creates fixed dark spots and distorted vision. The stasis may also cause a dull, fixed ache around the eye that worsens with rest.

### Heart and Spleen Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/heart-and-spleen-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Blurred or distorted central vision, Palpitations or awareness of heartbeat, Fatigue and lack of energy, Poor appetite with bloating, Difficulty falling asleep or staying asleep, Sallow or pale complexion
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Overwork and mental strain, Irregular eating habits, Cold, raw foods, Worry and excessive thinking, Late nights and sleep deprivation
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warm, easily digested meals, Adequate sleep and rest, Gentle movement or walking, Stress reduction, Nourishing congee or soups
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  The Spleen transforms food into Blood and Qi, while the Heart governs Blood and houses the mind. When both organs are depleted, Blood production falters, and the eyes-which depend on abundant Blood for sharp vision-become undernourished. This leads to blurred central vision and visual distortion, often alongside palpitations, poor sleep, and digestive weakness.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Pale, tender tongue body with teeth marks and a thin white coating. Pulse is fine and weak, possibly choppy, especially weak at the left front (Heart) and right middle (Spleen) positions.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: The Spleen is easily damaged by irregular eating, cold foods, and excessive mental work, which directly impair its ability to produce Blood. The Heart is exhausted by late nights and chronic worry, depleting Blood and disturbing sleep. Nourishing, warm foods and adequate rest rebuild both Spleen and Heart, allowing Blood to reach the eyes and clear vision to return.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat regular, warm, cooked meals to support Spleen digestion-think congee, soups, and stews. Avoid raw, cold, and greasy foods that weaken the Spleen. Prioritize restful sleep and avoid late nights to conserve Heart Blood. Gentle daily movement like walking or tai chi helps Qi circulation without depleting energy.

## คำถามที่พบบ่อย (Frequently Asked Questions)
### Can acupuncture really help with fluid under the retina?
Yes. Acupuncture works by improving local circulation around the eye and correcting the systemic imbalance that allowed the fluid to accumulate. Points around the eye like Jingming (BL-1) and Qiuhou (EX-HN-7) help drain fluid and bring fresh nourishment to the retina, while body points address the underlying pattern - whether that’s strengthening the Spleen, nourishing Yin, or moving stagnant Blood. Many patients notice their vision feels clearer and the distortion softens over a course of weekly sessions.

### How long until I see improvement in my vision?
Most people begin to notice subtle changes within 3 to 6 weeks of consistent herbal treatment and weekly acupuncture. The fluid itself may take longer to fully resolve on OCT - often 8 to 12 weeks - but visual symptoms like blur and distortion often start to ease before the fluid is completely gone. Deficiency patterns require more patience, as rebuilding Yin and Qi is a gradual process.

### Will I need to change my diet?
Diet plays a supportive role and can make a real difference, especially if your pattern involves Dampness or Spleen weakness. In general, it helps to avoid greasy, fried, and heavily processed foods that create turbid dampness, and to eat warm, easily digested meals like soups and congees. Specific foods like goji berries, black sesame, and lightly cooked leafy greens are often recommended to nourish the eyes. Your practitioner will give you more targeted advice based on your pattern.

### Can I combine TCM with my conventional eye treatment?
Yes, TCM can be used safely alongside conventional monitoring and most treatments. If you are receiving laser therapy or photodynamic therapy, acupuncture and herbs can support healing and reduce the chance of recurrence. Always tell both your ophthalmologist and your TCM practitioner about all treatments you are receiving. If you are taking any blood-thinning medications, certain herbs like Dan Shen or Chuan Xiong may need to be avoided or adjusted - your herbalist will check for this.

### What if my vision suddenly gets worse during treatment?
Any sudden change in vision - such as a new curtain-like shadow, a shower of floaters, flashes of light, or a rapid drop in central vision - needs immediate evaluation by an eye doctor. This could indicate a different problem like retinal detachment. TCM treatment does not cause such events, but you should always prioritize an urgent eye exam if your symptoms change abruptly. See our Safety section for a full list of red-flag symptoms.

### Will the fluid come back after TCM treatment?
TCM aims to reduce the likelihood of recurrence by addressing the underlying pattern that made you susceptible in the first place. Many patients who complete a full course of treatment - and maintain the dietary and lifestyle changes - find that episodes become less frequent or stop altogether. However, if the root imbalance is deep (for example, a constitutional Yin deficiency), you may need occasional maintenance treatments during periods of high stress or overwork to keep the eyes stable.
