## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนปัจจุบัน (Conventional Medicine View)
### คำอธิบาย (Description)
In Western medicine, lower abdominal masses are typically discovered during a pelvic exam or imaging study. They may represent uterine fibroids, ovarian cysts, endometriomas, or other gynecological conditions, and their exact nature is determined through ultrasound, MRI, or CT scans. Symptoms can include pelvic pressure, pain, abnormal bleeding, or urinary frequency, though some masses are entirely asymptomatic.

### การรักษาหลัก (Treatments)
Depending on the diagnosis, conventional management may range from watchful waiting and pain relief to hormonal therapies such as birth control pills or GnRH agonists that aim to shrink the mass or control symptoms. Surgical options-myomectomy, cystectomy, or hysterectomy-are considered when masses are large, symptomatic, or suspicious. Treatment is guided by size, symptoms, and the patient's desire for future fertility.

## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนจีน (Traditional Chinese Medicine View)
### ความเข้าใจเกี่ยวกับการแพทย์แผนจีน (TCM Understanding)
TCM views lower abdominal masses as a manifestation of stagnation in the lower burner-the region of the uterus, ovaries, and surrounding tissues. The Liver is primarily responsible for the smooth flow of Qi and Blood; when its function is disrupted by emotional stress, Qi stagnates, and over time Blood follows, forming a fixed mass. The Spleen transforms fluids; if it weakens, dampness and phlegm accumulate and can coalesce into soft, movable masses. Thus, the root lies in the organ systems above, but the mass appears below.
The nature of the mass reveals the underlying pathogen. A hard, fixed, stabbing mass suggests blood stasis, often complicated by cold or heat. Cold congeals blood like ice, causing sharp pain relieved by warmth. Heat scorches blood, creating a painful, warm mass with dark clots.
A soft, mobile, doughy mass points to phlegm-dampness from Spleen deficiency, often accompanied by obesity and heavy vaginal discharge. Even deficiency-when Liver Blood and Kidney Essence fail to nourish the uterus-can lead to a soft mass due to tissue weakness and poor repair.
Because these patterns can overlap, a single Western diagnosis like a fibroid may correspond to different TCM patterns in different women. One woman's fibroid might stem from cold stagnation, another's from Qi and Blood stagnation, and a third's from damp-phlegm. TCM treatment therefore targets the specific pattern to dissolve the mass and correct the underlying imbalance, not just the mass itself.

### การวินิจฉัยโดยแพทย์แผนจีน (Practitioner Diagnosis)
A TCM practitioner begins by asking about the nature of the lump and the pain. Is the mass hard or soft, fixed or movable? Does pain feel cold, hot, or distending? These details, along with menstrual changes and any vaginal discharge, quickly narrow the possibilities.
If the mass feels fixed and causes a distending, aching pain that worsens with stress, Qi and blood stagnation is likely. Menstrual blood is often dark with clots, and the tongue looks purplish with a wiry-choppy pulse. This pattern is common in early stages and responds to moving Qi and blood.
A hard, fixed mass accompanied by cold, stabbing pain that improves with warmth points to cold congealing the blood. The tongue coating is white and greasy, the pulse deep and slow. The person may feel chilled and have scanty, dark menstrual flow.
A soft, ill-defined mass with a heavy sensation suggests damp-phlegm accumulation. Often the person is overweight, has profuse white vaginal discharge, and a swollen tongue with tooth marks. The pulse feels slippery. This pattern stems from a sluggish spleen failing to transform fluids.
When the mass is painful and may feel warm to the touch, with yellow, sticky vaginal discharge, heat and blood stasis are the culprits. The tongue is red with a yellow coating, the pulse rapid and slippery. This often follows an infection or prolonged emotional heat.
A softer mass, scanty pale menses, and a dragging sensation in the lower back and knees suggest deficiency. The tongue is pale and the pulse weak. This pattern reflects a lack of nourishment rather than an excess blockage, and it often develops over a long period.
The most severe presentation is a hard, intensely painful mass with purulent, foul-smelling discharge and systemic signs like fever. The tongue is deep red with a thick yellow coating, the pulse rapid and forceful. This pattern demands urgent medical attention.

### Qi And Blood Stagnation
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/qi-and-blood-stagnation)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Fixed, stabbing pain in the lower abdomen, Pain worse with pressure, Dark, clotted menstrual blood, Distending pain in the chest or rib area, Irritability or mood swings
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Emotional stress and anger, Cold, raw foods, Overwork and exhaustion, Sedentary lifestyle
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warm compress on the lower abdomen, Rest and stress reduction, Gentle movement, Eating warm, cooked meals
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When emotional stress, frustration, or pent-up anger persists, the Liver's ability to keep Qi flowing smoothly becomes blocked. Over time, this stagnant Qi fails to push Blood through the vessels, causing it to congeal and accumulate in the lower abdomen. The result is a fixed, hard mass that causes stabbing pain, dark clotted menstrual blood, and the characteristic purplish tongue.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: purple or dark body with stasis spots, distended sublingual veins, thin white coat. Pulse: wiry (xian) and choppy (se).
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Emotional stress and anger directly aggravate Liver Qi stagnation, the root of this pattern, making the mass and pain worse. Cold congeals and slows blood flow, so warmth and warm foods help dissolve stagnation. Gentle movement promotes Qi circulation, preventing further stasis.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat warm, cooked foods and avoid cold, raw items that can congeal blood. Gentle exercise like walking or tai chi helps keep Qi moving. Manage stress through meditation or breathing exercises, as emotional tension directly worsens this pattern.

### Blood Stagnation in the Lower Burner  caused by Cold
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/blood-stagnation-in-the-lower-burner-caused-by-cold)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Fixed, cold stabbing pain in the lower abdomen, Pain relieved by warmth and worsened by cold, Dark purplish menstrual blood with clots, Cold sensation in the lower belly, hands and feet, Pale or dusky complexion
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Cold weather or drafts, Iced drinks and raw, cold foods, Sedentary lifestyle, Emotional stress and anger
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warm compress on the lower abdomen, Hot drinks and warm soups, Gentle walking, Warm clothing and socks
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Cold has a contracting, freezing nature. When it invades the lower burner-the uterus and surrounding tissues-it constricts blood vessels and slows circulation, causing blood to congeal into a hard, fixed mass. This stagnation produces the characteristic cold, stabbing pain that feels better with warmth.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale-purple or bluish-purple body with possible stasis spots and a white slippery coat. Pulse: deep, tight, and choppy.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Cold constricts blood vessels and freezes the flow of Qi and Blood, which directly causes the stasis and mass in this pattern. Warmth relaxes the vessels, thaws the congealed blood, and restores movement, which is why heat provides such clear relief. Gentle movement also encourages blood flow, while prolonged sitting allows stasis to settle deeper.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Keep the lower abdomen warm with a hot water bottle or warming belt, especially during menstruation. Eat cooked, warm foods like stews, ginger, and cinnamon; strictly avoid raw, cold, and iced items. Gentle daily walking helps keep Qi and blood moving.

### Damp-Phlegm in the Uterus
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/damp-phlegm-in-the-uterus)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Soft, movable lower abdominal mass, Overweight or obesity, Copious thick white vaginal discharge, Heaviness and fullness in the lower abdomen, Chest stuffiness and nausea, Fatigue, drowsiness, and poor appetite
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Cold, damp environments, Sedentary lifestyle, Overeating or rich, greasy foods, Excessive worry or pensiveness, Iced drinks and raw, cold foods
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warm, dry weather, Light, regular exercise, Eating warm, cooked meals, Avoiding dairy and sweets, Ginger or cardamom tea
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When the Spleen is weak, it cannot properly transform food and fluids, leading to a buildup of dampness and phlegm. This heavy, sticky phlegm-dampness sinks into the lower abdomen and obstructs the uterus and its channels, forming soft, movable masses. The mass feels doughy or boggy rather than rock-hard, and it is often accompanied by weight gain, a heavy sensation, and thick white vaginal discharge because the body is overloaded with dampness.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale, swollen body with teeth marks and a white greasy coating thicker at the root. Pulse: slippery, deep, and possibly soft.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Dampness and phlegm are heavy, sticky substances that thrive on cold and stagnation. Cold, raw, and greasy foods weaken the Spleen's digestive fire, causing more dampness to accumulate. A sedentary lifestyle allows Qi to stagnate, which further congeals fluids into phlegm. In contrast, warmth, gentle movement, and light, cooked foods support the Spleen's transforming function, dry out existing dampness, and keep Qi flowing so that masses cannot form or grow.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Avoid cold, raw, greasy, and sweet foods, which create dampness and overwhelm the Spleen. Favor warm, cooked, easily digestible meals like soups, stews, and lightly steamed vegetables. Include small amounts of aromatic spices such as ginger and cardamom to help dry dampness. Gentle daily exercise like walking or yoga is essential to move Qi and reduce phlegm accumulation. Avoid overeating and late-night eating, which burden the Spleen.

### Heat and Blood Stagnation in the Lower Burner
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/heat-and-blood-stagnation-in-the-lower-burner)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Fixed stabbing or cutting pain in the lower abdomen, Lower abdominal hardness that resists pressure, Irritability and mental restlessness, Dark clotted menstrual blood, Fever that worsens at night, Dark purple lips
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Anger and frustration, Spicy, greasy, or fried foods, Alcohol and coffee, Hot weather or saunas, Overwork and exhaustion
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Cooling foods and drinks, Gentle movement, Rest and stress reduction, Cool environment
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When heat accumulates in the lower burner-often from unresolved damp-heat or emotional fire-it scorches the blood, causing it to congeal and stagnate. This heat-driven stasis forms a hard, fixed mass in the lower abdomen, similar to how extreme heat can thicken and clot a liquid. The trapped heat also rises to disturb the mind, producing the restlessness and agitation that set this pattern apart from cold-type stasis.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: reddish-purple body with stasis spots, distended sublingual veins, dry yellow coating. Pulse: deep, choppy, and rapid when heat is pronounced; left chi position full, tight, or wiry.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Emotional stress and anger generate Liver fire, which adds heat to the blood and worsens stasis. Spicy, greasy foods and alcohol introduce more damp-heat, fueling the underlying heat pathology. Cooling foods and a calm environment help clear heat, while gentle movement keeps Qi and blood flowing so stasis cannot deepen. Rest preserves Yin, preventing the internal heat from flaring.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Avoid spicy, greasy, and grilled foods, alcohol, and coffee-they fan the internal heat. Eat cooling, blood-moving foods like celery, eggplant, mung beans, and small amounts of hawthorn berry. Gentle daily movement such as walking or yoga helps prevent Qi stagnation, but avoid overexertion that depletes Yin and worsens heat.

Overwork, stress, and poor diet drain these reserves further, allowing the undernourished tissues to form a mass. Rest, warm nourishing foods, and gentle movement replenish Blood and Essence, giving the uterus the support it needs to gradually dissolve the soft mass.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat warm, cooked, nourishing foods like bone broth, black sesame, goji berries, dark leafy greens, and eggs. ### Toxic-Heat Stagnation
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/toxic-heat-stagnation)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Severe, fixed pain with a sensation of heat in the mass, High fever and a persistent feeling of intense heat, Foul-smelling, yellow, purulent vaginal discharge, Intense thirst with a desire for cold drinks, Restlessness and irritability
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Spicy, greasy, or fried foods, Alcohol and coffee, Physical overexertion, Emotional stress and anger, Hot, humid weather
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Cooling foods and drinks, Complete rest, Plenty of fluids, Cool environment
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  In this serious pattern, a virulent heat toxin invades the body during a time of weakness, such as after childbirth or during menstruation. This toxic heat dives deep into the lower burner, scorching the blood and fluids, and combining with stagnant blood to form a hard, intensely painful mass. The heat boils the body's fluids into pus, leading to a foul-smelling discharge and a high fever as the body's righteous Qi battles the invading toxin.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: deep red or crimson body with a thick, dry, yellow coating. Pulse: rapid (shu) and forceful (shi), reflecting the fierce fight between the body's Qi and the toxic heat.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Toxic heat is a powerful, fiery pathogen that consumes the body's cooling fluids. Spicy, greasy foods and alcohol act like 'adding fuel to the fire,' making the heat and pus formation worse. Rest and a cool environment conserve the body's Yin fluids and Qi, which are needed to fight the infection. Cooling foods like mung beans directly counteract the heat and help flush out the toxins, providing a natural, gentle support to the body's healing process.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: This is an acute, serious condition that requires urgent medical attention. During recovery, eat only light, cooling, easy-to-digest foods like mung bean soup, cucumber, and watermelon. Strictly avoid all spicy, greasy, and rich foods, as well as alcohol, which add more heat and dampness to the body. Rest is essential to allow the body's Qi to fight the infection.

## คำถามที่พบบ่อย (Frequently Asked Questions)
### Can acupuncture and herbs really shrink a fibroid or cyst?
Yes, many women experience measurable reduction in mass size on follow-up ultrasound after 3-6 months of consistent treatment. TCM works by improving circulation, dissolving stagnation, and restoring hormonal balance, which can cause the mass to gradually soften and shrink. Results vary depending on the pattern and the size of the mass, but even when the mass does not disappear completely, symptoms like pain and heavy bleeding often improve significantly.

### Is it safe to use TCM for lower abdominal masses while trying to conceive?
In most cases, yes. Many TCM treatments for masses also support fertility by regulating the menstrual cycle and improving uterine blood flow. However, certain strong blood-moving herbs are avoided during the luteal phase or if pregnancy is possible. Always inform your practitioner if you are actively trying to conceive so the formula can be adjusted accordingly.

### Will the mass come back after TCM treatment?
Because TCM addresses the underlying imbalance that allowed the mass to form-such as Liver Qi stagnation, Spleen weakness, or Cold in the uterus-recurrence is less likely than with approaches that only remove the mass. Maintaining a healthy diet, managing stress, and periodic follow-up with your practitioner can help sustain the results long-term.

### How soon can I expect to see results?
Most women notice improvements in pain, bloating, or menstrual flow within the first 4-6 weeks. The mass itself typically takes longer to shrink, with measurable changes often seen on imaging after 3-6 months. Consistency with herbs and acupuncture sessions is the most important factor in achieving results.

### Can TCM be combined with hormonal therapy or surgery?
Yes, TCM can complement conventional treatments. It may help reduce side effects of hormonal medications and support recovery after surgery. If you are taking hormonal drugs or blood thinners, always inform both your doctor and TCM practitioner, as some herbs may interact. Herbs are often paused just before and after surgery to avoid any risk of increased bleeding.

### Do I need to change my diet?
Diet plays an important role. In general, avoid cold, raw foods and icy drinks, which can congeal Qi and Blood. Reduce greasy, sweet, and dairy foods that create dampness and phlegm. Favor warm, cooked meals and include moderate amounts of blood-nourishing foods like dark leafy greens, beets, and lean meats. Your practitioner will give more specific guidance based on your pattern.

### What if I have no symptoms but an ultrasound found a mass?
Even without symptoms, a mass indicates an underlying stagnation that may eventually cause problems. TCM can be used to gently move Qi and Blood and prevent the mass from growing or becoming symptomatic. Early treatment is often simpler and faster than waiting until pain or bleeding develops.

## การวิจัยทางวิทยาศาสตร์สมัยใหม่ (Modern Scientific Research)
1. **Chinese herbal medicine for uterine fibroids** ([ลิงก์](https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD008258.pub2))
   *วารสารวิชาการ: Liu J, Yang M, Du X. Chinese herbal medicine for uterine fibroids. Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews 2013, Issue 2. Art. No.: CD008258.*
   A Cochrane systematic review evaluating the effectiveness and safety of Chinese herbal medicine for uterine fibroids. The review found limited evidence that some herbal preparations may improve symptoms and reduce fibroid size, but the quality of included trials was generally poor.

2. **Guizhi Fuling Wan for uterine fibroids: a systematic review and meta-analysis** ([ลิงก์](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jep.2019.111882))
   *วารสารวิชาการ: Gao J, et al. Guizhi Fuling Wan for uterine fibroids: a systematic review and meta-analysis. Journal of Ethnopharmacology 2019; 238: 111882.*
   This meta-analysis of randomized controlled trials assessed Gui Zhi Fu Ling Wan for uterine fibroids. It concluded that the formula, alone or with conventional therapy, significantly reduced fibroid volume and improved symptoms like menorrhagia and dysmenorrhea, with a favorable safety profile.

3. **Acupuncture for pain in endometriosis** ([ลิงก์](https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD007864.pub2))
   *วารสารวิชาการ: Zhu X, Hamilton KD, McNicol ED. Acupuncture for pain in endometriosis. Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews 2011, Issue 9. Art. No.: CD007864.*
   A Cochrane review examining acupuncture for pain associated with endometriosis, a condition that often presents with pelvic masses. The review found that acupuncture may offer short-term pain relief, but the evidence was insufficient to recommend it as a definitive treatment for the underlying masses.
