## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนปัจจุบัน (Conventional Medicine View)
### คำอธิบาย (Description)
Cerebral vascular insufficiency refers to a chronic reduction in blood flow to the brain, often caused by atherosclerosis, small vessel disease, or other vascular narrowing. It can produce a range of symptoms including dizziness, brain fog, poor concentration, headaches, and in more advanced cases, transient ischemic attacks (TIAs). Diagnosis is typically made through imaging such as MRI, CT angiography, or Doppler ultrasound to assess blood flow and vessel health.

### การรักษาหลัก (Treatments)
Standard treatment aims to manage underlying risk factors and prevent stroke. This often includes antiplatelet medications like aspirin or clopidogrel, cholesterol-lowering statins, and strict blood pressure control. In some cases, surgical procedures such as carotid endarterectomy may be recommended. Lifestyle changes - quitting smoking, improving diet, and increasing exercise - are also emphasized to slow disease progression.

## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนจีน (Traditional Chinese Medicine View)
### ความเข้าใจเกี่ยวกับการแพทย์แผนจีน (TCM Understanding)
In TCM, the brain is seen as the "sea of marrow," nourished by the upward flow of clear Qi and Blood. This nourishment depends on the smooth functioning of several organ systems. The Liver stores Blood and ensures the free flow of Qi; if Liver Qi stagnates or Liver Yang flares upward, the head can feel throbbing, dizzy, and pressured. The Spleen transforms food into Qi and Blood; when it's weak, dampness and Phlegm can form, rising to cloud the brain and causing a heavy, foggy sensation.
The Kidneys store essence and produce marrow, which fills the brain. As we age or after prolonged overwork, Kidney Yin and Essence can become depleted, leaving the brain undernourished and overheated - this produces dizziness with tinnitus, poor memory, and a feeling of emptiness. Blood Stagnation is another common mechanism: when circulation is sluggish due to chronic Qi stagnation or deficiency, the brain's vessels become obstructed, leading to fixed, stabbing headaches and mental dullness.
Because so many different organ imbalances can reduce cerebral blood flow, the same Western diagnosis can arise from opposite TCM patterns. One person's dizziness may flare with stress and anger (Liver Yang Rising), while another's worsens with fatigue and poor appetite (Qi and Blood Deficiency). This is why TCM doesn't treat "cerebral vascular insufficiency" as one disease - it identifies the exact pattern and tailors the treatment accordingly, using herbs, acupuncture, and dietary changes to correct the root imbalance and restore healthy brain circulation.

### การวินิจฉัยโดยแพทย์แผนจีน (Practitioner Diagnosis)
A TCM practitioner starts by asking about the sensation itself: is the dizziness a spinning vertigo, a floating lightness, or a heavy, foggy feeling? What makes it flare up or settle down-stress, fatigue, certain foods? The tongue and pulse then confirm which pattern is driving the brain's insufficient blood supply.
If dizziness comes with a throbbing headache, a flushed face, and a short temper, and the pulse feels tight like a guitar string (wiry, 脉弦), Liver Yang Rising is likely. Stress is a major trigger, and the tongue may appear red with a yellow coating.
When the head feels heavy as if wrapped in a cloth, accompanied by chest tightness and nausea, a greasy tongue coating and a slippery pulse (脉滑) point to Phlegm obstructing the collaterals. This pattern often follows a diet rich in fatty, sweet, or damp-producing foods.
A fixed, stabbing headache that worsens at night, together with a dark purple tongue and a choppy pulse, signals Blood Stagnation in the brain's vessels. This typically develops in chronic cases where circulation has been sluggish for a long time.
Dizziness that intensifies with even gentle activity, combined with deep fatigue and a pale, washed-out complexion, suggests Qi and Blood Deficiency. The pulse is weak and thin, and the tongue looks pale-signs that the brain is simply not receiving enough nourishment.
When dizziness is paired with ringing in the ears, a sensation of heat in the palms and soles, and a red tongue with very little coating, the picture is Liver Yang Rising with Blood and Yin Deficiency. The pulse is thin and rapid, reflecting yin that is too depleted to anchor the rising yang.
In older adults, a gradual onset of dizziness with dry eyes, night sweats, and a red tongue with scant coating often reveals Kidney and Liver Yin Deficiency. This deep, underlying depletion is a common root that predisposes the brain's vessels to insufficiency.

## กลุ่มอาการการแพทย์แผนจีนและการรักษา (TCM Patterns and Treatment)

### Liver Yang Rising
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/liver-yang-rising)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Throbbing or distending headache, especially at the temples, Dizziness with a sensation of heavy head and light, unsteady feet, Flushed face and red eyes, Irritability and quick temper, Ringing in the ears (tinnitus)
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Stress and anger, Spicy, greasy, or fried foods, Alcohol and caffeine, Overwork and insufficient sleep, Hot weather
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Rest in a quiet, dark room, Cooling foods like cucumber and celery, Chrysanthemum or peppermint tea, Stress management and relaxation, Gentle movement (e.g., walking or Tai Chi)
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When Liver Yin is depleted-often from aging, prolonged stress, or overwork-it can no longer anchor Liver Yang. The unmoored Yang surges upward to the head, bringing heat and wind that disturb the clear senses. This upward rush disrupts the steady nourishment of the brain, leading to the dizziness, throbbing headaches, and unsteadiness that are characteristic of cerebral vascular insufficiency.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red body, especially at the sides, with a thin dry yellow or scanty coating. Pulse: wiry (弦 xián), taut like a guitar string; may also be fine and rapid if Yin deficiency is marked.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Stress and anger directly provoke Liver Qi to surge upward, which is the core mechanism of this pattern, so calming the mind and avoiding emotional spikes are essential. Spicy, greasy foods and alcohol generate internal heat, further fueling the rising Yang. Overwork and lack of sleep deplete Liver Yin, weakening its ability to anchor Yang, so rest and Yin-nourishing foods are protective. Gentle exercise helps keep Qi moving smoothly, preventing stagnation that can later burst upward.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Reduce spicy, fried, and greasy foods; limit alcohol and caffeine. Enjoy cooling, Yin-nourishing foods like cucumber, celery, and chrysanthemum tea. Practice gentle daily movement such as walking or tai chi to keep Qi flowing, and prioritize winding down before bed to calm the mind.

### Phlegm in the Channels joints and muscles
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/phlegm-in-the-channels-joints-and-muscles)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Heavy, foggy head sensation, Chest tightness and nausea, Greasy white tongue coating, Limb heaviness or numbness, Pulse: slippery or wiry-choppy
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Greasy, dairy-rich foods, Cold and raw foods, Damp weather, Sedentary lifestyle, Stress and worry
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Gentle movement (e.g., walking or Tai Chi), Warmth on the neck and head, Light, warm meals, Ginger tea, Dry weather
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When Phlegm and Blood Stasis accumulate in the Channels, they can obstruct the delicate vessels that supply the brain. This blockage reduces blood flow, causing a heavy, foggy sensation in the head, dizziness, and sometimes fixed stabbing headaches. The Phlegm contributes to a feeling of mental cloudiness and chest tightness, while the Stasis leads to a purplish tongue and a choppy pulse.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: purple body with stasis spots, white greasy coating, distended sublingual veins. Pulse: wiry and choppy (Se) or wiry and slippery (Hua).
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Phlegm is formed when the Spleen fails to transform fluids, often due to overconsumption of greasy, dairy, or cold foods. These substances directly worsen the Phlegm obstruction. Warmth and gentle movement stimulate Qi circulation, helping the body transform Dampness and move Blood. Dry weather reduces external Dampness, while damp conditions add to the burden.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Avoid greasy, dairy, and cold foods that create Phlegm. Eat lightly cooked vegetables and warming spices like ginger to transform Dampness. Gentle daily movement like walking helps keep Qi and blood flowing. Keep the neck and head warm to prevent stagnation.

### Blood Stagnation
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/blood-stagnation)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Fixed, stabbing head pain, Pain worsens at night or with rest, Dark purple tongue with stasis spots, Dark circles under the eyes, Rough, dry skin
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Cold weather, Sedentary lifestyle, Stress and frustration, Heavy, greasy foods, Nighttime (pain intensifies)
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Gentle movement (e.g., walking or Tai Chi), Warmth on the neck and head, Stress reduction, Light massage
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When blood flow stagnates inside the brain's delicate vessels, the brain does not receive the fresh nourishment it needs. This produces a characteristic fixed, stabbing headache that often feels worse at night or when resting. The stasis can build slowly from chronic stress, unresolved Qi stagnation, or long-standing vascular strain, leaving the tongue dark purple and the pulse rough and choppy.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: purple or dusky body, possible stasis spots, engorged dark sublingual veins, thin white coat. Pulse: choppy (涩 Sè), rough like a knife scraping bamboo; may also be wiry (弦 Xián) if Qi stagnation is present.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Cold and inactivity congeal blood, making stasis worse, while gentle movement and warmth encourage blood to flow more freely. Stress causes Qi stagnation, which then leads to blood stasis, so calming the mind helps break the cycle. Nighttime is Yin time, when circulation naturally slows, which is why stabbing pain often intensifies then; moving and warming during the day counteract that tendency.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat warming, blood-moving foods like turmeric, ginger, dark leafy greens, and moderate amounts of onion or garlic. Avoid cold, raw foods and excessive dairy, which can congeal blood and worsen stasis. Gentle daily exercise such as walking or tai chi keeps Qi and blood flowing, while prolonged sitting should be broken up with short movement breaks.

### Qi and Blood Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/qi-and-blood-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Dizziness worse with exertion, Deep fatigue and weakness, Pale or sallow complexion, Heart palpitations, Poor appetite
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Overwork and stress, Skipping meals, Cold and raw foods, Excessive standing or exertion
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Rest and naps, Warm, nourishing meals, Gentle movement (e.g., walking or Tai Chi)
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When Qi and Blood are both deficient, the body lacks the energy and nourishment needed to fuel the brain. The upward flow of clear Yang is weak, so the brain is not properly supplied, leading to dizziness that worsens with any exertion. The heart, which governs blood and houses the mind, also suffers, causing palpitations, poor memory, and a pale complexion.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale body, may be thin or show teeth marks, with a thin white coating. Pulse: fine (thin) and weak, especially in the right middle position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Qi and Blood are produced by the Spleen from food and rest. Overwork and skipping meals deplete them, while warm, easy-to-digest foods and adequate rest allow the body to rebuild. Gentle movement helps Qi circulate without draining it, while raw cold foods impair Spleen function, further weakening production.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat warm, cooked meals that are easy to digest, such as soups, stews, and congees with lean meats, ginger, and red dates. Avoid raw, cold foods and icy drinks, which weaken the Spleen. Prioritize rest and gentle activities like walking or tai chi; avoid overwork and intense exercise that drain Qi. A regular sleep schedule helps the body rebuild Blood.

### Liver Yang Rising with Blood and Yin Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/liver-yang-rising-with-blood-and-yin-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Throbbing temple headache, Dizziness with spinning sensation, Ringing in the ears (tinnitus), Irritability and quick to anger, Weak aching lower back and knees
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Stress and anger, Spicy, greasy, or fried foods, Overwork and insufficient sleep, Alcohol and caffeine, Hot weather
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Rest in a quiet, dark room, Cool drinks, Nourishing Yin foods (black sesame, goji), Gentle movement (e.g., walking or Tai Chi), Cool environment
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  The Liver stores Blood, and Kidney Yin is the root of all Yin. When these reserves run low, the Liver's Yang has nothing to anchor it and surges upward. This upward rush of heat disturbs the head, causing the throbbing dizziness and pressure-like headache of cerebral vascular insufficiency, while the brain itself remains undernourished because the body's foundation is depleted.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red, thin body with red sides, scanty or peeled coating, dry surface. Pulse: wiry and fine, especially at the chi position; left guan position wiry and forceful.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Stress and anger stir up Liver Yang, which is already unanchored, worsening the upward rush of heat. Spicy, fried, and heating foods and drinks add more heat and deplete Yin further, while overwork and late nights consume the body's Yin reserves. In contrast, rest and cool, Yin-nourishing foods help replenish the depleted foundation, and a calm environment allows the rising Yang to settle back down.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat cooling, Yin-nourishing foods like black sesame, goji berries, mulberries, spinach, and pears. Avoid spicy, fried, or greasy foods, and limit alcohol and coffee. Prioritize rest and avoid overwork or late nights. Gentle exercises like Tai Chi or walking help circulate Qi without depleting Yin.

### Kidney and Liver Yin Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/kidney-and-liver-yin-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Dizziness that is worse when standing or tired, Ringing in the ears (tinnitus), Night sweats, Heat in the palms, soles, and chest (five-center heat), Soreness and weakness of the lower back and knees, Dry eyes with blurred vision
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Overwork and insufficient sleep, Stress and emotional strain, Spicy, greasy, or fried foods, Alcohol and caffeine, Hot weather
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Rest and quiet, Cool environment, Moistening foods (pear, black sesame), Staying well hydrated, Gentle movement (e.g., walking or Tai Chi)
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  The Kidneys store essence and the Liver stores blood, and together they nourish the brain and sensory organs. When Kidney and Liver Yin become deeply depleted, the brain is starved of the cooling, moistening nourishment it needs, leading to dizziness, poor concentration, and tinnitus. The resulting deficiency heat floats upward, causing night sweats, a dry mouth, and a sensation of heat in the palms, soles, and chest. Over time, this root dryness weakens the lower back and knees, classic signs of Kidney deficiency.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red, thin body with cracks, little or no coating, dry surface. Pulse: thin, rapid, and often wiry; left Chi position weak.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Kidney and Liver Yin are the body's cooling, moistening foundation. Overwork, stress, and late nights burn through this Yin, while heating foods and alcohol add more heat, worsening the dryness and deficiency heat. Rest, a cool environment, and moistening foods help preserve and slowly rebuild Yin, allowing the brain to be properly nourished again.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat cooling, moistening foods like pears, black sesame, goji berries, and cucumber. Avoid spicy, fried, and overly stimulating foods that consume Yin. Prioritize restful sleep and avoid overwork; exhaustion deepens Yin deficiency. Gentle exercise like Tai Chi or walking can help, but avoid sweating heavily.

## คำถามที่พบบ่อย (Frequently Asked Questions)
### Can TCM really improve blood flow to my brain?
Yes. Acupuncture has been shown to increase cerebral blood flow by dilating blood vessels and improving microcirculation. Herbal formulas are designed to address the specific pattern causing sluggish flow - whether it's by dissolving Phlegm, invigorating Blood, or nourishing Qi and Yin. Many patients report feeling clearer-headed and less dizzy after several weeks of consistent treatment.

### How long until I feel less dizzy?
For excess patterns like Liver Yang Rising or Phlegm obstruction, dizziness often begins to lift within 2-4 weeks of starting herbs and acupuncture. If your dizziness is due to deeper deficiency (Qi and Blood or Kidney Yin), it may take 6-8 weeks to notice a steady improvement, and full resolution can take several months. Your practitioner will track your progress and adjust the formula as needed.

### Can I continue my blood pressure or blood-thinning medication?
Absolutely - never stop prescribed medications without consulting your doctor. TCM treatment can safely complement most conventional therapies. However, some herbs like Dang Gui and Chuan Xiong have mild blood-thinning effects, so your TCM practitioner must know all your medications to avoid interactions. Always inform both your doctor and your TCM practitioner about everything you're taking.

### Is acupuncture safe if I've had a mini-stroke (TIA)?
Acupuncture is generally safe after a TIA, and it's often used to support recovery and prevent recurrence by improving circulation and calming the nervous system. However, you should always tell your acupuncturist about your full medical history, including any TIAs or strokes. In the early days after a TIA, treatment may be gentle and focused on distal points rather than the head.

### Will I need to change my diet?
Diet plays a supporting role. Generally, you'll be advised to avoid greasy, fried, and sugary foods that create Phlegm and dampness. If your pattern involves Liver Yang Rising, you'll also want to limit alcohol, caffeine, and spicy foods. Nourishing, warm, easily digested meals - soups, congees, steamed vegetables - help build Qi and Blood. Your practitioner will give you specific guidance based on your pattern.

### How many acupuncture sessions will I need?
Most patients start with one or two sessions per week for 6-12 weeks. After that, the frequency often reduces to once every two weeks or monthly for maintenance. The total number depends on your pattern and how long you've had symptoms - recent-onset excess patterns may need fewer sessions, while chronic deficiency patterns benefit from longer, consistent care.
