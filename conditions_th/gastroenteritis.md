## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนปัจจุบัน (Conventional Medicine View)
### คำอธิบาย (Description)
Gastroenteritis is inflammation of the stomach and intestines, usually caused by a viral or bacterial infection. Common symptoms include sudden onset of watery diarrhea, nausea, vomiting, abdominal cramps, and sometimes low-grade fever. Most cases are self-limiting and resolve within a few days with rest and rehydration. Diagnosis is typically based on symptoms, though stool tests may be done if symptoms are severe or prolonged.

### การรักษาหลัก (Treatments)
Standard treatment focuses on preventing dehydration with oral rehydration solutions, along with rest and a bland diet. Over-the-counter antiemetics or antidiarrheals may be used cautiously, but are often avoided in infectious cases to allow the body to clear the pathogen. Antibiotics are reserved for confirmed bacterial infections or severe cases.

## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนจีน (Traditional Chinese Medicine View)
### ความเข้าใจเกี่ยวกับการแพทย์แผนจีน (TCM Understanding)
In TCM, gastroenteritis is seen as an invasion of external pathogens-Dampness, Heat, Cold, or Summer Heat-into the Spleen and Stomach. The Spleen is responsible for transforming food and fluids into usable energy and transporting them upward. When it's overwhelmed by dampness or cold, it fails to hold things up, and fluids rush downward as diarrhea. The Stomach, which normally sends food downward, rebels upward when irritated, causing nausea and vomiting.
The specific pathogen determines the pattern. Damp-Heat, often from contaminated food or hot, humid weather, produces explosive, foul-smelling diarrhea with a burning sensation and thick yellow tongue coating.
Cold-Damp, triggered by cold drinks or exposure to damp cold, leads to watery, painless diarrhea that feels better with warmth and a pale, swollen tongue. Summer Heat with Dampness, unique to hot, humid seasons, adds heavy fatigue and fever that doesn't break with sweating. And when the acute infection passes but leaves the digestive system depleted, Spleen Qi Deficiency sets in, causing chronic loose stools, bloating, and fatigue.
Because each pattern has its own root-heat, cold, dampness, or deficiency-the treatment must match. Using warming herbs for a damp-heat infection will worsen the burning and diarrhea, while cooling herbs for cold-damp will further chill the gut. A TCM practitioner reads the tongue and pulse to pinpoint the exact imbalance, then selects formulas and acupuncture points to clear the pathogen and restore the Spleen and Stomach's normal rhythm.

### การวินิจฉัยโดยแพทย์แผนจีน (Practitioner Diagnosis)
A practitioner will first examine the stool and the sensation of heat. In Damp-Heat in Stomach and Spleen, the diarrhea is typically explosive, foul-smelling, and may contain mucus or blood. The tongue is red with a thick, yellow, greasy coating, and the pulse feels rapid and slippery. The person often feels burning in the anus, has a bitter taste in the mouth, and may vomit sour or bitter fluid.
If the diarrhea is watery, without strong odor, and the abdominal pain is relieved by a hot water bottle, the pattern is likely Cold-Damp invading the Spleen. The tongue appears pale with a white, slippery coat, and the pulse is slow and soft. This pattern often follows consuming cold drinks or raw foods, and the person may feel chilled and have no thirst.
When gastroenteritis becomes chronic or lingers after an acute episode, the focus shifts to Spleen and Stomach Qi Deficiency. The stools are loose but not urgent, and fatigue, poor appetite, and a sense of heaviness after eating dominate. The tongue is pale with a thin white coat, and the pulse is weak and thready. This pattern reflects a weakened digestive fire that cannot transform food properly.
In hot, humid weather, Summer Heat with Dampness can strike suddenly. The person feels nauseated, heavy-limbed, and may have a mild fever with a stifling sensation in the chest. Diarrhea is urgent and watery, but the tongue may be red with a thin yellow or greasy coat, and the pulse is rapid and soft. This pattern is distinguished by the seasonal context and the combination of heat signs with a distinct feeling of damp oppression.

### Damp-Heat in Stomach and Spleen
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/damp-heat-in-stomach-and-spleen)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Foul-smelling or sticky diarrhea that feels incomplete, Bitter or slimy taste in the mouth, Yellow greasy tongue coating, Heavy, foggy head and body
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Greasy, fried, or spicy foods, Alcohol, Damp, humid weather, Overeating or heavy meals
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Light, bland meals, Rest, Cool, dry environment
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  External damp-heat invades the Stomach and Spleen, often through contaminated food or hot, humid environments. The dampness and heat combine to disrupt the normal ascending and descending of Qi, causing the Stomach Qi to rebel upward as nausea and vomiting. Simultaneously, the Spleen's ability to separate clear from turbid fluids fails, sending the turbid damp-heat downward as foul, sticky diarrhea.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red body, possibly swollen, with a thick yellow greasy coating especially in the centre. Pulse: soggy (Ru), rapid (Shu), and often slippery (Hua), strongest at the right Guan position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Damp-Heat thrives on rich, greasy, and spicy foods, which add more heat and dampness to the body and overwhelm the Spleen and Stomach. Humid environments do the same externally. Resting and eating bland, easily digested foods reduces the burden on the digestive system, while cooling and drying measures help the body clear the pathogenic factors and restore normal Qi flow.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Stick to a light, bland diet of congee, steamed vegetables, and rice water. Avoid all greasy, fried, spicy, and sweet foods, as well as alcohol and dairy, which generate dampness and heat. Drink warm water at room temperature; ice-cold drinks will only trap the dampness. Rest as much as possible to allow your digestive system to recover.

### Cold-Damp invading the Spleen
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/cold-damp-invading-the-spleen)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Watery diarrhea without strong odor, Dull abdominal pain that improves with warmth, Heavy sensation in body and limbs, Nausea or vomiting of clear fluid
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Cold, raw foods and iced drinks, Cold damp weather, Overeating or heavy meals, Dairy and greasy food
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warmth on the abdomen, Warm, easily digestible foods, Rest, Ginger tea
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When cold and dampness invade the body-often from eating cold raw foods or being exposed to cold wet weather-they lodge in the Spleen and Stomach. The Spleen's job is to transform fluids and transport nutrients, but cold-damp obstructs this function like a frozen pipe. Fluid accumulates and rushes downward as watery diarrhea, while the cold causes dull pain that feels better with warmth.
The heavy limbs and nausea result from dampness weighing down the body and disrupting the Stomach's downward flow.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale and swollen with teeth marks, thick white greasy coating. Pulse: soggy (Ru) or slippery (Hua) and slow (Chi).
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Cold-damp obstructs the Spleen's yang energy, which is like a digestive fire. Cold and raw foods douse that fire, while warmth and cooked foods gently rekindle it. Rest conserves Qi so the body can focus on recovery.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Keep the abdomen warm and avoid all cold and raw foods. Sip warm ginger tea or rice porridge to gently warm the digestive fire. Wear a belly band or use a hot water bottle if needed.

### Spleen and Stomach Qi Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/spleen-and-stomach-qi-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Bloating and fullness that worsens after eating, Loose stools without strong odor, Poor appetite, Fatigue and physical weakness, Sallow or pale complexion
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Cold, raw foods and iced drinks, Overeating or heavy meals, Overwork and exhaustion, Damp or cold weather, Prolonged stress or worry
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warm, easily digestible foods, Small frequent meals, Rest, Warmth on the abdomen
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  After an acute bout of gastroenteritis, the Spleen and Stomach are often left depleted. Their ability to transform food into usable Qi and to transport fluids is weakened. Without that digestive power, food is not properly processed, leaving you with chronic loose stools, bloating that worsens after eating, and a deep fatigue that lingers long after the stomach bug has passed.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale, puffy, tooth-marked body with a thin white coating. Pulse: weak, soft, and forceless, especially at the right guan position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: When Spleen Qi is weak, it lacks the warmth and power to transform food. Cold and raw foods further chill the digestive fire, while overeating overwhelms an already fragile system. Rest conserves the Qi that would otherwise be spent on activity, allowing the body to redirect energy toward healing. Stress and worry directly weaken the Spleen through the overacting Liver, so a calm mind protects the digestive center.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Focus on warm, easily digestible foods like congee, soups, and stewed vegetables. Eat small, frequent meals rather than large portions, and avoid raw, cold, or greasy foods that tax the digestive fire. Gentle exercise like walking can help, but prioritize rest until energy returns.

### Summer Heat with Dampness
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/summer-heat-with-dampness)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Fever with body heaviness, Sweating that does not relieve fever, Thirst with little desire to drink, Headache with a heavy, wrapped sensation, Irritability and restlessness, Loose sticky stools, Greasy tongue coating
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Hot, humid weather, Greasy, fried, or spicy foods, Cold, raw foods and iced drinks, Overexertion in the heat
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Light, bland meals, Resting in a cool, dry place, Sipping warm fluids, Avoiding greasy and heavy foods
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Summer Heat is a seasonal pathogen that combines with environmental Dampness to invade the body. It attacks the Spleen and Stomach, disrupting their ability to transform fluids and food. The Heat causes fever and irritability, while the Dampness creates a heavy, sticky sensation and obstructs the chest and abdomen. This leads to nausea, loose sticky stools, and a fever that sweating cannot relieve because the Dampness traps the Heat.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red body with a white greasy coating (may turn yellow greasy if Heat predominates). Pulse: soggy (Ru) and rapid (Shu).
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Summer Heat and Dampness are heavy, sticky pathogens that clog the Spleen and Stomach. Hot, humid weather and heavy foods add more Dampness and Heat, making symptoms worse. Cold drinks may feel soothing but they shock the digestive system and trap Dampness inside, preventing the body from clearing it. Light, warm meals and rest in a cool, dry place give the Spleen a chance to recover and drain the Dampness, while gentle hydration helps the body clear the Heat without adding to the burden.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat light, easily digestible foods like congee, steamed vegetables, and mung bean soup. Avoid heavy, greasy, spicy, or raw cold foods that burden the Spleen. Sip warm water or herbal teas such as chrysanthemum, but avoid ice-cold drinks. Rest in a cool, well-ventilated space, but avoid direct drafts or excessive air conditioning that can trap Dampness.

## คำถามที่พบบ่อย (Frequently Asked Questions)
### Can TCM treat food poisoning?
Yes, TCM is very effective for mild to moderate food poisoning, especially when caused by damp-heat pathogens. Herbs like Huang Lian (coptis) and Ge Gen (kudzu) have strong antimicrobial and anti-inflammatory properties that target the gut. Acupuncture can quickly relieve cramping and vomiting. However, if you suspect severe food poisoning with high fever, bloody stools, or botulism symptoms, seek emergency medical care immediately.

### What should I eat during a stomach flu according to TCM?
Stick to warm, easily digestible foods like plain congee (rice porridge), steamed vegetables, and ginger tea. Avoid raw, cold, greasy, spicy, and dairy foods-these introduce more dampness and cold, which your weakened Spleen cannot handle. Even after symptoms subside, continue with bland foods for a couple of days to let your digestion fully recover.

### How does acupuncture stop diarrhea?
Acupuncture points like ST-25 (Tianshu) and ST-36 (Zusanli) directly regulate the Large Intestine and Stomach, calming spasms and restoring the normal upward and downward movement of Qi. For damp-heat, points like LI-11 are added to clear heat; for cold-damp, moxibustion (warming the points with an herb stick) is used to warm the gut. Many patients feel their cramping and urgency decrease within minutes of needle insertion.

### Is it safe to take Chinese herbs with antibiotics?
Yes, but take them at least two hours apart to avoid any potential interaction. Always inform both your TCM practitioner and your doctor about all medications and supplements you are taking. Certain herbs like Huang Lian have natural antibacterial effects and may complement antibiotic therapy, but should only be used under professional guidance.

### How quickly can TCM relieve vomiting?
Acupuncture can often stop or significantly reduce vomiting within one session, especially when using points like PC-6 (Neiguan) and REN-12 (Zhongwan). Herbs taken as a tea or powder may take a few hours to take effect, but together they provide rapid relief. For acute gastroenteritis, many patients feel much better within 24 hours of starting treatment.

### Does TCM work for viral gastroenteritis?
Absolutely. TCM doesn't distinguish between viral and bacterial in the same way-it treats the pattern of symptoms. Whether the pathogen is a virus or bacteria, if it presents as damp-heat with burning diarrhea and yellow tongue coating, the same cooling, damp-drying herbs are used. This is why TCM can be effective even when conventional medicine has no specific antiviral treatment.

### Why do I feel so tired after a stomach bug and how does TCM help?
After an infection, your Spleen and Stomach Qi are often depleted. The Spleen is the main organ for extracting energy from food, so when it's weak, you feel fatigued and may have lingering loose stools. TCM uses tonifying formulas like Shen Ling Bai Zhu San to rebuild digestive Qi, along with dietary therapy to restore your energy over a few weeks.
