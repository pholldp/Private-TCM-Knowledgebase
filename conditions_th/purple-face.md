## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนปัจจุบัน (Conventional Medicine View)
### คำอธิบาย (Description)
In conventional medicine, a purple or dusky facial complexion can be a sign of poor circulation, low oxygen levels, or certain blood disorders. It may be linked to conditions like chronic heart or lung disease, where the blood carries less oxygen, or to vascular issues that slow blood flow in the small vessels of the face. A doctor might evaluate oxygen saturation, heart function, and blood tests to rule out serious underlying disease.

### การรักษาหลัก (Treatments)
When a purple complexion is linked to a diagnosed medical condition, treatment focuses on managing that condition - for example, improving heart function, treating lung disease, or addressing blood disorders. For cosmetic concerns, options like laser therapy or topical creams may be used to reduce visible discoloration.

## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนจีน (Traditional Chinese Medicine View)
### ความเข้าใจเกี่ยวกับการแพทย์แผนจีน (TCM Understanding)
In TCM, the face is considered the mirror of the Heart and the blood. The Heart governs the blood vessels, and its health is reflected in the complexion. When blood circulates smoothly, the face is rosy and lustrous. But when blood flow becomes sluggish or congealed - what TCM calls Blood Stagnation - the stagnant blood darkens the skin, creating a purple or dusky hue. Because the face is rich in small vessels, it is often the first place this stagnation becomes visible.
However, not all purple faces are the same. Blood stagnation can arise from different roots. Sometimes the blood simply congeals without a clear organ trigger - a pattern of simple Blood Stagnation. In other cases, the Heart itself is the source, with chest pain and palpitations accompanying the purple lips and cheeks. If emotional stress and frustration are the main drivers, the Liver's Qi becomes stuck and fails to move the blood, producing a dull purple complexion with chest distension. When the body's vital energy (Qi) is too weak to push the blood, a pale-purple face with exhaustion appears - this is Qi Deficiency causing Blood Stagnation. And if internal Heat thickens the blood, the face takes on a deeper red-purple tone with restlessness. Each pattern requires a different treatment strategy, even though the outward sign looks similar.
This is why TCM never treats a purple face in isolation. A practitioner will ask about your energy, emotions, pain, and other symptoms, then examine your tongue and pulse to pinpoint the exact type of stagnation. The goal is not just to lighten the skin, but to restore the free flow of blood and Qi throughout the body - which, in the process, brings back a healthy, natural color.

### การวินิจฉัยโดยแพทย์แผนจีน (Practitioner Diagnosis)
A practitioner begins by looking closely at the shade of purple and where it appears. A dark, bluish-purple face with no strong accompanying organ symptoms points toward simple Blood Stagnation. The tongue is often purplish with small dark spots, and the pulse feels wiry or choppy, like a stream full of pebbles.
If the purple concentrates around the lips and cheeks and the person mentions palpitations, chest tightness, or a heavy sensation in the heart area, Heart Blood Stagnation is suspected. The tongue may be dark red with purple dots, and the pulse is thin and hesitant, reflecting the heart’s struggle to move blood smoothly.
When the face looks dull purple and the person sighs frequently, feels chest or rib-side distension, and links flare-ups to stress or frustration, Qi and Blood Stagnation is the likely picture. The tongue is dusky purple, and the pulse feels tight like a guitar string, showing that stuck Qi is failing to push the blood.
A pale-purple, dusky complexion paired with noticeable fatigue, weak voice, and breathlessness points to Qi Deficiency causing Blood Stagnation. Here the blood pools not because of a blockage but because there is not enough Qi to keep it moving. The tongue is pale with a faint purple tinge, and the pulse is weak and thin.
When the face takes on a deeper red-purple hue and the person feels hot, thirsty, or has a dry mouth, Blood Stagnation with Heat may be at play. The tongue is red-purple with a yellow coat, and the pulse is choppy and rapid. This less common pattern often accompanies inflammation or skin eruptions.

## กลุ่มอาการการแพทย์แผนจีนและการรักษา (TCM Patterns and Treatment)

### Blood Stagnation
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/blood-stagnation)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Dusky or purplish facial complexion, Purple or dark lips and nails, Fixed, stabbing pain elsewhere in the body, Pain that worsens at night, Dark menstrual blood with clots, Rough, dry, scaly skin
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Cold weather, Prolonged sitting or inactivity, Emotional stress, Injury or trauma, Heavy, greasy foods
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Gentle exercise like walking, Warmth, Warm, cooked meals, Rest and relaxation
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When blood flow becomes sluggish and congeals, the stagnant blood shows through the skin as a dark or purplish hue. The face is richly supplied with small blood vessels, so stasis is often visible there first, especially if the stagnation affects the upper body or Heart.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: purple or dusky body with possible stasis spots; sublingual veins distended and dark. Pulse: choppy (涩, Se) or wiry (弦, Xian).
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Blood Stagnation means blood flow is sluggish. Cold congeals and slows blood further, while warmth and movement invigorate circulation. Stress causes Qi stagnation, which then leads to blood stasis, so calming the mind helps free the flow. Gentle exercise and blood-moving foods directly counteract stasis, gradually restoring a healthy complexion.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Gentle daily movement like walking or tai chi helps keep Blood flowing. Keep warm, especially the face and neck, and avoid cold raw foods that can congeal Blood. Include warming spices such as turmeric, ginger, and black pepper, along with dark leafy greens and a small amount of black vinegar to gently move stasis.

### Heart Blood Stagnation
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/heart-blood-stagnation)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Stabbing chest pain in a fixed spot, Purple or dark lips and nails, Palpitations with anxiety or restlessness, Pain radiates to shoulder, upper back, or inner left arm, Cold hands
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Emotional stress, Cold weather, Heavy, greasy foods, Overexertion
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Gentle exercise like walking, Warmth, Rest and relaxation, Warm, cooked meals
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  The Heart governs the blood and vessels, and its health is directly reflected in the facial complexion. When Heart Blood becomes stagnant, the blood fails to flow smoothly and nourish the face, causing a purple or dark discoloration, especially around the lips and cheeks. This stagnation often stems from emotional stress, cold congealing the blood, or prolonged illness, and it also produces the classic chest pain and palpitations because the Heart itself is affected.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: dark purple body with stasis spots and distended sublingual veins. Pulse: choppy (涩 Sè) or knotted (结 Jié), especially at the left Cun position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: <p>Cold constricts blood vessels and slows circulation, worsening stagnation, while warmth dilates vessels and promotes flow. Emotional stress tightens the chest and disrupts Heart Qi, aggravating blood stasis and pain. Gentle movement encourages blood circulation without overstraining the Heart, and light, warm meals prevent phlegm and dampness that can further obstruct blood flow.</p>
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Warm, cooked meals with gentle spices like turmeric, ginger, and cinnamon support blood flow. Avoid cold, raw foods and heavy, greasy meals that can clog circulation. Gentle exercise like walking or tai chi keeps blood moving without overstraining the Heart. Managing stress through meditation or breathing helps prevent Qi stagnation that worsens blood stasis.

### Qi And Blood Stagnation
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/qi-and-blood-stagnation)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Dull purplish facial complexion, Distending pain in the chest or rib area, Irritability or restlessness, Dark purple tongue with stasis spots, Fixed, stabbing pain that worsens with pressure
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Emotional stress, Prolonged sitting or inactivity, Heavy, greasy foods, Cold, raw foods and drinks
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Gentle exercise like walking, Rest and relaxation, Warm, cooked meals
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Qi is the body's mover; when it gets stuck, Blood cannot flow freely. This pattern combines emotional tension and frustration with sluggish circulation, so the face takes on a dull, purplish hue. The stagnation often settles in the Liver channel, which runs through the chest and flanks, explaining why the complexion change frequently comes with a tight, distending feeling in the ribs and mood swings.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: dark purple body with stasis spots, distended sublingual veins, thin white coat. Pulse: wiry (弦 xián) and choppy (涩 sè), like a taut guitar string scraping bamboo.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Emotional stress and frustration directly knot the Liver Qi, which is the driving force behind this pattern; once Qi is stuck, Blood stagnates and the face darkens. Movement and warmth gently unblock the flow, while cold and inactivity congeal Blood further. Light, nourishing meals provide the raw material for smooth circulation without burdening the digestive system.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Gentle daily movement like walking or tai chi helps Qi push Blood along. Eat warm, lightly cooked foods and include small amounts of blood-nourishing ingredients like dark leafy greens and a little high-quality red meat. Avoid heavy, greasy meals that clog circulation and cold, raw foods that constrict flow.

### Qi Deficiency causing Blood Stagnation
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/qi-deficiency-causing-blood-stagnation)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Pale-purple or dusky facial tone, Persistent fatigue and weakness, Shortness of breath on exertion, Fixed, stabbing pain that worsens with pressure, Reluctance to speak or weak voice, Dizziness or lightheadedness
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Overexertion, Emotional stress, Cold, raw foods and drinks, Prolonged sitting or inactivity
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Gentle exercise like walking, Warm, cooked meals, Rest and relaxation, Deep breathing or Qi Gong
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Qi is the body's vital force that moves blood through the vessels. When Qi is chronically weak, it lacks the strength to push blood forward, so circulation becomes sluggish and blood begins to pool. This stagnant blood darkens the complexion, producing a pale-purple or dusky facial hue. At the same time, the underlying Qi deficiency causes persistent fatigue, shortness of breath, and a weak voice.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale-purple body with stasis spots and teeth marks. Pulse: deep, weak, and choppy (涩 sè).
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Qi is like the engine that moves blood. Overexertion, stress, and poor diet deplete Qi further, leaving blood even more stagnant and the face darker. Rest, gentle movement, and warm nourishing foods rebuild Qi, giving it the strength to push blood smoothly again. Cold foods and prolonged inactivity both slow circulation, making stagnation worse.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Build Qi with warm, easily digestible foods like rice congee, bone broths, and soups with a little astragalus root (黄芪, huáng qí). Avoid cold, raw foods and icy drinks, which weaken the Spleen and Qi. Gentle daily movement such as walking, Tai Chi, or Qi Gong supports circulation without exhausting your Qi. Prioritize rest and avoid pushing through fatigue.

### Blood Stagnation with Heat
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/blood-stagnation-with-heat)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Deep reddish-purple facial complexion, Pain that worsens at night, Sensation of internal heat, especially at night, Irritability or restlessness, Dark menstrual blood with clots, Dry mouth with urge to rinse but not swallow
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Spicy and greasy foods, Emotional stress, Hot weather, Alcohol
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Cooling foods like cucumber and celery, Gentle exercise like walking, Cool environment, Rest and relaxation
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When heat enters the blood, it thickens and congeals it, causing the blood to move sluggishly and stagnate in the vessels. This stagnant, heat-affected blood shows through the skin as a deep reddish-purple facial hue. The heat also pushes blood outward and upward, intensifying the color and often bringing a sensation of internal warmth or restlessness.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: reddish-purple body with stasis spots and distended sublingual veins; thin dry yellow coating. Pulse: choppy (涩 sè) and rapid (数 shuò), often wiry (弦 xián).
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Heat thickens the blood, so cooling foods, environments, and rest help thin the blood and ease stasis. Spicy, greasy, or alcoholic intake adds heat, worsening the stagnation. Emotional stress stirs up Liver fire and stagnates Qi, which further impedes blood flow, while gentle movement promotes circulation without generating excess heat.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Avoid spicy, greasy, and fried foods that add heat. Eat cooling, blood-moving foods like cucumber, celery, hawthorn, and radish. Gentle daily movement such as walking or stretching helps blood flow without overheating. Keep calm and avoid overwork.

## คำถามที่พบบ่อย (Frequently Asked Questions)
### Is a purple face dangerous?
Not always - but it should never be ignored. In TCM, a purple face signals blood stagnation, which can range from mild, localized stasis to deeper involvement of the Heart or other organs. If your purple face appears suddenly, is accompanied by chest pain, breathing difficulty, or confusion, seek emergency medical care immediately. A long-standing, stable purple hue that improves with rest and warmth is less urgent but still warrants a TCM evaluation to prevent progression.

### Can acupuncture really change my complexion?
Yes - because acupuncture works by improving circulation and moving stagnant blood. When needles are placed at points like Xuehai (SP-10) or Geshu (BL-17), they help dissolve blockages and encourage fresh blood to reach the face. Many patients notice their skin looks brighter and more even-toned after several sessions, especially when combined with blood-moving herbs. The change is gradual, not instant, and reflects deeper internal rebalancing.

### Will I need to take herbs forever?
No. Herbal formulas are prescribed to correct the specific pattern causing your purple face. Once the stagnation is resolved and your complexion stabilizes, the formula is typically discontinued or tapered. Some people with chronic, deep-seated stagnation may need periodic short courses of herbs during stressful periods or seasonal changes, but lifelong daily use is not the norm.

### Can I combine TCM with my heart medication?
In most cases, yes - but it is essential that both your TCM practitioner and your cardiologist know exactly what you are taking. Certain blood-moving herbs (like Dan Shen, Chuan Xiong, or Hong Hua) can interact with anticoagulant or antiplatelet drugs (warfarin, aspirin, clopidogrel), potentially increasing bleeding risk. A qualified TCM practitioner will adjust the formula or choose safer alternatives. Never stop or change your prescribed heart medication without your doctor's approval.

### What if my face is only purple when I'm cold?
Cold constricts blood vessels and slows circulation, so it can worsen any underlying blood stagnation. In TCM, this often points to a pattern where cold has entered the channels or where Qi is too weak to warm the body. Treatment would focus on warming and moving the blood with herbs like cinnamon twig (Gui Zhi) or acupuncture with moxibustion (heat therapy). Keeping your face and body warm in cold weather is a simple but helpful supportive measure.

### Does diet play a role?
Absolutely. Cold, raw foods and icy drinks can constrict blood vessels and worsen stagnation, so switching to warm, cooked meals is often recommended. Blood-nourishing foods like dark leafy greens, beets, and small amounts of lean red meat support healthy circulation. Spices like turmeric and ginger gently move the blood. On the other hand, greasy, fried, or sugary foods create dampness and phlegm that can further clog the system.

### How is this different from rosacea?
Rosacea typically presents with redness, flushing, and visible blood vessels, often triggered by heat, alcohol, or stress. A purple face from blood stagnation is usually a more even, dusky discoloration that doesn't come and go as quickly. However, the two can overlap - especially if Heat is involved. TCM differentiates them by looking at the exact shade, accompanying symptoms, and tongue appearance, then treats accordingly.

1. **Xue Fu Zhu Yu Tang for stable angina pectoris: a systematic review and meta-analysis**
   *วารสารวิชาการ: Chen J, et al. Evid Based Complement Alternat Med. 2014;2014:984671.*
   This meta-analysis pooled randomized controlled trials evaluating Xue Fu Zhu Yu Tang for stable angina. The formula significantly reduced angina attacks and improved ECG results compared to conventional medication alone, with a favorable safety profile.

2. **Acupuncture for primary dysmenorrhoea: a systematic review**
   *วารสารวิชาการ: Cho SH, Hwang EW. BJOG. 2010;117(5):509-521.*
   This systematic review of 27 trials found that acupuncture significantly reduced menstrual pain compared to no treatment or NSAIDs, supporting its use for blood-stasis patterns that often accompany a purple complexion.
