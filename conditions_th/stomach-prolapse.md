## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนปัจจุบัน (Conventional Medicine View)
### คำอธิบาย (Description)
Gastroptosis, or stomach prolapse, occurs when the stomach drops from its normal position in the upper abdomen. This can happen when the ligaments and muscles that support the stomach become weak or stretched. Common symptoms include a sensation of fullness or heaviness after eating, early satiety, bloating, nausea, and sometimes a visible bulge in the lower abdomen. The condition is often associated with other forms of visceral ptosis, such as a prolapsed kidney or transverse colon.
Diagnosis is usually made through an upper GI series (barium X-ray) or endoscopy, which can show the stomach sitting lower than normal. In many cases, the condition is mild and may not cause significant symptoms, but when it does, it can interfere with digestion and quality of life.

### การรักษาหลัก (Treatments)
Conventional management of stomach prolapse is largely supportive. Doctors typically recommend dietary adjustments - eating small, frequent meals and avoiding heavy, fatty foods - along with postural changes like lying down after meals to reduce the dragging sensation. Prokinetic medications may be prescribed to help with gastric emptying, and abdominal binders can offer some physical support. In rare, severe cases where symptoms are disabling, a surgical procedure called gastropexy may be considered to anchor the stomach in place.

## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนจีน (Traditional Chinese Medicine View)
### ความเข้าใจเกี่ยวกับการแพทย์แผนจีน (TCM Understanding)
In TCM, the Spleen is far more than a digestive organ - it is the powerhouse that produces Qi and has the vital job of holding everything in the body where it belongs. When Spleen Qi is strong, it provides an upward, lifting force that keeps the stomach, uterus, and other organs from sinking. Chronic Spleen Qi deficiency weakens this force, allowing the stomach to drop. This is why the core pattern in stomach prolapse is Spleen Qi Sinking: the bearing-down sensation, fatigue after eating, and pale, puffy tongue all point to a system that has run out of lift.
But the Spleen doesn't work in isolation. Emotional stress can cause Liver Qi to stagnate and then invade the Stomach, disrupting the Spleen's ability to hold things up - this is why some people feel their symptoms flare after a frustrating day. Over time, if Spleen Yang deficiency goes unchecked, it can drain the Kidney Yang, the body's deepest source of warmth and power, leading to a cold, heavy prolapse with lower back soreness. In long-standing cases, sluggish Qi flow may also lead to Blood Stagnation, creating sharp, fixed pain, or Yin Deficiency, where the stomach lining becomes dry and irritated.
This is why TCM never treats all stomach prolapse the same way. The same Western diagnosis can arise from a simple Qi sink, a Liver-Spleen disharmony, a deep Yang chill, or a complex mix of stasis and dryness. By identifying the exact pattern, a practitioner can use herbs, acupuncture, and lifestyle guidance to lift what has fallen - not just mechanically, but energetically.

### การวินิจฉัยโดยแพทย์แผนจีน (Practitioner Diagnosis)
A practitioner begins by listening to the quality of the discomfort. In stomach prolapse, the central clue is a bearing-down, dragging sensation in the upper abdomen. How that feeling behaves-when it worsens, what eases it, and what other signals accompany it-quickly steers the diagnosis toward one pattern rather than another.
If the heaviness is constant, worsens after eating or standing for long periods, and comes with fatigue and a poor appetite, the picture points strongly to Spleen Qi Sinking. The tongue is often pale with teeth marks on the sides, and the pulse feels weak or thready, revealing that the body’s lifting energy is simply too depleted to hold the stomach in place.
When emotional stress is a clear trigger and the distension feels more like bloating that moves around, Liver Qi Stagnation invading the Stomach becomes the likely pattern. The tongue may have a thin white coating, and the pulse takes on a wiry quality. Here the practitioner will ask about frustration, sighing, or rib-side tension, because the liver’s energy is disrupting the Spleen’s ability to lift and hold the stomach in place.
In long-standing cases where coldness and soreness creep in-cold limbs, a sore lower back, and a preference for warmth and pressure on the belly-the deficiency has deepened to the Kidney and Spleen Yang level. The tongue is pale and the pulse is deep and slow. This pattern tells the practitioner that the body’s internal fire is too low to warm and lift the organs.
If the pain is fixed and stabbing rather than merely heavy, Stomach Blood Stagnation may be complicating the picture. The tongue often looks purplish or shows dark spots, and the pulse feels choppy. This pattern indicates that long-term sinking has slowed blood flow locally, creating a painful, stuck sensation that distinguishes it from the duller ache of pure qi deficiency.
A very different presentation emerges when the stomach lining has lost its moisture. In Stomach Yin Deficiency, a person feels uncomfortably full yet has little desire to eat, and may notice a dry mouth or a subtle burning sensation. The tongue is red with little or no coating, and the pulse is thin and rapid. This pattern signals that the delicate yin fluids that nourish the stomach have dried up, often after prolonged illness.

## กลุ่มอาการการแพทย์แผนจีนและการรักษา (TCM Patterns and Treatment)

### Spleen Qi Sinking
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/spleen-qi-sinking)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Bearing-down or dragging sensation in the stomach, Abdominal bloating worse after eating, Chronic fatigue and lethargy, Dizziness or lightheadedness, Poor appetite, Shortness of breath and reluctance to speak
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Overexertion, Heavy, greasy meals, Cold or raw foods, Prolonged standing, Stress and worry
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Resting after meals, Lying down, Warm, easily digestible foods, Small, frequent meals, Gentle abdominal massage, Avoiding prolonged standing
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  The Spleen produces Qi and uses its lifting power to hold organs in place. When Spleen Qi becomes chronically weak, it sinks downward instead of rising, allowing the stomach to drop from its normal position. This creates the characteristic bearing-down sensation that worsens after eating or standing, because these activities further strain the already depleted Spleen Qi.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale, puffy, and tender with teeth marks, thin white coating. Pulse: weak and empty, especially at the right Guan position; may feel deep.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Rest, especially lying down, allows the Spleen Qi to recover and prevents it from being pulled further downward. Warm, easily digestible foods do not tax the Spleen's transformative function, whereas heavy or cold foods demand more Qi and can damage Spleen Yang. Overexertion and prolonged standing deplete Qi and exacerbate the sinking, while stress and worry stagnate the Liver, which can further weaken the Spleen and disrupt the ascent of clear Qi.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat small, frequent meals of warm, cooked foods like soups and congees. Avoid cold, raw, or greasy foods that burden the Spleen. Rest for 10-15 minutes after meals, preferably lying down. Gentle daily walking helps Qi flow, but avoid overexertion and prolonged standing. Keep the abdomen warm with a belly band.

### Liver Qi Stagnation invading the Stomach
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/liver-qi-stagnation-invading-the-stomach)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Distending pain in upper abdomen that radiates to the ribs, Symptoms worsen with emotional stress or frustration, Frequent belching or acid reflux, Irritability, moodiness, and frequent sighing, Sensation of a lump or blockage in the throat
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Emotional stress, anger, or frustration, Greasy, spicy, or heavy foods, Overeating or large meals, Sedentary lifestyle
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Stress reduction and relaxation, Gentle movement like walking or stretching, Warm compress on the abdomen, Small, frequent meals
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When emotional stress or frustration builds, Liver Qi becomes stuck and flows abnormally, rushing sideways to invade the Stomach. This invasion disrupts the Stomach's downward movement and weakens the Spleen's ability to lift and hold organs in place. The result is a heavy, distended sensation in the upper abdomen that often feels like a sinking or dragging, and it flares up whenever tension arises.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: normal or slightly red body with redder sides and a thin white coat. Pulse: wiry (弦 xián), like a taut guitar string, most pronounced at the left middle position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Emotional stress is the main trigger because it directly causes Liver Qi to stagnate and then invade the Stomach, worsening the distension and sinking sensation. Relaxation and gentle movement help unblock Qi flow, while warm compresses soothe the Stomach and promote circulation. Small meals reduce the burden on the weakened Spleen and Stomach, preventing further sinking.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Manage stress with gentle activities like tai chi, yoga, or walking. Eat small, frequent meals and avoid rich, greasy, or spicy foods that can aggravate stagnation. Practice deep breathing to keep Qi moving smoothly.

### Kidney and Spleen Yang Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/kidney-and-spleen-yang-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Dragging, heavy stomach sensation with a cold feeling, Sore, cold lower back and weak knees, Chronic loose stools or early-morning diarrhea, Cold hands and feet, especially below the knees, Abdominal cold pain that feels better with warmth and pressure, Poor appetite and bloating after eating
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Cold or raw foods, Prolonged standing, Overwork and fatigue, Cold weather, Stress
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warmth on the abdomen, Resting after meals, Warm, cooked foods, Gentle abdominal massage or binding, Lying down
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When Spleen Yang deficiency persists, it eventually drains the Kidney Yang-the body's root of warmth and power. Without enough Yang to lift and hold, the stomach sinks downward, creating a heavy, dragging sensation. The cold nature of this pattern adds soreness in the lower back and knees, loose stools, and an intolerance of cold.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale, swollen, puffy with teeth marks and a white slippery coating. Pulse: deep, weak, slow; especially feeble at both Chi positions and the right Guan.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: <p>Cold and raw foods directly damage Spleen Yang, while warmth and cooked foods support digestion and energy. Standing and fatigue strain the already weak lifting Qi, so lying down and resting allow the Qi to rise. Stress can cause Liver Qi to invade the Spleen, further weakening the digestive fire and worsening the sinking.</p>
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat warm, cooked foods like soups, stews, and congees; avoid cold drinks, raw salads, and icy foods. Keep your abdomen and lower back warm, especially in cold weather. Gentle daily movement such as walking or qigong helps Qi flow, but avoid overexertion and prolonged standing. A short rest lying down after meals can ease the dragging sensation.

### Stomach Blood Stagnation
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/stomach-blood-stagnation)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Sharp stabbing pain fixed in one spot, Pain worsens with pressure on the abdomen, Dark purplish tongue with stasis spots, Pain worse after eating
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Overeating, Cold foods and drinks, Emotional stress, Prolonged standing
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warmth on the abdomen, Gentle movement like walking, Small, frequent meals, Rest after eating
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When the stomach prolapses, Spleen Qi is chronically weak and fails to lift the organ. Over time, this sluggish Qi flow leads to blood stasis in the stomach region. The stagnant blood produces sharp, fixed pain that worsens with pressure and eating, and a purplish tongue.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: purple or dark dusky body with possible purple stasis spots; distended sublingual veins; thin white coat. Pulse: choppy (Sè) like a knife scraping bamboo, or wiry (Xián) reflecting Qi stagnation and pain.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Cold constricts blood vessels and worsens stasis, while warmth relaxes and promotes flow. Overeating burdens the stomach, causing more Qi stagnation and blood stasis. Gentle movement helps move Qi and blood, whereas prolonged standing increases the downward pull on the prolapsed stomach, aggravating stasis. - **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat small, frequent, lightly cooked meals that are easy to digest. Avoid cold, raw, and greasy foods that congeal blood and burden the stomach. Gentle daily movement like walking helps circulate Qi and blood, preventing further stasis.

### Stomach Yin Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/stomach-yin-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Gnawing, burning stomach discomfort, Dry mouth and throat, Hungry but no desire to eat, Thirst with preference for small sips
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Spicy, fried, or drying foods, Alcohol and coffee, Large, heavy meals, Stress and anxiety, Hot, dry weather
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Small, frequent meals, Moistening foods like pear and honey, Resting after meals, Warm fluids sipped slowly
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When stomach prolapse persists, the stomach's delicate lining may lose its nourishing yin fluids over time. This leads to a dry, irritated state where you feel a dull burning pain and a gnawing discomfort, yet you don't want to eat because the stomach feels full and inflamed.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red, especially in the centre, with little or no coating and possible cracks. Pulse: fine (细 xi) and rapid (数 shu).
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Stomach Yin is like the cooling, moistening fluid that protects the stomach lining. Spicy and fried foods generate heat and consume these precious fluids, worsening dryness and burning. Stress also stirs up internal heat, further depleting Yin. Rest and small, moist meals reduce the digestive burden, allowing the body to preserve and rebuild Yin, while warm fluids gently soothe the stomach without shocking it.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat small, frequent meals that are warm and moist, like congee with pear or honey. Avoid spicy, fried, and drying foods, as well as alcohol and coffee. Rest after meals and stay hydrated with small sips of warm water throughout the day.

## คำถามที่พบบ่อย (Frequently Asked Questions)
### How does TCM treat a prolapsed stomach?
TCM uses a combination of acupuncture, herbal formulas, and dietary therapy to strengthen the Spleen Qi and restore its upward lifting force. Acupuncture points like Zhongwan (REN-12) and Zusanli (ST-36) directly tonify the Spleen and Stomach, while Baihui (DU-20) at the top of the head helps raise sinking Qi. Herbal formulas like Bu Zhong Yi Qi Tang are specifically designed to lift prolapse. The exact treatment depends on your underlying pattern, but the goal is always to rebuild your body's ability to hold the stomach in place - not just manage symptoms.

### Can acupuncture really lift the stomach?
Acupuncture cannot physically pull the stomach back into position like a surgical lift, but it can strengthen the Spleen's energetic function of holding organs up. Many patients report that the heavy, dragging sensation lessens significantly after a series of treatments, and diagnostic imaging sometimes shows an improved stomach position. The effect is gradual and works best when combined with herbs and lifestyle changes, but it addresses the root cause rather than just the symptom.

### How long until I feel better?
You may notice small improvements - like less bloating after meals or more energy - within the first 2 to 3 weeks. The characteristic bearing-down sensation usually takes 4 to 6 weeks to noticeably ease. Full stabilization can take several months, especially if you have a long-standing deficiency pattern. Consistency is key: weekly acupuncture and daily herbs give the best results.

### Do I need to change my diet?
Yes, diet plays a crucial role in recovery. The Spleen loves warmth and simplicity, so you'll be advised to eat warm, cooked, easily digestible foods like congee, soups, and steamed vegetables. Small, frequent meals are better than large ones, and you should avoid raw, cold, greasy, or spicy foods that tax the digestive system. Resting - even lying down for a few minutes after eating - can also help reduce the dragging sensation.

### Can I combine TCM with my regular medical treatment?
Absolutely. TCM can work alongside conventional advice like dietary changes and postural adjustments. If you are taking medications, especially blood thinners, let both your TCM practitioner and your doctor know, as some herbs can interact. Never stop prescribed medications without consulting your doctor. If surgery is being considered, TCM can help strengthen your body beforehand and support recovery afterward.

### Will the results last once I stop treatment?
Because TCM aims to correct the underlying imbalance, the improvements can be long-lasting - provided you continue the dietary and lifestyle habits that support a strong Spleen. Many patients find that after a course of treatment, they only need occasional tune-ups or can maintain their progress with a gentle herbal formula. However, if you return to a diet of cold, raw foods and chronic overwork, the prolapse may recur.

### Is TCM safe if I'm pregnant and have stomach prolapse?
Many acupuncture points and herbs are safe during pregnancy, but some are contraindicated because they can stimulate uterine contractions. It is essential to tell your practitioner if you are pregnant or trying to conceive so they can modify your treatment. Generally, gentle Spleen-tonifying approaches are safe and can help support both you and your baby, but always work with an experienced practitioner.

## การวิจัยทางวิทยาศาสตร์สมัยใหม่ (Modern Scientific Research)
1. **Clinical observation on stagnation-moving acupuncture combined with modified Bu Zhong Yi Qi Tang for gastroptosis**
   *วารสารวิชาการ: 滞动针联合补中益气汤加减治疗胃下垂疗效观察. Shanghai Journal of Acupuncture and Moxibustion, 2021.*
   This clinical trial combined a specialized acupuncture technique (stagnation-moving needling) with the classic formula Bu Zhong Yi Qi Tang to treat patients with gastroptosis. The combined treatment group showed significantly greater improvement in gastric prolapse symptoms and radiographic stomach position compared to the control group receiving conventional medication.

2. **Systematic review of Chinese herbal medicine for gastroptosis**
   *วารสารวิชาการ: Systematic review of Chinese herbal medicine for gastroptosis. Chinese Journal of Integrated Traditional and Western Medicine on Digestion, 2019.*
   A systematic review of randomized controlled trials examining Chinese herbal medicine for gastroptosis found that Bu Zhong Yi Qi Tang was the most frequently used formula. The meta-analysis indicated significant symptom improvement with herbal treatment compared to conventional prokinetic drugs, though the authors noted that study quality was generally low and larger, well-designed trials are needed.
