## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนปัจจุบัน (Conventional Medicine View)
### คำอธิบาย (Description)
Cluster headache is a primary headache disorder characterized by attacks of excruciating, strictly one-sided pain, usually centered around the eye or temple, lasting 15 minutes to three hours. The pain is accompanied by autonomic symptoms on the same side - tearing, redness of the eye, nasal congestion or runny nose, and eyelid swelling. Attacks often occur in clusters, with daily or near-daily episodes for weeks or months, followed by remission periods. Diagnosis is made clinically based on the International Classification of Headache Disorders criteria, and imaging is used only to rule out secondary causes.

### การรักษาหลัก (Treatments)
Acute attacks are typically treated with high-flow oxygen via a non-rebreather mask or subcutaneous sumatriptan. For prevention during a cluster period, verapamil is the first-line medication, sometimes combined with a short course of corticosteroids as a bridging therapy. Lithium, topiramate, and occipital nerve blocks are used for refractory cases, and galcanezumab, a CGRP monoclonal antibody, has been approved for episodic cluster headache.

## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนจีน (Traditional Chinese Medicine View)
### ความเข้าใจเกี่ยวกับการแพทย์แผนจีน (TCM Understanding)
In TCM, cluster headache is understood primarily through the Liver and its relationship with external pathogens and the digestive system. The Liver governs the smooth flow of Qi throughout the body, and its channel travels up to the eyes and temples - the very territory where cluster pain strikes. When emotional stress, frustration, or overwork disrupt this flow, Qi can stagnate and transform into Heat or rising Yang that surges upward, creating the intense, one-sided, stabbing pain with red eyes and tearing that is so characteristic of the condition.
But the Liver is only part of the story. External pathogens like Wind-Cold or Wind-Heat can invade the head and neck channels, triggering sudden, severe attacks that feel constricting or distending. The Spleen, which transforms fluids, plays a role too: when weakened by poor diet or fatigue, it can generate Dampness and Phlegm that rise to cloud the head, producing a heavy, wrapped sensation with nausea and brain fog.
This is why two people with the same Western diagnosis of cluster headache may have completely different TCM patterns. One might flare with anger and a red face (Liver Yang Rising), another with a cold draft and stiff neck (Wind-Cold), and a third after a heavy meal with a thick tongue coating (Damp-Phlegm). Recognizing these differences allows the practitioner to choose the herbs, acupuncture points, and lifestyle advice that address the root of the problem, not just the pain.

### การวินิจฉัยโดยแพทย์แผนจีน (Practitioner Diagnosis)
A TCM practitioner starts by asking you to describe the pain in your own words - is it a sharp, stabbing sensation or more of a heavy, dull ache? They will also want to know what seems to bring it on, such as stress, cold weather, or feeling run‑down, because the trigger often points directly to the underlying pattern.
If the headache is intensely severe, one‑sided and pounding, and you notice a red face, bloodshot eyes, and a short temper, that points to Liver Yang Rising. The tongue is usually red with a thin or yellowish coating, and the pulse feels wiry and rapid, reflecting internal heat and upward‑surging Yang.
When the attack feels more like a distending, hot pressure that comes on suddenly, with a flushed face and eyes, and you may feel feverish, the picture shifts to Wind‑Heat invading the head. Here the tongue is red with a thin yellow coat and the pulse is floating and rapid, showing an external heat pathogen.
If your cluster headaches are clearly triggered by a cold draft or chilly weather, and you feel chilled with a stiff neck, the pattern is more likely Wind‑Cold. The tongue coating looks white and slippery, and the pulse is floating and tight, signaling that cold has blocked the channels in the head and neck.
A heavy, foggy kind of head pain that comes with chest tightness, nausea, and a thick, greasy tongue coating suggests Damp‑Phlegm is obstructing the clear orifices. The tongue body often looks puffy, and the pulse feels slippery, like pearls rolling under the fingers.
In chronic cases where the headache is dull, feels worse after a long day, and you also look pale, feel tired, and perhaps have heart palpitations, Qi and Blood Deficiency is likely. The tongue is pale and the pulse is weak, showing that the head is under‑nourished.
When the pain is fixed in one spot, feels like a knife, and you notice a dark purple tongue or tiny dark spots on the tongue body, Qi and Blood Stagnation is at play. The pulse is often choppy, reflecting that blood is not moving smoothly through the collaterals in the head.

## กลุ่มอาการการแพทย์แผนจีนและการรักษา (TCM Patterns and Treatment)

### Liver Yang Rising
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/liver-yang-rising)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Stabbing or throbbing pain on one side of the head, often behind or around the eye, Eye redness, tearing, and facial flushing on the painful side, Irritability, quick temper, and restlessness during an attack, Attacks triggered or worsened by emotional stress and anger, Dizziness or a sense of heat rushing to the head
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Emotional stress or anger, Alcohol, Spicy, greasy or fried food, Lack of sleep, Hot, stuffy environment
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Rest in a dark, quiet room, Cold or cool compress on head, Deep breathing, meditation or relaxation, Cooling foods or herbal teas
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  The Liver is responsible for keeping Qi flowing smoothly. When stress, frustration, or overwork build up, that flow stalls and then surges upward as a wave of heat and Yang. This rising force slams into the head, concentrating along one side-often behind or around the eye-and produces the intense, stabbing pain that defines a cluster attack.
At the same time, the upward rush of Liver fire irritates the eyes, causing redness, tearing, and a flushed face. The whole episode is like a pressure cooker blowing its lid: heat and tension escape through the head, leaving you agitated and restless until the storm passes.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red body with redder sides, a thin dry yellow or scanty coat. Pulse: wiry (弦 xián) and forceful, possibly fine and rapid if Yin deficiency is pronounced.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Anger and emotional stress directly stir Liver Qi to surge upward, which is the core mechanism of this pattern-so anything that calms the mind and cools the body helps pull that rising Yang back down. Alcohol and spicy foods are heating and yang in nature; they act like throwing gasoline on a fire, making the head pain and flushing worse. Rest in a dark quiet room and cold applications soothe the nervous system and physically cool the head, countering the heat and wind that drive the attack.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Avoid alcohol, spicy foods, and excessive coffee-they add fuel to the Liver fire. Establish a consistent sleep schedule and build in short, calming breaks during the day. Gentle exercise like walking or tai chi helps keep Qi moving without overstimulating it.

### Wind-Heat
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/wind-heat)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Distending, bursting headache, worse around the eyes or temples, Red face and bloodshot eyes, Nasal congestion or runny nose with yellow discharge, Feeling of heat, thirst, and slight chills, Sore, red throat
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Hot, stuffy environment, Spicy, greasy or fried food, Alcohol, Emotional stress or anger, Exposure to cold wind or drafts
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Cold or cool compress on head, Rest in a dark, quiet room, Cooling foods or herbal teas, Deep breathing, meditation or relaxation
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Wind-Heat is an external pathogen that strikes the upper body first. It rushes into the head and face, where the channels of the eyes and nose are especially vulnerable. The Wind drives sudden, intense pain, while the Heat inflames the tissues, causing distension, redness, and a burning sensation.
In a cluster headache attack, this combination produces a severe, bursting pain around the eye or temple, along with red eyes, tearing, and a stuffy or runny nose with yellow discharge. The floating, rapid pulse and red tongue tip with yellow coating are classic signs that the attack is driven by an exterior Heat invasion.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red tip and edges with a thin yellow coating. Pulse: floating and rapid (浮数 fú shuò).
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Wind-Heat is an exterior pathogen that thrives on heat and movement. Hot weather, spicy food, and alcohol add more Heat to the body, while wind and drafts carry the pathogen deeper into the head. Emotional stress stirs up internal Yang, which can combine with the external Wind-Heat to make the headache worse. Cooling measures, like cold compresses and mint tea, help clear the Heat, while rest and darkness reduce sensory stimulation, giving the body a chance to expel the pathogen.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Drink plenty of cooling fluids like chrysanthemum or peppermint tea. Avoid spicy, greasy, or fried foods, as well as alcohol and coffee, which can fan internal Heat. Rest in a cool, quiet, dark room during an attack, and shield your head and neck from drafts and wind.

### Wind-Cold
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/wind-cold)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Headache triggered or worsened by cold or wind exposure, Strong aversion to cold and chills, Stiff, tight neck and upper back, Clear, watery nasal discharge, No sweating, No thirst, preference for warm drinks
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Exposure to cold wind or drafts, Cold, raw foods and icy drinks, Washing hair with cold water
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warmth or warm compress on head, Warm drinks or cooked meals, Gentle sweating, Rest in a warm, draft-free room
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Wind-Cold attacks the body's surface, first entering the neck, upper back, and head. The cold contracts the channels and obstructs the flow of Qi and Blood, causing the sharp, boring pain typical of cluster headache. Because the pathogen is still at the exterior, the body's defensive Qi fights back, creating chills, aversion to wind, and a stiff neck.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: normal or slightly pale body with a thin, white, moist coating. Pulse: floating and tight (fu jin), like a taut string felt at the surface.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Wind-Cold invades the exterior and constricts the channels, so warmth and covering the head relax the tightness and push the pathogen out. Cold exposure contracts the channels further, locking the pain in place. Warm drinks and gentle sweating open the pores and release the trapped Wind-Cold, easing the headache.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Keep the neck and head covered in windy or cold weather. Sip warm ginger and scallion tea at the first sign of an attack to induce a light sweat. Avoid cold drinks, raw foods, and ice cream. Gentle movement that warms the body without drafts is helpful.

### Damp-Phlegm
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/damp-phlegm)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Heavy, wrapped or distending head pain, Chest stuffiness and nausea, Thick, white, greasy tongue coating, Poor appetite and loose stools, Fatigue, drowsiness, and brain fog
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Damp, humid weather, Spicy, greasy or fried food, Overeating, Sedentary lifestyle or prolonged sitting, Cold, raw foods and icy drinks
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warm, dry weather, Light, easily digestible meals, Gentle movement, walking or stretching, Avoiding dairy and greasy foods
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When the Spleen's digestive function is weak, it fails to transform fluids, which accumulate into Dampness and then condense into Phlegm. This heavy, sticky Phlegm rises and obstructs the clear orifices of the head, creating a sensation of pressure and a heavy, wrapped pain. The same Phlegm-Dampness can also block the chest and stomach, causing nausea and a feeling of stuffiness.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: Pale, swollen body with tooth marks, thick white greasy coating that looks oily. Pulse: Slippery (滑 huá), like beads rolling under the fingers.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Dampness and Phlegm are heavy, sticky substances that thrive on moisture and inactivity. Heavy, greasy, or cold foods overwhelm the Spleen and generate more Dampness, while a sedentary lifestyle lets it stagnate. Warmth, movement, and light foods do the opposite-they strengthen the Spleen, dry up Dampness, and keep Qi moving so the head clears.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat warm, cooked meals and skip cold, raw, or greasy foods that create Dampness. Dairy products, fried foods, and sweets are especially problematic. Gentle daily walking helps move Qi and transform Dampness. Dry, airy environments feel best.

### Qi and Blood Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/qi-and-blood-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Dull, lingering headache, Worsens with fatigue or overwork, Pale or sallow complexion, Heart palpitations, Persistent fatigue and weakness
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Overwork and exhaustion, Skipping meals, Cold, raw foods and icy drinks, Emotional stress or anger
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Rest and sleep, Warm drinks or cooked meals, Gentle movement, walking or stretching, Moxibustion on lower abdomen
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Qi and Blood are the body's fundamental nourishment. When they become deficient over time, the head is not properly supported, leading to a dull, lingering pain that lacks the sharp, stabbing quality of other patterns. This pain typically worsens with fatigue because the body's reserves are already low, and any extra demand depletes them further.
At the root, the Spleen fails to produce enough Qi and Blood, so the entire body shows signs of undernourishment - pale face, heart palpitations, and breathlessness - alongside the headache.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale body, may be thin or teeth-marked, thin white coating. Pulse: fine (thin) and weak, especially in the right Spleen position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Qi and Blood are like the body's fuel. Overwork and stress burn through that fuel rapidly, leaving the head undernourished and triggering dull pain. Rest and nourishing foods replenish the reserves, so the pain eases. Cold and raw foods weaken the Spleen, the factory that produces Qi and Blood, so avoiding them protects the supply. Gentle movement encourages circulation without draining the body's limited energy.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat warm, cooked, nourishing meals like rice congees, bone broths, and dark leafy greens. Include small amounts of red dates, goji berries, and a little high-quality protein to steadily rebuild Blood. Avoid cold, raw foods and iced drinks, which weaken the Spleen and slow down Blood production. Prioritize gentle daily movement like walking or tai chi, and never push through exhaustion - rest is medicine for this pattern.

### Qi And Blood Stagnation
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/qi-and-blood-stagnation)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Fixed, stabbing pain in the same location every attack, Pain worsens with pressure or touch, Dark purplish tongue body with stasis spots, Distended, dark sublingual veins, Irritability and mood swings, Chest or rib-side distension
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Emotional stress or anger, Cold, raw foods and icy drinks, Sedentary lifestyle or prolonged sitting, Exposure to cold wind or drafts
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Gentle movement, walking or stretching, Warmth or warm compress on head, Warm drinks or cooked meals, Deep breathing, meditation or relaxation
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When Qi flow becomes stuck, often from prolonged emotional stress or frustration, it can no longer push Blood smoothly through the vessels. Over time, this leads to Blood stasis, which creates intense, fixed, stabbing pain in the head. In cluster headaches, this stasis typically lodges in the same spot-often behind or around one eye-causing a relentless boring sensation that feels like a knife twisting in place. The pain is worse with pressure because the area is already congested with stagnant Blood.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: dark purple body with scattered stasis spots, distended dark sublingual veins, and a thin white coat. Pulse: wiry (弦, xián) and choppy (涩, sè), like a taut string scraping bamboo.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Blood Stasis arises when Qi is stuck, so anything that moves Qi and warms the vessels helps dissolve the stagnation. Cold and inactivity cause Blood to congeal, making the stabbing pain worse. Emotional stress directly tightens the Liver Qi, which is the main driver of Blood flow; calming the mind and moving the body gently unwinds that tension and lets Blood circulate freely again.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat warming, blood-moving foods like turmeric, ginger, black pepper, and small amounts of lean red meat or liver. Avoid cold, raw foods and icy drinks, which congeal Blood. Gentle daily exercise like walking or qigong keeps Qi and Blood flowing smoothly. Manage stress with deep breathing or meditation, as emotional tension directly worsens stagnation.

## คำถามที่พบบ่อย (Frequently Asked Questions)
### Can acupuncture stop a cluster headache attack once it starts?
Some patients do experience relief when acupuncture is applied during an attack, especially with points like Taiyang, Fengchi, and Hegu, but it is not as reliably abortive as high-flow oxygen or injectable triptans. The greater strength of acupuncture in cluster headache lies in reducing the overall frequency and severity of attacks when used regularly throughout a cluster period. Many people find that sessions 2-3 times per week help shorten the cycle and make the individual headaches more bearable.

### How long does it take to see results with TCM for cluster headaches?
For excess patterns such as Liver Yang Rising or Wind-Heat, many patients notice a reduction in attack frequency and intensity within 2-4 weeks of weekly acupuncture combined with daily herbal formulas. Damp-Phlegm patterns may take 4-6 weeks because clearing Phlegm is a slower process. Deficiency or Blood stasis patterns often require 1-3 months of consistent treatment to rebuild reserves or move deep stagnation, but the improvement tends to be steady and lasting. The goal is not just to get through the current cluster but to extend the remission period and make future cycles milder.

### Is it safe to take Chinese herbs with my verapamil or lithium?
In most cases, yes, but you must inform both your prescribing neurologist and your TCM practitioner about everything you are taking. Herbs that calm the Liver and subdue Yang, like Tian Ma and Gou Teng, may have a mild blood-pressure-lowering effect, so your verapamil dose might need adjustment if your blood pressure drops. Lithium requires regular blood level monitoring, and while commonly used herbs do not directly interact with it, dehydration from any cause can increase lithium levels, so always stay well hydrated. Never stop or adjust your preventive medication without medical supervision.

### Can TCM help prevent the next cluster cycle?
This is one of the most valuable roles of TCM. Between cycles, when you are in remission, treatment shifts to correcting the underlying imbalance that made you susceptible in the first place - nourishing Liver and Kidney Yin, strengthening the Spleen, or resolving chronic Phlegm or stasis. Many patients find that after several months of interictal herbal therapy and acupuncture, the next cluster period is shorter, less intense, or occasionally does not arrive at all. Consistency during the quiet periods is key.

### Will I need to change my diet?
Dietary adjustments are an important part of TCM treatment and are tailored to your specific pattern, but some general rules apply to nearly all cluster headache patients. Alcohol, especially beer and red wine, is a well-known trigger and should be avoided during cluster periods. Cold, raw foods and icy drinks can constrict the channels and provoke Wind-Cold type attacks, so favor warm, cooked meals. For Damp-Phlegm patterns, reducing dairy, greasy foods, and sugar helps prevent Phlegm from accumulating. Your practitioner will give you more detailed guidance based on your diagnosis.

### Does TCM work for chronic cluster headache?
Yes, but the approach and timeline differ. In chronic cluster headache, where remission periods last less than three months or are absent, the root imbalance is usually deeper - often a mix of Liver Yang rising with underlying Kidney Yin deficiency, or long-standing Blood stasis. Treatment focuses on gradually unwinding these layers with regular acupuncture and daily herbs over several months. While complete remission is not guaranteed, many chronic patients achieve a meaningful reduction in attack frequency and severity, and some are able to re-enter a more typical episodic pattern.
