## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนปัจจุบัน (Conventional Medicine View)
### คำอธิบาย (Description)
Atopic dermatitis is a chronic, relapsing inflammatory skin condition that usually begins in infancy or childhood. It is characterized by intensely itchy, dry, red patches that often appear in the creases of the elbows and knees. The condition is associated with a defective skin barrier, immune dysregulation, and a personal or family history of allergies, asthma, or hay fever.
Diagnosis is typically made by clinical examination, based on the appearance and distribution of the rash and the presence of itching. While the exact cause is not fully understood, a combination of genetic and environmental factors is thought to trigger the condition, and flares are often linked to stress, irritants, or allergens.

### การรักษาหลัก (Treatments)
Standard management focuses on restoring the skin barrier and controlling inflammation. This includes regular use of emollients and moisturizers, topical corticosteroids for flares, and calcineurin inhibitors for sensitive areas. For moderate-to-severe cases, systemic treatments such as oral immunosuppressants, phototherapy, or biologic agents like dupilumab may be used. Antihistamines are sometimes prescribed to help with itching, though their benefit is often limited.

## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนจีน (Traditional Chinese Medicine View)
### ความเข้าใจเกี่ยวกับการแพทย์แผนจีน (TCM Understanding)
TCM views atopic dermatitis through the lens of congenital constitution and the interplay between internal organs and external pathogens. The condition's Chinese name, 四弯风 (sì wān fēng), means "four bends wind," referring to the characteristic location in the flexural creases of the elbows and knees. This is where internal Dampness - a pathological accumulation of fluids - tends to settle, and where External Wind can most easily lodge when the body's defenses are weak.
The root of the problem often lies in a constitutional weakness of the Spleen and Lung. The Spleen is responsible for transforming food into Qi and Blood and for managing fluid metabolism; when it is weak, Dampness builds up internally. The Lung governs the skin and the body's protective Wei Qi; if it is deficient, the surface is vulnerable to invasion by Wind, Heat, or Dampness from the environment. This combination of internal Dampness and external invasion is what creates the characteristic itchy, weeping, or dry skin lesions.
Because the underlying imbalance can take several forms, the same Western diagnosis of atopic dermatitis can manifest very differently. When Dampness combines with Heat, the skin is red, hot, and oozing. When the Spleen is too weak to manage fluids, the skin is moist and pale, and digestive symptoms like bloating and loose stools are prominent. In the chronic dry phase, Blood or Yin deficiency leaves the skin undernourished, dry, and scaly, and itching is often worse at night. In infants, a pattern of Heart Fire blazing can cause bright red, intensely itchy skin with marked irritability and crying.

### การวินิจฉัยโดยแพทย์แผนจีน (Practitioner Diagnosis)
A TCM practitioner begins by looking closely at the skin itself-its color, moisture, and location-and by asking about what makes the itching better or worse. Because atopic dermatitis (四弯风, sì wān fēng) can shift between wet and dry phases, the appearance of the rash and the accompanying whole-body clues are the first signals that point toward one underlying pattern rather than another.
When the skin is red, hot, swollen, and weeping yellow fluid or crusting, especially during a sudden flare, the picture suggests a Damp-Heat pattern. The tongue is often red with a thick, greasy yellow coat, and the pulse feels slippery and fast. This pattern is driven by a combination of internal Dampness and Heat, and it is especially common in humid weather or during infancy.
If the rash is persistently moist, pale, and macerated, and the person also struggles with bloating, loose stools, or fatigue, the root is likely Spleen Deficiency with Dampness. Here the Spleen is too weak to transform fluids, so Dampness accumulates and surfaces on the skin. The tongue appears pale and swollen, often with a thin, white coat, and the pulse is soft or weak.
In the chronic, dry phase, the skin becomes rough, scaly, fissured, and intensely itchy, especially at night and in the inner elbows and knees. This points to Blood Deficiency with External Wind. The blood is too depleted to nourish the skin, allowing Dry-Wind to stir. The tongue is pale with a thin coat, and the pulse is thin and wiry. In some long-standing cases or in older adults, deep dryness with dark, lichenified patches, night itching, dry eyes, and a sore back can reveal an underlying Kidney and Liver Yin Deficiency, where the tongue is red with little or no coat.
In infants and young children, a different picture can emerge: bright red, hot, and very itchy lesions alongside irritability, crying, and disturbed sleep. This pattern is called Heart Fire blazing, and the tip of the tongue is especially red, with a rapid pulse. The practitioner uses these distinct emotional and tongue signs to separate Heart Fire from the other patterns.

### Blood Deficiency with External Wind
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/blood-deficiency-with-external-wind)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Dry, flaky, or lichenified skin in flexural creases, Itching that is worse at night, Pale complexion and dull, brittle nails, Aversion to wind or drafts, Dizziness or light-headedness, Scanty menstrual flow or dry, rough hair
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Dry, windy, or cold weather, Stress, anxiety, or frustration, Spicy, greasy, or fried foods, Hot showers and excessive scrubbing, Premenstrual phase
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Moisturizing the skin regularly, Eating warm, nourishing soups and stews, Adequate, restful sleep, Gentle movement such as walking or Tai Chi, Avoiding wind and drafts
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When the body's Blood is insufficient, the skin loses its nourishment and becomes dry, flaky, and fragile. This internal Dryness creates an opening for External Wind to invade the surface, stirring up itching that comes and goes and shifts location. The flexural creases of the elbows and knees are especially vulnerable because they are areas where Qi and Blood flow more sluggishly, making them the first to show the dry, lichenified patches typical of chronic atopic dermatitis.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale, thin body with a thin white and slightly dry coat. Pulse: fine (thin), may be wiry at the left middle position and floating at the right front position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Blood Deficiency leaves the skin undernourished and dry, making it vulnerable to External Wind. Dry weather and stress further deplete Blood and stir up internal Wind, intensifying itch. Nourishing foods and rest replenish Blood and Yin, while moisturizing and avoiding wind protect the surface. Gentle movement encourages Blood circulation without overstraining, and avoiding hot showers prevents further stripping of the skin's natural moisture.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat blood-nourishing foods like dark leafy greens, beets, black sesame, and bone broths. Avoid spicy, fried, or overly drying foods that can deplete Blood and fluids. Protect your skin from wind and dry environments by wearing natural, breathable fabrics and using gentle, fragrance-free moisturizers. Avoid long hot showers or harsh scrubbing, and maintain a regular sleep schedule to support Blood regeneration.

### Damp-Heat
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/damp-heat)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Red, hot, weeping lesions with yellow crusting, Heavy body and greasy mouth taste, Worse in humid weather, Loose, sticky stools
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Hot or humid weather, Spicy, greasy, or fried foods, Stress, anxiety, or frustration, Sweating
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Cool environment, Light, easily digestible meals, Keeping skin clean and dry, Rest and avoiding overheating
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Damp-Heat often starts with a weak Spleen that fails to transform fluids, letting internal Dampness build up. When external Heat or humid weather invades, or when diet adds fuel, Dampness and Heat brew together and surface on the skin. They settle in the warm, moist flexural creases of the elbows and knees, causing the acute red, hot, swollen, oozing lesions with yellow crusting and intense itching that define a flare.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: Red body with a yellow greasy coating, may be swollen with teeth marks. Pulse: Soggy-rapid (Ru Shu) or slippery-rapid (Hua Shu).
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Damp-Heat thrives in humid, hot conditions and is fueled by rich, greasy foods and emotional stress that generates internal Heat. A cool, dry environment and light diet starve the Damp-Heat and allow the body to clear it, reducing the skin inflammation.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat light, easily digestible foods like barley, mung beans, and bitter greens. Avoid dairy, greasy, spicy, and sugary foods that create Dampness and Heat. Keep skin clean and dry, and use cool compresses during flares. Avoid humid environments and excessive sweating.

### Spleen Deficiency with Dampness
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/spleen-deficiency-with-dampness)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Moist, weeping, oozing skin patches, Pale or skin-colored lesions, not bright red, Bloating and loose stools, Poor appetite and fatigue after eating, Feeling of heaviness in the body, Pale, swollen tongue with teeth marks and a greasy white coating
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Cold, raw, or greasy foods, Damp, humid environments, Overeating or large, heavy meals, Sedentary lifestyle, Worry and overthinking
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warm, dry weather, Light, easily digestible meals, Moderate, consistent exercise
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  In atopic dermatitis (四弯风, sì wān fēng), the Spleen is the organ responsible for transforming food into Qi and blood, and for moving fluids. When the Spleen is constitutionally weak, it cannot manage this fluid metabolism, so dampness builds up inside the body. This internal dampness then overflows to the skin, surfacing in the flexural creases - the elbows and knees - as weeping, macerated patches that ooze clear or slightly sticky fluid.
Unlike the hot, red, angry rashes of acute flares, this dampness-driven eczema feels heavy and moist. The skin lesions are often pale pink or the same color as the surrounding skin, and they linger because the underlying Spleen weakness remains unaddressed. The digestive symptoms that accompany it - bloating, loose stools, and fatigue - are direct signs that the Spleen is struggling.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale and swollen body with teeth marks on the edges, a white greasy coating, and a moist surface. Pulse: soggy (濡, ru) and slow (缓, huǎn), possibly slippery (滑, huá) and lacking force.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: The Spleen's ability to transform and transport fluids is easily bogged down by cold, dampness, and dietary overload. Cold raw foods and heavy meals demand more digestive fire than a weak Spleen can provide, causing dampness to accumulate and eventually spill onto the skin. Humid weather adds external dampness that mirrors the internal problem. Warm, easily digestible foods and moderate exercise gently stoke the Spleen's fire and keep Qi moving, so fluids are processed properly rather than stagnating and surfacing as weepy eczema.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Favor warm, cooked foods that are easy to digest - think congee, soups, and steamed vegetables. Avoid cold, raw foods, dairy, greasy fried items, and excessive sweets, all of which burden the Spleen and generate more dampness. Gentle daily movement like walking or stretching supports the Spleen's energy without exhausting it.

### Heart Fire blazing
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/heart-fire-blazing)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Bright red, hot skin lesions, Intense itching with burning sensation, Irritability and crying, especially at night, Disturbed sleep with frequent waking, Red tongue tip with prickles, Mouth or tongue ulcers
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Stress, anxiety, or frustration, Spicy, greasy, or fried foods, Hot or humid weather, Lack of sleep or overexertion, Excessive stimulation
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Cool environment, Cold drinks and cooling foods, Calm, quiet surroundings, Gentle cooling compresses on skin, Adequate, restful sleep
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  In this pattern, excess Heat accumulates in the Heart, often from a congenital tendency or emotional upset. The Heart governs the blood and vessels, so when Heart Fire flares, it transmits heat through the blood to the skin, causing bright red, hot, and intensely itchy lesions. The heat also disturbs the Shen (Spirit), leading to the characteristic irritability, crying, and disturbed sleep, especially at night.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red body with a distinctly redder tip, often with swollen red points at the tip and a yellow dry coating; a midline crack may extend to the tip. Pulse: rapid (数 shuò) and forceful, overflowing (洪 hóng) especially at the left front position, reflecting excess Heart Fire.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Heart Fire is an excess Heat pattern that flares with emotional agitation, dietary heat, and environmental warmth. Cooling foods and environments directly counteract the internal Fire, while calm and rest prevent the agitation of the Shen that fans the flames. These measures help to drain Fire from the Heart and prevent it from spreading to the skin.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Avoid spicy, fried, and greasy foods that generate internal Heat. Keep the living environment cool and calm. For infants, avoid over-wrapping and ensure a peaceful bedtime routine. Gentle cooling foods like mung bean soup or chrysanthemum tea can help.

### Kidney and Liver Yin Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/kidney-and-liver-yin-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Dry, scaly, lichenified patches in flexural areas, Intense itching that worsens at night, Dry eyes with blurred vision, Soreness and weakness of the lower back and knees, Night sweats and heat in the palms, soles, and chest, Insomnia or restless sleep with vivid dreams
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Stress, anxiety, or frustration, Spicy, greasy, or fried foods, Hot, dry environments, Lack of sleep or overexertion, Alcohol and caffeine
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Adequate, restful sleep, Cool, humidified air, Moistening foods like pear and cucumber, Gentle movement such as walking or Tai Chi, Stress reduction
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When the Yin of the Liver and Kidneys runs low, the body loses its deepest reserves of cooling, moistening fluid. This deep dryness allows deficiency heat and internal wind to rise, which surface on the skin as stubbornly dry, scaly, lichenified patches. The itching is often worst at night, when Yin energy is naturally at its lowest ebb and cannot restrain the heat.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red, thin, and cracked, with little or no coating and a dry surface. Pulse: fine (thin) and rapid, often wiry, and weak at the left Chi (Kidney) position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Late nights and overwork directly consume Kidney Yin, the body's cooling reserve, making skin drier and itchier. Spicy foods and alcohol add heat that flares deficiency fire. In contrast, restful sleep and gentle movement allow Yin to regenerate, while moistening foods replenish the fluids that keep skin supple. Cool, humid air calms the internal heat without chilling the body.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat plenty of moistening, cooling foods such as pear, cucumber, black sesame, goji berries, and bone broth. Avoid spicy, fried, and drying foods like coffee and alcohol. Go to bed early and protect your sleep; late nights deplete Kidney Yin. Gentle exercise like Tai Chi or walking supports Yin without overheating.

## คำถามที่พบบ่อย (Frequently Asked Questions)
### Can TCM cure my atopic dermatitis?
TCM aims to correct the underlying internal imbalances that make the skin vulnerable to flares. While many patients achieve long periods of clear skin and a significant reduction in severity and frequency of outbreaks, the constitutional tendency may remain, especially if there is a strong genetic component. The goal is lasting remission, not a one-time cure.

### How long until I see results?
Itching often begins to ease within the first one to two weeks of herbal treatment and acupuncture. Visible healing of the skin, especially in chronic, thickened patches, takes longer - typically several weeks to months. Acute, weeping rashes tend to respond faster than dry, lichenified ones.

### Is acupuncture safe for children with atopic dermatitis?
Yes, when performed by a qualified practitioner. For infants and young children, needles are often inserted only briefly or replaced with non-insertive techniques like acupressure, pediatric tui na massage, or low-level laser stimulation. Herbal medicine may be given as a bath or topical wash rather than taken internally.

### Do I need to change my diet?
Diet plays a key role in TCM treatment. You'll likely be advised to avoid foods that create Dampness and Heat, such as greasy, spicy, or heavily processed foods, dairy, and excess sugar. Instead, the focus shifts to warm, easily digestible meals with cooked vegetables, whole grains, and small amounts of lean protein. Specific recommendations are tailored to your pattern.

### Can I use TCM alongside steroid creams?
Yes. TCM treatment can be safely combined with conventional topical treatments. Many patients find they can gradually reduce their reliance on steroid creams as the skin improves. Always keep your dermatologist informed about any herbs you are taking, and never stop prescribed medications abruptly without medical supervision.

### What does the tongue tell you about my eczema?
The tongue is a key diagnostic tool in TCM. A red, greasy yellow coating points to Damp-Heat, while a pale, swollen tongue with a white coat suggests Spleen deficiency. A dry, cracked, red tongue with little coating indicates Yin deficiency. These clues help your practitioner pinpoint the exact pattern driving your skin symptoms.

## การวิจัยทางวิทยาศาสตร์สมัยใหม่ (Modern Scientific Research)
1. **Adjunctive Use of VGH4 for Moderate-to-Severe Atopic Dermatitis: A Randomized, Double-Blind, Placebo-Controlled Crossover Pilot Trial**
   *วารสารวิชาการ: Chen YC, et al. Life (Basel). 2024; 14(4):680.*
   This pilot trial evaluated the herbal formula VGH4 (a variant of Dang Gui Yin Zi) as an add-on therapy for moderate-to-severe atopic dermatitis. Patients receiving VGH4 showed significant reductions in SCORAD scores and improvements in quality of life compared to placebo, with a favorable safety profile.

2. **Chinese herbal medicine for atopic eczema: an overview of clinical evidence**
   *วารสารวิชาการ: Gu S, et al. Journal of Dermatological Treatment. 2017; 28(3):246-252.*
   This systematic review summarized randomized controlled trials of Chinese herbal medicine for atopic eczema. The review concluded that oral herbal medicine significantly reduced eczema severity and topical steroid use, though it noted that most trials were of moderate quality and further rigorous studies are needed.
