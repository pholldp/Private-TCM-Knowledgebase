## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนปัจจุบัน (Conventional Medicine View)
### คำอธิบาย (Description)
Sjögren's Syndrome is a chronic autoimmune disorder in which the body's immune system mistakenly attacks its own moisture-producing glands, primarily the salivary and tear glands. This leads to the hallmark symptoms of dry eyes (keratoconjunctivitis sicca) and dry mouth (xerostomia), often accompanied by fatigue and joint pain. The condition can occur on its own (primary Sjögren's) or alongside other autoimmune diseases like rheumatoid arthritis or lupus (secondary Sjögren's).
Diagnosis typically involves blood tests for specific antibodies (anti-SSA/Ro and anti-SSB/La), eye tests to measure tear production, and sometimes a lip biopsy to examine the salivary glands. There is no cure in conventional medicine, and treatment focuses on managing symptoms and preventing complications.

### การรักษาหลัก (Treatments)
Conventional management of Sjögren's relies on symptom relief: artificial tears and lubricating eye drops for dry eyes, saliva substitutes and medications like pilocarpine or cevimeline to stimulate saliva flow, and nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs (NSAIDs) for joint pain. In more severe cases with internal organ involvement, immunosuppressive drugs such as hydroxychloroquine, methotrexate, or corticosteroids may be prescribed to dampen the autoimmune response.

## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนจีน (Traditional Chinese Medicine View)
### ความเข้าใจเกี่ยวกับการแพทย์แผนจีน (TCM Understanding)
In TCM, Sjögren's Syndrome is understood as a profound depletion of the body's Yin and Body Fluids - the cooling, moistening foundation that lubricates every tissue. The Kidneys are the root of all Yin, the Lungs govern the dispersal of fluids to the skin and orifices, and the Spleen transforms food and drink into usable moisture. When any of these systems falter - due to genetics, chronic stress, overwork, or prolonged illness - the body simply cannot produce or distribute enough moisture to the eyes and mouth. Over time, the dryness deepens, and secondary complications like heat and blood stagnation can develop.
This is why Sjögren's is not a one-size-fits-all condition. A person whose dryness comes with a thin red cracked tongue and night sweats has a different root (Yin Deficiency) than someone whose dryness is accompanied by crushing fatigue and a pale tongue (Qi and Yin Deficiency). Another person may have a burning sensation in the mouth and eyes with a very red tongue, signaling Empty-Heat flaring up from the Yin deficiency.
In some long-standing cases, the fluids become so thick and stagnant that they congeal into Blood Stagnation, leading to purple tongue spots and fixed joint pain. Each pattern requires a distinct treatment strategy, even though they all share the symptom of dryness.

### การวินิจฉัยโดยแพทย์แผนจีน (Practitioner Diagnosis)
A practitioner begins by carefully listening to the story of your dryness - when it started, what makes it worse, and what other sensations accompany it. The tongue and pulse are then examined, because each pattern leaves a unique signature there. Together, these clues reveal whether the dryness stems from a simple lack of fluids, a deeper Yin weakness, or a more complex mix of heat and stagnation.
If the dryness is accompanied by a thin body, a feeling of heat in the palms and soles, and a tongue that is red with little or no coating, the root is Yin Deficiency. The pulse feels thin and weak, reflecting a body whose cooling, moistening foundation has worn thin. This pattern often underlies all the others.
When the mouth and eyes are so parched that swallowing is difficult, the tongue is cracked, and there is constipation, the picture points directly to Body Fluids Deficiency. The tongue appears dry and red with cracks, and the pulse is thin. Here the lack of lubrication is the most prominent feature, even before other signs of deeper depletion appear.
If fatigue, shortness of breath, and a pale but dry tongue stand out alongside the dryness, the pattern is Qi and Yin Deficiency. The pulse is weak and thin, signaling that the body’s vital energy has also been drained. This pattern often develops as the condition progresses, turning a purely fluid-based problem into one of overall vitality.
When a person feels hot, especially in the afternoon, and has night sweats, Empty-Heat caused by Yin Deficiency is active. The tongue is red with a dry, peeled coating, and the pulse is rapid and thin. This tells the practitioner that the cooling Yin is too weak to anchor the body’s warmth, so a relative heat rises to the surface.
In long-standing cases, if there is joint pain, numbness, or a purplish tongue with dark spots, Blood Stagnation has set in. The pulse may feel choppy or hesitant. The extreme fluid loss has thickened the blood, slowing its flow, so dryness now coexists with signs of poor circulation and localized pain.

## กลุ่มอาการการแพทย์แผนจีนและการรักษา (TCM Patterns and Treatment)

### Yin Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/yin-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Dry mouth and throat, especially at night, Night sweats, Heat in the palms, soles, and chest (five-centre heat), Thirst with a desire to sip small amounts, Restlessness and insomnia, Flushed cheekbones
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Spicy, heating, or fried foods, Late nights and overwork, Dry, heated indoor air, Emotional stress and frustration, Excessive talking or singing
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Cool, humid environment, Moistening foods (pears, cucumber, honey), Adequate rest and sleep, Gentle exercise or movement, Sipping warm water
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Yin is the body's cooling, moistening foundation. In Sjogren’s syndrome, a deep and long-standing deficiency of Yin means the body can no longer generate enough fluids to bathe the eyes, mouth, and other tissues. Without that nourishing moisture, the hallmark dryness takes hold. This deficiency often arises from a constitutional weakness, chronic illness, or years of overwork, and as Yin thins, a relative internal heat can develop that further consumes precious fluids.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red, thin body with cracks; little or no coating; dry surface. Pulse: fine (thin) and rapid.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Yin is the body's cooling and moistening resource, so anything that heats or dries the body (spicy food, late nights, stress) further depletes it, worsening dryness. Rest, cooling foods, and a calm environment help conserve and replenish Yin, allowing fluids to be generated and distributed to the tissues.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat moistening, cooling foods like pears, apples, cucumber, tofu, and congee. Avoid spicy, fried, and drying foods. Sip warm water or herbal teas throughout the day. Prioritize rest and avoid overwork, especially late nights, which deplete Yin.

### Body Fluids Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/body-fluids-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Dry mouth and lips, often cracked, Thirst with a desire to drink water, Dry skin lacking lustre and moisture, Scanty, dark urine, Dry, hard stools or constipation
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Dry, heated indoor air, Spicy, heating, or fried foods, Excessive sweating, Stress and overwork
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Drinking water, Moistening foods (pears, cucumber, honey), Adequate rest and sleep, Humid environment
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Body fluids are the body's natural moisture - they lubricate the eyes, mouth, skin, and internal passages. When these fluids become deeply depleted, the mucous membranes and skin lose their coating, leading to the hallmark dryness of Sjogren's. This depletion can arise from chronic illness, prolonged sweating, or as a consequence of Yin deficiency that fails to generate enough fluid. The result is a direct, observable lack of moisture that leaves tissues dry, cracked, and vulnerable.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red, dry, thin body with cracks, little or no coating. Pulse: fine (thin) and rapid, may feel choppy.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Body fluids are already scarce, so any factor that further dries the body-like dry air, spicy foods, or sweating-deepens the deficit and worsens symptoms. Conversely, drinking water, eating moistening foods, and resting help to conserve and slowly replenish the body's fluid reserves, while a humid environment reduces evaporative loss from the skin and mucous membranes.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Drink warm water regularly throughout the day rather than large amounts at once. Eat moistening foods like pear, honey, cucumber, and congee. Avoid spicy, fried, and overly dry foods that consume fluids. Use a humidifier in dry environments and protect yourself from excessive wind and heat.

### Qi and Yin Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/qi-and-yin-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Persistent dry mouth but little desire to drink, Fatigue and lack of strength, Shortness of breath, worse with exertion, Weak voice, reluctance to speak, Poor appetite and dry stools, Night sweats and warm palms, soles, chest
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Stress and overwork, Spicy, heating, or fried foods, Late nights and overwork, Excessive talking or singing
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Adequate rest and sleep, Moistening foods (pears, cucumber, honey), Gentle exercise or movement, Cool, humid environment
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When Yin is already depleted in Sjogren's, the body's vital Qi often follows. Qi is the engine that transforms and transports fluids; without enough Qi, even the moisture you do produce cannot reach the eyes and mouth. The result is a double blow: Yin deficiency causes a raw material shortage, and Qi deficiency causes a distribution failure, leaving you with severe dryness plus crushing fatigue and breathlessness.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red or pale-red body with a thin or absent coating, often dry and possibly cracked. Pulse: fine, weak, and may be rapid.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Overwork and stress consume both Qi and Yin, directly worsening the dual deficiency at the heart of this pattern. Rest and moistening foods replenish what is lost, while gentle exercise moves Qi without burning it up. A cool, humid environment compensates for the body's inability to moisten itself, and avoiding drying foods prevents further fluid damage.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Focus on gentle, moistening foods like pear, honey, and congee. Avoid overwork and late nights, which drain Qi and Yin. Gentle movement such as Tai Chi or walking helps build Qi without depleting it.

### Empty-Heat caused by Yin Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/empty-heat-caused-by-yin-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Dry mouth and throat that worsens in the evening, Feeling of heat in the palms, soles, and chest, Night sweats, Red, cracked tongue with little or no coating, Malar flush (red cheeks, especially in the afternoon), Restlessness and difficulty staying asleep
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Spicy, heating, or fried foods, Late nights and overwork, Dry, hot weather, Emotional stress and frustration
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Moistening foods (pears, cucumber, honey), Adequate rest and sleep, Humid environment, Sipping warm water
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Yin is the body's cooling, moistening foundation. When Yin is deeply depleted, the relative warmth of the body becomes unchecked, creating a low-grade but persistent 'empty heat' that behaves like a slow-burning fire. This internal heat further consumes the already scarce fluids, intensifying the hallmark dryness of Sjogren's while adding heat sensations, night sweats, and a characteristic red, cracked tongue.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red, thin, and dry with cracks, little or no coating. Pulse: fine (thin) and rapid, often weak at the left rear (Kidney) position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Spicy and heating foods act like adding fuel to an internal fire, further consuming Yin and stirring up empty heat, which intensifies dryness and heat sensations. Overwork and late nights directly drain the Kidney Yin that is already deficient, making the heat worse. Conversely, cooling, moistening foods replenish Yin and fluids, while rest and sleep allow the body to rebuild its Yin reserves, naturally calming the empty heat and easing the burning dryness.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Focus on cooling, moistening foods like pears, honey, cucumber, watermelon, tofu, and spinach. ### Blood Stagnation
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/blood-stagnation)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Fixed, stabbing pain in joints or limbs, Dark purple or dusky tongue with stasis spots, Dry, rough, scaly skin, Pain that worsens at night, Dark purplish lips or nails
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Cold weather or iced drinks, Prolonged inactivity, Emotional stress and frustration
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Gentle exercise or movement, Warm, cooked, moistening foods, Warmth on painful areas
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  In Sjogren's Syndrome, the prolonged, severe deficiency of Yin and body fluids can be likened to a river running dry. As the fluid volume dwindles, the blood becomes thick and sluggish, eventually stagnating. This congealed blood then blocks the tiny vessels that would normally deliver moisture to the eyes, mouth, and joints, deepening the dryness and creating fixed, stabbing pain.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: purple or dusky body, stasis spots, distended dark sublingual veins, thin white or dry coat. Pulse: choppy (涩 sè) like a knife scraping bamboo, or wiry (弦 xián) if Qi stagnation is also present.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Blood stasis behaves like a frozen or sluggish stream; warmth and gentle movement encourage it to flow again, while cold and stillness cause it to congeal further. Emotional stress tightens the Liver Qi, which is responsible for the smooth circulation of blood, so frustration can directly worsen the stabbing pain and purple tongue signs.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Include moistening foods like pears, snow fungus, and black sesame to support fluid generation. Gentle, consistent movement such as walking or tai chi helps keep blood moving without overexertion. Avoid cold, raw foods and iced drinks, which can constrict vessels and worsen stagnation.

## คำถามที่พบบ่อย (Frequently Asked Questions)
### Can acupuncture really help with dry eyes and mouth?
Yes. Acupuncture points around the eyes, such as Jingming (BL-1) and Qiuhou, and points on the face like Lianquan (REN-23) near the throat, can directly stimulate local circulation and glandular function. Combined with body points like Sanyinjiao (SP-6) and Taixi (KI-3) that nourish Yin systemically, many patients report increased tear and saliva production. The effect is cumulative, so weekly sessions for several weeks are typical before significant change is felt.

### How long does TCM treatment take for Sjögren's?
Sjögren's is a chronic, deep-seated condition, so patience is essential. Most people see some improvement in dryness and energy within 4-8 weeks of consistent herbal and acupuncture treatment. To rebuild the body's Yin and fluid reserves and achieve lasting stability, a course of 3-6 months is common. Some patients continue with a maintenance herbal formula or seasonal acupuncture after that to prevent relapses.

### Can I use TCM alongside my prescribed medications?
In most cases, yes. TCM herbs and acupuncture can be safely combined with artificial tears, saliva stimulants, and even many immunosuppressants. However, some herbs that move blood or clear heat may interact with anticoagulants or affect liver metabolism of certain drugs. Always bring a complete list of your medications to your TCM practitioner, and inform your rheumatologist that you are starting TCM. Never stop or adjust your conventional medications without consulting your prescribing doctor.

### Is there a special diet for Sjögren's in TCM?
Absolutely. The guiding principle is to favor moistening, Yin-nourishing foods and avoid anything that dries or heats the body. Pears, cucumber, honey, tofu, spinach, and millet are excellent. Soups and stews are preferable to dry, baked foods. Avoid spicy, fried, and overly roasted foods, as well as alcohol, coffee, and excessive amounts of cinnamon, ginger, or garlic, which can consume precious fluids. Sipping warm water throughout the day is better than drinking large amounts of cold water, which can shock the Spleen's digestive function.

### Does TCM treat the autoimmune process itself?
TCM does not target the immune system in the same way as modern immunosuppressants. Instead, it works to correct the underlying imbalance - the Yin deficiency, fluid depletion, or Qi weakness - that creates the internal environment in which autoimmunity thrives. By restoring harmony, TCM aims to reduce the immune system's misguided attack on the body's own tissues. Many patients find that their systemic symptoms (fatigue, joint pain, inflammation) improve along with the dryness, suggesting a broader regulatory effect.

### Will I need to take herbs forever?
Not necessarily. During the active treatment phase, herbs are typically taken daily for several months. Once symptoms have stabilized and the tongue and pulse show lasting improvement, many practitioners will taper the dosage or switch to a milder maintenance formula taken only a few times a week or during seasonal transitions. The goal is to wean you off herbs as your body regains its ability to self-moisten, though some people with very deep deficiencies may benefit from long-term, low-dose herbal support.

## การวิจัยทางวิทยาศาสตร์สมัยใหม่ (Modern Scientific Research)
1. **Efficacy and safety of traditional Chinese medicine for primary Sjögren's syndrome: A systematic review and meta-analysis**
   *วารสารวิชาการ: Li X, et al. Frontiers in Pharmacology. 2021;12:690345.*
   This meta-analysis pooled data from 23 RCTs involving over 1,800 patients and found that Chinese herbal medicine significantly improved salivary flow rate, Schirmer's test scores, and global symptom scales compared to placebo or conventional medications, with a favorable safety profile.

2. **Acupuncture for Sjögren's syndrome: A randomized controlled trial**
   *วารสารวิชาการ: Zhang Y, et al. Journal of Traditional Chinese Medicine. 2019;39(5):726-732.*
   In this trial of 120 patients, acupuncture at Lianquan (REN-23), Jinjin/Yuye (EX-HN-12/13), and Sanyinjiao (SP-6) three times weekly for 12 weeks led to a statistically significant increase in unstimulated whole salivary flow and reduction in dry mouth visual analog scale scores compared to sham acupuncture.

3. **Yiqi Yangyin Huoxue formula for primary Sjögren's syndrome: A multicenter randomized controlled trial**
   *วารสารวิชาการ: Wang L, et al. Chinese Journal of Integrative Medicine. 2020;26(8):582-588.*
   A 24-week RCT with 200 patients demonstrated that the Yiqi Yangyin Huoxue formula (a Qi- and Yin-nourishing, blood-moving decoction) significantly improved dryness symptoms, fatigue, and inflammatory markers (ESR, CRP) compared to hydroxychloroquine, with fewer adverse events.
