## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนปัจจุบัน (Conventional Medicine View)
### คำอธิบาย (Description)
From a conventional medical perspective, a subjective sensation of a lump or something stuck in the abdomen, without a palpable mass, is often termed a functional gastrointestinal disorder. It falls under the umbrella of conditions like functional dyspepsia, irritable bowel syndrome, or globus sensation when felt higher. The sensation is real, but standard imaging-endoscopy, ultrasound, or CT scans-often shows no structural blockage.
A diagnosis is typically made after ruling out organic causes, based on symptom criteria and the absence of alarm features. Common Western contributors are thought to include visceral hypersensitivity, where the gut nerves overreact to normal stimuli, and dysmotility, where the muscular contractions of the digestive tract become uncoordinated. The approach focuses on managing the nerve-signal dysfunction and spasms.

### การรักษาหลัก (Treatments)
When no structural cause is found, treatment often starts with reassurance and dietary modification. Mainstream pharmacologic options include prokinetic agents to stimulate stomach emptying, antispasmodics to calm gut muscle cramps, and medications that modulate visceral pain, such as low-dose tricyclic antidepressants. Acid-suppressing drugs may be tried if dyspepsia is suspected. Cognitive behavioral therapy and gut-directed hypnotherapy are also evidence-based strategies for managing the brain-gut axis dysfunction underlying these functional sensations.

## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนจีน (Traditional Chinese Medicine View)
### ความเข้าใจเกี่ยวกับการแพทย์แผนจีน (TCM Understanding)
Chinese medicine understands the sensation of a blockage in the abdomen primarily through the Spleen, Stomach, and Liver organ systems. The Spleen and Stomach are the central digestive engine: the Stomach takes in food and sends the pure portion downward, while the Spleen extracts nourishment and lifts it upward. When this rhythmic descending and ascending gets disrupted, the middle burner jams. Material that should be moving-whether food, fluids, or Qi-sits still and generates the subjective feeling of a lump.
The Liver is the other key player. It is responsible for the free and unblocked flow of Qi everywhere in the body, like a traffic controller. Emotional stress, frustration, or long-held tension cause the Liver to stagnate, and its Qi backs up directly into the digestive tract.
This is why the sensation often shifts, moves, or worsens with mood-the blockage is not a physical mass but a knot of stuck, pressurized energy. If the Liver's stagnation goes on long enough, it can even slow the circulation of blood, creating a deeper, fixed stabbing sensation of a mass.
Where conventional medicine sees a functional disorder, TCM sees a spectrum of distinct mechanisms. A heavy blockage that feels better after vomiting is likely the excess of Food Stagnation. A cold, dull blockage that aches and feels better with a hot pack points to the deficiency of Spleen Yang. A sticky, burning bloated blockage is the mixed pattern of Damp-Heat. Each of these-excess, deficiency, cold, hot, stuck Qi, stuck blood-requires a completely different treatment strategy, even though they all feel like "something stuck."

### การวินิจฉัยโดยแพทย์แผนจีน (Practitioner Diagnosis)
A TCM practitioner begins by asking how the blocked sensation actually feels and what makes it better or worse. A moving distension that shifts with belching points toward Liver Qi Stagnation, while a heavy, fixed lump that eases after vomiting suggests Food Stagnation.
If the blockage feels cold and empty, worsens after eating, and improves with warmth and gentle pressure, the picture shifts toward Spleen Yang Deficiency. Here the tongue is pale and swollen, and the pulse feels weak and deep, confirming the internal cold and deficiency.
When the sensation is a severe, cramping pain that is fixed in one spot and feels better with heat, Cold-Damp invading the Spleen is suspected. The tongue coating is white and greasy, and the pulse feels tight, reflecting the external pathogenic cold trapped inside.
A sticky, burning obstruction with fullness that feels worse with pressure points to Damp-Heat in the Stomach and Spleen. The tongue has a yellow greasy coating and the pulse is slippery and rapid, guiding the practitioner toward clearing heat and drying dampness.
If the sensation is a stabbing, fixed pain like a knife piercing one spot, Blood Stagnation is the key. The tongue appears dark or purple with a choppy pulse, and the practitioner will focus on moving blood and breaking up the stasis.

### Liver Qi Stagnation
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/liver-qi-stagnation)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Sensation of a lump that moves around, Distension along the ribs and flanks, Belching or passing gas relieves the feeling, Worsens with stress, frustration, or anger, Irritability or emotional mood swings, Frequent sighing
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Stress or anger, Irregular eating habits, Greasy, heavy foods
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Belching or passing gas, Gentle exercise or stretching, Emotional calm and stress relief
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Your Liver is responsible for keeping Qi flowing smoothly throughout your body. When emotional stress, frustration, or anxiety builds up, that flow gets stuck - a state called Liver Qi Stagnation. The stuck Qi accumulates in your middle burner, creating pressure and a subjective sensation that something is lodged in your abdomen. Because Qi is formless and moves, the sensation often shifts around, feels like a swelling or a lump that comes and goes, and is frequently accompanied by belching or passing gas, which temporarily relieves the pressure.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: normal or slightly dark body, red or slightly purple sides, thin white coat. Pulse: wiry (弦, xián), taut like a guitar string.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Emotional stress and anger are the primary causes of Liver Qi Stagnation, directly creating the sensation of a stuck lump. Belching and passing gas are the body's natural way of releasing trapped Qi, which is why they offer temporary relief. Gentle exercise moves Qi in the channels, counteracting the stagnation that causes the blockage feeling.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Prioritize gentle, regular movement like walking or yoga to keep Qi flowing. Avoid greasy or heavy foods that bog down digestion. Warm, cooked meals with aromatic spices like mint, fennel, and cardamom help to gently move stagnant Qi.

### Food Stagnation in the Stomach
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/food-stagnation-in-the-stomach)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Fullness and distension worse after eating and with pressure, Sour, rotten-smelling belching, Nausea or vomiting of sour, undigested food, Relief after vomiting, Aversion to food and its smell, Foul-smelling flatulence like rotten eggs, Loud rumbling sounds in the abdomen, Loose stools with a sour, foul odour
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Overeating or rich meals, Cold, raw, or greasy foods, Pressure on the abdomen, Eating late at night
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Vomiting, Temporary fasting, Warm, plain congee, Gentle abdominal massage downward
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When you eat more than your stomach and intestines can handle - or when meals are too rich, too rushed, or poorly combined - the digestive system becomes overwhelmed. Food sits and ferments instead of being properly broken down, forming a physical accumulation. This accumulation obstructs the normal downward movement of Stomach Qi, creating the distinct feeling that something is stuck or blocked in the upper abdomen.
The rotting food also generates turbid, sour-smelling vapours that rise upward, producing putrid belching and acid reflux. The blockage is a genuine excess - the abdomen feels full and uncomfortable, especially after eating, and pressing on it makes it worse.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: body is usually normal in colour, with a thick, greasy or curd-like coating that is thickest in the centre. Pulse: slippery (滑 huá), especially strong at the right middle position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: This pattern is caused by a physical backlog of undigested food sitting in the stomach and intestines. Vomiting provides immediate relief by expelling the stuck material. Fasting and light congee give the digestive system a break so it can work through the accumulation. Rich, cold, or greasy foods add more bulk and dampness, making the blockage even harder to shift. Pressure aggravates the sensation because this is a true excess condition - the blockage is real, not just a Qi spasm.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Temporarily fast or eat only light, warm congee until the fullness passes - this gives the digestive system a chance to clear the backlog. When appetite returns, eat small, simple meals and chew thoroughly. Avoid cold drinks, raw foods, fried foods, dairy, and sweets until the tongue coating returns to normal, as these create more dampness and congestion.

### Spleen Yang Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/spleen-yang-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Dull, persistent sensation of blockage, relieved by warmth and pressure, Worse after eating, especially heavy or cold foods, Loose, watery stools with undigested food, Poor appetite and abdominal bloating, Cold hands and feet, feeling cold easily, Fatigue and a bland taste in the mouth
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Cold, raw, or greasy foods, Overeating or rich meals, Cold or damp weather, Overwork and fatigue
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Applying warmth (hot water bottle), Gentle abdominal massage downward, Warm, plain congee, Ginger or cinnamon in cooking
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  The Spleen is the body's main digestive engine, transforming food into energy and lifting Qi upward. When Spleen Yang is deficient, this internal fire grows weak, and cold settles in the middle burner. Digestion slows to a crawl, fluids accumulate, and both food and dampness stagnate, creating a subjective feeling of a cold, immobile obstruction deep in the abdomen.
Because Yang provides warmth and movement, its deficiency means the blockage sensation worsens as digestion demands more effort - particularly after eating, when the weakened Spleen simply cannot cope. Unlike sharp or cramping pains, this is a dull, persistent sense of fullness and stuffiness that the person instinctively wants to press and warm.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale, puffy, and tender with teeth marks on the edges; white, slippery coating. Pulse: deep, slow, and weak, especially soft or empty at the right Guan position (Spleen and Stomach).
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: In this pattern, the Spleen's warming, transforming function is too weak to manage the digestive load. Cold and raw foods directly extinguish the already-dim Yang fire, causing fluids and undigested matter to accumulate into a sensation of blockage. Overeating or fatty meals overwhelm the Spleen's capacity, making the post-meal bloating much worse. Conversely, external warmth, warm foods, and gentle pressure temporarily compensate for the missing Yang, promoting movement and easing the dull, stuck sensation.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat warm, cooked, easily digestible foods like soups, stews, congee, and root vegetables. Add warming spices gently - a little ginger, cinnamon, or black pepper in cooking goes a long way. Strictly avoid iced drinks, raw salads, dairy, and greasy foods, all of which douse the digestive fire. Never skip meals but eat smaller, more frequent portions to avoid overwhelming the Spleen. Applying a warm compress or hot water bottle to the abdomen after meals can bring quick comfort.

### Cold-Damp invading the Spleen
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/cold-damp-invading-the-spleen)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Dull, cold, heavy sensation in the upper abdomen, Bloating and fullness that is fixed and not relieved by belching, Loose or watery stools, Heavy feeling in the limbs and head as if wrapped, Thick white greasy tongue coating
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Cold, raw, or greasy foods, Cold or damp weather, Overeating or rich meals, Greasy, heavy foods
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Applying warmth (hot water bottle), Warm drinks and foods, Light, easily digested meals, Rest and lying down
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When external cold and dampness invade the body, they can directly attack the Spleen and Stomach, the organs responsible for digestion and fluid metabolism. The cold congeals Qi, while dampness clogs the channels, causing a sensation of something heavy and stuck in the upper abdomen. This blockage leads to bloating, dull pain, and a feeling of fullness that does not move around like gas.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale swollen body with teeth marks, thick white greasy coating. Pulse: soggy (Ru) or slippery (Hua) and slow (Chi).
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Cold and dampness are heavy, congealing forces that block the Spleen's ability to transform and transport. Warmth and light foods help dispel cold and dry dampness, restoring movement. Cold, raw, and greasy foods add more dampness and cold, worsening the obstruction and the sensation of something stuck.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat warm, cooked foods like soups and stews. Avoid cold drinks, raw vegetables, dairy, and greasy foods that generate dampness. Gentle walking after meals can help move Qi and prevent stagnation.

### Damp-Heat in Stomach and Spleen
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/damp-heat-in-stomach-and-spleen)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Sticky, bloated sensation of obstruction rather than a sharp lump, Bitter or slimy taste in the mouth, Fullness in the upper abdomen with nausea, Loose, sticky, or incomplete bowel movements, Heavy, foggy head and aching limbs
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Greasy, heavy foods, Cold or damp weather, Sedentary habits
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Light, easily digested meals, Gentle exercise or stretching, Avoiding humid environments
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When Dampness and Heat lodge together in the middle burner, they create a heavy, sticky congestion. The accumulated pathological fluids obstruct the normal descending and ascending flow of the Spleen and Stomach, producing a sensation of something lodged or stuck. Unlike a sharp, fixed mass, this obstruction feels bloated, full, and unclean, because the underlying problem is turbid, gluey Damp-Heat rather than a solid lump.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red body, possibly swollen with teeth marks, covered by a thick, yellow, greasy coating that is heaviest at the center and root. Pulse: soggy (濡, ru) and rapid (数, shu), often with a slippery (滑, hua) quality.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Greasy, rich, and sweet foods directly create Dampness and Heat in the Spleen and Stomach, thickening the internal turbidity that produces the stuck sensation. A damp, hot environment mirrors the internal imbalance and makes it worse. Light eating, ample movement, and drying the living space all work to transform Dampness and clear Heat, freeing the abdomen.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Avoid heavy, greasy, fried, and sweet foods - these feed Damp-Heat. Keep meals light and simple: cooked whole grains, well-steamed vegetables, and modest amounts of cooling fruits like pear. Drink small sips of warm water or unsweetened barley tea, not icy drinks, which trap moisture inside. Gentle daily movement like walking helps transform Dampness.

### Blood Stagnation
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/blood-stagnation)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Fixed, stabbing pain that does not move, Sensation of a fixed mass or lump, Pain worse at night and with pressure, Dark or purplish lips and nails
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Sedentary habits, Cold, raw, or greasy foods, Stress or anger, Pressure on the abdomen
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Gentle movement and stretching, Applying warmth (hot water bottle), Light, easily digested meals
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When Qi flow in the abdomen stalls for a long time, blood flow eventually slows and congeals, creating deeper, fixed obstruction. Chronic emotional tension, physical injury, or surgery can cause this stasis. The sensation of something stuck is not just a vague bloating but often a fixed, immovable mass-like feeling because the blood is literally pooling in one spot and cannot move freely through the vessels.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: dark purple body with possible stasis spots, distended dark sublingual veins. Pulse: choppy (涩 Sè) and uneven, possibly wiry.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Blood stasis is a condition of congealed, static flow. Inactivity allows blood to pool, while cold causes further congealing, both worsening the fixed pain. Stress and anger stagnate Liver Qi, which is the root cause of blood stasis. Gentle movement, warmth, and a calm mind all help to invigorate Qi and blood, gradually breaking up the stasis and restoring smooth flow.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Light, warm, and easily digestible foods like soups and stews are best. Avoid cold, raw foods and dairy, which can congeal and worsen stagnation. Gentle movement like walking or tai chi helps keep blood flowing smoothly.

## คำถามที่พบบ่อย (Frequently Asked Questions)
### Could this stuck sensation really just be stress?
Yes, in Chinese medicine a very common cause is exactly that-stress. The Liver system, which governs the smooth flow of Qi, becomes jammed by chronic emotional tension and frustration. This backed-up energy can lodge in the digestive tract, creating the physical sensation of a moving lump or blockage that often worsens before a stressful event and improves with belching, a good walk, or emotional calm.

### How do I know if the blockage is from too much food or something else?
Food Stagnation has a very distinctive signature: sour, rotten-smelling belches, a greasy thick tongue coat, and the sensation often gets dramatically better after vomiting or skipping a meal. If belching brings pure relief without the sour smell, and the sensation shifts locations, it points more toward Liver Qi Stagnation. A cold, aching blockage that craves pressure and heat leans toward Spleen Yang Deficiency.

### Will acupuncture help move the sensation of a blockage?
Acupuncture is particularly effective at re-establishing the downward movement of the Stomach and the unblocked flow of Liver Qi. Many patients feel a physical release-deep gurgling, a sense of the blockage "dissolving"-during or shortly after a session. The points used, often on the abdomen (like Zhongwan REN-12) and the legs, are selected to directly target the stuck Qi and restore the normal rhythm of the digestive tract.

### Do I need to change my diet even if I eat healthily?
Almost always yes, but not necessarily in the way you think. The change depends on your specific pattern. Someone with Spleen Yang Deficiency needs warm, cooked foods and should completely avoid raw cold salads and iced drinks, even if those are conventionally "healthy." Someone with Damp-Heat needs to cut out greasy, rich, and spicy foods even if they don't overeat. The right diet supports your specific herbal plan and is crucial for lasting relief.

### What if my doctor says all my tests are normal?
This is a classic presentation for what Chinese medicine diagnoses as a functional blockage of Qi, food, or dampness. It means the sensation is not caused by a tumor or stricture, which is good news. It also means your condition falls squarely in the category Chinese medicine excels at treating: an energetic and physiological imbalance in how your organs are coordinating, which herbs and acupuncture can directly correct, often when conventional approaches have little to offer beyond symptom management.

### Can I take Chinese herbs with my digestive medication?
In most cases, yes, but you must inform your TCM practitioner about every medicine and supplement you are taking. Herbal formulas for moving Qi and dissolving food stagnation are generally safe alongside standard antacids or prokinetics, but must be taken at least an hour apart to prevent interaction in the stomach. If you are on stronger medication, always coordinate the integrative plan with both your prescribing doctor and your herbalist.

## การวิจัยทางวิทยาศาสตร์สมัยใหม่ (Modern Scientific Research)
1. **Acupuncture for functional dyspepsia** ([ลิงก์](https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD008587.pub2))
   *วารสารวิชาการ: Lan L, Zeng F, Liu GJ, et al. Acupuncture for functional dyspepsia. Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews 2014, Issue 10. Art. No.: CD008587.*
   This Cochrane systematic review and meta-analysis evaluated acupuncture versus sham acupuncture or medication in patients with functional dyspepsia. Results suggested acupuncture may significantly improve symptoms such as postprandial fullness and epigastric discomfort, with a low risk of adverse events.

2. **Chinese herbal medicine for functional dyspepsia: a systematic review of randomized controlled trials**
   *วารสารวิชาการ: Chen M, et al. Chinese herbal medicine for functional dyspepsia: a systematic review of randomized controlled trials. Journal of Gastroenterology and Hepatology. 2010;25(3):469-476.*
   This systematic review assessed the efficacy of Chinese herbal medicine in functional dyspepsia. The analysis found that herbal formulas were more effective than placebo or prokinetic drugs in relieving dyspeptic symptoms, though the overall quality of included studies was low, highlighting a need for better-designed trials.
