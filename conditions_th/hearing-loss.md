## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนปัจจุบัน (Conventional Medicine View)
### คำอธิบาย (Description)
Hearing loss is a partial or total inability to hear sounds in one or both ears. It can be conductive (problems in the outer or middle ear), sensorineural (damage to the inner ear or auditory nerve), or mixed. Common causes include ageing, noise exposure, infections, certain medications, and genetic factors. Diagnosis typically involves audiometry and physical examination. Sudden sensorineural hearing loss is a medical emergency treated with corticosteroids.

### การรักษาหลัก (Treatments)
Treatment depends on the type and cause. Conductive losses may be treated with surgery or medication. Sensorineural hearing loss is often permanent; hearing aids or cochlear implants are the mainstays. Sudden hearing loss may be treated with steroids. Tinnitus management and auditory rehabilitation are also used.

## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนจีน (Traditional Chinese Medicine View)
### ความเข้าใจเกี่ยวกับการแพทย์แผนจีน (TCM Understanding)
In TCM, the ears are the external opening of the Kidneys, and their ability to hear depends on the nourishment provided by Kidney Essence. But they are also connected to the Liver and Gallbladder channels, which run around the ears, and rely on the Spleen's ability to transform food into Qi and Blood that rise to the head. So hearing loss is rarely just an ear problem - it reflects a deeper imbalance in the organ systems that supply and protect the ears.
When hearing fades gradually, especially with age, it often points to Kidney Essence Deficiency: the body's deepest reserves are running low, and the ears starve. A sudden loss after an angry outburst suggests Liver Fire Blazing upward. A gradual loss that worsens with fatigue and poor digestion points to Spleen Qi Deficiency. Muffled hearing with a clogged sensation and phlegm in the throat may be Phlegm-Heat. And if the loss follows a head injury or long-standing stress, Qi and Blood Stagnation may be blocking the ear channels.
This is why TCM doesn't have one 'hearing loss' treatment. A practitioner will look at the whole picture - your energy, digestion, emotions, tongue, and pulse - to identify which pattern is dominant, and then choose herbs and acupuncture points that address that specific root. The goal is not just to amplify sound, but to restore the body's natural ability to hear by fixing the internal imbalance.

### การวินิจฉัยโดยแพทย์แผนจีน (Practitioner Diagnosis)
A TCM practitioner begins by listening carefully to your story - when the hearing loss started, whether it came on suddenly or crept in slowly, and what else you feel in your body. The speed of onset, the quality of any tinnitus, and the presence of triggers like anger or exhaustion are the first clues that point toward a particular pattern.
If the loss is gradual, with a high‑pitched ringing that worsens at night, and you notice a deep ache in the lower back and knees, the picture points to Kidney Essence Deficiency. The ears are the “orifice of the kidneys,” so when the body’s foundational essence runs low the ears lose their nourishment. The tongue may be pale or red with little coating, and the pulse feels thin and weak.
A sudden drop in hearing, especially after an outburst of frustration, suggests Liver Fire Blazing. You might feel a swollen, blocked sensation in the ear, a bitter taste in the mouth, and a hot temper. The tongue is red with a yellow coat, and the pulse is wiry and rapid - signs that fierce heat is flaring upward to disturb the ear orifices.
When hearing fades gradually and worsens whenever you feel run‑down, along with poor appetite, loose stools, and a heavy, tired body, a practitioner thinks of Spleen Qi Deficiency. The Spleen is supposed to lift clear Qi to the head, but when it is weak the ears starve for nourishment. The tongue looks pale and puffy, and the pulse is soft and weak.
If the hearing loss is accompanied by a pale, dull complexion, shortness of breath, and a general sense of being drained, Qi and Blood Deficiency may be the root. Both Qi and blood are too scanty to ascend and moisten the ears. The tongue is pale and thin, and the pulse is fine and forceless - a picture of overall emptiness.
A sensation of fullness or blockage in the ear, together with a heavy chest, lots of phlegm, and a sticky yellow tongue coat, points to Phlegm-Heat. Here, thick, hot phlegm rises and clouds the ear passages. The pulse is often slippery and rapid, reflecting the sticky obstruction inside.
Lastly, when the ear feels distended or painful and the hearing loss follows a head injury or a long‑standing illness, Qi and Blood Stagnation may be the culprit. The tongue can be dark or show purple spots, and the pulse feels choppy - signs that the flow of Qi and blood through the ear channels has become stuck.

### Kidney Essence Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/kidney-essence-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Gradual hearing loss that worsens with age, High-pitched ringing in the ears (tinnitus), Sore and weak lower back and knees, Dizziness or lightheadedness, Poor memory and difficulty concentrating
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Overwork, fatigue, or stress, Late nights, Excessive sexual activity, Cold environment, Worry and mental strain
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Rest and adequate sleep, Warm, nourishing meals, Gentle exercise like walking, Moderate warmth
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  In TCM, the Kidneys store Essence, the body's deepest reserve, and the ears are their external opening. When Kidney Essence runs low - through ageing, overwork, or chronic illness - it can no longer rise to nourish the ears. The result is a gradual, progressive hearing loss, often accompanied by a high-pitched ringing and a feeling of emptiness in the head.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale, thin body with little or no coating; fine cracks may appear. Pulse: fine, deep, and weak, especially at the Chi (rear) positions.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Kidney Essence is the body's deepest reserve, built slowly over a lifetime. Overwork, late nights, and excessive sexual activity drain it directly, while cold and stress slow its replenishment. Rest, nourishing warm foods, and gentle movement protect what remains and allow the Essence to gradually rise and nourish the ears again.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat kidney-nourishing foods like black beans, walnuts, goji berries, and bone broths. Avoid overworking, late nights, and excessive sexual activity, which deplete Essence. Gentle daily movement such as Tai Chi or walking supports vitality without draining reserves.

### Liver Fire Blazing
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/liver-fire-blazing)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Sudden hearing loss or muffled hearing, often triggered by anger, Sensation of fullness or pressure in the ear, Bitter taste in the mouth, Intense irritability and short temper, Throbbing headache at the temples
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Anger and frustration, Greasy, fried, spicy, or sweet foods, Alcohol, coffee, or spicy foods, Hot weather, Overwork, fatigue, or stress
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Cooling foods and drinks, Rest and stress reduction, Gentle exercise like walking, Cool environment
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  The Liver channel connects to the ears, and when intense anger or frustration causes Liver Qi to stagnate, it can rapidly transform into Fire. This Fire flares upward along the channel, surging into the head and ears, causing sudden swelling, fullness, and obstruction of the ear orifices. The result is a sudden hearing loss or muffled hearing, often accompanied by loud tinnitus.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: Red body with redder sides, dry yellow coating. Pulse: Wiry, rapid, and forceful, especially at the left Guan position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Anger and frustration cause Liver Qi to stagnate and then surge upward as Fire, directly attacking the ears. Spicy and greasy foods add Heat to the body, fueling the Fire. Cooling foods, a calm mind, and gentle exercise help clear Heat, soothe the Liver, and allow Qi to descend, relieving ear pressure and restoring hearing.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Avoid spicy, greasy, and fried foods that generate internal Heat. Limit alcohol and coffee. Eat cooling foods like cucumber, celery, bitter gourd, and chrysanthemum tea. Practice stress management techniques such as meditation or deep breathing to prevent anger from flaring up. Regular gentle exercise like walking or tai chi helps keep Liver Qi moving smoothly.

### Spleen Qi Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/spleen-qi-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Poor appetite or reduced food intake, Loose or soft stools, Hearing loss worsens with fatigue and exertion, Fatigue and lack of energy, heaviness in the limbs, Dull yellowish or pale complexion
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Overwork, fatigue, or stress, Skipping meals or poor diet, Raw, cold foods and iced drinks, Worry and mental strain
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Rest and adequate sleep, Warm, nourishing meals, Small, regular meals, Gentle exercise like walking
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  The Spleen transforms food into Qi and blood, which then rise to nourish the ears. When Spleen Qi is weak, this upward flow of nourishment is insufficient. The ears become malnourished, leading to a gradual decline in hearing that worsens whenever the body is tired or digestion is poor.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale, puffy and tender body with tooth marks, thin white coating. Pulse: weak and forceless, especially soft and deficient at the right Guan (Spleen) position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: The Spleen is most productive when the body is rested and receives gentle, warm nourishment. Overwork and raw cold foods directly weaken Spleen Qi, reducing the supply of clear Yang that rises to the ears. Emotional worry knots the Qi, further impairing digestion. Rest, warm meals, and gentle movement help the Spleen generate enough Qi to lift nourishment upward, which gradually improves hearing.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat warm, cooked meals at regular times. Favour easily digested foods like congee, root vegetables, and small amounts of lean protein. Avoid raw, cold, and greasy foods that tax the Spleen. Gentle daily movement such as walking supports Qi circulation without depleting energy.

### Qi and Blood Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/qi-and-blood-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Gradual hearing loss, worse after exertion, Pale complexion and lips, Heart palpitations, Numbness or tingling in limbs
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Overwork, fatigue, or stress, Skipping meals or poor diet, Worry and mental strain
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Rest and adequate sleep, Warm, nourishing meals, Gentle exercise like walking
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Qi and Blood are the body's fundamental nourishing forces. When both are deficient, the ears-which rely on a steady supply of Qi to hear and Blood to be properly nourished-begin to lose their acuity. This pattern often develops gradually from chronic illness, poor diet, or overwork, leading to a slow decline in hearing that worsens whenever the body is tired or depleted.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: Pale body, may be thin or have teeth marks, thin white coating. Pulse: Fine (thin) and weak, especially in the right Guan position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Qi and Blood are the fundamental substances that nourish the ears. Rest and nourishment replenish these resources, while overwork, poor diet, and emotional strain deplete them. When Qi and Blood are insufficient, the ear orifices lack the vital nourishment needed for clear hearing, so anything that conserves and builds these resources will improve symptoms.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat warm, cooked, nutrient-dense foods like bone broths, congees, dark leafy greens, and small amounts of red meat or liver. Avoid raw, cold, and processed foods that tax the Spleen. Prioritize adequate rest and gentle exercise like walking or tai chi to support Qi and Blood circulation.

### Phlegm-Heat
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/phlegm-heat)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Sensation of ear blockage or fullness, Chest tightness or oppression, Coughing up thick, yellow, sticky phlegm, Nausea or a heavy, foggy-headed feeling
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Greasy, fried, spicy, or sweet foods, Alcohol, coffee, or spicy foods, Damp or humid weather, Anger and frustration
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Light, bland diet, Cool, dry air, Deep breathing exercises
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When the diet is rich in greasy, sweet, or spicy foods, the Spleen and Stomach can become overwhelmed and produce Phlegm and Heat. This sticky, hot Phlegm accumulates in the middle burner and then rises upward, physically clogging the delicate channels that connect to the ears. The clear Yang Qi that should be nourishing the ears cannot ascend, resulting in a sensation of blockage, muffled hearing, and often tinnitus.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red body, possibly swollen, with a thick yellow greasy coating. Pulse: slippery (hua) and rapid (shu).
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Greasy, sweet, and dairy-rich foods directly create the sticky Phlegm that blocks the ear channels, while spicy food and alcohol add the Heat component. Emotional stress, especially anger, stirs up Liver Fire which can combine with Phlegm to make the blockage worse. A light diet and cool, dry air help the body break down existing Phlegm and prevent new buildup, while gentle movement and deep breathing keep Qi and fluids circulating so they don't stagnate.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Avoid greasy, fried, dairy-heavy, and overly sweet foods, as these directly generate Phlegm and Dampness. Cut back on alcohol, coffee, and spicy dishes to prevent adding Heat. Focus on light, easily digested meals with plenty of lightly cooked vegetables and grains like barley. Gentle daily exercise helps keep Qi and fluids moving so they don't stagnate into Phlegm.

### Qi And Blood Stagnation
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/qi-and-blood-stagnation)*

- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Emotional stress, Prolonged inactivity, Cold environment, Trauma or injury
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Gentle exercise like walking, Warm compress on the ears, Rest and stress reduction, Acupressure or massage
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When Qi and blood become stagnant in the ear channels-often after a head injury, chronic stress, or long-standing illness-the flow of nourishment to the ears is blocked. This stagnation creates a sensation of fullness or distension and can lead to a decline in hearing. Over time, the lack of fresh blood and Qi reaching the ear orifices results in persistent hearing loss.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: dark purple body with stasis spots, distended sublingual veins, thin white coat. Pulse: wiry (弦 xián) and choppy (涩 sè).
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Qi and blood stagnation in the ear channels is worsened by anything that constricts or slows circulation, like cold, inactivity, and emotional tension. Movement, warmth, and relaxation help blood flow and open the channels, relieving ear fullness and restoring hearing.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat foods that gently invigorate blood, such as black fungus, hawthorn, and turmeric. Avoid cold, raw foods that constrict circulation. Gentle daily movement like walking or tai chi helps prevent stagnation. Manage stress with breathing exercises to keep Qi flowing smoothly.

## คำถามที่พบบ่อย (Frequently Asked Questions)
### Can acupuncture really improve hearing?
Yes, acupuncture can improve hearing, especially when the loss is recent or due to excess patterns like Liver Fire or Phlegm-Heat. It works by opening the ear channels, reducing inflammation, and regulating the flow of Qi and Blood to the inner ear. For chronic, age-related loss, acupuncture may slow further decline and reduce tinnitus, but full restoration is less likely. Most patients notice a reduction in ear fullness and clearer sound perception within a few sessions.

### How long does TCM treatment take for hearing loss?
It depends on the pattern and how long you've had the loss. Acute, sudden hearing loss from Liver Fire can start improving in 2-4 weeks. Gradual loss from Kidney or Spleen deficiency typically requires 3-6 months of consistent treatment. We recommend weekly acupuncture and daily herbs for at least 8-12 weeks before evaluating progress.

### Can I combine TCM with my hearing aids or other treatments?
Absolutely. TCM complements hearing aids and medical treatments. Herbs and acupuncture aim to improve the health of the ear and its supporting organs, which may enhance the benefit you get from hearing aids. If you're taking steroids or other medications, always inform both your TCM practitioner and your doctor to avoid interactions.

### What can I eat to help my hearing?
In general, favour foods that nourish the Kidneys and Spleen: black sesame seeds, walnuts, bone broth, eggs, and dark leafy greens. Avoid greasy, fried, and overly spicy foods that create Phlegm-Heat and dampness, which can muffle hearing. Cold and raw foods can weaken the Spleen, so eat warm, cooked meals. Specific dietary advice will be tailored to your pattern.

### Does TCM work for age-related hearing loss?
While TCM cannot reverse structural damage to the inner ear, it can often slow the progression of age-related hearing loss and improve the clarity of sound by nourishing Kidney Essence and improving circulation. Many older patients report less tinnitus and better tolerance of hearing aids after treatment. The earlier you start, the better the results.

### Is TCM safe for sudden hearing loss?
Sudden sensorineural hearing loss is a medical emergency, and you should see a doctor immediately for evaluation and possible steroid treatment. TCM can be used alongside this conventional care to improve outcomes, but it should not replace urgent medical assessment. Herbs and acupuncture can help reduce inflammation and restore circulation, ideally started within the first few days.
