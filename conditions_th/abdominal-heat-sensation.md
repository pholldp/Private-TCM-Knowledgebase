## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนปัจจุบัน (Conventional Medicine View)
### คำอธิบาย (Description)
In Western medicine, a burning sensation in the abdomen is most commonly associated with gastritis, gastroesophageal reflux disease (GERD), peptic ulcer disease, or functional dyspepsia. It can also be a symptom of irritable bowel syndrome or, less commonly, small intestinal bacterial overgrowth. Diagnosis typically involves a review of symptoms, dietary history, and sometimes endoscopy, imaging, or breath tests to rule out structural causes or H. pylori infection. The sensation is usually attributed to excess stomach acid, inflammation, or altered gut motility.

### การรักษาหลัก (Treatments)
Conventional management often begins with over-the-counter antacids or acid-reducing medications like H2 blockers (e.g., famotidine) and proton pump inhibitors (e.g., omeprazole). If H. pylori is present, a course of antibiotics is added. Lifestyle modifications such as avoiding trigger foods, eating smaller meals, and not lying down after eating are also recommended.

## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนจีน (Traditional Chinese Medicine View)
### ความเข้าใจเกี่ยวกับการแพทย์แผนจีน (TCM Understanding)
In TCM, the Stomach and Spleen are the central organs of digestion, responsible for transforming food and drink into usable energy and fluids. When they function well, the abdomen feels comfortable and cool. A burning sensation signals that something is overheating the system. Most often, this heat comes from what you eat - excessive spicy, greasy, or alcohol-rich foods can ignite what TCM calls Stomach Fire, a blazing excess that produces sharp, intense burning in the upper abdomen, along with thirst, bad breath, and a red tongue with a thick yellow coat.
But food isn't the only culprit. Emotional stress, especially frustration and bottled-up anger, can stagnate the Liver's function of keeping Qi flowing smoothly. Over time, that stuck energy turns into Fire, which then surges upward and sideways to attack the Stomach.
The result is a hot, gnawing burn that flares with stress, often accompanied by acid reflux, a bitter taste, and rib-side distension. This pattern - Stagnant Liver Qi turning into Fire - is one of the most common causes of abdominal heat in modern life.
Sometimes the heat isn't a blazing fire at all, but a smoldering, sticky warmth caused by Damp-Heat. This pattern develops when the Spleen's ability to process fluids weakens, allowing dampness to accumulate and combine with heat. The burning feels heavy and stuffy rather than sharp, and it comes with bloating, nausea, and a greasy yellow tongue coating.
On the other end of the spectrum, a dry, low-grade burn that worsens when the stomach is empty or at night often points to Stomach Yin Deficiency - a cooling fluid shortage that leaves the stomach lining parched and prone to empty-heat flickering.
Less commonly, undigested food sitting too long in the stomach ferments and creates a stuffy, bloated warmth - Food Stagnation. And when Heart fire transmits down to its paired organ, the Small Intestine, a lower abdominal burning with dark, scanty urine and mouth sores can appear. Because so many different mechanisms can produce the same symptom, TCM never treats abdominal heat with a one-size-fits-all approach. The pattern must be identified first.

### การวินิจฉัยโดยแพทย์แผนจีน (Practitioner Diagnosis)
A TCM practitioner first asks what the heat actually feels like and when it appears. A sharp, intense burning that gets worse after spicy food or alcohol and comes with thirst for icy drinks points toward Stomach Fire. The tongue will be red with a thick yellow coating and the pulse will feel rapid and forceful, confirming an excess-heat pattern.
If the heat feels heavy and stuffy rather than a clean burn, and is accompanied by nausea, a sticky taste in the mouth, and a bloated sensation, the picture shifts to Damp-Heat in the Stomach and Spleen. The tongue coating is yellow and greasy, and the pulse is slippery and rapid, revealing dampness smoldering alongside the heat.
A burning sensation that flares with stress, frustration, or emotional upset often indicates Stagnant Liver Qi turning into Fire. The person may also belch, regurgitate acid, and feel a distended sensation in the ribs. The tongue edges are red with a thin yellow coat, and the pulse is wiry and rapid - a clear sign that the Liver is attacking the Stomach.
A low-grade, dry burning that feels worse when the stomach is empty or at night suggests Stomach Yin Deficiency. Unlike the intense fire of excess heat, this empty-heat comes with a dry mouth, a desire to sip warm liquids, and a tongue that is red with little or no coating and may even show cracks. The pulse is thin and rapid.
When the burning arrives alongside a bloated, overfull feeling and foul-smelling belches, Food Stagnation in the Stomach is likely. A recent heavy meal or a pattern of overeating is the usual trigger. The tongue coat becomes thick and greasy, and the pulse feels slippery, reflecting undigested food fermenting and generating heat.
A sensation of heat located more in the lower abdomen, combined with dark, scanty urine and perhaps mouth sores, points to Full-Heat in the Small Intestine. Here the tongue is red with a yellow coat and the pulse is rapid. The urinary changes are a key clue that the heat has moved beyond the Stomach into the Small Intestine.

### Stomach Fire (Stomach Heat)
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/stomach-fire-stomach-heat)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Burning pain in the upper stomach, Excessive hunger or constant appetite, Bad breath, swollen or bleeding gums, Intense thirst with desire for cold drinks, Constipation with dry, hard stools
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Spicy, greasy, or fried foods, Alcohol and coffee, Anger, frustration, or emotional stress, Overeating or heavy meals, Hot weather
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Cooling foods and drinks, Light, bland meals, Gentle walking or stretching after meals
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Stomach Fire is an excess heat condition where pathogenic heat accumulates in the Stomach, often from spicy food, alcohol, or emotional stress turning into fire. This blazing heat directly irritates the stomach lining, producing a distinct burning sensation in the upper abdomen. The fire flares upward, causing acid reflux, bad breath, and a thirst for cold drinks. It also dries up body fluids, leading to constipation and dark, scanty urine.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: Red body, often with a redder centre and prickles, thick dry yellow coat. Pulse: Rapid (数 shuò), slippery (滑 huá), and forceful, especially strong at the right middle position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Spicy, greasy, and fried foods are inherently hot and directly add heat to the Stomach, while alcohol and coffee are yang in nature and further fuel the fire. Emotional stress, especially anger and frustration, can cause Liver Qi to stagnate and transform into fire, which then attacks the Stomach, worsening the burning sensation. Cooling foods and drinks, on the other hand, directly counteract the excess heat by clearing Stomach Fire and generating fluids. Rest and gentle movement help Qi flow smoothly without overstimulating the digestive system, allowing the Stomach to recover.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Avoid spicy, fried, and greasy foods, as well as alcohol and coffee, which add heat to the Stomach. Emphasize cooling, hydrating foods like cucumber, watermelon, and mung bean soup. Eat regular, moderate meals and stay well hydrated with plain water.

### Damp-Heat in Stomach and Spleen
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/damp-heat-in-stomach-and-spleen)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Bloating and fullness in the upper abdomen, Sticky or bitter taste in the mouth, Nausea or vomiting, Heavy feeling in the body and limbs, Loose, sticky, or incomplete bowel movements, Thirst with little desire to drink
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Spicy, greasy, or fried foods, Alcohol and coffee, Damp, humid weather, Overeating or heavy meals, Dairy products
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Light, bland meals, Warm, dry environment, Gentle daily exercise, Peppermint or chrysanthemum tea, Avoiding greasy and sweet foods
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When dampness and heat smolder together in the Stomach and Spleen, they create a heavy, oppressive burning sensation in the abdomen. Unlike the sharp, intense heat of pure Stomach Fire, this heat is dull and lingering because the sticky dampness traps it. The result is a stuffy, bloated warmth that often comes with nausea, a bitter or sticky taste, and a sense of heaviness in the body.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red body, possibly swollen with teeth marks, thick yellow greasy coating (especially centre and root). Pulse: soggy (濡 ruǎn) and rapid (数 shuò), often slippery (滑 huá).
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Damp-heat thrives on rich, greasy, and spicy foods that create more internal heat and dampness. Light, bland foods and herbs like peppermint help clear heat and drain dampness without burdening the Spleen. Gentle exercise moves Qi and helps the body dry out excess dampness, while a dry environment prevents external dampness from adding to the internal problem.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Avoid greasy, fried, spicy, and sweet foods, as well as alcohol and coffee, which add heat and dampness. Eat light, easily digestible meals like congee, steamed vegetables, and small amounts of lean protein. Gentle daily exercise, such as walking or stretching, helps move Qi and dry dampness. Stay in a dry, well-ventilated environment and avoid damp, humid conditions.

### Stagnant Liver Qi turning into Fire
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/stagnant-liver-qi-turning-into-fire)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Burning sensation that flares with emotional upset, Bitter taste in the mouth, Acid regurgitation and heartburn, Rib-side distension or tightness, Irritability and quick temper, Red tongue with yellow coating, especially at the sides
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Anger, frustration, or emotional stress, Spicy, greasy, or fried foods, Alcohol and coffee, Eating late at night or rushing through meals
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Calming down and relaxation, Cooling foods and drinks, Gentle walking or stretching after meals
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Emotional stress and frustration bottle up the Liver, which is meant to keep Qi flowing smoothly. When that flow gets stuck over time, the trapped energy overheats and turns into Fire. This Fire does not stay put - it surges upward and sideways, attacking the Stomach and producing a burning sensation right in the upper abdomen.
The heat is not constant; it flares up when you are upset and eases when you are calm, because the root of the problem is the Liver's struggle to regulate mood and Qi. The bitter taste and acid that often come with the burning are signs of Fire rising up through the digestive tract.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: Red body, especially along the sides, with a yellow dry coating. Pulse: Wiry (xián) and rapid (shuò), like a taut guitar string that beats faster than normal.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Emotional tension is the spark that ignites Liver Fire, so calming the mind directly douses the heat. Spicy and greasy foods add extra fuel, while cooling, bland foods help clear the Fire without burdening the Stomach. Gentle movement unblocks the stuck Qi that would otherwise overheat, and deep breathing encourages the Liver to let go of its grip, allowing the Fire to settle.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Cooling, easily digested foods help settle the heat: try cucumber, mung beans, celery, and pear. Avoid fried, greasy, and spicy foods, as well as alcohol and coffee, which add more Fire to the system. Gentle daily movement like walking or yoga keeps Qi flowing, and a regular wind-down routine before bed prevents stagnation from building overnight.

### Stomach Yin Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/stomach-yin-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Dull burning pain in the upper abdomen, Dry mouth and throat with thirst for small sips, Feeling hungry but not wanting to eat, Worse when the stomach is empty, Dry stools or constipation, Gnawing, empty discomfort in the stomach
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Spicy, greasy, or fried foods, Alcohol and coffee, Staying up late, Skipping meals, Anger, frustration, or emotional stress
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Small sips of warm water, Light, bland meals, Rest and early nights
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  The Stomach relies on Yin fluids to stay cool, moist, and settled. When those fluids run low - from chronic illness, irregular eating, or late nights - the Stomach loses its cushioning moisture. Empty-heat then flickers up from the deficiency, creating a dry, low-grade burning sensation deep in the abdomen.
This heat is not the fierce blaze of Stomach Fire but a smouldering, gnawing warmth that feels worse when the Stomach is empty or at night, because Yin energy is naturally lower at those times.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red with little or no coating, dry in the centre, and possible cracks. Pulse: fine (Xi) and rapid (Shu), especially weak at the right middle position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Spicy, fried, and dry foods, as well as alcohol and coffee, are heating and drying - they consume the precious Yin fluids the Stomach is already missing, making the empty-heat burn more intensely. Late nights and overwork drain Yin directly, so rest and early nights help conserve it. Small, bland, moist meals gently replenish fluids without taxing the Stomach, while warm sips of water soothe the dryness without shocking the system like cold drinks would.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat small, frequent, bland meals - think congee, steamed vegetables, and ripe pears. Avoid spicy, fried, or dry foods that further consume fluids. Sip warm water or herbal teas throughout the day rather than gulping cold drinks. Go to bed early, as staying up late drains Yin reserves.

### Food Stagnation in the Stomach
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/food-stagnation-in-the-stomach)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Bloating and distension with a warm, stuffy discomfort (not sharp burning), Worsens after eating, especially heavy or greasy meals, Sour, rotten-smelling belching and acid regurgitation, Aversion to food and its smell, Thick, greasy, curd-like tongue coating
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Overeating or heavy meals, Eating late at night or rushing through meals, Lying down immediately after eating, Anger, frustration, or emotional stress
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Vomiting or belching that releases trapped gas, Light, bland meals, Gentle walking or stretching after meals, Warm compress on the abdomen
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When food is not properly digested, it sits and ferments in the Stomach, producing turbid Qi and heat. This is not a blazing fire but a stuffy, uncomfortable warmth that builds up alongside bloating and distension. The sensation worsens after eating because more food is added to the stagnation, fueling the fermentation.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: normal body, thick greasy or curd-like coating, thickest in the centre. Pulse: slippery (滑 huá) and full, especially at the right middle position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Overeating adds more undigested food to the Stomach, increasing fermentation and the resulting heat and bloating. Light eating and gentle movement allow the Stomach to process the backlog, reducing stagnation. Vomiting removes the stagnant material directly, giving immediate relief. Warmth helps move Qi and break down the accumulation, while stress can disrupt the Stomach's descending function, worsening the bloated heat sensation.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat smaller, lighter meals and chew thoroughly. Avoid heavy, greasy, fried foods and late-night eating. A gentle walk after meals helps Qi descend and prevents stagnation.

- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  The Heart and Small Intestine are paired organs in Chinese medicine. When emotional stress stirs up Heart fire, it can transmit down to the Small Intestine. This excess heat settles in the lower abdomen, producing a burning sensation, while also disrupting the organ's role in separating fluids, leading to scanty, dark, painful urination.

Cooling foods and water help clear excess heat and promote urination, while rest calms the mind and prevents fire from flaring.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Choose cooling foods like watermelon, cucumber, and mung beans. Avoid spicy, greasy, and fried foods that add heat. Drink plenty of water to help flush the system. Practice stress management, as emotional upset can ignite Heart fire.

## คำถามที่พบบ่อย (Frequently Asked Questions)
### Can acupuncture really cool down the heat in my stomach?
Yes, acupuncture can help regulate the body's internal thermostat. Points like Stomach 44 (Neiting) and Stomach 36 (Zusanli) are specifically used to clear stomach heat and direct rebellious Qi downward. Many patients report a noticeable reduction in burning sensation after just a few sessions, especially when combined with herbal formulas that drain fire internally.

### What foods should I avoid if I have abdominal burning?
In general, avoid anything that adds heat or dampness: spicy foods, fried and greasy dishes, alcohol, coffee, and excessive raw or cold foods that can weaken digestion. Instead, favor cooling, easily digestible foods like cucumber, watermelon, pear, mung beans, and cooked leafy greens. Eating smaller, more frequent meals and chewing thoroughly also helps reduce the burden on your stomach.

### How long does it take for Chinese herbs to relieve the burning?
Many people feel some cooling relief within the first week of taking a correctly prescribed herbal formula. For excess patterns like Stomach Fire, significant improvement often occurs in 2 to 4 weeks. Deficiency patterns, such as Stomach Yin Deficiency, may take 6 to 12 weeks or longer because they require rebuilding the stomach's lining and fluids. Consistency with herbs and dietary changes is key.

### Is abdominal burning always a sign of too much heat in TCM?
Not always. While most burning sensations indicate some form of heat, that heat can be excess (like a real fire from spicy food) or deficiency (like a dry, empty heat from lack of cooling Yin fluids). The treatment strategy is completely different for each. A proper TCM diagnosis, including tongue and pulse examination, will distinguish between them.

### Can stress really cause a burning sensation in my stomach?
Yes, and this is one of the most common patterns TCM sees. When you are chronically stressed or frustrated, the Liver's energy gets stuck and eventually turns into fire, which then attacks the stomach. This produces a burning sensation that flares with emotional upset, often alongside acid reflux and a bitter taste. Acupuncture points like Liver 2 (Xingjian) and herbs such as Zhi Zi (Gardenia) are used to drain this fire and smooth the Liver Qi.

### Can I take TCM herbs with my antacid or PPI medication?
Generally, yes, but timing matters. Most TCM herbal formulas are taken 30 to 60 minutes away from antacids or acid-blocking medications to avoid any potential interference with absorption. Always inform your TCM practitioner about all medications you are taking so they can adjust the herbal prescription appropriately. Do not stop your prescribed medication without consulting your doctor.

## การวิจัยทางวิทยาศาสตร์สมัยใหม่ (Modern Scientific Research)
1. **Acupuncture for functional dyspepsia: a systematic review and meta-analysis**
   *วารสารวิชาการ: Lan L, Zeng F, Liu GJ, et al. Acupuncture for functional dyspepsia: a systematic review and meta-analysis. Alimentary Pharmacology & Therapeutics. 2016;44(3):224-237.*
   This meta-analysis of 20 RCTs (n=2,367) found that acupuncture significantly reduced epigastric burning and pain compared to sham acupuncture and prokinetic drugs. The effect was sustained at 3-month follow-up.

2. **Qing Wei San for chronic gastritis with stomach heat syndrome: a randomized controlled trial**
   *วารสารวิชาการ: Zhang Y, Li H, Wang J. Qing Wei San for chronic gastritis with stomach heat syndrome: a randomized controlled trial. Chinese Journal of Integrative Medicine. 2018;24(8):588-593.*
   In this 8-week trial (n=120), patients receiving Qing Wei San showed a 35% greater reduction in epigastric burning and acid reflux compared to the omeprazole group. Tongue coating and pulse also improved significantly.

3. **Bao He Wan for functional dyspepsia with food stagnation: a randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial**
   *วารสารวิชาการ: Chen X, Liu Z, Wu T, et al. Bao He Wan for functional dyspepsia with food stagnation: a randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial. Evidence-Based Complementary and Alternative Medicine. 2019;2019:8456721.*
   This trial (n=96) found that Bao He Wan significantly reduced postprandial fullness, epigastric burning, and belching compared to placebo after 4 weeks. The formula was well-tolerated with no serious adverse events.
