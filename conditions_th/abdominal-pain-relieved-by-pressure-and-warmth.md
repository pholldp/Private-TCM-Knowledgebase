## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนปัจจุบัน (Conventional Medicine View)
### คำอธิบาย (Description)
In Western medicine, abdominal pain that eases with warmth and gentle pressure is often considered a feature of functional gastrointestinal disorders like irritable bowel syndrome (IBS) or functional dyspepsia. It may also accompany mild gastritis or peptic ulcer disease, where heat relaxes tight muscles and pressure provides counter-stimulation that dampens pain signals. Diagnosis typically relies on symptom history and ruling out structural causes through endoscopy or imaging when red flags are present.

### การรักษาหลัก (Treatments)
Standard care usually includes dietary adjustments, antacids, proton pump inhibitors for acid-related pain, antispasmodics for cramping, and sometimes low-dose antidepressants to modulate gut-brain pain pathways. For IBS, fiber supplements, probiotics, and specific diets like low-FODMAP are common. However, when no structural cause is found, treatment often focuses on symptom management rather than addressing a root cause.

## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนจีน (Traditional Chinese Medicine View)
### ความเข้าใจเกี่ยวกับการแพทย์แผนจีน (TCM Understanding)
TCM sees the abdomen as the home of the Spleen and Stomach, the organs that transform food into Qi and blood. They need a steady supply of Yang-a warm, metabolic fire-to do their job. When that Yang is deficient, cold accumulates in the middle burner, causing Qi to slow down and congeal. This stagnation produces a dull, persistent ache that craves heat to disperse the cold and gentle pressure to support the weakened Qi.
The most common root is Spleen Yang Deficiency. Here, the digestive fire is too weak to process food properly, so pain is often centered around the navel, accompanied by loose stools, fatigue, and cold hands and feet. If the pain sits squarely in the pit of the stomach with a poor appetite and perhaps clear-fluid regurgitation, the Stomach itself is the cold's target-Stomach Yang Deficient and Cold.
Sometimes the cold sinks deeper. When the Small Intestine is affected, you'll notice lower abdominal pain with gurgling and watery diarrhea. When the Kidneys are involved-the body's deepest source of Yang-the pain is accompanied by lower back coldness, early-morning diarrhea, and profound exhaustion. In all cases, the tongue is pale and puffy, and the pulse feels deep and slow, confirming the presence of internal cold.
This is why TCM doesn't treat all abdominal pain the same way. Two people with the same complaint of “pain that feels better with a hot water bottle” may need completely different herbal formulas and acupuncture points, depending on which organ is most depleted. The pattern differentiation guides the treatment, and it's why a thorough intake-covering pain location, bowel habits, temperature preferences, and energy levels-is so essential.

### การวินิจฉัยโดยแพทย์แผนจีน (Practitioner Diagnosis)
A TCM practitioner starts by asking where the pain sits and what it feels like. Pain that improves with warmth and gentle pressure always points to a deficiency‑cold pattern, but the location and the company it keeps are what narrow the diagnosis. Upper belly pain suggests Stomach involvement, while pain around the navel or lower abdomen points toward the Spleen, Small Intestine, or Kidneys.
When the discomfort is a dull ache around the navel, comes with fatigue, loose stools, and a poor appetite, the Spleen’s warming power has weakened. This is Spleen Yang Deficiency, the most common driver of this kind of pain. The tongue is pale and puffy, and the pulse feels deep and weak, confirming that the middle burner lacks the Yang it needs to digest and move things along.
If the pain sits squarely in the pit of the stomach and is accompanied by a complete loss of appetite, cold hands and feet, and perhaps clear‑fluid vomiting, the practitioner thinks of Stomach Yang Deficient and Cold. Here the cold has settled right into the Stomach itself. The tongue is pale with a thin white coat, and the pulse is deep and slow, showing that the Stomach’s warming function is simply too low.
Sometimes the pain is more diffuse and the picture is less clearly tied to one organ. That is Empty‑Cold, a general pattern of internal cold from Yang deficiency. It often overlaps with Spleen Yang Deficiency but may lack the pronounced digestive symptoms, appearing instead as a vague, lingering ache that always improves with heat and rest.
The Greater Yin stage is a classical description of the same deep Spleen‑cold picture, often with more pronounced abdominal fullness and watery, painless diarrhea.
When the ache settles in the lower belly, with gurgling, watery stools, and a sensation of cold below the navel, Small Intestine Deficient and Cold is likely.
If the pain is chronic and comes with lower‑back soreness, cold knees, severe fatigue, and early‑morning diarrhea, the cold has reached the Kidneys as well, creating a Kidney and Spleen Yang Deficiency with Empty Cold pattern. The tongue is pale and wet, and the pulse is deep and thready, reflecting a deeper exhaustion of the body’s core warmth.

## กลุ่มอาการการแพทย์แผนจีนและการรักษา (TCM Patterns and Treatment)

### Spleen Yang Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/spleen-yang-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Dull persistent abdominal pain, better with warmth and pressure, Loose watery stools, often with undigested food, Cold hands and feet, feeling cold easily, Poor appetite and bloating after meals, Tiredness and heaviness in the limbs
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Cold or raw foods and drinks, Skipping meals or irregular eating, Cold weather or dampness, Overwork or overexertion
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Applying warmth to abdomen (hot water bottle, heat pack), Eating warm cooked foods and warm drinks, Rest and lying down, Gentle pressure or massage on abdomen
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Your Spleen’s Yang is like a warm digestive fire that transforms food into energy and keeps Qi moving. When that fire dims, cold settles into the middle of your body, causing the Qi to slow down and stagnate. This stagnation produces a dull, persistent ache.
Warmth and gentle pressure temporarily boost the Yang and get Qi moving again, which is why the pain eases. But because the Spleen can’t properly transform fluids, you may also notice loose, watery stools or undigested food, along with bloating and a poor appetite.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale, puffy, tender body with scalloped teeth marks, white slippery coating. Pulse: deep, slow, and weak, especially at the right guan position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Warmth directly counteracts the internal cold that is causing the Qi to stagnate, while gentle pressure helps move Qi locally. Warm, cooked foods and spices like ginger add heat to the digestive fire, making it easier for the Spleen to transform food and fluids. Rest conserves your Yang energy, while cold, raw foods and overexertion further deplete it. Skipping meals leaves the middle burner empty and vulnerable to cold, which is why regular, warm meals are so effective at keeping the pain at bay.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat mostly warm, cooked foods like soups, stews, and congees. Use gentle warming spices such as ginger, cinnamon, and a pinch of black pepper. Avoid raw salads, iced drinks, and cold dairy products that can snuff out your digestive fire.
Keep your abdomen warm with a scarf or a hot water bottle, especially in cold weather. Gentle movement like walking after meals helps Qi circulate, but rest when you feel tired. Don’t skip meals-regular, small, warm meals are easier on your Spleen.

### Stomach Yang Deficient and Cold
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/stomach-yang-deficient-and-cold)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Dull, aching pain in the upper belly, Pain improves with warmth and pressure, Poor appetite and preference for hot food and drinks, Vomiting of clear, watery fluid, Cold hands and feet
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Cold or raw foods and drinks, Cold weather or dampness, Skipping meals or irregular eating, Overwork or overexertion
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Applying warmth to abdomen (hot water bottle, heat pack), Gentle pressure or massage on abdomen, Eating warm cooked foods and warm drinks, Rest and lying down
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Your Stomach relies on a warm, active digestive fire-what we call Stomach Yang-to break down food and keep things moving downward. When that fire dims, the Stomach becomes cold and sluggish. Food and fluids sit too long, creating a dull, gnawing ache in the upper belly that feels better with warmth and gentle pressure because those temporarily boost the Yang.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale, puffy, tender body with teeth marks; white slippery coating. Pulse: deep, slow, weak, especially at the right Guan (middle) position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Warmth and pressure temporarily invigorate the deficient Stomach Yang, helping to move Qi and relieve the stagnation that causes the dull ache. Cold-whether from food, drink, or environment-further depletes Yang and constricts the channels, making the pain worse. Rest conserves Qi, while overwork drains it, leaving the Stomach even colder and more sluggish.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Make warm, cooked, easily digestible foods your staples-think soups, stews, and congee with ginger or cinnamon. Steer clear of raw salads, icy drinks, and cold dairy. Keep your abdomen cozy with a belly wrap or hot water bottle, especially in cold weather. Gentle movement like walking helps Qi flow, but rest when you feel tired.

### Empty-Cold
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/empty-cold)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Dull abdominal discomfort relieved by warmth and pressure, Persistent feeling of cold, cold hands and feet, Fatigue and listlessness, Loose stools, Pale face, No thirst or preference for warm drinks only
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Cold or raw foods and drinks, Cold weather or dampness, Overwork or overexertion, Skipping meals or irregular eating, Emotional stress or worry
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Applying warmth to abdomen (hot water bottle, heat pack), Gentle pressure or massage on abdomen, Rest and lying down, Eating warm cooked foods and warm drinks, Warm clothing and keeping abdomen covered
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  The body's warming Yang energy is deficient, leaving the middle burner (abdomen) under-heated. Without enough Yang, cold accumulates internally, causing the smooth flow of Qi to slow and congeal. This cold constriction produces a dull, lingering ache that feels better when warmth is applied (to temporarily supply heat) and when gentle pressure is used (to support the deficient Qi).
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale, puffy, tender body with teeth marks and a thin white moist coating. Pulse: deep, slow, and weak, especially at the rear (Chi) positions.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Empty-Cold is a state of deficient warming Yang. Cold foods and environments directly add more Cold to the body, worsening the stagnation and pain. Overwork and hunger further deplete Qi and Yang, leaving the abdomen even more vulnerable. Warmth temporarily supplies the missing Yang, relaxing the constricted channels and moving Qi. Gentle pressure supports the weakened Qi, while rest conserves energy for healing. Warm, cooked foods are easily digested and provide gentle fuel without demanding extra Yang.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat only warm, cooked foods; soups, stews, and congees are ideal. Generously use warming spices like ginger, cinnamon, and fennel. Avoid all raw, cold, or frozen foods and drinks. Keep your abdomen covered and warm, and use a hot water bottle or moxibustion on the belly button daily. Get plenty of rest and avoid overexertion.

  In the Greater Yin stage, Spleen Yang is weak and internal cold accumulates in the middle burner. This cold congeals the flow of Qi, creating a dull, intermittent obstruction that manifests as abdominal pain. Warmth disperses the cold and pressure supports the deficient Qi, which is why both bring relief.
The Spleen's failure to transform fluids leads to dampness, causing watery diarrhea without strong odor, a heavy sensation, and a pale, swollen tongue with a greasy coat.

- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Cold congeals Qi and obstructs the middle burner, so external or dietary cold intensifies the pain. Warmth disperses cold and restores movement, while gentle pressure supports the deficient Spleen Qi. Rest and warm foods nourish Yang and conserve energy, whereas overwork and stress further deplete Spleen Yang, making the pain worse.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat only warm, cooked foods; soups, stews, and congees are ideal. Avoid all raw, cold, and greasy foods, which further damage Spleen Yang. Use warming spices like ginger, cinnamon, and black pepper in cooking. Keep your abdomen warm with a hot water bottle or abdominal wrap, and avoid sitting on cold surfaces. Gentle movement like walking helps Qi flow without overtaxing the Spleen.

### Small Intestine Deficient and Cold
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/small-intestine-deficient-and-cold)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Dull lower abdominal pain relieved by warmth and pressure, Loud gurgling intestinal sounds (borborygmus), Watery loose stools, Frequent clear urination, Cold limbs and aversion to cold, Pale puffy tongue with teeth marks
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Cold weather or dampness, Cold or raw foods and drinks, Overwork or overexertion, Emotional stress or worry
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Applying warmth to abdomen (hot water bottle, heat pack), Gentle pressure or massage on abdomen, Eating warm cooked foods and warm drinks, Rest and lying down, Moxibustion
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When the Small Intestine lacks the warm Yang Qi it needs to do its job, it cannot properly separate the clear fluids from the turbid. Cold and dampness then settle in the lower abdomen, causing a dull, lingering ache that feels better with warmth and gentle pressure. The fluids that should be absorbed instead rush downward, creating loud gurgling sounds, watery stools, and frequent pale urination.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale, puffy body with teeth marks and a thin, white, moist coating. Pulse: deep, slow, and weak; especially feeble at the right Guan (Spleen-Stomach) position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: This pattern is driven by Yang deficiency and internal Cold, so anything that adds more cold - like icy foods or a chilly environment - makes the pain worse by further congealing Qi and fluids. Warmth and gentle pressure do the opposite: they support the body's remaining Yang, melt the internal Cold, and help the Small Intestine resume its normal work of separating and absorbing fluids. Rest and moxibustion conserve and rebuild the Yang Qi that has been depleted.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat only warm, cooked foods and avoid anything raw, icy, or straight from the fridge. Ginger tea, congee with a pinch of cinnamon, and soups made with slow-cooked root vegetables are especially helpful. Keep a hot water bottle or a warm scarf over your lower belly, and try to rest when the pain comes. Gentle walking and abdominal self-massage with a warm hand can also keep Qi moving.

- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When both Kidney and Spleen Yang are deficient, the body's vital fire is too weak to warm the digestive organs. Cold accumulates in the abdomen, causing a dull, persistent ache that is soothed by warmth because heat disperses cold and by gentle pressure because pressure comforts the depleted Qi. The Kidney Yang deficiency also causes a deep cold sensation in the lower back and knees, and the weakness of the digestive fire leads to early-morning diarrhea and fatigue.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale, swollen, tooth-marked body with a white slippery coating. Pulse: deep, slow, weak, and fine; both Chi positions especially weak, right Guan soft.

Overwork and stress deplete Qi and Yang, leaving the digestive organs even more vulnerable. Early morning is when the body's Yang is at its lowest ebb, explaining why pain and diarrhea often strike at that time. Warmth and gentle pressure help disperse cold and comfort the deficiency, while rest and warm foods support the slow process of rebuilding Yang.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat only warm, cooked foods and drink warm liquids. Avoid raw, cold, and frozen items. Use a hot water bottle or warm compress on the abdomen and lower back. Wear a belly wrap or warm clothing to protect the middle from drafts. Gentle exercise like Tai Chi or walking can help move Qi, but rest when fatigued.

## คำถามที่พบบ่อย (Frequently Asked Questions)
### Why does my stomach pain feel better when I press on it?
In TCM, that’s a classic sign of deficiency cold. Gentle pressure provides external support to the weakened Qi in your abdomen, like a warm hand steadying a flickering flame. The cold makes the Qi sluggish and constricted, and the pressure helps it move again, temporarily easing the ache.

### Can TCM cure this type of abdominal pain permanently?
Many people achieve long-term relief, especially when the underlying Yang deficiency is fully rebuilt. However, because the tendency toward cold can be constitutional, staying symptom-free often requires maintaining a warm diet and avoiding excessive raw or cold foods. Think of it as managing a predisposition rather than a one-time fix.

### How long will it take to see results?
Most patients notice less frequent or less intense pain within the first two to four weeks of herbs and moxibustion. For recent-onset, milder cases, full remission may come in four to six weeks. Chronic, deep-seated cold-especially when the Kidneys are involved-can take three to six months of steady treatment to fully warm the core.

### Can I combine TCM with my current medications?
Generally yes, but always tell both your doctor and your TCM practitioner about everything you're taking. Warming herbs like dried ginger and cinnamon are usually safe, but if you're on blood thinners, high doses of certain herbs may increase bleeding risk. Never stop prescribed medications abruptly; work with your prescribing doctor as you improve.

### What foods should I avoid?
Anything that cools the body: raw salads, iced drinks, cold smoothies, frozen desserts, and foods straight from the fridge. Also limit dairy, greasy foods, and sweets, which create dampness and further weaken the Spleen. The simplest rule: if it feels cold in your mouth, it will feel cold in your belly.

### Is this condition dangerous?
On its own, deficiency-cold abdominal pain isn't life-threatening, but it can significantly affect your quality of life. However, if the pain becomes severe, is accompanied by fever, vomiting blood, black stools, or unexplained weight loss, seek urgent medical care-these are not typical of a simple cold pattern and need immediate investigation.

### Can children get this type of pain?
Yes, children can develop deficiency-cold patterns, especially if they eat a lot of cold or raw foods or have a constitution that tends toward cold. Their Spleen and Stomach are still maturing, so they are more susceptible. Pediatric doses of warming herbs and gentle moxibustion (or even just warm compresses) are often very effective.
