## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนปัจจุบัน (Conventional Medicine View)
### คำอธิบาย (Description)
Functional amenorrhea is the absence of menstruation for three months or more in a woman who previously had regular cycles, after pregnancy and anatomical causes have been ruled out. It typically results from a disruption in the hypothalamic-pituitary-ovarian axis - the hormonal chain of command that tells the ovaries to ovulate and the uterus to shed its lining.
Common triggers include significant emotional stress, rapid weight loss, excessive exercise, or stopping hormonal contraceptives. Diagnosis is made through a careful history, blood tests (FSH, LH, estradiol, prolactin, TSH), and sometimes a progesterone challenge to see whether the uterine lining is capable of bleeding. The condition is considered reversible once the underlying trigger is removed, but conventional treatment often focuses on hormonal replacement rather than addressing the root cause.

### การรักษาหลัก (Treatments)
Conventional management depends on the suspected cause. If weight loss or excessive exercise is the trigger, restoring a healthy body weight and reducing training intensity is recommended. Hormonal therapy - such as oral contraceptive pills or cyclic progestins - is often prescribed to induce artificial withdrawal bleeds and protect bone density. For women trying to conceive, ovulation induction with medications like clomiphene or letrozole may be used. Cognitive behavioral therapy or stress management may be suggested if stress is a major factor. However, these approaches often do not restore a natural, ovulatory menstrual cycle.

## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนจีน (Traditional Chinese Medicine View)
### ความเข้าใจเกี่ยวกับการแพทย์แผนจีน (TCM Understanding)
TCM understands menstruation as a monthly expression of the body's overall vitality. For a period to come, three things are needed: enough Qi and Blood to fill the uterus, a free-flowing pathway through the Chong and Ren channels (the main meridians that connect the uterus to the rest of the body), and a strong Kidney essence to drive the process. When any of these three elements is compromised, the period can stop.
The Liver is often the first organ involved in functional amenorrhea. It's responsible for the smooth flow of Qi and Blood throughout the body, and it's especially sensitive to emotional stress. When you're under prolonged pressure, frustrated, or anxious, Liver Qi can stagnate. This stagnation acts like a traffic jam in the Chong and Ren channels - the blood can't get to the uterus, so the period doesn't come. This is why so many women lose their cycles during times of intense stress.
The Spleen and the Kidneys play equally important roles. The Spleen transforms food into Qi and Blood; if it's weakened by poor diet, overthinking, or irregular eating, the body can't produce enough blood to sustain a period. The Kidneys store the essence that is the foundation of menstrual blood, and they govern the hormonal axis from a TCM perspective. Depletion from overwork, inadequate sleep, or constitutional weakness can leave the uterus without the drive to bleed.
In some cases, the problem isn't deficiency but blockage. When the Spleen fails to manage fluids properly, Dampness and Phlegm can accumulate in the body, physically obstructing the uterus. This pattern is common in women with polycystic ovary syndrome (PCOS) or weight gain. In others, long-standing Qi stagnation can congeal into Blood stasis, creating a more stubborn physical obstruction. Each of these patterns - Liver Qi Stagnation, Qi and Blood Stagnation, Damp-Phlegm, Qi and Blood Deficiency, and Kidney Qi Deficiency - requires a different treatment strategy, which is why TCM always starts by differentiating the pattern, not just the symptom.

### การวินิจฉัยโดยแพทย์แผนจีน (Practitioner Diagnosis)
A TCM practitioner begins by listening to your story - when your periods stopped, what was happening in your life at that time, and how you feel day to day. The emotional landscape, diet, energy levels, and any physical discomfort all offer clues that point toward one pattern rather than another.
If your cycle vanished during a period of intense stress, frustration, or emotional upheaval, Liver Qi Stagnation (肝气郁结, gān qì yù jié) is a prime suspect. You may notice breast distension, mood swings, and premenstrual tension. The tongue often looks normal or has slightly red edges, and the pulse feels wiry - signs that emotional Qi is knotted up and blocking the menstrual flow.
When the stagnation deepens, it becomes Qi and Blood Stagnation (气滞血瘀, qì zhì xuè yū). Here the pain is sharper and more fixed, often with dark clots when bleeding does occur. The tongue turns purplish with dark spots, and the pulse remains wiry but may also feel choppy. This pattern tells the practitioner that not just energy but the blood itself is stuck.
In women who carry extra weight or struggle with metabolic issues, Damp-Phlegm in the Uterus (痰湿阻滞, tán shī zǔ zhì) is often the answer. A heavy sensation in the body, a greasy coating on the tongue, and a slippery pulse all point to phlegm-dampness physically obstructing the uterus. Cravings for rich or sweet foods and a foggy-headed feeling reinforce this picture.
If instead the period faded away gradually alongside fatigue, pale skin, dizziness, and a poor appetite, Qi and Blood Deficiency (气血两虚, qì xuè liǎng xū) is more likely. The tongue is pale with a thin coating, and the pulse is deep, thready, and weak. The body simply lacks the raw materials to build a menstrual flow.
Kidney Qi Deficiency (肾气亏虚, shèn qì kuī xū) often shows up in women with delayed puberty, a weak lower back, or a history of prolonged overwork and constitutional frailty. The tongue may be pale or slightly red with a thin coat, and the pulse is weak and deep. This pattern reflects a deeper depletion of the body’s foundational reserves.

### Liver Qi Stagnation
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/liver-qi-stagnation)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Periods stop after emotional stress, Breast tenderness or swelling, Irritability and mood swings, Distension or bloating along the ribs, Frequent sighing
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Emotional stress or upset, Alcohol and greasy foods, Sedentary lifestyle
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Adequate rest and stress reduction, Gentle exercise or stretching, Peppermint or chrysanthemum tea, Sour foods like lemon or vinegar
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When stress, frustration, or long-held resentment build up, the Liver's ability to keep Qi moving smoothly falters. This stagnation blocks the Chong and Ren channels-the main pathways that deliver Qi and Blood to the uterus. Without that free flow, the uterus isn't properly filled, and periods stop.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: normal or slightly dark body with red or slightly purple sides, thin white coat. Pulse: wiry (弦 xián), taut like a guitar string.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Liver Qi Stagnation arises from constrained emotions, particularly stress and frustration. These triggers cause Qi to knot up, blocking the channels that supply the uterus. Relaxation and gentle movement unknot the Qi, while sour and cooling foods help soothe the Liver and prevent it from overheating. Avoiding alcohol and heavy foods reduces the burden on the Liver, allowing it to resume its smooth flow.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Focus on stress reduction: gentle yoga, walking, or meditation can help move stagnant Qi. Limit alcohol and caffeine, which can heat the Liver and worsen irritability. Include dark leafy greens, peppermint tea, and small amounts of sour foods like lemon to soothe the Liver.

### Qi And Blood Stagnation
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/qi-and-blood-stagnation)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Absent periods for months, Stabbing, fixed lower abdominal pain, Dark, clotted menstrual blood when periods do occur, Breast distension and tenderness, Irritability and mood swings
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Emotional stress or upset, Exposure to cold environments, Cold, raw foods and drinks, Sedentary lifestyle, Overwork and exhaustion
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Gentle exercise or stretching, Warmth on the abdomen, Adequate rest and stress reduction, Warm, nourishing meals, Avoiding cold drinks
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When emotional stress or frustration drags on, Liver Qi stagnates and can no longer push blood smoothly. Over time, that stuck Qi causes blood to congeal into actual stasis, which physically blocks the Chong and Ren channels-the main vessels that carry blood to the uterus. With the pathway obstructed, menstrual blood cannot descend, and periods stop. The result is amenorrhea that comes with sharp, fixed pain and dark clots whenever bleeding does break through.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: dark purple with stasis spots, distended sublingual veins. Pulse: wiry (弦 xián) and choppy (涩 sè).
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Qi and Blood Stagnation is driven by emotional tension and cold. Stress and anger tighten the Liver, causing Qi to jam, while cold congeals blood like ice freezes water. Gentle movement and warmth relax the Liver, open the vessels, and encourage blood to flow again, dissolving stasis and restoring menstruation. Cold, raw foods and inactivity do the opposite, worsening the blockage.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat warm, cooked foods like soups and stews; avoid icy drinks and raw salads that congeal blood. Gentle daily movement-walking, yoga, tai chi-keeps Qi flowing. Manage stress with meditation or deep breathing. A warm compress on the lower abdomen can ease pain and encourage circulation.

### Damp-Phlegm in the Uterus
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/damp-phlegm-in-the-uterus)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Overweight or obesity, Copious thick white vaginal discharge, Heaviness and fullness in the lower abdomen, Poor appetite and loose stools, Fatigue and drowsiness
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Greasy, sweet, or dairy foods, Cold, raw foods and drinks, Damp or humid weather, Sedentary lifestyle
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warm, nourishing meals, Gentle exercise or stretching, Warmth on the abdomen, Dry, well-ventilated environment
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When the Spleen is weak, it cannot transform fluids properly. These fluids accumulate into Dampness and thick Phlegm, which settle in the uterus and physically block the Chong and Ren channels. This obstruction prevents menstrual blood from flowing down, causing periods to stop. The pattern is often seen in women who are overweight, feel heavy, and have a thick white vaginal discharge.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale, swollen body with teeth marks, white greasy coating thicker at the root. Pulse: slippery (滑 huá) and deep, often soft at the right guan position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Dampness and Phlegm are sticky, heavy substances that obstruct the uterus. Greasy, sweet, dairy, and cold raw foods burden the Spleen and directly generate more Dampness, while a sedentary lifestyle causes Qi stagnation that prevents fluid transformation. Warmth and moderate movement invigorate Yang Qi, helping the Spleen transform and transport fluids, drying internal Dampness. Light, warm meals support Spleen function without adding to the Phlegm load.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Avoid heavy, greasy, sweet, and dairy foods, which create Dampness and Phlegm. Eat warm, cooked meals like soups, congees, and lightly steamed vegetables with ginger. Gentle daily exercise such as walking or yoga helps move Qi and dry internal Dampness.

### Qi and Blood Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/qi-and-blood-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Pale, scanty menstrual flow that gradually stops, Persistent fatigue and weakness, worse with exertion, Dizziness or lightheadedness, Heart palpitations, Pale complexion, lips, and nail beds, Poor appetite
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Overwork and exhaustion, Excessive exercise, Cold, raw foods and drinks, Emotional stress or upset, Blood loss (heavy menses, injury)
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warm, nourishing meals, Adequate rest and stress reduction, Gentle exercise or stretching, Acupuncture and moxibustion
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When Qi and Blood are deficient, the body lacks the raw materials and the driving force to produce menstrual blood. The uterus is not adequately filled, so periods become scanty and eventually stop. This often develops after long-term poor nutrition, chronic illness, overwork, or heavy blood loss that has not been properly replenished.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale body, may be thin or have teeth marks, with a thin white coating. Pulse: fine (thin) and weak, often empty or moderate.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Qi and Blood are the foundation of menstrual health; they are produced from food and rest. Overwork, poor diet, and stress deplete these resources, starving the uterus of the material needed for a period. Warm, nourishing foods and adequate rest rebuild Qi and Blood, while gentle movement encourages circulation without further depletion. Acupuncture and moxibustion directly stimulate the body's ability to generate and move Qi and Blood to the uterus.

### Kidney Qi Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/kidney-qi-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Lower back and knee soreness, Frequent urination, especially at night, Deep fatigue and lack of stamina, Dizziness and tinnitus, Shortness of breath on exertion
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Overwork and exhaustion, Cold, raw foods and drinks, Exposure to cold environments, Prolonged standing
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Adequate rest and stress reduction, Warm, nourishing meals, Gentle exercise or stretching, Warmth on the lower back
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  The Kidney stores essence and is the root of menstrual blood. When Kidney Qi is deficient, the body lacks the energy to transform essence into blood and to fill the uterus. The Chong and Ren channels, which govern menstruation, become weak and fail to initiate the period. This often happens after prolonged stress, overwork, or constitutional weakness, leading to amenorrhea accompanied by deep fatigue and lower back soreness.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale, slightly puffy body with possible tooth marks and a thin white coating. Pulse: deep, weak, and fine, especially weak at the chi (Kidney) positions.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Overwork and cold drain Kidney Qi, which is already deficient. Rest and warmth conserve and restore this vital energy. Nourishing foods provide the building blocks for essence and blood, while gentle movement encourages Qi flow without depleting reserves.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Prioritize rest and avoid overwork. Eat warm, nourishing foods like bone broths, black beans, walnuts, and goji berries. Avoid cold, raw foods and icy drinks. Gentle exercise such as walking or yoga can help, but avoid exhausting workouts.

## คำถามที่พบบ่อย (Frequently Asked Questions)
### How does TCM treat missed periods?
TCM uses acupuncture, herbal medicine, dietary therapy, and lifestyle guidance to address the underlying pattern causing amenorrhea. For stagnation patterns, treatment focuses on moving Qi and Blood; for deficiency, it nourishes and strengthens. The goal is to restore the body's natural rhythm, not just induce a bleed.

### Will acupuncture bring my period back?
Acupuncture helps regulate the hypothalamic-pituitary-ovarian axis and improve blood flow to the uterus. When combined with herbs and lifestyle changes, it can help restore ovulation and menstruation. Many women notice subtle signs of hormonal activity first - like increased cervical mucus or basal body temperature shifts - before their period returns.

### Can I use TCM alongside birth control pills or hormone therapy?
It's possible, but it's important to discuss with your TCM practitioner. Because TCM aims to restore your natural cycle, using hormonal contraceptives at the same time can mask progress and make it difficult to assess whether your body is ovulating on its own. If you're taking hormones for bone protection, your practitioner will work with your doctor to monitor your response.

### How long does it take to see results with Chinese herbs?
The timeline varies by pattern. Stagnation-related amenorrhea often responds within 2-3 months, while deficiency patterns may require 4-6 months or more of consistent treatment. The first signs of progress are often premenstrual symptoms or changes in cervical fluid, not an immediate period.

### Are there any side effects from Chinese herbs for amenorrhea?
When prescribed by a qualified practitioner based on your specific pattern, Chinese herbal formulas are generally safe and well-tolerated. Some women may experience mild digestive changes or temporary spotting as the body adjusts. Always inform your practitioner of any medications you're taking to avoid interactions.

### Do I need to change my diet to get my period back?
Diet is a cornerstone of TCM treatment for amenorrhea. In general, you'll be advised to avoid cold, raw foods and icy drinks, which can constrict blood flow and weaken digestion. Instead, focus on warm, cooked meals, nourishing soups, and blood-building foods like dark leafy greens, beets, and moderate amounts of red meat. Your practitioner may give more specific guidance based on your pattern.
