## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนปัจจุบัน (Conventional Medicine View)
### คำอธิบาย (Description)
Retinitis is inflammation of the retina, the light-sensitive tissue at the back of the eye. It can be caused by infections (such as cytomegalovirus or toxoplasmosis), autoimmune conditions, or sometimes occurs without a clear trigger. Typical symptoms include blurred or distorted vision, floaters, light sensitivity, and in some cases, a gradual or sudden loss of central or peripheral vision.
Diagnosis usually involves a dilated eye exam, optical coherence tomography (OCT) to visualize retinal layers, and sometimes fluorescein angiography to assess blood flow and leakage. The condition can be acute or chronic, and in some forms, it may recur or lead to permanent scarring if not controlled.

### การรักษาหลัก (Treatments)
Standard treatment depends on the cause. Infectious retinitis is managed with antiviral, antibiotic, or antiparasitic medications. Non-infectious or autoimmune-related retinitis is often treated with corticosteroids (oral, injected, or implant) to reduce inflammation, sometimes alongside immunosuppressant drugs. Laser therapy or anti-VEGF injections may be used if there are complications like abnormal blood vessel growth or macular edema.

## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนจีน (Traditional Chinese Medicine View)
### ความเข้าใจเกี่ยวกับการแพทย์แผนจีน (TCM Understanding)
In TCM, the eyes are intimately connected to the internal organs through a network of channels. The Liver opens into the eyes, and its blood nourishes them for clear vision.
The Kidney stores the essence that fuels the entire visual system. The Spleen transforms food into the Qi and Blood that rise to the head. When these organs fall out of balance, the retina can become inflamed - not because of a single pathogen, but because the internal environment has become a breeding ground for either excess (Heat, Dampness, Phlegm, Stagnation) or deficiency (Yin, Blood, Qi).
The specific quality of vision loss tells the practitioner which organ is struggling. A dry, burning eye with worsening night vision suggests Yin Deficiency and empty Heat rising. A dull, distended ache with a sense of pressure points to Liver Blood Stagnation. Heavy, foggy vision with a sensation of swelling reflects Dampness or Phlegm clouding the clear orifices.
And a slow, progressive dimming accompanied by overall fatigue indicates the eye is simply being starved of Qi and Blood. This is why one Western diagnosis of retinitis can have multiple TCM patterns - the inflammation is a final common pathway, but the road that got there is different for each person.
Importantly, TCM doesn't see the eye in isolation. A pattern like Damp-Heat in the Liver Channel will often come with a bitter taste in the mouth, irritability, and a greasy yellow tongue coating. Qi and Blood Deficiency will show up as a pale tongue, weak pulse, and chronic tiredness.
By treating the whole person, TCM aims not just to clear the current inflammation, but to restore the internal balance so the eye can heal itself and resist future flares.

### การวินิจฉัยโดยแพทย์แผนจีน (Practitioner Diagnosis)
When someone comes in with retinal inflammation, a TCM practitioner first asks what the vision disturbance actually feels like and when it began. The quality of the blurring, any pain or dryness, and what makes it worse help to separate the root patterns. They also look at the tongue body and coating, feel the pulse, and ask about energy, digestion, and emotions to build a full picture of the internal imbalance driving the eye symptoms.
If the eyes are red, vision is blurred, and night vision is especially poor, with a tongue that is red with little coating and a pulse that feels thin and rapid, the practitioner suspects Kidney and Liver Yin Deficiency causing Heat in the Blood. This pattern often comes from long-term overwork or constitutional weakness that dries up the nourishing Yin, allowing empty heat to flare upward and disturb the retina.
When the person describes a dull, heavy ache around the eyes and their vision seems dim or shadowy, especially after emotional stress, and the tongue looks dark purple with possible stasis spots, the picture points to Liver Blood Stagnation. The pulse will feel wiry or choppy, reflecting the stuck Qi and blood in the eye collaterals that needs to be moved.
A very different presentation, with pronounced retinal swelling, fluid leakage, and a sensation of heat and congestion in the eye, together with a thick, greasy yellow tongue coating and a slippery, rapid pulse, indicates Damp-Heat in the Liver Channel. This pattern is often linked to rich or spicy food, alcohol, or a damp constitution, and the heat and dampness rise along the Liver meridian to the eyes.
In a person who looks pale and feels generally weak, with gradual, progressive vision loss and dry, uncomfortable eyes, the practitioner will look for a pale tongue with a thin coating and a fine, weak pulse. This is Qi and Blood Deficiency, where the eyes simply aren’t receiving enough nourishment, often seen in chronic or recurring retinitis.
When the vision is cloudy as if looking through a film, and the tongue has a thick, greasy coating with a slippery pulse, the problem is Damp-Phlegm obstructing the collaterals. This pattern usually develops from long-standing Spleen weakness that fails to transform fluids, leading to turbid phlegm that clouds the retina and slows recovery.

### Kidney and Liver Yin Deficiency causing Heat in the Blood
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/kidney-and-liver-yin-deficiency-causing-heat-in-the-blood)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Blurred vision that worsens at night, Dry, red eyes, Floaters or dark spots in vision, Night sweats, Tidal heat sensation in the afternoon or evening, Low back and knee soreness
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Emotional stress and frustration, Spicy, fried, or greasy food, Overwork and late nights, Hot, dry weather
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Cool, dark environment, Adequate sleep and rest, Yin-nourishing foods (pear, sesame), Gentle eye rest
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  The eyes are the opening of the Liver, and the Kidney supplies the essence that nourishes them. When Liver and Kidney Yin run low, the cooling, moistening fuel for the eyes dries up. This emptiness lets a restless, deficient heat rise upward, irritating the retina and causing blurred vision, night blindness, and a dry, red sensation - much like a lamp burning without oil. As the heat pushes into the blood, tiny vessels in the eye can become fragile and leak, leading to floaters or dark spots.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red, thin, and dry with little or no coating; redder at the sides; possible cracks. Pulse: fine (thin) and rapid; may feel wiry at the left Guan position and weak at the left Chi.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: This pattern is driven by a deep Yin deficit that lets empty heat rise and disturb the eyes. Rest, cooling foods, and a calm environment preserve Yin and subdue fire, while stress, overwork, and heating foods further consume Yin and fan the flames, worsening eye symptoms.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat cooling, Yin-nourishing foods like pear, black sesame, and goji berries. Avoid spicy, fried, or greasy foods that generate heat. Rest your eyes frequently, especially from screens, and try to sleep before 11pm to preserve Yin. Gentle stress management like meditation can help keep Liver Qi smooth.

### Liver Blood Stagnation
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/liver-blood-stagnation)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Eye distension and dull pain, Dull or darkened vision, Worse with emotional stress, Dark purple tongue with stasis spots, Dark menstrual blood with clots (in women)
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Emotional stress and frustration, Spicy, fried, or greasy food, Alcohol, Sedentary lifestyle
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Quiet time away from stress, Gentle exercise like walking or tai chi, Warm compress on eyes
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Emotional stress, frustration, or unresolved anger can cause the Liver Qi to stagnate. Over time, this stagnant Qi fails to move Blood, leading to Blood stasis that obstructs the tiny collaterals feeding the retina. The result is a dull, darkened vision and a sensation of pressure or distension in the eyes, which often worsens under stress.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: dark purple body with stasis spots, especially on the sides; sublingual veins distended. Pulse: wiry (弦 xián) and choppy (涩 sè), like a knife scraping bamboo.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Emotional stress and frustration directly stagnate Liver Qi, which is the root of Blood stasis in this pattern. Spicy, greasy foods and alcohol generate heat and dampness that further obstruct Qi flow. Gentle movement and relaxation smooth the Liver and promote the free flow of Qi and Blood, helping to clear the stasis that clouds vision.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Avoid alcohol, spicy, and greasy foods that can aggravate Liver stagnation. Incorporate stress-reducing practices like meditation, deep breathing, or gentle movement such as tai chi to keep Liver Qi flowing smoothly.

### Damp-Heat in the Liver Channel
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/damp-heat-in-the-liver-channel)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Eye redness and swelling, Blurred vision with possible floaters, Bitter taste in the mouth, Feeling of heaviness in the body, Pain or fullness along the ribs
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Spicy, fried, or greasy food, Alcohol, Damp or humid weather, Emotional stress and anger
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Cooling foods and drinks, Rest and reduced eye strain, Dry, airy environment
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  The Liver channel opens into the eyes. When damp-heat accumulates in this channel - often from a rich, greasy diet or alcohol - it rises and congests the delicate tissues of the retina. This leads to swelling, exudation, and a heavy, blurred sensation in the eyes.
Unlike purely dry heat, the dampness makes the inflammation sticky and lingering, often producing a thick yellow tongue coating and a feeling of bodily heaviness alongside the vision changes.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red body with redder sides, yellow greasy coating thicker at the center and root. Pulse: wiry, slippery, and rapid.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Damp-heat accumulates from rich, greasy foods and alcohol, which directly feed the pathogenic factors. Emotional stress stirs Liver Qi, worsening heat and stagnation. Cooling foods and rest help clear heat and drain dampness, reducing the inflammatory load on the eyes.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Avoid greasy, spicy, and fried foods, as well as alcohol and dairy - all of which generate dampness and heat. Eat more cooling, light foods like mung beans, cucumber, celery, and leafy greens. Keep a regular routine and avoid prolonged eye strain.

### Qi and Blood Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/qi-and-blood-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Gradual, progressive blurring of vision, Dry, tired eyes, Pale or sallow complexion, Persistent fatigue and weakness, Dizziness or lightheadedness, Poor appetite, Heart palpitations
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Overwork and exhaustion, Cold, raw, or processed foods, Chronic stress and worry, Prolonged screen time without breaks
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Adequate sleep and rest, Warm, light, cooked meals, Gentle exercise like walking or tai chi, Nourishing herbal tonics
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  The eyes rely on a steady supply of Qi and Blood to see clearly. When the body becomes depleted-often after a long illness, poor diet, or chronic overwork-the Spleen can no longer produce enough Qi and Blood. The eyes are then starved of nourishment, leading to a slow, progressive decline in vision. This is why retinitis in this pattern feels like a gradual fading or blurring, with dry eyes and floaters, rather than a sudden, fiery flare-up.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale body, may be thin or show teeth marks, with a thin white coating. Pulse: fine (thin) and weak, especially in the right Guan position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Qi and Blood are the body’s fundamental nourishment. Overwork and stress burn through them, while poor diet fails to replenish them, leaving the eyes undernourished. Rest, warm foods, and gentle movement support the Spleen-the organ that turns food into Qi and Blood-allowing the body to slowly rebuild its reserves and send vital energy and substance up to the eyes.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat warm, cooked, nourishing foods such as bone broths, congee, dark leafy greens, eggs, and small amounts of red meat. Avoid raw, cold, and greasy foods that weaken the Spleen. Prioritize deep rest and avoid overwork or late nights, as Qi and Blood are replenished during sleep. Gentle daily movement like walking or tai chi helps circulation without depleting reserves.

### Damp-Phlegm
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/damp-phlegm)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Blurred or cloudy vision, like looking through fog, Heavy, wrapped sensation in the head, Chest and upper abdominal stuffiness or fullness, Poor appetite and loose, sticky stools, Thick, white, greasy tongue coating
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Damp, humid weather, Greasy, fried, or dairy-rich foods, Sedentary lifestyle, Cold, raw, or processed foods
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warm, light, cooked meals, Gentle exercise like walking or tai chi, Dry, airy environment
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When the Spleen is weak, it fails to transform and transport fluids properly. These fluids stagnate and congeal into a heavy, turbid substance called Damp-Phlegm (痰湿, tán shī). This phlegm-dampness can rise and cloud the clear orifices, settling in the delicate collaterals of the eyes and obscuring vision - rather like a foggy mist that makes everything look cloudy or distorted.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale and swollen with tooth marks, wet surface, and a thick, white, greasy coat that looks oily or pasty. Pulse: slippery (滑, huá), like beads rolling under the fingers; may also feel soft or soggy.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Damp-Phlegm is heavy, sticky, and obstructive by nature. It thrives on cold, raw, and greasy inputs that overwhelm the Spleen's digestive fire, and it stagnates further when the body is inactive. Warm, dry foods and regular movement do the opposite - they gently fan the Spleen's transforming power and keep Qi and fluids moving, which helps clear the fog from the eyes.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Keep meals warm, light, and cooked - think soups, congees, and steamed vegetables. Avoid cold, raw foods, dairy, and greasy or fried dishes, which all encourage Dampness. Gentle daily movement like walking or stretching helps circulate Qi and dry out internal dampness.

## คำถามที่พบบ่อย (Frequently Asked Questions)
### Can I use TCM alongside my current eye drops or oral medications?
Yes, in most cases TCM can be safely combined with conventional treatments for retinitis. Herbal medicine and acupuncture work on a different level - they aim to correct the underlying imbalance rather than directly suppress inflammation. Many patients start TCM while still using prescribed eye drops or medications. However, some herbs that move blood or clear heat may interact with anticoagulants or immunosuppressants. Always bring a complete list of your medications to your TCM consultation, and keep both your ophthalmologist and TCM practitioner informed about everything you are taking.

### How long will it take to see improvement in my vision?
Most patients notice some improvement within 4 to 8 weeks of consistent treatment, though this varies by pattern. Excess patterns like Damp-Heat often respond more quickly - sometimes in as little as 3 to 4 weeks. Deficiency patterns, where the body's reserves need to be rebuilt, may take 3 months or more before vision stabilizes and begins to clear. It's important to understand that TCM treats the root cause, not just the symptom. Even when vision starts to improve, continuing treatment for the full recommended course helps prevent recurrence.

### Is acupuncture around the eyes safe?
Yes, when performed by a licensed and experienced TCM practitioner, acupuncture around the eyes is very safe. The needles used are extremely fine - much thinner than a hypodermic needle - and are inserted gently. Common points like Jingming (BL-1) and Qiuhou (EX-HN-7) are located near the orbit but do not touch the eyeball itself. Patients are asked to close their eyes and relax during treatment. You may feel a mild sensation of pressure or heaviness, but it should not be painful. Any minor bruising that occurs is typically small and resolves quickly.

### Will I need to change my diet?
Diet plays a supportive role in TCM treatment for retinitis. In general, you'll be advised to avoid foods that create Dampness and Heat - such as greasy, fried, or heavily spiced foods, alcohol, and excessive dairy. These can worsen patterns like Damp-Heat or Damp-Phlegm. Instead, the focus is on easy-to-digest, nourishing foods. Dark leafy greens, goji berries, black sesame seeds, and cooked whole grains are often recommended to support the Liver and Kidney. Your practitioner will give you specific guidance based on your pattern.

### Does TCM treatment prevent retinitis from coming back?
TCM aims to do exactly that. By correcting the underlying imbalance - whether it's a deficiency of Yin, a stagnation of Blood, or an accumulation of Dampness - the goal is to create an internal environment where inflammation is less likely to flare up again. Many patients find that after a course of treatment, not only does their vision improve, but their overall health and resilience feel stronger. That said, retinitis can be a complex condition, and some people may need periodic maintenance treatments, especially during stressful periods or seasonal changes. Your practitioner will help you develop a long-term plan.

### Are there side effects from the herbal formulas?
When prescribed by a qualified TCM practitioner based on a correct pattern diagnosis, herbal formulas are generally well-tolerated. Digestive upset is the most common mild side effect and can often be resolved by adjusting the formula or taking it with food. Formulas that clear Heat or Dampness can sometimes be slightly cooling to the stomach, so your practitioner will monitor your response closely. It is crucial to only take herbs prescribed specifically for you. Self-prescribing or buying generic formulas online can be ineffective at best and harmful at worst, especially with a condition as delicate as retinitis.
