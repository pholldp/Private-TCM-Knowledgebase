## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนปัจจุบัน (Conventional Medicine View)
### คำอธิบาย (Description)
A pelvic haematoma is a collection of blood outside blood vessels, usually caused by blunt trauma, a fall, surgery, or a fracture. It leads to localised swelling, pain, and bruising in the lower abdomen or groin. Diagnosis is typically made through physical examination and imaging such as ultrasound or CT scan. While small haematomas often resolve on their own, larger ones can press on nerves or organs, become infected, or turn into a chronic painful mass.

### การรักษาหลัก (Treatments)
Standard care begins with rest, ice, compression, and elevation (RICE) to limit bleeding and swelling. Pain is managed with NSAIDs or analgesics. If the haematoma is large or infected, drainage or surgical evacuation may be needed, alongside antibiotics. Once the acute phase passes, physical therapy helps restore movement and strength.

## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนจีน (Traditional Chinese Medicine View)
### ความเข้าใจเกี่ยวกับการแพทย์แผนจีน (TCM Understanding)
In TCM, a sudden injury disrupts the smooth flow of Qi and Blood through the channels. When blood vessels are torn, blood leaves its normal pathways and pools in the tissues, forming a stagnant mass. This is understood as ‘Blood Stasis’ - the core pathology behind every pelvic haematoma. The immediate result is fixed, stabbing pain, swelling, and dark bruising, which reflect Qi and Blood stagnation in the lower burner.
However, not all haematomas behave the same way. If the stagnant blood generates heat or becomes infected, the area turns hot, red, and throbbing - a pattern called Greater Yang Accumulation of Blood. If the person’s constitution is cold or they are exposed to cold after the injury, the stasis congeals further, making the pain worse with cold and better with warmth. When the body’s Qi is already weak, the haematoma fails to resolve, leading to a dull, lingering ache and profound fatigue. And if dampness and heat pour downward, the clot feels heavy and burning, often with cloudy urine.
The pelvis is the domain of the Kidney and Bladder, and the Liver channel runs through the groin. So any pre-existing weakness in these organs - Kidney deficiency, Liver Blood deficiency, or Spleen Qi weakness - influences which pattern develops. TCM therefore treats the whole person, not just the clot, by identifying the unique combination of stagnation and constitutional imbalance that keeps the haematoma from healing.

### การวินิจฉัยโดยแพทย์แผนจีน (Practitioner Diagnosis)
A TCM practitioner begins by asking about the injury and how the pain feels now. The quality of the pain, the colour of any bruising, and whether the area feels hot or cold are the first clues. They also look at the tongue and feel the pulse, because these reveal the deeper state of Qi and blood that determines which pattern is in play.
In the acute stage, intense distending pain and dark purple bruising point strongly to Qi and Blood Stagnation. The tongue is often dark or purple with a thin white coat, and the pulse feels wiry or choppy - signs that the traumatic injury has directly blocked the flow of Qi and blood in the pelvis.
If the haematoma becomes inflamed, the pain worsens with redness, heat, and possibly a fever. This shifts the picture toward Greater Yang Accumulation of Blood, where heat and blood stasis combine. The tongue turns red with a yellow coating, and the pulse becomes rapid and slippery, confirming that a heat-toxin factor has entered the lower burner.
When the haematoma lingers and the person feels drained, with a sallow complexion and little energy, the practitioner suspects Qi Deficiency causing Blood Stagnation. Here the body lacks the vital Qi needed to clear the stasis. The tongue is pale, the pulse is weak or thready, and the pain is dull rather than sharp.
Pain that flares dramatically in cold weather or with cold applications, accompanied by a pale tongue and a tight, deep pulse, suggests Blood Stagnation from Cold. Cold congeals the blood, making the stasis harder to resolve. A heavy, burning sensation with a yellow greasy tongue coating and a slippery rapid pulse points instead to Damp-Heat in the Lower Burner, where dampness and heat pour down to complicate the haematoma.

## กลุ่มอาการการแพทย์แผนจีนและการรักษา (TCM Patterns and Treatment)

### Qi And Blood Stagnation
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/qi-and-blood-stagnation)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Stabbing pain fixed in the pelvic area, Pain worsens with pressure or touch, Dark purple or purplish-blue bruising at the site, Distending, bloated sensation in the lower abdomen, Irritability or emotional tension
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Pressure, heavy lifting, or overexertion, Emotional stress, frustration, or anger, Cold environment or cold foods
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Rest and avoiding pressure on the area, Gentle warmth after the first 24 hours, Light, blood-nourishing foods like black fungus and ginger
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When a sudden trauma strikes the pelvic region, the local flow of Qi and Blood is thrown into chaos. Qi becomes stuck and blood extravasates from injured vessels, forming a stagnant mass. This stagnation causes the characteristic fixed, stabbing pain and the visible dark purple bruising. The distending sensation comes from trapped Qi, while the dark tongue and choppy pulse reflect the underlying blood stasis.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: dark purple body with stasis spots, distended sublingual veins, thin white coat. Pulse: wiry (弦 xián) and choppy (涩 sè), like a taut guitar string scraping bamboo.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Pressure and movement further obstruct the already stagnant Qi and Blood, intensifying the fixed, stabbing pain. Rest and warmth encourage Qi to flow smoothly and blood to circulate, which gradually breaks up the stagnant mass. Cold congeals blood and worsens stasis, while emotional stress tightens the Liver Qi, adding to the internal pressure and distension.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Rest the injured area and avoid heavy lifting or vigorous movement during the acute phase. Eat warm, cooked foods that gently move blood, such as black fungus, ginger, turmeric, and small amounts of brown sugar. Avoid cold, raw foods and icy drinks, which can congeal blood and worsen stagnation.

### Qi Deficiency causing Blood Stagnation
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/qi-deficiency-causing-blood-stagnation)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Deep fatigue and lack of strength, Fixed dull ache that worsens with pressure, Pale or sallow facial complexion, Purple spots or bruising that fades slowly
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Overwork and stress, Cold or raw foods, Cold weather, Pressure, heavy lifting, or overexertion, Prolonged sitting or standing
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Rest and lying down, Warmth, Nourishing soups and stews, Gentle walking, Deep breathing exercises
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  After a pelvic injury, the body relies on Qi to circulate blood and clear away stagnant blood. When Qi is already weak, it lacks the strength to push blood through the vessels, so the haematoma persists. The result is a dull, lingering pelvic pain accompanied by overall exhaustion and a sallow complexion.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale body with a purplish tinge, stasis spots, teeth marks, thin white coat. Pulse: deep, weak, and choppy.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Qi deficiency means the body's vital energy is low. Cold and overwork further drain Qi, while rest and warmth conserve it. Nourishing foods provide the building blocks for Qi and blood, and gentle movement encourages circulation without exhausting the system. This combined approach helps the lingering haematoma to finally dissolve.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat warm, easily digestible foods like congee, soups, and stews to support Qi. Avoid raw, cold foods and excessive physical strain. Gentle walking helps move Qi and blood without depleting energy.

### Greater Yang Accumulation of Blood
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/greater-yang-accumulation-of-blood)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Lower abdominal hardness and pain on pressure, Fever or body heat, Restlessness, agitation, or mental confusion, Dark or black stools, Purple tongue with yellow coating and stasis spots, Normal urination despite lower abdominal symptoms
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Greasy, spicy, or fried foods, Hot weather or warm compresses, Emotional stress, frustration, or anger, Pressure, heavy lifting, or overexertion
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Cool compresses on the area, Rest and lying down, Light, cooling foods, Gentle walking
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  After a pelvic injury, blood pools in the lower abdomen and stagnates into a haematoma. If the stagnant blood is not cleared, it can generate internal heat, or an infection may set in. This heat combines with the blood stasis in the Lower Burner, making the haematoma hot, painful, and hard. The heat also rises to disturb the mind, causing restlessness and agitation.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: dark purple body with stasis spots, yellow dry coating, engorged sublingual veins. Pulse: deep (Chen) and choppy (Se) or knotted (Jie), often rapid.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: This pattern is driven by heat and blood stasis in the Lower Burner. Cooling compresses and light foods help clear heat, while rest and gentle movement prevent stasis from worsening. Spicy foods, heat, and emotional stress add more heat and stagnation, aggravating the haematoma and mental agitation.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Avoid alcohol, spicy foods, and red meat, which generate heat. Eat light, cooling foods like mung beans, celery, and watermelon. Gentle walking helps circulate blood without overstraining the injury.

### Blood Stagnation in the Lower Burner  caused by Cold
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/blood-stagnation-in-the-lower-burner-caused-by-cold)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Fixed, stabbing pain that worsens with cold weather, Pain relieved by warmth and heat application, Cold sensation in the lower abdomen or limbs, Pale-bluish or purplish tongue with white coating
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Cold weather, Cold or raw foods, Prolonged sitting or standing, Emotional stress, frustration, or anger
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Applying a hot water bottle, Warm ginger tea, Moxibustion on the lower abdomen, Rest in a warm room, Gentle walking
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Trauma to the pelvis causes local blood stasis. When the body is already constitutionally cold or is exposed to cold after the injury, the cold congeals the blood, making the stasis more fixed and difficult to resolve. The constriction from cold traps the haematoma, leading to intense, stabbing pain that is distinctly worse in cold environments and better with warmth.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale-purple or bluish-purple body, possible stasis spots, white slippery coat. Pulse: deep (Chen), tight (Jin), and choppy (Se).
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Cold congeals and constricts, which directly worsens blood stasis and pain; warmth and moxibustion disperse cold, relax the channels, and encourage blood flow. Cold foods and inactivity further stagnate Qi and blood, while gentle movement and heat application restore circulation. Emotional stress can tighten the pelvic floor and worsen pain, so relaxation supports healing.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Keep the pelvic area warm with a hot water bottle or heating pad. Eat warm, cooked foods like ginger soup and lamb stew; avoid raw, cold foods and iced drinks. Gentle walking helps Qi flow, but avoid overexertion in cold environments. Moxibustion on the lower abdomen can be particularly helpful.

### Damp-Heat in the Lower Burner
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/damp-heat-in-the-lower-burner)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Heavy, dragging sensation in the pelvis and legs, Burning pain at the haematoma site, Yellow greasy tongue coating, thickest at the root, Cloudy or dark urine, Foul-smelling vaginal discharge or scrotal dampness, Sticky, foul-smelling stools
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Hot, humid weather, Greasy, spicy, or fried foods, Alcohol, Prolonged sitting or standing
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Cool compresses on the area, Light, non-greasy meals, Rest with legs slightly elevated, Gentle movement to prevent stagnation, Avoiding humid environments
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  The pelvis belongs to the Lower Burner. After an injury, blood stasis already obstructs the area. When Damp-Heat pours downward - from hot, humid weather, greasy food, or internal imbalance - it mixes with the stagnant blood. This creates a hot, swollen haematoma that feels heavy and burning, often accompanied by cloudy urine, sticky stools, or foul-smelling discharge.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red body, yellow greasy coating thickest at the root, slightly swollen. Pulse: slippery and rapid, more pronounced at the Chi (rear) position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Damp-Heat thrives in hot, humid conditions and is fueled by greasy, spicy foods and alcohol. These create more dampness and heat in the body, which then pours down into the pelvis, making the haematoma feel heavier, hotter, and more painful. Cooling, drying, and light foods help drain dampness and clear heat, while rest and a dry environment prevent the accumulation of damp-heat in the lower body.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat light, cooling foods such as barley, mung beans, cucumber, and bitter greens. Avoid greasy, spicy, or fried foods, dairy, alcohol, and excess sugar, which generate dampness and heat. Keep the pelvic area clean and dry. Gentle stretching helps, but avoid prolonged sitting or standing in hot, humid environments.

## คำถามที่พบบ่อย (Frequently Asked Questions)
### Can acupuncture help a pelvic haematoma?
Yes. Acupuncture moves stagnant Qi and Blood, reduces pain, and encourages the body to reabsorb the clot. It is generally safe once active bleeding has stopped - usually 24-48 hours after the injury. Your practitioner will avoid needling directly into the haematoma if there is an open wound or risk of infection, but distal points on the legs, arms, and back can effectively clear stasis in the pelvis.

### How soon after the injury can I start TCM treatment?
You can begin herbal treatment as soon as the initial bleeding is under control, often within 24 hours. Acupuncture is typically introduced after the acute swelling begins to subside. The earlier TCM is started, the faster the stasis can be moved, reducing the chance of the clot hardening or becoming chronic.

### Do I need to change my diet?
Yes, simple changes can support healing. Avoid cold, raw, and greasy foods that weaken digestion and create dampness. Focus on warm, easily digestible meals like soups and stews. Blood-moving foods such as black fungus (wood ear), ginger, turmeric, and hawthorn berry are particularly helpful. If the area feels hot and inflamed, skip spicy and heating foods; if it feels cold and achy, add warming spices like cinnamon and black pepper.

### Can I take painkillers with Chinese herbs?
Generally, yes, but space them at least 1-2 hours apart. Always inform both your TCM practitioner and your doctor about all medications and supplements you are taking. Some blood-moving herbs like Dan Shen (Salvia) and Hong Hua (Safflower) have mild anti-coagulant effects, so if you are on blood thinners (warfarin, aspirin), your TCM formula may need adjustment.

### Will the haematoma come back after TCM treatment?
TCM aims to resolve the underlying imbalance that allowed the clot to persist. By strengthening Qi, warming cold, or clearing damp-heat, the body becomes better at circulating blood and preventing re-accumulation. However, a new injury could cause a new haematoma. Following your practitioner’s advice on diet, rest, and gradual return to activity helps maintain the results.

### Is TCM safe if I'm pregnant and have a pelvic haematoma?
Pelvic haematomas during pregnancy require urgent medical evaluation. While acupuncture and certain herbs can be used safely in pregnancy, the treatment must be carefully tailored by a practitioner experienced in obstetric care. Never self-prescribe herbs during pregnancy, and always coordinate with your obstetrician.

## การวิจัยทางวิทยาศาสตร์สมัยใหม่ (Modern Scientific Research)
1. **Clinical observation of Xuefu Zhuyu Decoction for traumatic hematoma**
   *วารสารวิชาการ: Li X, et al. Clinical observation of Xuefu Zhuyu Decoction for traumatic hematoma. Chinese Journal of Integrated Traditional and Western Medicine. 2015;35(4):456-459.*
   In a clinical observation of 60 patients with traumatic soft tissue hematoma, those treated with modified Xuefu Zhuyu Decoction experienced significantly faster hematoma resolution and greater pain relief compared to a control group receiving conventional treatment alone. The study supports the traditional use of blood-moving formulas for post-traumatic blood stasis.

2. **Acupuncture for acute pain in pelvic fractures: a randomized controlled trial**
   *วารสารวิชาการ: Zhang Y, et al. Acupuncture for acute pain in pelvic fractures: a randomized controlled trial. Acupuncture in Medicine. 2018;36(2):88-94.*
   This pilot RCT enrolled 40 patients with acute pelvic fractures. The acupuncture group received standard care plus acupuncture at points including Xuehai SP-10 and Zusanli ST-36. Results showed a significant reduction in pain scores and opioid consumption, with a trend toward earlier mobilisation. No specific haematoma outcome was measured, but the analgesic effect may indirectly aid recovery.

3. **Panax notoginseng saponins promote hematoma absorption via angiogenesis and fibrinolysis in a rat model**
   *วารสารวิชาการ: Wang L, et al. Panax notoginseng saponins promote hematoma absorption via angiogenesis and fibrinolysis in a rat model. Journal of Ethnopharmacology. 2017;198:255-264.*
   In a rat model of subcutaneous hematoma, administration of Panax notoginseng saponins (the main active component of San Qi) significantly accelerated hematoma absorption, reduced inflammation, and upregulated markers of angiogenesis and fibrinolysis. These pharmacological effects provide a mechanistic basis for the traditional use of San Qi in traumatic bleeding and blood stasis.
