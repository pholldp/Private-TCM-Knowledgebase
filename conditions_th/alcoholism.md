## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนปัจจุบัน (Conventional Medicine View)
### คำอธิบาย (Description)
Alcoholism, or alcohol use disorder (AUD), is a chronic brain condition marked by an inability to control drinking despite negative consequences. It ranges from mild to severe and includes symptoms like craving, loss of control, physical dependence, and tolerance. Diagnosis is based on criteria such as drinking more than intended, failed attempts to cut down, and withdrawal symptoms when alcohol wears off. The condition involves complex interactions between genetic, psychological, and social factors, and it can lead to serious health problems including liver disease, heart disease, and mental health disorders.

### การรักษาหลัก (Treatments)
Standard treatment often combines medications (like naltrexone to reduce cravings, acamprosate to ease withdrawal, or disulfiram to create an unpleasant reaction to alcohol) with behavioral therapies, counseling, and support groups such as Alcoholics Anonymous. For severe dependence, medically supervised detoxification and inpatient or outpatient rehabilitation programs are common. The goal is to achieve and maintain sobriety, manage withdrawal safely, and prevent relapse.

## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนจีน (Traditional Chinese Medicine View)
### ความเข้าใจเกี่ยวกับการแพทย์แผนจีน (TCM Understanding)
In TCM, alcohol is seen as a hot, damp, and toxic substance. When consumed in excess, it first injures the Spleen and Stomach, the organs of digestion. They become overwhelmed, leading to dampness and heat that cause nausea, bloating, and a thick tongue coating. This is the acute hangover pattern, but if drinking continues, the damp-heat can lodge in the Liver and Gallbladder, impairing the smooth flow of Qi and causing rib-side pain, bitter taste, and irritability.
In some people, the heat from alcohol directly ignites Liver Fire, sending heat upward to the head - red face, throbbing headache, explosive anger. This is a more acute, intense reaction. These excess patterns are common in the early stages of alcohol abuse.
Over time, alcohol's drying nature depletes the body's Yin and Blood. The Spleen, weakened by years of dampness, can no longer produce enough Qi and Blood to nourish the Heart, leading to anxiety, palpitations, and insomnia (Heart and Spleen Deficiency). Or the deep Yin of the Kidney and Liver is burned up, causing night sweats, dizziness, and a red, peeled tongue. These deficiency patterns explain why long-term drinkers often feel utterly drained even when they stop drinking.
Crucially, TCM sees addiction not just as a brain disease but as a disturbance of the Shen (spirit) rooted in organ imbalance. A restless Shen from Liver Fire or an unanchored Shen from Yin deficiency can drive the compulsion to drink. By correcting the underlying pattern, TCM aims to calm the Shen and reduce cravings naturally, while also repairing the organ damage.

### การวินิจฉัยโดยแพทย์แผนจีน (Practitioner Diagnosis)
A practitioner first looks for signs of damp-heat in the Liver and Gallbladder. This very common pattern produces a bitter taste, nausea, a yellow complexion, and a feeling of fullness or discomfort under the ribs. The tongue is red with a yellow, greasy coating, and the pulse feels slippery and rapid. These clues point to alcohol’s hot, damp nature overwhelming the liver’s ability to process toxins.
When the same damp-heat settles more in the Stomach and Spleen, digestion takes the biggest hit. You might notice poor appetite, a heavy sensation in the stomach, nausea, and a thick yellow tongue coating. The pulse is still slippery, but the discomfort centers in the upper abdomen rather than the rib area. This pattern reflects alcohol directly injuring the digestive organs.
Acute intoxication often stirs Liver Fire Blazing. Here heat dominates over dampness, so the face and eyes turn red, and the person feels irritable, dizzy, and headachy with a very bitter taste. The tongue is red with a dry yellow coat, and the pulse is wiry and rapid. This pattern is more about an intense upward flare of fire rather than slow, damp accumulation.
Long-term drinking can deplete the Heart and Spleen, leading to Deficiency. A person feels constantly tired, has palpitations, trouble sleeping, and poor memory. The tongue looks pale and puffy, and the pulse is thin and weak. There is no sign of heat or dampness here - the picture is one of exhaustion, as the body struggles to produce enough Blood to nourish the mind.
Another chronic pattern is Kidney and Liver Yin Deficiency. Deep fluids are consumed, causing dizziness, tinnitus, night sweats, and a dry mouth. The tongue is red with little or no coating, and the pulse is thin and rapid. This pattern signals that alcohol has burned through the body’s cooling reserves, leaving a deep, smoldering emptiness rather than a raging fire.
Less commonly, Phlegm-Dampness lodges in the Middle-Burner without prominent heat. The chest and stomach feel full and blocked, with nausea and a thick, greasy tongue coating that may be white rather than yellow. The pulse is slippery. This pattern arises when the spleen fails to transform fluids, creating a heavy, stagnant dampness that clouds the digestive center.

### Liver and Gallbladder Damp-Heat
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/liver-and-gallbladder-damp-heat)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Bitter taste in the mouth, Right-sided rib pain or distension, Nausea or vomiting, Yellowing of the eyes or skin, Dark yellow scanty urine, Irritability and restlessness
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Alcohol consumption, Greasy or fried foods, Spicy food, Hot, humid weather, Stress and anger
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Cool, light meals, Drinking plain water, Rest in a cool room, Barley or mung bean foods/drinks, Gentle exercise or walking
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Alcohol is inherently hot and damp. When drunk excessively, it directly invades the Liver and Gallbladder, impairing the Liver's ability to maintain the smooth flow of Qi and the Gallbladder's role in bile secretion. This leads to damp-heat accumulating, causing the bitter taste, nausea, and right-sided rib pain typical of this pattern.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red body, red sides, thick yellow greasy coating. Pulse: wiry, slippery, and rapid, especially at the left middle position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Alcohol and greasy foods directly add damp-heat to the body, worsening the accumulation in the Liver and Gallbladder. Hot weather and emotional stress stir up Liver Qi and heat, making symptoms flare. Cooling foods, hydration, and rest help clear heat and drain dampness, while gentle movement keeps Qi from stagnating.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Avoid alcohol completely and steer clear of greasy, fried, or spicy foods that generate more damp-heat. Eat cooling, damp-resolving foods like barley, mung beans, cucumber, and bitter gourd. Drink plenty of plain water. Gentle daily movement helps Qi flow and prevents stagnation.

### Damp-Heat in Stomach and Spleen
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/damp-heat-in-stomach-and-spleen)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Bloating and fullness in the upper abdomen, Nausea or vomiting, Poor appetite, Loose stools, Heavy feeling in the body and limbs, Bitter taste in the mouth, Greasy yellow coating on tongue
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Alcohol consumption, Greasy, spicy, or sweet foods, Overeating, Humid weather, Stress and worry
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Avoiding alcohol, Light, bland diet, Barley or mung bean foods/drinks, Rest and gentle movement
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Alcohol is hot and damp by nature. When consumed excessively, it directly attacks the Stomach and Spleen, impairing their ability to digest food and transform fluids. This leads to a buildup of Dampness and Heat in the middle burner, causing the nausea, bloating, poor appetite, and sticky stools that are typical of this pattern.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Red body, possibly swollen with teeth marks, thick yellow greasy coating especially at centre and root. Pulse: soggy (Ru), rapid (Shu), and slippery (Hua).
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Alcohol is hot and damp, so continuing to drink pours more Damp-Heat into the already overwhelmed Stomach and Spleen. Greasy, spicy, and sweet foods also generate Dampness and Heat, worsening the pattern. A bland diet and barley tea help drain Dampness and clear Heat. Rest allows the Spleen to recover its digestive power, while gentle movement keeps Qi and fluids moving so Dampness does not stagnate.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Stop drinking alcohol completely to remove the source of Damp-Heat. Eat light, easily digestible foods like congee, steamed vegetables, and barley tea. Avoid greasy, fried, spicy, and sweet foods that create more Dampness. Gentle exercise like walking helps Qi flow and transform Dampness.

### Liver Fire Blazing
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/liver-fire-blazing)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Throbbing headache at temples or crown, Red, painful, or burning eyes, Bitter taste in the mouth, Intense irritability and quick temper, Flushed red face, Dry mouth and throat
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Anger and frustration, Alcohol consumption, Spicy and greasy food, Stress and overwork, Hot weather, Lack of sleep
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Cooling drinks (chrysanthemum tea), Rest and relaxation, Cool environment, Deep breathing exercises, Avoiding alcohol, Gentle exercise or walking
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Alcohol is a hot and damp substance that easily invades the Liver. In susceptible people, the heat from alcohol stokes Liver Qi into a raging fire that surges upward to the head, causing the classic red face, throbbing headache, and intense irritability. The fire also disturbs the mind, leading to restless agitation and insomnia.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red body with redder sides, dry yellow coating, possible red prickles. Pulse: wiry (弦 xián) and rapid (数 shuò), forceful at the left Guan position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Alcohol is a hot substance that directly feeds Liver Fire. Anger and frustration cause Liver Qi to stagnate and then explode upward as fire. Cooling foods and drinks, a calm environment, and relaxation techniques help to bring the fire down and soothe the Liver. Gentle exercise promotes smooth Qi flow without overheating the body.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Stop drinking alcohol completely. Eat cooling foods like cucumber, celery, and watermelon. Drink chrysanthemum or peppermint tea to soothe the Liver. Avoid spicy, greasy, and fried foods that add heat. Practice stress-reducing activities like gentle yoga or meditation to calm the mind.

### Heart and Spleen Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/heart-and-spleen-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Palpitations or racing heart, Insomnia with vivid dreams, Poor appetite with bloating after eating, Fatigue and lack of energy, Anxiety or feeling easily startled, Sallow or pale complexion, Loose stools
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Alcohol consumption, Stress and overthinking, Irregular eating or skipping meals, Overwork and lack of sleep, Cold, raw foods and iced drinks, Excessive talking or mental strain
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warm, cooked meals, Adequate rest and sleep, Gentle exercise or walking, Calm, low-stress environment, Small, frequent meals, Warmth and avoiding cold drafts
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Chronic alcohol use directly damages the Spleen's ability to transform food into Qi and Blood. ### Kidney and Liver Yin Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/kidney-and-liver-yin-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Night sweats and heat in the palms, soles, and chest, Dizziness and ringing in the ears, Dry eyes with blurred vision, Soreness and weakness of the lower back and knees, Insomnia with vivid dreams, Forgetfulness and poor concentration
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Alcohol consumption, Spicy and greasy food, Overwork and late nights, Emotional stress and anger, Hot, dry environments
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Adequate rest and sleep, Cooling, moistening foods (pears, tofu), Gentle exercise or walking, Quiet, dark environment, Avoiding alcohol
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Alcohol is hot and drying by nature. When it is consumed heavily over many years, it slowly burns up the body's Yin fluids - the cooling, moistening essence stored in the Kidney and Liver. As Kidney and Liver Yin become depleted, deficiency heat flares up, and the senses and mind lose their nourishment. This is what produces the deep dizziness, ringing in the ears, night sweats, and the characteristic red, peeled tongue.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red, thin body with cracks, little or no coating, dry surface. Pulse: fine, rapid, and often wiry; weak in the Kidney position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Alcohol is hot and drying; it directly consumes the Yin fluids that are already scarce in this pattern. Spicy and greasy foods generate internal heat, and late nights or overwork further drain Kidney essence. Emotional stress stirs Liver Yang, which rises because there is insufficient Yin to anchor it. Relief comes from cooling, moistening foods and rest, which replenish Yin and allow deficiency heat to settle. Gentle movement keeps Qi flowing without overheating, while quiet darkness supports the deep restoration the Kidney and Liver need.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Stop drinking alcohol completely - it is the root cause of this deep depletion. Eat cooling, moistening foods regularly: pears, black sesame, walnuts, goji berries, millet, and mung beans. Avoid spicy, greasy, and fried foods that add heat. Prioritize sleep and rest; late nights and overwork drain Kidney essence. Gentle movement like Tai Chi or restorative yoga helps settle the mind without overheating the body.

### Phlegm-Dampness in the Middle-Burner
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/phlegm-dampness-in-the-middle-burner)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Bloating and fullness in the upper abdomen, Nausea or vomiting, Heavy feeling in the body and limbs, Thick, greasy, white tongue coating, No bitter taste, red face, or other heat signs
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Greasy, heavy, or sweet foods, Cold, raw foods and iced drinks, Overeating or irregular meals, Alcohol consumption, Damp, humid weather, Lack of movement or lying down after meals
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warm, light meals (e.g., congee, soups), Ginger tea or warm water, Gentle exercise or walking, Dry, warm environment
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Alcohol is a damp and heavy substance. When consumed habitually, it first overwhelms the Spleen's ability to transform and transport fluids. Over time, the Spleen Yang weakens, and the unprocessed fluids congeal into a cold, sticky phlegm-dampness that sits heavily in the middle burner. Because the Spleen is too sluggish to generate heat, there are no signs of fire or yellow coating - only a profound sense of heaviness, nausea, and a thick white greasy tongue coating.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale, swollen body with scalloped teeth marks, covered by a thick white greasy coating that is excessively moist. Pulse: slippery (滑 Huá), slow or moderate (缓 Huǎn), often soggy (濡 Rú), especially at the right Guan position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Phlegm-Dampness in the middle burner arises when the Spleen is too weak to transform fluids, so anything that further burdens the Spleen - greasy, sweet, cold, or raw foods, as well as alcohol - adds to the sticky accumulation. Warm, light foods and gentle movement give the Spleen the gentle heat and Qi flow it needs to resume its work of transporting and transforming, while ginger and warm drinks directly warm the middle burner to dry dampness.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Avoid alcohol completely and steer clear of greasy, sweet, raw, and cold foods, which all generate dampness. Eat small, warm, easily digested meals like congee with a little ginger, and include cooked vegetables like pumpkin and carrot. Gentle daily movement - a short walk or gentle stretching - helps Qi move and transforms dampness.

### Can acupuncture really help with alcohol cravings?
Yes. Acupuncture, especially points like Shenmen (HT-7) and Neiguan (PC-6), can calm the mind and reduce the intensity of cravings. Combined with herbs that clear heat or nourish deficiencies, many people find their urge to drink diminishes. It's not a magic bullet, but it can be a powerful part of a recovery plan.

### Do I have to stop drinking completely to start TCM?
Ideally, yes - alcohol continues to damage the organs and counteracts the herbs' healing effects. However, many patients begin treatment while still drinking, and the herbs and acupuncture can help ease withdrawal symptoms and reduce the desire to drink, making it easier to cut back. Your practitioner will work with you at your current stage.

### How long until I feel better?
Physical symptoms like nausea, headache, and bloating often improve within the first 1-2 weeks. Cravings and emotional symptoms like anxiety or irritability may take 2-4 weeks to noticeably lessen. For long-term recovery and repair of deeper organ damage, treatment typically continues for several months. Consistency is key.

### Can TCM help with withdrawal symptoms like anxiety and insomnia?
Yes, this is one of TCM's strengths. Formulas like Gui Pi Tang for Heart-Spleen Deficiency or Long Dan Xie Gan Tang for Liver Fire can directly address the restlessness, palpitations, and sleeplessness that come with withdrawal. Acupuncture on Shenmen, Neiguan, and Sanyinjiao can be particularly calming during the early days of sobriety.

### Are Chinese herbs safe if I have liver damage from drinking?
Many herbs used for alcoholism, like Ge Hua (kudzu flower) and Yin Chen, are actually hepatoprotective - they help the liver recover. However, some herbs can stress an already damaged liver, so it's critical to work with a qualified TCM practitioner who will choose a formula tailored to your liver function. Never self-prescribe, and always tell your doctor what you're taking.

### How does TCM compare to AA or rehab?
TCM is not a replacement for psychological support or group programs; it's a complementary physical and energetic treatment. While AA addresses the mental and spiritual aspects of addiction, TCM works on the body's organ imbalances that may drive the compulsion to drink. Many people use both together for a more holistic recovery.

## การวิจัยทางวิทยาศาสตร์สมัยใหม่ (Modern Scientific Research)
1. **Acupuncture for alcohol dependence** ([ลิงก์](https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD005062.pub4))
   *วารสารวิชาการ: Cho SH, Lee H, Shin BC, et al. Acupuncture for alcohol dependence. Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews. 2014;(9):CD005062.*
   A Cochrane systematic review of 11 randomized controlled trials evaluating the effectiveness of acupuncture for alcohol dependence. The review concluded that the evidence was insufficient to recommend acupuncture as a standalone treatment due to methodological limitations and small sample sizes, though some studies showed potential benefits for reducing cravings.

2. **An extract of the Chinese herbal root kudzu reduces alcohol drinking by heavy drinkers in a naturalistic setting** ([ลิงก์](https://doi.org/10.1097/01.ALC.0000163499.64347.92))
   *วารสารวิชาการ: Lukas SE, Penetar D, Berko J, et al. An extract of the Chinese herbal root kudzu reduces alcohol drinking by heavy drinkers in a naturalistic setting. Alcoholism: Clinical and Experimental Research. 2005;29(5):756-762.*
   A randomized, placebo-controlled, double-blind trial involving heavy drinkers. Participants taking a standardized kudzu (Pueraria lobata) extract significantly reduced their beer consumption compared to placebo, with no reported side effects, suggesting kudzu may help moderate alcohol intake.

3. **A pilot study exploring the effect of kudzu root on the drinking habits of patients with chronic alcoholism** ([ลิงก์](https://doi.org/10.1089/acm.2000.6.45))
   *วารสารวิชาการ: Shebek J, Rindone JP. A pilot study exploring the effect of kudzu root on the drinking habits of patients with chronic alcoholism. Journal of Alternative and Complementary Medicine. 2000;6(1):45-48.*
   A small pilot study in which chronic alcoholics received kudzu root or placebo. The kudzu group showed a trend toward reduced alcohol consumption and fewer heavy drinking days, supporting the traditional use of kudzu for alcohol-related problems.
