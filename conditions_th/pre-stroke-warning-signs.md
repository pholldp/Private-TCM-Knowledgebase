## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนปัจจุบัน (Conventional Medicine View)
### คำอธิบาย (Description)
In Western medicine, pre-stroke warning signs are most often transient ischemic attacks (TIAs) - brief episodes of neurological dysfunction caused by a temporary blockage of blood flow to part of the brain. Symptoms typically come on suddenly, last only a few minutes, and resolve completely within 24 hours, but they are a serious red flag that a full stroke may follow. Diagnosis relies on imaging (CT or MRI), blood tests, and assessment of cardiovascular risk factors such as high blood pressure, high cholesterol, atrial fibrillation, and diabetes.

### การรักษาหลัก (Treatments)
Standard treatment after a TIA focuses on preventing a future stroke. This usually includes antiplatelet medications like aspirin or clopidogrel, anticoagulants if atrial fibrillation is present, and aggressive management of blood pressure, cholesterol, and blood sugar. Lifestyle changes - smoking cessation, a heart-healthy diet, and regular exercise - are also central to the plan. In some cases, surgery such as carotid endarterectomy may be recommended to clear narrowed arteries.

## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนจีน (Traditional Chinese Medicine View)
### ความเข้าใจเกี่ยวกับการแพทย์แผนจีน (TCM Understanding)
In TCM, pre-stroke warning signs are understood as a disturbance of the brain's orifices by internal Wind. The brain depends on a steady supply of Qi and Blood to function clearly. When something disrupts this flow - whether it's rising Liver Yang, sticky Phlegm, or stagnant Blood - the result is the sudden, transient symptoms that warn of an impending stroke. The Liver is most often involved, because it governs the smooth movement of Qi and is prone to generating internal Wind when its Yang flares or its Yin is depleted.
The Spleen and Stomach also play a critical role. If they are weak, they fail to transform fluids properly, and dampness accumulates into Phlegm. This Phlegm can then combine with internal Wind and rise to obstruct the channels, creating the heavy, numb sensations and foggy thinking that characterize another common pre-stroke pattern. Even the Kidney is implicated, because it stores the body's foundational Yin. When Kidney Yin is exhausted, it cannot anchor the Liver Yang, and empty Wind stirs, causing dizziness and tremors.
This is why a single Western diagnosis - a TIA - can look so different from person to person in TCM. One patient's warning signs may come with a pounding headache, red face, and explosive temper (Liver Yang Rising). Another may feel heavy, foggy, and full of phlegm (Wind-Phlegm). A third may be deeply fatigued, pale, and weak (Qi Deficiency with Blood Stagnation). Each pattern requires a different treatment strategy, and the tongue and pulse are the keys that unlock which one is active.

### การวินิจฉัยโดยแพทย์แผนจีน (Practitioner Diagnosis)
A TCM practitioner begins by listening carefully to the story of each episode - what the dizziness feels like, whether there is numbness or weakness, and what makes it better or worse. The tongue and pulse are then examined, because they reveal the internal landscape that the symptoms only hint at.
When the warning signs flare with a pounding headache, a red face, and intense irritability, a Liver Yang Rising pattern is likely. The tongue is red with a yellow coating, and the pulse feels wiry and rapid - signals of heat and upward-rushing energy that need to be anchored.
If instead the dizziness is accompanied by a dry mouth, a feeling of unsteadiness like something is stirring inside, and perhaps a slight tremor, the picture shifts to Empty-Wind agitating in the Interior. The tongue is red but with little or no coating, and the pulse is thin and rapid, pointing to a deep yin deficiency that fails to hold the yang.
In an overweight person with a heavy, numb sensation, chest tightness, and a lot of phlegm, Wind-Phlegm obstructing the channels is the prime suspect. The tongue coating is thick and greasy, and the pulse is slippery and wiry - classic signs of phlegm-dampness mixing with internal wind.
When the same dizziness comes with a bitter taste, constipation, and a thick yellow tongue coating that looks greasy, the pattern is Phlegm-Heat. The pulse is rapid and slippery, telling the practitioner that heat has bound with phlegm and is blocking the bowels and disturbing the head.
Finally, if the main complaint is overwhelming fatigue, a pale or dull complexion, and limb weakness that feels like the body simply cannot power through, Qi Deficiency causing Blood Stagnation is at play. The tongue is pale with possible dark spots, and the pulse is weak and choppy - a sign that the life force is too low to keep blood moving smoothly.

### Liver Yang Rising
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/liver-yang-rising)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Throbbing or distending headache, especially at the temples, Sudden dizziness or vertigo, Irritability and quick temper, Flushed face and red eyes
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Emotional stress, Alcohol and coffee, Spicy, greasy or fried foods, Overwork and lack of sleep, Hot weather
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Rest and sleep, Cooling herbal teas like chrysanthemum, Gentle movement or exercise, Managing anger and frustration
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  In Liver Yang Rising, the Liver's upward-moving energy surges out of control, often because there is not enough Yin to anchor it. This upward rush of Yang and Heat disturbs the head, causing the sudden dizziness, throbbing temple headaches, and irritability that are classic pre-stroke warning signs. When the internal Wind is stirred, it can also produce transient numbness, weakness, or speech difficulty.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: Red body, red sides, thin dry yellow or scanty coat, possibly trembling. Pulse: Wiry (弦 xián), forceful at the left guan position; may be fine and rapid if Yin deficiency is marked.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Stress and anger directly stir Liver Yang to rise, triggering headaches and dizziness. Cooling foods and calming activities help anchor the Yang and prevent it from flaring upward. Rest and gentle exercise nourish Yin, which is the foundation for holding Yang in check.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat cooling, light foods like celery, spinach, and chrysanthemum tea. Avoid alcohol, coffee, and spicy or greasy meals. Practice stress management through meditation or tai chi, and maintain regular sleep to nourish Yin.

### Wind-Phlegm
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/wind-phlegm)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Severe dizziness or vertigo, sometimes with a spinning sensation, Numbness, tingling, or trembling in the hands and feet, Copious white or frothy sputum, rattling throat, Heavy, distending headache or feeling of heaviness in the body, Chest stuffiness or epigastric bloating, Slurred or difficult speech
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Damp, cold weather, Greasy, sweet, or dairy-rich foods, Sedentary lifestyle, Overeating, Emotional stress
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Light, easily digestible meals, Warm, dry environment, Gentle movement or exercise, Acupuncture and herbal treatment
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When the Spleen is weak and fails to transform fluids, dampness accumulates into phlegm. This phlegm then combines with internal Wind, often stirred up by the Liver, and the mixture rises to obstruct the channels and orifices of the head and limbs. The result is the sudden, heavy dizziness, numbness, and slurred speech that serve as a warning that a full-blown stroke may be approaching.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: normal or slightly pale body, swollen with teeth marks, white greasy coating. Pulse: wiry and slippery (Xian Hua).
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Wind-phlegm forms when a weak Spleen fails to transform fluids, creating dampness that congeals into phlegm. Greasy, sweet, and cold foods further burden the Spleen and feed the phlegm, while a sedentary lifestyle allows dampness to accumulate. Stress and emotional upset stir the Liver, generating the internal Wind that sweeps phlegm upward. Warming, easily digested meals and gentle movement support the Spleen, dry dampness, and keep Qi moving, while acupuncture and herbs directly expel wind and resolve phlegm to calm the warning signs.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Avoid damp-producing foods like dairy, greasy fried dishes, and excessive sweets or raw cold items. Eat warm, cooked meals with aromatic spices such as ginger and cardamom to help the Spleen transform dampness. Gentle daily movement like walking or Tai Chi keeps Qi flowing and prevents phlegm stagnation. Stay in a dry, warm environment and avoid prolonged exposure to dampness.

### Empty-Wind agitating in the Interior
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/empty-wind-agitating-in-the-interior)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Trembling or twitching of hands and feet, Severe fatigue and listlessness, Dry mouth and throat, Night sweats, Dizziness and blurred vision, Transient speech difficulties
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Overwork and lack of sleep, Spicy, greasy or fried foods, Emotional stress, Hot weather
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Rest and sleep, Cool, quiet environment, Hydrating foods (pears, congee), Gentle movement or exercise
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When the body's Yin-the cooling, nourishing foundation-is severely depleted, it can no longer anchor the Yang. This allows internal Wind to stir, rising upward and disturbing the brain's orifices. The result is dizziness, transient speech difficulties, or fleeting numbness that come and go, all set against a backdrop of deep exhaustion and dryness.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: crimson, thin, dry, cracked, with little or no coating (mirror-like). Pulse: fine, rapid, and wiry at the left guan position, lacking force on deep palpation.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Yin deficiency means the body lacks cooling, nourishing fluids. Overwork, stress, and spicy food generate heat that consumes Yin further, stirring internal Wind. Rest, cooling foods, and a calm environment preserve Yin and allow the body to anchor Yang, calming the wind and reducing pre-stroke symptoms.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Prioritize rest and avoid overwork, as exhaustion deepens Yin deficiency. Eat moistening, cooling foods like pears, tofu, black sesame, and congee. Avoid spicy, fried, and drying foods that consume Yin. Gentle activities like tai chi or walking are better than intense exercise. Keep the bedroom cool and dark to support restful sleep.

### Qi Deficiency causing Blood Stagnation
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/qi-deficiency-causing-blood-stagnation)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Persistent tiredness and lack of strength, Fixed, dull pain or numbness that improves with light movement, Shortness of breath on mild exertion and reluctance to speak, Pale or dusky complexion, sometimes with a greyish tinge, Limb heaviness or weakness, often one-sided
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Overwork and lack of sleep, Cold, raw foods, Prolonged standing or sitting still, Emotional stress
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Gentle movement or exercise, Warm, cooked meals, Rest and sleep, Light massage of affected limbs
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Qi is the body's vital force that pushes blood through the vessels. When Qi becomes weak, it lacks the strength to keep blood moving smoothly. The sluggish circulation allows blood to pool and stagnate, especially in the delicate vessels of the brain. This creates a risk of blockage, producing the fleeting numbness, weakness, or dizziness that warn of a possible stroke.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale body with a purplish or dark tinge, stasis spots, teeth marks on the sides, distended veins beneath. Pulse: deep (沉 chén), fine or weak (细 ruò), and choppy (涩 sè) as if hesitating under the finger.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Qi deficiency means the body's engine is underpowered. Overwork drains it further, while rest rebuilds it. Cold and raw foods chill the digestive fire that makes Qi, and inactivity allows blood to pool. Gentle movement, warmth, and nourishing foods supply the energy needed to push blood through the vessels, directly counteracting the pattern's root cause.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat warm, cooked foods like soups and stews with ginger, small amounts of lean protein, and dark leafy greens to gently nourish Qi and blood. Avoid icy drinks, raw salads, and greasy foods that chill digestion and promote stagnation. Gentle daily walking or tai chi helps keep blood moving without exhausting the system.

### Phlegm-Heat
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/phlegm-heat)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Dizziness with a heavy, foggy sensation, Bitter taste in the mouth, Constipation with foul breath, Thick yellow greasy tongue coating, Restlessness and irritability, Chest oppression and epigastric fullness
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Rich, greasy meals, Spicy food and alcohol, Emotional stress, Irregular eating habits
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Light, easily digestible meals, Regular bowel movements, Stress reduction, Gentle movement or exercise
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Phlegm-Heat builds up when rich, greasy foods and emotional stress disrupt the Spleen and Stomach. The resulting sticky Phlegm combines with Heat, obstructing the bowels and disturbing the clear orifices of the head. This creates a dangerous internal environment where dizziness, bitter taste and constipation act as warning signs that the turbid Phlegm-Heat may soon rise and block brain vessels, triggering a major stroke.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red body, possibly swollen, with a thick yellow greasy coating. Pulse: slippery (滑 huá) and rapid (数 shuò).
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Phlegm-Heat arises when the Spleen and Stomach are overwhelmed by heavy, greasy, and spicy foods, or by emotional stress that disrupts the smooth flow of Qi. These triggers create a sticky, hot internal environment that rises to disturb the head. Light, cooling foods and regular bowel movements help drain Phlegm-Heat downward, while stress reduction and gentle movement keep Qi flowing and prevent the buildup that can precipitate a stroke.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Avoid greasy, fried, sweet, and spicy foods that generate Phlegm and Heat. Eat plenty of lightly cooked vegetables, mung beans, and cooling fruits like pear. Drink warm water throughout the day. Regular, gentle exercise like walking helps Qi move and prevents stagnation. Stress management is essential, as emotional upset can fan the flames of Heat.

## คำถามที่พบบ่อย (Frequently Asked Questions)
### Can acupuncture and herbs really prevent a stroke?
Yes, when the warning signs are caught early and the correct pattern is treated. TCM works by calming internal Wind, resolving Phlegm, nourishing Yin, and moving Blood - essentially removing the conditions that make a stroke likely. Many patients find that their transient dizziness, numbness, and other symptoms fade as their underlying pattern is corrected. However, TCM is a complement to, not a replacement for, conventional stroke prevention measures like blood pressure control and antiplatelet medications.

### I had a TIA - how soon should I start TCM treatment?
As soon as possible. The period after a TIA is a window of opportunity to address the root imbalance before a full stroke occurs. A TCM practitioner will evaluate your tongue, pulse, and symptom pattern and begin treatment right away, often while you continue your prescribed Western medications. The earlier the intervention, the better the long-term outcome.

### Will I have to stop my blood thinners or blood pressure medication?
No, you should never stop prescribed medications on your own. TCM herbal formulas can often be used alongside conventional drugs, but certain herbs - especially those that invigorate Blood like Dang Gui, Chuan Xiong, and Dan Shen - may interact with anticoagulants like warfarin or clopidogrel. Always inform both your TCM practitioner and your prescribing doctor about everything you are taking so they can adjust doses safely.

### What foods should I avoid to lower my stroke risk?
In general, TCM advises avoiding greasy, fried, and heavily spiced foods that create Phlegm and Heat - the very substances that can clog the channels and stir internal Wind. Reduce alcohol, caffeine, and salt. Instead, favor light, easily digestible meals with plenty of cooked vegetables, whole grains like millet, and small amounts of lean protein. Your practitioner may give you more specific guidance based on your pattern.

### What does a TCM practitioner look for on my tongue?
The tongue is a map of your internal landscape. A red tongue with a yellow coating points to Heat and Liver Yang rising. A puffy tongue with a thick, greasy coating suggests Phlegm-Dampness. A pale, purplish tongue with stasis spots indicates Qi deficiency and Blood stagnation. These signs, together with the pulse, confirm which pre-stroke pattern is active and guide the choice of herbs and acupuncture points.

### Are the warning signs different in TCM compared to Western medicine?
The symptoms themselves - dizziness, numbness, weakness, speech trouble - are the same, but TCM pays closer attention to the accompanying sensations and triggers. For example, a headache that pounds with stress and makes you irritable is a Liver Yang sign, while a heavy, foggy dizziness that worsens after a rich meal is Phlegm. These details matter because they point to entirely different treatments.

## การวิจัยทางวิทยาศาสตร์สมัยใหม่ (Modern Scientific Research)
1. **Randomized trial of acupuncture to lower blood pressure** ([ลิงก์](https://doi.org/10.1161/CIRCULATIONAHA.106.661140))
   *วารสารวิชาการ: Flachskampf FA, Gallasch J, Gefeller O, et al. Circulation. 2007;115(24):3121-3129.*
   This randomized controlled trial assigned 160 patients with uncomplicated hypertension to either a course of active acupuncture or sham acupuncture. After six weeks, the active acupuncture group showed a significant reduction in both systolic and diastolic blood pressure compared to sham, with a mean 24-hour ambulatory blood pressure decrease of 6.4/3.7 mmHg. The study suggests acupuncture can be an effective adjunct for managing hypertension, a major risk factor for stroke.

2. **Tianma Gouteng Yin as adjunctive treatment for essential hypertension: a systematic review and meta-analysis** ([ลิงก์](https://doi.org/10.1155/2015/706123))
   *วารสารวิชาการ: Wang J, Xiong X, Liu W. Evid Based Complement Alternat Med. 2015;2015:706123.*
   This meta-analysis included 18 randomized controlled trials with 1,578 patients and evaluated the effect of Tianma Gouteng Yin, a classic formula for Liver Yang Rising, added to conventional antihypertensives. The formula significantly reduced systolic and diastolic blood pressure and improved clinical symptoms such as dizziness and headache compared to conventional treatment alone. The evidence quality was moderate, highlighting the need for larger, well-designed trials.
