## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนปัจจุบัน (Conventional Medicine View)
### คำอธิบาย (Description)
Skin erosion refers to a shallow loss of the surface layer of skin (the epidermis). It often appears as a raw, moist, or crusted area that may ooze clear fluid or bleed slightly. Erosions can result from many conditions - eczema, contact dermatitis, intertrigo, fungal or bacterial infections, or even friction and scratching.
Conventional diagnosis is usually made by looking at the skin and taking a history. Sometimes a swab or biopsy is done to rule out infection or autoimmune blistering diseases. Treatment focuses on protecting the area, controlling inflammation, and addressing any underlying cause.

### การรักษาหลัก (Treatments)
Standard care includes keeping the area clean and dry, applying barrier creams or ointments, and using topical corticosteroids to reduce inflammation. If infection is present, topical or oral antibiotics, antifungals, or antivirals may be prescribed. For chronic conditions like eczema, calcineurin inhibitors or systemic immune-modulating drugs are sometimes used. Wet dressings or medicated baths can help soothe and dry oozing erosions.

## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนจีน (Traditional Chinese Medicine View)
### ความเข้าใจเกี่ยวกับการแพทย์แผนจีน (TCM Understanding)
In TCM, the skin is seen as an extension of the internal organs, nourished by Blood and Qi and protected by the body's defensive energy (Wei Qi). When internal harmony is disturbed, the skin is often the first place problems show up. Skin erosion specifically points to a breakdown in the body's ability to manage fluids and nourish the surface.
The Spleen is the key organ here - it transforms food and fluids into usable energy and moisture. If the Spleen is weak, it fails to handle fluids properly, and dampness accumulates inside. That dampness can seep out through the skin, causing oozing, weeping erosions that are slow to heal. If the dampness combines with Heat (from spicy food, stress, or infection), the erosion becomes red, hot, and intensely inflamed.
But not all erosions are wet. When the body's Blood or Yin (the deep cooling and moistening energy) is deficient, the skin loses its nourishment and becomes dry, cracked, and fragile. This type of erosion itches intensely and heals poorly because there simply isn't enough moisture and building material to repair it. Meanwhile, emotional stress can knot up the Liver's Qi, causing Blood to stagnate - leading to dark, purplish erosions with fixed, stabbing pain.
So the same Western symptom - a patch of eroded skin - can be caused by Damp-Heat, Spleen Deficiency, Blood Deficiency, Qi and Blood Stagnation, or Kidney and Liver Yin Deficiency. Each pattern has its own tongue appearance, pulse quality, and accompanying symptoms, which is why TCM treatment is always tailored to the individual, not the skin lesion alone.

### การวินิจฉัยโดยแพทย์แผนจีน (Practitioner Diagnosis)
A TCM practitioner begins by asking about the onset and appearance of the erosion. Is it acute, red, and wet, or chronic, pale, and dry? The quality of the oozing, the presence of vesicles, and the degree of itching or pain are the first clues that point toward one pattern rather than another.
If the skin is bright red, swollen, and weeping copiously, and the person feels hot and thirsty with a bitter taste in the mouth, the pattern is likely Damp-Heat. The tongue will often have a yellow, greasy coating and the pulse will feel slippery and rapid, confirming an excess condition that needs clearing and draining.
When the erosion is more subacute or chronic, with pale, slightly oozing lesions and accompanying digestive complaints like bloating and loose stools, the picture shifts to Spleen Deficiency with Dampness. Here the tongue is pale and puffy with a white, greasy coat, and the pulse is soft, showing that the body is failing to transform fluids properly.
A dry, cracked erosion with intense itching that worsens at night points to Blood Deficiency with External Wind. The skin may be rough and scaly, and the person often has a pale complexion, dizziness, or dry nails. The tongue is pale with a thin coat, and the pulse is thin, indicating that blood is not nourishing the skin and wind has stirred up.
Dark red or purplish lesions that are thick, painful, or stubbornly itchy suggest Qi and Blood Stagnation. Emotional stress, menstrual irregularities, or a history of trauma often accompany this pattern. The tongue may appear dark purple, and the pulse feels wiry or choppy, reflecting obstructed flow in the channels.
If the erosion appears in the genital area or lower limbs and is accompanied by night sweats, tinnitus, or lower back soreness, the practitioner considers Kidney and Liver Yin Deficiency. The tongue is red with little or no coating, and the pulse is thin and rapid, revealing a deep lack of cooling, moistening yin fluids.

## กลุ่มอาการการแพทย์แผนจีนและการรักษา (TCM Patterns and Treatment)

### Damp-Heat
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/damp-heat)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Red, swollen skin with oozing and vesicles, Thick, greasy yellow tongue coating, Heavy, sluggish body sensation, Burning or stinging pain at the erosion site, Poor appetite, nausea, or loose sticky stools
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Spicy, greasy, or fried foods, Alcohol, Hot, humid weather, Scratching or rubbing the skin
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Light, easily digested meals, Cooling compresses, Keeping the skin dry and clean, Rest in a cool, dry environment
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When Dampness and Heat combine in the body, they behave like sticky, hot steam that rises and saturates the skin. The Heat brings redness, swelling, and a burning sensation, while the Dampness creates oozing, vesicles, and a heavy, wet feeling. This pathogenic pair clogs the skin’s pores and channels, causing the tissue to break down into the raw, moist erosions typical of this pattern.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red body with a yellow, greasy coating that is thickest in the centre and root, and may be swollen with teeth marks. Pulse: slippery and rapid (Hua Shu), or soggy and rapid (Ru Shu).
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Damp-Heat is like a sticky, hot steam inside the body. Spicy, greasy foods and alcohol generate more internal Heat and Dampness, directly feeding the condition. Hot, humid weather adds external Damp-Heat, overwhelming the skin. Scratching irritates the skin, spreads Heat, and breaks the tissue further. Cooling, bland foods and a dry environment help drain Dampness and clear Heat, giving the skin a chance to heal. Keeping the area dry and clean prevents external dampness from accumulating and worsening the oozing.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Avoid spicy, greasy, and fried foods, as well as alcohol and sugar-they add fuel to the Damp-Heat fire. Instead, eat cooling, damp-draining foods like mung beans, cucumber, watermelon, and barley. Keep the affected skin clean and dry, and resist the urge to scratch, which spreads heat and damages tissue further.

### Spleen Deficiency with Dampness
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/spleen-deficiency-with-dampness)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Oozing, weeping lesions, Pale or normal skin color around erosion, Abdominal bloating and loose stools, Heaviness of the body and limbs, Fatigue and poor appetite
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Damp, humid environments, Overeating or rich, greasy food, Raw and cold foods, Overwork and fatigue, Worry and overthinking
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warm, dry weather, Light, easily digested meals, Gentle exercise, Elevating the affected limb
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Your Spleen is in charge of transforming food and fluids into usable energy. When it is weakened, it cannot manage moisture properly, allowing Dampness to build up inside your body. This excess dampness seeps out into the skin, creating the pale, oozing, and weeping erosions that are slow to heal.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: Pale, swollen body with teeth marks and a white, greasy coating. Pulse: Soggy (Ru) and slowed-down (Huan).
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: The Spleen is easily damaged by cold, raw foods, and mental overwork, which impair its ability to manage fluids. Damp environments add external dampness to the internal dampness, making the skin weep more. Warmth, gentle movement, and easy-to-digest foods support the Spleen's function, helping it dry out the excess moisture.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat warm, cooked foods like soups and stews. Avoid raw salads, cold drinks, dairy, and greasy foods which overwhelm the Spleen and create more dampness. Incorporate gentle daily movement to help your body process fluids.

### Blood Deficiency with External Wind
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/blood-deficiency-with-external-wind)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Dry, cracked erosions with little or no oozing, Intense itching that worsens at night, Pale or sallow complexion, Brittle nails and dry, rough hair, Dizziness or light-headedness, Aversion to wind or drafts
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Windy or dry weather, Spicy, greasy, or fried foods, Overwork and fatigue, Excessive blood loss (e.g., heavy periods)
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Moisturizing and hydrating skincare, Eating warm, nourishing foods, Adequate sleep and rest, Gentle exercise
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When Blood is deficient, the skin loses its nourishment and becomes dry, rough, and fragile. This internal dryness generates an internal Wind, which manifests as intense itching that comes and goes. The skin cracks easily, and those cracks can deepen into dry, non-oozing erosions that are slow to heal because the underlying nourishment is missing.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale and thin body with a thin white and slightly dry coating. Pulse: fine (thin), wiry at the left middle position, and floating at the right front position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Blood Deficiency means the skin is undernourished, so dryness and wind arise. Windy, dry environments and spicy foods worsen the internal dryness and stir up wind, intensifying itching and cracking. Rest, nourishing foods, and moisturizing help replenish Blood and fluids, calming the wind and allowing the skin to heal. Overwork and heavy blood loss further deplete Blood, aggravating the root deficiency.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat blood-nourishing foods like dark leafy greens, beets, eggs, liver, black sesame, and goji berries. Avoid spicy, dry, or overly heating foods that consume fluids. Protect skin from wind and dry environments, and use gentle, fragrance-free moisturizers. Gentle exercise like walking or tai chi supports Blood circulation without exhausting Qi.

### Qi And Blood Stagnation
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/qi-and-blood-stagnation)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Dark red or purplish skin lesions, Fixed, stabbing pain that worsens with pressure, Thickened, rough skin around the erosion, Irritability, mood swings, or depression, Distending pain in the chest or rib area
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Emotional stress and frustration, Spicy, greasy, or fried foods, Sedentary lifestyle, Alcohol
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Gentle exercise, Stress reduction, Warm compress, Light, easily digested meals
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When emotional stress or frustration knots up the Liver's Qi flow, the blood can no longer circulate smoothly. Over time this stagnation creates dark, purplish patches in the skin where blood has pooled. The trapped blood irritates the tissues, producing erosion that feels fixed and stabbing, and the skin thickens as the body tries to wall off the stuck energy.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: purple or dark body with scattered stasis spots, distended sublingual veins, and a thin white coat. Pulse: wiry (弦 xián) and choppy (涩 sè), like a taut guitar string scraping bamboo.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Emotional tension knots the Liver Qi, which is the root cause of blood stasis in this pattern. A sedentary lifestyle allows blood to pool, while greasy and spicy foods create dampness and heat that further obstruct circulation. Gentle movement and stress relief help unwind the Qi and get blood moving again, reducing the dark, painful lesions.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat lightly and avoid greasy, spicy, or deep-fried foods that can gum up the blood. Gentle daily movement like walking or stretching keeps Qi and blood flowing. Practice a calming routine-stress is the main driver of this pattern.

### Kidney and Liver Yin Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/kidney-and-liver-yin-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Erosion mainly on genitals or lower limbs, Dry mouth and throat, especially at night, Night sweats and heat in palms, soles, and chest, Dizziness, tinnitus, or sore lower back
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Spicy, greasy, or fried foods, Overwork and fatigue, Emotional stress and frustration, Dry, heated indoor air
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Cool, moist environment, Moistening foods (pears, congee), Adequate sleep and rest, Moisturizing and hydrating skincare
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Your Liver and Kidneys are the body's deep reservoirs of moisture and nourishment. When their Yin (cooling, moistening energy) runs low over time, the skin loses its natural hydration and becomes dry, thin, and fragile. This is especially true in the lower body and genital area, where the Liver and Kidney channels travel. Without enough Yin fluid to protect and repair the skin, even minor friction can cause erosion. The deficiency heat that often accompanies Yin depletion further dries and damages the area, leaving it prone to cracking and slow-healing sores.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red, thin body with cracks, little or no coating, dry surface. Pulse: fine (thin), rapid, and often wiry; weak at the left Chi (Kidney) position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Yin is the body's cooling, moistening foundation. Anything that generates heat or consumes fluids-like spicy food, anger, or staying up late-further depletes Yin and worsens the dryness and erosion. Conversely, rest, cooling foods, and hydration help preserve and rebuild Yin, allowing the skin to heal and stay supple.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Focus on foods that build Yin fluids, such as pears, honey, black sesame, goji berries, and congee. Avoid spicy, fried, or drying foods, as well as alcohol and coffee. Keep the skin gently moisturized with natural emollients and avoid harsh soaps. Prioritise restful sleep and manage emotional stress, as frustration and overwork burn through Yin reserves.

## คำถามที่พบบ่อย (Frequently Asked Questions)
### Can TCM help with oozing, wet skin erosions?
Yes - this is one of TCM's strengths. Oozing erosions are typically a sign of Dampness or Damp-Heat. Herbal formulas like Long Dan Xie Gan Tang drain dampness and clear heat from the inside, while external washes with herbs like Ku Shen (Sophora root) can dry and soothe the skin directly. Most people see a reduction in weeping within the first two weeks.

### What does it mean if my skin erosion is dry and itchy rather than wet?
Dry, cracked, intensely itchy erosions usually point to Blood Deficiency with internal Wind, or sometimes Kidney and Liver Yin Deficiency. The skin lacks nourishment and moisture, so it becomes fragile and itchy. Treatment focuses on nourishing Blood and Yin with formulas like Dang Gui Yin Zi or Zuo Gui Yin, combined with moisturizing acupuncture points. Itching often improves before the skin fully heals.

### How long until I see results from TCM for skin erosion?
Many patients notice less oozing and itching within 2-4 weeks. The skin itself may take longer to close and strengthen - usually 1-3 months for acute patterns, and 3-6 months or more for chronic, deficiency-based patterns. Consistency with herbs and dietary changes makes a big difference in speed.

### Can I use my steroid cream alongside Chinese herbs?
In most cases, yes. Topical steroids can be used to manage flares while the herbs work on the internal imbalance. Always inform both your TCM practitioner and your dermatologist about all treatments you're using. As the skin improves, you and your doctor may be able to taper the steroid under supervision - never stop suddenly on your own.

### Does diet really matter for skin erosion?
Absolutely. In TCM, diet is a primary source of internal Dampness and Heat. Greasy, fried, spicy, and sugary foods, as well as dairy and alcohol, can directly worsen oozing erosions. Eating warm, easily digested foods like congee, steamed vegetables, and moderate amounts of lean protein helps the Spleen function better and reduces dampness. Many patients see a clear difference within days of changing their diet.

### Is acupuncture safe on or near open skin?
Acupuncture needles are rarely inserted directly into an open erosion. Instead, your practitioner will use points on the limbs, back, and abdomen to treat the internal pattern - for example, points like Quchi LI-11 and Yinlingquan SP-9 for damp-heat, or Zusanli ST-36 and Sanyinjiao SP-6 for deficiency. This is both safe and effective, and avoids irritating the lesion.

### Will my skin erosion come back after TCM treatment?
TCM aims to correct the underlying imbalance, so recurrence is less likely than with treatments that only suppress symptoms. However, if you return to a lifestyle that generates a lot of dampness and heat (poor diet, chronic stress, lack of sleep), the pattern can re-emerge. Many people choose periodic maintenance sessions or seasonal tune-ups to stay balanced.

1. **Chinese herbal medicine for atopic eczema** ([ลิงก์](https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD008642.pub2))
   *วารสารวิชาการ: Zhang W, et al. Chinese herbal medicine for atopic eczema. Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews. 2013.*
   This Cochrane review assessed randomized controlled trials of Chinese herbal medicine for atopic eczema. While some individual trials showed benefit, the overall evidence was limited by small sample sizes and high risk of bias, preventing firm conclusions.

2. **Efficacy and safety of Chinese herbal medicine for atopic dermatitis: a systematic review and meta-analysis** ([ลิงก์](https://doi.org/10.1089/acm.2012.0159))
   *วารสารวิชาการ: Gu S, et al. Efficacy and safety of Chinese herbal medicine for atopic dermatitis: a systematic review and meta-analysis. Journal of Alternative and Complementary Medicine. 2013.*
   This meta-analysis of randomized controlled trials found that Chinese herbal medicine significantly improved symptoms of atopic dermatitis compared to placebo or conventional medications, with a favorable safety profile.

   *วารสารวิชาการ: Lee JH, et al. Acupuncture for atopic dermatitis: a systematic review and meta-analysis. Evidence-Based Complementary and Alternative Medicine. 2016.*
   This systematic review concluded that acupuncture is an effective treatment for atopic dermatitis, reducing itching and lesion severity, though the authors called for larger, more rigorous trials.
