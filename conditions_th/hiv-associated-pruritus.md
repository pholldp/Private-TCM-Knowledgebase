## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนปัจจุบัน (Conventional Medicine View)
### คำอธิบาย (Description)
Conventional medicine understands HIV-associated pruritus as a common and often distressing symptom. It can stem from several causes: the direct effect of HIV on the immune system, opportunistic skin infections, medication side effects, dry skin (xerosis), or systemic conditions like kidney or liver dysfunction that are more common in people living with HIV. Diagnosis usually involves a physical exam, a review of current medications, and sometimes blood tests or a skin biopsy to rule out infections or other dermatological conditions. Treatment is typically aimed at the presumed cause - antihistamines for allergic reactions, antifungals or antibiotics for infections, and emollients for dry skin - but often the itch persists despite these measures.

### การรักษาหลัก (Treatments)
Standard treatment typically begins with topical emollients and oral antihistamines. If a specific skin infection is identified, antifungal, antibacterial, or antiviral creams or pills are prescribed. For more severe or persistent cases, doctors may use corticosteroids, either topical or systemic. When pruritus is linked to a specific medication, adjusting the antiretroviral regimen is considered. Phototherapy is sometimes used for widespread itching that does not respond to other treatments.

## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนจีน (Traditional Chinese Medicine View)
### ความเข้าใจเกี่ยวกับการแพทย์แผนจีน (TCM Understanding)
TCM understands pruritus in HIV as a sign of deeper internal disharmony, most often involving the Blood, the Spleen, and the Liver.
The skin is seen as an extension of the body's internal environment - when the Blood is deficient, the skin becomes dry and undernourished, leading to a fine, shifting itch. When the Spleen is too weak to transform fluids, Dampness accumulates and can combine with Heat to create red, oozing, burning eruptions. The Liver, which governs the smooth flow of Qi and stores the Blood, is also central: emotional stress or long-term illness can cause Liver Qi to stagnate, generating internal Wind that manifests as an unpredictable, moving itch.
In HIV, the underlying Wei Qi (defensive Qi) is often compromised, making the skin more vulnerable to external pathogens like Wind-Heat, which can trigger sudden, red, itchy wheals. This is why the same diagnosis of HIV-associated pruritus can present so differently from person to person - the skin is a mirror reflecting a unique combination of deficiencies (Blood, Qi, Yin) and excesses (Dampness, Heat, Wind).
This is why TCM practitioners pay close attention to the quality of the itch: a dry, night-time itch with pale skin points to Blood Deficiency; a persistent, low-grade itch with crushing fatigue suggests Qi and Blood Deficiency; an intermittent itch with thirst and night sweats indicates Qi and Yin Deficiency; and a burning, red, oozing rash points to Damp-Heat. Each pattern has its own distinct tongue and pulse presentation, and each requires a different treatment strategy.

### การวินิจฉัยโดยแพทย์แผนจีน (Practitioner Diagnosis)
A TCM practitioner begins by looking at the skin itself and asking about the timing and quality of the itch. Dry, flaky skin that worsens at night points toward Blood Deficiency with External Wind, whereas red, oozing, burning eruptions suggest Damp-Heat. The tongue and pulse are then checked to confirm the underlying pattern.
When the skin is very dry, pale, and scaly, with a pale tongue and a thin or wiry-thready pulse, the root is Blood Deficiency failing to nourish the skin, allowing external Wind to invade. The itch is often worse at night and improves with moisturizing. Dizziness and a pale face may accompany it.
If the itch is persistent but low-grade, and the person feels profoundly tired with a pale complexion, the picture is Qi and Blood Deficiency. The tongue is pale with a thin white coat, and the pulse is weak or thready. The skin may be slightly dry but not as scaly as in pure Blood Deficiency with Wind.
An intermittent itch with dry mouth, thirst, and poor appetite, alongside a pale tongue with little coating, indicates Qi and Yin Deficiency. This pattern often arises from long-term illness or medication use. The pulse is thready and may be rapid or weak, reflecting the dual depletion of energy and fluids.
Red, moist, or weeping lesions that feel hot and burn intensely signal Damp-Heat. The tongue is red with a thick, greasy yellow coat, and the pulse is slippery and rapid. This pattern is more inflammatory and acute-feeling than the deficiency patterns, and the itch is often accompanied by a heavy sensation.
Purplish, dark, or stubbornly thickened plaques that itch with a stabbing pain suggest Blood Stagnation. The tongue may be dark with purple spots, and the pulse choppy or wiry. This type of itch resists simple anti-itch remedies and is more common in advanced or chronic cases.
A sudden outbreak of red wheals that appear quickly, often after a new drug or infection, points to an acute Wind-Heat invasion. The tongue is red with a thin yellow coat, and the pulse is floating and rapid. The itch is intense but tends to come and go more abruptly than the chronic patterns.

### Blood Deficiency with External Wind
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/blood-deficiency-with-external-wind)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Itching that shifts location and comes and goes, Dry, flaky, or rough skin, Worse at night, Aversion to wind or drafts, Pale or sallow complexion, Dizziness or light-headedness
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Exposure to wind or drafts, Dry environment, Nighttime, Spicy, fried, or drying foods, Overwork or overexertion, Blood loss (e.g., heavy menses)
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Moisturising the skin, Eating blood-nourishing foods, Gentle movement, such as Tai Chi or walking, Avoiding drafts, Warmth and adequate rest
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Chronic HIV gradually depletes the body's Blood, leaving the skin undernourished and dry. This dryness generates internal Wind, and because the surface is weak, external Wind easily invades. The result is a shifting, parched itch that worsens at night and feels worse in drafts.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale, thin body with a thin white and slightly dry coating. Pulse: fine (thin) overall, wiry at the left middle position (Liver), and often floating at the right front position (Lung/Exterior).
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Blood Deficiency leaves skin dry and defenceless, so wind and dryness easily provoke itching. Nighttime is Yin time, and Blood belongs to Yin, so the deficiency is felt most then. Overwork and blood loss further deplete Blood. Nourishing foods and moisturising replenish Blood and Yin, while avoiding drafts and resting protect the surface. Gentle movement circulates Blood without draining it, helping to calm the internal Wind.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat blood-nourishing foods like dark leafy greens, bone broth, eggs, and moderate amounts of red meat. Avoid drying, spicy, or fried foods and limit alcohol. Keep skin well-moisturised and protect it from wind and drafts. Gentle daily movement like Tai Chi or walking helps Blood flow without exhausting your reserves.

### Qi and Blood Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/qi-and-blood-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Persistent, low-grade generalized itch, Worse with exertion or at night, Profound fatigue and weakness, Pale, sallow complexion, Dizziness and heart palpitations, Poor appetite
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Overwork or overexertion, Raw, cold foods and drinks, Stress and worry, Excessive sweating
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Adequate rest and sleep, Warm, nourishing foods, Gentle movement, such as Tai Chi or walking, Moxibustion
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  HIV gradually depletes the body's Qi and Blood, the fundamental substances that nourish and moisten every tissue. When the Spleen cannot produce enough Qi and Blood, the skin loses its nourishment, becoming dry, pale, and papery. Without sufficient Blood to anchor the mind, the persistent low-grade itch is often accompanied by fatigue, dizziness, and a sallow complexion.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale body, may be thin, with a thin white coating. Pulse: fine and weak (细弱, xì ruò), lacking force.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Qi and Blood are produced by the Spleen from the food we eat. Warm, cooked foods and rest make this production efficient, while raw, cold foods and overwork put out the digestive fire. The itch subsides when the skin is finally moistened by newly generated Blood; moxibustion and acupuncture on ST-36 and SP-6 directly stimulate this generation process, while stress and exhaustion burn through Qi faster than the body can replenish it.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Focus on warm, easily digestible, and nourishing foods like bone broths, congees with goji berries and red dates, and gently cooked dark leafy greens. Avoid raw, cold foods and overwork; gentle walking and regular rest are better than intense exercise, which can further deplete Qi.

### Qi and Yin Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/qi-and-yin-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Intermittent itching, often worse at night, Dry skin with fine scaling, Dry mouth and throat, thirst with little desire to drink, Night sweats and warm palms, soles, or chest, Fatigue, shortness of breath, and poor appetite
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Overwork or overexertion, Spicy, fried, or drying foods, Late nights and insufficient sleep, Stress and worry
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Adequate rest and sleep, Moistening foods (pears, congee), Gentle movement, such as Tai Chi or walking, Cool, humid environment
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Chronic HIV infection and the long-term use of medications gradually deplete the body's Qi (vital energy) and Yin (cooling, moistening fluids). When Qi is too weak to nourish the skin and Yin fluids run dry, the skin loses its natural moisture and becomes under-nourished, leading to a dry, intermittent itch. The mild Empty Heat that arises from Yin deficiency can also cause night sweats and a subtle warm sensation, making the itch more noticeable when the body is at rest.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale-red or pale body with a thin or absent coating, possibly cracked and dry. Pulse: fine (thin) and weak, may be slightly rapid.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Qi and Yin are like the body's fuel and coolant. Overwork, stress, and lack of sleep burn through them, leaving the skin dry and itchy. Spicy and fried foods add heat that consumes Yin, making the itch worse. Rest, moistening foods, and gentle activity help replenish Qi and Yin, cooling the mild empty heat and restoring the skin's nourishment.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat small, frequent, easily digested meals like congee, soups, and steamed vegetables. Include moistening foods such as pears, honey, and lily bulb. Avoid spicy, fried, or drying foods that consume Yin. Prioritize rest and avoid overwork; gentle practices like Tai Chi or Qi Gong can help conserve Qi.

### Damp-Heat
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/damp-heat)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Red, weeping, burning skin lesions or vesicles, Body feels heavy and sluggish, Chest and upper abdominal stuffiness, Sticky or bitter taste in the mouth, Loose, sticky stools that feel incomplete, Dark yellow, scanty urine
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Hot, humid weather, Greasy, spicy, or fried foods, Alcohol, Stress and frustration, Sedentary lifestyle
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Cool, dry weather, Light, non-greasy meals, Cooling foods (cucumber, mung beans), Gentle movement, such as Tai Chi or walking
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When the body fails to transform fluids properly, Dampness accumulates and combines with Heat-often from medications, secondary infections, or a rich, greasy diet. This Damp-Heat stews internally and then erupts outward into the skin, creating red, oozing, burning lesions that itch intensely. The sticky, heavy nature of Dampness makes the itching cling and worsen in humid conditions, while the Heat adds redness and a prickling sensation.

Manage stress with breathing or quiet time, as emotional strain tightens the Liver Qi and worsens stasis.

- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:

## คำถามที่พบบ่อย (Frequently Asked Questions)
### Can TCM cure my HIV-associated itching?
TCM can be very effective at managing and significantly reducing HIV-associated pruritus, but it is not a cure for the underlying HIV infection. The goal of TCM treatment is to correct the internal imbalances that cause the itch - whether that's nourishing deficient Blood, clearing Damp-Heat, or expelling Wind. Many patients find their itching resolves completely or becomes much milder, and this improvement can be long-lasting when the underlying pattern is addressed. However, because HIV is a chronic condition that can shift over time, occasional maintenance treatments may be needed.

### Is it safe to take Chinese herbs with my HIV medications?
Safety is the top priority. Many herbs can be used safely alongside antiretroviral therapy (ART), but there are known interactions with certain plants, particularly St. John's Wort, which can reduce the effectiveness of some HIV drugs. Always bring your complete medication list to your TCM practitioner, and inform your HIV doctor you are considering or using herbal medicine. A qualified TCM practitioner will select formulas that support your health without interfering with your life-saving medications. Never stop or adjust your ART without consulting your prescribing doctor.

### Will acupuncture make my itching worse?
Acupuncture is generally very safe and should not worsen your itching. In fact, many patients feel a soothing, cooling sensation during treatment for Damp-Heat or Wind-Heat patterns. Your practitioner will use points that calm the mind, clear heat, and stop itch. The needles are hair-thin and single-use, so infection risk is not a concern. If you have a very low platelet count, tell your practitioner so they can use gentler needle techniques.

### How many acupuncture sessions will I need?
For acute flare-ups of itching, one or two sessions a week for 2-4 weeks is a common starting point. For chronic, long-standing pruritus, especially from deficiency patterns, weekly sessions for 8-12 weeks are more typical. Your practitioner will track your progress and taper the frequency as your skin improves. Many patients transition to a maintenance schedule of once a month or once every other month once the itching is under control.

### What dietary changes can help my itching?
Diet plays a key role in TCM because food directly influences the body's internal environment. Generally, avoid foods that create Dampness and Heat, such as fried foods, alcohol, spicy dishes, and excessive sugar. Focus on easily digestible, nourishing foods like congee, steamed vegetables, and soups. If your skin is dry and scaly, add blood-nourishing foods like dark leafy greens, small amounts of organ meats, and black sesame seeds. If your rash is red and oozing, cooling foods like cucumber, mung beans, and watermelon are very helpful. Your TCM practitioner will give you specific guidance based on your pattern.

### Can I use TCM alongside antihistamines or steroid creams?
Yes, TCM is often used alongside conventional treatments. Many patients begin TCM while still using their prescribed antihistamines or topical steroids. As the underlying imbalance improves and the itching subsides, you can work with your doctor to safely reduce or taper these medications. Do not stop any prescribed medication abruptly without your doctor's supervision. Herbs with sedative properties should be used carefully with antihistamines to avoid excessive drowsiness.
