## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนปัจจุบัน (Conventional Medicine View)
### คำอธิบาย (Description)
Dementia is a progressive decline in cognitive function - memory, thinking, orientation, language, and judgment - that is severe enough to interfere with daily life. It is not a normal part of aging. Alzheimer’s disease accounts for the majority of cases, followed by vascular dementia, Lewy body dementia, and frontotemporal dementia. Diagnosis typically involves cognitive testing, brain imaging, and sometimes spinal fluid analysis to identify the underlying pathology.
Conventional medicine views dementia as a neurodegenerative process with no cure. Available medications aim to temporarily slow symptom progression by altering neurotransmitter levels, but they do not address the complex interplay of vascular, metabolic, and inflammatory factors that contribute to cognitive decline.

### การรักษาหลัก (Treatments)
Standard treatment includes cholinesterase inhibitors (donepezil, rivastigmine, galantamine) and the NMDA receptor antagonist memantine, which may modestly improve cognition or delay decline for a period. Management also focuses on controlling cardiovascular risk factors (hypertension, diabetes, high cholesterol), treating depression, and providing behavioral and environmental support. Caregiver education and safety planning are essential components.

## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนจีน (Traditional Chinese Medicine View)
### ความเข้าใจเกี่ยวกับการแพทย์แผนจีน (TCM Understanding)
In TCM, the brain is called the “sea of marrow” (髓海, suǐ hǎi). It is nourished by Kidney Essence, which produces marrow to fill the brain and support memory and clear thinking. When Kidney Essence declines - whether through aging, chronic illness, or overwork - the sea of marrow runs dry, and cognitive function fades. This is the most common root of dementia: a deep, slow emptiness that leaves the mind hollow and unfocused.
But the Kidneys are not the only players. The Spleen transforms food into Qi and Blood, which the brain also depends on. If the Spleen is weak, it fails to produce enough nourishment, or it allows dampness to accumulate into thick, turbid Phlegm. This Phlegm can rise to the head and physically cloud the mind’s orifices, creating a heavy, dull confusion - a different kind of cognitive decline altogether.
Long-standing emotional stress can also play a role. When the Liver’s smooth flow is obstructed, Qi stagnates and may turn into Fire, which flares upward to agitate the Heart’s spirit (Shen). This results in the restless, agitated, and sometimes aggressive behaviors seen in some forms of dementia. Meanwhile, poor circulation or a history of stroke can lead to Blood Stagnation, where sluggish blood obstructs the tiny vessels that feed the brain, causing a fixed and unremitting cognitive fog.
Because these mechanisms are so different, TCM does not treat all dementia the same way. A person whose memory loss feels like a hollow emptiness with weak knees and tinnitus needs deep Kidney nourishment. Someone with a heavy, phlegm-like fog and a greasy tongue coating needs to clear Phlegm and awaken the mind. A person with agitation, red face, and a rapid pulse needs to drain Fire. Recognizing the pattern is the key to choosing a treatment that truly fits.

### การวินิจฉัยโดยแพทย์แผนจีน (Practitioner Diagnosis)
A TCM practitioner begins by listening carefully to the story of the memory loss - when it started, how it feels, and what else has changed. The quality of the decline, along with physical signs and emotional shifts, reveals whether the root is a deficiency that starves the brain or an excess that blocks it. Tongue and pulse findings then confirm the pattern.
If the decline is gradual and accompanied by dizziness, tinnitus, and sore lower back, Kidney Essence Deficiency is likely. The tongue appears pale with a thin coat, and the pulse feels deep and weak. This pattern reflects a sea of marrow running dry, leaving the brain unsupported.
When apathy, fatigue, and loose stools join the memory loss, Spleen and Kidney Qi Deficiency is a stronger fit. The tongue is pale and puffy, and the pulse is soft and weak - signs that both organs are too depleted to generate the qi and essence the brain needs.
If poor concentration and forgetfulness come with a pale face and general weakness, Qi and Blood Deficiency may be the culprit. The tongue is pale and thin, and the pulse is fine and weak. Here the brain simply lacks the nourishment that rich, abundant blood should provide.
When confusion and mental dullness appear together with a heavy, greasy tongue coating and a slippery pulse, Phlegm Misting the Heart is the key. The person may drool or have a thick phlegmy sensation. This pattern points to turbid dampness clouding the mind’s clarity.
Fixed memory gaps, speech difficulties, and a dark or purplish tongue with stasis spots signal Blood Stagnation. The pulse feels choppy or wiry. In this case, chronic blockage in the brain’s tiny vessels prevents fresh nourishment from reaching the mind.
Agitation, insomnia, and a red tongue with yellow coating point to Heart Fire blazing. The pulse is rapid and forceful. Here emotional distress or liver fire has transmitted heat upward, disturbing the spirit and causing erratic behavior.
If irritability and restlessness dominate, with a bitter taste, red eyes, and a wiry, rapid pulse, Liver Fire Blazing is the likely pattern. Long-standing frustration has turned into fire that flares upward, harassing the mind and accelerating cognitive decline.

### Kidney Essence Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/kidney-essence-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Gradual memory decline and mental dullness, Soreness and weakness of the lower back and knees, Dizziness, tinnitus, or hearing loss, Premature greying or hair loss, loose teeth
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Overwork and chronic stress, Excessive sexual activity, Aging and prolonged illness
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Adequate rest and sleep, Warm, nourishing foods, Gentle exercise like walking
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  The Kidneys store our deepest Essence, which produces marrow to fill the brain. When this Essence runs low, the 'sea of marrow' becomes empty, and the brain loses its nourishment. That is why memory fades, thinking slows, and the mind feels hollow and dull.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale, thin body with little or no coating, possibly with fine cracks. Pulse: fine, deep, and weak, especially at both rear (Kidney) positions.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Kidney Essence is our deepest constitutional reserve; it naturally declines with age but is also drained by overwork, stress, and excessive sexual activity. Rest and nourishing foods help to slow the depletion and support the remaining Essence, while gentle movement keeps Qi flowing without further exhausting the Kidneys.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat foods that build Essence, such as black sesame, walnuts, goji berries, bone broth, and dark leafy greens. Avoid overwork and late nights, as these deplete the Kidneys. Gentle, regular movement like tai chi or walking supports the flow of Qi without draining reserves.

### Phlegm Misting the Heart
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/phlegm-misting-the-heart)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Mental dullness or confusion, Blank staring expression, Rattling or gurgling sound in the throat, Chest stuffiness and feeling of oppression, Nausea or urge to vomit, Slurred or unclear speech, Excessive drooling or saliva, Dizziness and heavy-headedness, Emotional flatness or apathy
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Raw, cold foods and drinks, Damp living space, Overeating or heavy meals, Sedentary lifestyle, Excessive worry
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Light, warm meals, Gentle daily exercise, Dry, airy environment, Mental stimulation, Ginger or cardamom tea
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When the Spleen is weak, it fails to transform fluids properly, and dampness collects into thick, turbid Phlegm. This Phlegm rises to the chest and head, clouding the Heart's orifices and obstructing the brain's connection to the mind. The result is a heavy mental fog, dulled thinking, and a sense of being cut off from the world.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale and swollen body with a thick, white, greasy coating (白腻, bái nì), sometimes stiff or sluggish. Pulse: slippery (滑, huá), like beads rolling under the fingers.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Phlegm arises when the Spleen is too weak to transform fluids, and dampness thickens into turbid obstruction. Cold and raw foods further weaken the Spleen's digestive fire, while damp environments add external moisture. Warm, light meals and gentle movement support the Spleen and help Qi move, which dries dampness and dissolves phlegm. Mental activity and dry air keep the orifices clear.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Avoid cold, raw, and greasy foods that create more dampness and phlegm. Focus on warm, cooked meals with light grains, soups, and small amounts of pungent spices like ginger. Gentle daily movement - such as walking or tai chi - helps Qi circulate and transforms dampness. Keep the living space dry and airy.

### Blood Stagnation
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/blood-stagnation)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Dark purple tongue with stasis spots, Easily startled or fearful, Dusky complexion and purple lips
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Sedentary lifestyle, Cold weather, Emotional suppression
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Gentle exercise, Warmth, Mental stimulation
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When Qi becomes sluggish or blocked over a long period, blood cannot flow smoothly. Stagnant blood then obstructs the tiny collaterals that nourish the brain, cutting off the supply of clear Yang and essence to the mind. This produces a fixed, stubborn cognitive decline that does not fluctuate much, often after a stroke, head injury, or long-standing illness.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: dark purple body with stasis spots, distended sublingual veins, thin white coat. Pulse: choppy (涩, sè) or wiry (弦, xián).
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Blood stasis is like a traffic jam in the vessels. Movement physically pumps blood through the body, warmth dilates vessels and improves flow, and mental stimulation encourages Qi to rise to the head. Inactivity lets blood pool, cold constricts vessels and thickens blood, and suppressing emotions causes Qi stagnation that worsens stasis.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Stay gently active with daily walks or tai chi to keep blood moving. Eat warming, blood-nourishing foods like dark leafy greens, small amounts of lean red meat, and use spices like turmeric and ginger. Avoid prolonged sitting and cold raw foods. Keep your mind engaged with puzzles or social activities, but avoid excessive stress.

### Spleen and Kidney Qi Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/spleen-and-kidney-qi-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Profound fatigue and low stamina, Poor appetite and loose stools, Soreness and weakness of the lower back and knees, Frequent urination, especially at night, Mental dullness and apathy
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Overwork and exhaustion, Raw, cold foods and drinks, Excessive sexual activity, Prolonged standing, Chronic stress
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warm, cooked meals, Adequate rest and sleep, Gentle exercise like walking, Warmth
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  The Spleen transforms food into Qi and Blood, while the Kidney stores Essence that produces marrow to fill the brain. When both organs are deficient, the brain is starved of the nourishment it needs, leading to gradual memory loss, mental dullness, and apathy. The physical decline mirrors the cognitive decline, with deep fatigue and weakness making it hard to stay mentally engaged.

### Qi and Blood Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/qi-and-blood-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Mental fatigue and forgetfulness that worsens with exertion, Pale face, lips, and nail beds, Dizziness or lightheadedness upon standing, Heart palpitations and poor sleep, Poor appetite and spontaneous sweating
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Overwork and exhaustion, Skipping meals, Raw, cold foods and drinks, Excessive worry, Prolonged standing
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Adequate rest and sleep, Warm, nourishing foods, Gentle exercise like walking, Quiet calm environment, Warmth
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  The brain depends on a steady supply of Qi and Blood to think clearly and remember. When Qi is too weak to lift clear energy upward and Blood is too thin to nourish the mind, mental fog, forgetfulness, and difficulty concentrating set in. This is a gradual decline, often accompanied by physical exhaustion and a pale, undernourished appearance.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale body, may be thin or have teeth marks, thin white coating. Pulse: fine (thin) and weak.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Qi and Blood are produced by the Spleen and Stomach from food and rest. Overwork and skipping meals deplete them, while raw cold foods damage the Spleen’s ability to transform food into energy. Warm, nourishing meals and adequate rest replenish Qi and Blood, and gentle movement circulates them without draining reserves. A calm mind prevents further Qi stagnation and Blood consumption.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat warm, cooked meals with plenty of nourishing broths, dark leafy greens, and moderate amounts of quality protein. Avoid raw, cold, or iced foods that weaken the Spleen. Prioritize regular rest and gentle daily movement like walking or tai chi; avoid pushing through exhaustion. A short afternoon nap can help preserve Qi and Blood for the brain.

## คำถามที่พบบ่อย (Frequently Asked Questions)
### Can acupuncture really help with memory loss?
Acupuncture cannot reverse advanced brain damage, but it can support remaining cognitive function by improving blood flow to the brain, reducing inflammation, and calming agitation. Many patients and families notice improvements in alertness, sleep quality, and mood, which can make daily life easier. The effect is gradual and works best when combined with herbal medicine and lifestyle adjustments.

### Is Chinese herbal medicine safe to take with my dementia medications?
Generally, yes, but it requires careful coordination. Some herbs used for dementia, such as those that invigorate Blood (e.g., Dan Shen, Chuan Xiong), can interact with blood thinners or antiplatelet drugs. Other herbs with sedative properties may amplify the effects of medications that cause drowsiness. Always inform both your neurologist and your TCM practitioner about everything you are taking, and never stop prescribed medications without medical supervision.

### How long before I see results?
Most people need at least 2-3 months of consistent treatment before noticing any change. The first signs are often subtle: better sleep, less irritability, or a bit more engagement in conversation. Cognitive improvements, if they occur, tend to be modest and slow. The primary goal is often to stabilize the condition and slow further decline rather than to regain lost memories.

### Can TCM prevent dementia?
While nothing can guarantee prevention, TCM’s emphasis on nourishing Kidney Essence, strengthening the Spleen, and keeping Qi and Blood moving freely aligns with many protective lifestyle factors. Regular acupuncture, a balanced diet, and appropriate herbs may help maintain cognitive health, especially if started early when mild cognitive impairment is first noticed. The earlier the intervention, the more likely it is to make a difference.

### What will happen during an acupuncture session?
The practitioner will insert very thin, sterile needles into specific points on the scalp, face, and body - often including points like Baihui (top of the head) and Sishengcong (four points around it). The sensation is usually mild. Many patients find the sessions deeply relaxing. The needles are typically retained for 20-30 minutes, and treatment is usually given once or twice a week.

### Will I need to change my diet?
Dietary adjustments are a helpful support, not a strict requirement. In general, TCM recommends warm, cooked foods that are easy to digest, such as soups, stews, and whole grains. Foods that nourish the brain include walnuts, black sesame, goji berries, and eggs. It’s best to avoid greasy, heavy, or very cold foods, which can promote Phlegm and weaken digestion. Your practitioner will give you specific guidance based on your pattern.

## การวิจัยทางวิทยาศาสตร์สมัยใหม่ (Modern Scientific Research)
1. **Qi Fu Yin-a Ming Dynasty Prescription for the Treatment of Dementia** ([ลิงก์](https://doi.org/10.1007/s12035-018-0957-6))
   *วารสารวิชาการ: Li W, et al. Qi Fu Yin-a Ming Dynasty Prescription for the Treatment of Dementia. Mol Neurobiol. 2018;55(8): 6689-6701.*
   This review examines the traditional formula Qi Fu Yin (Seven Blessings Decoction), which combines ginseng, rehmannia, angelica, and other herbs to tonify Qi and Blood and calm the spirit. Preclinical and clinical evidence suggests neuroprotective effects and improvement in cognitive function for Alzheimer’s disease and vascular dementia, supporting its use in Kidney-Spleen-Heart deficiency patterns.

2. **Acupuncture for Alzheimer’s disease: a systematic review and meta-analysis of randomized controlled trials** ([ลิงก์](https://doi.org/10.3233/JAD-132036))
   *วารสารวิชาการ: Zhou J, et al. Acupuncture for Alzheimer’s disease: a systematic review and meta-analysis of randomized controlled trials. J Alzheimers Dis. 2015;43(2): 431-441.*
   A meta-analysis of 12 RCTs involving over 800 participants found that acupuncture, particularly when added to conventional medication, significantly improved cognitive function as measured by the Mini-Mental State Examination (MMSE) compared to medication alone. The review noted that acupuncture was safe and well-tolerated, but called for larger, more rigorous trials.
