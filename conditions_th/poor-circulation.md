## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนปัจจุบัน (Conventional Medicine View)
### คำอธิบาย (Description)
Western medicine attributes poor circulation most often to peripheral artery disease (narrowed arteries reducing blood flow to the limbs), Raynaud's phenomenon (spasms of small arteries in response to cold or stress), or venous insufficiency. Symptoms include cold hands and feet, numbness, tingling, skin color changes, and slow-healing wounds. Diagnosis typically involves physical examination, ankle-brachial index measurement, Doppler ultrasound, or angiography. While these tools reveal the structural and functional state of the vessels, they don't always explain why one person develops sluggish flow while another doesn't - which is where TCM's pattern-based approach can add valuable insight.

### การรักษาหลัก (Treatments)
Conventional care focuses on improving blood flow and managing risk factors. This may include lifestyle changes (quitting smoking, regular exercise), medications like antiplatelet agents (aspirin, clopidogrel), vasodilators, or cholesterol-lowering drugs, and in advanced cases, procedures such as angioplasty or bypass surgery. For Raynaud's, calcium channel blockers are often prescribed to relax small arteries. Compression stockings and leg elevation help venous insufficiency.

## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนจีน (Traditional Chinese Medicine View)
### ความเข้าใจเกี่ยวกับการแพทย์แผนจีน (TCM Understanding)
In TCM, blood is understood as a dense, nourishing substance that relies on the smooth, rhythmic push of Qi - the body's vital force - to circulate through every vessel. The Heart is the emperor of this process, pumping blood through a network of channels, but it depends on the Liver to keep Qi flowing freely and the Spleen to produce enough blood in the first place. When any link in this chain weakens, circulation can falter. The result is what patients experience as cold, purplish limbs, numbness, or fixed stabbing pains - classic signs that blood is stuck and not reaching the periphery.
What makes TCM's view so practical is that it doesn't stop at 'poor circulation.' It asks why the blood is stuck. In some people, emotional stress or frustration knots up Liver Qi, and that trapped Qi can no longer push blood - this is Qi and Blood Stagnation. In others, a physical injury or surgery leaves behind a local stagnation that refuses to clear.
Chronic fatigue can weaken Qi so profoundly that it simply lacks the horsepower to move blood, a pattern called Qi Deficiency causing Blood Stagnation. Even the quality of the blood matters: if it's too scanty (Blood Deficiency) or has been scorched thick and sticky by internal Heat, it can't flow smoothly. Each of these scenarios produces a slightly different picture on the tongue and pulse, and each responds to a different combination of herbs and acupuncture points.

### การวินิจฉัยโดยแพทย์แผนจีน (Practitioner Diagnosis)
A TCM practitioner starts by asking about the quality of your discomfort and what makes it better or worse. Blood stasis patterns share a few hallmark signs: pain that is fixed, stabbing, and worse at night, along with a purplish tongue and a choppy or wiry pulse. The next step is to tease apart what caused the stagnation, because the underlying trigger determines the pattern and the treatment.
If your poor circulation appeared after an injury or surgery, with a very localized, sharp pain and a tongue that is dark purple with stasis spots, pure Blood Stagnation is likely. The pulse here is typically choppy or hesitant.
When the problem flares with emotional stress and you feel a distending, moving pain that later becomes fixed, Qi and Blood Stagnation is the more probable pattern. The tongue may show purple spots, and the pulse feels wiry.
If fatigue is the main story and your limbs feel cold and heavy, Qi Deficiency causing Blood Stagnation is suspect. The tongue is pale with purple spots, and the pulse is weak and thready. A person with this pattern often feels better after rest and worse after exertion.
When poor circulation is accompanied by chest tightness, palpitations, and cold hands that turn bluish, Heart Blood Stagnation may be the root. The tongue tip is often purplish, and the pulse is knotted or intermittent.
If you notice a pale complexion, numbness, and dry skin alongside the cold limbs, Blood Deficiency and Stagnation could be the cause. The tongue is pale and thin, and the pulse is thready and choppy.
In cases where the skin feels hot, the tongue is red with a yellow coating, and the pulse is rapid, Blood Stagnation with Heat is considered. This pattern often arises in inflammatory conditions that thicken the blood and slow its flow.

## กลุ่มอาการการแพทย์แผนจีนและการรักษา (TCM Patterns and Treatment)

### Blood Stagnation
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/blood-stagnation)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Fixed stabbing pain that worsens at night, Cold extremities with purplish lips or nails, Dark or purplish tongue body with stasis spots, Dark menstrual blood with clots
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Cold weather, air conditioning, or drafts, Prolonged sitting, standing, or inactivity, Greasy, heavy, or icy foods, Emotional stress, anger, or frustration
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Gentle movement (walking, stretching), Warmth on the abdomen and limbs, Warm spices and herbal teas (ginger, cinnamon, turmeric), Rest and relaxation
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Blood Stagnation is the direct TCM pattern for poor circulation. When blood becomes thick or its flow is obstructed, it cannot reach the farthest parts of the body. This leaves the hands and feet cold, purplish, and often numb, while the stuck blood causes sharp, fixed pains that feel worse at night.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: purple or dusky body with stasis spots, distended dark sublingual veins, thin white coat. Pulse: choppy (涩 Se), rough like a knife scraping bamboo; may be wiry (弦 Xian) if Qi stagnation is present.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Cold constricts the vessels, making already sluggish blood even stickier, while warmth relaxes the vessels and encourages flow. Movement is the natural opposite of stagnation - gentle exercise pumps Qi through the channels, which then pushes blood to the extremities. Stress knots the Liver Qi, which is the chief driver of blood circulation; when Qi stagnates, blood follows. So calming the mind and moving the body are the simplest ways to break the cycle of stagnation.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Keep the body warm and avoid icy drinks. Eat warming, blood-moving foods like turmeric, ginger, cinnamon, and small amounts of black vinegar. Gentle daily movement - walking, stretching, or tai chi - prevents stagnation. Avoid long periods of sitting and manage emotional stress through deep breathing or journaling.

### Qi And Blood Stagnation
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/qi-and-blood-stagnation)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Fixed, stabbing pain that worsens with pressure, Distending pain or tightness in the chest and ribs, Irritability, mood swings, or emotional tension, Dark purplish complexion or lips, Painful periods with dark, clotted blood
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Emotional stress, anger, or frustration, Prolonged sitting, standing, or inactivity, Cold, raw foods and icy drinks, Cold weather, air conditioning, or drafts
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Gentle movement (walking, stretching), Warmth on the abdomen and limbs, Rest and relaxation, Light, easily digestible meals
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Qi is the commander of Blood; when emotional stress or frustration causes Liver Qi to stagnate, it can no longer propel Blood smoothly through the vessels. This stuck Qi creates a traffic jam in the circulation, leading to sluggish blood flow, cold extremities, and the characteristic fixed, stabbing pains. Over time the stagnation deepens, giving the tongue a dark purple hue and making the pulse feel wiry and rough.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: purple or dark body with stasis spots, distended sublingual veins, thin white coat. Pulse: wiry and choppy.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Qi stagnation is the root of this pattern: emotional upset tightens the Liver Qi, which then fails to move Blood. Warmth and gentle movement unblock the stagnation by encouraging Qi to flow, while relaxation eases the grip of the Liver. Cold and inactivity, on the other hand, congeal Blood and allow stagnation to settle deeper, making the circulation even more sluggish.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Favor warming, blood-moving foods: turmeric, ginger, cinnamon, dark leafy greens, and small amounts of lean red meat. Avoid dairy, icy drinks, and raw foods that chill and congeal blood. Gentle daily movement - walking, qigong, or yoga - keeps Qi flowing. Practice deep breathing or meditation to unwind the emotional tension that knots Liver Qi.

### Qi Deficiency causing Blood Stagnation
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/qi-deficiency-causing-blood-stagnation)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Fixed stabbing pain that worsens with pressure, Deep fatigue and lack of strength, Shortness of breath with reluctance to speak, Dull or dusky facial complexion with a greyish tinge, Numbness or tingling in the limbs, Spontaneous sweating with mild activity
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Overexertion, heavy lifting, or fatigue, Cold weather, air conditioning, or drafts, Prolonged sitting, standing, or inactivity, Pressing on the painful spot
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Rest and relaxation, Warmth on the abdomen and limbs, Gentle movement (walking, stretching), Warm, nourishing soups and stews
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Qi is the body's vital force that pushes blood through the vessels. When Qi becomes too weak to keep blood moving, circulation slows down and the blood begins to pool and stagnate. This leads to cold extremities, numbness, a dusky complexion, and fixed stabbing pains that feel worse when you press on them because the stagnant blood cannot disperse.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale body with a purplish or dark tinge, stasis spots, teeth marks, thin white coat. Pulse: deep (沉 chén), fine or weak (细弱 xì ruò), and choppy (涩 sè).
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Qi is the engine that moves blood. Overexertion drains that engine further, leaving blood even more stagnant, so resting naturally restores the force needed to push blood along. Cold causes vessels to constrict and blood to congeal, while warmth relaxes vessels and encourages flow. Gentle movement activates Qi and blood without exhausting it, but pressing on a stasis site adds obstruction that the weak Qi cannot overcome, making pain worse.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat warm, cooked meals and sip warm water throughout the day; cold and raw foods chill the blood and worsen stagnation. Gentle daily movement like walking or tai chi helps Qi circulate without exhausting it, but stop before you feel tired. Protect yourself from cold and wind, especially the neck and lower back.

### Heart Blood Stagnation
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/heart-blood-stagnation)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Stabbing or pricking chest pain in a fixed location, Purple or dark discolouration of lips and nails, Cold hands, Palpitations with anxiety during pain episodes, Pain radiating to the shoulder, upper back, or inner left arm, Shortness of breath and feeling of suffocation
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Emotional stress, anger, or frustration, Cold weather or cold drinks, Overexertion, heavy lifting, or fatigue, Greasy, heavy, or icy foods
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Gentle movement (walking, stretching), Warmth on the abdomen and limbs, Rest and relaxation, Warm spices and herbal teas (ginger, cinnamon, turmeric)
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  The Heart is the emperor of blood circulation, pumping blood through every vessel. When Heart Qi and Yang are weak, the blood cannot be propelled with enough force, and it begins to pool and stagnate right inside the chest. This sluggish flow shows up as cold, purple hands and feet, while the stabbing chest pain and palpitations are the heart's own distress signal of blocked vessels.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: dark purple body with stasis spots, distended sublingual veins, thin white coat. Pulse: choppy (涩 Sè) at the left Cun position, or knotted (结 Jié) with irregular pauses in more established cases.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: <p>Cold causes blood vessels to constrict and blood to congeal, directly worsening the stagnation that defines this pattern. Warmth, on the other hand, encourages vessels to dilate and blood to flow more smoothly. Emotional stress knots up the Liver Qi, which then impedes the Heart's ability to pump blood, so calming the mind and moving gently helps keep Qi and blood moving. Overexertion depletes the already weak Heart Qi, making it even harder to push blood through the chest.</p>
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Keep the chest and hands warm; cold constricts blood vessels and worsens stasis. Gentle daily movement like walking or tai chi helps keep blood flowing without overstraining the Heart. Include warming, blood-moving foods such as ginger, turmeric, cinnamon, and a little black pepper, while avoiding icy drinks and heavy, greasy meals that can clog Qi.

### Blood Deficiency and Stagnation
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/blood-deficiency-and-stagnation)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Pale or sallow face with a dusky tinge, Numbness or tingling in the limbs, Fatigue and low energy, Scanty or late menstruation with dark clots, Fixed dull or stabbing pain, Brittle or ridged nails
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Overexertion, heavy lifting, or fatigue, Cold weather, air conditioning, or drafts, Cold, raw foods and icy drinks, Emotional stress, anger, or frustration, Prolonged sitting, standing, or inactivity
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Gentle movement (walking, stretching), Warm, nourishing soups and stews, Rest and relaxation, Warmth on the abdomen and limbs
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When the body lacks enough blood to fill the vessels, tissues become undernourished and the pulse feels thin and weak. At the same time, that scanty blood does not flow smoothly, so it pools and stagnates, especially in the smallest vessels of the hands and feet. The result is a circulation that is both too little and too sluggish - limbs feel cold, numb, and tingly, and the face looks pale with a faint purple undertone.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale-purple body, thin, may have purple stasis spots, distended sublingual veins, thin white coat. Pulse: fine (细 xì) and choppy (涩 sè), possibly wiry on the left Guan position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Blood deficiency needs deep nourishment and rest to rebuild; pushing through fatigue only drains reserves further. Gentle movement is essential to keep blood moving, but overexertion worsens the deficiency. Cold constricts vessels and thickens blood, directly aggravating stasis, while warmth relaxes vessels and invites flow. Stress stagnates Liver Qi, which in turn worsens blood stasis, so calming the mind is a key part of treatment. Warm, nutrient-dense foods provide the raw material for new blood and gently warm the channels.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat warm, iron-rich foods like slow-cooked bone broths, dark leafy greens, beets, goji berries, and small amounts of red meat or liver. Avoid raw, cold foods and icy drinks, which constrict vessels and slow flow further. Gentle daily movement - walking, tai chi, or restorative yoga - helps blood move without exhausting your reserves. Prioritize sleep and rest; pushing through fatigue deepens the deficiency.

### Blood Stagnation with Heat
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/blood-stagnation-with-heat)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Fixed stabbing pain that worsens at night, Sensation of internal heat, especially at night, Dark or clotted menstrual blood, Thirst with desire to rinse the mouth but not swallow, Irritability and restlessness
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Spicy, greasy, or fried food, Emotional stress, anger, or frustration, Hot weather or overheated rooms, Alcohol and coffee, Overexertion, heavy lifting, or fatigue
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Cooling foods and drinks, Rest and relaxation, Gentle movement (walking, stretching), Cold compresses on painful areas
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Excess heat enters the blood and scorches it, making the blood thick and sticky. This thickened blood cannot flow smoothly through the vessels, so it stagnates and obstructs circulation. The result is a distinctive kind of poor circulation that feels hot rather than cold, with a burning quality to the pain and dark, clotted bleeding.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: reddish-purple body with stasis spots, distended sublingual veins, and a thin dry yellow coat. Pulse: choppy (涩 sè) and rapid (数 shuò), often wiry at the left Guan position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Heat is the driving force behind this pattern: it scorches the blood and makes it congeal. Cooling foods and calm rest directly reduce that heat, letting the blood thin and flow more freely. Spicy foods, alcohol, stress, and hot environments all add more heat, worsening the stasis and the burning pain. Gentle movement helps Qi circulate without generating the friction that excessive activity can cause.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Avoid spicy, greasy, and fried foods, as well as alcohol and coffee, which add heat and thicken the blood. Emphasize cooling, blood-nourishing foods like cucumber, watermelon, mung beans, and dark leafy greens. Keep calm and avoid emotional outbursts; gentle movement like walking or tai chi helps Qi flow without overheating the body.

## คำถามที่พบบ่อย (Frequently Asked Questions)
### How long does it take for acupuncture to improve poor circulation?
Most people notice a difference within 4-8 weeks of weekly sessions. You might feel your hands and feet warming up during the very first treatment, but lasting change comes as the pattern is corrected over time. Excess patterns tend to respond faster; deficiency patterns need more time to rebuild Qi and Blood.

### Can Chinese herbs really warm my hands and feet?
Yes, and they do it by addressing the root cause, not just by dilating vessels temporarily. Herbs like Dang Gui and Chuan Xiong actively move stagnant blood, while Huang Qi boosts the Qi that pushes blood to your extremities. When the formula matches your pattern, the warmth tends to feel natural and sustained rather than fleeting.

### Is it safe to take Chinese herbs if I'm already on blood thinners?
This is very important: many blood-moving herbs (Dan Shen, Dang Gui, Chuan Xiong, Hong Hua) can have an additive effect with anticoagulant or antiplatelet medications like warfarin, aspirin, or clopidogrel. Always tell both your TCM practitioner and your prescribing doctor about all medications and supplements you take. Your herbal formula can be adjusted for safety, but it must be done with full disclosure.

### What foods should I eat to help my circulation?
Think warm and moving. Ginger, turmeric, cinnamon, garlic, and onion all gently invigorate blood. Hawthorn berry (shān zhā) is a classic food-herb for sluggish circulation. Avoid cold, raw foods and icy drinks, which constrict and slow flow. Greasy, heavy meals can also create internal dampness that gums up the works - light, cooked meals are your best friend.

### Does TCM work for Raynaud's disease?
Yes, many people with Raynaud's find significant relief through TCM. The color changes in the fingers - white, blue, then red - are a textbook sign of blood stasis with underlying Qi or Blood deficiency. Herbal formulas and regular acupuncture can reduce the frequency and severity of attacks, often making the hands more resilient to cold triggers.

### Will the results last after I stop treatment?
That's the goal. TCM aims to correct the underlying imbalance, not just suppress symptoms. Once your Qi is strong enough to move blood and the stagnation is cleared, the improvement should hold - especially if you maintain the dietary and lifestyle habits you learned during treatment. Some people choose to return for seasonal tune-ups, but many enjoy lasting relief.

### Can poor circulation be a sign of something serious in TCM?
In TCM, chronic poor circulation is a pattern that can contribute to other problems - painful obstruction, masses, or organ damage - if left unaddressed. However, it also serves as an early warning that your body's flow is compromised. Treating it early often prevents more serious issues down the road. If you ever experience sudden, severe symptoms like chest pain or one-sided weakness, those are medical emergencies - see our Safety section for red flags.

## การวิจัยทางวิทยาศาสตร์สมัยใหม่ (Modern Scientific Research)
1. **Xuefu Zhuyu decoction for angina pectoris: A systematic review of randomized controlled trials**
   *วารสารวิชาการ: Chen J, et al. Complementary Therapies in Medicine. 2012;20(5):364-374.*
   This systematic review evaluated the efficacy and safety of Xuefu Zhuyu decoction in treating angina pectoris. The meta-analysis found that the decoction, when added to conventional treatment, significantly improved angina symptoms and electrocardiogram results compared to conventional treatment alone. The review included 15 RCTs with a total of 1,356 participants and concluded that Xuefu Zhuyu decoction appears effective, though the quality of included studies was generally low.

2. **Acupuncture for primary dysmenorrhea: A systematic review and meta-analysis of randomized controlled trials**
   *วารสารวิชาการ: Smith CA, et al. BJOG: An International Journal of Obstetrics & Gynaecology. 2018;125(7):818-828.*
   This meta-analysis assessed acupuncture's effect on primary dysmenorrhea, a condition frequently attributed to Blood Stagnation in TCM. The analysis of 30 RCTs involving over 3,000 women showed that acupuncture significantly reduced menstrual pain intensity compared to no treatment or NSAIDs, with fewer side effects. The study noted that many trials used points such as Sanyinjiao SP-6 and Xuehai SP-10, which are standard for moving blood and alleviating pain.

3. **The effect of Bu Yang Huan Wu Tang on hemorheology and neurological function in patients with ischemic stroke: A systematic review**
   *วารสารวิชาการ: Li X, et al. Journal of Ethnopharmacology. 2015;174:497-508.*
   This systematic review examined Bu Yang Huan Wu Tang for ischemic stroke recovery, a condition characterized by Qi Deficiency and Blood Stagnation. Pooled results from 19 RCTs indicated that the formula improved neurological deficit scores and reduced blood viscosity and plasma fibrinogen levels compared to conventional rehabilitation alone. The review highlighted the formula's dual action of boosting Qi and invigorating blood as a potential mechanism for improving cerebral circulation.
