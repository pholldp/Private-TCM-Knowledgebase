## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนปัจจุบัน (Conventional Medicine View)
### คำอธิบาย (Description)
In Western medicine, a bleeding tendency is typically evaluated through blood tests that measure platelet count, clotting factors (PT, aPTT), and sometimes genetic testing for disorders like von Willebrand disease or hemophilia. Common causes include medication side effects (anticoagulants, NSAIDs), liver disease, vitamin K deficiency, or bone marrow disorders. The focus is on identifying a specific hematologic or vascular defect and correcting it, often with clotting factor concentrates, vitamin supplements, or drug adjustments.

### การรักษาหลัก (Treatments)
Treatment depends on the underlying cause. For clotting factor deficiencies, replacement therapy is used; for low platelets, corticosteroids or immunosuppressants; for medication-induced bleeding, dose adjustment or discontinuation. Topical hemostatic agents and tranexamic acid are used for acute bleeds. Iron supplements may be given for anemia. In many cases of mild, unexplained bruising, no treatment is offered beyond watchful waiting.

## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนจีน (Traditional Chinese Medicine View)
### ความเข้าใจเกี่ยวกับการแพทย์แผนจีน (TCM Understanding)
TCM doesn't view bleeding as simply a vessel or clotting problem - it sees it as a failure of the body's internal 'containing' forces. The Spleen is the primary organ responsible for keeping blood within the vessels; it produces the Qi that acts like an invisible net. When this Qi is weakened by overwork, poor diet, or chronic worry, blood begins to leak out, leading to chronic, oozing bleeds like easy bruising and heavy periods.
Excess Heat is another key driver. Heat can build up from spicy foods, alcohol, or emotional stress, and when it enters the blood, it agitates and forces it out of the vessels. This causes sudden, bright red bleeding - often nosebleeds or heavy menstrual flow - accompanied by a restless, hot sensation. The Liver, which stores blood, is particularly vulnerable to this kind of Heat, and anger or frustration can trigger a bleed.
Sometimes blood doesn't flow smoothly; it stagnates. Stagnant blood blocks the normal pathways, building pressure until fragile vessels rupture. This causes dark, clotted bleeding with sharp, fixed pain. And when Kidney Yin is depleted, a different kind of heat - a low-grade 'empty fire' - rises and scorches the vessels, leading to slow, oozing bleeds like bleeding gums or recurrent nosebleeds, often with night sweats and a dry throat.
Because these mechanisms - Spleen weakness, Heat, Stagnation, and Yin deficiency - can overlap, the same Western diagnosis of 'bleeding tendency' can have multiple TCM causes. A single patient may have both Spleen Qi deficiency and Blood Stagnation, requiring a nuanced formula that addresses both. That's why a proper TCM diagnosis looks beyond the bleed itself to the whole person.

### การวินิจฉัยโดยแพทย์แผนจีน (Practitioner Diagnosis)
A TCM practitioner begins by asking about the color, volume, and location of the bleeding, as well as what makes it better or worse. These details offer the first clues that point toward one pattern rather than another. The tongue and pulse are then examined to confirm the underlying imbalance.
If the bleeding is chronic and oozing, with pale blood and symptoms like fatigue, poor appetite, and loose stools, the pattern is likely Spleen not controlling Blood. A pale, swollen tongue and a weak, thready pulse support this picture, showing that the Spleen Qi is too weak to hold blood in the vessels.
When bleeding is sudden, profuse, and bright red, often triggered by spicy food, alcohol, or anger, Heat in the Blood is suspected. The person may feel hot, thirsty, and restless. The tongue is red with a yellow coat, and the pulse is rapid and forceful, indicating that excessive heat is agitating the blood and forcing it out.
Bleeding that is dark, clotted, and accompanied by sharp, fixed pain that worsens with pressure points to Blood Stagnation. The tongue may be purple or show dark spots, and the pulse feels choppy or wiry. Here, stagnant blood blocks the normal pathways, forcing blood to escape through abnormal routes.
A slow, trickling bleed that comes with night sweats, a dry throat, warm palms and soles, and a feeling of heat in the evening suggests Kidney Yin Deficiency with Empty-Heat. The tongue is red with little coating, and the pulse is thready and rapid. The body’s cooling Yin is too weak to anchor the Yang, creating a low-grade heat that seeps out blood.
When bleeding involves the lower body-such as bloody stools, hemorrhoids, or urinary bleeding-and is accompanied by a heavy, burning sensation, foul discharge, or a sticky taste in the mouth, Damp-Heat in the Lower Burner is the likely pattern. The tongue coat is thick, yellow, and greasy, and the pulse is slippery and rapid.

### Spleen not controlling Blood
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/spleen-not-controlling-blood)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Easy bruising or subcutaneous bleeding, Heavy or prolonged menstrual periods with pale blood, Pale, sallow complexion, Chronic fatigue and weakness, Poor appetite, Loose stools
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Overwork and exhaustion, Raw, cold foods and icy drinks, Stress and worry, Prolonged standing
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Adequate rest and sleep, Warm meals, Small, frequent meals, Gentle movement
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  The Spleen is responsible for producing Qi and holding blood within the vessels. When Spleen Qi is weakened - often by overwork, poor diet, or chronic worry - it can no longer perform this anchoring function. Blood then leaks out, leading to chronic, oozing bleeds such as easy bruising, heavy periods, or blood in the stool.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale, puffy, and tender with teeth marks and a thin white coating. Pulse: fine and weak, especially at the right Guan (Spleen) position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: The Spleen needs warmth and rest to generate Qi. Overwork, stress, and cold foods drain Spleen Qi, causing it to lose its grip on the blood. Warm, nourishing foods and adequate rest rebuild Spleen Qi, restoring its ability to hold blood in the vessels.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat warm, cooked, easily digestible foods like soups, stews, and congees. Avoid raw, cold, and greasy foods that tax the Spleen. Get plenty of rest and avoid overexertion; gentle exercise like walking or tai chi can help build Qi without depleting it.

### Heat in the Blood
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/heat-in-the-blood)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Sudden, bright red bleeding (nosebleeds, heavy periods, blood in stool), Feeling of heat, worse at night, Restlessness and irritability, Thirst with desire for cool drinks, Flushed red face, Red skin rashes or eruptions
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Spicy, fried, or greasy foods, Alcohol, Emotional stress and anger, Hot weather or overheated rooms, Overwork and exhaustion
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Cool foods and drinks, Adequate rest and sleep, Cool weather
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Excess heat builds up in the body - often from spicy food, alcohol, or emotional stress - and enters the blood. This heat agitates the blood, forcing it out of the vessels and causing sudden, bright red bleeding. The heat also stirs up restlessness, thirst, and a flushed face.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red body with red spots or prickles, thin yellow dry coating. Pulse: rapid (数 shuò), often wiry (弦 xián) and forceful.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Heat in the Blood is an excess heat pattern. Spicy food, alcohol, anger, and hot environments add more heat, which further agitates the blood and worsens bleeding. Cooling foods, rest, and a calm mind reduce internal heat, helping the blood settle back into the vessels.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Avoid spicy, greasy, and fried foods, alcohol, and coffee. Eat cooling foods like cucumber, watermelon, mung beans, and leafy greens. Practice calming activities like gentle yoga or meditation to manage stress and anger.

### Blood Stagnation
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/blood-stagnation)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Dark, clotted blood (menstrual or other), Sharp, fixed pain that worsens with pressure, Dark or purplish complexion, lips, or nails, Palpable lumps or masses
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Cold weather, Prolonged sitting, Stress and anger, Raw, cold foods and icy drinks
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Gentle movement, Warmth, Warm meals
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When blood flow becomes sluggish or stuck, the vessels can no longer contain it. The blocked blood builds up pressure and forces its way out through fragile vessels, causing bleeding that is dark and clotted. This is why the bleeding is often accompanied by sharp, fixed pain that worsens with pressure-the hallmark of stasis.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: purple or dusky body with stasis spots, distended dark sublingual veins. Pulse: choppy (涩, sè) or wiry (弦, xián).
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Cold and inactivity cause blood to congeal and stagnate further, increasing pressure in the vessels and worsening bleeding. Warmth and gentle movement promote smooth blood flow, which reduces the pressure that forces blood out. Stress tightens the Liver Qi, which is a major cause of blood stasis, so calming the mind helps blood move freely.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Warm, cooked meals with gentle spices like turmeric and ginger help keep blood moving. Avoid icy drinks and raw foods that congeal blood. Daily gentle movement-walking, stretching-prevents stagnation. Avoid prolonged sitting, and manage stress with relaxation techniques.

### Kidney Yin Deficiency With Empty-Heat Blazing
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/kidney-yin-deficiency-with-empty-heat-blazing)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Slow, oozing bleeds (gums, nose, or heavy periods), Night sweats, Five-palm heat (palms, soles, chest), Lower back soreness and weakness
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Overwork and late nights, Spicy, fried, or greasy foods, Emotional stress and frustration, Hot weather or overheated rooms
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Cooling, moistening foods (pear, tofu, black sesame), Adequate rest and sleep, Gentle, cooling exercise like swimming or tai chi
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When Kidney Yin is too weak to anchor the body's warming Yang, Empty Heat rises and scorches the blood vessels. This heat does not cause the forceful, gushing bleeds of excess fire, but a slow, oozing seepage as fragile vessels give way. You may notice bleeding gums when brushing, recurrent nosebleeds, or menstrual periods that drag on with dark or bright red blood, all accompanied by the classic signs of Yin deficiency like night sweats and a dry throat.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red, thin, dry body with little or no coating, possible cracks. Pulse: fine (细 xì) and rapid (数 shuò), may feel floating-empty; Chi (rear) position weak.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Kidney Yin is the body's cooling and anchoring force; overwork and late nights directly consume it, while spicy and warming foods add heat that agitates the blood. Emotional stress stirs up Empty Heat, making the bleeding worse. Cooling, moistening foods and adequate rest help replenish Yin and calm the upward-blazing fire, allowing the blood to stay within the vessels.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Avoid spicy, fried, or warming foods and alcohol, which add fuel to the empty fire. Embrace cooling, moistening foods like pear, black sesame, goji berries, and tofu. Prioritize rest and go to bed early - late nights and overwork are the biggest drain on Kidney Yin.

### Damp-Heat in the Lower Burner
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/damp-heat-in-the-lower-burner)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Burning pain in the anus or urethra during bleeding, Bleeding hemorrhoids or dark, sticky bloody stools, Heavy, dragging sensation in the lower abdomen and legs, Foul-smelling vaginal discharge or scrotal dampness, Dark yellow, cloudy, or frequent urgent urination
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Spicy, fried, or greasy foods, Alcohol, Hot, humid weather, Prolonged sitting
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Cool, dry weather, Light, bland diet (e.g., barley, cucumber), Gentle movement
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Dampness and Heat combine and sink to the lower body, where they congest the Intestines and Bladder. The Heat element scorches and damages the delicate blood vessels, while the heavy, sticky Dampness blocks normal flow and creates swelling. This dual pressure causes the vessels to rupture, leading to bleeding that is typically dark or mixed with foul-smelling discharge, often seen in hemorrhoids, bloody stools, or blood in the urine.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red body, slightly swollen, with a thick yellow greasy coating that is most prominent at the root. Pulse: slippery (滑 huá) and rapid (数 shuò), often more pronounced at the rear position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Damp-Heat is a heavy, sticky, and hot pathogen. Alcohol, greasy foods, and humid environments directly add more Dampness and Heat to the body, thickening the pathogenic mixture and increasing pressure on the blood vessels. Sitting for long periods further stagnates Qi and blood in the lower burner, worsening congestion. Cooling, drying foods and gentle movement help drain Dampness and clear Heat, while a cool, dry climate naturally counteracts the environmental triggers, allowing the vessels to heal.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Avoid greasy, fried, and spicy-hot foods, as well as alcohol and excessive sugar, all of which brew Dampness and Heat. Emphasize cooling, damp-draining foods like mung beans, celery, cucumber, and barley. Stay active with gentle, regular movement to prevent Dampness from stagnating in the lower body, but avoid sitting for long periods, especially on hot surfaces.

## คำถามที่พบบ่อย (Frequently Asked Questions)
### Can TCM help with easy bruising and bleeding gums?
Yes, these are classic signs of Spleen Qi deficiency or Yin deficiency with empty heat. Herbal formulas like Gui Pi Tang strengthen the Spleen to hold blood, while Zhi Bo Di Huang Wan cools empty fire. Many patients notice fewer bruises and less gum bleeding within a few weeks, especially when combined with dietary changes.

### Is it safe to take Chinese herbs if I'm on blood thinners?
Caution is essential. Many TCM herbs that stop bleeding are safe, but herbs that invigorate blood (like Dang Gui, Chuan Xiong, Dan Shen) can interact with warfarin, apixaban, aspirin, and similar medications, potentially increasing bleeding risk. Always inform both your TCM practitioner and prescribing doctor of all substances you take. Never stop prescribed anticoagulants without medical supervision.

### How does TCM treat heavy menstrual bleeding?
TCM distinguishes between heavy bleeding that is bright red and gushing (often Heat in the Blood) and that which is pale and chronic (often Spleen deficiency). Treatment uses herbs to cool blood or strengthen Qi, along with acupuncture points like Sanyinjiao (SP-6) and Xuehai (SP-10). Most women see lighter, less painful periods within 2-3 cycles.

### What foods should I avoid if I bleed easily?
Avoid raw, cold foods and icy drinks, which weaken the Spleen's blood-holding ability. Cut back on spicy, greasy, and fried foods that generate Heat. Alcohol and coffee can aggravate Heat and Yin deficiency, so limit those. Instead, favor warm, cooked meals with root vegetables, lean proteins, and dark leafy greens.

### How long until I see results?
Many patients notice fewer bleeding episodes within 3-4 weeks, though deeper patterns like Spleen deficiency may take 6-12 weeks to fully stabilize. Acupuncture and herbs work cumulatively, so consistency is key. Your practitioner will adjust your formula as your pattern shifts.

### Can acupuncture stop active bleeding?
Acupuncture is not an emergency measure to stop a sudden, severe bleed - for that, seek urgent medical care. However, regular acupuncture can reduce the frequency and severity of bleeding episodes by addressing the underlying pattern. Points like Yinbai (SP-1) are traditionally used to calm uterine bleeding, but this is part of a preventive strategy, not a replacement for first aid.
