## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนปัจจุบัน (Conventional Medicine View)
### คำอธิบาย (Description)
In conventional medicine, chronic diarrhea is defined as loose or watery stools persisting for more than four weeks. It can result from a wide range of conditions including irritable bowel syndrome (IBS), inflammatory bowel disease (Crohn's, ulcerative colitis), malabsorption disorders, chronic infections, food intolerances, or side effects of medications.
Diagnosis typically involves stool tests, blood work, and sometimes colonoscopy to rule out structural or inflammatory causes. Many cases, however, are classified as functional diarrhea - meaning no clear organic disease is found - which can leave patients without a satisfying explanation or effective long-term solution.

### การรักษาหลัก (Treatments)
Standard treatment depends on the underlying cause. Antidiarrheal medications like loperamide are used to reduce stool frequency, while bile acid binders help if bile acid malabsorption is suspected. Dietary changes such as a low-FODMAP diet, avoiding trigger foods, or increasing soluble fiber are commonly recommended. For specific diseases, anti-inflammatory drugs, immunosuppressants, or antibiotics may be prescribed. In functional cases, management often focuses on symptom control rather than addressing a root cause.

## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนจีน (Traditional Chinese Medicine View)
### ความเข้าใจเกี่ยวกับการแพทย์แผนจีน (TCM Understanding)
TCM views chronic diarrhea as a failure of the body's digestive and fluid-management systems, centered on the Spleen. The Spleen's job is to transform food and drink into Qi and to send the clear, useful fluids upward while directing the turbid waste downward.
When Spleen Qi is weak - from poor diet, overwork, or chronic illness - it can't manage moisture properly. Dampness accumulates and sinks into the intestines, mixing with stool and causing persistent loose or watery diarrhea. This is the most common root, and it often comes with fatigue, bloating, and a heavy sensation.
But the Spleen doesn't work alone. The Kidneys provide the body's fundamental warming fire, which is needed to 'cook' food and dry out dampness. When Kidney Yang is deficient, the digestive fire is too weak, leading to chronic loose stools that are often worse in the early morning - the time when Yang Qi is at its lowest ebb. This pattern often includes cold limbs, a sore lower back, and feeling chilled.
Emotional stress also plays a major role. The Liver is responsible for the smooth flow of Qi throughout the body. When stress, frustration, or anxiety cause Liver Qi to stagnate, it can 'invade' the Spleen, disrupting its function. This creates a pattern of diarrhea that strikes with cramping pain before a bowel movement and improves after. It's closely tied to emotional ups and downs.
Other factors include Damp-Heat, a sticky, inflammatory combination that causes urgent, foul-smelling diarrhea with a burning sensation, and Cold-Damp, which freezes the Spleen's yang and produces watery, clear stools that worsen in cold weather. Even undigested food can accumulate and cause loose stools with a sour smell. Because these patterns can overlap or shift over time, a TCM practitioner will carefully examine your tongue, pulse, and symptoms to identify the dominant imbalance and create a personalized treatment plan.

### การวินิจฉัยโดยแพทย์แผนจีน (Practitioner Diagnosis)
A TCM practitioner starts by asking about the stool itself - its consistency, color, smell, and any undigested food. They also want to know the timing of the diarrhea, what makes it better or worse, and any accompanying sensations like pain, cold, or burning. The tongue and pulse are examined to confirm the underlying imbalance.
If the diarrhea is long-standing with loose, watery stools, fatigue, poor appetite, and a sense of heaviness, the pattern is likely Spleen Deficiency with Dampness. The tongue is pale with a white, greasy coat, and the pulse feels weak and soft. This is the most common root of chronic diarrhea.
When diarrhea strikes predictably in the early morning (around 5 am) and is accompanied by cold limbs, a sore lower back, and feeling chilled, it points to Kidney and Spleen Yang Deficiency. The tongue is pale and swollen, and the pulse is deep and slow. The body lacks the warming fire needed to transform fluids.
If episodes are clearly triggered by emotional stress, frustration, or anxiety, and there is abdominal pain that eases right after a bowel movement, the pattern is Rebellious Liver Qi invading the Spleen. The tongue may look normal or slightly pale, and the pulse has a wiry, tense quality that reflects the internal tension.
Diarrhea that is urgent, foul-smelling, and accompanied by a burning sensation around the anus, possibly with mucus or blood, suggests Damp-Heat in the Large Intestine. The tongue is red with a thick yellow, greasy coat, and the pulse is rapid and slippery. This pattern is less common in chronic cases but can flare during acute episodes.
Watery, clear diarrhea that worsens with cold weather or cold drinks, along with a dull, cold pain in the abdomen that feels better with warmth, indicates Cold-Damp invading the Spleen. The tongue has a white, greasy coating, and the pulse is slow and soft. The body’s internal cold and dampness are overwhelming the digestive fire.
When stools contain recognizable undigested food and have a sour, putrid smell, with a sensation of fullness and bloating after meals, it points to Food Stagnation in the Stomach. The tongue coating is thick and greasy, and the pulse is slippery and full. This pattern is rare in chronic diarrhea but can recur with habitual overeating.

### Spleen Deficiency with Dampness
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/spleen-deficiency-with-dampness)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Loose or unformed stools, often watery, Abdominal bloating that worsens after eating, Heaviness in the body and limbs, Poor appetite and fatigue, Sticky or greasy sensation in the mouth
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Overeating or rich, greasy food, Cold, raw foods and iced drinks, Damp, humid weather, Sedentary lifestyle, Sweet and dairy products
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warm, cooked meals, Rest after eating, Gentle walking, Warm compress on abdomen
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  The Spleen is meant to transform food into Qi and send fluids upward. When it is weak, it fails to manage moisture, and fluids accumulate into internal Dampness. This heavy, sticky Dampness sinks downward into the intestines, mixing with stool and causing the persistent loose, watery diarrhea. The Dampness further traps the Spleen's energy, creating a self-reinforcing cycle of fatigue, bloating, and chronic loose stools.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale, swollen body with teeth marks, white greasy coating, moist surface. Pulse: soggy (Ru) and slow (Huan), soft and weak.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Spleen Qi deficiency with Dampness is a pattern of too little energy and too much moisture. Cold, raw, greasy, and sweet foods are difficult to digest and directly increase internal Dampness, while a sedentary lifestyle allows Qi and fluids to stagnate. Warmth and easily digested foods support Spleen Yang, helping it to dry Dampness and firm up the stools. Gentle movement stimulates the Spleen's Qi to circulate, transforming and transporting fluids rather than letting them pool.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Base meals around warm, cooked, easily digestible foods like congee, soups, and steamed vegetables. Avoid raw, cold, greasy, dairy, and overly sweet items that generate Dampness. Eat small, frequent portions and take a gentle walk after meals to keep Qi moving.

### Kidney and Spleen Yang Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/kidney-and-spleen-yang-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Early-morning diarrhea (around 5 AM), Cold hands and feet, especially below the knees, Sore and cold lower back and knees, Undigested food in stools, Frequent urination with clear, pale urine, Feeling cold and aversion to cold
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Cold, raw foods and iced drinks, Cold weather or drafts, Overwork and exhaustion, Late nights and irregular sleep, Excessive sexual activity
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warm compress on abdomen, Hot drinks and soups, Rest and early nights, Moxibustion
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  The body's warming fire, rooted in the Kidneys, is too weak to properly heat the digestive system. Without this warmth, the Spleen cannot transform food and fluids, leading to chronic loose stools. Because Kidney Yang is at its lowest point at dawn, the characteristic early-morning diarrhea occurs, often with undigested food and a deep, internal chill.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale, swollen, puffy body with teeth marks and a white slippery coating. Pulse: deep, weak, and slow, particularly feeble at the rear (Kidney) positions.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Kidney and Spleen Yang Deficiency creates a deep internal cold that fails to warm the digestive fire. Cold foods and environments directly aggravate this cold, while warmth from food, clothing, and moxibustion provides the heat needed to restore digestion. Overwork, late nights, and excessive sexual activity further drain the Kidneys, the root of Yang, so resting and conserving energy are essential for recovery.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat warm, cooked foods like soups, stews, and congees. Avoid all cold, raw, and chilled items. Keep your lower back and abdomen warm, especially in the early morning. Gentle daily walking helps, but overexertion and late nights deplete your reserves.

### Rebellious Liver Qi invading the Spleen
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/rebellious-liver-qi-invading-the-spleen)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Abdominal cramping that eases after bowel movement, Diarrhea triggered by emotional upset, Rib-side fullness or discomfort, Frequent sighing and irritability, Alternating constipation and loose stools
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Stress, anger, or frustration, Irregular eating or skipping meals, Cold, raw foods and iced drinks, Overwork and exhaustion, Greasy, fried, or rich foods
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Emotional calm and relaxation, Warm, cooked meals, Gentle walking, Warm compress on abdomen, Regular meal times
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Emotional stress causes Liver Qi to stagnate, and because the Liver and Spleen are closely linked, that stuck Qi can lash sideways and attack the Spleen. This disrupts the Spleen's ability to transform food and fluids, leading to loose stools and diarrhea. The cramping pain before a bowel movement comes from the Liver Qi fighting to move, and the relief afterwards happens because the pressure is released.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: normal or slightly pale body with possible teeth marks; thin white coating that may be slightly greasy. Pulse: wiry (弦 xián) overall, especially wiry in the left middle position, and softer in the right middle position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Emotional stress is the main driver of this pattern, causing Liver Qi to stagnate and then attack the Spleen. Calming the mind and using herbs or points that soothe the Liver while strengthening the Spleen break the cycle. Warmth and regular, gentle nourishment support Spleen Yang, while cold, irregular, or heavy eating further weakens it, making diarrhea more likely.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat warm, cooked meals at regular times and avoid raw, cold, or greasy foods that burden the Spleen. Gentle daily movement like walking helps keep Liver Qi flowing smoothly. Sip chamomile or peppermint tea to ease tension, and build in small relaxation practices throughout the day.

### Damp-Heat in the Large Intestine
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/damp-heat-in-the-large-intestine)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Abdominal cramping with urgent need to defecate, Burning sensation around the anus, Stools are yellow-brown, foul-smelling, and may contain mucus or blood, Incomplete evacuation after bowel movements, Feeling of heaviness and heat in the body, Thirst and scanty dark urine
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Spicy, greasy food, Alcohol, Hot, humid weather, Stress, anger, or frustration, Overeating or rich, greasy food
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Cooling, light meals, Rest and reduced activity, Cool environment, Drinking plenty of water, Eating mung bean soup
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Damp-Heat is a sticky, inflammatory combination that lodges in the Large Intestine. The Heat causes urgency, burning, and foul-smelling stools, while the Dampness makes the diarrhea sticky and difficult to fully evacuate. Over time, this lingering Damp-Heat can damage the intestinal lining, leading to chronic diarrhea with mucus or blood.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red body with a thick, yellow, greasy coating, thickest in the centre and root. Pulse: slippery (滑 huá) and rapid (数 shuò).
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Damp-Heat is aggravated by foods that are greasy, spicy, or warming, as they add more Heat and Dampness to the body. Alcohol and stress also generate Heat and disrupt Qi flow, worsening intestinal inflammation. Cooling, light foods help clear Heat and drain Dampness, while rest conserves Qi for healing. A cool environment prevents external Damp-Heat from invading and adding to the internal condition.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Avoid greasy, fried, spicy, and rich foods, as well as alcohol and dairy products, which worsen Damp-Heat. Eat light, cooling foods like mung beans, barley, cucumber, and celery. Drink plenty of water to help flush out Heat. Keep the environment cool and dry, and avoid prolonged sitting in damp places.

### Cold-Damp invading the Spleen
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/cold-damp-invading-the-spleen)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Watery, clear stools, Abdominal cold pain relieved by warmth, Heaviness in the body and limbs, Head feels heavy, as if wrapped in cloth, Sticky or greasy sensation in the mouth, Thick white greasy tongue coating
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Cold, raw foods and iced drinks, Damp, humid weather, Overwork and exhaustion
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warm compress on abdomen, Ginger tea, Warm, dry weather, Warm, cooked meals, Rest
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When Cold-Damp invades the Spleen, it freezes the Spleen's Yang and clogs its ability to transform and transport fluids. The Spleen can no longer separate the pure from the turbid, so water and undigested food rush downward, producing watery, clear diarrhea. The Cold makes the abdomen feel cold and painful, while the Dampness creates a heavy, bloated sensation throughout the body.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale, swollen body with teeth marks and a thick, white, greasy coating that is excessively moist. Pulse: soggy (Ru, 濡) or slippery (Hua, 滑) and slow (Chi, 迟), especially weak at the right Guan position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Cold and dampness are the very pathogens causing this pattern, so anything that adds cold or dampness to the body (like raw foods or damp weather) will worsen diarrhea. Warmth and dryness counteract these pathogens, helping the Spleen recover its function.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat warm, cooked foods like congee and soups. Avoid raw, cold, and greasy foods that burden the Spleen. Sip ginger tea to warm the middle. Keep your abdomen warm and avoid damp environments.

### Food Stagnation in the Stomach
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/food-stagnation-in-the-stomach)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Loose stools with sour, foul smell and undigested food, Abdominal bloating that worsens after eating, Sour, rotten-smelling belching, Aversion to food and its smell, Thick, greasy or curd-like tongue coating, thickest in the centre, Bloating and pain relieved after vomiting
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Overeating or rich, greasy food, Greasy, fried, or rich foods, Late-night eating, Eating while stressed or rushing
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warm, cooked meals, Short fast or skipping a meal, Warm compress on abdomen, Gentle walking
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When you habitually overeat or consume heavy, greasy foods that are difficult to digest, the stomach cannot process them properly. The undigested food sits and stagnates, generating turbid dampness that disrupts the Spleen's ability to transform and transport fluids. This causes loose stools that contain recognizable bits of undigested food and carry a sour, rotten smell. While food stagnation typically triggers acute episodes, recurrent dietary overload can make loose stools a chronic issue until the pattern of eating changes.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: normal body, thick greasy or curd-like coating, thickest in the centre. Pulse: slippery (滑 huá), forceful at the right middle position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Food stagnation is a direct result of overloading the digestive system. Light, easily digested foods reduce the burden, while short fasting allows the stomach to process the backlog. Greasy and heavy meals add more turbidity, and eating under stress or late at night disrupts the stomach's natural descending rhythm, worsening the stagnation and the loose stools that follow.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat small, light meals and chew thoroughly. Avoid greasy, fried, and cold foods. A short period of fasting or a simple congee diet can give the stomach a chance to clear the stagnation. Gentle walking after meals aids digestion.

## คำถามที่พบบ่อย (Frequently Asked Questions)
### How long does it take for acupuncture to stop chronic diarrhea?
Most patients notice a reduction in stool frequency and improved consistency within 4-6 weekly acupuncture sessions. However, because chronic diarrhea often has a deep-rooted cause, full resolution typically requires 8-12 weeks of combined acupuncture and herbal therapy. Your practitioner will monitor progress and adjust treatment as your pattern shifts.

### Can Chinese herbs cause diarrhea?
Some herbs, particularly those that clear Heat or move stagnation, can temporarily loosen stools - but this is usually intentional when treating constipation. For chronic diarrhea, your formula will be carefully chosen to stop loose stools, not cause them. If you experience new or worsening diarrhea after starting herbs, contact your practitioner immediately; a simple adjustment often resolves it.

### Is it safe to take TCM herbs with my prescription medications?
In most cases, yes, but you must inform both your TCM practitioner and your doctor about all medications and supplements you are taking. Some herbs may interact with anticoagulants, immunosuppressants, or diabetes medications. Never stop or reduce your prescribed medication without consulting your doctor. Your TCM practitioner can design a formula that works safely alongside your conventional treatment.

### What foods should I avoid if I have chronic diarrhea?
Avoid raw, cold, and greasy foods, as well as dairy products, excessive sugar, and spicy foods, which can all worsen dampness and weaken the Spleen. Instead, eat warm, cooked, easily digestible meals like soups, congee, and steamed vegetables. Small, frequent meals are gentler on your digestion. For personalized dietary advice, see the pattern-specific guidance in your treatment plan.

### Can TCM help if my diarrhea is caused by IBS?
Yes. TCM has a long history of treating IBS-type diarrhea, which often fits the Liver-Spleen disharmony pattern (stress-triggered) or Spleen Deficiency with Dampness. By addressing the root imbalance - whether emotional, dietary, or constitutional - many patients experience significant and lasting relief, often with fewer dietary restrictions than conventional approaches require.

### Will the diarrhea come back after I stop treatment?
The goal of TCM treatment is to correct the underlying imbalance so that your digestion functions normally on its own. Once your practitioner confirms that your pattern has resolved, the risk of relapse is low - but maintaining a healthy diet and managing stress are important for long-term stability. Some patients benefit from occasional 'tune-up' treatments during periods of high stress or seasonal changes.
