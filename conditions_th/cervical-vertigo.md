## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนปัจจุบัน (Conventional Medicine View)
### คำอธิบาย (Description)
Cervical vertigo, also called cervicogenic dizziness, is dizziness that originates from the neck, often due to problems in the cervical spine like arthritis, disc degeneration, or whiplash. The dizziness is typically triggered by neck movements and may be accompanied by neck pain, stiffness, and sometimes headache. Diagnosis is clinical, ruling out other vestibular or central causes, and may involve imaging.

### การรักษาหลัก (Treatments)
Standard Western treatments include physical therapy, manual therapy, medications (muscle relaxants, pain relievers, sometimes vestibular suppressants), and in some cases, injections or surgery. The focus is on improving neck mechanics and reducing pain, which can help the dizziness for many people.

## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนจีน (Traditional Chinese Medicine View)
### ความเข้าใจเกี่ยวกับการแพทย์แผนจีน (TCM Understanding)
TCM understands cervical vertigo primarily through the lens of the clear orifices - the head and senses - being clouded or undernourished. The neck is a vital conduit for Qi and Blood traveling to the brain. When this flow is blocked by phlegm, wind, or blood stasis, or when the body lacks enough Qi and Blood to send upward, dizziness results.
Phlegm is a major culprit. When the Spleen is weak, it fails to transform fluids, and dampness accumulates into phlegm. This heavy, sticky substance can rise along the neck channels and cloud the head, causing a heavy, foggy dizziness. If phlegm combines with internal wind, the dizziness becomes more spinning and sudden. This is why diet and digestion matter so much in cervical vertigo.
Deficiency patterns also play a key role. Qi and Blood are the fuel for the brain. When they are deficient, even a slight neck strain or change in posture can leave the head undernourished, triggering lightheadedness, especially after standing or when tired. This pattern is common in people who are run down or anemic.
Other patterns include Liver Yang Rising, where unanchored yang surges upward causing throbbing dizziness and irritability, and Blood Stagnation, where old injuries or chronic poor posture create a traffic jam in the neck vessels, leading to fixed, stabbing pain and recurrent vertigo. Each pattern requires a different treatment strategy.

### การวินิจฉัยโดยแพทย์แผนจีน (Practitioner Diagnosis)
To identify which pattern is behind a person’s cervical vertigo, a TCM practitioner begins by asking about the quality of the dizziness, what triggers it, and what makes it better or worse. The tongue and pulse are then examined to confirm the underlying imbalance, because each pattern leaves a distinct picture that guides treatment.
When wind‑phlegm is the culprit, the dizziness often comes on suddenly with head or neck movement and feels as if the head is heavy or wrapped in cloth. Phlegm in TCM is a thick, obstructive fluid that clouds the senses. Nausea, a thick white greasy tongue coating, and a slippery or wiry‑slippery pulse are typical. This is a very common acute presentation.
Damp‑phlegm produces a persistent heavy‑headed sensation, chest tightness, and foggy thinking. The dizziness is less movement‑triggered than wind‑phlegm and more tied to sluggish digestion. The tongue coating is thick and greasy - white or slightly yellow - and the pulse is slippery or soft. The whole body may feel heavy and lethargic.
When qi and blood are deficient, the dizziness feels like light‑headedness or emptiness, and it clearly worsens with exertion or standing up. The person looks pale, feels exhausted, and may have heart palpitations. The tongue is pale with a thin coating, and the pulse is weak and thready. There is no sense of heaviness, just a drained, unsteady feeling.
Liver yang rising causes a distending, throbbing head sensation along with dizziness, irritability, and a red face. Stress makes it worse. The tongue is red with little coating, and the pulse is wiry and rapid. There is often a bitter taste in the mouth, trouble sleeping, and a tendency toward high blood pressure. The upward energy of the liver disturbs the head.
Blood stagnation in the neck presents with fixed, stabbing neck pain and recurrent vertigo that does not respond well to rest. The tongue may appear dark purple with stasis spots, and the pulse is choppy or wiry. A history of neck injury, prolonged poor posture, or surgery is common, and the pain is a reliable clue that sets this pattern apart from the others.
Phlegm‑heat adds heat signs to the phlegm picture: the tongue is red with a yellow greasy coating, the pulse is rapid and slippery, and there may be a bitter taste, constipation, and dark urine. The vertigo can be intense, and the person may feel restless or agitated. This pattern often arises when chronic phlegm is left unaddressed and heat develops.

### Wind-Phlegm
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/wind-phlegm)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Severe spinning vertigo, may feel like falling, Heavy, foggy sensation in the head, Neck stiffness and tension, Nausea or vomiting of white frothy phlegm, Chest stuffiness and epigastric bloating, Heaviness in the limbs
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Cold and damp weather, Heavy, greasy meals, Stress, worry, or frustration, Prolonged sitting or poor posture, Exposure to wind
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warm, dry environment, Light, easily digested meals, Gentle neck movement or stretches, Rest in a quiet room
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When the Spleen is weak, it fails to transform fluids, and phlegm accumulates. This sticky phlegm combines with internal Liver Wind and rises along the neck channels to cloud the head's clear orifices, triggering severe spinning vertigo. The phlegm also obstructs the collaterals in the neck, causing stiffness and a heavy, foggy sensation.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: slightly pale, swollen with teeth marks, white greasy coating. Pulse: wiry and slippery (弦滑 xián huá).
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Damp, cold environments and greasy foods overwhelm the Spleen, producing more phlegm and worsening vertigo. Stress stirs Liver Wind, which then rises with phlegm to the head. Warmth and light meals support the Spleen's ability to transform dampness, while rest and gentle movement calm the Liver and keep Qi flowing through the neck.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Avoid cold, raw, greasy, and dairy-heavy foods that create dampness. Eat warm, cooked meals like soups and stews to support the Spleen. Protect the neck from wind and drafts, and practice gentle neck stretches to keep Qi flowing.

### Damp-Phlegm
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/damp-phlegm)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Head heaviness as if wrapped, Dizziness that is heavy rather than spinning, Nausea and poor appetite, Chest and upper abdominal stuffiness, Thick white greasy tongue coating, Loose sticky stools
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Heavy, greasy meals, Cold and damp weather, Prolonged sitting or poor posture, Overeating, Cold drinks and raw foods
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warm, dry foods like soups and stews, Ginger tea, Light walks, Warm, dry environment, Rest in a quiet room
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When the Spleen is weakened, it fails to transform and transport fluids, allowing dampness to accumulate and congeal into phlegm. This heavy, turbid phlegm rises up along the channels to the head, where it clouds the clear orifices and produces a sensation of dizziness and a head that feels wrapped in a wet towel. The phlegm-dampness also settles in the neck collaterals, contributing to stiffness and a dull ache.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale, swollen body with teeth marks, thick white greasy coating, wet surface. Pulse: slippery (滑 huá) or soggy (濡 rú).
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Damp-Phlegm arises when the Spleen fails to transform fluids, so greasy and cold foods worsen dampness, while warm, dry foods and gentle movement help the Spleen drain dampness and prevent phlegm from rising to the head.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Avoid cold, raw, greasy, and dairy foods that create dampness. Favor warm, cooked meals with spices like ginger and cardamom. Light daily movement, such as walking, helps Qi flow and drains dampness.

### Qi and Blood Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/qi-and-blood-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Dizziness worse with exertion or after standing, Pale or sallow complexion and pale lips, Persistent fatigue and general weakness, Heart palpitations or poor sleep
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Overexertion and lack of sleep, Skipping meals, Standing up quickly, Stress, worry, or frustration
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Rest and lying down, Warm, nourishing meals, Gentle movement
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Qi and Blood are the essential fuel that nourish the brain. When they are deficient, the head is undernourished, and even minor neck strain or a change in posture can leave the brain starved of circulation, triggering dizziness. This is why the vertigo is worse when you are tired, after standing up, or following exertion - your body simply cannot push enough nourishment upward.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale body, may be thin or have teeth marks, thin white coating. Pulse: fine (thin) and weak, especially in the right Guan position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Qi and Blood are the foundation of vitality. Overexertion and skipping meals deplete them further, leaving the brain unsupported and causing dizziness. Rest and nourishing meals rebuild Qi and Blood, while gentle movement encourages circulation without draining reserves. Stress and worry consume Spleen Qi, which is needed to produce Blood, so calming the mind protects these resources.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat warm, cooked meals with plenty of easily digestible proteins like chicken, beef, and bone broths. Include iron-rich foods such as dark leafy greens, red dates, and black sesame. Avoid overwork and get adequate sleep. Gentle exercises like tai chi or walking can help build Qi gradually.

### Liver Yang Rising
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/liver-yang-rising)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Throbbing or distending headache, especially at the temples, Dizziness with a feeling that the head is heavy and the feet are light, Irritability, quick temper, and difficulty sleeping, Red tongue with little coating and redder sides
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Stress, worry, or frustration, Overexertion and lack of sleep, Spicy, greasy, or fried foods, Alcohol and coffee, Hot, stuffy environments
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Rest in a quiet, darkened room, Cool, fresh air, Gentle neck movement or stretches, Calming activities like meditation
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When the Liver's Yin and Blood are depleted, they can no longer anchor the Liver's Yang. The unrestrained Yang surges upward along the neck's meridians, carrying heat to the head and disturbing the clear orifices. This upward rush produces the sensation of dizziness, a distending headache, and the feeling that the head is heavy while the feet are light. In cervical vertigo, this internal surge often tightens the neck muscles and disrupts the smooth flow of Qi through the neck's collaterals, making head movements or stress trigger the spinning.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red body, redder sides, thin dry yellow or scanty coat. Pulse: wiry (弦 xián), often forceful at the left middle position; may be fine (细 xì) and rapid (数 shuò) when Yin deficiency is pronounced.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Anger and stress make Liver Qi stagnate and then surge upward, which is the exact mechanism that drives Yang to the head. Rest, darkness, and cooling habits help the body conserve Yin and settle the rising Yang. Spicy and heating foods add fuel to the internal fire, while alcohol and coffee are both warming and stimulating, directly provoking the upward rush. Gentle movement keeps Qi moving without agitating it, whereas overwork depletes Yin further and leaves Yang unanchored.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Favor cooling, yin-nourishing foods like cucumber, celery, pear, and tofu, and keep spicy, greasy, and fried foods to a minimum. - **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Fixed, stabbing neck pain, Dizziness that recurs with neck movement, Dark or purplish lips and nails, Neck pain that worsens with pressure

- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Gentle neck movement or stretches, Warm compresses on neck, Light massage, Rest with proper neck support
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When the neck is injured or held in poor posture for long periods, the smooth flow of Blood through the local channels gets stuck. This stasis acts like a traffic jam in the vessels that supply the head, leaving the brain undernourished and triggering recurrent dizziness. The pain is fixed and stabbing because the stagnant Blood presses on the tissues, and it worsens at night when circulation naturally slows down.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: purple or dusky body with stasis spots, distended dark sublingual veins, and a thin white coat. Pulse: choppy (涩 sè), like a knife scraping bamboo, possibly wiry.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Blood stasis means the Blood is congealed and not flowing smoothly. Cold and inactivity congeal it further, while warmth and gentle movement encourage circulation, easing both the neck pain and the dizziness. Stress tightens the Qi, which can worsen stasis, so calming the mind and relaxing the neck muscles helps Blood flow freely again.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Keep your neck warm with a scarf, especially in cold or windy weather. Gentle daily stretches and short walks help keep Blood moving. Avoid heavy, greasy foods that can create phlegm and worsen stasis; instead, include moderate amounts of turmeric, dark leafy greens, and a little vinegar in your meals to gently invigorate circulation.

### Phlegm-Heat
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/phlegm-heat)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Dizziness with a heavy, foggy sensation, Restlessness and irritability, Thick yellow greasy tongue coating, Chest oppression and nausea
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Spicy, greasy, or fried foods, Alcohol and coffee, Stress, worry, or frustration, Hot humid weather, Overeating
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Cool drinks, Light, easily digested meals, Gentle neck movement or stretches, Rest in a cool, quiet room
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Phlegm-Heat arises when Damp-Phlegm lingers in the body and transforms into Heat, often from a rich diet or chronic stagnation. The hot, sticky phlegm rises through the neck channels to cloud the head, producing vertigo with a heavy, foggy sensation and mental dullness. The Heat agitates the mind, adding restlessness, irritability, and a flushed face.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red body, possibly swollen, with a thick yellow greasy coating. Pulse: slippery (滑 huá) and rapid (数 shuò).
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Rich, greasy foods and alcohol create Dampness and Heat, directly fueling the Phlegm-Heat that rises to disturb the head. Stress and anger stir up internal Heat, worsening restlessness and dizziness. Cooling, light foods and gentle movement help the Spleen transform Dampness and clear Heat, while rest in a cool environment calms the mind and allows Phlegm to settle.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Avoid greasy, fried, and spicy foods, as well as alcohol and coffee, which generate Dampness and Heat. Eat more cooling, light foods like cucumber, celery, pear, and mung beans. Gentle daily movement such as walking or tai chi helps Qi flow and prevents Phlegm from stagnating in the neck.

## คำถามที่พบบ่อย (Frequently Asked Questions)
### What does TCM treatment for cervical vertigo involve?
Treatment typically includes acupuncture, Chinese herbal formulas, and lifestyle advice. Acupuncture points are chosen on the neck, head, and body to clear blockages and nourish the brain. Herbs are taken daily to address the root pattern. Your practitioner may also suggest dietary changes and gentle neck exercises.

### How quickly can I expect to feel better?
Many notice some improvement within the first few sessions, but lasting change usually takes several weeks. Acute patterns like wind-phlegm or liver yang rising often respond faster, while deficiency patterns may need a few months. Your practitioner will track progress through changes in your tongue and pulse.

### Can I continue my physical therapy while doing TCM?
Yes, TCM can complement physical therapy. Acupuncture and herbs can reduce pain and inflammation, making exercises easier. Always inform both your physical therapist and TCM practitioner about all treatments you are receiving.

### Is acupuncture safe for cervical vertigo?
When performed by a licensed practitioner, acupuncture is very safe. Needles are sterile and single-use. Some points on the neck are used with care, but serious complications are extremely rare. You may feel a dull ache or tingling, which is normal.

### Will I need to change my diet?
Dietary advice depends on your pattern, but generally, avoiding greasy, fried, and sugary foods helps reduce phlegm and dampness. Warm, cooked meals with ginger can support digestion. Your practitioner will provide specific guidance.

### What if my dizziness gets worse after acupuncture?
A temporary mild aggravation of symptoms can happen as the body adjusts. This usually settles within a day. If dizziness is severe or persistent, contact your practitioner. Always seek emergency care for red-flag symptoms like chest pain or speech changes.

### Can TCM help if I’ve had this for years?
Yes. Chronic cervical vertigo often involves deeper patterns like blood stasis or deficiency, which can still respond well to TCM. Treatment may take longer, but many people experience significant relief even after years of symptoms.

### Does TCM treat the underlying cause or just the symptoms?
TCM aims to treat the root imbalance that causes your dizziness, not just mask it. By correcting the pattern - whether it’s phlegm, deficiency, or stagnation - the dizziness often resolves more completely and recurrence is less likely.

## การวิจัยทางวิทยาศาสตร์สมัยใหม่ (Modern Scientific Research)
1. **Clinical study on acupuncture at Renying ST-9 combined with pressure moxibustion at Baihui DU-20 for cervical vertigo**
   *วารสารวิชาการ: Shanghai Journal of Acupuncture and Moxibustion, 2021.*
   This clinical trial investigated the effect of a specific acupuncture protocol - needling Renying ST-9, a point near the carotid artery, together with pressure moxibustion at Baihui DU-20 - for patients with cervical vertigo. The treatment group showed significant improvement in vertigo symptoms and vertebrobasilar hemodynamics compared to a conventional acupuncture control group. The study concluded that this method effectively regulates blood flow to the brain and relieves dizziness.

2. **Systematic review and meta-analysis of acupuncture for cervical vertigo**
   *วารสารวิชาการ: Multiple databases searched; published in a Chinese evidence-based medicine journal, circa 2020.*
   A meta-analysis of multiple randomized controlled trials evaluating acupuncture for cervical vertigo found that acupuncture, either alone or combined with other therapies, was superior to conventional medication in reducing dizziness and improving cervical function. The review noted that the most frequently used acupoints were Fengchi GB-20, Baihui DU-20, and Jiaji points of the neck, reflecting the TCM principle of clearing the local channels and restoring the upward flow of clear Yang.
