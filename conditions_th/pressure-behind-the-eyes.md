## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนปัจจุบัน (Conventional Medicine View)
### คำอธิบาย (Description)
In Western medicine, pressure behind the eyes is a symptom rather than a disease itself. It is commonly linked to eye strain from prolonged screen use, uncorrected vision problems, sinus congestion, tension headaches, or migraines. Less frequently, it can signal more serious conditions such as glaucoma, optic neuritis, or thyroid eye disease. Diagnosis typically involves an eye exam, measurement of intraocular pressure, and sometimes imaging to rule out structural causes.

### การรักษาหลัก (Treatments)
Treatment depends on the suspected cause. For eye strain, rest, artificial tears, and adjusting screen habits are recommended. Sinus-related pressure may be treated with decongestants or antihistamines. Tension headaches and migraines are managed with over-the-counter pain relievers, prescription medications, and stress reduction. If glaucoma is diagnosed, medicated eye drops or surgery may be needed to lower eye pressure.

## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนจีน (Traditional Chinese Medicine View)
### ความเข้าใจเกี่ยวกับการแพทย์แผนจีน (TCM Understanding)
TCM understands pressure behind the eyes primarily through the Liver and its opening into the eyes. The Liver governs the smooth flow of Qi, and when emotional stress or frustration stagnate that flow, Qi can back up and create a dull, distending pressure. If the stagnation heats up, it transforms into Liver Fire that flares upward, bringing a burning, red, and intensely painful sensation to the eyes.
The Spleen plays a key role too. When the Spleen is weak and fails to transform fluids, a heavy, sticky substance called Dampness accumulates, which can congeal into Phlegm. This turbid Phlegm-Dampness rises to the head and settles around the eyes, causing a puffy, heavy pressure - often worse in damp weather and accompanied by brain fog and chest stuffiness.
Deficiency patterns tell a different story. Chronic overwork, late nights, or aging can deplete the body's Yin fluids, allowing an ungrounded, 'empty' heat to rise and dry out the eyes, creating a gritty, evening-worse pressure. Similarly, when Spleen Qi is too weak to lift clear Yang to the head, the eyes feel undernourished and develop a dragging pressure that worsens with fatigue.
Finally, long-standing Qi stagnation or past injury can lead to Blood Stagnation - a fixed, stabbing pressure that doesn't move, often with a dark tongue. That's why a TCM practitioner asks not just about the pressure but about its quality, timing, and what makes it better or worse. The same Western diagnosis of 'eye strain' could be Liver Qi Stagnation in one person and Spleen Qi Deficiency in another, and the treatments are completely different.

### การวินิจฉัยโดยแพทย์แผนจีน (Practitioner Diagnosis)
A TCM practitioner first asks about the quality of the pressure. Is it a sharp, burning distension, or a dull, heavy ache? The character of the sensation is the first clue that separates patterns like Liver Fire Blazing from Liver Qi Stagnation. They also ask what makes it worse: emotional stress, fatigue, or rich foods.
If the eyes feel hot, red, and painfully distended, with a bitter taste in the mouth, thirst, and a red tongue with a yellow coating, the picture points to Liver Fire Blazing. The pulse will be wiry and rapid. When the pressure is duller, comes and goes with stress, and lacks strong heat signs, Liver Qi Stagnation is more likely.
A sensation of heavy, puffy pressure around the eyes, together with chest stuffiness, a greasy tongue coating, and a slippery pulse, suggests Damp-Phlegm is obstructing the eye collaterals. If the pressure is chronic, dry, and gritty, with night sweats, a red tongue with little coating, and a thready rapid pulse, Empty-Heat from Yin Deficiency is the culprit.
Fixed, stabbing pressure or pain that does not move, along with a dark tongue showing stasis spots and a choppy pulse, signals Blood Stagnation. Finally, when the pressure is a dragging heaviness that worsens with tiredness, accompanied by poor appetite and a pale, enlarged tongue, the root is Spleen Qi Deficiency failing to lift clear Yang to the eyes.

## กลุ่มอาการการแพทย์แผนจีนและการรักษา (TCM Patterns and Treatment)

### Liver Fire Blazing
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/liver-fire-blazing)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Red, burning eyes, Throbbing temple headache, Bitter taste in mouth, Intense irritability, Dry mouth and throat
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Anger and frustration, Alcohol and spicy food, Staring at screens for long periods, Hot, stuffy environments, Suppressed emotions
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Cool compress over eyes, Rest in a dark quiet room, Chrysanthemum or peppermint tea, Gentle eye exercises and breaks, Reducing emotional stress
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  In TCM, the Liver opens into the eyes. When intense emotions like anger or chronic stress are not released, they can cause Liver Qi to stagnate and then transform into Fire. This Fire has a natural tendency to flare upward, rushing along the Liver channel to the head and directly attacking the eyes. The result is a sudden, intense sensation of heat, distension, and pressure behind the eyes, often accompanied by redness and a throbbing headache at the temples or crown.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red body with redder sides, dry yellow coating, possible red prickles. Pulse: wiry, rapid, and forceful, especially at the left Guan (Liver) position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Anger and frustration directly fuel Liver Fire, causing it to flare upward into the eyes, while alcohol and spicy foods add heat to the body, worsening the burning sensation. Screen time strains the Liver blood and eyes, making them more vulnerable to heat. Cooling compresses, rest in a dark room, and calming activities help drain heat and settle the mind, while chrysanthemum tea specifically clears the Liver and brightens the eyes, directly counteracting the rising Fire.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Avoid alcohol, coffee, spicy and greasy foods, which add heat to the body. Drink cooling teas like chrysanthemum or peppermint. Include plenty of leafy greens, cucumber, and celery. Manage anger and frustration through meditation, deep breathing, or gentle exercise like walking. Take frequent screen breaks and use a cool compress over closed eyes to soothe burning and pressure.

### Liver Qi Stagnation
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/liver-qi-stagnation)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Dull, distending pressure behind the eyes, Worse with emotional stress, Frequent sighing, Rib-side distension or discomfort, Irritability or mood swings
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Stress and frustration, Angry outbursts, Prolonged screen time, Sedentary lifestyle
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Emotional calm, Gentle stretching or yoga, Warm compress on eyes, Sighing or deep breathing
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  The Liver is responsible for the smooth flow of Qi throughout the body. When emotional stress, frustration, or a sedentary lifestyle cause the Liver Qi to stagnate, this upward-moving energy gets stuck. The Qi fails to rise properly to the head and eyes, creating a dull, distending pressure behind the eyes that feels like a trapped balloon.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: normal or slightly dark body with red or slightly purple sides, thin white coat. Pulse: wiry (弦 xián), taut like a guitar string, especially at the left middle position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Liver Qi stagnation is directly caused by emotional constraint and a lack of physical movement, so stress, frustration, and sitting for long periods worsen the trapped energy and increase pressure behind the eyes. Activities that calm the mind and allow Qi to flow freely-like deep breathing, stretching, and sighing-help to unblock the stagnation and naturally relieve the distension.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Practice stress management techniques like meditation, deep breathing, or gentle exercise such as Tai Chi or walking. Avoid excessive alcohol, coffee, and greasy foods that can burden the Liver. Include green leafy vegetables and drink peppermint or chrysanthemum tea to gently move Qi.

### Damp-Phlegm
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/damp-phlegm)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Heavy, dull pressure (not sharp or throbbing), Puffiness or swelling around the eyes, Feeling of heaviness in the head and limbs, Chest stuffiness, nausea, or a feeling of phlegm in the throat, Thick, white, greasy tongue coating
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Damp or rainy weather, Heavy, greasy, or sweet foods, Overeating or late-night eating, A sedentary lifestyle
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warm, dry weather, Light, easily digestible meals, Gentle daily movement, Avoiding dairy and greasy foods
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When the Spleen is weak and cannot transform fluids properly, a heavy, sticky substance called Dampness builds up and congeals into Phlegm. This turbid Phlegm-Dampness can rise along the channels to the head and settle around the eyes.
Because Phlegm is thick and obstructive, it blocks the free flow of Qi and fluids behind the eyes. This is what creates the sensation of dull, heavy pressure rather than a sharp pain, often with puffiness and a foggy-headed feeling.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale and swollen with tooth marks, a thick white greasy coating, and a wet surface. Pulse: slippery (滑 huá), like beads rolling under the fingers; may feel soggy in the right middle position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Phlegm-Dampness is a heavy, sticky pathogen that loves damp environments and rich foods. Damp weather and greasy meals add more Dampness to the body, thickening the Phlegm and making the pressure worse. Warm, dry conditions and light, cooked foods do the opposite - they lighten the load on the Spleen so it can clear the turbidity. Movement activates the Spleen Qi and helps it transform and transport fluids, which is why a walk can clear the head when it feels heavy and foggy.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Warm, cooked foods are your best friend. Avoid dairy, sugar, fried foods, and ice-cold drinks, which all create more Dampness and Phlegm. Gentle daily movement like walking helps the Spleen Qi move and clear the fog. Try not to sleep or sit in damp environments, and eat your last meal at least three hours before bed.

### Empty-Heat caused by Yin Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/empty-heat-caused-by-yin-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Pressure behind the eyes with a dry, gritty sensation, Blurred vision and dry mouth worse in the evening, Night sweats and heat in the palms and soles, Malar flush (red cheekbones), Restlessness and difficulty staying asleep
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Overwork and late nights, Excessive screen time, Spicy and fried foods, Hot weather, Emotional stress and frustration
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Getting enough sleep, Cooling eye compresses, Eating moistening foods (pears, cucumber), Gentle eye exercises, Avoiding alcohol and spicy foods
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When the body's Yin fluids are chronically depleted-from overwork, late nights, or aging-the cooling, moistening aspect that normally anchors the body's warmth becomes insufficient. This allows an ungrounded, 'empty' heat to rise upward, much like steam from a drying pot. When this heat drifts into the eyes, it creates a sensation of dry, gritty pressure and blurred vision, often worse in the evening when Yin is naturally at its lowest.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red, thin, dry body with cracks, little or no coating. Pulse: fine (thready) and rapid, weak on deep pressure.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Yin deficiency means the body's cooling, moistening reserve is already low. Late nights, overwork, and excessive screen use further consume Yin, making the empty heat flare. Spicy, fried foods and hot weather add external heat, worsening the rising heat. Rest, sleep, and cooling, moistening foods help replenish Yin and anchor the warmth, while local cooling compresses and gentle eye exercises soothe the heat and stagnation directly at the eyes.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Prioritize rest and avoid staying up late, as nighttime is when Yin is restored. Include moistening, cooling foods like pear, cucumber, tofu, and goji berries. Sip chrysanthemum tea to gently clear eye heat. Reduce screen time and take frequent breaks to rest the eyes; a cool, damp cloth over closed eyes can soothe the gritty sensation.

### Blood Stagnation
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/blood-stagnation)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Fixed, stabbing pressure behind the eyes, Pain worsens at night, Dark tongue with purplish stasis spots, Dark circles under the eyes, Dark menstrual blood with clots (in women)
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Cold environment or foods, Emotional stress and frustration, Prolonged screen time, Sedentary lifestyle
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warm compresses on the eyes, Gentle eye massage, Light exercise, Warm, cooked meals
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When the circulation of blood becomes sluggish or stuck, the tiny vessels that nourish the eyes can become obstructed. This creates a fixed, stabbing sensation of pressure behind the eyes, as if something is trapped and cannot move. The stasis often develops from chronic Qi stagnation or a past injury, and it tends to feel worse at night when circulation naturally slows.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: purple or dusky body, often with stasis spots; sublingual veins may be distended and dark. Pulse: choppy (涩, sè), rough and uneven like a knife scraping bamboo.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Cold causes blood to congeal and stagnate further, so warmth and movement are essential to restore smooth flow. Emotional stress tightens the Liver Qi, which is the root of many blood stasis issues, so calming the mind helps unblock the vessels. Gentle exercise and eye massage physically move stagnant blood, while warm foods and compresses dilate the tiny vessels and encourage circulation.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Keep the eye area warm with a gentle warm compress to promote circulation. Include regular gentle movement like walking or stretching to keep blood flowing. Avoid prolonged screen time and take frequent breaks to rest the eyes. Eat warm, cooked foods and use mild spices like turmeric and ginger to support blood movement.

### Spleen Qi Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/spleen-qi-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Dull, dragging pressure or heaviness behind the eyes, Worse with fatigue and prolonged near work, Poor appetite, bloating after meals, Loose stools, Fatigue and heaviness in the limbs, Pale or sallow complexion
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Overwork and fatigue, Skipping meals, Cold, raw foods, Damp environment
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Rest and sleep, Warm, cooked meals, Gentle movement, Lying down
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  The Spleen transforms food into Qi and blood, and its clear Yang rises to nourish the eyes. When Spleen Qi is weak, that clear Yang fails to reach the head, leaving the eyes undernourished and causing a dull, dragging pressure behind them. This sensation often worsens with fatigue because the Spleen's energy is already depleted, and it may be accompanied by a heavy sensation if early dampness accumulates.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale, puffy, and tender with tooth marks on the edges; thin white coating. Pulse: soft, weak, and forceless, especially at the right middle position (Spleen).
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Spleen Qi is built by regular, warm, nourishing food and adequate rest. Overwork and cold foods drain Spleen Qi, worsening the deficiency and the eye pressure. Rest and warm meals replenish Spleen Qi, allowing clear Yang to rise to the head and relieve the dull pressure behind the eyes.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat regular, warm, cooked meals that are easy to digest, such as soups, stews, and congee. Avoid raw, cold, and greasy foods, which tax the Spleen. Gentle daily movement like walking helps Qi circulate, but prioritize rest and avoid overwork or staying up late.

## คำถามที่พบบ่อย (Frequently Asked Questions)
### Can acupuncture really relieve pressure behind the eyes?
Yes. Acupuncture works by redirecting the flow of Qi and blood around the eyes and along the affected channels. Points like Taiyang, Zanzhu, and Taichong are commonly used to release stagnation, clear heat, or nourish deficiency depending on your pattern. Many patients notice a softening of the pressure within a few sessions.

### How quickly will I feel better?
Excess patterns like Liver Fire or Damp-Phlegm often respond within 2-4 weeks of consistent treatment. Deficiency patterns, such as Spleen Qi Deficiency or Yin Deficiency, take longer - typically 3-6 months - because the body needs time to rebuild its reserves. Your practitioner will give you a more precise estimate after assessing your tongue and pulse.

### Do I need to change my diet?
Diet plays a supporting role. In general, avoid greasy, spicy, or very cold foods that can create Dampness or Fire. For Liver Fire, cooling foods like chrysanthemum tea, cucumber, and pear help. For Spleen deficiency, warm, cooked meals like congee and soups are ideal. Your practitioner will tailor recommendations to your specific pattern.

### Is it safe to combine Chinese herbs with my eye drops or medications?
In most cases, yes. However, always inform both your TCM practitioner and your eye doctor about everything you are taking. Some blood-moving herbs used for Blood Stagnation patterns may interact with anticoagulant medications. Never stop prescribed eye drops abruptly - work with your doctors to adjust as your condition improves.

### What if the pressure is from too much screen time?
Screen time can strain the eyes and contribute to Liver Qi Stagnation or Blood Deficiency. TCM treatment will still address the underlying pattern, but you'll also be advised to take regular breaks, practice eye exercises, and perhaps use warm or cool compresses. Reducing screen time speeds up recovery significantly.

### Can children get treated with TCM for this?
Yes, TCM is safe for children when administered by a qualified practitioner. Acupuncture may be replaced with acupressure or gentle tuina massage, and herbal formulas are adjusted for smaller doses. The underlying patterns are the same, though children more often present with Spleen deficiency or Damp-Phlegm related to diet.

## การวิจัยทางวิทยาศาสตร์สมัยใหม่ (Modern Scientific Research)
1. **Acupuncture for glaucoma** ([ลิงก์](https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD006030.pub3))
   *วารสารวิชาการ: Law SK, Li T. Acupuncture for glaucoma. Cochrane Database Syst Rev. 2013;(5):CD006030.*
   This Cochrane review assessed the evidence for acupuncture in treating glaucoma. It included one randomized controlled trial and concluded that there is currently insufficient evidence to support or refute the use of acupuncture for patients with glaucoma, though some positive effects on intraocular pressure were noted. Larger, well-designed studies are needed.

2. **Acupuncture for asthenopia: a systematic review and meta-analysis** ([ลิงก์](https://doi.org/10.1089/acm.2015.0196))
   *วารสารวิชาการ: Kim TH, Kang JW, Kim KH, et al. Acupuncture for asthenopia: a systematic review and meta-analysis. J Altern Complement Med. 2016;22(5):349-358.*
   This systematic review evaluated acupuncture for asthenopia (eye strain), a common cause of pressure and discomfort behind the eyes. The meta-analysis of six RCTs found that acupuncture significantly improved total effective rate compared to conventional treatments, but the quality of evidence was low due to risk of bias. The results suggest acupuncture may be beneficial for eye strain symptoms.
