## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนปัจจุบัน (Conventional Medicine View)
### คำอธิบาย (Description)
Cholecystitis is inflammation of the gallbladder, a small organ under the liver that stores bile. It most often occurs when a gallstone blocks the cystic duct, trapping bile and causing irritation, swelling, and sometimes infection. Less commonly, it can develop without stones (acalculous cholecystitis) in people who are critically ill or have other conditions. Typical symptoms include sudden, intense pain in the upper right abdomen that may radiate to the back or right shoulder, nausea, vomiting, and fever. Diagnosis is usually made with an abdominal ultrasound, blood tests, and sometimes a HIDA scan to assess gallbladder function.

### การรักษาหลัก (Treatments)
Standard treatment for acute cholecystitis involves hospitalization, fasting to rest the gallbladder, intravenous fluids, and antibiotics if infection is present. Pain is managed with medication. In most cases, surgical removal of the gallbladder (cholecystectomy) is recommended, often within a few days of the attack. For chronic or mild cases, a low-fat diet and watchful waiting may be advised, but surgery remains the definitive conventional solution to prevent recurrence.

## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนจีน (Traditional Chinese Medicine View)
### ความเข้าใจเกี่ยวกับการแพทย์แผนจีน (TCM Understanding)
In TCM, the gallbladder is much more than a storage pouch for bile. It is paired with the Liver and shares the job of ensuring the smooth, downward flow of Qi and bile. When this flow is disrupted - whether by emotional stress, dietary overload, or external pathogens - the gallbladder loses its ability to drain properly. The result is stagnation, pressure, and pain, which TCM calls 'obstruction leading to pain' (不通则痛, bù tōng zé tòng).
The Liver plays the lead role here. It governs the free flow of Qi throughout the body, and when it gets stuck - often from frustration, anger, or prolonged tension - the gallbladder feels it immediately. This is why stress so reliably triggers right-sided rib pain. But the digestive system (Spleen and Stomach) is equally involved. A rich, fatty meal or chronic poor diet can generate dampness and heat that congeal in the gallbladder, creating the classic acute attack with intense, burning pain and a thick yellow tongue coating.
This is why one Western diagnosis of cholecystitis can look so different from person to person. A stressed executive with a moving, distending ache has Liver Qi Stagnation. A person who overindulges in fried food and develops a sudden, severe, fixed pain with nausea and a bitter taste likely has Damp-Heat in the Gallbladder. And someone with a low-grade, dull ache that worsens with fatigue and loose stools may have Spleen Qi Deficiency at the root. TCM’s pattern-based lens explains the variation and guides treatment to the true source.

### การวินิจฉัยโดยแพทย์แผนจีน (Practitioner Diagnosis)
A TCM practitioner starts by asking about the nature and location of the pain. Sudden, severe, fixed pain that feels burning or colicky points toward an acute excess pattern like Damp-Heat in the Gallbladder, while a dull ache or a distending, wandering pain suggests a more chronic condition rooted in Qi stagnation or deficiency. The timing of symptoms after meals, especially fatty or greasy foods, and the presence of emotional stress are also key clues.
In Damp-Heat in the Gallbladder, the pain is intense and constant, often radiating to the right shoulder blade, and is accompanied by a bitter taste in the mouth, nausea or vomiting, and a thick, greasy yellow coating on the tongue. The pulse feels slippery and rapid. This pattern reflects heat and dampness obstructing the gallbladder’s normal downward flow, causing acute inflammation.
If the condition escalates to Toxic-Heat, the pain becomes excruciating and the person develops a high fever, chills, and possibly jaundice with a yellowing of the skin or eyes. The tongue turns deep red with a dry yellow coating, and the pulse is rapid and wiry. This is a serious progression where heat toxins have damaged the organ, requiring immediate medical attention.
For chronic cholecystitis, a practitioner looks for patterns like Liver Qi Stagnation, where emotional upset triggers a distending, moving pain in the rib area, along with belching and relief after passing gas.
When this stagnation generates internal Heat, the pain intensifies and irritability, a red tongue with a thin yellow coating, and a rapid pulse appear.
Over time, stabbing, fixed pain and a purplish tongue indicate Blood Stagnation.
Ongoing dull pain, fatigue, loose stools, and poor appetite point to an underlying Spleen Qi Deficiency that fails to support digestion.

### Liver Qi Stagnation
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/liver-qi-stagnation)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Distending, bloating pain in the right hypochondrium, Worsens with emotional stress or upset, Frequent sighing and irritability, Belching and discomfort after fatty or greasy meals, Poor appetite and abdominal bloating
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Emotional stress, anger, or frustration, Greasy, fried, or heavy meals, Alcohol, Overeating or large meals, Prolonged inactivity or sedentary lifestyle
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Gentle movement or walking, Deep breathing and relaxation, Small, light meals, Warm compress on the affected area
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Your Liver is responsible for the smooth flow of Qi throughout your body, including the secretion of bile by the gallbladder. When emotional stress, frustration, or prolonged tension builds up, the Liver Qi gets stuck and cannot flow freely. This stagnation directly affects the gallbladder's ability to release bile, leading to a feeling of distension and bloating under the right ribs, especially after eating rich foods.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: normal or slightly dark body, red or slightly purple sides, thin white coat. Pulse: wiry (弦 xián), like a taut guitar string, most prominent at the left middle position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Emotional stress is the primary driver of Liver Qi Stagnation, so anger and frustration directly worsen the stuck feeling under your ribs. Heavy, fatty foods demand more bile and strain the already sluggish gallbladder, leading to bloating and discomfort. Gentle movement and relaxation help unblock the Qi, while warm compresses soothe the local area and encourage smooth flow. Light, simple meals reduce the workload on your digestive system, allowing the Liver to regain its rhythm.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Avoid greasy, fried, and heavy foods that burden the gallbladder. Eat smaller, more frequent meals and chew thoroughly. Gentle daily movement like walking or tai chi helps keep Qi flowing. Limit alcohol and caffeine. Prioritize stress management through deep breathing, meditation, or enjoyable hobbies to prevent Qi from stagnating.

### Damp-Heat in the Gallbladder
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/damp-heat-in-the-gallbladder)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Sudden, severe right-sided rib pain or distension, Bitter taste in the mouth, Thick, yellow, greasy tongue coating, Nausea and vomiting, especially after fatty meals, Dark, scanty urine and a heavy, sluggish feeling
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Greasy, fried, or heavy meals, Alcohol, Emotional stress, anger, or frustration, Hot, humid weather
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Light, cooling foods or meals, Rest and lying on the left side, Warm barley or corn silk tea, Gentle movement or walking
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Damp-Heat is a sticky, turbid combination that obstructs the Gallbladder's job of storing and releasing bile. When Damp-Heat settles in the Gallbladder, the flow of bile and Qi becomes blocked, creating intense pressure and pain under the right ribs. The Heat stirs up symptoms like a bitter taste, nausea, and feverish chills, while the Dampness adds heaviness, bloating, and a greasy coating on the tongue.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red body, often slightly swollen, with redder sides; coating is yellow and greasy (sticky). Pulse: wiry (弦 xián), slippery (滑 huá), and rapid (数 shuò), most wiry at the left middle position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Greasy, spicy, and fatty foods, along with alcohol, directly introduce or create more Dampness and Heat in the body, which is exactly the pathology behind this pattern. Emotional stress, especially anger, causes Liver Qi to stagnate and generate Heat, further inflaming the Gallbladder. Cooling, light meals and draining teas help mop up the excess Damp-Heat, while rest and gentle movement keep Qi flowing without overstimulating the inflamed organ.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Avoid greasy, fried, spicy, and overly sweet foods, as well as alcohol, which all generate more Damp-Heat. Eat light, cooling meals like steamed vegetables, congee, and mung beans. Drink plenty of warm water and consider barley or corn silk tea to gently drain Dampness.

### Liver Qi Stagnation that transforms into Heat
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/liver-qi-stagnation-that-transforms-into-heat)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Distending or burning pain in the right hypochondrium, Irritability and explosive anger, Bitter taste in the mouth, Red tongue with yellow coating, Wiry, rapid pulse, Worsens with emotional stress or upset
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Emotional stress, anger, or frustration, Greasy, fried, or heavy meals, Alcohol, Prolonged inactivity or sedentary lifestyle
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Calm emotional state, Light, cooling foods or meals, Gentle movement or walking
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When stress and frustration persist, the Liver’s smooth flow of Qi stalls. In the gallbladder, this stagnation means bile cannot move freely, leading to a distending ache under the right ribs. Over time, the pent-up energy generates heat, which inflames the gallbladder and intensifies the pain into a burning sensation. The heat rises, causing a bitter taste, irritability, and a reddened tongue, while the internal dryness leads to constipation and dark urine.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red body, especially redder at the sides, with a yellow coating. Pulse: wiry and rapid (xian shu), forceful at the left middle position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Anger and frustration directly aggravate Liver Qi stagnation, which is the root of this pattern, and the resulting heat then flares the pain. Greasy and fried foods overwhelm the gallbladder, adding to the stagnation and heat. Cooling, light foods and emotional calm help douse the internal heat and allow the Qi to flow smoothly again, which is why they bring relief.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Focus on cooling, easily digested foods like cucumber, celery, and mung beans. Avoid alcohol, spicy foods, and fried fats that add heat. Regular, gentle exercise like walking helps prevent Qi stagnation, and a consistent wind-down routine before bed keeps the Liver calm.

### Toxic-Heat
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/toxic-heat)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: High, persistent fever, Intense, constant right hypochondriac pain, Jaundice (yellow skin/eyes), Restlessness and irritability, Dark, scanty urine and constipation
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Greasy, fried, or heavy meals, Alcohol, Spicy, hot-natured foods, Emotional stress, anger, or frustration
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Complete rest, Cooling fluids, Mung bean soup
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  In cholecystitis, Toxic-Heat develops when damp-heat or stagnant Qi in the gallbladder intensifies into a fierce, destructive fire. This fire toxin inflames the gallbladder wall, often leading to pus formation, and spills heat into the entire body. The result is a severe, constant pain, high fever, and sometimes jaundice as the bile flow is obstructed and the body's systems are overwhelmed by heat.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red body with prickly thorns and a dry yellow coating. Pulse: rapid (数 shuò) and forceful (有力 yǒu lì), often full (实 shí) or overflowing (洪 hóng).
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Fatty, fried, spicy foods and alcohol are damp-heat generators that directly feed the fire toxin in the gallbladder, making inflammation and pain explode. Rest and cooling foods like mung bean soup help drain heat, soothe the inflamed organ, and give the body a chance to clear the toxins.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: During an acute attack, rest completely and avoid all solid food until pain and fever subside. Sip warm water or mild herbal teas. Afterwards, stick to a bland, cooling diet: congee, steamed vegetables, and mung bean soup. Strictly avoid alcohol, fried foods, spicy dishes, and rich meats that can rekindle heat.

### Blood Stagnation
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/blood-stagnation)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Fixed, stabbing pain in the right hypochondrium, Pain worsens at night and with pressure, Dark purple tongue with stasis spots, Dark facial complexion or purplish lips
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Emotional stress, anger, or frustration, Greasy, fried, or heavy meals, Prolonged inactivity or sedentary lifestyle, Cold and raw foods/drinks
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Gentle movement or walking, Warm compress on the affected area, Rest and relaxation
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When Liver Qi stagnation becomes chronic, the Qi can no longer push the Blood through the vessels. Blood then congeals and stagnates in the channels around the gallbladder, much like a deep bruise. This creates a fixed, knifelike pain that does not move because the blockage is physical and unchanging.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: purple or dusky body, possible stasis spots; distended dark sublingual veins. Pulse: choppy (涩 Sè) or wiry and choppy.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Stress and anger worsen Liver Qi stagnation, which is the root of Blood stasis; when Qi is stuck, Blood congeals. Greasy food overloads the Gallbladder and creates more stagnation. Movement and warmth, in contrast, activate Qi and Blood circulation, helping to dissolve the stasis and relieve the knifelike pain. Rest reduces the emotional strain that tightens the Liver channel.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Gentle daily movement like walking or tai chi keeps Blood flowing. Avoid heavy, greasy meals that tax the Liver and Gallbladder. A warm compress over the right rib area can ease pain by dilating vessels and encouraging circulation.

### Spleen Qi Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/spleen-qi-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Dull, persistent ache rather than sharp or stabbing pain, Worse after eating, especially heavy or fatty meals, Poor appetite and loose stools, Fatigue and lack of energy, Pale, puffy tongue with tooth marks
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Overeating or large meals, Cold and raw foods/drinks, Emotional stress, anger, or frustration, Excessive fatigue, Irregular eating habits
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warm, easily digested foods, Small frequent meals, Gentle movement or walking, Rest and relaxation, Warm compress on the affected area
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  The Spleen is responsible for transforming food into Qi and transporting it. When Spleen Qi is weak, digestion becomes sluggish, leading to dampness and stagnation that can impair the gallbladder's ability to release bile. This results in a dull, persistent ache in the right upper abdomen, especially after eating, along with fatigue, bloating, and loose stools. Over time, the weakened middle burner fails to support proper bile flow, contributing to chronic gallbladder discomfort.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale, puffy, tender body with tooth marks and a thin white coating. Pulse: weak and soft, especially at the right middle position (Spleen).
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Spleen Qi is like a digestive fire that transforms food into energy. When it is weak, cold and heavy foods further dampen the fire, while warm, simple foods gently nourish it. Overeating and stress drain Spleen Qi directly, whereas rest and gentle movement help conserve and circulate it. By supporting the Spleen, the body can better manage bile flow and reduce gallbladder discomfort.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat warm, cooked foods like soups, stews, and congee. Avoid raw, cold, and greasy foods that burden the Spleen. Eat small, frequent meals and chew thoroughly. Gentle daily movement like walking helps Qi circulate without exhausting you. Manage stress and get adequate rest.

## คำถามที่พบบ่อย (Frequently Asked Questions)
### Can TCM help me avoid gallbladder surgery?
In many chronic or mild acute cases, yes. TCM aims to reduce inflammation, restore bile flow, and resolve the underlying stagnation or damp-heat that causes attacks. If the gallbladder is not severely damaged or obstructed by a large stone, consistent herbal and acupuncture treatment can significantly reduce symptoms and prevent recurrence. However, in cases of a completely blocked duct, gangrene, or perforation, surgery is necessary and should not be delayed. Always discuss your options with both your TCM practitioner and your surgeon.

### What should I eat during a gallbladder attack?
During an acute flare-up, it is best to give your digestive system a rest. Stick to clear, warm liquids like rice water or light vegetable broth. Avoid all fatty, greasy, fried, or spicy foods, as well as alcohol and caffeine. Once the pain subsides, reintroduce easy-to-digest, low-fat foods like congee, steamed vegetables, and small portions of lean protein. Long-term, a diet that avoids heavy, damp-producing foods is essential - see our dietary guidance for details.

### How quickly can acupuncture and herbs relieve the pain?
For acute Damp-Heat or Qi stagnation patterns, many patients feel significant pain reduction within a few days of starting herbs, and a single acupuncture session can sometimes ease a mild attack. Chronic patterns, especially those with underlying Spleen Qi Deficiency, may take 2-4 weeks of consistent treatment to see steady improvement. The goal is not just pain relief but correcting the imbalance so attacks stop happening altogether.

### Is it safe to combine TCM herbs with antibiotics for cholecystitis?
Generally yes, but you must inform both your TCM practitioner and your medical doctor about all medications and supplements you are taking. Some herbs can alter liver enzyme activity or have mild blood-thinning effects, which could interact with antibiotics or other drugs. Your TCM practitioner will select formulas that complement the antibiotic treatment and avoid any potential conflicts. Never stop prescribed antibiotics without medical advice.

### Can TCM treat gallstones, or just the inflammation?
TCM can address both. By moving Qi, clearing Damp-Heat, and softening hardness, certain herbal formulas aim to shrink or dissolve small stones and promote their passage. However, large, calcified stones may not dissolve completely. Even then, TCM can manage the inflammation and pain, reducing the frequency of attacks and making the condition more livable. Acupuncture also helps regulate bile secretion and relieve duct spasms.

### Are there any warning signs that I need emergency care instead of TCM?
Yes. If you experience sudden, severe upper right abdominal pain that does not ease, especially with high fever, chills, or jaundice (yellowing of skin or eyes), go to an emergency room immediately. These can indicate a serious infection or a blocked bile duct that requires urgent medical intervention. See our Safety section for a full list of red-flag symptoms.
