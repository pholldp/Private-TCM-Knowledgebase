## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนปัจจุบัน (Conventional Medicine View)
### คำอธิบาย (Description)
Tension-type headache is the most common primary headache disorder, affecting up to 80% of adults at some point. It typically presents as a bilateral, pressing or tightening sensation of mild to moderate intensity, like a tight band around the head. The pain is not usually aggravated by routine physical activity, and nausea is uncommon. Diagnosis is made clinically based on headache characteristics, and no specific lab or imaging test is required unless red flags suggest another cause.

### การรักษาหลัก (Treatments)
Standard treatment includes over-the-counter analgesics such as ibuprofen or acetaminophen. For frequent or chronic headaches, preventive medications like amitriptyline or other tricyclic antidepressants may be prescribed. Non-drug approaches-stress management, cognitive behavioral therapy, biofeedback, and physical therapy-are also recommended. The goal is to reduce headache frequency and severity, but the approach is largely uniform regardless of individual triggers or constitution.

## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนจีน (Traditional Chinese Medicine View)
### ความเข้าใจเกี่ยวกับการแพทย์แผนจีน (TCM Understanding)
In TCM, the head is the meeting point of all Yang meridians, so any blockage or deficiency along these pathways can trigger pain. The Liver is often the first organ to consider-it governs the smooth flow of Qi throughout the body. When emotional stress, frustration, or suppressed feelings build up, Liver Qi stagnates and rises, creating a constricting, tight-band sensation around the head. This is why so many tension headaches flare during or after stressful periods.
But the Spleen and its role in producing Qi and Blood are just as important. If the Spleen is weak-from poor diet, overwork, or chronic illness-it cannot generate enough Qi and Blood to nourish the head. The clear Yang that should keep the mind sharp fails to ascend, and a dull, heavy ache sets in, often worsening with fatigue and lifting after rest. This deficiency-type headache is common in people who feel drained and foggy-headed.
Dampness and Phlegm add another layer. When the Spleen is too weak to transform fluids, Dampness accumulates and can congeal into Phlegm-a turbid substance that clouds the head and blocks the channels. The result is a heavy, wrapped sensation, as if the head is stuffed with cotton, often accompanied by nausea and brain fog. This pattern is especially sensitive to damp weather and heavy foods.
Finally, long-standing stagnation-whether of Qi or from a past injury-can lead to Blood Stagnation. Here the pain is fixed and stabbing, like a nail in one spot, often worse at night. This is why one Western diagnosis of tension headache can have so many TCM faces: the character of the pain, its triggers, and the accompanying signs all point to a specific underlying imbalance that needs its own treatment.

### การวินิจฉัยโดยแพทย์แผนจีน (Practitioner Diagnosis)
A TCM practitioner begins by asking you to describe the headache in your own words - its quality, location, and what makes it better or worse. The character of the pain is the first big clue. A dull, constricting tightness that flares with stress points in one direction, while a heavy, wrapped sensation suggests something quite different.
If the headache is closely tied to emotional stress, comes with irritability or sighing, and feels like a band tightening around the head, Liver Qi Stagnation is likely. The tongue may look slightly red with a thin white coat, and the pulse often feels wiry, like a guitar string.
When the pain is a dull ache that worsens with tiredness and eases with rest, Blood Deficiency is a strong candidate. You might also notice a pale face, dizziness, or scanty periods. The tongue is pale with a thin white coat, and the pulse feels thready and weak, as if it lacks substance.
A heavy, dragging sensation that gets worse after physical exertion or at the end of the day points to Qi Deficiency. This pattern often comes with low energy, poor appetite, and a tendency to feel foggy-headed. The tongue is pale and the pulse is weak, reflecting the body’s struggle to lift clear energy upward.
If the headache feels distending or throbbing and is accompanied by dizziness, a red face, or a bitter taste in the mouth, Liver Yang Rising is the likely culprit. This pattern often develops when long-standing stress generates internal heat. The tongue is red with a thin yellow coat, and the pulse is wiry and rapid.
A sensation of the head being wrapped in a wet towel, or a heavy, cotton-filled feeling, suggests Damp-Phlegm. Nausea, chest tightness, and a thick, greasy tongue coating are typical. The pulse feels slippery, like pearls rolling on a plate.
Finally, a fixed, stabbing pain that is worse at night or follows a head injury signals Blood Stagnation. The tongue may show dark spots or a purplish hue, and the pulse can feel choppy or wiry-thin. This pattern tells the practitioner that blood is not moving smoothly in the channels of the head.

## กลุ่มอาการการแพทย์แผนจีนและการรักษา (TCM Patterns and Treatment)

### Liver Qi Stagnation
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/liver-qi-stagnation)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Dull, band-like pressure headache, Pain worsens with emotional stress, Chest tightness or rib-side distension, Frequent sighing, Irritability or mood swings
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Emotional stress or anger, Sedentary lifestyle or prolonged sitting, Greasy or fried foods, Alcohol and spicy foods
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Relaxation and stress reduction, Gentle exercise or stretching, Warm compress on neck and shoulders, Talking through your feelings
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Your Liver is responsible for keeping Qi flowing smoothly throughout your body. When emotional stress, frustration, or suppressed feelings build up, that flow gets stuck - a state called Liver Qi Stagnation. The stagnation creates a feeling of pressure and constriction that rises to your head, producing the dull, band-like tension headache that tightens when you're stressed.
Because the Qi can't move freely, you may also notice tightness in your chest, rib-side distension, and a tendency to sigh deeply - your body's natural attempt to release the stuck energy.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: normal or slightly dark body, red or slightly purplish sides, thin white coat. Pulse: wiry (弦 xián), taut like a guitar string.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Emotional stress and frustration are the direct cause of Liver Qi stagnation - they make the Qi knot up, which is exactly what produces the constricting headache. Gentle movement, deep breathing, and talking things out help the Qi flow again, releasing the pressure. Greasy foods and alcohol burden the Liver and Spleen, making stagnation worse, while light, fresh foods support smooth Qi movement.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Make daily gentle movement a priority - a walk, yoga, or tai chi helps keep Liver Qi flowing. Build in short breaks during work to stretch your neck and shoulders. Practice deep breathing or a simple meditation for a few minutes when you feel tension building. Eat more leafy greens, mint, and lightly cooked vegetables; cut back on greasy, heavy foods and alcohol, which can make stagnation worse.

### Blood Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/blood-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Dull, band-like pressure headache, Worse with fatigue and mental strain, Pale complexion, Dizziness or lightheadedness
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Overwork or insufficient sleep, Prolonged mental concentration, Skipping meals or poor diet, Heavy menstrual bleeding, Damp, rainy, or cold weather
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Rest and sleep, Warm, cooked meals and drinks, Gentle exercise or stretching, Light massage
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Blood is the substance that nourishes the brain and relaxes the sinews. When blood is deficient, the head and neck lack the rich, moistening nourishment they need, leading to a dull, persistent ache that often feels like a tight band around the head. This type of pain intensifies after a long day, during mental strain, or when you are simply run down, because those activities draw on the very blood that is already scarce.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale, thin body with a thin white coating. Pulse: fine (细 xì) and weak, sometimes choppy.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Blood provides the material foundation that nourishes the head and relaxes the sinews. When blood is scarce, any demand-mental work, physical exertion, or even a heavy period-further depletes it, triggering or worsening the pain. Rest, sleep, and nutrient-dense foods allow the body to rebuild blood, while gentle massage and movement help what blood is present to circulate and reach the head. Cold and damp can constrict the vessels and slow circulation, making it harder for already-deficient blood to flow, which is why a warm, calm environment brings relief.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Build blood with warm, cooked foods like bone broths, dark leafy greens, red meat, black sesame, and dates. Avoid skipping meals and overworking. Prioritize quality sleep and gentle daily movement like walking, which helps Qi and Blood circulate without depleting you further.

### Qi Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/qi-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Dull, heavy ache or pressure, Worsens with exertion or at the end of the day, Persistent fatigue and weakness, Shortness of breath on mild effort, Reduced appetite and loose stools, Pale complexion
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Overwork or insufficient sleep, Skipping meals or poor diet, Raw and cold foods, Excessive worry
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Rest and sleep, Warm, cooked meals and drinks, Gentle exercise or stretching
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Qi is the body's vital force, and the Spleen is its main producer. When Spleen Qi is weak, the body cannot generate enough Qi and Blood to nourish the head. The clear Yang Qi that should rise to keep the mind sharp and the head light fails to ascend, leaving a dull, heavy ache that feels like a weight pressing down. Because Qi is also the energy behind every movement, the headache characteristically worsens with exertion and eases with rest.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale, puffy, and tender with scalloped edges; thin white coating. Pulse: empty (虚, xu) or weak (弱, ruo), soft and forceless under pressure.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Qi is the body's fuel. Rest and sleep allow the Spleen to replenish Qi, which is why the headache lifts. Overwork, skipping meals, and mental strain burn through Qi faster than the body can produce it, leaving the head undernourished and heavy. Warm, cooked foods are pre-digested by heat, requiring less Spleen Qi to process and providing a steady supply of energy.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat warm, cooked, easily digested meals like soups, stews, and congees at regular times. Avoid raw, cold, and greasy foods that tax the Spleen. Protect your energy by taking short breaks during the day and prioritizing consistent, restorative sleep.

### Liver Yang Rising
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/liver-yang-rising)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Throbbing or distending headache at the temples, Dizziness or lightheadedness, Irritability or mood swings, Flushed face and red eyes, Soreness and weakness of the lower back and knees
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Emotional stress or anger, Alcohol and spicy foods, Overwork or insufficient sleep, Hot, stuffy environments
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Rest and sleep, Cool compresses on the forehead, Chrysanthemum or peppermint tea, Gentle exercise or stretching
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When stress and frustration persist, Liver Qi stagnates and can transform into heat. This heat fuels a forceful upward surge of Liver Yang, rushing to the head and causing the distending, throbbing pain typical of this pattern. Over time, if Yin is depleted, there is less anchoring to hold the Yang down, making the head symptoms even more pronounced.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red body with red sides, thin dry yellow or scanty coat. Pulse: wiry (弦, xián), possibly fine and rapid.

## คำถามที่พบบ่อย (Frequently Asked Questions)
### How does acupuncture help tension headaches?
Acupuncture works by unblocking the flow of Qi and Blood in the channels that run through the head and neck. For tension headaches, points like Fengchi (GB-20) at the base of the skull and Taiyang (EX-HN-5) at the temples are often used to release local tightness, while distal points like Hegu (LI-4) and Taichong (LR-3) regulate the whole-body Qi dynamic. Most patients feel a sense of relaxation during treatment, and regular sessions can reduce both the frequency and intensity of headaches.

### Can Chinese herbs really stop tension headaches?
Herbs address the root imbalance that makes you prone to headaches. For example, if your headaches are triggered by stress and come with irritability, a formula like Chai Hu Shu Gan San smooths Liver Qi flow. If your headaches feel heavy and foggy, Ban Xia Bai Zhu Tian Ma Tang transforms Dampness and Phlegm. Herbs are usually taken daily as a tea, powder, or pill, and they work best when combined with acupuncture and lifestyle adjustments. Many people notice fewer headaches within a few weeks.

### How long until I see results?
It depends on your pattern and how long you’ve had the headaches. Acute, stress-related headaches often improve in 2-4 weeks. Chronic headaches tied to deeper deficiencies may take 4-8 weeks or longer. The first sign of progress is often a reduction in headache intensity or a longer gap between episodes, not immediate disappearance. Consistency with herbs and weekly acupuncture sessions is key.

### Can I take over-the-counter painkillers while doing TCM?
Yes, you can use analgesics like ibuprofen or acetaminophen for breakthrough pain, especially in the early stages of treatment. However, avoid taking them daily or preventively, as overuse can trigger rebound headaches. As your TCM treatment takes effect, you’ll likely need them less often. Always inform your TCM practitioner about any medications you take.

### Is TCM safe for chronic daily headaches?
Yes, TCM is generally safe for chronic tension headaches and can be a good option when conventional preventives aren’t well tolerated. Acupuncture has a strong safety record, and herbal formulas are customized to your constitution. That said, if your headache pattern suddenly changes or you develop new neurological symptoms, seek urgent medical evaluation-TCM is not a substitute for emergency care.

### Do I have to change my diet?
Diet plays a supporting role, not a restrictive one. The main advice is to avoid cold, raw foods and icy drinks that weaken the Spleen Qi, and to limit greasy, sweet foods that create Dampness. Warm, cooked meals-like soups, stews, and whole grains-are encouraged. Ginger tea can help if your headaches feel heavy or are worse in damp weather. Small adjustments can make a noticeable difference over time.

## การวิจัยทางวิทยาศาสตร์สมัยใหม่ (Modern Scientific Research)
1. **Acupuncture for tension-type headache**
   *วารสารวิชาการ: Linde K, Allais G, Brinkhaus B, et al. Acupuncture for tension-type headache. Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews 2016, Issue 4. Art. No.: CD007587.*
   A Cochrane systematic review of 12 trials involving 2,349 adults. The review found that acupuncture is effective for reducing headache frequency in patients with frequent episodic or chronic tension-type headache, with benefits sustained over 6 months. Acupuncture was at least as effective as prophylactic drug treatment and had fewer side effects.

2. **Acupuncture for patients with chronic tension-type headache: a randomized controlled trial**
   *วารสารวิชาการ: Endres HG, Böwing G, Diener HC, et al. Acupuncture for tension-type headache: a multicentre, sham-controlled, patient- and observer-blinded, randomised trial. Journal of Neurology. 2007;254(11):1518-25.*
   A large German RCT with 409 participants comparing acupuncture, minimal acupuncture, and a waiting list control. Both real and minimal acupuncture significantly reduced headache days per month compared to the waiting list, suggesting that acupuncture has a powerful effect beyond placebo.

3. **Chinese herbal medicine for tension-type headache: a systematic review of randomized controlled trials**
   *วารสารวิชาการ: Zhang Y, Wang C, Liu J, et al. Chinese herbal medicine for tension-type headache: a systematic review. Journal of Alternative and Complementary Medicine. 2019;25(8):785-797.*
   A systematic review of 18 RCTs evaluating Chinese herbal formulas for tension-type headache. Most studies reported positive effects on headache intensity and frequency, but the overall methodological quality was low. The review highlighted the need for larger, placebo-controlled trials.
