## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนปัจจุบัน (Conventional Medicine View)
### คำอธิบาย (Description)
In Western medicine, dyspepsia refers to persistent or recurrent pain or discomfort in the upper abdomen, often described as bloating, early fullness during meals, or a burning sensation. When no structural cause - such as an ulcer, inflammation, or tumor - can be found on endoscopy, it is labeled functional dyspepsia. Diagnosis is typically made based on symptom history and the exclusion of other conditions like gastritis, gallstones, or reflux disease.
While the exact cause is not fully understood, Western medicine links functional dyspepsia to factors like abnormal stomach motility, heightened sensitivity to stomach distension, low-grade inflammation, and infection with H. pylori in some cases. Treatment is largely symptomatic, aiming to reduce acid, improve stomach emptying, or modulate pain perception.

### การรักษาหลัก (Treatments)
Standard treatment often begins with acid-suppressing medications such as proton pump inhibitors (PPIs) or H2 blockers. If bloating and early satiety are prominent, prokinetic agents that stimulate stomach emptying may be prescribed. For patients whose symptoms are closely tied to stress or anxiety, low-dose antidepressants can be used to modulate gut-brain signaling. When H. pylori is present, eradication therapy with antibiotics is recommended. Antacids and lifestyle advice - eating smaller meals, avoiding trigger foods - are also common first steps.

## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนจีน (Traditional Chinese Medicine View)
### ความเข้าใจเกี่ยวกับการแพทย์แผนจีน (TCM Understanding)
TCM understands dyspepsia primarily through the lens of the Middle Burner - the Spleen and Stomach, which are together responsible for receiving food, transforming it into usable energy (Qi), and transporting the clear upward and the turbid downward. The Stomach is meant to send food downward; when this descending function fails, Qi and undigested matter stagnate in the upper abdomen, creating the sensation of fullness and bloating known in TCM as "pǐ" (痞). This is never a random event - it always reflects an underlying disruption in the smooth flow of digestive Qi.
The Liver plays a surprisingly central role. One of its key jobs is to ensure the free flow of Qi throughout the body, including the digestive tract. Emotional stress, frustration, or pent-up anger can cause Liver Qi to stagnate and then "invade" the Stomach, blocking its downward movement and producing that stress-related bloating that eases with a sigh or belch. This is why so many people notice their digestion worsens during tense periods - the gut and the mind are directly connected through the Liver's regulatory function.
Dietary factors create another set of patterns. Overeating, eating too quickly, or consuming rich, greasy, or cold foods can overwhelm the Stomach's capacity, leading to Food Stagnation with its characteristic foul belching and thick tongue coating. In a warmer, wetter climate or with a diet heavy in damp-producing foods, Dampness and Heat can accumulate in the digestive system, causing a heavy, sticky fullness with a bitter taste. When the Spleen is constitutionally weak, fluids are not properly transformed and instead congeal into Phlegm-Dampness, which clogs the Middle Burner and creates a persistent, heavy sensation of oppression.
Finally, there are deficiency patterns where the digestive engine simply lacks the power to do its job. Spleen and Stomach Qi Deficiency leads to intermittent bloating that feels better with rest and warmth, often accompanied by fatigue after meals. When the Stomach's nourishing Yin fluids run low - from chronic heat, irregular eating, or prolonged stress - the digestive lining becomes dry and irritable, producing a gnawing fullness with a dry mouth and a red, peeling tongue. Each of these patterns requires a fundamentally different treatment strategy, which is why TCM does not offer a one-size-fits-all remedy for indigestion.

### การวินิจฉัยโดยแพทย์แผนจีน (Practitioner Diagnosis)
A TCM practitioner first explores the nature of the fullness-when it strikes, what makes it better or worse, and whether it’s linked to eating, emotions, or fatigue. The tongue and pulse are examined to confirm the underlying pattern, as each one leaves distinct marks on these diagnostic windows.
Stress-related fullness that moves around, with belching and sighing, points to Liver Qi Stagnation invading the Stomach. A wiry pulse supports this. When the same emotional trigger also brings a bitter taste, irritability, and a burning sensation, the picture shifts to Qi Stagnation in Gallbladder and Stomach with Phlegm Heat, where the tongue may have a yellow coating and the pulse is wiry and rapid.
Fullness that worsens after meals, with foul belching and a thick greasy tongue coating, suggests Food Stagnation in the Stomach. If there’s also a sticky mouth, bitter taste, and a yellow greasy coating, Damp-Heat in Stomach and Spleen is likely. A heavy, oppressive sensation with nausea and a thick white greasy coating points to Phlegm-Dampness in the Middle-Burner. The coating’s color and the presence of heat signs help narrow it down.
Intermittent fullness that feels better with pressure, warmth, or rest, along with fatigue and a pale tongue, indicates Spleen and Stomach Qi Deficiency; the pulse is weak. A gnawing, burning discomfort with dry mouth, a red tongue with little coating, and a thin rapid pulse reveals Stomach Yin Deficiency. These chronic patterns improve with nourishment, unlike the acute, heavy sensations of excess patterns.

## กลุ่มอาการการแพทย์แผนจีนและการรักษา (TCM Patterns and Treatment)

### Liver Qi Stagnation invading the Stomach
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/liver-qi-stagnation-invading-the-stomach)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Distending pain in the upper abdomen that radiates to the ribs, Worse with emotional stress or frustration, Frequent belching and sighing, Irritability and easy anger, Sensation of a lump in the throat
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Emotional stress, Cold, raw foods, Irregular eating patterns, Suppressing emotions, Overwork
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Gentle exercise or walking, Deep breathing and relaxation, Warm, cooked meals, Sighing or belching
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  The Liver is responsible for keeping Qi flowing smoothly throughout the body. When emotional stress, frustration, or anger build up, Liver Qi stagnates and can surge sideways, invading the Stomach.
This invasion disrupts the Stomach's normal downward movement, causing Qi to rebel upward. The result is bloating, distending pain in the upper abdomen that often radiates to the ribs, frequent belching, acid reflux, and a feeling of fullness that worsens with stress.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: normal or slightly red body, redder at the sides, thin white coat. Pulse: wiry (弦 xián), taut like a guitar string, especially at the left middle position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Emotional stress directly causes Liver Qi to stagnate, which then invades the Stomach, so reducing stress and moving Qi through gentle exercise or relaxation eases symptoms. Cold and raw foods weaken the Spleen and Stomach, making them more vulnerable to the Liver's attack; warm, cooked meals support digestive fire. Sighing and belching are the body's way of releasing trapped Qi, providing temporary relief from the pressure and fullness.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat smaller, regular meals and avoid rushing. Limit spicy, greasy, and acidic foods that can aggravate reflux. Practice stress-reducing activities like gentle walking, yoga, or deep breathing to keep Qi flowing smoothly. Sip warm water with a slice of fresh ginger to aid digestion.

### Food Stagnation in the Stomach
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/food-stagnation-in-the-stomach)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Epigastric fullness and distension that worsens after eating, Belching with a sour, rotten odour, Thick, greasy, curd-like tongue coating, Foul-smelling flatulence like rotten eggs
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Overeating, Greasy or fried foods, Late-night eating, Lying down after meals
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Vomiting, Gentle exercise or walking, Light, easily digestible meals, Warm water
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When you overeat or eat irregularly, the Stomach can't break down the food properly. The undigested food sits and ferments, causing Qi to stagnate and rise as foul belching. This creates a feeling of fullness and distension that worsens after meals, along with a thick greasy coating on the tongue reflecting the turbid accumulation.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: normal body with a thick, greasy or curd-like coating, thickest in the centre. Pulse: slippery (滑 huá) and full, especially at the right middle (Stomach) position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Overeating and rich foods overwhelm the Stomach's capacity to ripen and decompose, leading directly to food accumulation and Qi stagnation. Vomiting temporarily removes the stagnant mass, relieving pressure. Light movement encourages the Stomach Qi to descend, while small, easily digestible meals reduce the burden on the digestive system, allowing it to recover.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat smaller, more frequent meals and chew food thoroughly. Avoid greasy, sweet, and heavy foods, and don't eat late at night. A short, gentle walk after meals helps Qi descend and prevents stagnation.

### Damp-Heat in Stomach and Spleen
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/damp-heat-in-stomach-and-spleen)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Bloating and fullness in the upper abdomen that feels heavy and stuck, Bitter or sticky taste in the mouth, bad breath, Loose, sticky bowel movements that feel incomplete, Thick, yellow, greasy tongue coating, Heavy feeling in the body and limbs, foggy head, Thirst with little desire to drink
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Greasy or fried foods, Alcohol and sugary drinks, Hot, humid weather, Overeating or irregular meals, Emotional stress
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Light, easily digestible meals, Bitter and cooling foods (e.g., bitter gourd, mung beans), Cool environment, Gentle exercise or walking
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When rich, greasy foods, alcohol, or hot, humid environments overload the digestive system, Dampness and Heat build up in the Stomach and Spleen. This sticky, turbid obstruction bogs down the Spleen’s ability to transform food and fluids, while Heat disturbs the Stomach’s downward movement. The result is a heavy, bloated fullness in the upper abdomen that lingers, often with a bitter or sticky taste in the mouth.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red body, possibly swollen with teeth marks, thick yellow greasy coating especially at the centre and root. Pulse: soggy (Ru) and rapid (Shu), often also slippery (Hua).
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: <p>Rich, greasy, and spicy foods directly create Dampness and Heat, so they immediately worsen the bloating and sticky sensation. Alcohol and hot weather add more Heat and Dampness to an already overloaded system. On the other hand, light, cooling, and bitter foods help clear Heat and dry Dampness, while gentle exercise stimulates Qi flow and helps the Spleen transform fluids, gradually lifting the heavy, stuck feeling.</p>
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Steer clear of greasy, fried, and heavily spiced foods, as well as alcohol and dairy, which all generate Damp-Heat. Choose light, easily digested meals with cooling, bitter vegetables like bitter gourd and celery, and grains like barley. Gentle, regular exercise such as walking or yoga helps move Qi and drain Dampness.

### Phlegm-Dampness in the Middle-Burner
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/phlegm-dampness-in-the-middle-burner)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Feeling of fullness and stuffiness in the upper abdomen, White, thick, greasy tongue coating, Heavy sensation in the body and limbs, No thirst or desire to drink, Bland taste in the mouth
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Cold, raw foods, Greasy, heavy meals, Dairy products, Damp or humid weather, Sedentary habits
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warmth on the abdomen, Gentle exercise or walking, Warm, cooked meals, Ginger tea
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When the Spleen is weak, it cannot properly transform and transport fluids. These fluids accumulate into Dampness and then thicken into Phlegm, which obstructs the Middle Burner. The result is a heavy, stuck feeling of fullness and oppression in the upper abdomen, often accompanied by nausea and a thick, greasy tongue coating.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale, swollen with teeth marks, and a thick white greasy coating that is excessively moist. Pulse: slippery (滑 Huá), often slow or soggy, especially at the right middle position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Cold, raw, and greasy foods overload the Spleen’s ability to transform fluids, generating Dampness and Phlegm that clog the Middle Burner. A sedentary lifestyle further slows Qi movement, allowing Dampness to accumulate. Warmth and light exercise stimulate Qi and help dry out Dampness, while ginger and warm cooked foods support the Spleen’s digestive fire, restoring normal transport and relieving the stuck, heavy sensation.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat warm, cooked, easily digestible meals. Avoid cold, raw, and greasy foods, as well as dairy and sweets, which create Dampness. Gentle daily movement like walking helps Qi flow and dries Dampness. Include warming spices such as ginger, cardamom, and black pepper in your cooking.

### Spleen and Stomach Qi Deficiency

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Bloating that worsens after eating, Feeling better with warmth and gentle pressure, Poor appetite and loose stools, Fatigue and physical weakness, Sallow or pale complexion
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Overeating or large meals, Raw, cold, or greasy foods, Overwork, Worry and mental strain
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warmth on the abdomen, Small, frequent, warm meals, Rest and lying down briefly after eating, Gentle abdominal massage
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Your Spleen and Stomach are responsible for turning food into Qi and moving it through your digestive system. When their Qi is weakened, they lack the strength to transform and transport food properly. This leads to sluggish digestion, where food and fluids sit heavily, creating a sensation of bloating and fullness that worsens after meals because the weak system is easily overwhelmed. The bloating tends to come and go, often feeling better with rest or gentle warmth.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale, puffy, and tooth-marked with a thin white coating. Pulse: weak, soft, and forceless, especially empty at the right middle (Guan) position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Spleen Qi is like a gentle digestive fire that needs to be protected. Cold and raw foods douse that fire, while overeating overwhelms it, causing bloating. Warmth and small meals support the Spleen's transformative function, and rest conserves the Qi that would otherwise be diverted from digestion. Worry knots the Qi and further weakens the Spleen, so mental calm is as important as dietary care.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat warm, cooked foods like soups, stews, and congees that are easy to digest. Avoid raw, cold, or greasy foods that tax the Spleen. Eat smaller, regular meals and chew thoroughly. Gentle daily movement like walking supports Qi flow without depleting energy. Rest after meals when possible.

### Stomach Yin Deficiency

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Gnawing, burning discomfort in the upper abdomen, Dry mouth and throat with a thirst for small sips, Feeling hungry but not wanting to eat, Dry stools or constipation, Red tongue with little or no coating, dry centre
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Spicy or fried foods, Alcohol and coffee, Late nights and overwork, Emotional stress, Large, heavy meals
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warm water, Moistening foods like congee or pear, Rest and quiet, Cool environment, Gentle abdominal massage
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  The Stomach relies on a delicate layer of Yin fluids to moisten and protect its lining, much like oil in an engine. When those fluids run low - from chronic heat, irregular eating, or prolonged stress - the Stomach becomes dry and irritable. Food sits heavily without enough lubrication, creating a sensation of stuffiness and a dull, gnawing, burning discomfort.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red, thin body, dry centre, little or no coating, possibly with cracks. Pulse: fine (细 xì) and rapid (数 shuò), especially weak at the right middle position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Stomach Yin acts like the body's cooling and moistening reservoir. Spicy, fried, and drying habits burn through that reserve, making the burning sensation worse, while rest and small sips of fluid preserve it. Moist, easily digested foods gently replenish Yin without taxing the weakened digestion, allowing the Stomach to heal.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat small, frequent meals that are moist and easy to digest - congee, stews, steamed vegetables, and ripe pears are ideal. Avoid spicy, fried, or dry foods, and limit alcohol and coffee. Sip warm water or herbal teas throughout the day rather than drinking large amounts at once. Prioritize rest and avoid staying up late, as sleep is when Yin rebuilds itself.

### Qi Stagnation in Gallbladder and Stomach with Phlegm Heat

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Bitter taste in the mouth, Epigastric burning sensation and fullness, Irritability, anxiety, and frequent sighing, Restless sleep with vivid or disturbing dreams, Yellow greasy tongue coating
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Emotional stress, Greasy, fried, or spicy food, Alcohol and coffee, Late-night eating
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Light, easily digestible meals, Cool environment, Gentle exercise or walking, Bitter greens and small, frequent meals
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When emotional tension and stress block the Gallbladder's smooth flow, Qi gets stuck and turns into Heat. This stagnant Heat then disturbs the Stomach, preventing it from descending properly, while the body's fluids thicken into Phlegm. The combination of Heat and Phlegm clogs the middle burner, creating a heavy, burning fullness in the epigastrium that is often accompanied by a bitter taste, nausea, and chest tightness.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red body with a yellow greasy (sticky) coating; the sides may be slightly redder. Pulse: wiry (弦 xián) and slippery (滑 huá), and may also be rapid (数 shuò) when Heat is more pronounced.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Stress and emotional upset are the primary drivers of Qi stagnation in the Gallbladder, which then generates Heat and disrupts the Stomach. Greasy, spicy, or rich foods directly create Dampness and Phlegm, while alcohol and coffee add more Heat, thickening the Phlegm-Heat obstruction that causes the burning, bitter fullness. Light, bland foods and a calm routine take the burden off the digestive system, allowing the Stomach to recover its downward-moving function without adding more fuel to the fire. Gentle movement helps unstick stagnant Qi, and bitter foods naturally drain Heat from the body.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Steer clear of greasy, fried, or spicy foods, as well as alcohol and coffee, which all generate more Phlegm and Heat. Choose light, easily digested meals with plenty of lightly cooked vegetables. A regular eating schedule and a calm, unhurried mealtime help the Stomach do its job. Gentle movement like walking or yoga can keep Qi flowing smoothly.

## คำถามที่พบบ่อย (Frequently Asked Questions)
### How does acupuncture help with indigestion?
Acupuncture works by restoring the smooth flow of Qi in the digestive organs. Points on the abdomen, legs, and arms are stimulated to help the Stomach Qi descend, strengthen the Spleen's transforming ability, and release Liver Qi stagnation that may be interfering with digestion. Many patients feel a sense of relaxation and gentle movement in the belly during or after treatment, and regular sessions can retrain the gut-brain connection over time.

### Can I take TCM herbs alongside my antacids or PPIs?
In most cases, yes. TCM herbal formulas and conventional acid reducers can be used together, but they should be taken at least one to two hours apart to avoid interference. Always inform both your TCM practitioner and your prescribing doctor about all medications and supplements you are taking. Certain herbs with cooling or bitter properties may enhance the acid-lowering effect, so your practitioner may adjust dosages accordingly.

### Will I need to change my diet?
Diet is a cornerstone of TCM treatment for any digestive condition. You will likely be advised to eat warm, cooked foods and avoid cold, raw, greasy, or overly spicy dishes while your digestion heals. The specific recommendations depend on your pattern - someone with Damp-Heat will benefit from bitter greens and mung beans, while someone with Spleen Qi Deficiency needs easily digestible congees and stews. These changes are not permanent restrictions but rather a reset to help your digestive fire recover.

### Can stress really cause bloating and indigestion?
Absolutely. In TCM, the Liver is responsible for the smooth flow of Qi throughout the body, and emotional tension directly causes Liver Qi to stagnate. When this stagnant energy "invades" the Stomach, it blocks the Stomach's normal downward movement, leading to bloating, belching, and a sensation of fullness that often moves around. This is one of the most common patterns we see, and it explains why many people's digestion worsens during stressful periods.

### How long until I feel better?
Most people notice some improvement within the first two to four weeks of consistent treatment. Excess patterns caused by recent dietary indiscretion or acute stress tend to resolve faster. If your digestion has been sluggish for years and involves a deeper deficiency, it may take three to six months to rebuild digestive strength. Your practitioner will track your tongue and pulse changes to gauge progress even before symptoms fully disappear.

### Is bloating always a sign of poor digestion?
Not always, but it is a core sign of Qi stagnation in the digestive system. In TCM, bloating that moves around and improves with belching often points to Liver Qi stagnation, while a fixed, heavy fullness after meals suggests food stagnation or dampness. If you experience bloating without eating, or if it is accompanied by other symptoms like weight loss or blood in the stool, it's important to see a doctor to rule out more serious conditions.
