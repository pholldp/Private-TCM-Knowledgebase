## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนปัจจุบัน (Conventional Medicine View)
### คำอธิบาย (Description)
In Western medicine, cardiac arrest is the sudden, unexpected loss of heart function, breathing, and consciousness. It is most often caused by an electrical malfunction in the heart - such as ventricular fibrillation - that disrupts the heart's pumping action. Without immediate treatment, including cardiopulmonary resuscitation (CPR) and defibrillation, it is fatal within minutes. Survivors often require intensive care to manage post-cardiac arrest syndrome, which can involve brain injury, heart dysfunction, and systemic inflammation.

### การรักษาหลัก (Treatments)
After cardiac arrest, conventional treatment focuses on stabilizing the patient in the intensive care unit. This includes targeted temperature management (therapeutic hypothermia), medications to support blood pressure and heart function, and mechanical ventilation. Once stable, doctors address the underlying cause - for example, with coronary angioplasty for a blocked artery. Long-term management may include beta-blockers, antiarrhythmic drugs, and an implantable cardioverter-defibrillator (ICD) to prevent recurrence.

## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนจีน (Traditional Chinese Medicine View)
### ความเข้าใจเกี่ยวกับการแพทย์แผนจีน (TCM Understanding)
In TCM, cardiac arrest is seen as a sudden, catastrophic collapse of the body's most fundamental energies - Yin and Yang. When Yang collapses, the body loses its warming, activating force. The person becomes deathly pale, the limbs turn ice-cold, and the pulse fades to almost nothing. This is the classic "Collapse of Yang" pattern, a state where the life-fire is nearly extinguished.
When Yin collapses, it is the body's cooling, moistening foundation that fails. The skin becomes dry and wrinkled, the mouth parched, and a deceptive heat may appear on the body's surface. This "Collapse of Yin" pattern reflects the final drain of the body's deepest fluids, leaving the heart without nourishment and the spirit unanchored.
After resuscitation, the immediate crisis may pass, but the heart's Yang often remains severely depleted - a pattern called Heart Yang Deficiency. This leaves the person feeling cold in the chest and limbs, with a weak pulse and lingering breathlessness. The circulatory standstill also commonly leads to Blood Stagnation, where purple lips, a dark tongue, and stabbing chest discomfort signal that blood is not moving freely through the vessels.
In some cases, the weakened body fluids congeal into Phlegm, which can rise to cloud the Heart's orifices - the gateways to consciousness. This pattern, Phlegm Misting the Heart, manifests as mental fog, slurred speech, and a gurgling sound in the throat. A fifth pattern, Qi Deficiency causing Blood Stagnation, combines profound fatigue with subtle signs of stasis, showing that the body lacks the energy to keep blood flowing smoothly. Each of these patterns requires a different treatment strategy, making accurate differentiation essential for recovery.

### การวินิจฉัยโดยแพทย์แผนจีน (Practitioner Diagnosis)
A TCM practitioner first looks at the immediate crisis. When the heart suddenly stops, the body's Yin and Yang collapse. The key question is whether the collapse is mainly a loss of warming Yang or a loss of cooling Yin. The answer shapes the emergency approach.
If the person is deathly pale, with ice-cold limbs and a pulse so faint it is barely felt, this points to Collapse of Yang (亡阳, wáng yáng). The tongue looks pale and shortened. The body's activating, warming Yang Qi has drained away, leaving coldness and stillness.
If instead the skin is dry and wrinkled, the mouth is parched, and the pulse is thin and rapid, that signals Collapse of Yin (亡阴, wáng yīn). The tongue may be pale or deep red with very little moisture. Here the body's nourishing, cooling Yin fluids have been exhausted, often from pre‑existing deficiency or severe fluid loss.
After resuscitation the picture shifts. A common lingering pattern is Heart Yang Deficiency. The person still feels cold in the chest and limbs, and the pulse remains deep and weak. The tongue is pale and puffy. The heart's warming force has not yet recovered, so circulation stays sluggish.
The circulatory collapse and resuscitation process almost always leave some Blood Stagnation. Bluish lips, a purplish tongue, and stabbing chest pain are tell‑tale signs. The pulse may feel hesitant or choppy, as if the blood is struggling to move smoothly through the vessels.
In some post‑arrest patients, Phlegm Misting the Heart clouds consciousness. The person remains unconscious or drifts in and out, with gurgling throat sounds and a slippery pulse. The tongue is dark red with a greasy coating. Turbid phlegm is obstructing the heart's orifices, blocking clear awareness.
During later recovery, Qi Deficiency causing Blood Stagnation may appear. Fatigue and shortness of breath are prominent, with milder blood stasis signs like a slightly purple tongue and a hesitant pulse. The body's vital energy is too weak to keep blood moving, so stagnation develops gradually.

### Collapse of Yang
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/collapse-of-yang)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Ice-cold limbs extending past elbows and knees, Profuse cold sweating, Ashen-white or greyish complexion, Pulse extremely faint or absent
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Cold environment, Overexertion, Cold foods and drinks, Emotional shock
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warmth, Moxibustion, Rest, Warm drinks
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  In cardiac arrest, the body's Yang Qi - the vital warming and activating force - suddenly collapses. Without Yang Qi, the heart cannot pump blood, consciousness fades, and the limbs become ice-cold. This is the classic picture of “death” in Chinese medicine, where the life-fire is extinguished and the pulse becomes almost imperceptible.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: Pale, moist, flaccid body with thin white slippery coating. Pulse: Minute (微 wēi), on the verge of disappearing, or completely impalpable.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Warmth and moxibustion add Yang Qi to the body, helping to rekindle the internal fire that has nearly gone out. Cold, exertion, and emotional shock further drain the already depleted Yang and Yin, making collapse worse. Warm broths gently nourish without overtaxing the weakened digestive system.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: This is a life-threatening emergency requiring immediate modern medical resuscitation. In the recovery phase, keep the person warm, offer small amounts of warm, easy-to-digest broths like ginger and chicken soup, and avoid all cold foods and drinks. Gentle moxibustion on the lower abdomen can support Yang recovery.

### Collapse of Yin
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/collapse-of-yin)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Profuse hot, sticky sweat (beading like oil), Body burning hot to the touch, warm hands and feet, Extreme thirst with craving for cold drinks, Deep red or crimson, completely dry tongue (mirror tongue), Dry, cracked lips and dry throat, Sunken eye sockets, wrinkled skin, Intense restlessness and agitation, Very reduced or absent urination
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Excessive sweating or fluid loss, Spicy, drying foods, Overexertion, Hot, dry environment, Emotional stress
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Cool, quiet environment, Sipping cool water slowly, Rest, Moistening foods (pears, congee)
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Yin is the body's cooling, moistening foundation. When it is severely drained - by high fever, profuse sweating, vomiting, or chronic wasting - the heart loses its nourishment. Unanchored Yang then flares upward, creating the illusion of heat and agitation. As the last reserves of Yin vanish, the heart can no longer sustain its rhythm, and cardiac arrest follows.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: deep red or crimson body, completely dry, no coating (mirror tongue), may be shrunken and cracked. Pulse: fine and rapid (数疾 shuò jí), weak and hollow on pressure.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Yin is the body's coolant and moisture. Anything that drains fluids or adds internal heat - like sweating, spicy foods, or overwork - deepens the Yin crisis, making collapse more likely. Cooling, rest, and gentle fluid replenishment help preserve the last drops of Yin, giving the heart a chance to stabilize.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: In an acute collapse, emergency medical care is paramount. After stabilization, avoid spicy, drying, and fried foods, as well as alcohol and coffee. Focus on moistening, Yin-nourishing foods such as pears, congee, bone broth, and small amounts of cool water. Rest deeply and avoid sweating or overexertion.

### Heart Yang Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/heart-yang-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Cold hands and feet that persist after resuscitation, Bright pale or white face, Chest stuffiness or mild chest pain, Fatigue and lack of strength, Spontaneous sweating, Mental listlessness, Palpitations that worsen with exertion
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Cold environment, Overexertion, Cold foods and drinks, Emotional stress
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warmth, Rest, Warm soups and stews, Moxibustion, Gentle walking
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  The Heart's Yang is the fire that drives circulation and keeps the body warm. After cardiac arrest, even when spontaneous circulation returns, the Heart Yang is often severely depleted. This leaves the body unable to stay warm, so cold limbs persist, and the heart's pumping remains weak, causing chest oppression, palpitations, and a deep, feeble pulse.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale, puffy, and tender with teeth marks and a white slippery coating. Pulse: deep and weak, especially at the left cun position; may be knotted (结代 jié dài) with irregular pauses.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Heart Yang is the body's warming fire. Cold, both environmental and dietary, directly depletes this fire, worsening cold limbs and chest oppression. Rest conserves the little Yang that remains, while overexertion drains it. Warm foods and moxibustion add heat to the system, helping to reignite the Heart's Yang and improve circulation.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Keep the body warm, especially the chest and feet. Eat warm, cooked foods like soups, stews, and congees with ginger and cinnamon. Avoid raw, cold, and iced foods and drinks. Gentle breathing exercises or walking when stable can help circulate Qi without exhausting it. Moxibustion at home on the lower abdomen (Guanyuan area) can be supportive under guidance.

### Blood Stagnation
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/blood-stagnation)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Fixed, stabbing chest pain, Pain worse at night or with pressure, Purple or dusky lips and nails, Dark purplish tongue with stasis spots, Dark or dusky complexion
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Cold weather or cold drinks, Sedentary lifestyle, Emotional stress, Heavy, greasy meals, Overexertion
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Gentle walking, Warmth on the chest, Warm drinks, Massage or acupressure, Rest
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When blood flow becomes sluggish or obstructed, it can form clots that block the heart's vessels. In cardiac arrest, this severe stagnation prevents the heart from pumping effectively, causing a sudden stop. The resulting lack of circulation leads to cyanosis, stabbing chest pain, and loss of consciousness. After resuscitation, residual blood stasis often persists, manifesting as a purplish tongue and fixed pain.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: dark purple body, possible stasis spots, distended dark sublingual veins, thin white coat. Pulse: choppy (涩 sè) or wiry (弦 xián).
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Cold causes blood to congeal and vessels to contract, worsening stasis. Inactivity and stress stagnate Qi, which then fails to move blood. Gentle movement and warmth invigorate blood and Qi, helping to dissolve stasis. A calm mind allows Qi to flow smoothly, supporting circulation.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat warming, blood-moving foods like hawthorn, turmeric, garlic, and small amounts of vinegar. Avoid cold, raw foods and icy drinks that congeal blood. Gentle daily exercise such as walking or tai chi helps keep circulation moving. Keep the chest warm and avoid prolonged sitting. Manage stress with deep breathing or meditation to prevent Qi stagnation.

### Phlegm Misting the Heart
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/phlegm-misting-the-heart)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Mental confusion or clouded consciousness, Rattling or gurgling sound in the throat, Thick white greasy tongue coating on a swollen tongue, Chest stuffiness and feeling of oppression, Nausea or urge to vomit, Slurred or unclear speech
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Heavy, greasy meals, Cold, damp weather, Sedentary lifestyle, Overeating, Emotional stress
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warm, dry environment, Light, warm meals (e.g., congee), Gentle walking, Quiet, calm atmosphere
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  After cardiac arrest, the body's Yang Qi is weakened, allowing body fluids to congeal into phlegm. This thick, turbid phlegm then rises to obstruct the Heart's orifices - the gateways to consciousness. As a result, the mind becomes clouded, speech slurred, and a characteristic gurgling sound appears in the throat.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale swollen body with a thick, white, greasy coating; may appear stiff. Pulse: slippery (滑 huá), like beads rolling, especially strong in the middle positions.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Phlegm is a Yin pathogen that thrives on dampness and stagnation. Heavy, greasy foods and cold damp environments encourage its formation, while warm, dry conditions and light meals help the body transform it. Gentle movement activates Qi, which is essential to move and clear phlegm, whereas a sedentary lifestyle allows phlegm to accumulate and cloud the Heart.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat warm, cooked, easily digestible foods like congee, steamed vegetables, and small amounts of lean protein. Strictly avoid dairy, greasy, fried, and sugary foods, which generate phlegm. Gentle daily movement, once stable, helps Qi flow and transforms phlegm. Keep the living space dry and well-ventilated; damp environments worsen the condition.

### Qi Deficiency causing Blood Stagnation
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/qi-deficiency-causing-blood-stagnation)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Fatigue and lack of strength, Fixed stabbing chest pain, Shortness of breath on exertion, Reluctance to speak or weak voice, Dark or dusky complexion, Pale face with a greyish tinge
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Overexertion, Cold environment, Cold foods and drinks, Emotional stress
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Gentle walking, Warm, nourishing meals, Rest, Warmth on the chest
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  After a cardiac arrest, the heart's Qi is profoundly depleted. Qi is the vital force that propels blood through the vessels. When Qi is too weak to move blood effectively, circulation becomes sluggish and stagnant, creating a state of blood stasis. This dual deficiency-and-stagnation pattern shows up as lingering fatigue, shortness of breath, and mild stasis signs like a purplish tongue and stabbing chest pain, which can slow recovery and keep the heart from regaining its full strength.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale-dark or pale-purple body with possible stasis spots, teeth marks, thin white coat. Pulse: deep (Chen), fine or weak (Xi/Ruo), and choppy (Se).
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Overexertion and stress drain Qi further, leaving even less force to move blood, which worsens stasis and fatigue. Cold constricts the vessels and slows circulation, aggravating blood stasis. Rest and warmth conserve Qi and gently open the vessels, allowing blood to flow more smoothly. Gentle movement like walking stimulates Qi and blood without depleting reserves, while warm, nourishing foods supply the raw materials to rebuild Qi and support circulation.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat warm, easily digestible foods like congee, chicken broth, and stewed root vegetables to gently rebuild Qi. Add small amounts of ginger, cinnamon, or turmeric to warm the interior and move blood. Avoid cold, raw, and greasy foods that tax digestion and slow circulation. Gentle daily movement such as walking or qigong helps move Qi and blood without exhausting your reserves. Rest deeply and avoid overexertion, especially in the early recovery phase.

## คำถามที่พบบ่อย (Frequently Asked Questions)
### Can TCM help after I've had a cardiac arrest?
Yes, TCM can play a supportive role during recovery. Once you are medically stable, acupuncture and herbal formulas can help address lingering symptoms like extreme fatigue, cold limbs, mental fog, and chest discomfort. The goal is to rebuild your body's Yang and Qi, move any blood stasis, and clear phlegm that may be clouding your mind. Many survivors find that TCM helps them regain warmth, energy, and mental clarity when conventional rehabilitation alone feels insufficient.

### Is it safe to take Chinese herbs with my heart medications?
It is often possible to combine Chinese herbs with conventional cardiac drugs, but this must be done under the supervision of both your cardiologist and a qualified TCM practitioner. Some herbs, like Dan Shen or Chuan Xiong, have mild blood-thinning effects and could interact with anticoagulants such as warfarin or antiplatelet drugs like aspirin. Always bring a complete list of your medications to your TCM consultation. Never stop or adjust your prescribed heart medications on your own.

### How soon after cardiac arrest can I start acupuncture?
Acupuncture can begin as soon as you are medically stable and out of intensive care, with your doctor's approval. In the early rehabilitation phase, treatments are gentle and focus on restoring Yang and calming the spirit. Points like Neiguan PC-6 and Guanyuan REN-4 are commonly used to support heart function and anchor the body's energy. Your acupuncturist will adjust the intensity based on your strength and tolerance.

### Will acupuncture help with the crushing fatigue I feel?
Yes, fatigue is one of the most common complaints after cardiac arrest, and acupuncture can be very helpful. In TCM, this exhaustion is usually a sign of Qi and Yang deficiency. Regular acupuncture, combined with warming moxibustion and Qi-tonifying herbs, can gradually rebuild your energy reserves. Most patients notice a subtle lift in stamina within a few weeks, though full recovery of vitality often takes several months.

### What dietary changes can support my recovery?
After cardiac arrest, the body needs gentle, warming, and easily digestible foods to rebuild Yang and Qi. Favor warm soups, stews, congee, and cooked vegetables. Ingredients like ginger, cinnamon, and black pepper can help warm the interior. Avoid raw, cold foods and icy drinks, which tax the digestive system and further deplete Yang. In patterns with Phlegm, reduce dairy, greasy foods, and sweets. Small, frequent meals are better than large ones to avoid overburdening the heart.

### Can TCM prevent another cardiac arrest?
TCM cannot guarantee prevention, but it can help strengthen your body's underlying resilience. By correcting patterns like Heart Yang Deficiency or Blood Stagnation, TCM aims to restore the smooth flow of Qi and blood, which may reduce the risk of future cardiac events. This is always done in conjunction with conventional preventive measures like medications and ICDs. A healthier, more balanced body is better equipped to withstand stress.
