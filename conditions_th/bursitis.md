## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนปัจจุบัน (Conventional Medicine View)
### คำอธิบาย (Description)
In Western medicine, bursitis is inflammation of a bursa, a small fluid-filled sac that cushions bones, tendons, and muscles near joints. It commonly affects the shoulder, elbow, hip, or knee, and causes localized pain, swelling, and limited movement. It can be triggered by repetitive motion, prolonged pressure, injury, or infection. Diagnosis is usually made through physical examination and sometimes imaging like ultrasound or MRI.

### การรักษาหลัก (Treatments)
Standard treatment includes rest, ice, compression, and elevation (RICE), along with anti-inflammatory medications like ibuprofen. If these don't help, a doctor may drain the bursa or inject a corticosteroid to reduce inflammation. Physical therapy is often recommended to strengthen surrounding muscles and prevent recurrence. In cases of infection, antibiotics are necessary.

## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนจีน (Traditional Chinese Medicine View)
### ความเข้าใจเกี่ยวกับการแพทย์แผนจีน (TCM Understanding)
TCM views bursitis as a form of "Bi syndrome" (painful obstruction), where the flow of Qi and blood in the channels around the joint is blocked. The blockage can be caused by external pathogens like wind, cold, and dampness invading the body, or by internal imbalances that generate dampness, heat, or phlegm. The specific quality of pain and swelling - hot vs. cold, throbbing vs. achy - reveals which factors are dominant.
The Spleen plays a central role because it transforms fluids; when weak, dampness accumulates and can settle in the bursa, causing swelling. The Kidney nourishes bones and joints; when Kidney Yin is depleted, the joint lubrication dries up, leading to chronic stiffness. The Liver ensures smooth flow of blood; stagnation from injury or stress leads to blood stasis and fixed pain. So bursitis is never just a local problem - it's a reflection of the whole-body balance.
For example, if you develop bursitis after sitting in a cold, damp room, and it feels better with a heating pad, Wind-Cold-Damp obstruction is likely. If it flares up after a spicy meal with redness and heat, Damp Heat is the culprit. A direct blow that leaves a lingering, stabbing pain points to Blood Stagnation. And if you have persistent, puffy swelling with fatigue and bloating, Spleen Deficiency is the root.
This is why TCM doesn't have one generic bursitis treatment - the formula and acupuncture points are tailored to your exact pattern.

### การวินิจฉัยโดยแพทย์แผนจีน (Practitioner Diagnosis)
A TCM practitioner starts by asking what the pain feels like and what makes it better or worse. The quality of the discomfort-whether it is hot and throbbing, cold and stiff, or a fixed stabbing sensation-is the first clue that points toward one pattern rather than another.
If the affected bursa is red, swollen, and warm to the touch, with a feeling of heat and a greasy yellow coating on the tongue, the picture is Damp Heat in the Channels. The pulse will often feel rapid and slippery, confirming that heat and moisture are trapped in the local area.
When the pain is fixed, stabbing, and there is a history of a direct blow or repetitive strain, Blood Stagnation is the likely pattern. The tongue may appear dark purple or show purple spots, and the pulse will feel wiry or choppy, indicating that blood is not flowing freely through the injured tissue.
If cold weather or a draft clearly worsens the aching and stiffness, and warmth brings relief, the pattern is Wind-Cold-Damp Obstruction. Here the tongue is often pale with a thin white coating, and the pulse feels tight or floating, reflecting an invasion of external cold and dampness that has settled in the joint.
Chronic bursitis with persistent, doughy swelling but little heat or redness often points to Phlegm in the Channels. The tongue coating is typically thick and white, and the pulse may feel slippery or deep, showing that congealed fluids have lodged in the soft tissues rather than acute inflammation.
A person who tires easily, has a poor appetite, and notices that the swelling feels heavy and worse after eating may have an underlying Spleen Deficiency with Dampness. The tongue is pale and puffy with a white coating, and the pulse is weak, revealing that the digestive system is not managing fluids properly.
In long-standing, low-grade bursitis accompanied by night sweats, a sore lower back, or a dry mouth, Kidney Yin Deficiency is a possible root cause. The tongue appears red with little or no coating, and the pulse is thin and rapid, signaling a deep depletion of the body's cooling and nourishing resources.

### Painful Obstruction due to Damp Heat in Channels
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/painful-obstruction-due-to-damp-heat-in-channels)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Joint pain with redness, heat, and swelling, Pain relieved by cold, aggravated by warmth, Heavy, aching sensation in limbs, Yellow greasy tongue coating, Fever or sensation of bodily heat, Irritability and restlessness
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Hot, humid weather, Spicy, greasy, or fried foods, Alcohol, Overuse or physical overwork
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Cold compress or ice, Rest, Cooling foods (cucumber, watermelon), Elevating the joint
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Bursitis in this pattern arises when Dampness and Heat combine and invade the body's channels, often due to hot, humid weather or a diet rich in greasy, spicy foods. These pathogenic factors settle into the bursa, where Dampness causes fluid accumulation and swelling, while Heat creates redness, inflammation, and intense pain. The result is an acutely hot, swollen, and painful joint that feels better with cold and worse with warmth.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red body, possibly slightly swollen, with a yellow greasy coating. Pulse: slippery (滑 huá) and rapid (数 shuò).
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Damp-Heat is a combination of pathogenic Dampness and Heat. Heat and humidity add fuel to the fire, while spicy, greasy foods and alcohol create internal Damp-Heat, directly worsening the condition. Cold and cooling foods counteract the Heat, and rest prevents further Qi stagnation and fluid accumulation in the bursa.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: During a flare, rest the joint and apply cold compresses to reduce heat and swelling. Avoid spicy, greasy, and fried foods, as well as alcohol, which all generate Damp-Heat. Instead, eat cooling, damp-draining foods like barley, mung beans, cucumber, and watermelon. Gentle, non-weight-bearing movement can help prevent stiffness once the acute pain subsides.

### Blood Stagnation
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/blood-stagnation)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Fixed stabbing pain that does not move, Pain worse at night and with pressure, Local swelling or a palpable hard lump, Dark purplish tongue with stasis spots
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Cold, damp weather, Overuse or physical overwork, Direct pressure on the bursa, Stress and frustration
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Applying warmth, Gentle movement or stretching, Rest
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When a bursa is injured by a direct blow, repetitive strain, or overuse, the local blood flow is disrupted, leading to blood stasis in the area. This stagnant blood acts like a bruise that doesn't resolve, causing a fixed, stabbing pain and a sense of fullness or a hard lump around the joint.
Because the blood isn't moving smoothly, fresh nutrients and oxygen can't reach the tissues, and metabolic waste builds up, prolonging inflammation and stiffness. The pain is often worse at night and with pressure, which are classic signs of blood stasis.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: dark purple or dusky body, may have stasis spots, sublingual veins distended and dark. Pulse: choppy (涩 sè) like a knife scraping bamboo, or wiry (弦 xián) if Qi stagnation is also present.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Blood stasis is like a traffic jam in the vessels. Cold and inactivity congeal the blood, making the jam worse and intensifying pain. Warmth and gentle movement act like a traffic cop, helping to disperse the stasis and restore smooth flow, which relieves pain and swelling. Stress can cause Qi to stagnate, and since Qi moves blood, this further slows circulation.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Avoid cold and raw foods, which can congeal blood and worsen stasis. Gentle, regular movement like walking or swimming helps keep blood flowing without overstraining the bursa. Warm compresses or a heating pad on the area can ease pain.

### Painful Obstruction with Wind-Cold-Damp
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/painful-obstruction-with-wind-cold-damp)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Deep, achy pain worse in cold and damp, Joint stiffness and limited movement, Heaviness or numbness in the limb, Swelling without redness or heat, Aversion to cold or wind
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Cold, damp weather, Prolonged inactivity, Exposure to wind, Cold or raw foods
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Applying warmth, Gentle movement or stretching, Warm, dry weather, Warm drinks and soups
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When your body is exposed to wind, cold, and dampness, these pathogens can invade the channels and settle around a bursa. Cold contracts and tightens the tissues, while dampness is heavy and congealing - together they block the flow of Qi and Blood. This stagnation produces a deep, achy pain and stiffness that feels worse in chilly or wet weather, much like a rusty hinge that seizes up.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale, slightly swollen with teeth marks, white greasy coating, moist. Pulse: wiry and tight (弦紧 xián jǐn) or soggy (濡 rú) if Dampness predominates.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Cold and dampness are the very pathogens that cause this pattern - they constrict and congest the channels, so any exposure intensifies the pain and stiffness. Warmth relaxes the tissues and encourages circulation, while gentle movement pumps Qi and Blood through the area, preventing stagnation. Dry, warm conditions and cooked foods reduce the external and internal dampness, lightening the load on your joints.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Keep the affected area warm and covered, especially in cold or damp weather. Gentle, regular movement like walking or tai chi helps keep Qi flowing, but avoid overexertion. Favor warm, cooked foods and warming spices such as ginger, cinnamon, and black pepper; steer clear of raw, cold, or greasy foods that create more dampness.

### Phlegm in the Channels joints and muscles
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/phlegm-in-the-channels-joints-and-muscles)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Fixed, stabbing pain in the joint, Swelling that feels firm or hard to the touch, Purplish or dark discoloration around the joint, Subcutaneous nodules or hard lumps near the joint, Stubborn numbness or heavy sensation in the limbs
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Cold, damp weather, Greasy, heavy foods, Prolonged inactivity, Overuse or physical overwork
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warmth and gentle movement, Light, warm meals, Gentle movement or stretching
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When the body's fluids are not properly transformed due to a weak Spleen or chronic dampness, they can thicken into Phlegm-a sticky, congealed substance that lodges in the joints. This Phlegm physically obstructs the flow of Qi and Blood, leading to Blood Stasis in the local area. Together, the Phlegm and stagnant Blood create a firm, persistent swelling and a fixed, stabbing pain deep within the bursa, often limiting movement.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: purple body with stasis spots, white greasy coating, distended sublingual veins. Pulse: wiry (Xian) and choppy (Se), or wiry and slippery (Hua) if Phlegm predominates.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Cold and dampness congeal Phlegm and Blood, worsening the obstruction, so warmth and circulation are essential. Greasy and heavy foods burden the Spleen, generating more Dampness and Phlegm, while light eating supports fluid transformation. Gentle movement keeps Qi and Blood flowing, preventing the stagnation that feeds the hard swelling, but too much strain can re-injure the area and add to the stasis.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Avoid cold, raw, and greasy foods that create Dampness and Phlegm. Favor warm, cooked meals with spices like ginger and turmeric to support fluid metabolism. Gentle daily movement helps circulate Qi and Blood, but avoid overexertion that could further irritate the bursa.

### Spleen Deficiency with Dampness
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/spleen-deficiency-with-dampness)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Dull, heavy ache in the bursa, Puffy, non-red swelling, Worse with damp weather or after heavy meals, Loose stools and abdominal bloating, Fatigue and sensation of heaviness in the limbs
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Damp, humid weather, Greasy, heavy foods, Overeating or eating late, Prolonged inactivity, Overuse or physical overwork
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warm, dry weather, Light, warm meals, Gentle movement or stretching, Applying warmth, Rest
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  The Spleen is responsible for transforming food and fluids into energy and clear fluids. When it is weak, fluids are not properly processed and instead accumulate as Dampness in the body. This heavy, sticky Dampness can settle in the bursae, causing persistent, puffy swelling and a dull, achy pain that lacks the redness and heat of acute inflammation.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale, swollen body with teeth marks, white greasy coating, moist surface. Pulse: soggy (Ru) and slowed-down (Huan), lacking force.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: <p>A weak Spleen cannot transform fluids properly, so Dampness accumulates. Damp weather and heavy foods add more Dampness, worsening swelling and heaviness. Warmth and light, cooked meals support the Spleen's function, while gentle movement helps Qi circulate and drain fluids. Rest prevents further depletion of Qi, allowing the Spleen to recover.</p>
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat warm, cooked meals and avoid raw, cold, or greasy foods that weaken the Spleen. Include barley, adzuki beans, and ginger to help drain Dampness. Gentle daily movement like walking helps Qi flow and prevents fluid stagnation.

### Kidney Yin Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/kidney-yin-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Dull, chronic joint pain that feels better with rest, Night sweats and heat in the palms, soles, and chest, Soreness and weakness of the lower back and knees, Dry mouth and throat, especially at night
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Overwork and late nights, Spicy, greasy, or fried foods, Hot weather or saunas
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Rest, Cooling, moistening foods, Gentle movement or stretching
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  In chronic bursitis, the body's deep reserves of Yin become depleted over time, often from overwork, ageing, or long-standing inflammation. Kidney Yin is responsible for nourishing the bones, joints, and sinews. When it runs low, the joint tissues lose their lubrication and flexibility, leading to a persistent dull ache and stiffness. The empty heat that arises from Yin deficiency can also create a mild, deep warmth around the joint, but without the acute redness and swelling seen in damp-heat patterns.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red, thin body with little or no coating, dry surface, possible cracks. Pulse: fine (细 xì) and rapid (数 shuò), weak at the left rear (Kidney) position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Kidney Yin is the body's cooling and moistening essence. Overwork and heat deplete it, worsening dryness and joint stiffness. Rest, cooling foods, and gentle movement preserve Yin and help the joints stay supple.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Include nourishing Yin foods like black sesame seeds, walnuts, goji berries, and cooked pears. Avoid spicy, fried, or overly heating foods that consume Yin. Gentle practices like Tai Chi or Qi Gong support joint health without exhausting Yin, and going to bed before 11pm preserves the body's Yin reserves.

## คำถามที่พบบ่อย (Frequently Asked Questions)
### How does acupuncture help bursitis?
Acupuncture works by unblocking the flow of Qi and blood in the channels surrounding the affected joint. By inserting fine needles into local points around the bursa and distal points on the body, it helps reduce swelling, relieve pain, and calm inflammation. It also addresses the underlying pattern - for example, clearing damp heat or moving blood stasis - so that the bursa can heal and the problem doesn't keep coming back.

### Can I still use ice or heat while doing TCM?
Yes, but which one helps depends on your pattern. If your bursitis is hot, red, and throbbing (Damp Heat), ice or cold compresses are generally more suitable. If it's achy, stiff, and worsens in cold weather (Wind-Cold-Damp), applying warmth is better. Your TCM practitioner can guide you on what's best for your specific pattern, but using the wrong temperature won't harm you - it just might not be as effective.

### Will Chinese herbs interact with my ibuprofen or other medications?
In most cases, herbs can be used safely alongside over-the-counter NSAIDs. However, certain blood-moving herbs (like Dang Gui, Chuan Xiong, Tao Ren) may increase the effect of blood-thinning medications such as warfarin or aspirin. Always bring a full list of your medications to your TCM consultation and tell your doctor you are taking Chinese herbs. Your practitioner can adjust the formula to avoid any potential interactions.

### How many acupuncture sessions will I need?
For acute bursitis, you may feel significant relief after just 2-3 sessions, though a full course of 6-8 weekly sessions is typical to resolve the flare-up and prevent recurrence. Chronic cases often require a longer commitment - weekly treatments for 4-8 weeks, then gradually spacing out to every other week or monthly as the joint stabilizes. Your practitioner will reassess your progress and adjust the plan accordingly.

### Is it safe to combine TCM with a cortisone injection?
Yes, many patients use TCM alongside cortisone injections. Acupuncture and herbs can help manage pain and swelling while you wait for the injection to take effect, and they may reduce the need for repeat injections over time. Just be sure to inform both your orthopedist and your TCM practitioner about all treatments you're receiving so they can coordinate your care safely.

### What should I eat to help heal bursitis?
In general, a diet that supports the Spleen and minimizes dampness is helpful. Focus on warm, cooked foods like soups, stews, and steamed vegetables, and limit cold, raw foods, ice-cold drinks, dairy, sugar, and greasy fried foods. If your bursitis is hot and red, favor cooling foods like cucumber and watermelon; if it's cold and achy, add warming spices like ginger and cinnamon. Staying well-hydrated with warm water or herbal teas also helps keep fluids moving.
