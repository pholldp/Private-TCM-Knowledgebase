## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนปัจจุบัน (Conventional Medicine View)
### คำอธิบาย (Description)
In Western medicine, anxiety disorders are characterized by excessive worry, fear, and apprehension that interfere with daily life. Common symptoms include restlessness, muscle tension, irritability, difficulty concentrating, sleep disturbances, and physical signs like a racing heart or upset stomach. Diagnosis is based on clinical criteria and may be categorized as generalized anxiety disorder, panic disorder, or other specific forms. While the exact cause is not fully understood, factors like genetics, brain chemistry, personality, and life events are thought to play a role.

### การรักษาหลัก (Treatments)
Standard treatment typically involves psychotherapy - especially cognitive behavioral therapy (CBT) - along with medications such as selective serotonin reuptake inhibitors (SSRIs), serotonin-norepinephrine reuptake inhibitors (SNRIs), or benzodiazepines for short-term relief. Lifestyle modifications like stress management and regular exercise are also recommended.

## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนจีน (Traditional Chinese Medicine View)
### ความเข้าใจเกี่ยวกับการแพทย์แผนจีน (TCM Understanding)
TCM understands anxiety through the lens of the Shen (the mind/spirit) and its relationship with the organ systems. The Shen resides in the Heart, and when it is calm and anchored, we feel at peace. But anything that disturbs the Heart - whether it's excess heat, phlegm, or a lack of nourishing blood and yin - can cause the Shen to become unsettled, leading to anxiety, restlessness, and insomnia.
The Liver plays a central role because it ensures the smooth flow of Qi (vital function) and emotions throughout the body. When stress, frustration, or suppressed feelings block that flow, Liver Qi stagnates, creating a sensation of pressure in the chest and a tendency to sigh. If the stagnation persists, it generates heat that rises to disturb the Heart, producing a more explosive, irritable type of anxiety with a bitter taste and red face.
Other patterns involve deeper deficiencies. The Spleen transforms food into Qi and Blood; excessive worry and overthinking weaken it, so it fails to produce enough Blood to nourish the Heart. This leaves the Shen unanchored, causing palpitations, poor sleep, and a pale, tired appearance. The Gallbladder governs courage and decisiveness; when its Qi is deficient, a person becomes timid, easily startled, and indecisive. Phlegm-Fire, a thick, hot substance that clouds the mind, can harass the Heart and create a restless, oppressive anxiety. And when Kidney Essence - the body's deep reserves - runs low, the brain loses its nourishment and the Heart-Kidney axis weakens, leading to a deep, fearful anxiety with dizziness and weak low back.
These patterns often overlap and evolve, which is why TCM treatment is always tailored to the individual, not the diagnosis.

### การวินิจฉัยโดยแพทย์แผนจีน (Practitioner Diagnosis)
A TCM practitioner begins by exploring the emotional texture of your anxiety, its physical companions, and what makes it better or worse. The quality of the feeling, the time of day it peaks, and the sensations in your chest, belly, or head all serve as clues that point toward one pattern rather than another.
If your anxiety feels like a tight knot in the chest that comes and goes with stress, and you catch yourself sighing often, Liver Qi Stagnation is the likely root. The tongue may look normal or have a thin white coating, and the pulse often feels wiry, like a guitar string, reflecting constrained energy.
When that bottled-up stress turns into heat, the picture shifts. You may feel more restless and irritable, with a bitter taste in your mouth, flushed face, or trouble staying asleep. The tongue becomes red with a yellow coating, and the pulse is not only wiry but also rapid, signaling that the stagnation has ignited internal fire.
If overthinking and worry have drained your energy, Heart and Spleen Deficiency emerges. Here the anxiety is accompanied by mental fog, poor memory, and a tired, heavy body that still can’t fall asleep easily. The tongue appears pale and puffy, and the pulse is thin and weak, telling the story of depleted Qi and blood.
For those who feel a sense of smothering, with a lump in the throat or sticky phlegm, Phlegm-Fire harassing the Heart is a key pattern. The agitation can feel intense and hard to control. The tongue is red with a thick, greasy yellow coat, and the pulse is slippery and rapid-like beads rolling on a plate-pointing to phlegm-heat agitating the mind.
If you are easily startled and jump at small noises, with a constant sense of dread, Gallbladder Deficiency is often at play. This pattern arises from a timid constitution and manifests as indecisiveness and a tendency to panic. The tongue may be pale with a thin coat, and the pulse is often fine and weak, reflecting the lack of steadying Gallbladder Qi.
When anxiety feels deep-seated and is paired with dizziness, low back soreness, or a hollow fear that seems to come from nowhere, Kidney Essence Deficiency should be considered. The tongue may be pale and thin with little coating, and the pulse is thin and weak, deep at the rear positions, indicating that the foundation of the body’s Essence has been weakened.

### Liver Qi Stagnation
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/liver-qi-stagnation)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Frequent sighing, Distension or bloating along the ribs and flanks, Emotional irritability or depression, Chest tightness, Feeling of a lump in the throat
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Stress and frustration, Alcohol and greasy food, Sedentary lifestyle, Suppressing emotions
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Gentle exercise, Deep breathing, Warmth on the ribs, Talking through feelings
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  The Liver is responsible for the smooth flow of Qi throughout the body and for keeping emotions balanced. When stress, frustration, or suppressed feelings accumulate, Liver Qi gets stuck instead of moving freely. This stagnation creates a sensation of pressure and distension in the chest and ribs, and the blocked Qi disrupts the Heart's ability to house the mind (Shen), leading directly to anxiety, irritability, and a constant need to sigh to release the tension.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: normal or slightly dark body, red or slightly purple sides, thin white coat. Pulse: wiry (弦 xián), taut like a guitar string, especially at the left middle position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Stress and suppressed emotions are the direct cause of Liver Qi Stagnation, so anything that encourages emotional expression or physical movement helps unblock the stuck Qi. Gentle exercise moves Qi through the channels, warmth relaxes the Liver organ and eases constriction, and deep breathing physically opens the chest to counteract the oppressive tightness. Conversely, alcohol and greasy foods create Dampness and Heat that further bog down Liver function, while a sedentary lifestyle allows Qi to congeal, making the anxiety feel more entrenched.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Prioritize regular, gentle movement like walking, yoga, or tai chi to keep Qi flowing. Avoid alcohol, coffee, and greasy foods which can clog the Liver's function. Sip peppermint tea and add fennel or citrus peel to meals. Most importantly, carve out time to talk through worries or journal-unexpressed emotions are the primary cause of this pattern.

### Liver Qi Stagnation that transforms into Heat
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/liver-qi-stagnation-that-transforms-into-heat)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Explosive anger and irritability, Bitter taste in the mouth, Temple headache, Red face and eyes
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Anger and frustration, Spicy or greasy food, Alcohol and coffee, Stressful situations, Overwork and lack of sleep
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Cooling foods and drinks, Gentle exercise, Deep breathing, A calm, quiet environment
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When Liver Qi stagnates for too long, the built-up pressure creates internal Heat. This Heat flares upward, disturbing the Heart and agitating the Shen (the mind/spirit). The result is a restless, explosive type of anxiety that feels like a pressure cooker ready to blow.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red body, especially red on the sides, with a yellow coating. Pulse: wiry and rapid (弦数, xian shu).
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Anger and stress cause Liver Qi to stagnate further, generating more Heat that disturbs the Shen. Spicy, greasy foods, alcohol, and coffee all introduce additional Heat into the body, intensifying the pattern. Cooling foods and drinks help clear that Heat, while relaxation and gentle movement smooth the flow of Liver Qi, addressing the root of the problem.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Avoid spicy, greasy, and fried foods, as well as alcohol and coffee, which add fuel to the internal Heat. Instead, choose cooling foods like cucumber, celery, chrysanthemum tea, and mint tea. Gentle daily movement such as walking, tai chi, or yoga helps keep Qi flowing smoothly and prevents stagnation.

### Heart and Spleen Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/heart-and-spleen-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Palpitations with a pale, sallow face, Poor appetite, bloating, and loose stools, Fatigue and mental exhaustion, Anxiety that worsens with overthinking, Difficulty falling asleep
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Overthinking and worry, Skipping meals or irregular eating, Cold, raw foods and drinks, Excessive mental work, Fatigue and exhaustion
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Rest and avoiding overthinking, Warm nourishing foods, Gentle exercise, Regular meal times, Calming meditation
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Excessive worry and mental overwork damage the Spleen, which is responsible for transforming food into Qi and Blood. When the Spleen becomes weak, it fails to produce enough Blood to nourish the Heart. The Heart Shen then loses its anchor, leading to palpitations, anxiety, and difficulty falling asleep.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale and tender with teeth marks, thin white coating. Pulse: fine and weak, possibly choppy.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Overthinking and worry directly damage the Spleen's ability to transform food into Qi and Blood, leaving the Heart Shen unanchored. Rest and nourishing foods rebuild Spleen Qi and Heart Blood, calming the mind. Raw and cold foods further weaken the Spleen, while gentle movement supports Qi flow without depleting energy.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat warm, cooked, easily digestible meals at regular times to support Spleen Qi. Include blood-nourishing foods like red dates, longan, and dark leafy greens. Avoid raw, cold, and greasy foods that burden digestion.
Gentle daily movement like walking or tai chi helps Qi flow without depletion. Prioritize rest and calming activities like meditation to reduce mental strain, which directly harms the Spleen and Heart.

### Phlegm-Fire harassing the Heart
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/phlegm-fire-harassing-the-heart)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Mental restlessness and agitation, Chest oppression with thick yellow phlegm, Insomnia or severely disturbed sleep, Bitter taste in the mouth, Red face and eyes
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Spicy or greasy food, Alcohol, Hot, humid weather, Emotional outbursts
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Cooling foods and drinks, Deep breathing, Clearing phlegm from the throat, Avoiding alcohol and greasy foods
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When Phlegm accumulates in the body and combines with Heat, it becomes thick, sticky, and turbulent. This Phlegm-Fire rises to the chest and head, where it harasses the Heart and disturbs the Shen (spirit). The result is a restless, agitated anxiety that feels oppressive and heavy, as if something is stuck in the chest, often with a racing mind and severe insomnia.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red body, swollen with a red prickly tip, and a thick yellow greasy coating. Pulse: slippery and rapid, full and overflowing at the left Cun (Heart) position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Greasy, spicy, and heavy foods directly generate Phlegm and Heat, which are the root of this pattern, so they make the agitation and chest oppression worse. Alcohol adds more Heat and turbidity. Emotional outbursts stir up internal Fire, further agitating the Heart Shen. Cooling, light meals and clearing phlegm reduce the pathogenic burden, while calming activities like deep breathing help settle the mind and allow the Shen to rest.

Gentle daily movement like walking or stretching helps Qi flow and prevents stagnation.

### Kidney Essence Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/kidney-essence-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Deep-seated fear and insecurity, Low back and knee soreness, Dizziness and tinnitus, Poor memory and concentration, Premature greying or hair loss, Frequent nighttime urination
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Overwork and chronic stress, Excessive sexual activity, Lack of sleep, Cold, raw foods and drinks
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Restful sleep, Warm nourishing foods, Gentle, grounding movement
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Your Kidneys store a deep, precious substance called Essence, which fuels the brain and anchors the mind. When Essence runs low-due to aging, overwork, or chronic stress-the brain's marrow loses its nourishment and the Heart-Kidney connection weakens. This leaves you feeling ungrounded, with a deep-seated fear and a sense of insecurity that is hard to shake, often accompanied by dizziness and a hollow ache in the lower back.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale, thin body with little or no coating, possible fine cracks. Pulse: fine (thin like a thread) and deep, weak at both rear (Chi) positions.

Overwork, chronic stress, and insufficient rest drain it further, leaving the brain undernourished and the mind ungrounded-this is why anxiety feels so profound and hard to escape. Rest, warm nourishing foods, and gentle movement help replenish that reserve, restoring the foundation that anchors the spirit and eases the fear.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Prioritize deep rest and avoid overwork, especially late nights. Eat warm, nourishing foods like black sesame, walnuts, goji berries, and bone broths. Gentle movement such as Tai Chi or walking helps preserve Essence without depleting it.

## คำถามที่พบบ่อย (Frequently Asked Questions)
### Can acupuncture really help with anxiety?
Yes, many people find acupuncture deeply calming. It works by regulating the nervous system, reducing stress hormones, and moving stuck Qi. You may feel more relaxed and less reactive after the first session. For persistent anxiety, a course of weekly treatments is usually recommended to build lasting change.

### Do I have to take herbs forever?
No. Herbs are typically taken for a few months to correct the underlying pattern, then you'll taper off or use them only as needed. The goal is to restore balance so your body can maintain calm on its own. Your practitioner will guide you on when and how to reduce the dose.

### Is TCM safe to combine with my anxiety medication?
Generally yes, but always tell both your TCM practitioner and your prescribing doctor about everything you're taking. Some herbs can interact with SSRIs or benzodiazepines, so your practitioner will adjust the formula accordingly. Never stop or change your medication without medical supervision.

### How long before I feel better?
Many people notice a reduction in the intensity of their anxiety within 2-4 weeks of starting herbs and acupuncture. Physical symptoms like muscle tension or poor sleep often improve first. Deeper emotional healing and pattern correction typically take 3-6 months, especially for long-standing anxiety.

### Will the anxiety come back after treatment?
TCM aims to address the root cause, so relapses are less likely once the pattern is resolved. However, severe stress or lifestyle factors can re-trigger it. Maintenance treatments or periodic check-ins can help you stay balanced. Many people find they become more resilient to stress after TCM treatment.

### Can diet really affect my anxiety?
Yes. Certain foods can aggravate internal heat or dampness, making anxiety worse. Avoiding spicy, greasy, or overly processed foods while eating warm, nourishing meals can support your treatment. Your practitioner may also recommend specific foods based on your pattern, like longan fruit for Heart Blood deficiency or cooling foods for heat patterns.
