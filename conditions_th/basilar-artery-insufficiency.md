## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนปัจจุบัน (Conventional Medicine View)
### คำอธิบาย (Description)
Basilar artery insufficiency refers to reduced blood flow in the vertebrobasilar system, which supplies the brainstem, cerebellum, and occipital lobes. This can lead to symptoms like vertigo, dizziness, visual disturbances, tinnitus, and in severe cases, stroke. Diagnosis typically involves imaging such as MRI, CT angiography, or ultrasound to assess blood flow and vessel structure.

### การรักษาหลัก (Treatments)
Conventional management often includes antiplatelet medications like aspirin to prevent clots, blood pressure control, and lifestyle modifications such as diet and exercise. In some cases, surgery or stenting may be recommended to open narrowed arteries.

## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนจีน (Traditional Chinese Medicine View)
### ความเข้าใจเกี่ยวกับการแพทย์แผนจีน (TCM Understanding)
TCM understands basilar artery insufficiency primarily as a disorder of brain nourishment. The brain relies on a steady supply of Qi and Blood, produced by the Spleen from food and stored by the Liver and Kidneys. When these organs are weakened by overwork, poor diet, or aging, the brain becomes undernourished, leading to dizziness and other symptoms. The quality of the dizziness - whether it's a floating emptiness, a heavy cloudiness, or a sudden spinning - reveals which organ system is most affected.
The Spleen transforms food into Qi and Blood. If digestion is weak or the body is exhausted, Qi and Blood become deficient, causing a floating, empty-headed dizziness that worsens with fatigue. This is the Qi and Blood Deficiency pattern. Conversely, when the Spleen fails to manage fluids, Dampness accumulates into Phlegm, which can obstruct the channels to the brain, resulting in a heavy, clouded sensation - the Phlegm in Channels pattern.
The Kidneys store essence that produces marrow for the brain, while the Liver ensures smooth Qi flow. When Kidney and Liver Yin are depleted from chronic stress or aging, the brain loses nourishment and dizziness with tinnitus and dry eyes occurs. Emotional stress can also cause Liver Yang to rise, bringing Heat to the head and causing sudden, severe vertigo with irritability - the Liver Yang Rising pattern. Blood Stagnation from sluggish circulation leads to fixed, stabbing dizziness, and in rare cases, Wind-Phlegm can cause sudden, severe spinning vertigo with phlegm production.

### การวินิจฉัยโดยแพทย์แผนจีน (Practitioner Diagnosis)
A practitioner begins by asking what the dizziness actually feels like and what makes it better or worse. With basilar artery insufficiency, the quality of the vertigo - whether it is a spinning sensation, a heavy cloudiness, or a feeling of floating - is the first clue that points toward one pattern rather than another.
If the dizziness is accompanied by marked fatigue, pale complexion, and a sensation of emptiness in the head, the picture suggests Qi and Blood Deficiency. The brain is undernourished, so symptoms worsen with exertion and improve with rest. The tongue is pale with a thin white coat, and the pulse is thin and weak, confirming the need to build up the body’s resources.
When the head feels heavy, as if wrapped in a wet cloth, and the vertigo is accompanied by chest tightness, nausea, and a greasy tongue coating, Phlegm in the Channels is likely the culprit. The pulse will feel slippery, reflecting the turbid dampness clouding the clear orifices. This pattern is common in people with a sluggish digestion and a tendency toward weight gain.
In older adults, a pattern of Kidney and Liver Yin Deficiency often underlies the problem. The dizziness is less heavy and more of a light-headed, floating sensation, paired with tinnitus, blurred vision, and soreness in the lower back and knees. The tongue is red with little coating, and the pulse is thin and rapid. If the dizziness suddenly intensifies with irritability, a red face, and a pounding headache, the picture has shifted to Liver Yang Rising, an acute flare that requires immediate attention.
When the dizziness feels fixed and stabbing, with dark spots on the tongue and a choppy pulse, Blood Stagnation is likely present, often as a complication of long-standing deficiency or Phlegm. Rarely, a sudden storm of Wind-Phlegm causes severe spinning vertigo with copious phlegm and neurological signs like numbness or slurred speech.

### Qi and Blood Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/qi-and-blood-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Dizziness that worsens with fatigue or standing up, Pale face, lips, and nail beds, Heart palpitations, Poor appetite and loose stools, Shortness of breath on exertion
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Overwork and prolonged standing, Skipping meals, Cold raw foods, Excessive worry or mental strain
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Rest and lying down, Nourishing soups and stews, Gentle walking, Eating small frequent meals
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  The Spleen produces Qi and Blood from the food you eat. When digestion is weak or the body is overworked, Qi and Blood become insufficient. Without enough Blood to anchor it, Qi fails to rise and nourish the brain, so the head feels empty and dizzy. This lack of nourishment also causes the pale complexion, fatigue, and heart flutters.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: Pale body, may be thin or have teeth marks, with a thin white coating. Pulse: Fine (thready) and weak, especially in the right Guan position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Overwork and mental strain directly consume Qi and Blood, while skipping meals and cold foods weaken the Spleen's ability to produce them. Rest and warm, easily digested foods rebuild these reserves. Gentle movement encourages Qi flow without depleting it, which helps the Spleen function and lifts clear Yang to the head.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat warm, cooked foods like congees, stews, and soups to support Spleen Qi. Include blood-nourishing foods such as red dates, goji berries, and dark leafy greens. Avoid raw, cold foods that tax digestion. Gentle daily movement like walking or tai chi helps Qi circulation without exhausting reserves.

### Phlegm in the Channels joints and muscles
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/phlegm-in-the-channels-joints-and-muscles)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Head heaviness, as if wrapped in a towel, Vertigo that worsens with dampness or greasy food, Chest tightness and nausea, Numbness or heavy sensation in the limbs, Greasy white tongue coating, Slippery (&#x6ED1; hu&#xE1;) or wiry (&#x5F26; xi&#xE1;n) pulse
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Damp or rainy weather, Greasy, fried, or dairy foods, Sedentary lifestyle, Overeating or late-night meals
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Dry, warm environment, Light, easily digested meals, Gentle daily exercise, Warm ginger tea
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When the Spleen fails to transform fluids, Dampness accumulates and congeals into Phlegm. This sticky Phlegm can lodge in the Channels that supply the head, physically obstructing the flow of Qi and Blood to the basilar artery territory. The result is a characteristic heavy, clouded dizziness - the brain is literally being "muddied" by turbid obstruction. Over time, the stagnation often traps Blood as well, creating a mixed Phlegm and Blood Stasis blockage that deepens the ischemia and makes the vertigo more stubborn.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: purple body with stasis spots, white greasy coating. Pulse: wiry (弦 xián) and slippery (滑 huá), reflecting Phlegm obstruction with underlying Blood Stasis.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Dampness and heavy foods directly feed Phlegm production, which then clogs the Channels and worsens the heavy vertigo. Movement and warmth stir Qi and fluids, preventing stagnation, while light meals and aromatic spices support the Spleen's ability to transform and transport - cutting off Phlegm at its source.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Avoid cold, raw, greasy, and dairy-heavy foods that generate Dampness and Phlegm. Favor warm, lightly cooked meals with aromatic spices like ginger and cardamom. Gentle daily movement - such as walking or tai chi - helps keep Qi and fluids moving so Phlegm cannot settle in the Channels.

### Kidney and Liver Yin Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/kidney-and-liver-yin-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Dizziness that comes on gradually and is worse with fatigue, Tinnitus and blurred vision, Night sweats and heat in the palms, soles, and chest, Soreness and weakness of the lower back and knees, Dry eyes and dry mouth, Insomnia with vivid dreams
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Overwork and lack of sleep, Spicy, greasy, or fried foods, Stress and frustration, Dry, hot weather
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Rest and lying down, Cool, quiet environment, Nourishing soups and stews, Gentle walking and stretching
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  The Kidneys store the essence that produces marrow and fills the brain, while the Liver opens into the eyes and ensures smooth flow of Qi and Blood upward. When Kidney and Liver Yin become depleted, the brain and sensory organs lose their nourishment. At the same time, the deficiency creates an empty heat that rises to the head, disturbing the clear orifices. This is what produces the chronic dizziness, tinnitus, and blurred vision typical of basilar artery insufficiency in this pattern.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red, thin body with cracks, little or no coating, dry surface. Pulse: fine, rapid, and wiry, especially weak at the left Chi (Kidney) position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: This pattern is rooted in a deep lack of cooling, moistening Yin fluids. Overwork and late nights consume Yin, while spicy and fried foods generate heat that further depletes it. Rest and nourishing foods help rebuild Yin, and a cool, calm environment prevents the empty heat from flaring upward to disturb the head and senses.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Prioritize rest and avoid overworking, especially late at night, as this further drains Yin. Include nourishing foods like black sesame, goji berries, walnuts, and dark leafy greens. Avoid spicy, fried, or drying foods and limit alcohol and coffee, which can stir up deficiency heat.

### Liver Yang Rising
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/liver-yang-rising)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Throbbing or distending temple headache, Sudden severe vertigo, Irritability and quick temper, Flushed face and red eyes, Worse with stress and anger
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Stress and anger, Alcohol, Spicy, greasy, or fried foods, Overwork and lack of sleep, Hot weather
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Rest in a dark, quiet room, Cooling foods and drinks, Gentle stretching or Tai Chi, Cold compress on forehead, Avoiding emotional outbursts
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  The Liver is responsible for keeping Qi flowing smoothly. When emotional stress, frustration, or anger build up, Liver Qi stagnates and transforms into Heat, which surges upward as Liver Yang. This uprising Yang Heat travels to the head, causing the blood vessels in the brain to spasm and constrict. In basilar artery insufficiency, this constriction further reduces blood flow through the already compromised artery, triggering sudden, severe vertigo and throbbing headache.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red body, red sides, thin dry yellow or scanty coat, possibly trembling. Pulse: wiry (弦 xián), left Guan position particularly forceful; may also be fine and rapid if Yin deficiency is prominent.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Stress and anger surge Liver Qi upward, fanning the flame of Liver Yang and causing blood vessels to constrict. Alcohol and spicy foods add Heat to the Liver, worsening the uprising. Rest, cooling foods, and calm environments help anchor the Yang back down, relaxing the vessels and restoring blood flow to the brain.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Avoid alcohol, coffee, spicy and greasy foods, which heat and agitate the Liver. Prioritize stress reduction through meditation or gentle exercise like Tai Chi. Ensure regular sleep and avoid overwork. Cooling foods like celery, cucumber, and chrysanthemum tea can help settle the uprising Yang.

### Blood Stagnation
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/blood-stagnation)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Fixed, stabbing head pain or dizziness, Pain or dizziness worsens at night, Dark purplish lips or nail beds, Dark circles under the eyes, Dark menstrual blood with clots (if female)
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Prolonged sitting or immobility, Stress and frustration, Cold and damp weather, Overwork or fatigue
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Gentle walking and stretching, Warm compress on the neck, Deep breathing, Rest in a dark, quiet room
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When blood flow becomes sluggish and congeals, it can no longer nourish the brain properly. This stasis obstructs the tiny vessels that feed the base of the brain, leading to dizziness that feels fixed and heavy, often with a stabbing quality. The pain or wooziness tends to be stubborn and unchanging, reflecting the stuck, unmoving nature of the stagnation.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: dark purple or dusky body with possible stasis spots; sublingual veins are engorged, tortuous, and dark. Pulse: choppy (Se) or wiry (Xian).
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Blood Stagnation is like a traffic jam in the vessels-movement and warmth help disperse it, while stillness and cold make it congeal. Gentle exercise and deep breathing pump Qi and blood, clearing the stasis. Stress constricts the Liver Qi, which in turn tightens the vessels and worsens stagnation, so a calm mind and relaxed neck are direct medicine for this pattern.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat warming, mildly spicy foods like turmeric, ginger, and black pepper to nudge blood flow. Avoid cold, raw foods and greasy meals that can thicken the blood. Gentle daily movement such as walking or tai chi is essential to keep blood circulating; long periods of sitting still will make symptoms worse.

### Wind-Phlegm
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/wind-phlegm)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Sudden, severe spinning vertigo (room spinning), Heavy, foggy sensation or distending headache, Copious white, frothy sputum, Nausea or vomiting of phlegm, Numbness, tingling, or trembling in the limbs, Slurred or difficult speech, Gurgling or rattling sound in the throat
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Heavy, greasy, or sweet foods, Cold and damp weather, Stress or emotional upset, Overwork or fatigue, Lying flat after eating, Exposure to wind
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Light, easily digested meals, Warm ginger tea, Gentle neck stretches, Keeping the head elevated, Avoiding dairy and greasy foods, Dry, warm environment
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When the Spleen is weak, it fails to transform fluids, leading to the accumulation of dampness and Phlegm. If Liver Wind is stirred-often by stress or aging-it carries this Phlegm upward to obstruct the clear orifices and the channels that supply the brain. The result is a sudden, severe spinning vertigo with a heavy, foggy head, as the basilar artery's flow is compromised by the Phlegm-Wind blockage.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: normal or slightly pale body, swollen with tooth marks, coated with a white, greasy fur. Pulse: wiry and slippery (弦滑 xián huá).
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Heavy, greasy, and sweet foods overburden the Spleen and generate dampness, which thickens into Phlegm-the root of this pattern. Cold and damp weather, fatigue, and stress stir up internal Wind or weaken the Spleen further, allowing Wind-Phlegm to rush upward. Light, warm meals and ginger tea strengthen the Spleen and dissolve phlegm, while a dry, warm environment and gentle movement keep Qi flowing smoothly, preventing Wind from rising.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat light, warm, and easily digestible meals. Avoid dairy, fried foods, and sugar, which create dampness and phlegm. Sip warm ginger tea daily to help transform phlegm. Keep the neck warm and avoid drafts. Gentle neck stretches and walking can help Qi flow.

## คำถามที่พบบ่อย (Frequently Asked Questions)
### Can acupuncture help with dizziness from basilar artery insufficiency?
Yes, acupuncture can help regulate blood flow to the brain. Points like Baihui DU-20 and Fengchi GB-20 are commonly used to calm the mind and improve circulation. Most patients feel a reduction in dizziness after a few sessions, though chronic cases may need longer treatment.

### How long does TCM treatment take to work?
Treatment duration varies by pattern. Acute symptoms may improve in 2-4 weeks, but chronic conditions often need 3-6 months of consistent care. Your practitioner will adjust the plan based on your progress and may combine acupuncture with daily herbal formulas.

### Is it safe to combine TCM herbs with my blood pressure medication?
Generally, yes, but always inform both your TCM practitioner and doctor. Some herbs like Dang Gui may interact with anticoagulants, so regular monitoring is important to adjust dosages safely. Never stop prescribed medications without consulting your physician.

### Will TCM treatment prevent strokes?
TCM aims to correct the imbalances that lead to poor circulation, which may reduce stroke risk. However, it should complement, not replace, conventional medical care. Always follow your doctor's advice on stroke prevention and report any new symptoms immediately.

### Can diet affect my condition?
Absolutely. Heavy, greasy foods can create Phlegm, worsening dizziness. A diet rich in nourishing foods like soups and stews supports Qi and Blood production. Your practitioner can provide specific guidance based on your pattern, such as avoiding cold foods for Spleen deficiency.
