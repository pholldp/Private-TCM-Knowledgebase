## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนปัจจุบัน (Conventional Medicine View)
### คำอธิบาย (Description)
Burns are tissue injuries caused by heat, chemicals, electricity, radiation, or friction. They are classified by depth - first-degree (superficial, like sunburn), second-degree (partial thickness with blisters), and third-degree (full thickness, destroying all skin layers) - and by the percentage of body surface area involved.
Symptoms range from pain, redness, and swelling to severe blistering, charring, and systemic shock. Diagnosis is clinical, based on appearance, history of injury, and sometimes imaging to assess deeper damage.

### การรักษาหลัก (Treatments)
Standard burn care begins with cooling the wound, gentle cleansing, and applying topical antibiotic ointments or advanced dressings to prevent infection and maintain a moist healing environment. Pain is managed with over-the-counter analgesics or prescription medications. Deeper burns may require intravenous fluids, tetanus prophylaxis, surgical debridement, and skin grafting. Severe burns are treated in specialized burn centers with multidisciplinary support.

## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนจีน (Traditional Chinese Medicine View)
### ความเข้าใจเกี่ยวกับการแพทย์แผนจีน (TCM Understanding)
TCM views a burn as a direct invasion of external Fire toxin (火毒, huǒ dú) that scorches the skin and penetrates deeper into the body. The initial injury is a fierce excess of Heat that flares up in the local tissues, causing the classic redness, swelling, heat, and intense pain. This Heat can quickly enter the Blood level, stirring up a systemic storm of high fever, thirst, and agitation - what TCM calls Toxic-Heat blazing in the Qi and Blood.
But the story doesn't end there. Fire toxin is a voracious consumer of fluids. As the burn progresses, the body's cooling, moistening Yin is rapidly depleted, especially if the person has a constitutional tendency toward dryness. This creates an Empty-Heat pattern where the fire is no longer balanced by Yin - the wound may still feel warm, but the person now has a dry mouth, night sweats, and a thin, rapid pulse.
The organs most affected are the Heart (which governs blood and houses the Shen), the Liver (which stores blood), and the Kidneys (the foundation of Yin).
If the burn is severe or prolonged, the Spleen's ability to produce Qi and hold blood within vessels can falter, leading to pale, oozing wounds and extreme fatigue. Eventually, both Qi and Yin may be depleted, leaving the person exhausted and slow to heal.
In the rarest and most severe cases, even the warming Yang of the Spleen can be exhausted, causing internal Cold and digestive collapse. This is why TCM doesn't treat a burn as a static wound - it treats it as a moving target, with different patterns dominating at different times.

### การวินิจฉัยโดยแพทย์แผนจีน (Practitioner Diagnosis)
A TCM practitioner first looks at the burn wound itself and asks about the timeline. Burns are understood as a progression of heat and fluid loss, so whether the injury happened hours ago, days ago, or weeks ago is the first big clue that points toward one pattern over another.
When the burn is very fresh and the area is bright red, swollen, intensely hot, and painful, the Toxic-Heat pattern dominates. The person may feel feverish and thirsty, and the tongue is red with a yellow coating while the pulse feels rapid and forceful. This is the body's acute defensive reaction to the fire toxin.
After a few days, if the initial heat has consumed fluids, an Empty-Heat from Yin Deficiency pattern can appear. The wound may still be warm but the person has a lingering low fever, a dry mouth, and a tongue that is red with very little coating. The pulse feels thin and rapid, signaling that cooling nourishment is needed rather than more strong heat-clearing.
When burns cause significant fluid loss and weaken the body's holding power, the Qi not controlling Blood pattern emerges. The person looks pale and feels exhausted, with oozing that seems slow to stop. The tongue is pale and the pulse is weak and thready, showing that the spleen Qi is too depleted to keep blood in the vessels.
During the longer recovery phase, many people slip into a Qi and Yin Deficiency pattern. They have poor appetite, feel bloated and listless, and the tongue stays red with a scant coat while the pulse remains thready and fast. This reflects a deep depletion of both energy and fluids that needs gentle rebuilding.
In very severe or prolonged cases, the body's warming fire can be exhausted, leading to Spleen Yang Deficiency. The person feels cold, especially in the limbs and abdomen, and the tongue appears puffy and pale with teeth marks. The pulse is deep and slow, a sign that the digestive fire has dimmed and needs to be rekindled.

## กลุ่มอาการการแพทย์แผนจีนและการรักษา (TCM Patterns and Treatment)

### Toxic-Heat
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/toxic-heat)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: High fever or burning sensation all over, Red, swollen, hot, and extremely painful burn site, Intense thirst with a strong craving for cold drinks, Dark, scanty urine or constipation, Restlessness, irritability, or agitation, Dry mouth and throat, flushed face
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Exposure to heat or sun, Spicy and fried foods, Alcohol or coffee, Emotional stress or anger
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Cool, clean compress on the burn, Cold or room-temperature drinks, Quiet, cool room, Mung bean soup or chrysanthemum tea
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Burns are a direct invasion of Fire toxin into the skin. This external Heat blazes fiercely, scorching the local tissues and creating the classic signs of redness, swelling, heat, and intense pain. As the toxin penetrates deeper, it enters the Qi and Blood levels, stirring up a systemic storm of high fever, raging thirst, and restlessness - the body's own Qi fighting a losing battle against a formidable pathogen.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red body, often with prickly red thorns on the tip and centre, and a dry yellow coating. Pulse: rapid (数 shuò) and forceful, full (实 shí) or overflowing (洪 hóng), especially at the right Guan position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Toxic-Heat is an aggressive, blazing pathogen. Anything that adds more Heat - spicy food, alcohol, hot environments, or emotional frustration - fans the flames and intensifies symptoms. Cooling measures, both internally (cold drinks, cooling foods) and externally (cool compresses, rest in a cool space), directly counteract the Heat, giving the body's Qi a chance to regain control and prevent the toxin from sinking deeper.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Keep the burn clean and cool, and avoid any greasy, spicy, or fried foods that add internal Heat. Drink plenty of room-temperature water or mild cooling teas like chrysanthemum or mung bean soup. Rest in a calm, cool environment and avoid alcohol completely until the acute Heat subsides.

### Empty-Heat caused by Yin Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/empty-heat-caused-by-yin-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Lingering low-grade fever or heat sensation, Heat in the palms, soles, and chest (five-palm heat), Night sweats, especially during sleep, Dry mouth and throat, worse in the evening, Flushed cheekbones (malar flush), Irritability and restlessness, Red tongue with little or no coating
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Spicy and fried foods, Overwork and overexertion, Emotional stress or anger
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Cooling, hydrating foods, Rest and sleep, Cool, humid environment
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Burns are a direct assault of fire and heat, which rapidly consumes the body's cooling and moistening fluids (Yin). When these Yin fluids are severely depleted, the fire is no longer balanced, leaving behind an 'empty heat' that lingers even after the initial burn is treated. This internal heat rises, causing a persistent low-grade fever, intense dryness, and a characteristic sensation of heat in the palms, soles, and chest.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red, thin, and dry body with cracks; little to no coating. Pulse: fine (thready) and rapid.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Burns inherently deplete the body's Yin fluids. Spicy foods, late nights, and stress further consume Yin and generate empty heat, worsening the dry, hot symptoms. Rest and cooling, moistening foods help rebuild the Yin fluids, which naturally balances the excess heat, much like adding water to a boiling pot to calm it down.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Prioritize rest, especially at night, as sleep is when the body recovers Yin. Eat cooling, moistening foods like pears, watermelon, cucumber, and mung bean soup. Avoid spicy, fried, and overly hot foods that consume fluids and stir up empty heat.

### Qi not controlling Blood
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/qi-not-controlling-blood)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Burn wounds that ooze and bleed easily, Pale, sallow complexion and lips, Extreme fatigue and shortness of breath, Poor appetite and loose stools, Easy bruising
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Overwork and overexertion, Cold or raw foods and iced drinks, Excessive bleeding, Worry and pensiveness
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Rest and sleep, Warm, easily digestible foods, Gentle movement or walking
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Severe burns cause a massive loss of fluids and Qi. When the Spleen Qi becomes too weak, it can no longer hold blood within the vessels, leading to persistent oozing from burn wounds and easy bruising. This pattern is marked by extreme fatigue, a pale complexion, and a weak pulse.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale, puffy body with teeth marks, thin white coating. Pulse: fine and weak, especially in the right middle position (Spleen).
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Overexertion and raw, cold foods deplete Spleen Qi, making it even harder to hold blood in the vessels. Rest and warm, easily digested foods strengthen the Spleen's ability to control blood, while gentle movement supports Qi flow without exhausting it.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat warm, easily digestible foods like congee, soups, and stewed meats to support Spleen Qi. Avoid raw, cold, and greasy foods that further weaken digestion. Rest is crucial; avoid heavy exertion until strength returns.

### Qi and Yin Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/qi-and-yin-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Persistent fatigue and weakness, Poor appetite and abdominal bloating, Dry mouth and throat with little desire to drink, Warm sensation in palms and soles
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Overwork and overexertion, Spicy, fried, or drying foods, Stress and emotional strain, Dry, hot environments
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Rest and sleep, Warm, easily digestible foods, Moderate warmth, avoiding chills, Gentle movement or walking
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  After severe burns, the intense heat consumes the body's fluids (Yin) and the prolonged recovery exhausts the vital energy (Qi). This double depletion leaves you with persistent fatigue, a dry mouth, and poor appetite, which slow down wound healing and overall recovery.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red or pale-red body with a thin or absent coating, dry and possibly cracked. Pulse: fine, weak, and often rapid.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Qi and Yin are already depleted after a burn, so any further drain from overexertion, sweating, or spicy foods worsens fatigue and dryness. Rest, gentle warmth, and nourishing foods help rebuild the body's energy and fluids, supporting healing.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat warm, easily digestible foods like congee with Chinese yam and goji berries. Small, frequent meals help rebuild energy without straining digestion. Gentle rest and short walks are beneficial; avoid overexertion and spicy, drying foods.

### Spleen Yang Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/spleen-yang-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Dull abdominal pain that feels better with warmth and pressure, Cold hands and feet, feeling chilly easily, Loose watery stools, sometimes with undigested food, Poor appetite and abdominal bloating after eating, Puffiness or swelling of the limbs
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Cold or raw foods and iced drinks, Cold weather or drafts, Overwork and overexertion, Excessive fluid loss or prolonged illness
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: A warm compress or hot water bottle on the abdomen, Warm, easily digestible foods, Rest and sleep, Warm environment
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Severe or prolonged burns consume the body's Qi and fluids, and over time this can exhaust the warming Yang energy of the Spleen. When Spleen Yang is deficient, the digestive fire weakens and internal Cold develops, so the body cannot generate enough warmth, fluids are not transformed properly, and wounds heal very slowly.
This pattern is a late-stage complication: the initial heat toxin has faded, but the damage to Yang leaves the person feeling cold, bloated, and depleted, with loose stools and a pale, puffy tongue.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: Pale, puffy, and tender, with scalloped edges and a white slippery coating. Pulse: Deep, slow, and weak, especially at the right Guan position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Spleen Yang is the body's digestive pilot light. Cold foods and environments extinguish that fire, while warmth and gentle nourishment stoke it back to life. Rest conserves the small amount of Qi and Yang remaining, allowing the body to direct energy toward healing rather than activity.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat only warm, cooked, easily digestible foods like congee, soups, and stews. Avoid all raw, cold, or iced items. Keep the abdomen and feet warm, and rest as much as possible to conserve Qi and Yang.

## คำถามที่พบบ่อย (Frequently Asked Questions)
### Can TCM help with the pain of a fresh burn?
Yes. In the acute stage, TCM focuses on clearing Heat and cooling the Blood. Herbal formulas like Huang Lian Jie Du Tang can rapidly bring down the fiery sensation, and distal acupuncture points like Hegu (LI-4) and Quchi (LI-11) are used to clear heat and relieve pain without touching the injured skin. Many patients feel a noticeable reduction in throbbing and burning within a day of starting herbs.

### Is acupuncture safe on burned skin?
Acupuncture needles are never inserted directly into burned tissue. Instead, practitioners use points on the limbs, back, and abdomen that influence the affected channels and organ systems. This allows them to cool heat, calm pain, and support healing without disturbing the wound. Topical herbal applications are also used with sterile technique and only on appropriate wounds.

### Can herbs prevent scarring?
While no treatment can guarantee zero scarring, TCM can help minimize it. By clearing lingering Heat and promoting the generation of new skin and flesh (through nourishing Qi and Blood), herbs and topical pastes can reduce the redness and thickness of scars. Formulas that move Blood and resolve stasis, used under professional guidance, may also soften existing scars. Early, consistent treatment is key.

### When should I see a TCM practitioner after a burn?
Ideally, as soon as possible after the burn has been stabilized medically. In the first 48 hours, a practitioner can use cooling herbs to limit the invasion of Fire toxin and reduce systemic symptoms like fever. Later, they can address lingering heat, fluid loss, and fatigue. Even weeks after the injury, TCM can help with slow-healing wounds or persistent weakness. Always seek emergency care first for any serious burn.

### Can I use TCM alongside antibiotics or pain medications?
Yes, TCM is generally safe to combine with conventional burn care, but full disclosure is essential. Tell both your TCM practitioner and your doctor about all medications and herbs you are taking. Some herbs that move blood (like Dang Gui) may interact with anticoagulants, and sedating herbs should be used carefully with pain medications that cause drowsiness. Your practitioners can coordinate to avoid interactions.

### Does TCM help with the fatigue and weakness after a burn?
Absolutely. This is one of TCM's strengths. Patterns like Qi and Yin Deficiency or Spleen Yang Deficiency are directly addressed with formulas like Sheng Mai San or Fu Zi Li Zhong Tang, which rebuild energy, warm the digestion, and restore fluids. Acupuncture and dietary therapy further support recovery, helping you regain strength much faster than rest alone.
