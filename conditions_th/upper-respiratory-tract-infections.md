## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนปัจจุบัน (Conventional Medicine View)
### คำอธิบาย (Description)
The common cold is a viral infection of the upper respiratory tract, primarily caused by rhinoviruses. Typical symptoms include runny or stuffy nose, sneezing, sore throat, cough, mild fever, and general malaise. It is self-limiting, usually resolving within 7-10 days without specific antiviral treatment.

### การรักษาหลัก (Treatments)
Conventional care focuses on symptom relief: rest, fluids, and over-the-counter medications like decongestants, antihistamines, and pain relievers. Antibiotics are ineffective against viruses. There is no cure, and prevention relies mainly on handwashing and avoiding contact with infected individuals.

## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนจีน (Traditional Chinese Medicine View)
### ความเข้าใจเกี่ยวกับการแพทย์แผนจีน (TCM Understanding)
TCM views a cold as an invasion of external pathogens (Wind, often combined with Cold, Heat, Dampness, or Dryness) that attack the body's outermost defensive layer, called Wei Qi. This protective shield is governed by the Lungs, which spread Wei Qi over the skin and open and close the pores. When the pathogen breaks through, the Lungs lose their ability to properly disperse and descend Qi, leading to the classic symptoms of nasal congestion, cough, and body aches.
The specific pathogen determines the symptom picture. Wind-Cold constricts the pores, causing chills, no sweating, and clear white mucus. Wind-Heat brings more fever, a sore throat, and yellow mucus. Summer Heat with Dampness creates a heavy, sticky sensation with a greasy tongue coating. Dry-Wind, typical of autumn, parches the nose and throat and produces a dry, non-productive cough. These are not different stages of the same illness - they are fundamentally different conditions that require different treatments.
Underlying constitutional weakness also plays a major role. A person with Yang Deficiency may develop a deep, hard-to-shake cold with severe chills, while someone with Blood and Yin Deficiency might experience a lingering, low-grade illness with a dry throat and pale complexion. This explains why some people get sick more often or take longer to recover - their internal terrain is simply more hospitable to pathogens.

### การวินิจฉัยโดยแพทย์แผนจีน (Practitioner Diagnosis)
A TCM practitioner starts by asking what the cold or flu actually feels like. The balance between feeling chilled and feeling feverish, the quality of mucus, and the presence of thirst are the first clues that point toward one pattern rather than another. The tongue and pulse are then examined to confirm the nature of the external invasion.
If chills are more prominent than fever, with no sweating, a tight headache, body aches, and clear watery nasal discharge, the picture is Wind-Cold invading the Lungs. The tongue coating is thin and white, and the pulse feels floating and tight - like a tightly stretched string on the surface.
When fever is the main complaint, with only mild aversion to wind, a sore throat, thirst, and yellow or thick nasal discharge, the pattern shifts to Wind-Heat invading the Lungs. Here the tongue tip is redder, the coating is thin and yellow, and the pulse feels floating and rapid, reflecting heat in the lung and wei-defensive exterior.
If the illness strikes in hot, humid summer weather and the fever does not break with sweating, with a heavy head, chest oppression, a sticky mouth, and a greasy yellow tongue coating, Summer Heat with Dampness is the pattern. The pulse is soft and rapid, showing that dampness and heat are tangled together and weighing the body down.
Dry-Wind typically appears in autumn, when dryness invades the lungs. The person complains of a dry nose and lips, a scratchy dry throat, and a dry cough. The tongue tip is red with a thin dry coating, and the pulse is floating. This pattern stands out because of the absence of mucus and the clear link to dry weather.
If someone with a history of always feeling cold and having low energy catches a cold with severe chills, cold limbs, and a deep slow pulse, the underlying Yang Deficiency shapes the pattern. The external Wind-Cold is layered on a body that lacks warming defensive Qi, so the chill is much deeper than in a simple wind-cold attack.
When a person with chronic dryness, night sweats, or pale lips gets a cold with low-grade fever, very little sweating, a dry throat, and a thready, forceless pulse, the pattern is Wind-Cold invading with Blood and Yin Deficiency. The tongue is pale, thin, and slightly dry with a thin white coating, and the pulse feels thready and weak, telling the practitioner that the body’s nourishing fluids are too depleted to mount a strong fever response.

### Wind-Cold invading the Lungs
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/wind-cold-invading-the-lungs)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Chills more prominent than fever, No sweating, Clear, watery nasal discharge, Cough with thin white phlegm, Body aches and sore limbs, Itchy throat
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Exposure to cold wind or drafts, Cold drinks and raw foods, Overexertion or lack of sleep, Damp or rainy weather
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warm ginger tea, Resting under a blanket, Light sweating, Warm room, Scallion and fermented soybean soup
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When Wind-Cold invades the body, it attacks the outermost defensive layer and the Lungs. The Cold constricts the pores and blocks the smooth flow of Lung Qi, trapping the pathogen inside. This produces the hallmark chills, stuffy nose, and a cough with thin white phlegm as the Lungs struggle to disperse and descend.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: normal or slightly pale body with a thin, white, moist coating. Pulse: floating (fú) and tight (jǐn), like a taut rope felt at the surface.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Wind-Cold invades by closing the pores and obstructing Lung Qi, so everything that warms and relaxes the surface helps push the pathogen out. Warm drinks and light sweating open the pores to release the Cold, while rest conserves the body's defensive Qi. Cold, damp, and drafts tighten the exterior further, locking the pathogen inside and prolonging the illness.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Sip warm ginger tea with a little brown sugar throughout the day to warm the interior and gently open the pores. Rest in a warm, draft-free room, and avoid cold drinks, raw salads, and icy foods. A warm foot soak or bath can help ease the chills and body aches.

### Wind-Heat invading the Lungs
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/wind-heat-invading-the-lungs)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Fever stronger than chills, Sore or painful throat, Yellow or turbid nasal discharge, Thirst or dry mouth, Cough with thick yellow phlegm, Headache with feeling of heat, Slight sweating
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Spicy or fried foods, Alcohol and coffee, Hot, stuffy environments, Overexertion or lack of sleep, Stress and frustration
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Cool fluids like chrysanthemum tea, Rest in a cool, airy room, Light, non-greasy foods, Gargling with warm salt water, Cool compresses on forehead
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Wind-Heat is an external pathogen that enters through the nose and mouth, attacking the Lung’s defensive exterior. The Heat overwhelms the body’s cooling mechanisms, causing a fever that is more pronounced than any chills. As Heat dries fluids, the throat becomes sore and thirst sets in, while the Lungs respond with a cough that produces thick, yellow phlegm.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red tip, thin yellow coating, body slightly red. Pulse: floating (Fu) and rapid (Shu), right Cun position prominent.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Wind-Heat is a hot, active pathogen. Spicy and fried foods, alcohol, and hot environments add more heat, fueling the fever and sore throat. Overexertion and emotional stress consume the body's Yin and defensive Qi, making it harder to push the pathogen out. Rest and cooling fluids help clear heat and support the Lung’s natural ability to descend and diffuse Qi, while light foods avoid burdening a digestive system already taxed by fighting an external invader.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Drink plenty of warm or room-temperature fluids such as chrysanthemum or mint tea to gently cool the body. Avoid spicy, greasy, and fried foods that add internal heat. Rest in a cool, well-ventilated room and keep physical activity light to allow your defensive Qi to focus on expelling the pathogen.

### Summer Heat with Dampness
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/summer-heat-with-dampness)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Fever that does not resolve with sweating, Headache with a heavy, wrapped sensation, Chest oppression and upper abdominal distension, Thirst with little desire to drink, Nausea or poor appetite, Heavy, tired body and limbs
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Hot, humid weather, Heavy, greasy, or dairy-rich foods, Cold drinks and raw foods, Overexertion or lack of sleep
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Light, warm, cooked meals, Light sweating, Rest in a cool, dry room
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  In hot, humid summer weather, the body's protective Qi can be overwhelmed by a combination of Summer Heat and environmental Dampness. The Heat raises your temperature and makes you restless, while the Dampness clings to the body and blocks the normal flow of Qi, especially in the chest and stomach. This double invasion causes a fever that won't break with sweating, a heavy, foggy-headed feeling, and a sticky, uncomfortable fullness in the chest and belly.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red body with a white greasy coating (may turn yellow greasy if Heat dominates). Pulse: soggy (Ru) and rapid (Shu).
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Summer Heat and Dampness are both heavy, sticky pathogens that clog the body's energy. Hot, humid weather directly feeds the illness, while greasy, cold, or damp-producing foods add to the internal dampness that the body is already struggling to clear. Light, warm meals and gentle sweating help the body push out the dampness and heat without overburdening the Spleen, which is the organ most responsible for managing fluids. Rest is essential because the body's Qi is already tied up fighting the external invasion.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat light, easily digested foods like congee, steamed vegetables, and small amounts of lean protein. Avoid cold drinks, raw salads, dairy, greasy foods, and sweets, which create more dampness. Sip warm water or mild barley tea throughout the day. Rest in a cool, well-ventilated space, but avoid direct drafts from fans or air conditioning.

### Dry-Wind
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/dry-wind)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Dry cough with little or no mucus, Dry nose, mouth, and throat, Dry, cracked lips, Low-grade fever with mild chills
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Dry, heated indoor air, Spicy or fried foods, Windy, dusty weather, Overexertion or lack of sleep
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warm fluids, Humidifier or steamy bathroom, Rest in a cool, moist environment, Sucking on lozenges
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Autumn dryness invades the body through the nose and mouth, directly attacking the Lungs, which are most sensitive to dry air. This external Dry-Wind consumes the body's fluids, leaving the respiratory passages parched and unable to produce enough moisture to protect themselves. As a result, the Lungs lose their ability to spread and descend Qi, leading to a dry, unproductive cough and a scratchy, irritated throat.

- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Exposure to cold wind or drafts, Cold drinks and raw foods, Overexertion or lack of sleep, Damp or rainy weather

- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  People with underlying Yang Deficiency have a weak internal fire that normally keeps the body warm and defends against illness. When Wind-Cold invades, the body lacks the Yang Qi to push the pathogen to the surface, so the cold lodges deeper and chills become severe-even blankets don't help. The lung's dispersing function is blocked by cold, causing nasal congestion and clear discharge, while the deep fatigue and drowsiness reflect the body's exhausted reserves.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale, puffy body with teeth marks and a thin white moist coating. Pulse: deep (felt only with firm pressure) and weak.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Yang Deficiency means the body's internal fire is already low, so any external cold or dampness penetrates more easily and chills become intense. Warmth, rest, and warming foods like ginger help conserve and restore that Yang, while cold and overexertion drain it further. Moxibustion directly adds Yang energy to the core, helping the body push the pathogen out.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Drink hot ginger and scallion tea to warm the interior and release the surface. Eat only warm, cooked foods like congee and soups; avoid all raw, cold, or iced items. Keep the lower back and feet warm, and rest as much as possible. Gentle moxibustion on the lower abdomen or back can speed recovery.

## คำถามที่พบบ่อย (Frequently Asked Questions)
### How do I know if my cold is Wind-Cold or Wind-Heat?
The quickest clues are chills, mucus, and thirst. If you feel more cold than hot, have clear watery mucus, and aren't thirsty, it's likely Wind-Cold. If fever is the main complaint, your mucus is yellow or thick, and you're thirsty with a sore throat, it's Wind-Heat. A TCM practitioner will also check your tongue (thin white coating for cold, thin yellow for heat) and pulse (floating tight vs. floating rapid) to confirm.

### Can Chinese herbs make a cold go away faster than over-the-counter medicine?
When the correct formula is matched to your specific pattern, many people find that herbs shorten the course of a cold and reduce the severity of symptoms more effectively than generic cold medicines. The key is precision: a formula that warms and releases the exterior for Wind-Cold won't work for Wind-Heat, and vice versa. Starting herbs at the very first sign of a cold gives the best results.

### Is it safe to take Chinese herbs with cold medicine?
In most cases, yes, but you should space them at least two hours apart to avoid overlapping effects. Be especially cautious with any formula containing Ma Huang (Ephedra), as it can interact with decongestants and raise blood pressure. Always tell both your TCM practitioner and your doctor about everything you're taking.

### What should I eat when I have a cold according to TCM?
The general rule is to eat warm, light, easily digestible foods and avoid anything cold, raw, greasy, or dairy-heavy, which can create Dampness and make congestion worse. For Wind-Cold, ginger-scallion tea and congee are ideal. For Wind-Heat, chrysanthemum tea and watery fruits like pear can help. Specific dietary advice for your pattern is in the treatment tab.

### Can acupuncture help with a cold?
Yes, acupuncture can relieve symptoms like sinus congestion, headache, and body aches almost immediately, and it helps regulate the immune response to expel the pathogen. Points like Fengchi (GB-20), Hegu (LI-4), and Lieque (LU-7) are commonly used. A single session at the onset of a cold can sometimes stop it in its tracks.

### Why does my cold always turn into a lingering cough?
From a TCM perspective, this often happens when the external pathogen isn't fully cleared and lodges deeper in the Lungs, or when the Lungs are already weak. Treating the cold with the wrong approach (like using cooling herbs for a Wind-Cold pattern) can also drive the pathogen inward, leading to a persistent cough. A properly matched formula aims to expel the pathogen completely, not just suppress symptoms.

## การวิจัยทางวิทยาศาสตร์สมัยใหม่ (Modern Scientific Research)
1. **Chinese medicinal herbs for the common cold** ([ลิงก์](https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD004782.pub2))
   *วารสารวิชาการ: Zhang X, Wu T, Zhang J, Yan Q, Xie L, Liu GJ. Chinese medicinal herbs for the common cold. Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews 2010, Issue 1. Art. No.: CD004782.*
   A Cochrane systematic review evaluating the efficacy of Chinese herbal medicines in treating the common cold. The review included 17 RCTs and found that some herbal preparations, particularly those with Andrographis paniculata, may be effective in reducing symptom severity and duration, though the overall quality of evidence was low.

2. **Acupuncture for symptom management in patients with common cold: a systematic review** ([ลิงก์](https://doi.org/10.1136/acupmed-2014-010568))
   *วารสารวิชาการ: Kim KH, Lee MS, Choi TY, Kim JI, Shin MS, Ernst E. Acupuncture for symptom management in patients with common cold: a systematic review. Acupuncture in Medicine. 2014;32(5):387-394.*
   A systematic review of 8 RCTs assessing acupuncture for the common cold. The review found that acupuncture was associated with reduced symptom duration and severity compared to usual care, but results were inconsistent when compared to sham acupuncture. The authors concluded that acupuncture shows potential but requires more rigorous studies.
