## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนปัจจุบัน (Conventional Medicine View)
### คำอธิบาย (Description)
In Western medicine, blurry vision is a symptom rather than a diagnosis. It can arise from refractive errors (nearsightedness, farsightedness, astigmatism), cataracts, glaucoma, macular degeneration, diabetic retinopathy, dry eye syndrome, or even migraines.
Diagnosis typically involves a comprehensive eye exam, including visual acuity tests, slit-lamp examination, and sometimes imaging like optical coherence tomography (OCT). Treatment depends on the underlying cause, ranging from corrective lenses to medications, laser procedures, or surgery.

### การรักษาหลัก (Treatments)
Standard treatments include prescription eyeglasses or contact lenses for refractive errors, artificial tears for dry eyes, and medications such as anti-VEGF injections for wet macular degeneration or prostaglandin analogs for glaucoma. Surgical options like cataract removal or LASIK are common. For systemic causes like diabetes or hypertension, managing the underlying condition is key.

## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนจีน (Traditional Chinese Medicine View)
### ความเข้าใจเกี่ยวกับการแพทย์แผนจีน (TCM Understanding)
The eyes are the "orifice of the Liver" and depend on Liver Blood for moisture and clarity. The Kidneys store Essence that supports all the body's fluids, including tears. The Spleen produces Qi and Blood that nourish the eyes. So blurry vision often reflects a deeper deficiency or disturbance in these organ systems.
When the body lacks enough Blood, Yin, or Qi, the eyes become dry, dim, and easily fatigued. This is the most common TCM presentation, seen in patterns like Liver Blood Deficiency, Kidney and Liver Yin Deficiency, and Qi and Blood Deficiency. Vision gets worse with overuse or at night.
Sometimes blurry vision is caused by something obstructing the clear flow to the eyes. Liver Yang Rising sends heat and pressure upward, causing sudden blurring with headaches and irritability. Damp-Phlegm clouds the vision like a mist. Blood Stagnation blocks the tiny vessels, leading to fixed dark spots or visual field loss.
Why one Western diagnosis has many TCM causes: For example, dry age-related macular degeneration might manifest as Kidney and Liver Yin Deficiency in one person, but as Qi and Blood Deficiency in another, depending on their constitution and lifestyle. TCM's pattern differentiation allows treatment to be tailored to the individual, not just the disease label.

### การวินิจฉัยโดยแพทย์แผนจีน (Practitioner Diagnosis)
A TCM practitioner begins by exploring the quality of the blurriness - when it started, what makes it worse, and what other sensations accompany it. The eyes are closely linked to the Liver, Kidneys, and the overall supply of Qi and Blood, so the whole-body picture is essential. The tongue and pulse provide a reliable map of the inner imbalance behind the visual change.
If the vision gradually dims and the eyes feel dry, with pale nails, dizziness, and a thready pulse, the focus is on Liver Blood Deficiency. When the same blurring appears alongside general exhaustion, a washed-out complexion, and worsens after a long day, Qi and Blood Deficiency is more likely. The tongue in both cases tends to be pale, but in pure blood deficiency it may look thinner and drier.
A different story emerges when the blurriness comes with dry, burning eyes, floaters, a sore back, and night sweats. That points to Kidney and Liver Yin Deficiency, often with a red tongue with little coating. If instead the vision suddenly blurs during moments of anger or stress, with eye distention, a bitter taste, and a wiry pulse, the pattern is Liver Yang Rising - the upward rush of heat clouds the senses.
Two less common patterns also appear. Blood Stagnation shows a fixed dark spot, a sudden visual field loss, or a sharp, stabbing eye pain, with a purplish tongue and a choppy pulse. Damp-Phlegm creates a sensation of fog or a veil over the eyes, often with chest tightness, a greasy tongue coating, and a slippery pulse. These signs help the practitioner distinguish obstruction from deficiency.

### Liver Blood Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/liver-blood-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Blurred vision that worsens with eye strain, Dry eyes, Poor night vision, Dizziness or lightheadedness, Pale complexion, Brittle, dry, or ridged nails, Scanty, pale menstruation (in women)
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Chronic stress and overwork, Prolonged screen time, Heavy menstrual bleeding or blood loss, Poor diet or skipping meals, Overwork and lack of sleep
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Closing the eyes and resting, Warm, cooked meals, Adequate sleep and rest, Blood-nourishing foods (liver, spinach, beets)
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  In TCM, the Liver opens into the eyes and Liver Blood is the substance that moistens and nourishes them. When Liver Blood is deficient, the eyes become undernourished and dry, leading to a gradual blurring of vision that often worsens with prolonged use or at night. This is the classic pattern for chronic, slowly progressive visual decline.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale body, especially the sides, thin and slightly dry with a thin white coating. Pulse: fine (xi) and choppy (se), often with a wiry quality at the left middle position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Liver Blood is produced from the essence of food and stored during sleep. Overwork, stress, and insufficient sleep deplete Blood, while excessive screen time directly strains the eyes and consumes the Blood that nourishes them. Heavy menstrual bleeding further drains Blood reserves. Rest and sleep allow the Liver to regenerate Blood, and warm, nutrient-dense foods provide the raw materials to build it. By avoiding drains and supporting production, the eyes receive the nourishment they need to see clearly.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat blood-nourishing foods like dark leafy greens, beets, black sesame, goji berries, and small amounts of organic liver. Avoid excessive screen time and get adequate sleep to allow the Liver to store and regenerate Blood.

### Kidney and Liver Yin Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/kidney-and-liver-yin-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Dry, gritty eyes with blurred vision, Floaters and sensitivity to light (photophobia), Lower back and knee soreness, Night sweats and heat in palms, soles, and chest, Dizziness and tinnitus, Red tongue with little coating
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Overwork and lack of sleep, Spicy, greasy, or fried foods, Prolonged screen time, Anger and frustration, Hot, stuffy environments
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Adequate sleep and rest, Cool, dark environment, Goji berries and black sesame, Gentle eye exercises, Moisturizing eye drops
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  The eyes rely on Liver blood and Kidney essence to stay moist and clear. When Liver and Kidney Yin run low, the eyes lose their lubrication, becoming dry, blurry, and sensitive to light. The lack of cooling Yin also lets deficiency heat rise, which further disturbs vision and causes floaters, night sweats, and a dry mouth.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red, thin body with cracks, little or no coating, dry surface. Pulse: fine and rapid, often wiry, weak at the left Chi (Kidney) position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Kidney and Liver Yin deficiency means the body's cooling, moistening resources are depleted. Late nights, overwork, and spicy foods consume Yin further and generate heat, worsening dryness and blur. Rest, cooling foods, and hydration replenish Yin, while a calm, dark environment soothes the eyes and prevents further strain.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Prioritize rest and avoid late nights, as sleep is the time the body replenishes Yin. Reduce screen time and take frequent breaks to rest the eyes. Gentle exercise like walking or tai chi can help, but avoid overexertion that depletes fluids.
Eat foods that nourish Yin and the eyes: goji berries, mulberries, black sesame, walnuts, spinach, and eggs. Avoid spicy, fried, and greasy foods that generate heat. Stay hydrated with warm water or chrysanthemum tea.

### Qi and Blood Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/qi-and-blood-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Blurry vision that worsens with fatigue, Pale complexion and lips, Dizziness and heart palpitations, Fatigue and shortness of breath
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Overwork and lack of sleep, Poor diet or skipping meals, Prolonged screen time, Stress and prolonged worry, Heavy menstrual bleeding or blood loss
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Adequate sleep and rest, Warm, cooked meals, Gentle walks in fresh air, Reducing screen time
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Qi and Blood are the fundamental substances that nourish every tissue, including the eyes. When they are deficient, the eyes do not receive enough nourishment, and the clear vision that depends on this supply gradually fades. This is why the blurring tends to come on slowly and worsens whenever you are tired or run-down-your body simply cannot keep the eyes properly fueled.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale body, may be thin or show teeth marks, thin white coating. Pulse: fine (thin) and weak.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Qi and Blood are like the fuel that keeps the eyes clear. Rest, sleep, and nutrient-dense food refill that fuel tank, while overwork, poor diet, and excessive eye strain drain it further. By protecting and building Qi and Blood, you give the eyes the steady supply they need to maintain clear vision.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Make rest a priority, as overwork directly drains Qi and Blood. Eat regular, warm meals that include blood-nourishing foods like red dates, goji berries, dark leafy greens, and small amounts of high-quality red meat or bone broth. Limit screen time and take frequent eye breaks to conserve what nourishment you have.

### Liver Yang Rising
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/liver-yang-rising)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Eye distention or pressure, Irritability and quick temper, Dizziness or vertigo, Sudden blurring triggered by anger or stress, Throbbing headache at the temples
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Anger and frustration, Alcohol, Spicy, greasy, or fried foods, Overwork and lack of sleep, Hot, stuffy environments
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Rest in a dark quiet room, Cold compresses on the eyes, Stress reduction, Cooling foods like cucumber, Gentle neck stretches
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  The Liver is responsible for the smooth flow of Qi and blood throughout the body and opens into the eyes. When stress, anger, or prolonged overwork deplete Liver Yin, the cooling, anchoring force weakens, allowing Liver Yang to surge upward like unanchored heat. This rising Yang disturbs the upper orifices, flooding the eyes with pressure and heat, which causes sudden or paroxysmal blurring, a sensation of eye distention, and often dizziness.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red body with redder sides, thin dry yellow or scanty coating. Pulse: wiry (弦 xián), possibly fine and rapid.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Anger and stress directly cause Liver Qi to stagnate and then surge upward as Yang, triggering blurring and eye pressure. Alcohol and spicy foods add heat to the system, further fueling the rising Yang. Cooling foods and rest help nourish Yin and anchor Yang, while calming activities reduce the emotional triggers that set off the pattern.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Avoid alcohol, coffee, and spicy, greasy foods that heat the Liver. Eat cooling, yin-nourishing foods like cucumber, celery, and chrysanthemum tea. Practice stress-reducing activities such as meditation or gentle yoga, and ensure adequate sleep to replenish Yin.

### Blood Stagnation
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/blood-stagnation)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Fixed dark spots or sudden visual field defects, Stabbing eye pain or distention that worsens at night, Purple or dusky tongue with stasis spots, Choppy (Se) pulse
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Prolonged screen time, Stress and prolonged worry, Cold exposure, Sedentary lifestyle
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warm compress on eyes, Gentle exercise, Adequate sleep and rest
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When blood flow becomes sluggish or obstructed, the delicate vessels that nourish the eyes can become blocked. This prevents clear nourishment of the visual system, leading to persistent blurring, fixed dark spots, or even sudden patches of vision loss. The obstruction is often accompanied by a stabbing sensation or a feeling of distention around the eye.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: Purple or dusky body with stasis spots, distended dark sublingual veins, and a thin white coat. Pulse: Choppy (Se), rough and uneven like scraping bamboo; may also be wiry (Xian) if Qi stagnation is present.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Blood stasis occurs when Qi and blood become stagnant. Cold and inactivity congeal the blood, while emotional stress causes Qi stagnation which leads to blood stasis. Warmth and gentle movement activate Qi and blood flow, helping to clear the obstructions. Rest allows the body to repair and regenerate, preventing further stasis.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Incorporate gentle daily movement like walking or tai chi to keep blood moving. Avoid prolonged sitting and eye strain. Eat warming, blood-invigorating foods such as black fungus, turmeric, hawthorn, and a small amount of red wine or vinegar. Keep the neck and shoulders warm to promote circulation to the head.

### Damp-Phlegm
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/damp-phlegm)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Foggy or veiled vision, like looking through a mist, Heavy sensation in head and limbs, Chest stuffiness and fullness, Thick white greasy tongue coating, Poor appetite, nausea
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Damp, greasy foods, Cold raw foods, Damp weather, Sedentary lifestyle
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warm, cooked meals, Gentle exercise, Ginger tea, Dry, airy environment
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When the Spleen's ability to transport and transform fluids is impaired, dampness accumulates and congeals into phlegm. This turbid phlegm-damp can rise upward, misting the clear orifices of the head and clouding the vision. The result is a distinctive foggy or veiled sensation, as if looking through a mist, often accompanied by a heavy head and chest stuffiness.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale, swollen body with teeth marks, thick white greasy coat, wet surface. Pulse: slippery (滑 huá) or soggy (濡 rú).
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Damp-Phlegm arises when the Spleen is too weak to transform fluids, and the resulting turbidity rises to cloud the eyes. Damp, greasy, and cold foods further overwhelm the Spleen and add more dampness, while a sedentary lifestyle allows Qi to stagnate. Warm, light foods and movement strengthen the Spleen, dry dampness, and lift the pathogenic mist away from the head.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Avoid damp-producing foods like dairy, greasy fried foods, and cold raw salads. Eat warm, cooked meals with aromatic spices like ginger and cardamom to help the Spleen transform dampness. Regular gentle exercise like walking or tai chi keeps Qi moving and prevents stagnation.

## คำถามที่พบบ่อย (Frequently Asked Questions)
### Can acupuncture really improve my eyesight?
Yes, many patients notice improved clarity and reduced eye strain after acupuncture, especially when blurriness is linked to Liver Blood deficiency or Liver Yang rising. Acupuncture points around the eyes and on the body work to increase local circulation, calm the nervous system, and redirect nourishment to the eyes. While it may not reverse structural damage like advanced cataracts, it can often enhance visual function and comfort.

### How long does it take for Chinese herbs to work for blurry vision?
You may notice subtle improvements - less eye fatigue, clearer vision in the morning - within 2 to 4 weeks of starting herbs. For chronic, long-standing blurriness, a full course of 3 to 6 months is typical to rebuild the underlying deficiency and stabilize results. Herbs are taken daily, and your formula may be adjusted every few weeks as your pattern shifts.

### Is it safe to combine TCM with my eye drops or medications?
Generally, yes. TCM herbs and acupuncture can be safely combined with most conventional eye treatments. However, if you are taking anticoagulant or antiplatelet drugs (like warfarin or aspirin), inform your TCM practitioner, as some blood-moving herbs (such as Dang Gui or Chuan Xiong) may enhance their effect. Always keep both your ophthalmologist and TCM practitioner informed of all treatments you are using.

### What foods should I eat for better vision?
Focus on foods that nourish Liver Blood and Kidney Yin: goji berries, chrysanthemum tea, dark leafy greens (spinach, kale), black sesame seeds, carrots, and liver (if you eat meat). Avoid greasy, fried, and spicy foods, which can generate Dampness and Heat that cloud the vision. Also limit cold, raw foods, which weaken the Spleen and reduce the production of Qi and Blood.

### Does TCM help with age-related macular degeneration?
Yes, TCM can be a valuable support for both dry and wet AMD. By addressing underlying patterns like Kidney and Liver Yin Deficiency or Blood Stagnation, acupuncture and herbs may slow progression, reduce fluid accumulation, and improve remaining vision. It works best as an integrative approach alongside regular monitoring by an ophthalmologist.

### Will I need to stop wearing my glasses during TCM treatment?
No. TCM aims to improve the health of your eyes and may reduce dependency on corrective lenses over time, but you should continue wearing your glasses or contacts as prescribed. The goal is to support your visual system so that your prescription may stabilize or even improve, but this is a gradual process and not a replacement for corrective lenses.

## การวิจัยทางวิทยาศาสตร์สมัยใหม่ (Modern Scientific Research)
1. **Acupuncture for dry eye: a systematic review** ([ลิงก์](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1755-3768.2009.01855.x))
   *วารสารวิชาการ: Lee MS, Shin BC, Choi TY, Ernst E. Acupuncture for dry eye: a systematic review. Acta Ophthalmologica. 2011;89(2):e101-e106.*
   This systematic review evaluated all randomized controlled trials of acupuncture for dry eye syndrome up to 2009. Acupuncture was associated with greater improvement in tear break-up time and Schirmer test scores compared to artificial tears, with minimal adverse events. The authors noted that the quality of included studies was generally low, and recommended larger, more rigorous trials.
