## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนปัจจุบัน (Conventional Medicine View)
### คำอธิบาย (Description)
Pericarditis is inflammation of the pericardium, the thin, two‑layered sac that surrounds the heart. It often causes sharp, stabbing chest pain that may improve when sitting up and leaning forward, and worsen when lying down or breathing deeply. Common causes include viral infections, autoimmune conditions, or heart surgery, though sometimes no cause is found. Diagnosis typically involves listening for a pericardial friction rub, an electrocardiogram (ECG), echocardiogram, and blood tests for markers of inflammation.

### การรักษาหลัก (Treatments)
Standard treatment for acute pericarditis usually includes high‑dose nonsteroidal anti‑inflammatory drugs (NSAIDs) like ibuprofen, often combined with colchicine to reduce inflammation and prevent recurrence. If symptoms persist, corticosteroids may be prescribed. In cases where fluid buildup compresses the heart (cardiac tamponade), emergency drainage (pericardiocentesis) is required.

## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนจีน (Traditional Chinese Medicine View)
### ความเข้าใจเกี่ยวกับการแพทย์แผนจีน (TCM Understanding)
In TCM, the pericardium is more than a protective sac - it is the Heart's outer guardian, a functional layer that absorbs shocks and protects the Shen (spirit). When a pathogen or imbalance breaches this defense, the pericardium becomes inflamed, causing chest pain, palpitations, and a range of symptoms that vary depending on the underlying pattern. TCM sees pericarditis not as one disease but as several distinct patterns, each with its own cause, its own characteristic pain, and its own treatment.
Acute pericarditis often begins when an external pathogen, such as a virus, invades the body. If the body's defenses fail to stop it at the surface, the Heat can travel deep inside and lodge directly in the pericardium, producing high fever, a sore, burning chest, and a rapid pulse. This pattern, Heat in Pericardium, is an excess condition that must be cleared quickly.
In other cases, the body's fluid metabolism falters, and thin, clear fluids called Phlegm‑Fluids accumulate in the chest cavity and pericardial sac. This fluid physically presses on the heart and lungs, causing a heavy, oppressive chest pain and severe breathlessness - especially when lying flat.
When pericarditis becomes chronic or constrictive, Blood may stagnate right inside the pericardium. This stasis acts like a physical blockage, producing a sharp, fixed pain that feels like a knife stabbing in one spot. The tongue often turns purplish with stasis spots, and the pulse feels wiry or hesitant.
After the acute inflammation subsides, the body may be left depleted: the prolonged fever burns up Qi and Yin fluids, leaving the Heart undernourished and prone to lingering low‑grade heat, night sweats, and palpitations. In severe cases, massive fluid accumulation can compress the heart to the point of extinguishing the body's Yang Qi - a rare but life‑threatening collapse that demands immediate emergency care.

### การวินิจฉัยโดยแพทย์แผนจีน (Practitioner Diagnosis)
A TCM practitioner starts by asking how the chest pain feels and what else accompanies it. A sharp, hot pain with fever and a racing pulse points toward an acute external invasion, while a heavy, oppressive ache with lots of phlegm suggests fluid has built up inside. The tongue and pulse are checked carefully, because they reveal what the eyes cannot see.
If the pain is stabbing and fixed in one spot, especially in a chronic or constrictive case, the practitioner looks for Pericardium Blood Stagnation. The tongue often appears purplish with dark spots, and the pulse feels wiry or hesitant - signs that blood is not moving freely through the pericardium.
When the onset is rapid with high fever, sweating, and a sore, burning chest, Heat in Pericardium is the likely pattern. The tongue coating is thin and yellow, and the pulse is rapid and slippery. This picture tells the practitioner that an external pathogen has lodged deep in the chest and must be cleared quickly.
If the chest feels tight and heavy, and breathing is difficult because of a sensation of fullness, Phlegm‑Fluids in the chest is the main concern. The person coughs up profuse white phlegm, and the tongue coating is thick, white, and greasy. The pulse is slippery or deep and slippery, signalling that fluids have accumulated and are obstructing the pericardium.
During recovery, when the fever drops but a low‑grade heat lingers along with night sweats, palpitations, and a dry mouth, the diagnosis shifts to Qi and Yin Deficiency. The tongue looks red with little coating, and the pulse is thin and weak. The practitioner recognizes that the illness has consumed the body’s nourishing fluids and vital energy.
In a person who has been ill for a long time and appears pale, easily fatigued, and short of breath, Heart and Spleen Qi and Blood Deficiency is suspected. The tongue is pale and puffy, and the pulse is weak or slow. The Spleen’s ability to produce Blood has been compromised, leaving the Heart undernourished.
The most urgent pattern, Collapse of Yang, is unmistakable: extreme breathlessness, cold limbs, a grey complexion, and an inability to lie flat. The tongue is pale and wet, and the pulse is so faint it is barely perceptible. This is a medical emergency where the body’s warming Yang Qi is failing, and immediate professional intervention is critical.

### Heat in Pericardium
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/heat-in-pericardium)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: High fever that worsens at night, Delirium, confusion, or incoherent speech, Sharp chest pain, worse with breathing or lying flat, Cold hands and feet despite high fever, Deep crimson (red) tongue, stiff or shortened
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Spicy, greasy, or fried food, Emotional stress or anger, Hot, stuffy environments, Overexertion or lack of sleep
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Cool, quiet rest, Cooling foods and drinks, Gentle deep breathing exercises, Acupuncture and herbal medicine
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  In acute pericarditis, a strong external pathogen like a virus or bacteria invades the body. If the body's defenses fail to stop it at the surface, the heat pathogen can travel inward and lodge directly in the Pericardium, the protective sac around the Heart. This deep level of heat, called the Nutritive level, disturbs the Heart's connection to consciousness, causing high fever, mental confusion, and a deep red tongue. The inflammation in the pericardial sac also produces sharp chest pain that worsens with movement or deep breathing.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: crimson (deep red) body, stiff or shortened, with a yellow dry coating or little coating. Pulse: fine (xi) and rapid (shu).
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Heat in the Pericardium is a deep internal fire that consumes body fluids and disturbs the mind. Cooling foods and drinks help clear heat and generate fluids, while rest prevents further depletion of Qi and Yin. Spicy or greasy foods add more heat, and emotional stress stirs up Heart fire, making the condition worse.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Focus on cooling, light foods like pear, watermelon, cucumber, and mung bean soup. Avoid spicy, greasy, or fried foods that add heat. Drink plenty of room-temperature water or chrysanthemum tea. Rest completely in a quiet, cool environment and avoid any emotional stress or physical exertion until the fever subsides.

### Phlegm-Fluids in the chest and hypochondrium
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/phlegm-fluids-in-the-chest-and-hypochondrium)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Oppressive chest pain, worse with coughing or movement, Fullness and distension on one side of the chest, Inability to lie flat; must lie on the affected side, Cough with thin white or watery sputum, Pale, swollen tongue with a white slippery coating
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Lying flat, Cold, raw, or icy foods and drinks, Greasy or heavy foods, Coughing or sudden movements, Cold, damp weather
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Propping up with pillows, Lying on the affected side, Warmth and warm drinks, Easily digested meals
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When the body's ability to transform and transport fluids is impaired, clear, thin fluids called Phlegm-Fluids build up in the chest cavity and pericardial sac. This fluid accumulation physically presses on the heart and lungs, causing the heavy, oppressive chest pain and severe breathlessness that are the hallmark of this pattern. The obstruction of Qi flow along the ribs creates the characteristic one-sided fullness and distension, while any movement that shifts the fluid-like coughing or lying flat-intensifies the pain.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale, swollen body with a white slippery or greasy coating, excessively moist. Pulse: deep (Chen) and wiry (Xian).
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Cold and damp foods further impair the Spleen's ability to transform fluids, causing more Phlegm-Fluid to accumulate in the chest. Lying flat allows the fluid to press directly on the heart and lungs, worsening the oppressive pain and breathlessness. Propping up and lying on the affected side use gravity to shift the fluid away, easing the pressure. Warmth and light, cooked meals support the Spleen's Yang energy, helping it to properly metabolize and transport body fluids.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Avoid cold, raw, and dairy-heavy foods that create Dampness and overwhelm the Spleen. Eat warm, cooked meals like congees and soups with a little ginger or pepper to support fluid transformation. Sleep propped up on pillows and, if possible, lie on the affected side to reduce pressure on the heart. Gentle, regular movement like walking helps keep Qi flowing, but avoid overexertion that triggers coughing.

### Pericardium Blood Stagnation
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/pericardium-blood-stagnation)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Stabbing chest pain in a fixed location, Pain may radiate to the left shoulder or inner arm, Purple lips or dark nails, Palpitations with a feeling of chest tightness, Insomnia or mental restlessness
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Emotional stress or anger, Cold weather or cold drinks, Prolonged inactivity, Greasy or heavy foods
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Gentle walking, Warmth on the chest, Rest and stress reduction, Spicy, warming foods
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  In pericarditis, chronic inflammation or constrictive changes can cause Blood to stagnate right inside the pericardium. This stasis acts like a physical blockage, producing a sharp, fixed pain that feels like a knife stabbing in one spot. Because the Heart and Pericardium are directly involved, the obstruction disrupts the Shen (spirit), leading to palpitations, anxiety, and restless sleep.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: purple or dusky body with stasis spots, distended sublingual veins, thin white coat. Pulse: choppy (Se) at the left Cun position, possibly wiry (Xian) or knotted (Jie) with irregular pauses.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Blood stasis congeals with cold and inactivity, so warmth and movement help to disperse it. Emotional stress causes Qi stagnation, which can lead to or worsen Blood stasis. Spicy, warming foods invigorate Blood circulation, while heavy, greasy foods burden the Spleen and create Dampness, further obstructing flow.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat warm, cooked foods and avoid cold, raw items that can congeal Blood. Include mild spices like turmeric and ginger to promote circulation. Gentle daily movement such as walking or Tai Chi helps prevent stagnation. Manage stress with meditation or deep breathing, as emotional tension can worsen Qi and Blood stasis.

### Qi and Yin Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/qi-and-yin-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Dull, mild chest ache rather than sharp pain, Low-grade fever, especially in the afternoon, Palpitations that worsen with even slight exertion, Night sweats, Warm sensation in palms, soles, and chest, Dry mouth with little desire to drink, Fatigue and reluctance to speak
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Overexertion, Spicy, fried, or drying foods, Emotional stress and worry, Late nights and insufficient sleep
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Rest and adequate sleep, Cool, quiet environment, Warm, moistening foods (congee, soups), Gentle deep breathing exercises
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  During the recovery phase of pericarditis, the prolonged fever and inflammation burn up the body's Qi and Yin fluids. The Heart is left undernourished - without enough Yin to cool and moisten it, and without enough Qi to keep its rhythm steady. This produces a lingering low-grade heat, palpitations, and a dull, empty ache in the chest that feels worse after exertion.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red body with a thin or absent coating, dry surface, possibly cracked. Pulse: fine (thready) and weak, often rapid.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Qi and Yin are the body's fuel and coolant. ### Heart and Spleen Qi and Blood Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/heart-and-spleen-qi-and-blood-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Pale or yellowish complexion with pale lips and nails, Poor appetite with bloating after meals, Loose stools, Palpitations worse with exertion, Insomnia with excessive dreaming, Dizziness and forgetfulness
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Overwork and prolonged stress, Skipping meals or eating on the run, Cold, raw, or icy foods and drinks, Overexertion
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Rest and adequate sleep, Easily digested meals, Gentle walking, Regular meal times
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  The Spleen is the body’s factory for Qi and Blood, transforming food into the nourishment that fuels the Heart and its protective sac, the pericardium. After a prolonged bout of pericarditis, the Spleen often becomes depleted, and its ability to generate Blood falters. The pericardium is left undernourished and weak, producing a dull, lingering chest discomfort, fluttering palpitations, and profound fatigue that worsens with the slightest exertion.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale, puffy, and tender with teeth marks and a thin white coating. Pulse: fine and weak; the left front (Heart) position feels especially empty, and the right middle (Spleen) position is soft.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: The Spleen is easily damaged by overwork, irregular eating, and cold or heavy foods - all of which drain its ability to produce Qi and Blood. Rest, warmth, and nourishing meals rebuild Spleen function, which in turn generates enough Blood to steady the Heart and calm the pericardium.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat warm, cooked, easily digested meals at regular times - think congee, soups, and stews - and avoid raw, cold, and greasy foods that tax the Spleen. Gentle daily walks and short rest breaks throughout the day help rebuild energy without depleting it. Prioritize a consistent sleep schedule to allow the Spleen and Heart to recover.

### Collapse of Yang
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/collapse-of-yang)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Ice-cold hands and feet that feel cold past the elbows and knees, Profuse cold, clammy sweating that does not stop, Ashen-white or greyish, lifeless complexion, Blue or purple lips and fingernails, Inability to lie flat, gasping for air when trying to rest, Mental apathy, drowsiness, or loss of consciousness
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Cold, damp weather, Overexertion, Cold, raw, or icy foods and drinks, Severe emotional shock or fright
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warm blankets and external heat, Complete rest, lying still, Warm ginger or cinnamon tea, Moxibustion on the lower abdomen
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  In severe pericarditis, massive fluid accumulation in the pericardial sac can physically compress the heart, crippling its ability to pump blood. In TCM terms, this extinguishes the body's Yang Qi-the warming, driving force that keeps you alive. Without that fire, the limbs turn ice-cold, sweat pours out uncontrollably, and the pulse fades to a thread.
This is not a gradual decline but a sudden, critical collapse. The Heart's Yang can no longer hold the spirit, leading to mental fog or loss of consciousness. It is the TCM equivalent of cardiogenic shock or cardiac tamponade.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale, moist, and flaccid with a thin white slippery coating. Pulse: minute (微, wēi) and on the verge of disappearing (欲绝, yù jué)-extremely faint, thread-like, and barely perceptible even with deep pressure.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Collapse of Yang is a state of extreme internal Cold and Deficiency where the body's metabolic fire is nearly extinguished. Cold environments and raw foods drain what little warmth remains, while exhaustion depletes the last reserves of Qi. Warmth, rest, and warming herbs and spices act like kindling, protecting and gently feeding the dying embers of Yang to stabilize the system.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: This is a life-threatening emergency. Seek immediate Western medical care; TCM can support recovery after stabilization. Once the crisis is past, sip warm ginger and cinnamon tea or lamb bone broth to gently rekindle Yang. Avoid all raw, cold, and icy foods and drinks, which snuff out the body's remaining warmth.

## คำถามที่พบบ่อย (Frequently Asked Questions)
### Can TCM help with the chest pain of pericarditis?
Yes. Acupuncture and herbal formulas can effectively relieve pericarditis pain by addressing its root cause. For acute burning pain from Heat, cooling herbs and points like Quze PC‑3 are used; for heavy, oppressive pain from fluid, diuretic herbs and points like Yinlingquan SP‑9 help drain the excess; for fixed, stabbing pain from Blood Stagnation, blood‑moving herbs and points like Ximen PC‑4 are selected. Many patients notice a reduction in pain intensity within the first few treatments.

### Is it safe to use Chinese herbs alongside my heart medications?
In most cases, yes, but full transparency is essential. Some herbs that move Blood (like Dan Shen) can interact with anticoagulants, so your TCM practitioner must know all your medications. Always inform your cardiologist that you are using TCM, and never stop prescribed medications without medical guidance. A qualified practitioner will tailor the formula to avoid interactions.

### How quickly can acupuncture relieve pericarditis symptoms?
Acute symptoms like chest pain and fever may improve after the first few sessions, often within a week. Chronic or recurrent pericarditis typically requires weekly sessions for several weeks to months, with steady, gradual improvement. Acupuncture works best when combined with herbal medicine and dietary adjustments.

### Does TCM treat the fluid around the heart?
Yes. The pattern of Phlegm‑Fluids in the chest specifically addresses pericardial effusion. Herbal formulas like Ting Li Da Zao Xie Fei Tang promote urination and transform fluids, while acupuncture points such as Fenglong ST‑40 and Yinlingquan SP‑9 help drain dampness. Significant effusions require close monitoring, and emergency drainage is still needed if tamponade develops - TCM is a complementary support, not a replacement for urgent procedures.

### I have recurrent pericarditis. Can TCM prevent future episodes?
Absolutely. TCM aims to correct the underlying imbalance that makes you susceptible. After clearing the acute pathogen, treatment focuses on strengthening Qi, Yin, and Blood, and resolving any lingering stasis or phlegm. Many patients find that a course of herbs and acupuncture during remission periods reduces the frequency and severity of flare‑ups.

### What should I eat to support recovery from pericarditis?
Generally, favor warm, easily digested foods like congee, soups, and steamed vegetables. Avoid spicy, greasy, or deep‑fried foods that can generate Heat and Phlegm. Reduce salt to discourage fluid retention. Specific foods like jujube dates, lotus seeds, and lily bulb can gently nourish the Heart and calm the spirit. Your TCM practitioner can offer more tailored advice based on your pattern.
