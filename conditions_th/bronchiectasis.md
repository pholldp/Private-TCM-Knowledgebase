## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนปัจจุบัน (Conventional Medicine View)
### คำอธิบาย (Description)
Bronchiectasis is a chronic lung condition where the airways become permanently widened, scarred, and thickened. This damage impairs the lungs' ability to clear mucus, leading to a persistent cough, daily production of sputum, and frequent chest infections. It is usually diagnosed with a high-resolution CT scan and can result from previous severe infections, immune disorders, or genetic conditions like cystic fibrosis.

### การรักษาหลัก (Treatments)
Conventional management focuses on controlling symptoms and preventing flare-ups. This includes daily airway clearance techniques (like chest physiotherapy or devices), inhaled bronchodilators and corticosteroids, mucolytics to thin mucus, and prompt antibiotic treatment for acute infections. In severe, localized cases, surgery or bronchial artery embolization may be considered for uncontrolled bleeding.

## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนจีน (Traditional Chinese Medicine View)
### ความเข้าใจเกี่ยวกับการแพทย์แผนจีน (TCM Understanding)
In TCM, bronchiectasis is understood as a disease of the "Lung collaterals" (肺络, fèi luò) - the fine, branching network of channels that distribute Qi and fluids throughout the lungs. When these delicate structures are damaged by repeated infections, emotional stress, or constitutional weakness, they lose their ability to keep the airways open and clean.
Fluids stagnate, transform into phlegm, and become a breeding ground for heat and toxins. This is the root of the chronic cough and sputum production. The type of phlegm - thick, yellow, and hot, or thin, white, and copious - reveals which internal imbalance is driving the disease.
An acute flare with thick yellow sputum signals Phlegm-Heat, a toxic inflammatory state. Chronic white watery sputum points to Damp-Phlegm from a weak Spleen. And if coughing up blood follows an angry outburst, it's often Liver Fire scorching the Lungs - stress directly injuring the lung tissue.
After years of illness, the body's Qi and Yin become depleted. This leads to fatigue, a dry tickling cough, and low-grade fever - not from infection, but from deep deficiency. TCM treats each pattern differently, aiming to clear the current phlegm while also rebuilding the lung collaterals' strength to prevent recurrence.

### การวินิจฉัยโดยแพทย์แผนจีน (Practitioner Diagnosis)
During an acute flare, a practitioner first asks about the sputum. Thick, yellow or green, purulent phlegm that is hard to bring up points strongly toward Phlegm-Heat in the Lungs. The person may also feel feverish, thirsty, and have a tight chest. The tongue will be red with a greasy yellow coating, and the pulse feels rapid and slippery. This pattern is often triggered by a respiratory infection.
When the condition is chronic and less fiery, the sputum tells a different story. Copious, white, watery or frothy phlegm that comes up easily suggests Damp-Phlegm in the Lungs. The person often feels heavy, easily fatigued, and may have a sensation of fullness in the chest. The tongue appears pale and swollen with a white greasy coat, and the pulse is slippery. This reflects the body’s struggle to transform fluids properly.
If coughing up blood is the main alarm, the practitioner looks for emotional triggers. Bright red blood, a bitter taste in the mouth, irritability, and rib-side pain after stress or anger point to Liver Fire insulting the Lungs. The tongue is redder on its edges with a yellow coat, and the pulse is wiry and rapid. This pattern shows heat rising from the Liver to attack the delicate lung tissues.
After repeated infections and prolonged coughing, the body’s reserves become drained. A weak, dry cough with scanty sticky sputum, night sweats, a low-grade afternoon fever, and deep fatigue signal Qi and Yin Deficiency. The tongue looks red and dry with little or no coating, and the pulse is thin and rapid. This is the underlying “empty” state that leaves the lungs vulnerable to further damage.
A less common but important picture involves poor digestion alongside lung symptoms. A weak cough with thin white sputum, poor appetite, loose stools, and a soft, breathless voice suggest Spleen and Lung Qi Deficiency. The tongue is pale with a thin white coat, and the pulse is weak. Here the root problem is a failure to produce enough Qi and manage fluids, leading to chronic phlegm and fatigue.

## กลุ่มอาการการแพทย์แผนจีนและการรักษา (TCM Patterns and Treatment)

### Phlegm-Heat in the Lungs
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/phlegm-heat-in-the-lungs)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Copious thick yellow or green sticky sputum, Chest fullness and feeling of oppression, Fever or sensation of body heat, Sputum with foul or fishy smell, Wheezing or gurgling sounds in the throat
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Greasy or fried foods, Spicy and heating foods, Dairy products, Damp or stuffy environments, Emotional frustration
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Cool fresh air, Light, bland diet, Drinking warm water, Rest and calm, Steam inhalation
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  In bronchiectasis, the airways (肺络, fèi luò) are already damaged and prone to collecting fluids. When external pathogens or internal imbalance generate Heat, that Heat combines with stagnant fluids to form sticky, toxic Phlegm-Heat. This thick phlegm blocks the airways, triggering violent coughing, chest oppression, and difficulty breathing. The Heat also inflames the lung tissue, causing fever and, if the tiny blood vessels are eroded, blood-streaked sputum.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red body, possibly swollen at the front, with a thick yellow greasy coating and sometimes red prickles on the front third. Pulse: slippery (滑, huá) and rapid (数, shuò), strongest at the right cun (Lung) position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Greasy, fried, and dairy foods create Dampness and Phlegm, while spicy and heating foods fan the flames of Heat - together they fuel the Phlegm-Heat that obstructs the Lungs. Cooling, bland foods and warm fluids help dissolve phlegm and clear Heat. Rest conserves Qi, which is needed to expel pathogens, and steam inhalation moistens the airways, making thick sputum easier to cough up.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Avoid greasy, fried, spicy, and dairy-rich foods - they generate Phlegm and add Heat. Instead, eat cooling, moistening foods like Asian pear, radish, water chestnut, and lily bulb. Drink plenty of warm water to thin mucus. Stop smoking and protect yourself from smoky or polluted air. Gentle pursed-lip breathing can help expel sputum without exhausting Qi.

### Damp-Phlegm in the Lungs
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/damp-phlegm-in-the-lungs)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Copious white sticky phlegm, easy to expectorate, Chest congestion and heaviness, Poor appetite, bloating, loose stools, Body heaviness and fatigue
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Cold and damp environments, Overeating cold or raw foods, Dairy products, Sedentary lifestyle, Excessive worry or pensiveness
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warm, dry environment, Gentle walking, Warm cooked grains and soups, Avoiding dairy and greasy food, Keeping chest warm
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  In bronchiectasis, a weakened Spleen fails to transform and transport fluids. These fluids accumulate as dampness, which then congeals into phlegm. The phlegm rises and settles in the Lungs, obstructing the airways and causing the hallmark chronic cough with copious white sputum. The persistent phlegm burden leads to chest heaviness and shortness of breath, especially between acute flare-ups.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale, swollen with teeth marks, thick white greasy coating, excessively moist. Pulse: slippery (hua), possibly soggy (ru), especially in the right cun position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Damp-Phlegm arises when the Spleen is too weak to transform fluids, causing dampness to accumulate and congeal. Cold, raw, and greasy foods further burden the Spleen and generate dampness, while worry knots Spleen Qi. Warmth and gentle movement help the Spleen transport fluids and the Lungs to descend Qi, clearing phlegm and relieving chest congestion. Avoiding damp environments and keeping the chest warm prevent external dampness from adding to internal dampness.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Avoid cold, raw, and greasy foods, dairy, and sweets that create dampness. Eat warm, cooked meals like congee, soups, and lightly spiced stews. Gentle daily exercise such as walking or tai chi helps the Spleen transform fluids and the Lungs to descend Qi. Keep the chest and neck warm and dry, and avoid damp living environments.

### Liver Fire insulting the Lungs
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/liver-fire-insulting-the-lungs)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Sudden forceful coughing fits, Pain or distension along the ribs that worsens with coughing, Irritability and quick temper, Bitter taste in the mouth, Blood-streaked or pink-tinged sputum
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Anger or frustration, Spicy and greasy foods, Alcohol
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Calming down after a stressful event, Deep, slow breathing, Cool drinks like chrysanthemum tea
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  In bronchiectasis, emotional stress and frustration can stagnate Liver Qi, which over time turns into Fire. This Liver Fire rises upward along its channel and attacks the Lungs, scorching the delicate lung tissues and forcing Qi to rebel upward. The result is sudden, forceful coughing fits, chest distension, and often blood-streaked sputum because the heat damages the small blood vessels of the lung collaterals.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red body with redder sides, thin dry yellow coating. Pulse: wiry (弦 xián) and rapid, strongest at the left middle (Guan) position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Anger and emotional stress directly ignite Liver Fire, which then surges upward to attack the Lungs, triggering the coughing and bleeding. Spicy, greasy foods and alcohol add heat to the body, feeding that Fire. Cooling foods and calming practices soothe the Liver and help the Lung Qi descend, breaking the cycle of irritation.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Avoid spicy, greasy, and fried foods, as well as alcohol and coffee, which add heat and stir up Liver Fire. Eat cooling, moistening foods like pear, apple, and cucumber. Sip chrysanthemum tea to gently clear heat. Practice stress-reducing activities such as slow breathing, walking, or gentle yoga to keep Liver Qi flowing smoothly.

### Qi and Yin Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/qi-and-yin-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Fatigue and lack of strength, Dry cough with scanty sticky sputum, Night sweats and warm palms/soles, Shortness of breath on exertion, Dry mouth and throat with little desire to drink, Spontaneous daytime sweating
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Overwork and exhaustion, Spicy or fried foods, Dry, heated indoor air, Smoking, Emotional stress
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Rest and sleep, Warm, non-spicy fluids, Humidified air, Gentle breathing exercises, Nourishing soups
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Chronic bronchiectasis gradually wears down the body's Qi and Yin through repeated infections, persistent coughing, and low-grade inflammation. When Qi is weak, the Lungs cannot properly descend and disperse, leading to fatigue and shortness of breath. When Yin is depleted, the Lungs lose their moistening function, resulting in a dry, tickling cough with scanty, sticky sputum. The combination of Qi failing to hold blood and Yin-deficiency heat can also cause blood-streaked sputum or mild hemoptysis.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red or pale-red body with a thin, dry coating, possibly cracked. Pulse: fine (thin), weak, and often rapid.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Qi and Yin Deficiency means the body lacks the energy and moisture to sustain normal functions. Rest and nourishing fluids help replenish these reserves, while overwork and drying habits further deplete them, worsening cough and fatigue.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat moistening, easy-to-digest foods like pear, white fungus, honey, and congee. Avoid spicy, fried, or drying foods. Prioritize rest and gentle breathing exercises like qigong. Keep indoor air humidified.

### Spleen and Lung Qi Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/spleen-and-lung-qi-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Chronic weak cough with thin, watery or white sputum, Poor appetite and abdominal bloating after eating, Fatigue, weak voice, and spontaneous sweating, Pale puffy tongue with teeth marks
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Overwork and exhaustion, Raw, cold, or greasy foods, Cold and damp environments, Mental stress and worry, Overeating
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warm, easily digestible meals, Rest and adequate sleep, Gentle walking, Warmth and avoiding cold drafts, Small frequent meals
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  The Spleen transforms food into Qi and transports fluids. When Spleen Qi is weak, fluids accumulate and turn into phlegm, which then settles in the Lungs. At the same time, Lung Qi deficiency fails to descend and disperse, so the phlegm is not cleared and a chronic, weak cough with thin watery sputum develops.
The weakened Lung Qi also leaves the body's defensive barrier open, making you prone to frequent colds and prolonged recovery.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale, puffy, and tender with teeth marks; thin white slippery coating. Pulse: weak and forceless, especially at the right cun (Lung) and guan (Spleen) positions.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Spleen Qi is easily damaged by raw cold foods, overwork, and worry, which further impair its ability to transform fluids and produce Qi. Rest, warmth, and easily digested meals support the Spleen's function and reduce phlegm formation. Lung Qi is weakened by cold and damp, so gentle movement and staying warm help keep the Lung's dispersing function active without exhausting Qi.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat warm, cooked foods like congee, soups, and steamed vegetables. Avoid raw, cold, and greasy foods that weaken Spleen Qi. Gentle daily movement such as walking or tai chi helps Qi flow without overexertion. Ensure adequate rest and avoid overwork.

## คำถามที่พบบ่อย (Frequently Asked Questions)
### Can TCM cure bronchiectasis?
TCM cannot reverse permanent lung damage, but it can greatly reduce symptoms, prevent infections, and improve quality of life. Many patients experience fewer flare-ups and less dependence on antibiotics. The goal is to restore balance and strengthen the lungs so they function as well as possible.

### How does TCM treat a bronchiectasis flare-up?
During an acute flare with fever and thick yellow sputum, an herbal formula to clear Phlegm-Heat is used, often with acupuncture to open the chest and reduce coughing. Treatment focuses on quickly resolving infection and inflammation without the side effects of repeated antibiotics.

### Is it safe to combine TCM with my usual medications?
Yes, in most cases. Herbal formulas can be taken alongside inhaled medications and airway clearance routines. However, always inform both your TCM practitioner and your pulmonologist about all treatments. Some herbs can interact with blood thinners if you are taking them for hemoptysis prevention, so coordination is essential.

### Will I need to change my diet?
Yes, diet plays a key role. In general, avoid dairy, greasy, and cold foods that create phlegm. Warm, cooked foods and soups support the Spleen and help reduce mucus. Specific advice depends on your pattern - for example, spicy foods are avoided in Liver Fire, while damp-forming foods are limited in Damp-Phlegm.

### How long do I need to take herbs?
For acute flares, only 1-2 weeks. For chronic patterns, a course of 3-6 months is typical to rebuild the constitution. Many patients continue with a maintenance formula during high-risk seasons like winter to prevent infections.

### Can acupuncture help with coughing fits?
Yes, acupuncture can calm a violent cough by redirecting rebellious Qi and relaxing the airways. Points on the Lung and Liver channels are often used to stop a coughing spasm, providing immediate relief in some cases. Regular sessions reduce overall cough frequency.

### What if I cough up blood?
A small amount of blood-streaked sputum can sometimes occur in bronchiectasis and may be managed with TCM herbs that cool the blood and stop bleeding. However, if you cough up more than a tablespoon of bright red blood, seek emergency medical care immediately - this could be a life-threatening hemorrhage. Please see our Safety section for other urgent warning signs.

   *วารสารวิชาการ: Wang L, et al. Acupuncture for stable bronchiectasis: A randomized sham-controlled trial. Journal of Traditional Chinese Medicine. 2021;41(4):567-574.*
   In this trial, 72 patients with stable bronchiectasis received either real acupuncture or sham acupuncture for 8 weeks. The real acupuncture group showed significantly greater reductions in 24-hour sputum volume, cough severity, and St. George’s Respiratory Questionnaire scores.
