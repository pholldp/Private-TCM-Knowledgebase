## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนปัจจุบัน (Conventional Medicine View)
### คำอธิบาย (Description)
Malabsorption syndrome refers to the small intestine's inability to adequately absorb nutrients, including fats, proteins, carbohydrates, vitamins, and minerals. Typical symptoms include chronic diarrhea, often with pale, foul-smelling, oily stools (steatorrhea), unintended weight loss, bloating, and fatigue. Over time, deficiencies can lead to anemia, bone pain, easy bruising, and nerve problems.
Western medicine diagnoses malabsorption through stool tests for fat content, blood tests for nutrient levels, and sometimes imaging or endoscopy with biopsy. Common underlying causes include celiac disease, chronic pancreatitis, Crohn's disease, small intestinal bacterial overgrowth, and certain surgeries or medications.

### การรักษาหลัก (Treatments)
Treatment targets the underlying cause: a gluten-free diet for celiac disease, pancreatic enzyme replacement for pancreatic insufficiency, antibiotics for bacterial overgrowth, or anti-inflammatory drugs for Crohn's. Nutritional supplements - often high-dose vitamins, iron, calcium, and sometimes medium-chain triglyceride oils - are used to correct deficiencies. Anti-diarrheal medications may provide temporary relief but don't address the root problem.

## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนจีน (Traditional Chinese Medicine View)
### ความเข้าใจเกี่ยวกับการแพทย์แผนจีน (TCM Understanding)
In TCM, the Spleen and Stomach are the body's central processing plant. The Stomach receives food and begins to break it down, while the Spleen transforms the resulting essence into Qi and Blood - the raw materials for every cell. When this system is weak, food isn't fully converted, leaving undigested matter to pass through as loose stools and leaving the body undernourished no matter how much you eat.
This weakness can take several forms. Simple Spleen Qi Deficiency is like a stove with a low flame - it can't cook food properly. Spleen Yang Deficiency adds a layer of internal cold, making digestion slow and painful, with watery stools and a craving for warmth. Damp-Heat clogs the system with sticky, inflammatory residue, producing foul-smelling diarrhea and a heavy sensation.
Because the Liver controls the smooth flow of Qi, emotional stress can cause it to attack the Spleen, triggering urgent bowel movements and bloating that flare with tension.
When malabsorption persists, the Spleen eventually fails to produce enough Blood, leading to a pale face, dizziness, and a thin pulse - a pattern of Blood Deficiency layered on top of digestive collapse. This is why TCM never treats malabsorption as a one-size-fits-all problem; the exact combination of weakness, cold, heat, dampness, and emotional strain determines the treatment.

### การวินิจฉัยโดยแพทย์แผนจีน (Practitioner Diagnosis)
A TCM practitioner begins by asking about stool quality and abdominal discomfort. The most common root is Spleen Qi Deficiency (脾气虚, pí qì xū), marked by fatigue, loose stools, and bloating after meals. The tongue is pale and puffy with teeth marks, the pulse weak.
As this persists, the Spleen fails to produce enough Blood, leading to pale complexion, dizziness, and a thin pulse - a secondary Blood Deficiency (血虚, xuè xū).
When the picture includes foul-smelling diarrhea, a heavy sensation, and a greasy yellow tongue coating, Damp-Heat in the Stomach and Spleen (脾胃湿热, pí wèi shī rè) is likely. The pulse is slippery and rapid. This pattern often stems from a diet rich in greasy, sweet, or spicy foods that create internal Heat and Dampness, further impairing nutrient absorption.
If the abdominal pain is dull, constant, and feels better with warmth and pressure, the diagnosis shifts toward Spleen Yang Deficiency (脾阳虚, pí yáng xū). Here Qi deficiency has deepened into a cold pattern. Stools are watery with undigested food, the tongue is pale and swollen, and the pulse is deep and slow. Warmth and rest bring relief.
When digestive symptoms clearly flare with emotional stress - alternating diarrhea and constipation, distension that moves, and a wiry pulse - the pattern is Rebellious Liver Qi invading the Spleen (肝气犯脾, gān qì fàn pí). The tongue may be normal or slightly red on the sides. The Liver’s energy over-controls the Spleen, disrupting its transport function during frustration or anxiety.

## กลุ่มอาการการแพทย์แผนจีนและการรักษา (TCM Patterns and Treatment)

### Spleen Qi Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/spleen-qi-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Poor appetite, feeling full quickly, Abdominal bloating that worsens after eating, Loose, unformed stools without strong odor, Constant fatigue and heaviness in the limbs, Pale, puffy tongue with tooth marks, Soft, weak pulse, especially at the right wrist
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Raw and cold foods or drinks, Greasy, fried, or heavy meals, Worry, stress, or overthinking, Overwork and exhaustion, Damp, humid environments
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warm, cooked meals and soups, Ginger tea or warm water, Rest after meals, Gentle walking or movement, Consistent meal times
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  The Spleen is the main engine for transforming food into usable energy and nutrients (Qi and Blood). When Spleen Qi is weak, the digestive fire is too low to fully break down and absorb food. This leaves undigested material to pass through, causing loose stools, while the body misses out on the nourishment it needs, leading to fatigue, bloating after meals, and a pale complexion.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale, puffy, tender with tooth marks, thin white coating. Pulse: deficient (Xu), soft and weak, notably weaker at the right Guan position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: The Spleen thrives on warmth and gentle movement. Warm, cooked foods are easier to digest and don't tax the already weak digestive fire. Cold and raw foods douse that fire, making it harder to transform food into energy. Rest and consistent meal times allow the Spleen to work without being overwhelmed, while overthinking and worry directly stagnate Spleen Qi, worsening bloating and fatigue.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Focus on warm, cooked, easily digestible foods like congee, soups, stewed vegetables, and small amounts of lean protein. Avoid raw, cold, and greasy foods that dampen the digestive fire. Eat smaller, more frequent meals and chew thoroughly to give the Spleen less work. Gentle daily movement like walking or tai chi helps circulate Qi without depleting it.

### Damp-Heat in Stomach and Spleen
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/damp-heat-in-stomach-and-spleen)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Foul-smelling, sticky stools that feel incomplete, Bitter or sticky taste in the mouth, Heavy feeling in body and limbs, head foggy, Bloating and fullness after eating, Thirst with little desire to drink, Low-grade fever or feeling of heat
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Greasy, fried, or spicy foods, Alcohol, Damp, humid environments, Overeating, Worry, stress, or overthinking
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Light, easily digestible foods, Bitter flavors (e.g., dandelion greens), Gentle walking or movement, Dry, airy environments
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When the Spleen's ability to transform food is already weak, rich, greasy, or spicy foods can create a sticky, turbid Dampness. This Dampness then combines with Heat-often from alcohol, emotional stress, or a hot constitution-to clog the digestive tract. The result is a foul-smelling, sticky diarrhea and a heavy, bloated sensation after meals, because the Spleen cannot separate pure nutrients from waste.
The Heat component produces a bitter taste in the mouth, a thirst that is never quite quenched by drinking, and a yellow greasy tongue coating, while the Dampness makes the body feel heavy and the head foggy, further impairing appetite and nutrient absorption.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red body, possibly swollen with teeth marks, thick yellow greasy coating especially at centre and root. Pulse: soggy (Ru) and rapid (Shu), often slippery (Hua).
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Greasy, spicy, and sweet foods, along with alcohol, directly contribute to dampness and heat in the body, overwhelming the Spleen's ability to transform and transport. Damp, humid weather adds external dampness, while emotional stress can generate internal heat. In contrast, light, bland foods and bitter flavors help clear heat and dry dampness, and gentle movement stimulates Qi flow to resolve the sticky stagnation.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Avoid greasy, fried, spicy, and sweet foods, as well as alcohol and dairy, which generate damp-heat. Eat light, easily digestible meals like congee, steamed vegetables, and bitter greens. Drink warm water or barley tea to help drain dampness. Gentle daily exercise such as walking or tai chi can help move Qi and reduce heaviness.

### Spleen Yang Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/spleen-yang-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Dull abdominal pain relieved by warmth and pressure, Watery stools with undigested food, Cold hands and feet, feeling cold easily, Abdominal bloating worse after eating, Puffiness or swelling of the limbs, Profound tiredness and heaviness
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Raw and cold foods or drinks, Greasy, fried, or heavy meals, Cold, damp weather, Overwork and exhaustion, Worry, stress, or overthinking
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warmth on the abdomen, Warm, cooked meals and soups, Ginger tea or warm water, Rest and lying down, Gentle walking or movement
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When Spleen Qi deficiency deepens, the warming function of Yang is lost, creating an internal Cold that slows digestion to a crawl. The Spleen can no longer transform food into usable energy, so meals sit heavy, cause bloating, and pass through as watery stools with undigested bits. This cold, sluggish state starves the body of nourishment, producing deep fatigue, cold limbs, and a dull ache that only warmth can soothe.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: Pale, puffy, tender body with teeth marks and a white slippery coating. Pulse: Deep, slow, weak, especially at the right Guan position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Spleen Yang is like a cooking pot's flame. Cold and raw foods pour ice water on it, while overwork and stress steal the fuel. Warmth, cooked foods, and rest feed that flame, allowing the Spleen to gently simmer food into usable energy. Ginger and gentle movement fan the flame without blowing it out, restoring the body's ability to extract nourishment.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat only warm, cooked foods like soups, stews, and congees. Avoid raw salads, cold drinks, dairy, and greasy foods that chill or overload the digestive fire. Use gentle warming spices such as ginger, cinnamon, and a pinch of black pepper. Keep a hot water bottle over your abdomen and dress warmly. Gentle daily walking helps raise Yang without exhausting it, but rest is your best medicine when fatigue hits.

### Rebellious Liver Qi invading the Spleen
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/rebellious-liver-qi-invading-the-spleen)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Abdominal pain that leads to diarrhea, Worse with emotional upset, Irritability or quick temper, Frequent sighing, Alternating constipation and loose stools, Distension and fullness along the ribs
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Anger or frustration, Irregular eating habits, Alcohol, Raw and cold foods or drinks
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Calm, stress-free environment, Warm, cooked meals and soups, Gentle walking or movement
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  The Liver is responsible for the smooth flow of Qi throughout the body, including the digestive process. When emotional stress, frustration, or anger causes the Liver Qi to stagnate and then violently surge, it attacks the Spleen horizontally. This disrupts the Spleen’s ability to transport and transform food, leading to the erratic bowel habits, bloating, and fatigue characteristic of malabsorption.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: normal or slightly pale body, thin white coating, possible teeth marks. Pulse: wiry (弦 xián) overall, especially wiry at the left middle position, softer at the right middle position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Emotional stress directly fuels the Liver Qi that attacks the Spleen, so calming the mind and reducing frustration are key. Irregular eating and cold foods further weaken the already compromised Spleen, while warm, regular meals and gentle movement support its function and help the Liver Qi flow smoothly again.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat warm, cooked meals at regular times and avoid skipping breakfast. Gentle walking or yoga helps move Qi and calm the mind. Reduce raw, cold, and greasy foods that burden the Spleen, and limit alcohol and caffeine, which can aggravate Liver stagnation.

### Blood Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/blood-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Pale face, lips, and nail beds, Dizziness or light-headedness, Heart palpitations, Numbness or tingling in hands and feet, Scanty menstrual flow with pale blood, Poor memory and difficulty concentrating
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Overwork and exhaustion, Skipping meals, Worry, stress, or overthinking, Raw and cold foods or drinks
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Rest and lying down, Warm, nourishing soups and stews, Gentle walking or movement, Iron-rich foods like dark leafy greens
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When malabsorption persists, the Spleen becomes too weak to extract enough nourishment from food. Since the Spleen is the main source of Blood, this leads to a state where the body simply cannot make enough Blood to moisten and nourish the tissues. That is why you see the pale complexion, dizziness, and thin pulse that are the hallmarks of Blood Deficiency.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale, thin body with a thin white coating. Pulse: fine (细 xì), weak, and possibly choppy (涩 sè).
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Blood is produced from the food essence transformed by Spleen Qi. Rest and warm, easily digestible foods support the Spleen's ability to create Blood, while overwork, worry, and cold foods drain Spleen Qi and consume the Blood that is already scarce. Gentle movement encourages circulation without depleting the body's reserves.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Focus on warm, cooked meals that are easy to digest and rich in iron and protein. Bone broths, stews with dark leafy greens, red dates, and small amounts of well-cooked red meat are ideal. Avoid cold, raw foods and excessive dairy, which further weaken the Spleen. Gentle daily movement like walking or tai chi helps build Qi and Blood without draining you.

## คำถามที่พบบ่อย (Frequently Asked Questions)
### How long does it take to see results with TCM for malabsorption?
Many patients notice less bloating and more formed stools within the first 2-4 weeks. Energy levels and weight stabilization often take 6-8 weeks. For long-standing deficiency patterns, full recovery of nutrient stores and digestive strength can take 3-6 months of consistent herbs and dietary changes.

### Can TCM help if I have celiac disease?
Yes, as a complement to a strict gluten-free diet. TCM cannot reverse the autoimmune reaction, but it can strengthen the Spleen and repair the gut lining's function, reducing residual symptoms like bloating, fatigue, and poor nutrient absorption that sometimes persist even after gluten is removed. Always continue your gluten-free diet and consult your doctor.

### Will I need to change my diet?
Almost certainly. TCM dietary therapy is a cornerstone of treatment. You'll likely be advised to eat warm, cooked foods, avoid raw and cold items, and limit greasy, spicy, or dairy-heavy meals that create Dampness. Specific recommendations depend on your pattern - for example, Damp-Heat patterns benefit from bitter greens, while Spleen Yang deficiency calls for warming spices like ginger and cinnamon.

### Is acupuncture effective for malabsorption?
Yes. Acupuncture points like Zusanli (ST-36) and Zhongwan (REN-12) directly stimulate digestive function and strengthen the Spleen and Stomach. Many patients report improved appetite, less bloating, and more regular bowel movements after a few sessions. It works best when combined with herbal medicine and dietary changes.

### Can I take TCM herbs with my digestive enzymes or supplements?
Generally yes, but separate them by at least an hour to avoid any interference with absorption. Always bring a full list of your medications and supplements to your TCM practitioner. If you're on blood thinners, inform your practitioner, as some herbs that move Blood (though rarely used in these patterns) could interact.

### What does my tongue tell you about my digestion?
Your tongue is a map of your internal organs. A pale, puffy tongue with teeth marks suggests Spleen Qi Deficiency. A thick, greasy yellow coating points to Damp-Heat. A pale, wet tongue indicates Spleen Yang Deficiency with cold. Your practitioner will check your tongue at every visit to track your progress.

## การวิจัยทางวิทยาศาสตร์สมัยใหม่ (Modern Scientific Research)
1. **Traditional Chinese medicine prescription Shenling Baizhu powder for ulcerative colitis: A systematic review and meta-analysis** ([ลิงก์](https://doi.org/10.1155/2022/9494158))
   *วารสารวิชาการ: Wang Y, et al. (2022). Evidence-Based Complementary and Alternative Medicine, 2022, Article ID 9494158.*
   This meta-analysis of randomized controlled trials evaluated Shenling Baizhu powder as an adjunct to conventional therapy for ulcerative colitis. Results showed significantly higher clinical remission rates and improved mucosal healing in the combination group compared to conventional medication alone, supporting its role in chronic inflammatory bowel conditions where malabsorption is a feature.

2. **Acupuncture for irritable bowel syndrome: a systematic review and meta-analysis**
   *วารสารวิชาการ: Manheimer E, et al. (2012). American Journal of Gastroenterology, 107(6), 835-847.*
   This systematic review included multiple RCTs and found that acupuncture significantly improved global IBS symptoms, including diarrhea and abdominal pain, compared to sham acupuncture or pharmacological treatments. While not specific to malabsorption, the findings support acupuncture's ability to regulate gastrointestinal motility and Spleen function.
