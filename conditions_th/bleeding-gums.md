## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนปัจจุบัน (Conventional Medicine View)
### คำอธิบาย (Description)
In conventional medicine, bleeding gums are most often a sign of gingivitis or periodontitis, caused by plaque buildup along the gumline. The bacteria in plaque irritate the gums, making them inflamed, tender, and prone to bleeding during brushing or flossing. Other possible causes include vitamin deficiencies (especially vitamin C or K), blood-clotting disorders, certain medications like blood thinners, hormonal changes during pregnancy, or systemic conditions such as leukemia.
Diagnosis typically involves a dental examination, measuring pocket depths around teeth, and sometimes X-rays to assess bone loss. Blood tests may be ordered if a systemic cause is suspected. Treatment focuses on removing plaque and tartar, improving oral hygiene, and managing any underlying medical condition.

### การรักษาหลัก (Treatments)
Standard treatment begins with professional dental cleaning (scaling and root planing) to remove plaque and tartar above and below the gumline. Patients are advised to brush twice daily, floss, and use an antiseptic mouthwash. For more advanced periodontitis, antibiotics or surgical procedures may be needed. If a nutritional deficiency or blood disorder is identified, supplementation or specific medical treatment is prescribed.

## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนจีน (Traditional Chinese Medicine View)
### ความเข้าใจเกี่ยวกับการแพทย์แผนจีน (TCM Understanding)
In TCM, the gums are closely connected to the Stomach, Kidney, and Spleen organ systems. The Stomach channel runs directly through the gums, so any excess heat in the Stomach - often from spicy, greasy food or emotional stress - can flare upward and scorch the gum tissue, causing bright red swelling and sudden bleeding. This is the most common acute pattern.
Chronic, low-grade oozing without much redness often points to a very different root: Kidney Yin deficiency. When the body's cooling, anchoring energy is depleted by overwork or insufficient rest, a subtle 'empty heat' rises to the gums, loosening teeth and making them bleed easily, especially at night. The Spleen, which holds blood within the vessels, can also be at fault; if its Qi is weak, blood seeps out passively, and the gums appear pale and puffy.
A fourth pattern, Heat in the Blood, is a deeper systemic condition where heat agitates the blood itself, causing reckless bleeding from multiple sites - gums, nose, skin, or heavy menstrual flow. Because one Western symptom can stem from such different TCM roots, treatment must always be tailored to the individual pattern.

### การวินิจฉัยโดยแพทย์แผนจีน (Practitioner Diagnosis)
If the bleeding is bright red, sudden, and comes with swollen, painful gums, a practitioner suspects Stomach Fire. This excess heat rises along the stomach channel, scorching the gum vessels. The person often has bad breath, thirst for cold drinks, and maybe constipation. The tongue is red with a yellow coating, and the pulse feels rapid and forceful - classic signs of a blazing internal fire.
When bleeding is a slow ooze rather than a gush, and the teeth feel loose or the mouth is dry especially at night, the picture shifts to Kidney Yin deficiency. Here the body’s cooling, anchoring energy is depleted, so a low-grade “empty heat” flares upward. The person may also notice warm palms and soles, night sweats, or a sore lower back. The tongue is red with little coating, and the pulse is thin and rapid.
If gum bleeding is part of a wider tendency to bleed - easy bruising, heavy periods, or tiny red skin spots - the practitioner considers Heat in the Blood. This deep-level heat agitates the blood so it escapes the vessels. The gums may look dark red, and the tongue is often deep red or purplish with a yellow coat. The pulse is rapid and slippery, reflecting turbulent blood.
When the bleeding is pale, scanty, and chronic, with no redness or swelling, the root is likely a Spleen that is too weak to hold blood in its vessels. The person typically feels tired, has a poor appetite, and looks pale. The tongue is pale with a thin white coating, and the pulse is weak and thready. This pattern lacks the heat signs seen in the others.

### Stomach Fire (Stomach Heat)
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/stomach-fire-stomach-heat)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Gums bleed bright red blood, often with swelling and pain, Foul breath and a bitter or sour taste in the mouth, Strong thirst for cold drinks, Burning sensation or pain in the stomach area, Frequent hunger or a large appetite, Constipation with dry, hard stools
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Spicy, fried, or greasy foods, Alcohol and coffee, Emotional stress, anger, or frustration, Overeating or eating late at night
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Cooling foods like cucumber or watermelon, Cool drinks (not iced), Gentle, regular meals without overeating, Rest and stress reduction
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  The Stomach channel runs through the gums. When excess heat builds up in the Stomach-often from rich, spicy food or emotional stress-that fire travels upward along the channel and scorches the delicate gum tissue. The heat agitates the blood, making the small vessels fragile and prone to rupture, which produces bright red bleeding that is often accompanied by swelling and pain.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red body, often with a deeper red in the centre and possible red prickles; thick, dry, yellow coating. Pulse: rapid, slippery, and forceful, especially strong at the right middle (Guan) position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Stomach Fire is a pattern of excess heat. Anything that adds more heat-spicy food, alcohol, emotional frustration-pours fuel on the fire, pushing it upward to the gums. Cooling, moistening foods and calm habits do the opposite: they drain away the excess heat and protect the Stomach’s Yin, allowing the gum tissue to heal and the bleeding to stop.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Avoid alcohol, coffee, chili, fried food, and overly rich meals-they all stoke Stomach Fire. Eat more cooling, moistening foods like cucumber, watermelon, pear, and leafy greens. Drink plenty of water at room temperature or slightly cool, and try to eat at regular times without rushing. Gentle exercise and stress management help prevent heat from stagnating.

### Kidney Yin Deficiency With Empty-Heat Blazing
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/kidney-yin-deficiency-with-empty-heat-blazing)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Mild, oozing gum bleeding, Teeth feel loose or wobbly, Dry mouth worse at night, Heat sensation in palms, soles, and chest (five-palm heat), Lower back soreness and weakness, Tinnitus or diminished hearing, Night sweats, Red tongue with little or no coating
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Spicy, fried, or greasy foods, Alcohol and coffee, Overwork, lack of sleep, or fatigue, Emotional stress, anger, or frustration, Hot weather or saunas
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Adequate sleep and rest, Cooling, moistening foods (pear, tofu), Gentle exercise (walking, tai chi), Rest and stress reduction
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Kidney Yin is the body's cooling reserve. When it becomes depleted through overwork, late nights, or chronic stress, the relative Yang becomes unanchored and floats upward as 'empty heat.' This empty heat travels along the Kidney channel to the teeth and gums, gently scorching the delicate blood vessels and causing a slow, persistent ooze of blood. Because the root is a deficiency, the bleeding is usually mild, and the teeth may feel loose due to poor nourishment of the bones.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red, thin, dry body with little or no coating, possible cracks. Pulse: fine (xi) and rapid (shu), especially weak at the rear (chi) position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Kidney Yin is depleted by overwork, late nights, and emotional strain. Spicy and heating foods stir up empty fire, making it flare to the gums. Cooling, moistening foods and adequate rest replenish Yin, helping the body anchor yang and quiet the heat. Gentle movement encourages circulation without further exhausting Yin.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Avoid late nights and overwork, which deplete Yin. Eat cooling, moistening foods like pear, tofu, black sesame, and seaweed. Sip warm water throughout the day, especially at night. Gentle practices like tai chi or restorative yoga help nourish Yin without overheating.

### Heat in the Blood
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/heat-in-the-blood)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Bright red gum bleeding, often spontaneous, Bleeding from other sites like nose or skin, Feeling of internal heat that worsens at night, Restlessness and irritability, Thirst for cold drinks
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Spicy, fried, or greasy foods, Alcohol and coffee, Hot weather or saunas, Emotional stress, anger, or frustration, Overwork, lack of sleep, or fatigue
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Cool drinks (not iced), Rest in a cool room, Cooling foods like cucumber or watermelon, Gentle exercise (walking, tai chi)
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Heat in the Blood is a deep, systemic excess heat that penetrates the blood level and makes it move recklessly. This reckless movement forces blood out of the vessels, including the delicate gum tissues. Unlike local Stomach Fire, this gum bleeding often appears alongside other bleeding signs like nosebleeds, heavy periods, or skin spots, and feels worse at night when the heat intensifies.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: deep red body with red spots or prickles, thin yellow dry coating. Pulse: rapid (数 shuò), often wiry (弦 xián) and forceful.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: <p>Heat in the Blood is an excess heat condition where the blood is already agitated. Spicy foods, alcohol, and hot environments add more heat to the body, making the blood move even more recklessly and worsening bleeding. Cooling foods and rest help dissipate heat and calm the blood. Stress and anger stir up Liver fire, which can further heat the blood, so staying calm and rested is crucial to prevent flare-ups.</p>
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat plenty of cooling foods like cucumber, watermelon, and mung beans. Avoid spicy, greasy, and deep-fried foods that generate internal heat. Stay hydrated with cool or room temperature water, and limit alcohol and coffee. Gentle exercise like walking or tai chi can help regulate Qi without overheating. Prioritize rest and avoid overwork to prevent aggravating the heat.

### Spleen not controlling Blood
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/spleen-not-controlling-blood)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Pale, chronically oozing gums with no redness or swelling, Fatigue, weakness, and shortness of breath, Poor appetite and loose stools, Pale or sallow complexion, Dizziness or lightheadedness
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Overwork, lack of sleep, or fatigue, Raw and cold foods, Stress and worry
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Adequate sleep and rest, Warm, easily digestible foods, Gentle exercise (walking, tai chi)
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  The Spleen is responsible for holding blood within the vessels. When Spleen Qi is weakened by overwork, poor diet, or chronic illness, it loses the ability to contain the blood. Blood then seeps out of the gum capillaries, resulting in chronic, pale oozing rather than the bright red, painful bleeding seen in heat patterns. The gums themselves are often pale and not inflamed.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale, puffy body with teeth marks and a thin white coating. Pulse: fine and weak, especially at the right Guan position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Spleen Qi is easily depleted by overwork, irregular eating, and cold foods. Rest and warm, nourishing foods help restore the Spleen's ability to hold blood within the vessels, reducing oozing.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat warm, cooked meals like soups and stews; avoid raw, cold, and greasy foods that burden the Spleen. Prioritize rest and avoid overwork to conserve Qi.

## คำถามที่พบบ่อย (Frequently Asked Questions)
### Why do my gums bleed when I brush but not otherwise?
This is often the earliest sign of an imbalance. In TCM terms, the gum vessels are already fragile from Heat or deficiency, but the mechanical stimulation of brushing is the final nudge that makes them break. If your gums are red and swollen, it points to Stomach Fire; if they are pale and puffy, it suggests Spleen weakness. Either way, the bleeding is a clue that the tissue is not as resilient as it should be, even if it does not hurt.

### Can Chinese herbs really stop gum bleeding?
Yes, but they work by correcting the internal pattern that causes the bleeding, not by simply clotting the blood. For Stomach Fire, cooling herbs like Huang Lian clear the heat and stop the scorching. For Spleen deficiency, tonics like Huang Qi strengthen the Spleen's ability to hold blood. The bleeding usually lessens within the first week or two, and the gums become less fragile over time as the underlying imbalance resolves.

### Do I need to change my diet?
Diet plays a big role, especially in heat patterns. Spicy, fried, and greasy foods, alcohol, and smoking all stoke internal fire and directly aggravate the gums. Cooling foods like cucumber, watermelon, pear, and leafy greens help clear heat. If your pattern is deficiency-based, warm, nourishing soups and stews support Spleen Qi. Your practitioner will guide you based on your specific pattern, but avoiding obvious heat-generating foods is a good start for almost everyone.

### Can acupuncture help with bleeding gums?
Yes. Acupuncture points on the Stomach and Large Intestine channels, like Hegu (LI-4) and Neiting (ST-44), can quickly reduce heat and inflammation in the gums. For deficiency patterns, points like Zusanli (ST-36) and Taixi (KI-3) strengthen the body's underlying energy. Many patients notice less gum sensitivity and bleeding after just a few sessions, especially when combined with herbs.

### Is it safe to combine TCM with my dental treatment?
Absolutely. TCM works well alongside regular dental cleanings and checkups. Just make sure to inform both your dentist and your TCM practitioner about all treatments you are receiving. If you are taking blood-thinning medication, your herbal formula must be carefully chosen to avoid herbs that also move blood, so full disclosure is essential.

### Will the bleeding come back after I stop treatment?
When the root pattern is fully corrected, the gums remain healthy. This is the goal of TCM - not just to stop the bleeding temporarily but to restore the body's balance so that the gums can withstand normal brushing and minor irritations. Some people may need occasional maintenance herbs or seasonal acupuncture if their constitution tends toward heat or deficiency, but recurrence is far less likely than with local treatments alone.
