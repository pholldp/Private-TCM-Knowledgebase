## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนปัจจุบัน (Conventional Medicine View)
### คำอธิบาย (Description)
Western medicine defines pruritus (itching) as an unpleasant sensation that provokes the desire to scratch. It can be generalized or localized, acute or chronic, and may occur with or without a visible rash. Common causes include dry skin (xerosis), allergic reactions, eczema, psoriasis, insect bites, liver or kidney disease, and certain medications. Diagnosis typically involves a physical exam, history, and sometimes blood tests or skin biopsy to rule out underlying systemic disease.
Itching is a complex symptom involving nerve fibers, immune cells, and chemical mediators like histamine. While antihistamines help with histamine-driven itch (like hives), many chronic itches are non-histaminergic and require different approaches, such as targeting nerve pathways or inflammation.

### การรักษาหลัก (Treatments)
Conventional treatment focuses on relieving the sensation and addressing any identified cause. Options include topical moisturizers, corticosteroid creams, antihistamines (oral or topical), and in severe cases, immunosuppressants, phototherapy, or nerve-targeting medications like gabapentin. For systemic causes, treating the underlying condition (e.g., liver or kidney disease) is key.

## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนจีน (Traditional Chinese Medicine View)
### ความเข้าใจเกี่ยวกับการแพทย์แผนจีน (TCM Understanding)
TCM sees the skin as an extension of the internal organs, particularly the Lungs, Spleen, and Liver. The Lungs govern the skin and control the defensive Qi that circulates at the surface, repelling external invaders like Wind and Heat. When this defense is weak, external pathogens can penetrate and cause acute, red, burning itching - what we call Wind-Heat. This is why itching often flares with changes in weather or exposure to wind.
But many itches come from within. The Spleen transforms food into Qi and Blood and manages fluid metabolism. When it's overloaded by rich, greasy foods or damp surroundings, it generates Damp-Heat, which can seep outward to the skin like steam from a boiling pot, causing weepy, oozing, and intensely itchy rashes. The Liver stores Blood and ensures the smooth flow of Qi; emotional stress, frustration, or repressed anger can stagnate Liver Qi, generating Heat that rises to the skin, making the itch flare with every emotional upset.
Chronic, dry, scaly itching - the kind that worsens at night and affects older adults - often points to a deeper deficiency. When the body's Blood is insufficient, the skin loses its nourishment and moisture, becoming dry and vulnerable. This internal dryness can generate its own 'Wind,' an internal disturbance that mimics the erratic, moving nature of external Wind, causing itching that shifts location and intensifies when the body is at rest. This is why TCM treats many chronic itches not by suppressing the sensation, but by nourishing the Blood and extinguishing the internal Wind.
Because the root cause can be so different - external invasion, internal Damp-Heat, Liver fire, or Blood deficiency - the same Western diagnosis of 'pruritus' can require completely opposite treatments. One person may need cooling herbs to clear Heat, while another needs warming, nourishing herbs to build Blood. That's why TCM always begins with pattern differentiation, not a one-size-fits-all anti-itch remedy.

### การวินิจฉัยโดยแพทย์แผนจีน (Practitioner Diagnosis)
A TCM practitioner starts by asking what the itch feels like and when it strikes. Dry, scaly skin that itches intensely at night, especially in older adults, points to Blood Deficiency with External Wind. The tongue is often pale with little coating, and the pulse is thin and weak, reflecting blood failing to nourish the skin and wind invading from outside.
If the itching is acute, with redness and a burning sensation that worsens in windy or hot conditions, the pattern is likely Wind‑Heat. The tongue looks red with a thin yellow coat, and the pulse feels floating and rapid. This is an exterior invasion that needs to be cleared and dispersed quickly.
When the skin is not only red but also oozing, with small blisters or a sticky discharge, and there is body heaviness, a sticky taste in the mouth, and digestive discomfort, the diagnosis shifts to Damp‑Heat in Stomach and Spleen. The tongue is red with a thick, yellow, greasy coating, and the pulse is slippery and rapid.
Emotional stress that reliably triggers or worsens itching, along with irritability, a bitter taste, and a feeling of fullness under the ribs, suggests Liver Qi Stagnation transforming into Heat. The tongue may be red on the sides, and the pulse is wiry and rapid. This pattern is less common but important because its root is emotional, not just skin‑deep.

## กลุ่มอาการการแพทย์แผนจีนและการรักษา (TCM Patterns and Treatment)

### Blood Deficiency with External Wind
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/blood-deficiency-with-external-wind)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Dry, flaky, or rough skin, Itching that worsens at night, Itching that shifts location or comes and goes, Pale, sallow complexion, Aversion to wind or drafts, Dizziness or light-headedness
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Wind and drafts, Spicy, fried, or drying foods, Overwork and exhaustion, Nighttime
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Rest and sleep, Warm, nourishing soups, Moisturizing the skin, Warmth, Gentle movement
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When Blood is deficient, it fails to nourish and moisten the skin, leaving it dry, flaky, and poorly protected. This emptiness at the surface allows external Wind to invade easily, stirring up itching that comes and goes or shifts location. The combination of internal dryness and external Wind creates a chronic, stubborn itch that often intensifies at night when Blood returns to the Liver and the skin is even less nourished.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale and thin with a thin white slightly dry coat. Pulse: fine (thin), often wiry at the left middle (Liver) position and floating at the right front (Lung) position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Blood is the body's moisture and nourishment for the skin. When it is deficient, the skin becomes dry, flaky, and vulnerable to external Wind, which triggers itching. Rest and sleep allow Blood to return to the Liver and replenish the skin, while warm, nourishing foods build Blood. Wind and drafts carry the external pathogen that directly provokes itching, so avoiding them is protective. Overwork consumes Blood, and nighttime is when Blood naturally retreats inward, leaving the skin even less nourished, which is why itching often worsens then.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Focus on nourishing Blood with foods like dark leafy greens, beets, black sesame seeds, and moderate amounts of organ meats or eggs. Avoid spicy, fried, and overly dry foods that can further deplete fluids. Keep skin well-moisturized with gentle, fragrance-free products, and protect yourself from wind and drafts. Gentle movement like walking or tai chi helps Blood circulate without overexerting.

### Wind-Heat
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/wind-heat)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Red skin with burning sensation, Itching worse with wind or heat, Sore throat, Mild fever or aversion to wind
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Hot weather, Exposure to wind, Spicy food, Alcohol, Hot baths
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Cool compresses, Rest and calm, Avoiding wind, Cool drinks
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When Wind-Heat invades the body, it attacks the skin's surface, where the defensive Qi fights back. This battle generates heat and redness, and the wind nature makes the itch move and intensify with any breeze or warmth. The throat may feel sore as the heat rises, and the pulse becomes floating and rapid as the body tries to push the pathogen out.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red at the tip and edges with a thin yellow coating. Pulse: floating and rapid.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Wind-Heat is an exterior pattern where the pathogen is at the skin's surface. Heat and wind directly aggravate it, so cooling the skin and avoiding wind calm the defensive Qi battle. Spicy foods and alcohol generate internal heat that fuels the fire, while rest allows the body's Qi to focus on expelling the invader.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Avoid spicy, greasy, and hot-natured foods like chili, lamb, and alcohol, which add internal heat. Drink plenty of cool water and eat cooling foods such as cucumber, mung beans, and watermelon. Protect the skin from direct wind and hot sun; wear light, breathable clothing.

### Damp-Heat in Stomach and Spleen
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/damp-heat-in-stomach-and-spleen)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Skin redness with oozing or tiny blisters, Sensation of heaviness in the body or limbs, Sticky or bitter taste in the mouth, Bloating, nausea, or loose sticky stools, Itching worsens in humid weather or after rich food
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Greasy, fried, or sugary foods, Alcohol or rich dairy products, Hot, humid weather
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Cool, dry environment, Light, bland meals (e.g., congee, steamed vegetables), Moderate exercise to break a light sweat
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When the Spleen and Stomach become overloaded by rich, greasy foods or damp surroundings, they struggle to transform fluids. This creates a stagnant stew of Dampness and Heat in the digestive system. That Damp-Heat then rises and spreads outward to the skin, much like steam from a boiling pot, producing redness, oozing, and an intense urge to scratch.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red body, possibly swollen with teeth marks, and a thick yellow greasy coating especially in the centre and root. Pulse: soggy (ru) and rapid (shu), often also slippery (hua).
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Damp-Heat in the Spleen and Stomach is essentially a digestive stagnation that steams upward to the skin. Rich, damp-producing foods and humid environments add fuel to the fire, while light, cooling foods and movement help the body drain the excess Dampness and clear the Heat. Cooling the skin and avoiding heat also directly calm the itch.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Avoid greasy, fried, and overly sweet foods, as well as dairy and alcohol, which all generate Dampness and Heat. Emphasize lightly cooked vegetables, whole grains like barley, and legumes like mung beans. Gentle, consistent exercise helps the Spleen move fluids and clear Dampness.

### Liver Qi Stagnation that transforms into Heat
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/liver-qi-stagnation-that-transforms-into-heat)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Itching triggered or worsened by emotional stress, Irritability and explosive anger, Bitter taste in the mouth, Distending or burning pain along the ribcage, Restlessness and difficulty falling asleep, Dark yellow urine
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Emotional stress or frustration, Anger or outbursts, Spicy, greasy, or fried foods, Alcohol, Lack of sleep
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Practicing stress reduction, Cooling foods like cucumber or watermelon, Gentle stretching or yoga, Chrysanthemum tea, A calm, quiet environment
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When stress, frustration, or repressed emotions are not expressed, the Liver's job of keeping Qi flowing smoothly is disrupted. This stuck Qi eventually generates Heat, which rises and spreads outward to the skin. The result is an itching sensation that flares up or intensifies with emotional upset, often without a visible rash.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red body with redder sides, yellow coating. Pulse: wiry and rapid (弦数 xian shu), especially prominent at the left middle (Liver) position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Stress and anger cause Liver Qi to stagnate and generate Heat, which then flares up to the skin. Cooling foods and relaxation techniques help clear that Heat and smooth the flow of Qi, directly addressing the root of the itch. Activities that calm the mind also prevent the emotional spikes that trigger symptoms.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Avoid spicy, greasy, and fried foods, as well as alcohol, which can fuel internal Heat. Sip chrysanthemum tea to gently cool the Liver. Prioritize stress-reducing activities like deep breathing, gentle yoga, or walking outdoors to keep Qi flowing smoothly.

### Spleen Deficiency with Dampness
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/spleen-deficiency-with-dampness)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Skin is moist, weeping, or has tiny vesicles, Dull, persistent itch, worse in humid weather, Abdominal bloating and loose stools, Heaviness in the body and limbs, Fatigue and poor appetite
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Damp, humid weather, Greasy, fried, or heavy foods, Raw or cold foods and drinks, Sedentary lifestyle, Overeating
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warm, dry environment, Light, easily digestible meals, Gentle movement like walking, Rest and adequate sleep
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Your Spleen is like the body's drainage system for fluids. When it becomes weak and sluggish, fluids start to pool into internal dampness. That dampness can overflow and seep outward to the skin, creating a moist, weeping environment that triggers a dull, persistent itch.
Unlike dry, scaly itching, this pattern produces skin that may feel wet, sticky, or even blister. The itch often flares in humid weather or after eating heavy, greasy foods that further tax the Spleen.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Pale, swollen tongue with teeth marks and a white greasy coating; pulse soggy (Ru) and slow (Huan).
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: A weak Spleen cannot handle dampness, so damp environments and damp-producing foods (greasy, raw, cold) directly worsen the condition by adding more dampness to the body. Warmth and gentle movement help the Spleen transform and transport fluids, reducing the dampness that surfaces on the skin. Rest and light, cooked meals conserve Spleen Qi, allowing it to recover its draining function and calm the itching.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat warm, cooked foods like soups, stews, and congees. Avoid cold, raw, and greasy foods that produce dampness. Incorporate spleen-friendly foods such as cooked grains, ginger, and small amounts of lean protein. Gentle daily movement like walking helps move Qi and drain dampness.

### Heat in the Blood
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/heat-in-the-blood)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Bright red skin eruptions that bleed easily, Feeling of internal heat, worse at night, Thirst with desire for cold drinks, Restlessness and irritability, Burning sensation in the skin
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Spicy or greasy food, Alcohol, Stress and anger, Hot weather, Overwork
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Cool environment, Rest and relaxation, Cool drinks, Gentle cooling compress
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When Heat enters the Blood level, it agitates the blood and makes it reckless. This internal heat rises to the skin, causing intense itching with bright red eruptions that may bleed easily. The heat also consumes the body’s cooling fluids, leading to thirst and a feeling of internal warmth that worsens at night.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red to deep red body with red spots or prickles, thin yellow dry coating. Pulse: rapid (Shu) and wiry (Xian), possibly slippery (Hua), forceful.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Heat in the Blood is an internal excess heat pattern. Spicy food, alcohol, and emotional stress add more heat to the body, making the blood more agitated and the itch worse. Cooling foods, rest, and a calm environment help clear heat and allow the blood to settle, which relieves itching.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Avoid heating foods like spicy dishes, alcohol, and coffee. Eat cooling foods such as cucumber, watermelon, and mung beans. Keep calm and avoid overexertion, as emotional stress and physical strain can stir up internal heat.

## คำถามที่พบบ่อย (Frequently Asked Questions)
### Can acupuncture really stop itching?
Yes, acupuncture can be very effective for many types of itching. It works by calming the nervous system, reducing inflammation, and addressing the underlying pattern - whether that's clearing Heat, draining Dampness, or nourishing Blood. Points like Quchi (LI-11) and Xuehai (SP-10) are commonly used to cool the blood and relieve itch. Many patients feel a reduction in itching during or immediately after a session, and regular treatments can lead to lasting relief.

### How long does it take for Chinese herbs to work for itchy skin?
Acute itching from Wind-Heat often responds within a few days. Damp-Heat patterns may take 2-4 weeks as the digestive environment is cleared. Chronic, dry itching from Blood deficiency typically requires 4-8 weeks to rebuild blood and moisture, with gradual reduction in itch intensity. Stress-related Liver Qi stagnation can improve quickly once emotional triggers are managed, but full resolution may take several weeks. Your practitioner will adjust the formula as your pattern shifts.

### Can I use TCM alongside my steroid cream or antihistamines?
Yes, TCM can safely complement conventional treatments. You can continue using prescribed moisturizers and topical medications while taking Chinese herbs, but always inform both your TCM practitioner and dermatologist about all treatments. Some blood-moving herbs may interact with anticoagulants, so full disclosure is essential. Never stop prescribed medications abruptly without medical guidance.

### What foods should I avoid if I have itchy skin?
From a TCM perspective, spicy, greasy, and fried foods generate Heat and Dampness, which can worsen itching. Alcohol and caffeine can stir up Liver Wind, triggering stress-related flares. Sugar and dairy tend to create Dampness and should be limited if your itch is weepy or moist. Instead, focus on cooling, moistening foods like cucumber, pear, and leafy greens. For dry, deficient patterns, include blood-nourishing foods like dark leafy greens and beets.

### Does TCM work for itching without a rash?
Absolutely. In TCM, itching without visible skin changes often points to internal Wind - either from Blood deficiency or Liver Qi stagnation. This type of itching can be just as distressing as a rash, and TCM's internal approach is well-suited to addressing it. Acupuncture and herbal formulas that nourish Blood, soothe the Liver, or extinguish Wind can provide significant relief even when there's nothing to see on the skin.

### Is it safe to use Chinese herbs for itching during pregnancy?
Many Chinese herbs are safe during pregnancy when prescribed by a qualified practitioner, but some are contraindicated. Always inform your practitioner if you are pregnant or trying to conceive. They will select gentle, pregnancy-safe herbs and avoid those that move blood or strongly clear Heat. Acupuncture is also generally safe during pregnancy when performed by a trained professional. Never self-prescribe herbs during pregnancy.
