## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนปัจจุบัน (Conventional Medicine View)
### คำอธิบาย (Description)
Night terrors, also known as sleep terrors, are episodes of sudden, intense fear and agitation during non-REM sleep, usually in the first third of the night. The person may sit up, scream, thrash, or appear to be awake but is not fully conscious and typically has no memory of the event afterward. They are more common in children but can occur in adults, often linked to stress, sleep deprivation, or fever.
Diagnosis is based on clinical history and ruling out other sleep disorders like nightmares or seizures. While frightening to witness, most night terrors are not dangerous and children often outgrow them. However, when they persist or cause significant distress, further evaluation is warranted.

### การรักษาหลัก (Treatments)
Conventional management focuses on reassurance, safety measures (like removing sharp objects from the bedroom), and addressing triggers such as stress or sleep deprivation. In severe cases, especially when episodes cause injury or significant distress, medications like benzodiazepines or antidepressants may be prescribed, though they are not curative and carry side effects such as daytime drowsiness or dependency.

## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนจีน (Traditional Chinese Medicine View)
### ความเข้าใจเกี่ยวกับการแพทย์แผนจีน (TCM Understanding)
In TCM, night terrors are seen as a disturbance of the Shén (神), the mind or spirit that resides in the Heart. Healthy sleep requires the Shén to be calm and anchored, but when it is agitated by heat, phlegm, or deficiency, it can startle awake in panic during the night. The source of that agitation can come from many different organ systems.
The Heart is the most direct player: if Heart Fire blazes or if the Heart is not nourished by enough Blood, the Shén becomes ungrounded. But the Liver often stirs the trouble. When stress and frustration cause Liver Qi to stagnate and then heat up, that fire can flare upward and shake the Shén. The Kidneys, which should cool and anchor the Heart, may fail to do so if their Yin is depleted, allowing empty heat to rise.
Even the Spleen and Stomach are involved: a weak Spleen can't make enough Blood to nourish the Heart, while undigested food in the Stomach generates turbid heat that rises to disturb the mind. Phlegm is another key culprit. When the body's fluids aren't moving properly, they can thicken into phlegm, which combines with heat to cloud the Heart's orifice. This produces a particularly agitated, confused type of terror.
Because so many different imbalances can lead to the same symptom, a person with night terrors might need a completely different treatment than another person with the same diagnosis - one might need to clear phlegm-heat, another to nourish Heart Blood, and another to calm Liver fire.

### การวินิจฉัยโดยแพทย์แผนจีน (Practitioner Diagnosis)
A TCM practitioner begins by asking about the nature of the night terror-what happens during the episode, how often it occurs, and what the person feels upon waking. The accompanying daytime signs, such as mood, digestion, and energy levels, are equally important clues that point toward which pattern is at play.
When Kidney Yin is insufficient to anchor Heart fire, empty heat rises to disturb the Shen at night. People with this pattern often wake with palpitations, a dry mouth and throat, and a feeling of heat in the palms and soles. The tongue appears red with a scanty or absent coat, and the pulse is typically thin and rapid. This pattern is more common in older adults or those with chronic overwork.
Phlegm combined with heat creates a turbulent, clouding influence on the mind. Night terrors here are vivid and frightening, often with chest oppression, a bitter taste in the mouth, and copious phlegm. The tongue body is red with a thick, yellow, greasy coating, and the pulse feels slippery and rapid. Irritability and restlessness are prominent during the day.
Emotional stress-frustration, anger, or resentment-causes Liver Qi to stagnate and eventually generate heat that flares upward. The night terrors strike suddenly, often with shouting or crying out, and the person is prone to irritability, hypochondriac distension, and a feeling of a lump in the throat. The tongue may be red on the sides with a thin yellow coat, and the pulse is wiry and rapid.
Excess heat directly in the Heart agitates the Shen intensely. This pattern produces severe restlessness, a flushed face, mouth and tongue sores, and a strong desire for cold drinks. The night terror is often accompanied by a racing heart and a sensation of unbearable heat. The tongue is deep red with a dry yellow coat, and the pulse is full and rapid.
When the Spleen fails to produce enough Qi and blood, the Heart is left undernourished and the Shen becomes ungrounded. Night terrors in this pattern are more about being easily startled, with a lingering sense of fear after waking. Daytime signs include fatigue, poor appetite, pale complexion, and loose stools. The tongue is pale with a thin white coat, and the pulse is weak and thready.
Especially common in children, this pattern arises when undigested food sits in the stomach and generates heat that rises to disturb sleep. Night terrors occur alongside abdominal bloating, foul-smelling belching, acid reflux, and a thick, greasy tongue coating. The person may have a history of overeating or eating late at night.

### Disharmony between Heart and Kidneys
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/disharmony-between-heart-and-kidneys)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Frequent waking with panic, Night sweats, Soreness and weakness of the lower back and knees, Dry mouth and throat, Heat in the palms, soles, and chest, Vivid or disturbing dreams
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Overwork or late nights, Spicy, greasy, fried, or sweet foods, Stress, anger, or frustration, Alcohol, Hot or stuffy environment
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Cool, quiet sleeping environment, Yin-nourishing foods (pear, black sesame), Consistent sleep schedule, Meditation or deep breathing
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  The Kidneys store Yin essence, which cools and anchors the Heart spirit. When Kidney Yin is depleted, it can no longer nourish the Heart, allowing empty heat to flare upward. This internal heat disturbs the Shen (spirit), causing the mind to startle awake in panic during the night. You may wake with palpitations, a dry mouth, and a hot, restless feeling in the chest.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red body with a redder tip, thin shape, little or no coating, possible midline crack. Pulse: fine and rapid, weaker at the rear (Kidney) positions.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: This pattern is rooted in a deep Yin deficit, so anything that further consumes Yin or adds heat - like staying up late, eating spicy food, or intense stress - makes the empty fire blaze higher and startle the Shen at night. Cooling, moistening, and calming habits replenish Yin, gently pull the fire downward, and give the spirit a stable foundation to rest.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Go to bed before 11pm and protect your sleep routine - late nights drain Yin further. Eat cooling, moistening foods like pears, black beans, sesame, and cucumber; avoid spicy, greasy, or fried foods that add heat. Gentle evening walks, meditation, or listening to calming music help settle the mind before sleep. Reduce mental overwork and excessive screen time at night.

### Phlegm-Fire harassing the Heart
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/phlegm-fire-harassing-the-heart)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Intense, vivid nightmares with confusion and fear, Chest oppression and a sensation of phlegm in the throat, Bitter taste in the mouth, Flushed red face and red eyes, Gurgling or rattling sound in the throat, Mental restlessness and agitation
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Spicy, greasy, fried, or sweet foods, Heavy meals late at night, Hot or stuffy environment, Stress, anger, or frustration, Overwork or late nights
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Cooling foods and drinks, Light, easily digested meals, Cool, quiet sleeping environment, Gentle exercise (walking, yoga), Stress reduction and mental relaxation
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When the body's fluids are not moving properly, they can thicken into a turbid substance called Phlegm. If this Phlegm combines with excess Heat - often from a rich diet or emotional stress - it rises like steam to cloud the Heart, which houses the mind (Shen). The result is a restless, agitated state where the mind loses its anchor, leading to sudden, intense night terrors filled with confusion and fear. The chest feels heavy and oppressed because Phlegm blocks the free flow of Qi in the upper body.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red body, swollen with a red prickly tip, and a thick yellow greasy coating. Pulse: slippery and rapid, full and overflowing at the left Cun position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Spicy, greasy, and heavy foods directly create more Phlegm and Heat, the two culprits that cloud the Heart and trigger night terrors. A hot, stuffy room adds external Heat, while emotional stress stirs up internal Fire. Cooling foods and a calm environment help clear that Heat, and light meals prevent new Phlegm from forming. Gentle movement keeps Qi flowing so Phlegm does not stagnate, and rest allows the Heart to settle.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Avoid spicy, greasy, and sweet foods that generate Phlegm and Heat. Favor light, cooling choices like pear, cucumber, mung beans, and leafy greens. Eat your last meal at least two hours before bed and keep portions modest. A cool, dark bedroom and a calming pre-sleep routine - such as gentle stretching or meditation - help settle the mind.

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Explosive anger and irritability, especially at night, Distending or burning pain along the ribcage, Bitter taste in the mouth, Red face and eyes, Vivid, disturbing dreams or nightmares
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Stress, anger, or frustration, Spicy, greasy, fried, or sweet foods, Alcohol, Hot or stuffy environment
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Cooling herbal teas (chrysanthemum, mint), Gentle exercise (walking, yoga), Stress reduction and mental relaxation, Cool, quiet sleeping environment
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When stress, frustration, or unexpressed anger build up, Liver Qi stagnates like a blocked river. Over time that stuck energy heats up, becoming a smoldering fire that flares upward toward the head and heart. This heat agitates the Heart's spirit (Shen), which is meant to anchor sleep, leading to sudden, intense night terrors with explosive anger and vivid disturbing dreams.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red body, especially redder along the sides, with a yellow coating. Pulse: wiry and rapid (弦数 xián shuò), forceful at the left middle position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Emotional stress and anger directly stagnate Liver Qi, which then turns into heat that rises to disturb the Heart's Shen during sleep - the very mechanism of this pattern. Cooling foods and herbs help clear that heat, while gentle movement and emotional release keep Qi flowing smoothly, preventing the buildup that triggers night terrors.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Keep the Liver cool and calm by avoiding alcohol, spicy foods, and fried dishes. Sip chrysanthemum or mint tea in the evening. Gentle daily movement like walking or stretching helps Qi flow. Find a healthy outlet for frustration - journaling, talking to a friend, or breathing exercises - so heat doesn't build up.

### Heart Fire blazing
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/heart-fire-blazing)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Sudden night terrors with a feeling of intense heat, Mouth or tongue ulcers, Red flushed face, Thirst with desire for cold drinks, Bitter taste in the mouth, Dark yellow urine, Irritability and anxiety
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Spicy, greasy, fried, or sweet foods, Alcohol, Stress, anger, or frustration, Hot or stuffy environment
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Cooling foods and drinks, Quiet dark room, Lukewarm bath before bed
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When Heart Fire blazes, excess heat directly agitates the Shen (spirit), causing severe restlessness and sudden, terrifying awakenings. The heat rises to the head, giving a flushed face and bitter taste, and can produce painful mouth ulcers. The mind becomes unsettled, leading to vivid nightmares and night terrors that feel intensely hot.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: Red body with a distinctly redder tip and swollen red points, yellow dry coating. Pulse: Rapid, forceful, and overflowing (Hong) especially at the left cun position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Heart Fire is an excess heat condition. Spicy foods, alcohol, and emotional stress add more heat, fanning the flames that disturb the spirit. Cooling foods and drinks, along with calming activities, reduce the internal heat and help the Shen settle, preventing the terrifying awakenings.

### Heart and Spleen Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/heart-and-spleen-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Night terrors with anxiety and being easily startled, Fatigue and physical exhaustion, Poor appetite, bloating, or loose stools, Sallow or pale complexion, Palpitations or awareness of heartbeat
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Excessive worrying and overthinking, Irregular eating or skipping meals, Overwork or late nights, Cold or raw foods
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warm, easily digestible meals, Gentle exercise (walking, yoga), Consistent sleep schedule, Stress reduction and mental relaxation
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  The Spleen is responsible for transforming food into Qi and Blood. When the Spleen is weak, it cannot produce enough Blood to nourish the Heart. At night, the Heart's spirit (Shen) lacks a stable anchor and becomes ungrounded, easily startled, resulting in night terrors. This pattern often develops after prolonged worry, mental strain, or irregular eating, which gradually deplete the body's resources.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale, tender body with teeth marks, thin white coating. Pulse: fine and weak, especially weak at the left cun (Heart) and right guan (Spleen) positions.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Worry and mental strain directly knot the Spleen Qi, while irregular eating and cold foods weaken its digestive fire, both reducing Blood production. Without enough Blood to house the Heart's spirit, the Shen becomes unmoored and easily startled at night. Rest, nourishing warm meals, and gentle movement rebuild Spleen Qi and Blood, giving the spirit a secure anchor so sleep can be deep and peaceful.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat regular, warm, cooked meals to support the Spleen. Include blood-nourishing foods like red dates, dark leafy greens, and moderate amounts of high-quality protein. Gentle daily walks help Qi circulate without depleting energy. Prioritize a consistent sleep schedule and avoid mental overwork, especially in the evening.

### Food Stagnation in the Stomach
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/food-stagnation-in-the-stomach)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Upper abdominal bloating and distending pain, worse with pressure, Sour, rotten-smelling belching and acid regurgitation, Foul-smelling flatulence and loose stools with a sour smell, Thick, greasy, curd-like tongue coating, Night terrors that occur with a feeling of epigastric fullness
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Overeating, Heavy meals late at night, Spicy, greasy, fried, or sweet foods, Lying down immediately after eating, Cold or raw foods
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Light, easily digested meals, Skipping a meal or fasting, Gentle abdominal massage, Warm water
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When the Stomach is overloaded with rich or heavy food, it cannot process everything properly. The undigested food sits and ferments, generating turbid heat that has nowhere to go but upward. This heat rises along the Stomach channel and disturbs the Heart and Shen, leading to sudden night terrors. Children are especially prone because their digestive systems are still maturing, and even a single heavy meal before bed can trigger an episode.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: normal body with a thick, greasy, curd-like coating, thickest in the centre. Pulse: slippery (滑 huá) and full, especially at the right middle position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: <p>Overeating and heavy, greasy foods overwhelm the Stomach, creating a backlog of undigested food that ferments into turbid heat. This heat rises to disturb the Heart and Shen, triggering night terrors. Eating lightly and avoiding food close to bedtime allows the Stomach to process food fully before sleep, preventing the buildup of pathogenic heat. Gentle abdominal massage helps move Qi downward and aids digestion.</p>
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat light, warm meals and avoid heavy, greasy, or sweet foods, especially in the evening. Stop eating at least 2-3 hours before bed. A short walk after dinner can help digestion. For children, limit snacks before sleep and ensure they don't overeat at dinner.

## คำถามที่พบบ่อย (Frequently Asked Questions)
### Can acupuncture and herbs really stop night terrors?
Yes, by addressing the root cause rather than just suppressing the symptom. When the underlying pattern - whether excess heat, phlegm, or deficiency - is corrected, the Shén settles and episodes naturally diminish. Many people see a significant reduction in frequency and intensity, and some experience complete resolution.

### How long does TCM treatment take for night terrors?
It varies by pattern. Excess patterns like Liver Fire or Phlegm-Heat often show improvement within 2-6 weeks. Deficiency patterns, such as Heart and Spleen Deficiency or Kidney Yin Deficiency, may take 2-4 months to rebuild the body's reserves. Most people notice a positive change within the first month of consistent treatment.

### Is TCM safe for children with night terrors?
Yes, TCM is widely used for children. Herbal formulas are typically given in lower doses and are gentle. Acupuncture is often replaced with non-needle techniques like pediatric massage (tuina) or acupressure on points like Shenmen HT-7 and Neiguan PC-6. Many children improve quickly, especially with dietary adjustments.

### Will I need to change my diet?
Dietary changes support treatment but are not usually drastic. In general, avoid heavy, greasy, or spicy foods in the evening, as they generate phlegm and heat that can disturb sleep. A light dinner eaten 2-3 hours before bed is ideal. Your practitioner may recommend specific foods based on your pattern - cooling foods for heat patterns or warm, nourishing foods for deficiency.

### Can I combine TCM with my current medications?
In most cases, yes. However, some calming herbs like Suan Zao Ren can have mild sedative effects. If you are taking benzodiazepines or other sedatives, inform both your doctor and TCM practitioner to avoid excessive drowsiness. Always bring a complete list of your medications to your TCM consultation.

### What if I don't remember my night terrors?
That's very common and doesn't hinder TCM diagnosis. Your practitioner will rely on daytime signs - mood, energy, digestion, tongue appearance, and pulse quality - to identify the underlying pattern. A partner or family member's description of the episodes can also be helpful, but it's not essential.
