## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนปัจจุบัน (Conventional Medicine View)
### คำอธิบาย (Description)
Urinary retention is the inability to empty the bladder completely. It can be acute - a sudden, painful inability to urinate - or chronic, where you can urinate but never fully empty, leading to frequent urges, dribbling, and discomfort. Common causes include an enlarged prostate in men, urethral strictures, nerve damage from diabetes or spinal injury, certain medications, or post-surgical effects. Diagnosis often involves a physical exam, ultrasound to measure residual urine, and sometimes urodynamic testing.

### การรักษาหลัก (Treatments)
Acute retention is a medical emergency treated with catheterization to drain the bladder. For chronic retention, treatment targets the underlying cause: alpha-blockers to relax the prostate and bladder neck, 5-alpha reductase inhibitors to shrink the prostate over time, antibiotics if infection is present, or surgery to remove obstructions. Intermittent self-catheterization may be taught for long-term management.

## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนจีน (Traditional Chinese Medicine View)
### ความเข้าใจเกี่ยวกับการแพทย์แผนจีน (TCM Understanding)
The Bladder in TCM is not just a storage bag; it's a dynamic organ that relies on Qi transformation (气化) to convert fluids into urine and push it out. This process depends on the Kidneys' Yang energy (the fire under the pot), the Spleen's lifting function, the Liver's free flow, and the Lungs' descending action. When any of these fail, retention occurs.
Damp-Heat can physically clog the lower burner, creating a burning urgency with scanty, dark urine. Liver Qi stagnation knots up the energy in the abdomen, often triggered by stress, causing sudden blockage with distension. Kidney Yang deficiency leaves the bladder without the warmth to open, leading to a slow, dribbling, cold pattern. Lung Heat disrupts the descending command, while Spleen Qi sinking fails to lift and propel urine. Blood stasis after injury or surgery creates a physical roadblock.
That's why the same Western diagnosis can have multiple TCM patterns - the root cause might be an excess of dampness, a deficiency of warmth, or an emotional knot. A TCM practitioner differentiates by looking at urine color, sensation, accompanying symptoms, tongue, and pulse. Treatment then targets that root, not just the symptom: clearing Damp-Heat with Ba Zheng San, soothing the Liver with Chen Xiang, or warming the Kidneys with Ji Sheng Shen Qi Wan. This approach not only relieves the immediate retention but also reduces recurrence.

### การวินิจฉัยโดยแพทย์แผนจีน (Practitioner Diagnosis)
A TCM practitioner begins by looking at the quality of the urine and what brings the blockage on. The color, sensation, and timing of symptoms are the first clues. A sudden, complete inability to urinate with a burning feeling points one way, while a slow, dribbling onset with cold limbs points another.
If the urine is scanty, deep yellow, and painfully hot, with a sensation of heaviness in the lower abdomen, the practitioner suspects Damp-Heat in the Bladder. The tongue will appear red with a thick, greasy yellow coating, and the pulse feels slippery and rapid. This pattern often flares after rich, spicy food or in hot, humid weather.
When the blockage comes on suddenly after a period of emotional stress, and is accompanied by a distended feeling in the ribs and lower belly, Liver Qi Stagnation is the likely culprit. The tongue coating is usually thin and white, while the pulse takes on a tight, wiry quality. The key here is the link to frustration or anger.
For an older person or someone with a long-standing weakness, a gradual loss of urinary strength that worsens over time points to Kidney Yang Deficiency. The urine may dribble and the person feels cold, especially in the lower back and knees. The tongue looks pale and swollen, and the pulse is deep and weak, reflecting the body's fading inner fire.
A history of stones, trauma, or chronic prostate issues can lead to Blood Stagnation, where the stream is thin, hesitant, or stops and starts. The pain is fixed and stabbing. The tongue may show dark purple spots or a dusky body, and the pulse feels choppy. This pattern points to a physical blockage in the passages.
If urinary difficulty appears alongside a cough, dry throat, and a sensation of heat in the chest, the problem may originate in the Lungs. Lung Heat obstructs the body's ability to send fluids downward. The tongue tip is red with a thin yellow coating, and the pulse is rapid, especially at the front position.
When a dragging, bearing-down sensation in the lower abdomen is the main complaint, along with fatigue and shortness of breath, Spleen Qi Sinking is considered. The urine is hard to pass but not necessarily burning. The tongue is pale, and the pulse is thin and weak. This pattern reflects a lack of lifting power rather than a blockage.

### Damp-Heat in the Bladder
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/damp-heat-in-the-bladder)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Burning or stinging pain during urination, Dark yellow or cloudy urine, Lower abdominal fullness and pain, Frequent, urgent urination with scanty output, Thirst with little desire to drink
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Spicy, greasy, or fried food, Alcohol, Hot, humid weather, Prolonged sitting or inactivity, Stress
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Cool environment or air, Drinking plenty of water, Light, bland foods, Gentle movement or walking, Rest or lying down
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Damp-Heat is a heavy, sticky combination of moisture and heat that sinks down into the Lower Burner. When it lodges in the Bladder, it obstructs the Bladder's Qi transformation - the very mechanism that opens the water passages and lets urine flow. The result is an urgent, burning need to urinate with very little output, and in severe cases, complete blockage.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red body with a yellow, greasy coating thickest at the root. Pulse: slippery (滑 huá) and rapid (数 shuò), possibly wiry if pain is severe.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Damp-Heat is a sticky, hot obstruction. Spicy, greasy foods and alcohol directly feed that heat and dampness, making the blockage worse. A cool environment and bland, water-rich foods help drain the dampness and cool the heat, allowing the Bladder's Qi to open the water passages again. Gentle movement keeps Qi flowing and prevents stagnation, while rest conserves energy for healing.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Avoid spicy, greasy, and fried foods, as well as alcohol and coffee, all of which generate Damp-Heat. Drink plenty of warm water to help flush the urinary tract. Eat light, cooling foods like cucumber, celery, and watermelon. Maintain good genital hygiene and avoid sitting for long periods without moving.

### Liver Qi Stagnation
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/liver-qi-stagnation)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Sudden urinary blockage after emotional upset, Distension or bloating in the lower abdomen and rib-sides, Frequent sighing, Irritability, mood swings, or depression
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Anger or frustration, Stress, Alcohol and spicy food, Prolonged sitting or inactivity
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Relaxation and stress relief, Gentle movement or walking, Warmth on lower abdomen, Talking through frustrations
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  The Liver is responsible for the smooth, free flow of Qi throughout the body. When emotional stress, frustration, or anger build up, the Liver Qi becomes knotted and stagnant. This trapped Qi disrupts the flow of energy in the lower abdomen, directly interfering with the Bladder's ability to open and release urine. The result is a sudden, often dramatic inability to urinate, accompanied by a feeling of pressure and distension.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: normal or slightly dark body, red or slightly purple sides, thin white coat. Pulse: wiry (弦 xián), taut like a guitar string.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Anger and emotional stress are the primary culprits that tighten and stagnate Liver Qi, which then blocks the Bladder's opening mechanism. Activities that calm the mind and relax the body help unknot this stagnation, restoring the smooth downward flow that allows urine to pass. Warmth relaxes the lower abdominal muscles and encourages Qi movement, while spicy and intoxicating substances can flare up Liver heat and worsen the stagnation.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Prioritize emotional well-being: gentle daily movement like walking or tai chi helps keep Liver Qi flowing. Avoid alcohol, coffee, and spicy, greasy foods, which can further heat and agitate the Liver. Sip on calming peppermint or chrysanthemum tea. Practice deep breathing or meditation to release mental tension before it physically knots up the Qi.

### Kidney Yang Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/kidney-yang-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Cold sensation in lower back and knees, Clear, dribbling urine, Worse in cold weather or at night, Fatigue and low spirits, Soreness and weakness in lower back, Dribbling after urination
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Cold weather or cold drinks, Cold foods and drinks, Overwork and fatigue, Prolonged standing or overexertion, Stress
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warmth on the lower back, Rest or lying down, Warm, cooked foods, Gentle movement or walking
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  The Kidneys are the source of the body's Yang energy, which provides the warmth needed to transform fluids into urine and open the Bladder. When Kidney Yang is deficient, this transformative fire is too weak, so water accumulates and the Bladder cannot expel it properly. The result is a gradual, chronic difficulty urinating, with a weak stream, dribbling, or complete retention, often worse in cold weather or at night.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale, puffy, tender body with teeth marks and a wet white coating. Pulse: deep, slow, and weak, especially at the Chi (third) position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Cold depletes Yang and causes the Bladder to contract, making urination even harder. Warmth and rest conserve the body's limited Yang, allowing it to focus on fluid transformation. Overwork and stress drain Kidney Qi, while gentle movement stimulates circulation without exhausting the reserves.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat warming, cooked foods like lamb, ginger, walnuts, and cinnamon. Avoid raw, cold, and icy foods and drinks. Keep the lower back and feet warm with clothing or a hot water bottle. Gentle movement like tai chi or walking helps circulate Yang without depleting it. Avoid excessive fluid intake late in the evening to reduce nighttime burden.

### Blood Stagnation
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/blood-stagnation)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Urine stream thin as a thread or completely blocked, Fixed, stabbing pain in the lower abdomen, Pain worse at night or with pressure, Purplish spots on the tongue or dark, distended veins under it
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Cold weather or cold drinks, Prolonged sitting or inactivity, Stress, Trauma or injury to the area, Heavy, greasy, or cold foods
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warmth on lower abdomen, Gentle movement or walking, Gentle abdominal massage, Warm, cooked foods
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Blood Stagnation in the lower abdomen acts like a physical roadblock in the urinary tract. When blood flow becomes sluggish or congealed - often after an injury, surgery, or from long-standing stones - it can narrow or completely obstruct the passage that carries urine out of the bladder. This leaves you straining to pass a thin, hesitant stream, or unable to urinate at all, with a sharp, fixed pain that gets worse at night when circulation naturally slows.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: purple or dusky body with stasis spots, distended dark sublingual veins, and a thin white coat. Pulse: choppy (涩 Sè) or wiry (弦 Xián).
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Cold congeals and slows blood, so cold weather, icy drinks, and raw foods all worsen the stasis that blocks urine. Inactivity and emotional stress stall Qi, which fails to push blood forward, allowing the blockage to harden. Warmth, gentle movement, and abdominal massage all encourage Qi and blood to flow again - like thawing a frozen pipe - which helps dissolve the stasis and reopen the urinary tract.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Keep the lower abdomen warm and avoid sitting for long stretches - gentle walking helps blood flow. Eat cooked, warming foods like hawthorn, turmeric, black fungus, and a little onion or garlic, while avoiding icy drinks, raw salads, and heavy greasy meals that can congeal blood.

### Lung Heat
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/lung-heat)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Sudden inability to urinate, Cough with thick yellow phlegm, Fever or feeling of body heat, Thirst with desire for cold drinks, Sore throat with redness and swelling
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Spicy, greasy, or fried food, Dry, hot weather, Smoking or smoky air, Overwork and fatigue
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Cool environment or air, Drinking plenty of water, Cooling foods like pears, Rest or lying down
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  The Lungs sit like a lid above the rest of the organs and are responsible for sending Qi downward and regulating the body's water passages. When Heat becomes trapped in the Lungs, it disrupts this descending function - the lid no longer opens properly. As a result, the Bladder loses the signal to release urine, leading to sudden urinary blockage even though the urge may be present.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red body, yellow dry coating, possibly prickly papillae on the front third. Pulse: rapid (shu), may be slippery (hua) or overflowing (hong) at the right cun position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Lung Heat is a pattern of excess Heat trapped in the Upper Burner. Heat and dryness - from food, weather, or smoke - add fuel to the fire, further obstructing the Lung's ability to descend and regulate the water passages. Cooling, moistening foods and humid air help clear that Heat and restore the Lung's natural downward movement, which in turn signals the Bladder to open.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Focus on cooling, moistening foods like pear, radish, cucumber, and plenty of room-temperature water. Avoid spicy, fried, or greasy foods that add Heat. Keep indoor air humidified and avoid smoking or dry, dusty environments. Gentle breathing exercises can help restore the Lung's descending rhythm.

### Spleen Qi Sinking
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/spleen-qi-sinking)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Bearing-down sensation in the lower abdomen, Weak urine stream with a feeling of incomplete emptying, Chronic fatigue and shortness of breath, Poor appetite and bloating after eating, Dizziness or lightheadedness
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Prolonged standing or overexertion, Heavy, greasy, or cold foods, Stress, Skipping meals
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Rest or lying down, Warm, cooked foods, Gentle abdominal massage, Relaxation and stress relief
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Spleen Qi is responsible for holding organs in place and raising clear Yang. When it becomes deficient and sinks, the lifting function fails, causing a bearing-down sensation in the lower abdomen and sometimes prolapse. This same sinking Qi cannot propel urine out of the bladder, leading to a weak, hesitant stream or retention. The overall Qi deficiency also produces profound fatigue, shortness of breath, and poor appetite.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale, puffy, and tender with teeth marks and a thin white coating. Pulse: weak, especially at the right Guan (middle) position, and may be deep.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Rest and lying down allow the sinking Qi to settle back into the middle, while standing and overexertion pull it further downward. Warm, cooked foods nourish the Spleen without draining its energy, whereas cold and greasy foods create Dampness and weaken digestion. Gentle upward massage and deep breathing help to raise the Qi and support its lifting function.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat small, frequent, warm meals to support Spleen Qi-cooked grains, root vegetables, and moderate lean protein. Avoid raw, cold, and greasy foods that tax the Spleen. Gentle walking or tai chi can help lift Qi, but stop before exhaustion. Rest when tired, and practice deep breathing to strengthen the middle Qi.

## คำถามที่พบบ่อย (Frequently Asked Questions)
### Can acupuncture help me urinate right away?
Acupuncture can sometimes trigger urination during a session by stimulating the bladder reflex, but it's not a guaranteed immediate fix. It works best over a series of treatments to address the root cause. For acute, complete retention, seek emergency catheterization first - TCM can be used afterward to prevent recurrence.

### Is it safe to use Chinese herbs while I'm using a catheter?
Yes, it is generally safe, and herbs may support bladder function and reduce infection risk. However, always inform both your TCM practitioner and your doctor. Never attempt to remove the catheter without medical guidance, even if you feel improvement.

### How long does TCM take to work for urinary retention?
Many patients notice easier urination within a few days to two weeks. Acute patterns like Damp-Heat tend to respond faster, while chronic, deficiency-based patterns may take 4-6 weeks for clear change. Consistency with herbs and acupuncture sessions is key.

### Will I need to change my diet?
Yes, dietary adjustments support treatment. Generally, avoid alcohol, caffeine, spicy, and greasy foods which can irritate the bladder. Favor warm, cooked meals and stay hydrated with warm water. Your practitioner will give specific advice based on your pattern.

### Can TCM help if I have an enlarged prostate?
Yes, TCM often treats urinary retention due to benign prostatic hyperplasia (BPH) by addressing underlying patterns like Damp-Heat or Kidney Yang Deficiency. It may reduce urinary frequency, improve stream, and lessen the sensation of incomplete emptying over several weeks.

### What if I can't urinate at all and I'm in pain?
This is a medical emergency. Go to the ER immediately for catheterization to prevent bladder damage. TCM can be safely integrated later for long-term management, but acute complete retention must be relieved urgently by conventional means.
