## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนปัจจุบัน (Conventional Medicine View)
### คำอธิบาย (Description)
Chronic cough is defined as a cough that persists for more than eight weeks in adults or four weeks in children. Common causes include postnasal drip from allergies or sinusitis, asthma (especially cough-variant asthma), gastroesophageal reflux disease (GERD), chronic bronchitis, and certain medications like ACE inhibitors.
Diagnosis typically involves a physical exam, chest X-ray, and sometimes lung function tests or a CT scan. In many cases, the cause is multifactorial, and conventional treatment focuses on managing the underlying condition or suppressing the cough reflex.

### การรักษาหลัก (Treatments)
Depending on the suspected cause, conventional treatment may include antihistamines and nasal sprays for postnasal drip, inhaled corticosteroids and bronchodilators for asthma, acid-suppressing medications for GERD, or antibiotics if a bacterial infection is present. Over-the-counter cough suppressants containing dextromethorphan or prescription codeine are sometimes used for symptom relief, though their effectiveness for chronic cough is debated. Speech therapy and breathing exercises may also be recommended for habit cough or laryngeal hypersensitivity.

## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนจีน (Traditional Chinese Medicine View)
### ความเข้าใจเกี่ยวกับการแพทย์แผนจีน (TCM Understanding)
In TCM, the Lungs are responsible for descending Qi and governing respiration. A cough is seen as rebellious Lung Qi - energy that should be moving downward is instead forced upward. But what causes this rebellion varies widely. The Lungs are a 'tender organ,' easily affected by external pathogens like Wind, Cold, or Heat, as well as internal imbalances from other systems like the Spleen, Liver, or Kidneys.
When the Lung's Qi is deficient, the cough is weak and breathless, often with thin clear sputum. When Lung Yin is depleted, the airways lose their moisture, causing a dry, hacking cough worse at night. Both deficiency patterns leave the Lung unable to perform its descending function properly.
Excess patterns involve something that shouldn't be there. Damp-Phlegm or Phlegm-Heat accumulates when the Spleen fails to transform fluids, creating a productive cough with sticky white or yellow sputum. Lingering Wind-Cold or Wind-Heat from an unresolved infection can also keep the cough going, often with chills or a scratchy throat. In every case, the cough is the body's attempt to expel what's obstructing the Lung.
This is why TCM diagnosis pays such close attention to the sound of the cough, the color and texture of sputum, the time of day it worsens, and accompanying signs like thirst or fatigue. A red, peeled tongue tells a very different story from a pale, puffy one with a greasy coating - and each story leads to a completely different herbal formula and acupuncture strategy.

### การวินิจฉัยโดยแพทย์แผนจีน (Practitioner Diagnosis)
A TCM practitioner begins by listening to the sound of the cough and asking about what you bring up. The texture, color, and amount of sputum, along with how the cough feels in your chest, are the first clues that separate the main patterns.
If the cough is weak and breathless with thin white sputum, and you tire easily, the picture points to Lung Qi Deficiency. The tongue is pale and the pulse is weak, confirming that the Lung’s energy is too low to clear the airways properly.
When the cough is persistently dry with only a little sticky phlegm, a dry throat, and night sweats, Lung Yin Deficiency is more likely. A red tongue with little coating and a thin rapid pulse show that dryness and heat have damaged the Lung’s moisture.
A cough that brings up lots of white, easy-to-spit sputum, along with chest tightness and a heavy feeling, suggests Damp-Phlegm in the Lungs. The tongue is often puffy with a greasy white coat, and the pulse feels slippery, reflecting fluids that have turned to phlegm.
If the sputum is yellow and thick, and you feel hot or oppressed in the chest, it points to Phlegm-Heat. The tongue is red with a yellow greasy coat and the pulse is rapid and slippery, signaling that phlegm has combined with heat and is clogging the Lung.
A lingering cough with thin white sputum, chills, and a floating tight pulse suggests that an old Wind-Cold invasion was never fully expelled. The tongue coating remains thin and white, and the cough often worsens in cold weather.
When the cough is more forceful, with thick yellow sputum, sore throat, and thirst, a persistent Wind-Heat pattern is at play. The tongue tip is red with a thin yellow coat, and the pulse is floating and rapid, showing that heat still lingers in the Lung.

### Lung Qi Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/lung-qi-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Weak, low-force cough that worsens with exertion, Thin, clear, watery phlegm, Spontaneous daytime sweating and sensitivity to wind, Fatigue and catching colds easily
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Overexertion and fatigue, Cold, raw foods and drinks, Exposure to wind, cold, or drafts, Emotional stress, Excessive talking
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warm congee and soups, Gentle exercise, Rest and avoiding overexertion, Warmth and covering up
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  The Lungs are responsible for descending Qi and spreading defensive energy across the body’s surface. When Lung Qi becomes depleted from prolonged illness or constitutional weakness, it can no longer hold Qi down firmly, so Qi rebels upward as a weak, lingering cough. The deficiency also leaves the defensive layer wide open, making you prone to repeated colds that keep the cough going.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale, tender body with teeth marks, thin white coating. Pulse: empty (虚 xū) and weak, especially in the right Cun (Lung) position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Lung Qi deficiency means the body lacks the strength to descend Qi and defend against external invasions. Overexertion and stress further drain Qi, while cold and raw foods weaken the Spleen’s ability to produce the Qi that supports the Lungs. Wind and cold easily penetrate the weak defensive layer, triggering more coughing. Rest, warm foods, and gentle movement conserve and slowly rebuild Qi, while a warm environment shields the body from the external factors that provoke the cough.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat warm, cooked foods like congee, soups, and stews that are easy to digest and gently nourish Qi. Avoid raw, cold, and dairy-heavy foods that tax the Spleen and create phlegm. Gentle daily movement such as walking or Tai Chi builds Qi without exhausting it, while overexertion should be avoided. Keep the neck and chest covered in windy weather and get plenty of rest to allow the Lungs to recover.

### Lung Yin Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/lung-yin-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Dry cough with scant sticky phlegm, Dry, scratchy throat and mouth, Night sweats, Afternoon low-grade fever or feeling of heat, Hoarse or weak voice
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Dry, heated indoor air, Spicy, fried, or greasy foods, Alcohol and smoking, Late nights and overwork, Emotional stress
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Cool, well-ventilated environment, Drinking warm water or pear juice, Rest and avoiding overexertion, Avoiding spicy and fried foods
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  The Lungs depend on Yin to stay moist and cool. When Lung Yin runs low - often after a long illness, years of overwork, or persistent dry heat - the delicate lining of the airways loses its lubrication. This dryness creates a constant tickle and an unproductive cough that is worse when the body is already dry, such as at night or in heated rooms.
Without enough Yin, the Lungs also generate a low-grade Empty Heat that rises in the afternoon and evening, stealing fluids and making the cough more persistent and the throat more scratchy.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red, thin body with cracks, little or no coating, dry surface. Pulse: fine (thin) and rapid, especially weak at the right cun (Lung) position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Yin is the body's cooling, moistening resource. Dry air, spicy heat, and stimulants like alcohol and tobacco consume Yin directly, leaving the Lungs even drier and the cough worse. Late nights and overwork deplete Yin at a deep level, so rest is essential. Cool, moist air and gentle fluids replenish Yin and soothe the irritated airways, while avoiding heat helps the body hold onto its precious moisture.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Focus on moist, cooling foods like pear, white fungus soup, lily bulb, honey, and congee. Sip warm water throughout the day. Avoid spicy, fried, and barbecued foods, as well as alcohol and smoking. Go to bed early - staying up late burns Yin. A humidifier in the bedroom can ease nighttime coughing.

### Damp-Phlegm in the Lungs
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/damp-phlegm-in-the-lungs)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Copious white, frothy or sticky phlegm that is easy to expectorate, Heaviness and oppression in the chest, Poor appetite and abdominal bloating, Loose stools, Fatigue and a sensation of heavy limbs
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Damp or humid weather, Dairy and sweet foods, Cold, raw foods and drinks, Overeating or heavy meals, Spicy, fried, or greasy foods, Sedentary lifestyle
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warm, dry weather, Light, easily digestible meals, Warm drinks like ginger tea, Gentle exercise, Rest and avoiding overexertion
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When the Spleen is weak, it cannot properly process fluids, leading to an accumulation of dampness in the body. This dampness congeals into phlegm, which settles in the Lungs and obstructs the flow of Qi. The result is a persistent, productive cough with abundant white phlegm that is relatively easy to bring up, along with a feeling of heaviness and congestion in the chest.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale swollen body with teeth marks, thick white greasy coating. Pulse: slippery (滑, huá), like beads rolling beneath the fingers.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Dampness and phlegm are heavy and sticky; they are worsened by damp environments and foods that burden the Spleen, like dairy and cold raw items. Warm, dry weather and light meals reduce the damp load, while movement activates the Spleen's ability to transform fluids. Ginger warms the middle and dries dampness, helping to resolve phlegm at its source.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat warm, cooked foods like soups and stews. Avoid dairy, cold raw foods, and greasy heavy meals that create dampness. Gentle daily movement like walking helps the Spleen transform fluids and keeps Qi moving.

### Phlegm-Heat in the Lungs
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/phlegm-heat-in-the-lungs)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Thick yellow or green sputum that is sticky and hard to expectorate, Chest oppression and feeling of fullness, Fever or sensation of body heat, thirst, Restlessness and irritability
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Spicy, fried, or greasy foods, Dairy and sweet foods, Smoky or polluted air, Damp or humid weather, Emotional stress, Overexertion and fatigue
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Cooling foods and drinks, Gentle breathing exercises, Cool, well-ventilated environment, Rest and avoiding overexertion
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When the body fails to transform fluids properly, they accumulate into phlegm. Over time this phlegm can stagnate and generate heat, creating a sticky, yellow phlegm that clogs the Lungs. The heat and phlegm together irritate the airways, forcing the Lungs to rebel upward - which is what produces the chronic, productive cough with thick yellow sputum.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red body, possibly swollen at the front, with a yellow greasy coating; red prickles may appear on the front third. Pulse: slippery and rapid (hua shu).
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: <p>Greasy, fried, and dairy foods are heavy and damp, directly contributing to phlegm formation. Spicy and heating foods add heat, worsening the phlegm-heat accumulation. Smoking and polluted air further irritate the Lungs and add heat. Emotional stress can cause Qi stagnation which generates heat, while overwork depletes Qi, making it harder for the body to clear phlegm.</p><p>Cooling, moistening foods help clear heat and resolve phlegm without damaging Yin. Gentle movement and breathing exercises promote the descent of Lung Qi and the expulsion of phlegm, while rest allows the body to recover its strength to transform fluids properly.</p>
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Avoid greasy, fried, spicy, and dairy foods that create phlegm and heat. Eat cooling, moistening foods such as pears, loquats, radish, and water chestnuts. Stay hydrated and avoid smoking or smoky environments. Gentle breathing exercises and light outdoor walks help Lung Qi descend.

### Wind-Cold invading the Lungs
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/wind-cold-invading-the-lungs)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Cough with thin white watery phlegm, Chills and aversion to cold, Nasal congestion with clear watery discharge, Itchy throat, Absence of sweating
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Exposure to wind, cold, or drafts, Cold, raw foods and drinks, Overexertion and fatigue
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warmth and covering up, Warm drinks like ginger tea, Rest and avoiding overexertion, Steam inhalation
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When Wind-Cold invades the body, it obstructs the Lung's ability to diffuse and descend Qi. This causes Qi to rebel upwards, producing a persistent cough with thin white watery phlegm. Because the pathogen is still at the exterior, chills and aversion to cold accompany the cough, and the pulse remains floating and tight.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: normal or slightly pale body with a thin white moist coating. Pulse: floating (fu) and tight (jin), easily felt with light pressure.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Wind-Cold constrains the Lung Qi, causing it to rebel upward as a cough. Cold and drafts tighten the exterior, worsening the obstruction, while warmth helps release the exterior and restore the Lung's descending function. Rest conserves Qi to fight the pathogen, and steam moistens the airways and eases the cough reflex.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Keep warm, especially the neck and chest. Drink warm fluids like ginger tea with a little brown sugar. Avoid cold, raw, and greasy foods that can worsen phlegm. Steam inhalation with eucalyptus or peppermint can help open the airways.

### Wind-Heat invading the Lungs
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/wind-heat-invading-the-lungs)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Cough with thick yellow or sticky phlegm, Sore, scratchy throat, Thirst and dry mouth, Low-grade fever or feeling of heat, Nasal congestion with yellow mucus
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Spicy, fried, or greasy foods, Hot, dry weather, Exposure to wind, cold, or drafts, Smoky or polluted air, Overexertion or excessive talking
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Cooling foods and drinks, Rest in a cool, quiet room, Gentle hydration, Avoiding wind exposure
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When Wind-Heat invades the Lungs and is not fully cleared, the Heat lingers, drying up the Lung's fluids and creating thick yellow phlegm. This ongoing irritation keeps the cough going long after the initial cold has passed. The Heat also causes a sore throat and thirst, while the lingering exterior pathogen makes the cough worse with exposure to wind or heat.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red tip, thin yellow coating, body slightly red. Pulse: floating (Fu) and rapid (Shu).
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Wind-Heat is an external pathogen that brings heat and dryness to the Lungs. Spicy and fried foods add internal heat, making the cough worse, while cool, light foods and drinks help clear heat and moisten the Lungs. Rest supports the body's defensive Qi to expel the lingering pathogen, and avoiding wind prevents further invasion that could re-aggravate the cough.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat light, cooling foods such as pear, apple, and cucumber. Drink plenty of water and herbal teas like chrysanthemum or peppermint. Avoid spicy, fried, and greasy foods that generate heat. Keep the throat moist and avoid smoky or dry environments.

## คำถามที่พบบ่อย (Frequently Asked Questions)
### How does TCM diagnose the cause of a chronic cough?
A TCM practitioner listens carefully to the sound of your cough - whether it is weak, barking, dry, or wet - and examines your sputum's color, thickness, and amount. They will also look at your tongue coating and feel your pulse. Questions about what triggers the cough, the time of day it is worst, and any accompanying symptoms like thirst, chills, or fatigue help pinpoint which organ systems are involved and whether the pattern is one of deficiency or excess.

### Can acupuncture help a cough that has lasted for months?
Yes, acupuncture can be very effective for chronic cough by regulating Lung Qi, reducing inflammation, and addressing the underlying pattern. Many patients feel some relief after just a few sessions, particularly if the cough is due to lingering pathogens or phlegm. For deficiency patterns, acupuncture helps strengthen the Lungs over time and is often combined with herbal medicine for deeper results.

### Will I need to take Chinese herbs forever?
No. Herbs are typically taken for a specific course - usually 8 to 12 weeks - and then stopped once the pattern has resolved and the cough is gone. Some people with chronic constitutional weakness may use a gentle maintenance formula during cold seasons, but long-term daily herbs are not the norm for a simple chronic cough.

### Is it safe to combine Chinese herbs with my inhaler or allergy medication?
In most cases, yes. Acupuncture and many herbal formulas can be used safely alongside inhalers, antihistamines, and acid blockers. However, some herbs have mild sedative or blood-moving properties that could interact with certain medications. Always bring a complete list of your prescriptions and over-the-counter drugs to your TCM consultation so your practitioner can choose formulas that are safe for you.

### What foods should I avoid if I have a chronic cough?
Generally, avoid cold, raw, greasy, and dairy-heavy foods, which tend to create dampness and phlegm in the body. Spicy foods can irritate a dry, Yin-deficient cough. Instead, focus on warm, cooked meals like soups and congees, and consider adding pears or a little ginger tea to your routine. Your practitioner will give you more specific advice based on your individual pattern.

### My cough gets worse at night - does that mean something specific?
Nighttime worsening is a valuable clue. It often points to Lung Yin deficiency, where the body's natural cooling and moistening functions are weakest at night, or to Phlegm-Heat, which can pool and irritate the airways when you lie down. A TCM practitioner will look at other signs, like whether you have night sweats or sticky yellow sputum, to tell them apart.
