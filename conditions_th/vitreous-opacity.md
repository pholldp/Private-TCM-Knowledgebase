## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนปัจจุบัน (Conventional Medicine View)
### คำอธิบาย (Description)
Eye floaters are small, shadow-like shapes that drift across your field of vision. They're caused by clumps of collagen fibers or cellular debris inside the vitreous humor, the clear gel that fills the eye. As we age, the vitreous naturally shrinks and can pull away from the retina, creating harmless floaters - but a sudden increase, flashes of light, or a curtain-like shadow can signal a retinal tear or detachment.
Diagnosis is made through a dilated eye exam. Most floaters are benign and require no treatment, though they can be distracting and anxiety-provoking. When floaters severely impair vision, surgical options like laser vitreolysis or vitrectomy may be considered, but they carry risks including cataract, infection, and retinal damage.

### การรักษาหลัก (Treatments)
For the majority of people, no medical treatment is necessary beyond reassurance and monitoring. If floaters significantly interfere with daily life, a laser procedure (YAG vitreolysis) can break up larger clumps, or a vitrectomy can remove the vitreous gel and replace it with a clear solution. Both procedures carry risks and are typically reserved for severe cases.

## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนจีน (Traditional Chinese Medicine View)
### ความเข้าใจเกี่ยวกับการแพทย์แผนจีน (TCM Understanding)
In TCM, eye floaters are called 'clouds and mist moving across the eye' (云雾移睛, yún wù yí jīng) - a name that captures the drifting, shadowy sensation. The vitreous humor, known as the 'spirit-gel' (神膏, shén gāo), is a clear, nourishing substance produced from the finest essences of the Kidney and Liver, and kept healthy by the transforming power of the Spleen. When any of these organ systems becomes depleted or blocked, the vitreous loses its clarity and floaters appear.
The Liver stores Blood and opens into the eyes, while the Kidney stores Essence and nourishes the entire visual system. As we age, overwork, or endure chronic stress, Kidney and Liver Yin can become deficient - the eyes then lose their lubrication and nourishment, producing fine, wispy floaters with dryness and blurred vision. On the other hand, if the Spleen is weak, it fails to transform food into clear Qi and Blood, and instead generates dampness and phlegm. This turbid dampness rises to cloud the vitreous, causing many dark, heavy floaters, often with a sluggish body and a greasy tongue coating.
Emotional stress plays a role too: when Liver Qi stagnates, Blood flow in the delicate eye collaterals becomes obstructed, creating fixed black spots or cobwebs and a sensation of distension behind the eye. Even dietary heat can combine with dampness to form Damp-Heat, which steams upward and disturbs the vitreous. So the same Western diagnosis of floaters can arise from very different imbalances - deficiency, dampness, or stagnation - and each requires a fundamentally different treatment strategy.

### การวินิจฉัยโดยแพทย์แผนจีน (Practitioner Diagnosis)
A TCM practitioner starts by listening to the full story beyond the floaters - when they appeared, what they look like, and which other sensations or symptoms accompany them. The tongue and pulse are then examined to confirm which internal imbalance is clouding the vitreous. The goal is to identify the root pattern, not just label the eye symptom.
If the floaters come with dry eyes, blurred vision, dizziness, tinnitus, and a sore lower back, the pattern is likely Kidney and Liver Yin Deficiency. The tongue is often red with little coating, and the pulse feels thin and rapid. This picture reflects a deep depletion of the nourishing Yin that normally keeps the eyes clear.
When floaters appear alongside marked fatigue, a pale face, heart palpitations, and poor appetite, Qi and Blood Deficiency is suspected. The tongue looks pale and the pulse is weak. Here the Spleen and Stomach are not producing enough Qi and blood to moisten the vitreous, so the eye becomes undernourished.
Many dark, shifting floaters together with chest fullness, heavy limbs, a sticky taste, and a thick greasy tongue coating point to Spleen Deficiency with Dampness. The pulse feels slippery. This pattern arises when a weak digestive system fails to transform fluids, and turbid dampness rises to cloud the eye.
Fixed black spots that do not move much, combined with eye distension or a history of stress or injury, suggest Qi and Blood Stagnation. The tongue may have a purplish hue or dark spots, and the pulse can feel wiry or choppy. Emotional tension or trauma has caused blood to congeal in the tiny vessels of the eye.
If the floaters are accompanied by a heavy body, bitter taste, chest oppression, and a greasy yellow tongue coating, Damp-Heat is steaming upward. The pulse is rapid and slippery. This pattern often flares with rich, greasy foods and a humid environment.
When Kidney Yin Deficiency generates empty-heat, the floaters may be joined by flashes of light, dry mouth, night sweats, and a red tongue with scant coating. The pulse is thin and rapid. The fire disturbs the eye collaterals, making the floaters feel more active and irritating.

### Kidney and Liver Yin Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/kidney-and-liver-yin-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Fine thread-like or wispy floaters, Blurred vision with dry eyes, Dizziness and tinnitus, Soreness and weakness of the lower back and knees, Night sweats and heat in the palms, soles, and chest
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Overwork and late nights, Prolonged screen time, Spicy, fried, greasy, or drying foods, Emotional stress, frustration, or anger, Aging
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Rest and adequate sleep, Cool, dark environment, Moistening foods like goji berries, Gentle eye rest, Gentle, non-exhausting exercise
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  The vitreous humor (神膏) relies on Kidney essence and Liver blood for its clarity and moisture. When Kidney and Liver Yin are depleted-often from aging, overwork, or chronic stress-the eyes lose their nourishment, causing the vitreous to become cloudy and generate floaters that look like fine threads or wisps.
At the same time, Yin deficiency creates empty heat that rises to disturb the eyes, adding dryness, blurred vision, and a sensation of heat in the palms, soles, and chest.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red, thin body with cracks, little or no coating, dry surface. Pulse: fine (thin), rapid, and wiry; weak in the left Chi (Kidney) and left Guan (Liver) positions.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Yin is the body's cooling, moistening, and nourishing energy, replenished during sleep and rest. Overwork, late nights, and prolonged eye strain consume Yin and generate empty heat, worsening floaters and dryness. Nourishing Yin foods and rest help restore the fluids that keep the vitreous clear, while stress and frustration can stir up Liver Yang, further depleting Yin and clouding vision.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat moistening, Yin-nourishing foods like black sesame seeds, goji berries, mulberries, and pears. Avoid spicy, fried, and drying foods. Prioritize restful sleep and avoid late nights, as Yin is replenished during sleep. Take frequent breaks from screens to rest the eyes, and practice gentle eye exercises.

### Qi and Blood Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/qi-and-blood-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Floaters worse when tired, better after rest, Pale or sallow complexion, Persistent fatigue and lack of energy, Heart palpitations, Poor appetite, Dizziness or lightheadedness
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Overwork and exhaustion, Skipping meals, Cold and raw foods, Prolonged screen time, Excessive worry
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Rest and adequate sleep, Warm, nourishing meals, Gentle walking, Eating at regular times
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Your Spleen and Stomach are the factories that produce Qi and blood from the food you eat. When they are weak, the body simply cannot make enough of these vital substances to nourish the eyes. The vitreous humor (神膏, shén gāo), which relies on a steady supply of blood and Qi to stay clear and healthy, begins to degenerate and cloud over, creating the drifting specks and threads you see.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale body, may be slightly thin or have teeth marks on the edges, with a thin white coating. Pulse: fine (thin) and weak, lacking force.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Qi and blood are replenished by the Spleen from the food we eat and the rest we take. Overwork, poor diet, and mental strain directly drain these resources, leaving the eyes undernourished and floaters more noticeable. Rest, regular warm meals, and gentle movement conserve and rebuild Qi and blood, stabilizing the vitreous and reducing the drifting shadows.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Make warm, cooked, easily digestible meals the foundation of your diet-think bone broths, congees, slow-cooked stews, and dark leafy greens. Small amounts of iron-rich foods like red dates (jujube) and goji berries can gently build blood. Avoid cold, raw foods and iced drinks that chill the Spleen and impair digestion. Prioritize regular rest, and never skip meals; your eyes need a steady stream of fuel.

### Spleen Deficiency with Dampness
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/spleen-deficiency-with-dampness)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Many dark floaters that seem to drift slowly, Feeling of heaviness in the body and limbs, Chest and upper abdominal fullness, Poor appetite with a sticky or pasty taste in the mouth, Loose or unformed stools, Fatigue and drowsiness, especially after meals
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Cold and raw foods, Dairy and greasy meals, Overeating or irregular meals, Damp or humid weather, Sedentary lifestyle
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warm, cooked meals, Gentle daily movement or light physical activity, Ginger or cardamom tea, Dry, warm weather, Resting after meals
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Your Spleen is responsible for transforming food and fluids into clear Qi and blood. When it becomes weak, fluids aren't processed properly and turn into dampness and phlegm-a heavy, cloudy substance. This turbid dampness drifts upward, clouding the vitreous humor inside the eye, which you see as many dark, drifting floaters.
The same dampness also weighs down the body, causing a heavy feeling in the limbs and head, and clogs the digestive system, leading to bloating, poor appetite, and loose stools.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale, swollen body with teeth marks on the sides, and a white, greasy coating that looks moist. Pulse: soggy (濡 ru) and slow (缓 huan), or slippery (滑 hua), and often weak at the right middle position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Dampness is like internal humidity-it thrives on cold, heavy, and stagnant conditions. Cold and raw foods further weaken the Spleen's digestive fire, allowing dampness to accumulate, while greasy and dairy-rich foods directly add to the phlegm load. Warm, cooked foods and digestive spices support the Spleen's transforming function, and gentle movement helps Qi circulate to dry out dampness, much like a breeze clears fog.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat warm, cooked foods that are easy to digest-think rice congee, soups, and stewed vegetables. Avoid cold, raw, and greasy foods that bog down the Spleen. Ginger, cardamom, and a little black pepper in cooking can help warm and dry dampness. Gentle daily movement like walking or tai chi helps circulate Qi and dry out internal dampness, but avoid overexertion. Eat at regular times and stop just before you feel completely full.

### Qi And Blood Stagnation
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/qi-and-blood-stagnation)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Fixed black spots or cobweb-like floaters, Eye distension or stabbing pain, Flashes of light in the vision, Chest or rib-side distension, Irritability or emotional tension, Dark purplish tongue with stasis spots
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Emotional stress, frustration, or anger, Prolonged eye strain, Sedentary lifestyle, Spicy, fried, greasy, or drying foods, Alcohol

### Damp-Heat
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/damp-heat)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Floaters with a sensation of heaviness in the body and head, Bitter taste in the mouth, sticky sensation, Chest and upper abdominal stuffiness, Dark, scanty urine, Yellow, greasy tongue coating
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Spicy, fried, greasy, or drying foods, Alcohol, Damp or humid weather, Overeating or irregular meals, Sedentary lifestyle
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Light, bland diet, Moderate exercise to sweat lightly, Cool, dry environment, Bitter foods like bitter gourd, Warm water, not iced
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Damp-Heat arises when the Spleen fails to transform fluids, allowing dampness to accumulate, and dietary or environmental heat combines with it. This turbid damp-heat steams upward along the channels to the eyes, clouding the vitreous humor and creating the sensation of floaters. The systemic heaviness, bitter taste, and greasy coating reflect the damp-heat trapped in the body.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: Red body with a thick, yellow, greasy coating that is difficult to scrape off. Pulse: Soggy-rapid (Ru Shu) or slippery-rapid (Hua Shu).
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Damp-Heat arises when the Spleen is overwhelmed by rich foods and damp environments, creating turbid fluids that steam upward. A light diet and moderate exercise help the Spleen transform and transport fluids, while bitter and cooling foods clear heat. Avoiding dampness and overeating prevents the accumulation that clouds the vitreous.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat a light, bland diet with plenty of cooked vegetables and whole grains like barley and millet. Include bitter greens and mung beans to clear heat. Avoid greasy, fried, spicy, and sweet foods that generate dampness. Gentle daily movement like walking or tai chi helps the Spleen transform fluids. Stay hydrated with warm water, not iced drinks, and avoid alcohol.

### Kidney Yin Deficiency With Empty-Heat Blazing
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/kidney-yin-deficiency-with-empty-heat-blazing)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Floaters with flashes of light, Dry, gritty eyes, Night sweats, Five-palm heat (heat in palms, soles, chest), Dry mouth and throat, worse at night, Lower back soreness and weakness, Mental restlessness and insomnia
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Spicy, fried, greasy, or drying foods, Overwork and late nights, Emotional stress, frustration, or anger, Alcohol, Hot, dry weather
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Cool, dark environment, Rest and adequate sleep, Yin-nourishing foods (pear, black sesame), Gentle, non-exhausting exercise
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When Kidney Yin is depleted, it can no longer anchor the body's warming Yang energy. This imbalance causes empty heat to blaze upward along the channels that connect to the eyes. The rising fire disturbs the vitreous humor (神膏, shén gāo), injuring the delicate eye collaterals and producing floaters that often appear with flashes of light. At the same time, the dryness of Yin deficiency leaves the eyes feeling parched and gritty.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red, thin, and dry with little or no coating, possibly cracked. Pulse: fine (细 xì) and rapid (数 shuò), often floating and empty at the superficial level.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Empty heat is like a flickering flame that flares up when the body is dry, tired, or stimulated. Spicy food, late nights, and stress add yang energy without the Yin to hold it, pushing heat upward to disturb the eyes. Cooling, rest, and Yin-nourishing foods replenish the body's deep moisture and anchor that heat, calming the floaters and flashes.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Avoid spicy, fried, and warming foods that fan empty heat. Emphasize cooling, Yin-nourishing foods like black sesame, goji berries, mulberries, and pear. Go to bed by 11 p.m. to preserve Yin, and take regular breaks from screens to rest the eyes. Gentle exercise like walking or swimming supports Kidney energy without overheating.

## คำถามที่พบบ่อย (Frequently Asked Questions)
### Can acupuncture really help with eye floaters?
Yes, many patients find that regular acupuncture reduces the prominence of their floaters or makes them less bothersome. By stimulating points around the eyes (like Jingming BL-1) and on the body, acupuncture improves local circulation, resolves dampness, and nourishes the eye tissues. The effect is gradual and works best when combined with herbal medicine and dietary changes that address the root pattern.

### Do I need to take herbs, or can I just do acupuncture?
For most patterns, herbs are an essential part of treatment. They provide daily, sustained nourishment or clearing action that acupuncture alone cannot maintain between sessions. For example, Yin deficiency requires months of gentle tonification, while dampness needs consistent drying and transforming. Acupuncture and herbs together produce the best results.

### How long before I see results?
Many people notice subtle improvements - less eye strain, better sleep, fewer new floaters - within 4 to 6 weeks. A noticeable fading of existing floaters often takes 2 to 3 months of consistent treatment. Deficiency patterns take longer than excess patterns because the body needs time to rebuild depleted reserves.

### Are there any foods that make floaters worse?
Yes. Greasy, fried, and dairy-heavy foods tend to create dampness and phlegm, which can cloud the vitreous. Spicy, heating foods and alcohol can stir up heat and aggravate Yin deficiency or Damp-Heat patterns. Your practitioner will give you specific guidance based on your pattern, but in general, a light, cooked, and easy-to-digest diet supports clearer vision.

### Can TCM prevent new floaters from forming?
By correcting the underlying imbalance - whether it's a deficiency, dampness, or stagnation - TCM can slow or halt the degenerative process in the vitreous. Many patients report that after a course of treatment, new floaters stop appearing. While it's not a guaranteed prevention, it addresses the terrain that allowed floaters to develop in the first place.

### Is it safe to use TCM alongside my eye doctor's monitoring?
Absolutely. TCM is complementary and works well alongside regular eye exams. Always inform both your TCM practitioner and your ophthalmologist about all treatments you're receiving. If you are taking anticoagulants, mention this before starting herbal medicine, as some blood-moving herbs may interact. Never stop prescribed eye drops or medications without consulting your doctor.

### What does the tongue tell you about my floaters?
The tongue is a key diagnostic tool. A red, thin tongue with little coating suggests Kidney and Liver Yin Deficiency. A pale, swollen tongue with a greasy coating points to Spleen Deficiency with Dampness. A purple tongue with stasis spots indicates Qi and Blood Stagnation. These clues help your practitioner pinpoint the exact pattern and choose the right herbs and points.
