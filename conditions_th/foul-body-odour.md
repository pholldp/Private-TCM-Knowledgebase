## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนปัจจุบัน (Conventional Medicine View)
### คำอธิบาย (Description)
In Western medicine, body odour is understood as the result of bacteria on the skin breaking down sweat, particularly the thicker, protein-rich sweat from apocrine glands in the armpits and groin. It's not a disease in itself but a normal physiological process that can become socially distressing when it's strong or persistent. Diagnosis is usually straightforward based on the smell and its location, and it's rarely a sign of serious illness - though sudden changes can occasionally point to metabolic disorders.

### การรักษาหลัก (Treatments)
Conventional management starts with good hygiene, antiperspirants (which block sweat ducts with aluminum salts), and deodorants (which mask smell or kill bacteria). For more severe cases, doctors may prescribe stronger topical agents, Botox injections to temporarily paralyze sweat glands, or even surgical procedures like sweat gland removal or sympathectomy. These approaches focus on reducing sweat or bacteria locally.

## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนจีน (Traditional Chinese Medicine View)
### ความเข้าใจเกี่ยวกับการแพทย์แผนจีน (TCM Understanding)
TCM traces foul body odour to the body's internal environment - specifically, to the accumulation of Dampness and Heat that steams outward through the skin. The Liver and Gallbladder channels run directly through the armpits, so when Damp-Heat congests these pathways, the sweat becomes yellow-tinged and pungent, often worsening with stress or greasy food. This is one of the most common patterns, and it explains why the odour feels so stubborn: the root is deep in the organ system, not on the skin's surface.
Another major player is the Spleen, which governs the transformation and transport of fluids. When the Spleen is weak - from overwork, poor diet, or constitutional tendency - it fails to manage moisture, and internal Dampness builds up like a slow leak. This Dampness seeps to the armpits and produces a sticky, musty sweat that lingers. Unlike the sharp heat-driven odour, this one feels heavy and is often accompanied by bloating and fatigue.
Emotions matter too. The Liver is easily affected by stress and frustration, and when its Qi stagnates it can generate Fire. That internal Fire rises and gives sweat a sharper, more acrid quality - the kind that flares up during arguments or anxiety. Even the Heart and Kidneys can be involved: when their balance is off and Yin is depleted, empty heat drives out night sweats that carry a faint sour note. So what Western medicine sees as one problem, TCM sees as a map of different internal landscapes.

### การวินิจฉัยโดยแพทย์แผนจีน (Practitioner Diagnosis)
A TCM practitioner begins by asking about the smell itself - when it is strongest, what the sweat looks like, and what makes it worse. The quality of the odour and the appearance of the sweat are the first important clues that point toward one pattern rather than another.
If the sweat stains clothing yellow and the odour is strong and pungent, that suggests Liver and Gallbladder Damp-Heat. The person often feels a sticky, heavy sensation in the armpits and may have a bitter taste in the mouth. The tongue is typically red with a greasy yellow coating, and the pulse feels slippery and rapid.
If the odour is very intense and the skin under the arms looks red, irritated, or even darkened, Toxic-Heat Stagnation may be brewing. This pattern often arises from long-standing damp-heat that has deepened into toxic heat. The tongue is red with a thick yellow coat, and the pulse is rapid and forceful.
When the sweat is sticky but the odour is more musty and persistent, and the person feels tired and heavy all over, Spleen Deficiency with Dampness is more likely. The tongue is pale and puffy with a white greasy coat, and the pulse is weak or soft, reflecting the Spleen’s struggle to manage fluids.
A pungent odour that flares up with stress, along with a bitter taste, irritability, and a feeling of fullness in the chest, points to Stagnant Liver Qi turning into Fire. The tongue is red with a thin yellow coating, and the pulse is wiry and rapid. Emotional ups and downs are a hallmark here.
In rarer cases where the odour is faint and sour, and the person sweats mainly at night or feels generally weak, Disharmony between Heart and Kidneys may be the root. The tongue often has little coating and looks redder at the tip, while the pulse is thin and rapid. This pattern is more common in people with a constitutional weakness or long-term exhaustion.

## กลุ่มอาการการแพทย์แผนจีนและการรักษา (TCM Patterns and Treatment)

### Liver and Gallbladder Damp-Heat
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/liver-and-gallbladder-damp-heat)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Pungent odor with yellow-tinged sweat, Bitter taste in the mouth, Distension or pain under the ribs, Irritability and restlessness, Dark yellow, scanty urine
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Greasy, spicy, or fried foods, Alcohol and coffee, Hot, humid weather, Anger and emotional stress, Sedentary lifestyle
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Cool, dry environment, Light, bland foods, Gentle exercise, Stress reduction
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  The Liver and Gallbladder channels run through the armpits. When damp-heat accumulates in these channels due to diet, emotions, or climate, the heat steams the dampness upward and outward, producing a pungent, yellow-tinged sweat that ferments into a strong body odor. This damp-heat also disturbs the flow of Qi, leading to the characteristic bitter taste, rib distension, and irritability.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red body with red sides and a thick, yellow, greasy coating. Pulse: wiry (弦 xián), slippery (滑 huá), and rapid (数 shuò).
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Greasy, spicy foods and alcohol generate dampness and heat internally, which directly fuels the damp-heat in the Liver and Gallbladder channels, making the sweat more pungent. Emotional stress, particularly anger and frustration, causes Liver Qi to stagnate and then flare upwards, carrying the damp-heat to the armpits. A cool, dry environment and light, bland foods help clear damp-heat, while gentle exercise moves Qi and promotes the discharge of dampness through urine and sweat, reducing the stagnation that causes the odor.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Avoid greasy, fried, and spicy foods, as well as alcohol and coffee, which add heat and dampness. Eat more cooling, damp-resolving foods like mung beans, job's tears (coix seed), cucumber, and bitter greens. Wear loose, breathable fabrics and shower after sweating to keep the armpits dry and clean.

### Toxic-Heat Stagnation
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/toxic-heat-stagnation)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Localized redness, swelling and heat in the armpit, Skin feels hot, irritated or tender to touch, Pungent, foul odour stronger than typical damp-heat, Thirst for cold drinks and a general feeling of heat
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Greasy, spicy, or fried foods, Alcohol and coffee, Hot, humid weather, Anger and emotional stress
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Cooling compresses, Cold drinks, Rest and calm
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When Damp-Heat lingers in the body for too long, it can thicken and brew into a more intense form called Toxic-Heat. This deep, aggressive heat concentrates in areas like the armpits, where it irritates the skin and gives sweat a much stronger, more pungent odour. The local skin often becomes red, swollen and tender as the toxins try to push outward.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: deep red or crimson body, often with prickly papillae and a thick, dry yellow coating. Pulse: rapid and forceful, sometimes overflowing.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Toxic-Heat is an intense internal fire that needs to be cooled and cleared, not fed. Spicy, greasy foods and alcohol add more heat, while hot weather traps it. Cooling foods, rest and cool water help the body disperse the excess heat, calming the skin and reducing the foul odour at its source.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Avoid spicy, greasy and deep-fried foods, as well as alcohol, which all add fuel to the toxic fire. Eat plenty of cooling, detoxifying foods like cucumber, watermelon, mung beans and bitter greens. Keep the armpits clean and dry, wear loose breathable cotton, and consider a gentle cool-water rinse during the day to soothe the skin.

### Spleen Deficiency with Dampness
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/spleen-deficiency-with-dampness)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Musty, persistent body odor, not sharp or pungent, Sticky, heavy-feeling sweat, Abdominal bloating after eating, Loose stools or diarrhoea, Fatigue and heaviness in the limbs, Poor appetite with a sticky taste in the mouth
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Greasy, sweet, or dairy-rich foods, Cold, raw foods and iced drinks, Damp, humid environment, Sedentary lifestyle, Worry and overthinking
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warm, dry weather, Light, bland foods, Gentle exercise, Avoiding overeating
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When the Spleen's ability to transform and transport fluids is weak, water and moisture accumulate inside the body as internal Dampness. This heavy, turbid Dampness seeps outward to the skin, especially in the armpits where sweat glands are concentrated. The result is a sticky, persistent, musty odor that clings to the body and clothing.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale, swollen body with teeth marks, white greasy coating, moist surface. Pulse: soggy (Ru) and slow (Huan), soft and lacking force.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Spleen Qi is easily weakened by dietary excess, cold, and mental strain, causing fluids to stagnate and turn into Dampness. Warm, simple foods and movement give the Spleen the support it needs to process moisture, while greasy, cold, or heavy foods overwhelm it and make the musty odor worse.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Warm, light, cooked foods are your best ally. Enjoy congee, steamed vegetables, and small amounts of ginger; avoid dairy, sugar, greasy fried foods, and icy drinks, which all create more Dampness. Gentle daily movement like walking or yoga helps the Spleen move Qi and dry out internal moisture.

### Stagnant Liver Qi turning into Fire
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/stagnant-liver-qi-turning-into-fire)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Irritability and angry outbursts, Bitter taste in the mouth, Rib-side distension or pain, Headache with throbbing or distending quality, Red eyes, Worsening of odor with stress or anger
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Anger and frustration, Greasy, spicy, or fried foods, Alcohol and coffee, Prolonged stress
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Cooling foods like chrysanthemum tea, Stress reduction, Gentle exercise, Bitter greens like dandelion
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Emotional stress and frustration cause the Liver Qi to stagnate, like a traffic jam in the body's energy highway. Over time, that stuck Qi overheats and turns into Fire, which rises and disturbs the body's fluids. This internal heat gives sweat a sharper, more pungent smell, especially when anger flares up.
The combination of stagnant Qi and blazing Fire often teams up with any underlying dampness, making the body odor more noticeable. The bitter taste in the mouth, irritability, and rib-side tension are all signs that the Liver is in distress and heating up.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red body, especially along the sides, with a yellow dry coating. Pulse: wiry (弦 xián) and rapid (数 shuò).
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Anger and stress are the very fuel that turns stagnant Liver Qi into Fire, so they directly intensify the sharp body odor. Cooling foods and bitter greens help clear that internal heat, while gentle movement and relaxation unblock the Qi flow, extinguishing the fire at its source and reducing the pungent smell.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Cool the fire with chrysanthemum tea, celery, and mung beans. Avoid spicy, greasy foods and alcohol, which add fuel. A daily walk or gentle stretching helps keep Liver Qi moving, and a short meditation can prevent anger from simmering into odor.

### Disharmony between Heart and Kidneys
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/disharmony-between-heart-and-kidneys)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Night sweats with a faint sour smell, Mental restlessness and irritability, Palpitations and vivid dreams, Soreness and weakness of the lower back and knees, Heat in the palms, soles, and chest, Dry mouth and throat
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Emotional stress and worry, Overwork and late nights, Greasy, spicy, or fried foods
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Consistent, early bedtime, Cool, quiet environment, Light, bland foods
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When the Kidneys and Heart fall out of balance, the cooling, moistening Yin of the body becomes depleted and empty heat rises. This heat disturbs the mind and pushes fluids out through the sweat glands, especially at night. Because the sweat is not anchored by healthy Yin fluids, it can carry a faint, sour undertone that differs from the strong, pungent odor of damp-heat patterns.
This type of odor is often more noticeable after a restless night or during periods of mental strain, reflecting the underlying constitutional weakness and the body's struggle to hold onto its vital fluids.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red, thin body with a redder tip, very little or no coating, and sometimes a midline crack. Pulse: fine (xi) and rapid (shu), weaker at the rear position, and slightly floating at the front left position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: This pattern is driven by a deep Yin deficiency, and Yin is the body's cooling, restorative energy. Stress, overwork, and late nights directly burn up Yin, allowing empty heat to flare and push out sweat that carries a sour note. Rest, a calm mind, and moistening foods replenish Yin and anchor the heat, so the body no longer leaks fluids and the odor fades.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Favor cooling, moistening foods like pear, cucumber, tofu, and millet. Avoid spicy, fried, or overly heating foods that consume Yin. Go to bed early and protect your sleep; late nights rapidly deplete Kidney Yin. Gentle exercise like walking or tai chi supports fluid circulation without overheating the body.

## คำถามที่พบบ่อย (Frequently Asked Questions)
### Can acupuncture really help with body odour?
Yes, acupuncture can be very effective because it addresses the internal imbalances that cause the sweat to smell in the first place. By clearing Damp-Heat, strengthening the Spleen, or soothing Liver Fire, acupuncture helps normalize the body's fluids so the odour-producing environment changes. Many patients notice a reduction in the intensity of the smell and a change in the texture of their sweat within a few sessions.

### Do I need to stop using deodorant while doing TCM treatment?
No, you can continue using your regular deodorant or antiperspirant. TCM treatment works from the inside out, so there's no conflict. In fact, as the internal pattern improves, you may find you need less external product over time. Just let your practitioner know what you're using, especially if you're applying any medicated creams.

### How long will it take to see a difference?
Most people start to notice a change in the quality and strength of their body odour within 2 to 4 weeks of starting herbs and acupuncture. Excess patterns tend to respond faster; deficiency patterns may take a bit longer as the body rebuilds. External applications like herbal powders can give almost immediate local relief while the internal treatment takes effect.

### Will the odour come back after I stop treatment?
If the underlying pattern has been fully resolved, the odour should stay away. However, if the lifestyle or dietary habits that created the imbalance return - like constant stress, greasy food, or irregular eating - the pattern can creep back. Your practitioner will give you guidance on how to maintain balance after treatment ends.

### Are there any foods I should avoid?
Yes, in general, you'll want to avoid foods that create Dampness and Heat in the body. This means cutting back on greasy, fried, and spicy dishes, as well as alcohol, excessive sugar, and dairy. Instead, focus on light, bland foods like mung beans, barley, bitter greens, and chrysanthemum tea, which help clear heat and drain dampness.

### Is TCM treatment for body odour safe during pregnancy?
Many acupuncture points and herbs are perfectly safe during pregnancy, but some are contraindicated because they strongly move Qi and Blood. It's essential to tell your practitioner if you are pregnant or trying to conceive, so they can tailor the treatment safely. Never self-prescribe herbs during pregnancy.
