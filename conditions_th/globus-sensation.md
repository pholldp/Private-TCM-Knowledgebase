## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนปัจจุบัน (Conventional Medicine View)
### คำอธิบาย (Description)
Globus sensation is the persistent feeling of a lump, tightness, or something stuck in the throat when no physical blockage is present. It is considered a functional disorder, meaning the sensation is real but not caused by a structural abnormality. It is often associated with stress, anxiety, gastroesophageal reflux disease (GERD), or muscle tension in the throat. Diagnosis is made after a doctor rules out serious causes through examination and sometimes imaging or endoscopy.

### การรักษาหลัก (Treatments)
After structural causes are excluded, conventional management focuses on reassurance - explaining that the sensation is benign. If acid reflux is present, proton-pump inhibitors may be prescribed. Speech therapy and relaxation techniques can help reduce throat muscle tension. In some cases, low-dose tricyclic antidepressants or selective serotonin reuptake inhibitors (SSRIs) are used to modulate nerve sensitivity in the throat.

## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนจีน (Traditional Chinese Medicine View)
### ความเข้าใจเกี่ยวกับการแพทย์แผนจีน (TCM Understanding)
TCM understands globus sensation primarily through the Liver and its relationship with the Spleen and Stomach. The Liver is responsible for the smooth flow of Qi throughout the body. When stress, frustration, or emotional upset block this flow, Qi stagnates and can surge upward to get stuck in the throat. This is why the sensation so often waxes and wanes with your mood - it is a direct reflection of Liver Qi stagnation.
But stuck Qi alone rarely creates the full picture. Over time, stagnant Qi disrupts the Spleen’s job of transforming fluids, and those fluids congeal into Phlegm. The Qi and Phlegm then bind together and rise along the channels to lodge in the throat, producing the classic lump sensation that TCM calls Plum-Pit Qi. This is the most common pattern, and it explains why the feeling can feel so physical even when no structural blockage is found.
Other patterns involve rebellious Stomach Qi, where digestive upset sends Qi and turbidity upward, or Phlegm-Heat, where long-standing Phlegm thickens and generates internal heat that makes the throat feel hot and sticky. In chronic cases, the stagnation can even obstruct Blood flow, creating a fixed, unyielding sensation. This is why two people with the same Western diagnosis of globus sensation may need completely different treatments - one needs to move Liver Qi, another needs to transform Phlegm, and a third needs to descend rebellious Stomach Qi.
Because the throat is a crossroads for the Lung, Stomach, and Liver channels, TCM always checks which organ system is most out of balance. The tongue and pulse give the clearest clues: a wiry pulse points to Liver Qi stagnation, a slippery pulse tells us Phlegm is involved, and a red tongue with a yellow coating suggests Heat. This pattern-level thinking is the foundation of TCM treatment for globus sensation.

### การวินิจฉัยโดยแพทย์แผนจีน (Practitioner Diagnosis)
A TCM practitioner begins by exploring the exact nature of the throat sensation and what makes it better or worse. They ask whether it feels like a lump, a blockage, or something stuck, and whether it fluctuates with mood, after meals, or with stress. The pattern of onset and relief provides the first clues, which are then confirmed by examining the tongue and feeling the pulse.
If the sensation is a persistent lump that cannot be swallowed or coughed up, often with chest tightness and a need to clear the throat, the classic Qi-Phlegm pattern (梅核气, méi hé qì) is likely. The tongue may appear normal pink-red or slightly pale and swollen, with a white greasy coating, and the pulse feels wiry and slippery, reflecting both stagnant Qi and accumulated phlegm lodging in the throat.
When the throat lump clearly worsens with frustration, stress, or emotional upset and eases when the person is calm, Liver Qi Stagnation is the root. The tongue body is usually normal or slightly dark with red or purplish sides and a thin white coating, and the pulse is distinctly wiry. This pattern often sets the stage for phlegm to form later.
If the throat sensation arrives alongside digestive complaints-bloating, poor appetite, loose stools, and a heavy body feeling-the pattern shifts to Obstruction of the Spleen by Dampness with Liver Qi Stagnation. The tongue is often swollen with teeth marks and a greasy coat, and the pulse is slippery or wiry, showing that dampness is rising from a weakened Spleen to obstruct the throat.
When belching, acid reflux, nausea, or a sensation of something rising from the stomach accompanies the throat lump, Rebellious Stomach Qi is the key. The practitioner asks about eating habits and digestive comfort. The tongue often has a normal body colour with a white coating (which may turn yellow if there is heat or greasy if phlegm), and the pulse is typically wiry and slippery, with the right middle position feeling full and taut, indicating that Stomach Qi is moving upward instead of descending.
If the lumpy feeling comes with a sense of heat in the throat, thirst, a bitter taste, or irritability, and the tongue is red with a yellow greasy coating and the pulse is rapid and slippery, Phlegm-Heat has developed. This pattern arises when stagnant phlegm transforms into heat, often after long-standing Qi stagnation and dampness.
In chronic cases where the throat sensation is fixed and may be joined by chest pain or a stabbing sensation, Qi and Blood Stagnation is present. The tongue looks dark purple with possible stasis spots, and the pulse is choppy. This develops when prolonged Qi stagnation begins to obstruct blood flow, deepening the throat blockage.

### Qi-Phlegm
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/qi-phlegm)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Sensation of a lump in the throat (globus sensation), Abdominal bloating or fullness, Nausea or queasiness, Frequent sighing, Symptoms worsen with emotional stress, White greasy or slippery tongue coating
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Emotional stress, frustration, or anger, Greasy, heavy, or fried foods, Overthinking and worry, Sedentary lifestyle
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warm, light meals, Gentle exercise or walking, Stress reduction and emotional calm, Ginger tea, Deep breathing
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Emotional frustration, worry, or long-standing stress causes Liver Qi to stagnate. Over time, this stagnant Qi fails to move fluids, and phlegm begins to accumulate. The stuck Qi and phlegm bind together and rise along the channel pathways to lodge in the throat, creating the classic sensation of a lump that can't be swallowed or coughed up - what traditional texts call 'Plum-Pit Qi.'
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: normal pink-red body, possibly slightly swollen, with a white greasy or slippery coating. Pulse: wiry (Xian) and slippery (Hua).
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Emotional stress tightens Liver Qi, which is the root of this pattern; when Qi stagnates, phlegm forms and rises to the throat. Damp, greasy foods overload the Spleen and generate more phlegm. Gentle movement and warm foods help Qi flow smoothly and support digestion, preventing phlegm accumulation. Relaxation and deep breathing directly unblock stagnant Qi and ease the throat sensation.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Avoid damp-producing foods such as dairy, greasy or fried foods, cold raw foods, and excessive sweets. Eat warm, cooked meals with moderate amounts of ginger and aromatic herbs to support digestion. Gentle daily movement like walking or yoga helps keep Qi flowing. Practice stress management through deep breathing, journaling, or talking with a friend; emotional tension directly aggravates this pattern.

### Liver Qi Stagnation
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/liver-qi-stagnation)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Symptoms worsen with emotional stress, Frequent sighing, Irritability or low mood, Chest oppression and rib distension
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Emotional stress, frustration, or anger, Greasy, heavy, or fried foods, Sedentary lifestyle, Tight clothing around the chest
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Stress reduction and emotional calm, Gentle exercise or walking, Warm, light meals, Deep breathing
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Your Liver is responsible for keeping Qi flowing smoothly throughout your body. When stress, frustration, or emotional upset block that flow, the stagnant Qi can surge upward and get stuck in the throat, creating a sensation of a lump that cannot be swallowed or coughed up. Because the root is emotional, the feeling often comes and goes with your mood.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: normal or slightly dark body with red or purplish sides and a thin white coat. Pulse: wiry (弦 xián), taut like a guitar string.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Stress and frustration are the primary cause of Liver Qi stagnation, so they directly trigger the throat lump. Gentle movement and deep breathing help circulate Qi, which releases the stagnation. Heavy foods burden the Spleen and can generate dampness, making the stagnation worse. Emotional calm allows the Liver to smooth out its flow.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Move your body daily with walking or gentle yoga to keep Qi flowing. Eat small, regular meals with plenty of lightly cooked greens and avoid heavy, greasy foods that can clog the system. Practice deep breathing or meditation to manage stress and prevent emotional buildup.

### Obstruction Of the Spleen By Dampness with Liver Qi Stagnation
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/obstruction-of-the-spleen-by-dampness-with-liver-qi-stagnation)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Lump sensation worsens after eating or with emotional stress, Abdominal bloating or fullness, Sticky, incomplete bowel movements or loose stools, Heaviness of the body and limbs, Irritability or low mood
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Emotional stress, frustration, or anger, Greasy, heavy, or fried foods, Overeating or large meals, Damp, cold weather, Sedentary lifestyle
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warm, light meals, Gentle exercise or walking, Stress reduction and emotional calm, Avoiding cold drinks and raw foods
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Emotional strain and frustration cause Liver Qi to stagnate, which then invades the Spleen. When the Spleen is disrupted, it fails to transform and transport fluids, allowing dampness and phlegm to accumulate. This phlegm-dampness rises and lodges in the throat, creating the classic sensation of a stuck lump. The digestive heaviness, bloating, and sticky stools that come with it are the hallmark clues that the Spleen is overwhelmed by dampness.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale and swollen with teeth marks, a white greasy coating that is excessively moist. Pulse: slippery (滑 huá) and wiry (弦 xián), with the right guan position soggy (濡 ruǎn) and the left guan position wiry.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Emotional stress directly aggravates Liver Qi stagnation, which is the driving force behind this pattern; when the Liver invades the Spleen, dampness builds even more. Greasy, heavy, or cold foods overwhelm a weakened Spleen and generate additional dampness and phlegm, making the throat sensation and bloating worse. Warm, light meals and gentle movement support the Spleen's ability to transform fluids and keep Qi moving, while emotional calm prevents the Liver from overacting on the digestive system.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Avoid cold, raw, and greasy foods that create dampness-think salads, ice cream, fried snacks, and excessive dairy. Favor warm, cooked meals like soups, stews, and lightly steamed vegetables. Gentle, regular movement such as walking or yoga helps keep Qi flowing and prevents stagnation. Protect your emotional wellbeing through adequate rest and activities that reduce stress.

### Rebellious Stomach Qi
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/rebellious-stomach-qi)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Lump sensation worsens after eating or with emotional stress, Frequent belching or burping, Nausea or queasiness, Sensation of Qi rising from the stomach to the throat, Abdominal bloating or fullness, Loss of appetite
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Overeating or large meals, Cold or raw foods, Greasy, heavy, or fried foods, Lying down immediately after eating, Emotional stress, frustration, or anger
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warm, light meals, Ginger tea, Sitting upright after eating, Gentle exercise or walking, Stress reduction and emotional calm
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  The Stomach is meant to send Qi downward. When stress, overeating, or dietary cold weaken it, that downward movement reverses. Rebellious Stomach Qi surges upward toward the throat, often carrying phlegm and turbidity with it, which creates the stuck, lump-like sensation of globus. This is why the feeling frequently comes with belching, nausea, or acid reflux - signs that the Stomach is pushing up instead of down.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: normal body colour with a white coating (may turn yellow if Heat or greasy if Phlegm). Pulse: wiry (弦 xián), often with a slippery (滑 huá) quality; the right middle position (Stomach/Spleen) may feel full and taut.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Overeating and cold foods weaken Stomach Qi, making it more likely to rebel upward. Stress stirs Liver Qi, which can invade the Stomach and push Qi the wrong way. Warm, cooked meals and ginger gently support the Stomach's natural downward movement. Sitting upright and walking after meals use gravity and gentle motion to keep Qi flowing down, while managing stress calms the Liver and stops it from interfering with the Stomach.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat smaller, more frequent meals and avoid lying down right after eating. Warm, cooked foods like soups and congees support Stomach Qi, while cold, raw, greasy, or spicy foods tend to provoke rebellion. Gentle movement after meals and managing stress help keep Qi flowing downward.

### Phlegm-Heat
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/phlegm-heat)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Sensation of a hot, sticky lump in the throat, Thick, yellow phlegm in the throat, Thirst with a desire for cold drinks, Irritability or low mood, Red, flushed face, Chest fullness or oppression
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Spicy food, Greasy, heavy, or fried foods, Emotional stress, frustration, or anger, Smoking, Hot weather
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Cool drinks, Light, bland meals, Deep breathing, Gentle exercise or walking, Cool environment
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Phlegm-Heat arises when stagnant phlegm - often from long-standing Qi stagnation and a weakened Spleen - thickens and generates internal heat. This hot, sticky phlegm rises along the Qi pathways and lodges in the throat, creating a sensation of a warm, immovable lump. The heat intensifies discomfort, adding thirst, irritability, and a flushed feeling.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red body, swollen with a thick yellow greasy coating. Pulse: slippery (滑 huá) and rapid (数 shuò).
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Phlegm-Heat is a mix of dampness and heat that thickens and rises. Spicy and greasy foods directly add heat and dampness, fueling the condition. Emotional stress stagnates Qi, which generates heat and encourages phlegm formation. Cooling foods and calm environments help clear heat and settle the Qi, while gentle exercise moves Qi and prevents stagnation from taking hold.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Avoid spicy, greasy, and fried foods that add heat and dampness. Choose cooling, light meals like pear, radish, and cucumber. Gentle daily movement helps Qi flow and prevents stagnation. Manage stress and avoid smoking.

Cold and inactivity further slow circulation, while gentle movement and warmth invigorate blood flow, helping to dissolve the fixed sensation in the throat.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat warm, cooked foods and avoid cold, raw items that congeal blood. Gentle daily movement like walking or tai chi helps keep Qi and blood flowing. Practice relaxation to prevent emotional tension from stagnating further.

## คำถามที่พบบ่อย (Frequently Asked Questions)
### What does TCM treatment for a throat lump actually involve?
Treatment usually combines a customized herbal formula with acupuncture. The herbs work internally to move stagnant Qi, transform Phlegm, and descend rebellious energy, while acupuncture points on the neck, wrists, and legs open the channels that pass through the throat. Most patients come once a week for acupuncture and take herbs daily. The exact points and formula depend entirely on your pattern - someone with Liver Qi stagnation will receive different herbs and points than someone with Phlegm-Heat.

### Will acupuncture needles go into my throat?
Not typically. While a point like Tiantu (REN-22) at the base of the throat may be used very gently, the majority of points are on the arms, legs, and back - places like Neiguan (PC-6) on the inner wrist and Zusanli (ST-36) below the knee. These points influence the channels that travel to the throat without direct needling into the neck. Any point near the throat is needled with extreme care and only when comfortable for you.

### Can I combine Chinese herbs with my anxiety or reflux medication?
In most cases, yes - but you must tell both your TCM practitioner and your prescribing doctor about everything you are taking. Some herbs that move Qi or transform Phlegm are very safe alongside common medications like SSRIs or proton-pump inhibitors. However, certain herbs can have mild sedative effects or influence liver enzymes, so your practitioner needs a full medication list. Never stop a prescribed medication abruptly without your doctor’s guidance.

### How long does it take to feel better?
Many patients notice the throat sensation softening within two to four weeks of consistent herbs and acupuncture. Pure Liver Qi stagnation often responds fastest. Patterns with significant Phlegm or Spleen deficiency may need six to eight weeks, and chronic cases with Blood stasis can take three to six months for lasting change. The key is that improvement is gradual - the lump doesn’t usually vanish overnight, but it becomes less intrusive and less frequent.

### Is the lump a sign of something dangerous?
Globus sensation itself is benign - it is a functional sensation, not a growth or blockage. However, you should always have a new or changing throat sensation evaluated by a doctor to rule out structural causes. TCM practitioners rely on that medical clearance. Once serious conditions are excluded, TCM can safely address the sensation. If you develop difficulty swallowing, pain, or weight loss, see a doctor immediately.

### Do I need to change my diet?
Diet plays a supporting role. Cold, raw, and greasy foods tend to weaken the Spleen and generate more Phlegm, while dairy and sugar can make Phlegm thicker. Warm, lightly cooked meals, ginger tea, and avoiding late-night eating help reduce the burden on your digestive system. Specific dietary advice will be tailored to your pattern - someone with Phlegm-Heat may need cooling foods, while someone with Spleen dampness needs warming, drying foods.
