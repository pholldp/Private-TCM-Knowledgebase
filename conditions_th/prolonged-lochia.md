## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนปัจจุบัน (Conventional Medicine View)
### คำอธิบาย (Description)
In Western medicine, lochia is the normal vaginal discharge after childbirth, consisting of blood, mucus, and uterine tissue. It typically lasts three to four weeks, transitioning from red to brown to yellowish-white. When bleeding persists beyond six weeks, it is considered abnormal and may be investigated for retained placental fragments, uterine atony, infection, or subinvolution of the uterus. Diagnosis usually involves ultrasound and pelvic examination.

### การรักษาหลัก (Treatments)
If retained products of conception are found, a dilation and curettage (D&C) may be performed. Antibiotics are used for infection. Uterotonics like oxytocin may be given to help the uterus contract. In cases of atony, ergometrine or misoprostol may be prescribed. Iron supplements may be recommended if blood loss has caused anemia.

## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนจีน (Traditional Chinese Medicine View)
### ความเข้าใจเกี่ยวกับการแพทย์แผนจีน (TCM Understanding)
In TCM, the uterus is governed by the Chong and Ren vessels, two deep meridians that regulate menstruation, pregnancy, and postpartum recovery. Childbirth depletes Qi and blood, leaving these vessels weakened. If the Spleen and Stomach - the organs responsible for producing new Qi and blood - are too slow to replenish what was lost, the uterus lacks the strength to contract and seal its vessels. The result is a pale, thin, and often profuse lochia that continues because the body simply cannot hold blood in.
But not all prolonged bleeding comes from weakness. Sometimes the problem is stagnation. If lochia is not fully expelled after delivery, or if cold enters the lower body, blood can congeal and form clots that obstruct the uterus. These clots prevent the uterus from contracting properly, so bleeding drags on - dark, clotted, and intermittent - with a characteristic fixed pain that eases after a clot passes. This is a pattern of excess, not deficiency, and it requires moving the blood rather than tonifying.
There is also a third common scenario: Yin deficiency with empty heat. Childbirth consumes a great deal of blood and Yin, the body's cooling and moistening resources. When Yin is too depleted, a low-grade internal heat can develop. This heat agitates the blood in the uterus, preventing the vessels from closing, so lochia continues longer than normal. The discharge tends to be red, thick, and sometimes slightly smelly, accompanied by night sweats, a dry mouth, and a feeling of heat in the palms and soles. Each of these patterns requires a completely different herbal strategy - which is why TCM always begins by reading the colour, consistency, and accompanying sensations of the lochia.

### การวินิจฉัยโดยแพทย์แผนจีน (Practitioner Diagnosis)
A TCM practitioner begins by asking about the colour, consistency, amount and smell of the lochia, as well as any accompanying sensations like pain or fatigue. Because postpartum recovery draws heavily on Qi and Blood, these clues quickly point toward one of the main patterns behind prolonged lochia.
When the lochia is pale, thin and profuse, and the person feels exhausted, breathless and looks pale, the picture is one of Qi and Blood Deficiency. The tongue tends to be pale with a thin white coating, and the pulse is thready and weak, confirming that the Chong and Ren vessels lack the strength to hold blood.
If the bleeding is dark, clotted and comes and goes with a fixed, pressing lower abdominal pain that feels worse with pressure, Blood Stagnation in the Directing and Penetrating Vessels is likely. The tongue may appear dark purple or show stasis spots, and the pulse is often deep and choppy, signalling that retained lochia or cold has obstructed the uterus.
When the discharge is red, thick and sticky, sometimes with a faint foul odour, and the person notices a dry mouth, warm palms or night sweats, the pattern shifts to Empty-Heat caused by Yin Deficiency. The tongue is red with little or no coating, and the pulse is thready and rapid, reflecting yin-blood loss that has allowed deficiency heat to agitate the blood.
Less commonly, if the lochia is purplish, sticky and distinctly foul-smelling, accompanied by lower abdominal distension and a sensation of heat, the pattern is Heat and Blood Stagnation in the Lower Burner. Here the tongue is reddish-purple with stasis spots and a dry yellow coating, and the pulse is deep, choppy and rapid, reflecting trapped damp-heat and blood stasis in the pelvis.

## กลุ่มอาการการแพทย์แผนจีนและการรักษา (TCM Patterns and Treatment)

### Qi and Blood Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/qi-and-blood-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Pale, thin, profuse lochia without foul odor, Overwhelming fatigue and weakness, Dizziness or lightheadedness, Pale or sallow complexion, Poor appetite
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Overexertion or standing too long, Cold foods and drinks, Emotional stress
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Rest and lying down, Warm nourishing soups, Adequate sleep
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Childbirth consumes a great deal of Qi and blood. When the body cannot replenish them quickly enough, the Chong and Ren vessels become too weak to hold blood in the uterus. As a result, lochia continues to flow - it is typically pale, thin, and profuse, without a foul smell, because there is no heat or stasis driving it, just a lack of containment.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale body, may be thin or have teeth marks, thin white coating. Pulse: fine (thready) and weak.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Qi and Blood Deficiency means the body lacks the strength to contain the lochia. Rest and nourishing foods help rebuild that strength, while overexertion and cold further drain it. Emotional stress taxes the Spleen, which is the source of Qi and blood, so staying calm supports recovery.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat warm, easily digestible, blood-nourishing foods like bone broth, red dates, goji berries, and dark leafy greens. Avoid cold, raw, or greasy foods. Rest as much as possible; lying down helps the uterus recover. Gentle postpartum exercises like deep breathing can help rebuild Qi without strain.

### Blood Stagnation in the Directing and Penetrating Vessels
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/blood-stagnation-in-the-directing-and-penetrating-vessels)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Dark purplish lochia with clots, Sharp, fixed lower abdominal pain, Pain worsens with pressure, Bleeding stops and starts, then pain eases after passing clots
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Cold weather or air conditioning, Cold foods and drinks, Prolonged sitting or lying still, Emotional stress
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warm compress on lower abdomen, Gentle walking, Warm ginger tea, Passing clots
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  After childbirth, the uterus and the Chong and Ren meridians are vulnerable. If lochia is not fully expelled, or if cold invades the lower body, the blood congeals and gets stuck. This blockage prevents the uterus from contracting properly, so bleeding drags on-dark, clotted, and intermittent-while the trapped blood causes sharp, fixed pain.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: purple or dusky body, possible stasis spots, engorged sublingual veins, thin white coat. Pulse: choppy (rough, uneven) and often wiry, especially at the left middle position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Cold makes blood congeal, which is exactly what causes this pattern-so warmth and warm foods directly counter the root problem by helping blood flow. Gentle movement keeps Qi and blood circulating, while staying still for too long lets stasis settle deeper. Emotional stress knots up Liver Qi, and since Qi moves blood, that knotting worsens the stagnation. Passing clots relieves the physical obstruction, which is why the pain eases afterward.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Keep the lower belly warm with a hot water bottle or a warm wrap. Eat warm, cooked foods like ginger, brown sugar, and lean meats; avoid cold drinks, raw salads, and icy foods. Gentle walking helps blood circulate, but skip strenuous exercise. Rest when tired, but don't lie in bed all day-some movement prevents stasis from settling.

### Empty-Heat caused by Yin Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/empty-heat-caused-by-yin-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Lochia is red or dark red, thick, sometimes malodorous, Five-palm heat (heat in palms, soles, and chest), Night sweats, Dry mouth and throat, Low-grade fever or feeling of heat in the afternoon
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Spicy or greasy foods, Hot weather or overheated environment, Overwork and lack of sleep, Emotional stress
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Cool, quiet environment, Sipping warm water, Yin-nourishing foods (pears, congee)
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Childbirth consumes a great deal of blood and Yin. When the body's Yin is too depleted, a low-grade "empty heat" can develop. This heat agitates the blood in the uterus, preventing the vessels from closing properly, so lochia continues longer than normal. The discharge tends to be red or dark red, thick, and may have a slight odor.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red, thin, dry body with cracks, little or no coating. Pulse: fine (thin) and rapid.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Yin deficiency is a state of cooling fluids being depleted. Spicy foods, heat, and overwork further consume Yin and stir up empty heat, making the bleeding worse. Cooling, resting, and eating moistening foods help rebuild Yin and calm the heat, allowing the vessels to close.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Prioritize rest and avoid overexertion. Eat cooling, Yin-nourishing foods like pears, black sesame, and congee. Strictly avoid spicy, greasy, or warming foods such as lamb, chili, and alcohol. Sip warm water throughout the day to ease dryness.

### Heat and Blood Stagnation in the Lower Burner
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/heat-and-blood-stagnation-in-the-lower-burner)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Purplish, sticky lochia with foul odor, Lower abdominal distension and pain that worsens with pressure, Mental restlessness or irritability, Fever that worsens at night, Dry mouth and throat
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Spicy or greasy foods, Emotional stress, Hot weather or overheated environment, Strenuous exercise too soon, Alcohol and coffee
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Cool compresses on lower abdomen, Light, non-greasy meals, Gentle walking, Rest in a cool environment
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  After childbirth, the uterus is vulnerable. If retained lochia or damp-heat from the environment invades the lower body, it can smolder and transform into heat, while the blood that should be expelled becomes stuck and forms stasis. Heat and static blood then obstruct the uterus and the Chong and Ren vessels, causing bleeding that is dark, sticky, and foul-smelling, with a heavy, painful sensation in the lower abdomen.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: reddish-purple body with stasis spots, distended sublingual veins, and a dry yellow coating. Pulse: deep, choppy, and rapid; the left chi position feels full and wiry.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Spicy, greasy foods and alcohol generate damp-heat, which fuels the fire and thickens the stasis. Stress and anger stir up liver fire, adding more heat to the blood. Cooling foods, compresses, and a cool environment help clear heat and calm the blood, while gentle movement encourages blood flow without depleting Qi. Rest allows the body to consolidate and heal, preventing further stasis.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Avoid spicy, greasy, and fried foods that can generate damp-heat. Eat cooling, blood-nourishing foods like mung beans, lotus root, cucumber, and plenty of fluids. Gentle walking after rest helps blood circulation without exhausting Qi. Keep the perineal area clean to prevent infection, and rest in a cool, well-ventilated room.

## คำถามที่พบบ่อย (Frequently Asked Questions)
### How long does it usually take for TCM to stop the bleeding?
Most women notice a change in the colour and amount of lochia within the first week of herbs and acupuncture. Bleeding typically tapers off over two to four weeks, though the exact timeline depends on the underlying pattern. Blood Stagnation patterns often clear fastest; Qi and Blood Deficiency can take a little longer because the body needs time to rebuild reserves.

### Can I take Chinese herbs while breastfeeding?
Yes, many postpartum herbal formulas are specifically designed to be safe during breastfeeding. In fact, they can support milk production by nourishing Qi and blood. However, it is essential to work with a qualified TCM practitioner who can select herbs appropriate for your pattern and avoid any that might pass into breast milk in amounts that could affect the baby. Always inform your practitioner that you are nursing.

### Is it safe to combine TCM with the medication my doctor gave me?
TCM can often be used alongside conventional medications, but coordination is critical. Herbs that move blood, such as Dang Gui and Yi Mu Cao, may increase bleeding risk if you are taking anticoagulants. If you have been prescribed antibiotics or uterotonics, tell both your doctor and TCM practitioner so they can adjust timing and dosages. Never stop a prescribed medication without medical advice.

### What if I pass large clots - should I be worried?
Passing small clots is common in Blood Stagnation patterns and may actually bring relief from pain. However, clots larger than a golf ball or any sudden increase in clot size can signal a more serious problem, such as retained placental tissue. In these cases, seek medical attention immediately. Your TCM practitioner can help you distinguish between normal stasis-related clotting and a red flag.

### Will acupuncture help my uterus shrink back to normal?
Yes. Acupuncture points such as Sanyinjiao (SP-6), Guanyuan (REN-4), and Zusanli (ST-36) are used to strengthen the Chong and Ren vessels, improve uterine contraction, and promote involution. Combined with herbs, acupuncture can accelerate the uterus's return to its pre-pregnancy size and help stop the bleeding more quickly.

### Do I need to change my diet during treatment?
Diet plays a major role in postpartum recovery from a TCM perspective. Focus on warm, nourishing foods like bone broths, congee, stewed meats, and dark leafy greens. Avoid cold, raw foods and icy drinks, which can constrict the uterus and promote stagnation. Spicy and greasy foods should be minimized as they can generate heat. Ginger tea with brown sugar is a classic postpartum drink that warms the uterus and supports circulation.

### Can TCM help if I had a C-section?
Absolutely. A C-section is a major surgery that depletes Qi and blood and can introduce cold into the lower body. TCM can be especially helpful in restoring energy, promoting healing of the incision, and addressing any lingering blood stasis from the surgery. The same pattern differentiation applies, and your practitioner will adjust herbs and points to account for the surgical recovery.
