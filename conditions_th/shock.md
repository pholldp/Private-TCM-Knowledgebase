## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนปัจจุบัน (Conventional Medicine View)
### คำอธิบาย (Description)
Shock is a life-threatening medical emergency where the circulatory system fails to deliver enough oxygen and nutrients to the body's tissues. This can happen due to severe blood loss (hypovolemic shock), heart failure (cardiogenic shock), overwhelming infection (septic shock), severe allergic reaction (anaphylactic shock), or spinal cord injury (neurogenic shock).
Common signs include dangerously low blood pressure, rapid heart rate, cold and clammy skin, confusion or loss of consciousness, and reduced urine output. Diagnosis is based on vital signs, blood tests, and identifying the underlying cause. Immediate treatment focuses on restoring blood flow and oxygen delivery with fluids, medications, and sometimes surgery.

### การรักษาหลัก (Treatments)
Conventional treatment depends on the type of shock but generally includes intravenous fluids to restore blood volume, vasopressor drugs to raise blood pressure, antibiotics for septic shock, blood transfusions for hemorrhage, and mechanical ventilation or oxygen support. The goal is to stabilize the patient quickly and treat the underlying cause.

## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนจีน (Traditional Chinese Medicine View)
### ความเข้าใจเกี่ยวกับการแพทย์แผนจีน (TCM Understanding)
In TCM, shock is understood as a severe disruption of the body's fundamental balance between Yin and Yang, Qi and Blood. It is not simply a circulatory failure but a crisis where the body's vital forces become disconnected. The classical term 'jue tuo' (厥脱) combines two ideas: 'jue' refers to a reversal of normal flow, where Qi moves in the wrong direction or becomes blocked, causing the limbs to turn cold and consciousness to fade; 'tuo' means collapse or exhaustion, indicating that the body's reserves have been depleted.
The core mechanism involves the Heart, Kidney, and Spleen systems. The Heart houses the Shen (spirit) and governs blood circulation; when Qi and Blood fail to reach the head, the person collapses. The Kidney is the root of Yang and Yin; if its fire is extinguished, the whole body turns cold. The Spleen produces Qi and Blood from food; if it is severely weakened, there is no source to replenish what is lost. Different patterns reflect which system is most affected and whether the problem is primarily a deficiency (not enough substance) or an excess (something blocking the flow).
For example, in Heat in the Blood, a violent infection stirs up internal fire that penetrates deep into the blood. This fire traps the Yang Qi inside, so the core burns with fever while the limbs feel icy cold - a classic 'true heat, false cold' presentation. In Empty-Cold, the body's warming Yang is simply too weak to reach the surface, so the whole body is uniformly cold. In Qi Collapse, the Spleen's ability to hold everything up fails, leading to sudden prostration and cold sweat. Blood Deficiency after heavy loss leaves the Heart and brain starved of nourishment, causing fainting. In the advanced stages, Yin or Yang may collapse entirely - Yin collapse brings hot, sticky sweat and a red mirror tongue, while Yang collapse brings icy limbs and a barely perceptible pulse.
Because shock can arise from such different internal landscapes, TCM does not offer a one-size-fits-all treatment. A formula that rescues Yang (like Shen Fu Tang) would be dangerous for a Heat in the Blood patient, just as cooling herbs would worsen an Empty-Cold crisis. This is why a rapid but precise pattern diagnosis is so critical - it determines whether the treatment must clear heat, warm yang, tonify Qi, nourish blood, or rescue Yin or Yang.

### การวินิจฉัยโดยแพทย์แผนจีน (Practitioner Diagnosis)
In shock, a traditional Chinese medicine practitioner first observes the person’s overall appearance, skin temperature, sweating, and level of consciousness. The tongue and pulse are then checked to confirm which pattern underlies the crisis. Because shock is a medical emergency, the diagnostic process is rapid and focused on the most striking signs.
If the limbs are cold but the face is flushed, the tongue is red with a yellow coat, and the pulse feels rapid and forceful, the picture points to Heat in the Blood. This pattern often arises when a severe infection drives intense internal heat, trapping it deep inside so the surface feels cold even though the body is overheated.
When the whole body is icy cold, the face is pale, and there is no hint of heat, the pattern is Empty-Cold. The tongue is pale with a thin white coat, and the pulse is deep and thready. This reflects a collapse of warming yang qi, leaving the body unable to generate heat, and is typical of shock from severe yang deficiency.
Qi Collapse shows up as sudden, profound weakness with spontaneous sweating and cold limbs. The tongue is pale with a thin white coat, and the pulse feels weak and rapid. A practitioner will ask whether the collapse followed extreme exertion or a sudden fright, because qi can scatter outward when the body is overwhelmed.
Blood Deficiency is suspected when shock follows a major hemorrhage. The skin and lips are strikingly pale, the tongue is pale with a thin coat, and the pulse is fine and weak. This pattern reflects the loss of the blood that anchors qi and nourishes the organs, leaving the body without its material foundation.
Collapse of Yin produces a different picture: the person may feel warm, restless, and severely dry, with a red tongue that has little or no coat. The pulse is thready and rapid. This happens when the body’s cooling, moistening yin fluids are exhausted, often in the later stages of shock.
Collapse of Yang is the most dangerous stage. The body is icy cold, covered in profuse cold sweat, and the pulse is barely perceptible. The tongue is pale and swollen. This terminal pattern demands urgent yang rescue, and the practitioner will look for the complete absence of warmth and the vanishing pulse to make the diagnosis.

### Heat in the Blood
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/heat-in-the-blood)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Facial flushing with cold extremities, Deep red tongue with dry yellow coating, Rapid, forceful pulse, Restlessness and irritability before collapse, Bleeding under the skin or from the nose
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Spicy or fried food, Anger or emotional stress, Hot, stuffy rooms
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Cool environment, Sipping cool water, Rest in a quiet, calm environment
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  In severe infections or inflammation, intense Heat penetrates deep into the Blood level. This internal fire overwhelms the body's cooling capacity and blocks the free movement of Yang Qi. Although the core burns with fever, the limbs turn cold because Yang Qi cannot reach them-a classic sign of Heat syncope.
The Heat agitates the Heart and disturbs the Shen (spirit), causing sudden collapse, restlessness, or loss of consciousness. It can also damage blood vessels, leading to bleeding that further drains Qi and Blood, deepening the state of shock.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: deep red body with red spots or prickles, thin yellow dry coating. Pulse: rapid (Shu), wiry (Xian), and forceful.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Heat in the Blood is an extreme internal fire that can block Yang Qi from reaching the limbs, creating the paradox of cold extremities with a flushed face. Cooling drinks and environments directly counter the Heat, while rest prevents emotional flare-ups that fan the fire. Spicy food and anger add fuel to the Heat, worsening the blockage and pushing the body deeper into shock.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Avoid all spicy, greasy, or fried foods that add internal Heat. Drink plenty of cool, clear fluids-chrysanthemum or mint tea can gently cool the system. Rest in a quiet, ventilated space and avoid emotional outbursts that stir Liver Fire.

### Empty-Cold
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/empty-cold)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Icy cold hands and feet that feel cold to the touch, Pale, puffy face and tongue with teeth marks, Deep, slow, weak pulse that is hard to find, Desire for warmth and warm drinks, worse with cold, Fatigue and listlessness, may faint after exertion or cold exposure
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Cold exposure, Overwork and exhaustion, Cold or raw foods and drinks, Emotional stress
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warmth applied to abdomen and feet, Lying down to rest, Warm foods and drinks
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  In Empty-Cold, the body's Yang Qi is too weak to generate warmth or sustain clear consciousness. When the warming force collapses, the limbs turn icy and the clear Yang fails to reach the head, causing sudden fainting. This is a form of cold syncope where the interior is deficient and cold, so the body cannot hold itself upright.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale, puffy body with scalloped edges and a thin, white, moist coating. Pulse: deep (沉 chén), slow (迟 chí), and weak (弱 ruò), especially at the rear positions.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Empty-Cold is a deep deficiency of Yang Qi, so anything that further drains warmth or energy-like cold, fatigue, or raw foods-pushes the body toward collapse. Conversely, warmth, rest, and nourishing foods gently feed the Yang, helping it rise and stabilize.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Keep the body warm, especially the abdomen and feet. Eat warm, cooked foods like soups, stews, and congees with ginger and cinnamon. Avoid raw, cold, and icy foods and drinks. Gentle movement like walking helps circulate Yang, but avoid exhaustion.

### Qi Collapsing or Qi Sinking
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/qi-collapsing-or-qi-sinking)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Sudden loss of consciousness, Cold extremities but not icy, Spontaneous, clammy sweating, Extreme fatigue and weak voice, Pale, puffy tongue with teeth marks, Weak, often rapid pulse that disappears under pressure
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Standing for long periods, Physical overexertion, Cold or raw foods and drinks, Emotional stress
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Lying down to rest, Warm foods and drinks, Moxibustion warmth
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Qi is the force that holds everything in place - organs, blood, and consciousness itself. When Spleen Qi becomes severely depleted, often after prolonged exhaustion, chronic illness, or sudden overwhelming stress, it can no longer lift and anchor the body's vital functions. The Qi sinks downward instead of rising to the head, leading to a sudden loss of consciousness, cold limbs, and a weak, fading pulse as the body's holding power fails.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: Pale, puffy, teeth-marked body with thin white coat. Pulse: Empty and weak, often rapid and thready, disappearing under pressure.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Qi sinking is a collapse of the body's upward-lifting energy. Lying down removes the fight against gravity, allowing Qi to gather back toward the head and chest. Warmth and easily digestible food support the Spleen's ability to produce and hold Qi, while raw coldness further drains that fire. Overexertion and prolonged standing force Qi downward, exactly the direction of this pathology.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Absolute rest is essential - lying flat helps Qi return upward. Eat small, frequent meals of warm, easily digested foods like congee with ginger. Avoid raw, cold, and greasy foods that further weaken the Spleen.

### Blood Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/blood-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Extreme pallor (face, lips, nail beds), Dizziness or light-headedness, Cold hands and feet, Heart palpitations, Fine, weak, threadlike pulse, Pale, thin tongue body
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Further blood loss, Overwork and exhaustion, Cold or raw foods and drinks, Emotional stress, Standing up too quickly
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Lying down with legs elevated, Warm, quiet environment, Iron-rich foods (meat, bone broth), Warm foods and drinks, Rest and sleep
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Blood is the material foundation that nourishes the heart, brain, and limbs. When a large amount of blood is lost-through injury, childbirth, or internal bleeding-the body’s blood volume plummets. This leaves the heart and brain without the nourishment they need to function, causing sudden collapse, extreme pallor, and cold extremities.
Without enough blood to fill the vessels, the pulse becomes fine and threadlike. The face, lips, and nail beds lose their rosy color, and dizziness or fainting occurs because clear Yang cannot rise to the head. This is a classic blood collapse pattern of shock.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: Pale, thin body with a thin white coating. Pulse: Fine (Xi), weak, and forceless, possibly choppy (Se).
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Blood is the material foundation for consciousness and warmth. Massive blood loss depletes blood, causing the heart and brain to lose nourishment, leading to collapse. Rest and lying down help conserve Qi and allow blood to return to vital organs. Warmth and nourishing foods support the Spleen to generate new blood, while cold and overwork further deplete Qi and blood. Emotional stress disturbs Qi flow, worsening the deficiency.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat warm, nutrient-dense foods like bone broth, red meat, dark leafy greens, and black sesame to rebuild blood. Avoid raw, cold, and greasy foods that weaken the Spleen. Rest completely and avoid any blood loss triggers. Gentle breathing exercises can help stabilize Qi.

### Collapse of Yin
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/collapse-of-yin)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Profuse hot sticky sweat that beads like oil, Burning hot body with warm extremities, Intense restlessness and agitation, Deep red, completely dry tongue with no coating, Extreme thirst with desire for cold drinks
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Excessive heat, Dehydration, Emotional agitation, Physical overexertion
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Cool environment, Sipping cool water, Rest in a quiet, calm environment
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  In the advanced stages of shock, the body's yin fluids-its cooling, moistening reserves-become utterly exhausted. Without yin to anchor yang, empty heat flares upward, causing the burning sensation, warm extremities, and intense thirst. The profuse, hot, sticky sweat is the last of the yin leaking out, while the deep-red mirror tongue and fine, rapid pulse signal that the body's fluid foundation has collapsed.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Deep red or crimson body, completely dry, no coating (mirror tongue), possibly cracked and shrunken. Pulse: fine, rapid, and lacking force, may feel scattered or hollow.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Yin collapse is a state of extreme fluid and cooling depletion. Heat, dehydration, and agitation further consume the last remnants of yin, accelerating collapse. Cooling measures and fluid intake help preserve yin and anchor yang, preventing the empty heat from flaring out of control.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Sip small amounts of cool water or electrolyte fluids frequently to replenish fluids. Avoid hot, spicy, or drying foods that further deplete yin. Rest completely in a cool, quiet environment. If able to eat, a thin congee with pear or lily bulb can gently nourish yin.

### Collapse of Yang
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/collapse-of-yang)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Profuse cold sweating that does not stop, Ice-cold hands and feet extending past elbows and knees, Ashen-white or greyish complexion, Blue or purple lips and fingernails, Loss of consciousness in severe cases, Extremely faint or absent pulse
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Cold exposure, Physical overexertion, Emotional stress, Cold or raw foods and drinks
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warmth and blankets, Complete rest, Warm foods and drinks, Moxibustion on lower abdomen
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Yang Qi is the body's warming and activating force. When it collapses entirely, the body loses its ability to stay warm, hold in fluids, and keep consciousness. This is the terminal stage of shock, where the vital flame flickers out, leaving icy limbs, cold sweat, and a pulse that is barely perceptible.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale, moist, flaccid body with a thin white slippery coating. Pulse: minute (wei) and on the verge of disappearing; at all positions it is extremely faint, deep, and may feel like a thin thread that comes and goes, or be completely impalpable.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: In Collapse of Yang, the body's warming Yang Qi is nearly exhausted. Cold environments further drain what little warmth remains, while warmth and blankets help conserve it. Rest prevents further Qi depletion, and warm fluids like ginger tea provide gentle Yang support. Moxibustion on points like Guanyuan directly infuses Yang Qi into the body, helping to reignite the vital flame.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: In the acute phase, this is a medical emergency requiring immediate hospital care. During recovery, eat warm, nourishing foods like congee with ginger, and avoid cold drinks and raw foods. Keep warm and rest completely.

## คำถามที่พบบ่อย (Frequently Asked Questions)
### Can TCM treat shock on its own?
No. Shock is a medical emergency that requires immediate hospital care. TCM is used as an adjunct to conventional treatment, not a replacement. In China, herbal injections like Shen Fu Tang are sometimes given in the ICU alongside standard resuscitation, but this must be done under strict medical supervision.

### What does a TCM practitioner do for someone in shock?
In an emergency setting, a TCM practitioner may apply acupuncture to specific resuscitation points like DU-26 (Renzhong) to help restore consciousness, or administer a prepared herbal formula such as Shen Fu Tang for Yang collapse. However, this is never a substitute for calling 911 and getting the person to a hospital.

### How can TCM help after I've been discharged from the hospital?
After the acute crisis is over, TCM can be very helpful for rebuilding strength and preventing future episodes. Herbal formulas and acupuncture target the underlying pattern that led to the shock - such as chronic Qi or Blood deficiency - and can improve energy, appetite, and overall resilience. Recovery typically takes several weeks to months.

### Is it safe to take Chinese herbs while I'm in the ICU?
Some Chinese herbs can interact with medications used in intensive care, such as vasopressors, sedatives, or anticoagulants. Always inform your ICU team and TCM practitioner about all treatments you are receiving. Never take herbs without the hospital's knowledge - some, like fu zi (aconite), require careful processing and dosing.

### Can acupuncture points be used to prevent fainting or shock?
For people who are prone to fainting (vasovagal syncope) or have a known pattern of Qi or Blood deficiency, regular acupuncture and moxibustion on points like Qihai REN-6 and Zusanli ST-36 may help strengthen the constitution and reduce the likelihood of collapse. However, this is a long-term strategy, not an emergency intervention.

### What are the warning signs that shock is coming back?
If you have recovered from shock and then notice symptoms like sudden dizziness, cold sweats, rapid heartbeat, or feeling faint, seek medical help immediately. Do not attempt to treat these with herbs or acupuncture alone. For a full list of red flags, see the Safety section below.
