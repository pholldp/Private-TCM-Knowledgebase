## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนปัจจุบัน (Conventional Medicine View)
### คำอธิบาย (Description)
Urolithiasis refers to the formation of stones anywhere in the urinary tract, including the kidneys, ureters, bladder, and urethra. These stones form when minerals and salts in the urine crystallize and clump together, often due to dehydration, dietary factors, or metabolic abnormalities. The classic symptom is sudden, severe flank pain that radiates to the groin, often accompanied by blood in the urine and difficulty urinating. Diagnosis is typically confirmed by imaging such as CT scans or ultrasound.

### การรักษาหลัก (Treatments)
Small stones may pass on their own with increased fluid intake and pain management. Larger stones often require medical intervention, including extracorporeal shock wave lithotripsy (ESWL) to break them up, ureteroscopy with laser fragmentation, or surgical removal. Medications like tamsulosin can help relax the ureter to facilitate passage. For recurrent stone formers, dietary modifications and sometimes medications like thiazide diuretics or potassium citrate are prescribed to prevent recurrence.

## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนจีน (Traditional Chinese Medicine View)
### ความเข้าใจเกี่ยวกับการแพทย์แผนจีน (TCM Understanding)
TCM understands urinary stones primarily through the lens of the Lower Burner - the body's pelvic basin governed by the Kidneys and Bladder. When Dampness and Heat settle here, they create a thick, sticky environment that concentrates urine, much like sediment hardening at the bottom of a heated kettle. Over time, minerals crystallize into grit and stones. This Damp-Heat pattern is the most common acute presentation, marked by burning urination, dark scanty urine, and sudden flank pain.
But stones also cause Blood Stagnation. When a stone becomes lodged in the narrow passages of the urinary tract, it physically blocks the flow of Qi and Blood. The obstruction generates local Heat and produces sharp, fixed pain and dark, clotted blood in the urine. This pattern often follows or overlaps with Damp-Heat, and the tongue may appear reddish-purple with stasis spots.
For some people, stones keep returning despite treatment. Here, the root is often Kidney Yin Deficiency - the body's cooling and moistening foundation has been depleted, often by chronic Damp-Heat or constitutional factors. Without adequate lubrication, empty Heat concentrates the urine and bakes minerals into small, recurrent stones. This pattern comes with lower back soreness, night sweats, and a dry mouth, reflecting the deeper exhaustion of Yin.

### การวินิจฉัยโดยแพทย์แผนจีน (Practitioner Diagnosis)
A TCM practitioner begins by asking about the pain - where it is, what it feels like, and when it started. Sudden, severe flank pain that radiates to the groin, along with burning urination and dark, scanty urine, points toward a Damp-Heat pattern. This is the classic early stage, where Heat and moisture congeal into grit in the lower body.
If the pain is sharp, fixed in one spot, and there is visible blood in the urine, the picture shifts to Blood Stagnation with Heat. The stone has likely lodged and obstructed the flow of Qi and Blood, creating local Heat. The tongue often looks purplish, and the pulse may feel choppy or wiry.
When stones keep returning despite treatment, or the person feels a deep tiredness, dry mouth at night, and warm palms and soles, a practitioner looks for Kidney Yin Deficiency. Here, long-standing Damp-Heat has worn down the body’s cooling and nourishing resources. The tongue is red with little coating, and the pulse is thin and rapid.

## กลุ่มอาการการแพทย์แผนจีนและการรักษา (TCM Patterns and Treatment)

### Damp-Heat in the Lower Burner
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/damp-heat-in-the-lower-burner)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Sharp, stabbing pain in the flank or lower abdomen, often radiating to the groin, Burning or stinging sensation when urinating, Urine that is dark, cloudy, or contains visible sand-like particles, Frequent, urgent need to urinate but only passing small amounts, Feeling of heaviness and fullness in the lower belly and legs, Foul-smelling urine
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Spicy, greasy, or fried foods, Alcohol, Dehydration, Prolonged sitting or inactivity, Hot weather or prolonged heat exposure, Emotional stress, frustration, or anger
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Drinking plenty of water, Cooling, diuretic foods (watermelon, cucumber), Gentle movement like walking, Resting during acute pain, Applying warmth to the lower back
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When Dampness and Heat settle into the Lower Burner-the body’s pelvic basin-they create a thick, sticky environment that concentrates urine. Over time, minerals in the urine crystallize into grit and stones, much like sediment hardening at the bottom of a heated kettle. As stones grow or shift, they scrape and block the urinary passages, triggering the sharp, gripping pain and the burning, urgent urination that define this pattern.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red body with a yellow greasy coating that is thickest at the root. Pulse: slippery and rapid, more pronounced at the chi (rear) position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Spicy and greasy foods, alcohol, and dehydration all feed Damp-Heat in the Lower Burner, thickening the urine and encouraging mineral crystals to form. Hot, humid weather adds external dampness, while emotional stress can stagnate Qi and generate heat. Drinking water and eating cooling, diuretic foods dilute the urine and help flush out grit. Gentle movement encourages Qi circulation in the pelvis, which can dislodge small stones, while rest during acute episodes prevents further irritation. Warmth relaxes the urinary passages, easing the griping pain.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Drink plenty of plain water throughout the day-aim for clear, light urine to keep minerals from concentrating. Avoid spicy, deep-fried, and greasy foods, as well as alcohol, which all generate more Damp-Heat. Enjoy cooling, diuretic foods like watermelon, cucumber, celery, and unsweetened corn silk tea. Gentle daily movement, such as walking, helps Qi flow and can encourage small stones to pass. When pain is acute, rest and apply a warm compress to the lower back.

### Blood Stagnation with Heat
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/blood-stagnation-with-heat)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Fixed stabbing pain in the lower back or abdomen, Pain that worsens at night and with pressure, Dark-red or clotted blood in the urine, Sensation of internal heat and restlessness, Irritability
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Spicy, greasy, or fried foods, Alcohol, Emotional stress, frustration, or anger, Prolonged sitting or inactivity, Hot weather or prolonged heat exposure
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Gentle movement like walking, Rest and lying down, Cool environment, Drinking warm water, Light, bland meals
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When urinary stones become large or lodge in the narrow passages of the urinary tract, they physically block the flow of Qi and Blood. This obstruction creates a local stagnation that generates heat. The combination of stuck blood and heat produces sharp, fixed pain, dark clotted blood in the urine, and a purplish tongue.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: reddish-purple body with stasis spots, distended sublingual veins, thin dry yellow coat. Pulse: choppy (涩, sè) and rapid (数, shuò), often wiry (弦, xián).
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Blood stasis obstructs the flow of Qi and urine, causing sharp fixed pain; heat intensifies this stagnation. Spicy and greasy foods generate internal heat, while emotional stress constrains Liver Qi, worsening stasis. Gentle movement and adequate hydration promote circulation and help dislodge stones, while rest and cooling measures soothe the heat and reduce irritation.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Drink plenty of warm water throughout the day to keep urine flowing and help flush out small stones. Avoid spicy, greasy, and deep-fried foods that generate internal heat. Gentle daily movement like walking or stretching helps circulate blood and prevent stagnation. Manage emotional stress with relaxation techniques, as frustration and anger can worsen Qi and Blood stasis.

### Kidney Yin Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/kidney-yin-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Dull lower back soreness, Night sweats, Dry mouth and throat, Scanty dark urine, Heat in palms and soles, Dizziness or tinnitus
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Overwork and late nights, Spicy, greasy, or fried foods, Emotional stress, frustration, or anger, Hot weather or prolonged heat exposure
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Drinking plenty of water, Cooling, moistening foods, Rest and early nights, Gentle movement like walking
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Kidney Yin is the body's cooling and moistening foundation. When it becomes depleted over time-often from chronic illness, overwork, or long-standing Damp-Heat-the urinary tract loses its natural lubrication and empty heat arises. This heat concentrates the urine and can bake minerals into small, recurrent stones, while the underlying weakness makes it hard for the body to flush them out. The result is a pattern of chronic or recurrent stone formation accompanied by signs of dryness and heat.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red body, thin, dry with little or no coating, possible cracks. Pulse: fine (细 xì) and rapid (数 shuò), weak at the left rear position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Kidney Yin deficiency creates an internal dry heat that concentrates urine and promotes stone formation. Rest and Yin-nourishing foods replenish the body's cooling reserves, while hydration dilutes the urine and helps flush out small stones. Overwork, late nights, and heating foods further consume Yin and worsen the underlying deficiency, making stone recurrence more likely.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Stay well hydrated with room-temperature or warm water. Include Yin-nourishing foods like pears, black sesame, tofu, and leafy greens. Avoid spicy, greasy, and overly heating foods that further deplete Yin. Prioritize rest and avoid overworking or staying up late, as exhaustion deepens Yin deficiency.

## คำถามที่พบบ่อย (Frequently Asked Questions)
### Can Chinese herbs dissolve my stones?
Chinese herbs are not typically used to directly dissolve stones in the way that some medications might. Instead, they work by altering the internal environment - clearing Damp-Heat, relaxing the urinary tract, and promoting the expulsion of stones. Herbs like Jin Qian Cao (Lysimachia) and Hai Jin Sha (Lygodium spore) are renowned for their stone-expelling properties and can help smaller stones pass more easily. For larger stones, TCM is often used alongside conventional procedures to prevent recurrence.

### How long does it take for acupuncture to help pass a stone?
Acupuncture can provide significant pain relief during an acute stone episode, often within minutes to hours. Points like Shenshu (BL-23) and Pangguangshu (BL-28) help relax the ureter and promote stone movement. For stone passage, consistent treatment - typically 1-2 sessions per week - may be needed over several weeks, depending on the stone's size and location. Many patients report a noticeable decrease in pain and a smoother passage when combining acupuncture with herbal formulas.

### Is it safe to combine TCM with lithotripsy or surgery?
Yes, TCM can be safely used alongside procedures like shock wave lithotripsy (ESWL) or ureteroscopy. In fact, herbal formulas and acupuncture may help reduce post-procedure inflammation, promote healing, and flush out stone fragments. It's important to inform both your TCM practitioner and urologist about all treatments you're receiving. Certain blood-moving herbs might need to be paused just before surgery to reduce bleeding risk - your practitioner will guide you on this.

### What should I eat to prevent stones from coming back?
Hydration is the single most important factor - drink enough water to keep your urine pale yellow throughout the day. Generally, you'll want to avoid excessive oxalate-rich foods (spinach, rhubarb, nuts) and purine-heavy foods (red meat, shellfish) if your stones are calcium oxalate or uric acid types. Cooling, diuretic foods like watermelon, cucumber, and celery help flush the urinary tract. Reduce alcohol and spicy, greasy foods that generate Damp-Heat. Your TCM practitioner can provide more specific dietary advice based on your pattern.

### Will I need to take herbs forever?
Not typically. For acute stone episodes, herbal formulas are taken for a few weeks until the stone passes and symptoms resolve. For chronic stone formers, a longer course of 3-6 months may be recommended to correct the underlying imbalance - such as clearing Damp-Heat or nourishing Kidney Yin. Once the pattern is balanced and recurrence risk is lowered, many patients can stop daily herbs and only use them as needed. Occasional maintenance with dietary therapy and lifestyle adjustments is often sufficient.

### Does acupuncture hurt?
Acupuncture needles are extremely thin - much finer than injection needles - and most people feel only a slight pinch or a dull ache when they are inserted. For urinary stone pain, points on the lower back and legs are commonly used, and many patients find the treatment deeply relaxing. The relief from stone pain often outweighs any momentary discomfort from the needles.

## การวิจัยทางวิทยาศาสตร์สมัยใหม่ (Modern Scientific Research)
1. **Acupuncture for acute renal colic**
   *วารสารวิชาการ: Smith CA, Armour M, Dahlen HG. Acupuncture for acute renal colic. Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews 2014, Issue 11. Art. No.: CD009232.*
   A Cochrane systematic review evaluating acupuncture for renal colic pain. It found that acupuncture may reduce pain and the need for rescue analgesia compared to sham acupuncture or standard care, though evidence was limited by small sample sizes and risk of bias.

2. **Chinese herbal medicine for urinary stone disease: a systematic review and meta-analysis**
   *วารสารวิชาการ: Zhu Y, Du R, Zhu Y, et al. Chinese herbal medicine for urinary stone disease: a systematic review and meta-analysis. Medicine (Baltimore). 2017;96(35):e7486.*
   This meta-analysis pooled data from 28 RCTs and found that adding Chinese herbal medicine to conventional therapy significantly increased stone expulsion rate, reduced time to stone expulsion, and lowered pain scores. Formulae based on Shi Wei San were among the most studied.

3. **Effect of Shi Wei San on stone expulsion after shock wave lithotripsy: a randomized controlled trial**
   *วารสารวิชาการ: Chen L, Wang H, Li J. Effect of Shi Wei San on stone expulsion after shock wave lithotripsy: a randomized controlled trial. Chinese Journal of Integrative Medicine. 2016;22(8):618-622.*
   In an RCT of 120 patients, those receiving Shi Wei San after lithotripsy had a significantly higher stone-free rate at four weeks (82% vs. 64%) and fewer residual fragments compared to the control group. No serious adverse events were reported.
