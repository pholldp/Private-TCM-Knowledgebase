## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนปัจจุบัน (Conventional Medicine View)
### คำอธิบาย (Description)
Cholera is an acute diarrheal infection caused by ingesting food or water contaminated with the bacterium Vibrio cholerae. It remains a global health threat, particularly in areas with poor sanitation. The hallmark symptom is profuse, painless, watery diarrhea often described as 'rice-water stool,' which can lead to severe dehydration and shock within hours if untreated. Diagnosis is confirmed by stool culture or rapid dipstick tests, and the cornerstone of treatment is immediate rehydration with oral rehydration salts or intravenous fluids.

### การรักษาหลัก (Treatments)
The primary treatment for cholera is rapid rehydration using oral rehydration solution (ORS) or intravenous fluids for severe cases. Antibiotics such as doxycycline or azithromycin can shorten the duration of diarrhea and reduce the volume of fluid loss, but they are secondary to fluid replacement. Zinc supplementation is also recommended for children to reduce the severity and duration of diarrhea.

## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนจีน (Traditional Chinese Medicine View)
### ความเข้าใจเกี่ยวกับการแพทย์แผนจีน (TCM Understanding)
In TCM, cholera is not seen as a single disease but as a sequence of patterns triggered by an invasion of external epidemic evils. These pathogenic factors - typically a mixture of dampness with either cold or heat - violently attack the Spleen and Stomach, the organs responsible for transforming food and fluids. When dampness overwhelms these organs, the normal downward flow of waste and upward flow of clear nutrients is thrown into chaos, resulting in the simultaneous vomiting and profuse watery diarrhea that defines the illness.
The nature of the invading evil determines the early pattern. If cold-damp predominates, the diarrhea is clear and odorless, the body feels heavy and cold, and the tongue develops a thick white coating. If damp-heat is the culprit, the stools are yellow and foul-smelling, there is burning thirst, and the tongue shows a greasy yellow coating. This distinction is critical because it guides the initial herbal strategy - warming and drying for cold-damp, cooling and drying for damp-heat.
If the vomiting and diarrhea continue unchecked, the body's fluids and vital energy can drain away completely. This leads to the collapse patterns: Collapse of Yin, where the cooling, moistening essence is exhausted, or Collapse of Yang, where the warming, activating force gives out.
These are life-threatening emergencies in both TCM and Western medicine. Once the acute storm passes, the patient often enters a recovery phase marked by Qi and Yin Deficiency, with lingering fatigue, dry mouth, and poor appetite - a state that TCM addresses with gentle, nourishing formulas.

### การวินิจฉัยโดยแพทย์แผนจีน (Practitioner Diagnosis)
A TCM practitioner investigating a cholera-like illness starts by asking about the diarrhea and vomiting: when they began, what they look and smell like, and what makes them feel better. The quality of the stools and vomit, along with thirst, temperature sensation, and the tongue and pulse, quickly point toward one of the distinct patterns that mark the disease’s progression.
In the early stage, if the diarrhea is clear, watery, and relatively odorless, and the person feels cold, craves warm drinks, and has a pale tongue with a thick white coating, the pattern is Cold-Damp invading the Spleen (寒湿困脾证). A slow, soft pulse (濡缓, rú huǎn) confirms the cold-damp blockage. By contrast, when the stools are yellow, foul-smelling, and accompanied by intense thirst, dark urine, a red tongue with a greasy yellow coating, and a rapid, slippery pulse (滑数, huá shuò), the pattern is Damp-Heat in Stomach and Spleen (中焦湿热证).
If the vomiting and diarrhea continue relentlessly, the body’s fluids become dangerously depleted. This leads to Collapse of Yin (亡阴证), where the skin is dry and wrinkled, the tongue is dry with a yellow or white coating, and the pulse feels rapid and thready (细数, xì shuò). The person may also experience leg cramps and a sunken appearance.
When the condition progresses into shock, the pattern shifts to Collapse of Yang (亡阳证). Here the face is pale, the limbs are cold, and a clammy sweat breaks out. The tongue is pale with a white greasy coating, and the pulse is deep and thready (沉细, chén xì). This is a critical emergency needing immediate warming resuscitation.
During recovery, lingering fatigue, poor appetite, and a pale, swollen tongue with a thin coating indicate Qi and Yin Deficiency (气阴两虚证). The pulse is thready and weak, reflecting the body’s slow rebuilding of energy and fluids after the acute crisis.

### Cold-Damp invading the Spleen
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/cold-damp-invading-the-spleen)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Profuse watery diarrhea (like rice water), Vomiting of clear fluids, Dull, cold abdominal pain, Heavy sensation in body and limbs, No thirst or preference for warm drinks, Fatigue, drowsiness, and feeling cold, Pale swollen tongue with thick white greasy coating
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Cold or raw foods and drinks, Dairy and greasy foods, Exposure to cold, damp weather, Physical overexertion, Irregular eating
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warm compress on abdomen, Sipping warm ginger tea, Light, easily digested warm foods, Complete rest, Moxibustion on lower abdomen
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Cholera begins when cold-damp epidemic evils invade the body and lodge in the Spleen and Stomach. These cold, heavy pathogens block the normal rising and descending of Qi in the middle burner, causing everything to rebel upward as vomiting and rush downward as profuse watery diarrhea. The Spleen's ability to transform fluids is overwhelmed, so the stool becomes clear and watery like rice water, while the body feels heavy, cold, and exhausted.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale, swollen body with teeth marks and a thick, white, greasy coating that is excessively moist. Pulse: soggy (Ru) or slippery (Hua) and slow (Chi), especially soft at the right middle position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Cold-Damp obstructs the middle burner like a cold, heavy fog that stalls the Spleen's digestive fire. Warmth and aromatic substances disperse this cold-damp fog, restoring the upward and downward flow of Qi. Cold, raw, or greasy inputs add more dampness and cold, thickening the obstruction and worsening the vomiting and diarrhea. Rest conserves the Spleen's Qi so it can focus on expelling the pathogen.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Keep the abdomen warm and avoid all cold, raw, or greasy foods. Sip warm fluids like ginger tea or light rice porridge to support the Spleen. Rest completely to conserve Qi while the body fights off the cold-damp evil.

### Damp-Heat in Stomach and Spleen
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/damp-heat-in-stomach-and-spleen)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Sudden, foul-smelling yellow or brown watery diarrhea, Vomiting of sour, bitter, or foul material, Intense thirst with a desire to drink cold fluids, Bitter or sticky taste in the mouth, Heavy, aching sensation in the limbs and head, Abdominal fullness and cramping pain, Scanty, dark yellow urine, Low-grade fever that does not clear with sweating
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Greasy, fried, or spicy foods, Dairy products and sweets, Hot, humid weather, Overexertion, Emotional stress and anger
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Cool, quiet rest, Sips of cool water or light herbal teas, Bland, easily digested congee, Loose, breathable clothing
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Cholera arises when damp-heat epidemic evils violently invade the Stomach and Spleen. The turbid heat and moisture churn inside the middle burner, forcing Qi to rebel upward as vomiting and downward as urgent, foul-smelling diarrhea. The rapid loss of fluids and the smoldering heat create intense thirst, while the sticky dampness leaves a heavy, sluggish feeling in the body.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red body, possibly swollen with teeth marks, thick yellow greasy coating especially at the centre and root. Pulse: soggy (Ru) and rapid (Shu), often slippery (Hua).
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Damp-heat thrives on moisture and heat, so greasy, sweet, and spicy foods fuel the internal fire and create more turbid dampness, while hot, humid weather mirrors the pathogenic evil and worsens symptoms. Rest and cooling fluids help disperse heat and settle the rebellious Stomach Qi. Bland congee gently supports the Spleen without adding burden, allowing the middle burner to recover and clear the lingering damp-heat.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: During the acute phase, small sips of cooled boiled water or oral rehydration solutions are essential to replace lost fluids. Avoid all solid food, dairy, greasy, spicy, and sweet items, which feed dampness and heat. As you recover, reintroduce bland, easy-to-digest foods like rice congee, steamed vegetables, and mung bean soup. Rest completely in a cool, well-ventilated room to let your Qi settle.

### Collapse of Yin
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/collapse-of-yin)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Hot sticky sweat that beads like oil, Burning hot body with warm hands and feet, Intense restlessness and agitation, Extreme thirst with desire for cold drinks, Dry, cracked lips and throat, Sunken eye sockets, wrinkled skin
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Heat exposure, Physical exertion, Spicy or drying foods, Emotional agitation
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Cool, quiet environment, Sipping cool water, Lying flat and resting, Cool compresses on forehead
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  In cholera, relentless vomiting and watery diarrhea drain the body's Yin - the cooling, moistening essence that nourishes tissues and anchors Yang. When Yin is critically depleted, it can no longer restrain Yang, allowing Empty Heat to flare upward. This creates a dangerous state of internal heat, extreme dryness, and restless agitation that can lead to shock if not reversed.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: deep red or crimson body, completely dry with no coating (mirror tongue), possibly cracked and shrunken. Pulse: fine, rapid, and weak, often racing or scattered.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Yin collapse is a crisis of fluid and cooling energy. Heat, activity, and emotional upset consume the last drops of Yin and fan the flames of Empty Heat, while coolness and rest help preserve what little Yin remains. Small sips of cool water gently replenish fluids without overwhelming the exhausted digestive system.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: This is a medical emergency - seek hospital care immediately. While awaiting help, offer small, frequent sips of cool water or oral rehydration solution. Keep the person lying down in a cool, quiet room with minimal stimulation. Avoid any hot, spicy, or drying foods and drinks.

### Collapse of Yang
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/collapse-of-yang)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Profuse cold sweating that does not stop, Ice-cold hands and feet extending past elbows and knees, Extreme desire to curl up under blankets, Watery diarrhoea with undigested food, Ashen-white or greyish complexion, Blue or purple lips and fingernails, Mental apathy or drowsiness, No thirst, or only desire for warm drinks
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Exposure to cold or drafts, Cold or raw foods and iced drinks, Overexertion, Emotional stress
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warm blankets and environment, Warm drinks like ginger tea, Absolute rest, Moxibustion on the abdomen
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  After relentless vomiting and watery diarrhea drain the body's fluids, the yang qi that keeps you warm and alive can give out completely. This collapse leaves the interior stone-cold, so the limbs turn icy, clammy sweat pours out, and the pulse fades to a thread. The body is essentially shutting down, and without urgent warming and yang rescue, shock deepens.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale, moist, flaccid body with a thin white slippery coating. Pulse: minute (wei), extremely faint, deep, or completely impalpable.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Warmth is the only thing that can revive collapsed yang - it pushes back the internal cold and coaxes the fading life-fire back. Cold, whether from the environment or food, douses that fire further, while rest conserves every scrap of qi. Stress and exertion drain what little yang remains, so stillness and gentle warmth are essential.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: This is a medical emergency - get immediate help. After stabilization, consume only warm, easily digested foods like rice congee with a little ginger. Keep the abdomen and limbs warm at all times, and avoid any raw, cold, or heavy foods that would further chill the middle burner.

### Qi and Yin Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/qi-and-yin-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Persistent tiredness and weakness after the acute phase, Poor appetite and weak digestion, Dry mouth with little desire to drink, Night sweats or spontaneous sweating
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Overexertion too soon, Cold, raw foods and iced drinks, Spicy or greasy foods, Emotional stress and worry
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Rest and gentle nourishment, Warm, easy-to-digest foods like congee, Mild hydration with warm water, Short naps and gradual return to activity
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  After the violent vomiting and diarrhea of cholera, the body loses both fluids (Yin) and the Spleen's driving energy (Qi). This leaves a lingering state where the Spleen cannot generate new Qi from food, and the depleted Yin fluids fail to moisten the mouth and tissues, resulting in persistent fatigue, dry mouth, and poor appetite during recovery.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale-red or red body with a thin or absent coating, possibly cracked and dry. Pulse: fine (thin) and weak, often rapid.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Rest conserves the small reserve of Qi the body has left, while warm, simple foods are gentle on a weakened Spleen and help it manufacture new Qi and fluids. Cold, raw, or heavy foods demand too much digestive fire and further damage the Spleen, while overexertion scatters Qi and deepens the deficiency. Emotional stress knots the Spleen Qi, slowing recovery.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat small, frequent meals of warm, easy-to-digest foods like rice congee, steamed vegetables, and well-cooked grains. Avoid raw, cold, or greasy foods that tax the weakened Spleen. Sip warm water or mild herbal teas throughout the day, and rest often to allow Qi to rebuild.

## คำถามที่พบบ่อย (Frequently Asked Questions)
### Can TCM cure cholera on its own?
No, TCM should not be used as the sole treatment for acute cholera. The rapid dehydration caused by cholera can be fatal, and modern oral rehydration therapy (ORT) or intravenous fluids are essential and life-saving. TCM herbs can be a valuable supportive treatment to reduce symptoms and speed recovery, but they must be used alongside proper rehydration, never as a replacement.

### What can TCM do for the weakness after cholera?
Many people feel drained for weeks or months after recovering from cholera, with poor appetite, fatigue, and a sensitive stomach. TCM excels in this recovery phase. Herbal formulas and dietary therapy aim to rebuild the Spleen's energy and replenish the fluids lost during the illness. With 2-4 weeks of tailored treatment, most patients see a significant improvement in their energy levels and digestion.

### Are there specific foods I should eat during recovery?
Yes, diet is a cornerstone of TCM recovery. Start with warm, bland, easily digested foods like rice congee. Gradually add well-cooked vegetables, small amounts of chicken, and grains like millet. Avoid all raw, cold, greasy, spicy, and dairy foods for at least two weeks, as these can overwhelm your weakened digestive system and cause a relapse. Your practitioner can give you a personalized list based on your specific pattern.

### Can I take TCM herbs with antibiotics for cholera?
Yes, they can usually be taken together, but it's best to separate them by at least two hours to prevent any potential interaction. Always inform both your doctor and your TCM practitioner about all medications and herbs you are taking. Some herbs, like licorice root, can affect fluid and electrolyte balance, so your practitioner may adjust the formula if you have underlying health conditions.

### How quickly can herbal treatment stop the diarrhea?
In the early stages, when the correct pattern is identified, a well-matched herbal formula can often reduce the frequency and severity of diarrhea within 24 to 48 hours. However, this is not a guarantee, and the most critical factor is maintaining hydration. If diarrhea persists or worsens, seek medical attention immediately.

### Is cholera always a 'hot' disease in TCM?
Not always. While many acute infections are seen as 'heat' in TCM, cholera can present as a cold-damp pattern, especially in cooler climates or in people with a constitution prone to cold. The telltale signs are clear, odorless diarrhea, a feeling of cold, and a thick white tongue coating. This distinction is important because the herbal treatment for cold-damp is warming and drying, very different from the cooling herbs used for damp-heat cholera.

1. **Chinese herbal medicine for acute infectious diarrhoea**
   *วารสารวิชาการ: Liu J, et al. Chinese herbal medicine for acute infectious diarrhoea. Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews 2013, Issue 7. Art. No.: CD009531.*
   Cochrane systematic review of randomized controlled trials assessing various Chinese herbal formulas for acute diarrhea. It included studies on Huo Xiang Zheng Qi San and found a trend toward faster symptom resolution, though evidence quality was limited by small sample sizes and risk of bias.

2. **Shengmai injection for septic shock: a systematic review and meta-analysis**
   *วารสารวิชาการ: Li M, et al. Shengmai injection for septic shock: a systematic review and meta-analysis. PLoS ONE. 2016;11(6):e0157153.*
   This meta-analysis pooled data from multiple RCTs and concluded that adding Shengmai injection to standard therapy improved mean arterial pressure and reduced mortality in septic shock, a condition that shares the TCM pattern of Collapse of Yin or Yang with advanced cholera.
