## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนปัจจุบัน (Conventional Medicine View)
### คำอธิบาย (Description)
Functional constipation is defined as difficult, infrequent, or incomplete bowel movements without an identifiable structural or biochemical cause. It is diagnosed using the Rome IV criteria, which include symptoms like straining, lumpy or hard stools, a sensation of incomplete evacuation, a feeling of blockage, and fewer than three spontaneous bowel movements per week. The condition is very common, affecting up to 20% of the population, and is more frequent in women and older adults.

### การรักษาหลัก (Treatments)
Standard treatment begins with lifestyle changes - increasing dietary fiber and fluid intake, and regular exercise. When these are insufficient, osmotic laxatives like polyethylene glycol or lactulose are often used to draw water into the bowel. Stimulant laxatives such as bisacodyl or senna may be recommended for short-term relief, while stool softeners like docusate can help with hard stools. For slow-transit constipation, prokinetic agents like prucalopride may be prescribed, and biofeedback therapy is a first-line treatment for dyssynergic defecation.

## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนจีน (Traditional Chinese Medicine View)
### ความเข้าใจเกี่ยวกับการแพทย์แผนจีน (TCM Understanding)
TCM sees constipation as a failure of the Large Intestine to transmit and excrete waste properly, but the root cause almost always lies in other organ systems. The Large Intestine depends on a steady supply of Qi from the Spleen and Lungs to push stool downward, on fluids from the Stomach and Kidneys to keep the passage lubricated, and on warmth from Kidney Yang to keep things moving. When any of these supports are weakened or blocked, constipation follows.
Excess patterns arise when something obstructs the bowel. Heat in the Large Intestine dries up fluids and bakes stools into hard, dry pebbles. Liver Qi Stagnation - often from stress or frustration - causes the Qi to jam up, creating a sensation of blockage and incomplete evacuation even when the stool is not particularly hard. These patterns tend to produce a more acute, uncomfortable constipation with signs of heat or tension.
Deficiency patterns are slower and more chronic. Spleen Qi Deficiency means the body simply lacks the energy to push, so you strain even with soft stools. Blood Deficiency and Yin Deficiency both lead to dryness - like a riverbed without enough water - producing hard, dry stools but with signs of malnourishment or heat from emptiness. Kidney Yang Deficiency is a deep internal cold that freezes the bowel's motion, causing difficult, often sticky stools with a feeling of chill and exhaustion.
This is why two people with functional constipation can have completely opposite needs: one may need cooling, moistening herbs, while the other needs warming, energizing ones. TCM treatment is not about forcing a bowel movement but about restoring the body's own ability to eliminate waste comfortably and regularly.

### การวินิจฉัยโดยแพทย์แผนจีน (Practitioner Diagnosis)
A practitioner starts by asking what the stool feels like and how the act of passing it plays out. The quality of the stool and the effort required are the first big clues. A stool that is dry, hard, and pellet-like points in a very different direction than a stool that is soft but still feels impossible to push out.
If the person complains of dry, hard stools along with thirst, a red face, and a feeling of heat, the practitioner looks for signs of Heat in the Large Intestine. The tongue will often be red with a dry yellow coating, and the pulse will feel rapid and slippery. These signs confirm that excess heat is drying up the fluids needed to lubricate the bowel.
When the main complaint is a sensation of incomplete evacuation, with bloating and belching that gets worse under stress, the focus shifts to Large Intestine Qi Stagnation. Here the stool may not be extremely dry, but the Qi is stuck and cannot descend properly. The tongue coating is usually thin and white, and the pulse feels wiry, like a guitar string.
A very different picture emerges when the person says they simply lack the energy to push. This suggests Spleen Qi Deficiency. The stool may be barely dry, but the person strains weakly and feels exhausted afterwards. The tongue is pale and puffy, and the pulse is weak. This pattern is about a lack of driving force, not a lack of fluids.
If the stools are dry and the person also looks pale, feels dizzy, or has heart palpitations, Blood Deficiency is likely. Blood fails to moisten the intestines, so the stool becomes dry, but the overall picture is one of pallor and thinness. The tongue is pale with a thin coat, and the pulse is thready and weak.
For dry, pellet-like stools that come with night sweats, a dry mouth at night, and a feeling of heat in the palms and soles, the practitioner thinks of Yin Deficiency. The body’s cooling, moistening resources are depleted. The tongue is red with little or no coating, and the pulse is thready and rapid.
When constipation is accompanied by cold hands and feet, lower back soreness, and an aversion to cold, Kidney Yang Deficiency is suspected. The warming fire that drives all movement is low, so the bowel becomes sluggish and cold. The stool may be dry or sticky, but the key is the coldness. The tongue is pale and swollen, and the pulse is deep and slow.

### Spleen Qi Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/spleen-qi-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Straining to pass soft stools, Bloating that gets worse after eating, Constant fatigue and lack of energy, Poor appetite, Shortness of breath on exertion
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Overthinking and worry, Raw, cold, or greasy foods, Overexertion, Damp, humid environments
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Rest and adequate sleep, Warm, cooked meals, Gentle abdominal massage, Regular meal times
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  The Spleen supplies the Qi that pushes food and waste through the intestines. When Spleen Qi is weak, that propulsive force is lacking, so bowel movements become sluggish and you may strain even when the stool is soft. The digestive system simply does not have the energy to move things along efficiently.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale, puffy, tender body with tooth marks and a thin white coating. Pulse: overall weak and soft, especially noticeable at the right middle (Guan) position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Overthinking, irregular eating, and cold foods directly weaken Spleen Qi, robbing the intestines of the energy needed to move stool. Rest, warm nourishment, and gentle movement support Qi production and circulation, allowing the bowels to function smoothly again.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Favor warm, well-cooked, easily digestible foods like soups, congees, and steamed vegetables. Eat small, regular meals and avoid skipping breakfast. Limit raw, cold, and greasy foods that tax the Spleen. Gentle daily movement, such as walking or tai chi, helps Qi flow without draining energy.

### Heat in the Large Intestine
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/heat-in-the-large-intestine)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Dry, hard stools that are difficult to pass, Burning sensation in the anus during or after bowel movement, Abdominal pain that worsens with pressure, Thirst with a desire for cold drinks, Afternoon tidal fever or feeling of heat, Dark, scanty urine, Restlessness and irritability
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Spicy, fried, or greasy foods, Alcohol and coffee, Emotional stress and anger, Hot weather or overheated rooms, Sedentary lifestyle
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Cooling foods (pear, cucumber), Drinking plenty of water, Light exercise, Cool environment
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When excess heat builds up in the Stomach and Large Intestine, it acts like a fire that boils away the body's fluids. This leaves the intestinal tract dry and the stools hard, like pebbles baked in the sun. The heat also creates pressure and pain in the abdomen, and the body tries to vent it through fever, thirst for cold drinks, and a burning sensation when passing stool.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red body with prickly papillae, thick dry yellow coating (may turn brownish in severe heat). Pulse: deep, rapid, and forceful; may feel slippery.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Excess heat is the root of this pattern, so anything that adds heat - spicy foods, alcohol, anger, hot environments - will intensify the drying and hardening of stools. Cooling, moistening foods and adequate hydration directly counteract the heat and replenish the fluids it has consumed. Gentle movement prevents Qi stagnation, which can trap heat, while a calm, cool setting helps the body's natural cooling mechanisms work without extra strain.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Avoid spicy, fried, and greasy foods as well as alcohol and coffee - these add heat and dry out the body. Instead, drink plenty of room-temperature water and enjoy cooling, moistening foods like pear, cucumber, banana, celery, and spinach. A gentle daily walk helps Qi move and prevents stagnation from feeding the fire.

### Large Intestine Qi Stagnation
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/large-intestine-qi-stagnation)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Cramping or colicky abdominal pain that shifts location, Sensation of incomplete bowel evacuation, Small, fragmented or ribbon-like stools, Excessive gas, belching and abdominal rumbling, Symptoms worsen with emotional stress, anger or frustration, Abdominal distension and bloating
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Emotional stress, anger or frustration, Sedentary lifestyle, Large, heavy meals, Cold or raw foods, Holding in gas or stool
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Passing gas or a bowel movement, Gentle exercise like walking, Abdominal massage, Warm compress on the belly, Emotional calm and stress relief
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  The Large Intestine relies on a smooth downward flow of Qi to move stool along. When emotional stress, frustration or a sedentary lifestyle cause Qi to become stuck, that downward movement stalls. The result is a bowel that feels blocked, with cramping, bloating and a frustrating sense that nothing comes out completely.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: normal light red body with a thin white coating, no significant markings. Pulse: wiry (弦 xián), especially noticeable at the left middle position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Qi stagnation is essentially traffic jam in the body’s energy highways. Emotional stress acts like a sudden brake, causing Qi to knot up and stop moving downward. Gentle movement, warmth and abdominal massage physically coax the Qi to flow again, while passing gas or stool releases the pressure that has built up. Cold and heavy foods further congeal the Qi, making the blockage worse, so warm, light and aromatic foods help keep things moving.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Gentle daily movement like walking or yoga is essential-it physically moves Qi in the abdomen. Eat warm, cooked foods and avoid raw, cold meals that can congeal Qi. Incorporate aromatic spices like fennel, ginger and peppermint to gently disperse stagnation. Most importantly, create space to decompress emotionally, as held-in tension directly tightens the bowel.

### Blood Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/blood-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Dry, hard stools, Pale or sallow complexion, Dizziness or lightheadedness, Heart palpitations, Scanty menstrual flow with pale blood, Poor memory or difficulty concentrating
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Overwork or prolonged stress, Heavy menstrual bleeding, Excessive dry, spicy foods, Chronic illness
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Rest and adequate sleep, Warm, nourishing meals, Gentle movement, Iron-rich foods
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Blood is the body's moistening and nourishing substance. When Blood is deficient, the Large Intestine lacks the lubrication it needs to move stool smoothly, leading to dry, hard stools that are difficult to pass. This often happens after heavy menstrual bleeding, prolonged illness, or overwork that depletes Blood. Because Blood also nourishes the brain and complexion, you may feel dizzy or look pale.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: Pale, thin body with a thin white coating. Pulse: Fine (Xi) and weak, like a thin thread.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Blood is produced from the Spleen's transformation of food and is easily depleted by overwork, stress, and heavy bleeding. Rest, nourishing foods, and moderate activity support Spleen function and Blood regeneration, which gradually restores intestinal moisture and normal bowel movements. Dry or spicy foods consume fluids, worsening dryness.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat blood-building foods like dark leafy greens, beets, dates, and moderate amounts of red meat. ### Yin Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/yin-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Stools are dry, hard, and often pellet-like, Night sweats, Warm palms, soles, and chest (five-centre heat), Dry mouth and throat, especially at night, Restlessness or feeling warm in the evening
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Spicy, fried, or dry foods, Late nights and overwork, Excessive sweating (e.g., sauna, intense heat)
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warm, moistening fluids (e.g., honey water), Cool, dark bedroom for sleep, Gentle, restorative exercise
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Your body relies on Yin - the cooling, moistening force - to lubricate the intestines and keep stools soft. When Yin runs low, the intestinal tract becomes dry and under-lubricated, like a riverbed losing its water. This causes stools to turn into hard, dry pellets that are difficult to pass. The same fluid shortage often creates a gentle internal heat (Empty Heat) that can show up as night sweats or warm palms and soles, further drying out the bowel.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red, thin body with cracks; little or no coating; dry surface. Pulse: fine (thin) and rapid.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Yin is the body’s coolant and moisture. Spicy or drying foods, late nights, and excessive sweating drain these precious fluids, making the bowel even drier. Moistening foods, rest, and gentle movement help conserve and rebuild Yin, restoring natural lubrication so stools can pass more easily.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat moistening, juicy foods like pears, apples, cucumber, spinach, and black sesame seeds; a spoonful of honey in warm water in the morning can help. Avoid drying foods such as spicy peppers, excessive coffee, alcohol, and dry baked goods. Prioritize good sleep and gentle exercise like walking or yoga - overwork and late nights deplete Yin further.

Gentle movement stimulates Qi flow without exhausting the Kidneys, helping the intestines to move.

Keep the abdomen and lower back warm with clothing or a hot water bottle. Gentle daily exercise like walking or tai chi helps move Qi without depleting energy. ## คำถามที่พบบ่อย (Frequently Asked Questions)
### Can Chinese herbs cause dependency like laxatives?
No, when prescribed correctly. The goal of TCM is to restore your body's own ability to move the bowels, not to force a bowel movement. Herbal formulas are adjusted as you improve, and the herbs that have a laxative effect (like Rhubarb) are used only in the short term for excess patterns. Once the underlying imbalance is corrected, you can stop the herbs without rebound constipation.

### Is acupuncture effective for constipation?
Yes, many studies and clinical experience show acupuncture can improve bowel movement frequency and reduce straining. Points like Tianshu (ST-25) and Zhigou (SJ-6) directly regulate the Large Intestine and promote peristalsis. For best results, acupuncture is usually combined with herbal medicine and dietary adjustments.

### How long does it take to see results?
Most people notice some improvement within 2-4 weeks. Excess-type constipation often responds faster, while long-standing deficiency patterns need more time to rebuild the body's reserves. Consistency with herbs and regular acupuncture sessions is key - stopping too early can allow the problem to return.

### Can I use TCM while taking my prescribed laxatives?
Yes, but under supervision. Your TCM practitioner will work with you to gradually reduce laxatives as your bowel function improves. Never stop prescribed medications abruptly. Because some TCM herbs also promote bowel movements, combining them with strong laxatives could cause diarrhea - always inform your practitioner about everything you are taking.

### What foods should I avoid for constipation?
In general, avoid cold, raw foods and iced drinks, which can constrict the digestive tract and weaken the Spleen. Spicy, greasy, and deep-fried foods can generate heat and dry out the intestines. If your constipation is due to cold or deficiency, raw fruits and salads may actually make it worse - your practitioner will give you specific guidance based on your pattern.

### Is constipation always a sign of heat?
Not at all. While Heat in the Large Intestine is a common cause, constipation can also be due to Qi stagnation, or to deficiency of Qi, Blood, Yin, or Yang. The latter types often involve no sensation of heat - instead you may feel cold, exhausted, or have a pale tongue. This is why self-treating with cooling laxatives can sometimes worsen the condition.

### Can stress cause constipation?
Absolutely. In TCM, stress and frustration cause the Liver Qi to stagnate, and since the Liver helps regulate the smooth flow of Qi throughout the body, this can jam up the Large Intestine. The result is often a sensation of incomplete evacuation, bloating, and cramping that gets worse with emotional upset. Acupuncture and herbs that move Qi are very helpful for this pattern.

## การวิจัยทางวิทยาศาสตร์สมัยใหม่ (Modern Scientific Research)
1. **Acupuncture for Chronic Severe Functional Constipation: A Randomized Trial**
   *วารสารวิชาการ: Liu Z, Yan S, Wu J, et al. Acupuncture for Chronic Severe Functional Constipation: A Randomized Trial. Ann Intern Med. 2016;165(11):761-769.*
   This randomized sham-controlled trial evaluated electroacupuncture in 1075 patients with chronic severe functional constipation. After 8 weeks of treatment, the acupuncture group had a significantly greater increase in mean weekly spontaneous bowel movements (from baseline 1.3 to 4.5) compared to the sham group (1.3 to 2.7). The effect persisted through 12 weeks of follow-up, demonstrating that acupuncture is a safe and effective therapy for severe constipation.

2. **Acupuncture for functional constipation: a systematic review and meta-analysis**
   *วารสารวิชาการ: Zhang T, Chon TY, Liu B, et al. Acupuncture for functional constipation: a systematic review and meta-analysis. Evid Based Complement Alternat Med. 2014;2014:637140.*
   This meta-analysis pooled data from multiple randomized controlled trials comparing acupuncture to sham acupuncture or conventional medication for functional constipation. The results indicated that acupuncture significantly improved bowel frequency, stool consistency, and quality of life scores with minimal adverse events. The authors concluded that acupuncture is a viable option, though they noted the need for larger, high-quality trials.
