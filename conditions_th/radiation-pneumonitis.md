## บทสรุปย่อ (Preview Summary)

## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนปัจจุบัน (Conventional Medicine View)
### คำอธิบาย (Description)
Radiation pneumonitis is an inflammation of the lung tissue that occurs as a side effect of radiation therapy, most commonly for cancers of the chest such as lung, breast, or lymphoma. It typically develops 1 to 6 months after treatment ends, though it can appear earlier or later. Symptoms include a dry cough, shortness of breath, low-grade fever, and a feeling of tightness or pain in the chest.
Diagnosis is usually made through a combination of imaging (CT scan showing ground-glass opacities in the radiation field) and by ruling out infection or tumor progression. The condition can range from mild and self-limiting to severe, and in some cases it progresses to radiation fibrosis - permanent scarring of the lung tissue.

### การรักษาหลัก (Treatments)
Standard treatment focuses on reducing inflammation. Mild cases may be managed with observation and cough suppressants. Moderate to severe cases are treated with corticosteroids such as prednisone, sometimes combined with immunosuppressive agents. Oxygen therapy is used if blood oxygen levels are low. For patients who develop fibrosis, antifibrotic drugs may be considered, though options are limited.

## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนจีน (Traditional Chinese Medicine View)
### ความเข้าใจเกี่ยวกับการแพทย์แผนจีน (TCM Understanding)
TCM sees radiation as a powerful heat toxin - a pathogenic factor that directly invades the Lungs. Unlike a common cold or infection, this heat is deep, penetrating, and intensely drying. In the acute phase, it scorches the Lung Qi and Body Fluids, leading to patterns of Lung Heat or Phlegm-Heat. The Lungs lose their ability to descend and disperse, causing fever, a harsh cough with thick yellow sputum, and chest oppression.
As the heat toxin lingers, it gradually consumes the Lung's Yin - the cooling, moistening essence that keeps the airways calm and lubricated. This shift from heat to dryness explains why many patients develop a persistent dry cough, night sweats, and a sensation of internal heat weeks or months after radiation ends. The tongue becomes red and dry, the pulse thready and rapid - classic signs of Lung Yin Deficiency.
In some cases, the heat enters the blood, making it thick and sluggish. This leads to Blood Stagnation in the chest, with sharp, fixed pain and even blood-tinged sputum. Over time, the Spleen and Kidneys - the body's foundation for Qi and fluid metabolism - can become depleted, resulting in profound fatigue, poor appetite, and a lingering, weak cough.
Each of these patterns represents a different stage and depth of injury, which is why TCM treatment must be tailored to the individual's current presentation.

### การวินิจฉัยโดยแพทย์แผนจีน (Practitioner Diagnosis)
A TCM practitioner begins by asking when the lung symptoms started in relation to the radiotherapy and what they feel like. The timing is crucial because radiation pneumonitis often evolves through stages, from an acute inflammatory phase to a later chronic phase. The quality of the cough, the colour and amount of sputum, and the presence of fever or chest pain all help narrow down which pattern is dominant.
If the onset is early and abrupt, with a fever, a harsh cough, yellow sputum, thirst, and a sensation of heat and oppression in the chest, the picture points to Lung Heat. In this pattern, radiation acts as a heat toxin that directly invades the Lungs, and the tongue is typically red with a thin yellow coating while the pulse feels rapid and forceful.
When the heat toxin intensifies and scorches body fluids into phlegm, the pattern shifts toward Phlegm-Heat in the Lungs. Here the sputum becomes profuse, thick, and yellow, and chest pain may appear. The tongue coating turns greasy yellow, and the pulse feels slippery and rapid - a sign that phlegm and heat are obstructing the Lung’s ability to descend and disperse.
As the acute heat begins to subside or if the person’s constitution is already weakened, the pattern often moves into Lung Yin Deficiency. The cough becomes dry and irritating, with scanty or no sputum, and there may be shortness of breath, night sweats, and a dry throat. The tongue looks red with little or no coating, and the pulse is thready and rapid, reflecting the consumption of Lung Yin by the heat toxin.
In some cases, the heat lingers and damages the blood vessels, leading to Blood Stagnation with Heat. This is more common later in the course, with chest pain that is fixed and stabbing, and sputum that may be streaked with blood. The tongue appears dark red with purple spots, and the pulse is choppy or wiry. This pattern suggests that stasis has developed, which can contribute to fibrosis.
When the illness is prolonged and the body’s core energy is drained, the pattern may involve Spleen and Kidney Qi Deficiency. The main clues are profound fatigue, poor appetite, loose stools, and a pale, swollen tongue with a thin white coating. The pulse is deep and thready. Here the root is weak, and the focus shifts to supporting the body’s vital Qi rather than just clearing pathogens.

### Lung Heat
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/lung-heat)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Cough with thick yellow sputum, Fever or feeling of body heat, Thirst with desire for cold drinks, Sore throat with redness, Chest tightness or pain, Flaring of the nostrils
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Spicy or fried foods, Exposure to heat or smoke, Emotional stress or anger, Overexertion, Alcohol and coffee
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Cool, fresh air, Drinking plenty of fluids, Rest and avoiding exertion, Cooling foods like pear juice, Avoiding smoke and irritants
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Radiation is a powerful heat toxin that directly invades the Lungs. It scorches the Lung Qi and Body Fluids, causing acute inflammation, fever, and a cough with thick yellow phlegm. The Lung's descending function is disrupted, leading to chest tightness and rapid breathing.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: Red body with a yellow, dry coating; prickly papillae may appear on the front third. Pulse: Rapid (shu) and slippery (hua) or overflowing (hong), especially forceful at the right cun position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Radiation is a heat toxin that depletes Yin and fluids. Cooling foods and drinks help clear heat and replenish fluids, while spicy and fried foods add more heat, worsening inflammation. Rest conserves Qi and allows the body to recover, while overexertion further damages Lung Qi. Emotional stress stirs up internal heat, aggravating the condition. Fresh air and avoiding irritants reduce the burden on the already inflamed Lungs.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat cooling, moistening foods such as pear, watermelon, cucumber, and tofu. Avoid spicy, greasy, and fried foods that generate internal heat. Stay hydrated with warm water or chrysanthemum tea. Rest in a cool, well-ventilated room and avoid smoke or irritants.

### Phlegm-Heat in the Lungs
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/phlegm-heat-in-the-lungs)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Copious thick yellow sticky sputum, Chest oppression and fullness, Rib-side pain when coughing, Wheezing or gurgling sounds in throat, Slippery rapid pulse, Red tongue with yellow greasy coating
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Greasy, fried foods, Spicy foods and alcohol, Stress and frustration, Hot, stuffy environments, Overexertion
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Light, easily digestible meals, Rest and lying semi-upright, Gentle breathing exercises, Warm pear juice, Cool, well-ventilated room
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Radiation acts as a fierce heat toxin that scorches Lung fluids into thick, sticky phlegm. This phlegm combines with the remaining heat, creating a hot, obstructive mass that clogs the airways and weighs down the chest. As the Lungs lose their ability to descend and disperse Qi, the classic picture of radiation pneumonitis emerges: a rattling cough with copious yellow sputum, labored breathing, and a feeling of heat and fullness in the chest.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red body, possibly swollen at front, yellow greasy coating, may have red prickles on front third. Pulse: slippery and rapid (滑数 huá shuò).
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Greasy, spicy, and fried foods generate dampness and heat, which directly thicken phlegm and aggravate the blockage in the Lungs. Stress stirs up Liver Qi, which can transform into fire and further scorch the Lung fluids. Cooling foods and rest help the body clear heat and resolve phlegm, while gentle breathing exercises encourage the Lungs to descend Qi and expel the sticky phlegm. A cool environment prevents additional heat from accumulating, making it easier for the Lungs to recover.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Avoid greasy, fried, and spicy foods, as well as dairy products, which tend to generate phlegm and heat. Focus on light, easily digestible meals with plenty of cooked vegetables and pear juice to moisten the Lungs. Gentle breathing exercises and short walks can help keep the chest open, but rest when fatigued.

### Lung Yin Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/lung-yin-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Dry cough with little or no phlegm, Dry mouth and throat, Night sweats, Afternoon tidal heat or low-grade fever, Hoarse or weak voice, Flushed cheeks in the afternoon
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Spicy, fried, or barbecued foods, Smoking or dry, smoky environments, Overwork and late nights, Emotional stress and frustration
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Cool, humidified air, Sipping warm water or pear juice, Afternoon rest, Eating white fungus or lily bulb soup
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Radiation is a fierce heat toxin that directly scorches the Lung's delicate tissues. Over the course of treatment, this heat gradually consumes the Lung's Yin-the cooling, moistening fluids that keep the airways calm and lubricated. As Yin runs dry, the Lung loses its ability to soothe and hydrate itself, leading to the persistent dry cough, scanty phlegm, and night sweats characteristic of this later-stage pattern.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: Red, dry body with little or no coating, often thin and cracked, especially in the front (Lung area). Pulse: Thready and rapid, particularly weak at the right cun (inch) position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Yin is the body's cooling, moistening substance. When it is depleted by radiation's heat toxin, any additional source of heat or dryness-such as spicy food, smoke, or overexertion-further consumes precious fluids, worsening the cough and night sweats. Cooling, moistening foods and rest help replenish Yin, calm the internal heat, and soothe the dry Lung tissue, allowing the cough to settle.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Focus on moistening, Yin-nourishing foods such as pear, white fungus soup, lily bulb, and tofu. Sip room-temperature water throughout the day. Avoid spicy, fried, or barbecued foods, as well as alcohol and coffee, which further dry out the body. Gentle breathing exercises and a short afternoon rest can help conserve Yin and reduce the evening heat sensation.

Cooling, calming, and gentle movement help disperse heat and keep blood flowing smoothly, breaking the cycle of pain and inflammation.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Focus on cooling, blood-nourishing foods like spinach, celery, and mulberries. Avoid spicy, fried, and greasy foods that generate more heat. Gentle walking or tai chi can help move qi and blood without overexertion. Stress management is crucial, as emotional tension can worsen blood stasis.

### Spleen and Kidney Qi Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/spleen-and-kidney-qi-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Chronic fatigue and lack of strength, Poor appetite and loose stools, Lower back and knee soreness or weakness, Cough with clear or white sputum (not yellow), Shortness of breath that worsens on exertion, Feeling cold, especially hands and feet, Pale or sallow complexion
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Overexertion and fatigue, Cold, raw foods, Exposure to cold, Stress and worry
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Rest and warmth, Warm, cooked meals, Gentle exercise like walking, Keeping the lower back warm
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Radiation is a heat toxin that, over time, damages the body's Qi. When the Spleen and Kidneys become depleted, the Spleen can no longer efficiently transform food into Qi and fluids, leading to dampness and phlegm that settle in the Lungs. The Kidneys lose their ability to grasp Qi, so breathing becomes shallow and labored. This creates a lingering, low-energy respiratory picture with chronic cough and profound fatigue.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale, swollen with teeth marks, and a white moist coating. Pulse: deep, weak, and thready.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: This pattern is a deep deficiency of Spleen and Kidney Qi, so the body lacks the energy to recover. Rest and warmth help conserve and build Qi, while overexertion and cold drain it further. Warm, nourishing foods support the Spleen's ability to transform food into Qi, whereas raw, cold foods damage Spleen Yang and worsen fatigue and loose stools.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat warm, cooked, easily digestible foods like soups, stews, and congee. Avoid raw, cold, and greasy foods that burden the Spleen. Gentle activities like walking or tai chi help build Qi without depleting it. Prioritize rest and keep the lower back warm.

## คำถามที่พบบ่อย (Frequently Asked Questions)
### Can TCM prevent radiation pneumonitis?
Yes, many patients start TCM at the beginning of radiotherapy to protect the lungs. Herbs that nourish Yin and clear heat can reduce the inflammatory response and lessen the likelihood of severe pneumonitis. Studies suggest that integrating TCM during radiation can lower the incidence and severity of lung injury.

### Is it safe to take TCM herbs alongside corticosteroids?
Generally, yes. TCM herbs can complement steroid treatment by addressing the root imbalance and may help reduce the dose of steroids needed over time. However, you must inform both your oncologist and TCM practitioner about all medications. Some herbs, particularly those that move blood, may interact with anticoagulants. Coordination is essential.

### How long does it take to see results with TCM?
Most patients notice improvement in cough and breathing within 2 to 4 weeks of starting herbs and acupuncture. Acute symptoms like fever and thick phlegm often clear faster. Chronic issues like dry cough and fatigue may take 4 to 8 weeks or longer, as the goal is to rebuild the body's reserves, not just suppress symptoms.

### What foods should I eat or avoid with radiation pneumonitis?
Focus on cooling, moistening foods that support the Lungs: pear, white fungus, lily bulb, tofu, radish, and honey. Drink plenty of warm fluids. Avoid spicy, greasy, or deep-fried foods, as they generate heat and phlegm. Alcohol and smoking are especially harmful and should be avoided completely.

### Can acupuncture help with the shortness of breath?
Yes. Acupuncture points on the Lung and Kidney channels can help relax the airways, improve oxygen exchange, and strengthen the breath. Many patients feel immediate relief after a session, and regular treatments build lasting improvement in respiratory function.

### Will TCM interfere with my cancer treatment?
When properly prescribed, TCM does not interfere with radiation or chemotherapy. In fact, it is often used to mitigate side effects and improve quality of life. However, it is crucial that your TCM practitioner is experienced in oncology and communicates with your medical team to avoid any herb-drug interactions.
