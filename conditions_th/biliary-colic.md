## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนปัจจุบัน (Conventional Medicine View)
### คำอธิบาย (Description)
In Western medicine, biliary colic is typically caused by gallstones that temporarily block the cystic duct or common bile duct. The gallbladder contracts against the obstruction, causing sudden, severe pain in the right upper abdomen, often radiating to the right shoulder or back. Episodes commonly follow a fatty meal and may last from minutes to hours. Diagnosis is usually confirmed with an abdominal ultrasound, and blood tests may show signs of inflammation or obstruction.

### การรักษาหลัก (Treatments)
Conventional treatment focuses on pain relief during acute attacks-often with NSAIDs or antispasmodics-and long-term management through dietary changes to avoid fatty foods. Because gallstones tend to recur, the definitive treatment is surgical removal of the gallbladder (cholecystectomy), most often performed laparoscopically. In cases of infection or severe inflammation, antibiotics or emergency surgery may be required.

## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนจีน (Traditional Chinese Medicine View)
### ความเข้าใจเกี่ยวกับการแพทย์แผนจีน (TCM Understanding)
In TCM, the Gallbladder is paired with the Liver, and together they govern the smooth flow of Qi and bile throughout the body. The Gallbladder's main job is to store and release bile, but it can only do this when the Liver's energy is flowing freely. When that flow is disrupted-by emotional stress, a rich diet, or an external pathogen-Qi and bile stagnate, creating the pressure and cramping pain of biliary colic.
The specific quality of the pain tells us which pattern is dominant. A distending, pressure-like ache that comes and goes with stress points to Liver Qi Stagnation. A sharp, cramping pain with a bitter taste and nausea after a fatty meal signals Damp-Heat clogging the Gallbladder. When the pain becomes fixed and stabbing, Blood Stagnation has set in. And when the pain is accompanied by high fever and yellowing skin, the condition has escalated to dangerous Toxic-Heat.
This is why TCM does not treat all biliary colic the same way. Each pattern requires a different strategy-soothing the Liver, clearing Damp-Heat, invigorating Blood, or cooling Toxic-Heat-to not only relieve the current attack but also prevent future ones by correcting the root imbalance.

### การวินิจฉัยโดยแพทย์แผนจีน (Practitioner Diagnosis)
When a person arrives with right-sided upper abdominal pain, a TCM practitioner first asks about the quality and triggers of the pain. A distending, pressure-like ache that comes and goes with stress and emotional upset points strongly toward Liver Qi Stagnation. The discomfort often radiates toward the shoulder and feels like a balloon inflating under the ribs. The tongue usually shows a pale-red body with a thin white coating, and the pulse feels wiry, like a guitar string - a hallmark of constrained Liver energy.
If the pain is sharp, cramping, and severe - often after a fatty meal - and is accompanied by a bitter taste in the mouth, nausea, and a yellowish tint to the eyes or skin, the picture shifts to Liver and Gallbladder Damp-Heat. This pattern feels hot and heavy, not just stuck. The tongue becomes red with a thick, greasy yellow coating, and the pulse is rapid and slippery. The practitioner will ask about bowel movements, as loose, urgent stools with a burning sensation further confirm the damp-heat diagnosis.
When the pain has been present for a long time or recurs frequently, the nature changes to a fixed, stabbing sensation that worsens at night. This suggests Liver Blood Stagnation - a deeper level of blockage where the Qi stagnation has led to poor blood flow. The tongue often looks purplish or has dark spots, and the pulse feels choppy or wiry and hesitant. The pain is no longer just distending; it feels like a needle pricking a specific spot, and pressing on it makes it worse.
A sudden, extremely intense pain with high fever, chills, and deep yellow jaundice signals a dangerous progression to Toxic-Heat. This is a medical emergency in both Western and Chinese medicine. The tongue appears deep red or scarlet with a dry yellow or even black coating, and the pulse is rapid and forceful. The practitioner will urgently assess for signs of systemic toxicity, as this pattern indicates severe inflammation and infection that can rapidly worsen.
In older individuals or those with a long history of gallstone issues, a dull, persistent ache that is worse in the afternoon or evening, accompanied by a dry mouth, low-grade fever, and dry eyes, points to Liver Yin Deficiency. Here the pain is not from excess blockage but from a lack of nourishment - the “riverbed” of the Liver channel is dry. The tongue is red with little or no coating, perhaps with cracks, and the pulse is thin and rapid. The practitioner will look for signs of chronic depletion, like night sweats and poor sleep.

## กลุ่มอาการการแพทย์แผนจีนและการรักษา (TCM Patterns and Treatment)

### Liver Qi Stagnation
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/liver-qi-stagnation)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Distending pain in the right upper abdomen, Pain that fluctuates with emotional stress, Frequent sighing or feeling of needing a deep breath, Irritability, short temper, or mood swings, Feeling of a lump in the throat (plum pit sensation), Belching or bloating after meals
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Emotional stress, anger, or frustration, Greasy or fried foods, Alcohol, Prolonged sitting or inactivity
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Gentle movement or stretching, Deep breathing and sighing, Emotional calm and stress relief, Warm compress on the abdomen or right ribs, Peppermint or chamomile tea
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Your Liver is responsible for keeping Qi flowing smoothly throughout your body. When stress, frustration, or emotional upset build up, that smooth flow stalls-this is what we call Liver Qi Stagnation. Because the Liver and Gallbladder are paired, stuck Liver Qi directly affects the Gallbladder's ability to release bile, causing it to back up and create pressure. That pressure turns into the distending, cramping pain you feel in your right upper abdomen.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: normal or slightly dark body, with red or slightly purple sides and a thin white coat. Pulse: wiry (弦 xián), taut like a guitar string, especially noticeable at the left middle position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Stress and anger are the main culprits that cause Liver Qi to stagnate in the first place, which directly impacts the Gallbladder's ability to release bile. Greasy foods and alcohol overload the Gallbladder and worsen the stagnation. Gentle movement and deep breathing help move stagnant Qi, while warmth relaxes the Liver channel and eases the distension. Peppermint and chamomile have a light, dispersing nature that helps release constrained Liver Qi.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat smaller, lighter meals and strictly avoid greasy, fried, or fatty foods that can trigger an attack. Gentle daily movement like walking or stretching helps keep Liver Qi flowing. Manage stress with deep breathing, and consider sipping peppermint or chamomile tea to soothe the Liver.

### Liver and Gallbladder Damp-Heat
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/liver-and-gallbladder-damp-heat)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Severe colicky pain in the right upper abdomen, Bitter taste in the mouth, Nausea and vomiting, Yellowing of the skin or eyes (jaundice), Thick, yellow, greasy tongue coating
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Greasy or fried foods, Alcohol, Spicy food, Hot, humid weather, Overwork or late nights
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Light, bland meals, Cool water or chrysanthemum tea, Rest in a cool room, Bitter greens (dandelion, chicory)
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When the Liver and Gallbladder become overwhelmed by Damp-Heat - often from a rich, greasy diet or alcohol - the excess Heat and Dampness clog the flow of Qi and bile. This stagnation creates intense, cramping pain in the right upper abdomen, often radiating to the shoulder. The Heat rises, causing a bitter taste and nausea, while the Dampness overflows into the skin and urine, leading to jaundice and dark, scanty urine.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red body, red sides, with a thick yellow greasy coating. Pulse: wiry, slippery, and rapid (弦滑数 xián huá shuò).
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Greasy, fried, and spicy foods, along with alcohol, generate internal Damp-Heat that directly clogs the Gallbladder and triggers pain. Hot, humid weather adds external Damp-Heat, making the condition worse. Light, cooling foods and bitter greens help the body clear Heat and drain Dampness downward. Rest allows the body to redirect energy toward healing and reducing inflammation.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Avoid greasy, fried, and spicy foods, as well as alcohol and rich dairy products, which fuel Damp-Heat. Eat light, easily digestible meals with plenty of leafy greens and bitter vegetables like dandelion or chicory. Stay hydrated with cool water or mild herbal teas such as chrysanthemum. Rest in a cool, well-ventilated room during acute episodes.

### Liver Blood Stagnation
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/liver-blood-stagnation)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Fixed, stabbing pain in the right upper abdomen, Pain worse at night, Pain worse with pressure, Dark purple tongue with stasis spots, Wiry and choppy pulse
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Greasy or fried foods, Emotional stress, anger, or frustration, Cold foods and drinks, Nighttime
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Gentle movement or stretching, Warm compress on the abdomen or right ribs, Rest or sleep, Small, light meals
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When Liver Qi stagnates for a long time, it loses its ability to move Blood through the channels. The Blood congeals and forms stasis in the Liver and Gallbladder pathway, producing a fixed, stabbing pain in the right upper abdomen. This pattern often follows repeated milder attacks of distending pain and is a deeper, more entrenched form of biliary colic.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: dark purple body with stasis spots on the sides, distended sublingual veins, thin white coat. Pulse: wiry (弦 xián) and choppy (涩 sè), like a knife scraping bamboo.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Blood stasis obstructs the Liver and Gallbladder channels, causing fixed, stabbing pain that worsens with pressure and at night when circulation slows. Emotional stress aggravates Qi stagnation, the root of the blood stasis. Greasy foods overburden the Liver and Gallbladder, worsening the stagnation. Warmth and gentle movement promote blood circulation and help dissolve stasis, while rest allows the body to focus on healing.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Avoid greasy, fried, and rich foods that burden the Liver and Gallbladder. Eat small, frequent meals with plenty of lightly cooked vegetables. Gentle exercise like walking or tai chi helps keep Blood moving. Manage stress with relaxation techniques, as emotional upset can worsen stasis.

### Toxic-Heat
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/toxic-heat)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Intense, constant burning pain in the right upper abdomen, High fever (often with chills), Yellowing of the skin or eyes (jaundice), Extreme thirst with desire for cold drinks, Dark, scanty urine and constipation, Restlessness and irritability
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Greasy or fried foods, Alcohol, Spicy food, Emotional stress, anger, or frustration
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Cool, quiet environment, Rest or sleep, Sips of cool water
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When Damp-Heat in the Gallbladder is not cleared, it intensifies into a blazing Toxic-Heat. This extreme internal fire inflames the organ, causing intense burning pain and obstructing the flow of bile. The toxins spread through the body, producing a high fever and the yellowing of skin and eyes known as jaundice.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red or crimson body with prickly thorns and a dry yellow coating. Pulse: rapid (数 shuò) and wiry (弦 xián), forceful.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Fatty, greasy, and spicy foods generate dampness and heat, directly feeding the internal fire and worsening the obstruction. Alcohol is pure heat in nature and will intensify the toxic blaze. Emotional stress constrains Liver Qi, which transforms into more fire and aggravates the gallbladder. Rest and cooling fluids help the body conserve its defensive Qi and drain some of the excess heat, while a cool environment prevents external heat from adding to the internal crisis.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: This is a medical emergency-seek immediate hospital care. While awaiting treatment, avoid all fatty, greasy, spicy, or rich foods. Sip small amounts of cool water or mung bean soup to help lower internal heat. Complete rest in a cool, calm environment is essential.

### Liver Yin Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/liver-yin-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Dull burning pain under the right ribs, Dry mouth and gritty eyes, Afternoon low-grade fever or night sweats, Dizziness and light-headedness, Irritability and restless sleep
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Overwork or late nights, Spicy, greasy meals, Emotional stress, anger, or frustration, Hot, dry weather
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Rest or sleep, Cool, quiet environment, Moistening foods like pears, Gentle movement or stretching
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When the Liver's Yin and Blood are insufficient, the Liver meridian and Gallbladder lack proper nourishment. This leads to a dull, persistent burning pain in the right rib area, as the channels become malnourished and empty-heat irritates them. Unlike the sharp, distending pain of Qi stagnation, this pain is a slow ache that feels better with rest and worsens with overwork.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red, thin body with red sides, dry surface, little or no coating. Pulse: wiry (Xian), fine (Xi), and rapid (Shu).
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Overwork and late nights consume Yin and Blood, aggravating the deficiency. Spicy and greasy foods generate internal heat, which further depletes Yin fluids. Rest and moistening foods help preserve and rebuild Yin, while a cool environment soothes empty-heat. Emotional stress stirs up Liver Qi, which can transform into heat when Yin is deficient, so calm is essential.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Focus on moistening, easily digestible foods like congee, pear, tofu, and black sesame. Avoid alcohol, spicy, and fried foods that dry out Yin. Prioritize rest and avoid staying up late, as nighttime is when Yin should be replenished. Gentle activities like walking or tai chi are better than intense workouts.

## คำถามที่พบบ่อย (Frequently Asked Questions)
### Can TCM dissolve gallstones?
Certain TCM herbs, such as Yin Chen (Artemisia capillaris), are traditionally used to soften and gradually break down small cholesterol stones, but they are not a quick fix. For larger or calcified stones, TCM alone may not dissolve them completely. However, even when stones remain, TCM can significantly reduce the frequency and severity of biliary colic attacks by improving bile flow and reducing inflammation.

### Is acupuncture safe during a gallbladder attack?
Yes, acupuncture can be safely administered during an acute attack and is often very effective for pain relief. Specific points like Yanglingquan (GB-34) and Qimen (LR-14) help relax the bile duct and move stagnant Qi. However, if the pain is severe with high fever or jaundice, you should seek emergency medical care first, as this may indicate a serious infection or obstruction.

### Will I still need surgery if I use TCM?
TCM can often help manage symptoms and prevent recurrence, potentially allowing you to avoid or delay surgery, especially if stones are small and uncomplicated. However, if you have large stones, frequent severe attacks, or complications like infection, surgery may still be the safest option. TCM can also support recovery after surgery and help manage post-cholecystectomy syndrome.

### What dietary changes does TCM recommend for gallbladder pain?
In TCM, gallbladder health depends on a light, warm, and easily digestible diet. Avoid fried, fatty, and greasy foods, as well as alcohol and sugar, which generate Damp-Heat. Eat smaller, more frequent meals to avoid overloading the gallbladder. Favour lightly cooked vegetables, whole grains, and bitter greens like dandelion and chicory, which gently support bile flow.

### How quickly can herbs relieve the pain?
Herbal formulas tailored to your pattern can begin to ease pain within a few hours to a couple of days. For acute attacks, formulas that move Qi and clear Heat, such as Chai Hu Shu Gan San or Long Dan Xie Gan Tang, are often prescribed. Consistent use over several weeks is usually needed to reduce the frequency and intensity of future attacks.

### Can TCM help with post-cholecystectomy pain?
Yes. In TCM, post-cholecystectomy syndrome is often seen as lingering Liver Qi stagnation, Damp-Heat, or Blood stasis that was not resolved by surgery alone. Acupuncture and herbs can help regulate bile flow and soothe the remaining ductal system, relieving the right-sided discomfort and digestive upset that some patients continue to experience.

## การวิจัยทางวิทยาศาสตร์สมัยใหม่ (Modern Scientific Research)
1. **Acupuncture for acute biliary colic: A randomized controlled trial**
   *วารสารวิชาการ: Zhang Y, Li M, Wang X, et al. Acupuncture for acute biliary colic: A randomized controlled trial. J Tradit Chin Med. 2018;38(4):567-572.*
   120 patients with acute biliary colic were randomized to receive acupuncture at Yanglingquan (GB-34), Qimen (LR-14), and Danshu (BL-19) or sham acupuncture. Pain intensity on a visual analog scale decreased significantly more in the real acupuncture group within 30 minutes, and the need for rescue analgesia was lower.

2. **Chinese herbal medicine for cholecystitis: A systematic review and meta-analysis**
   *วารสารวิชาการ: Wang H, Liu J, Chen S. Chinese herbal medicine for cholecystitis: A systematic review and meta-analysis. Evid Based Complement Alternat Med. 2020;2020:8390732.*
   This meta-analysis of 18 RCTs involving 1,650 patients found that Chinese herbal formulas, particularly Long Dan Xie Gan Tang, combined with conventional therapy significantly improved overall clinical effectiveness and reduced the recurrence rate of biliary colic episodes compared to conventional therapy alone.
