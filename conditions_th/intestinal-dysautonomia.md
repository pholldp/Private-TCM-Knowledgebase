## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนปัจจุบัน (Conventional Medicine View)
### คำอธิบาย (Description)
In Western medicine, Irritable Bowel Syndrome (IBS) is a chronic functional gastrointestinal disorder characterized by abdominal pain, bloating, and altered bowel habits - diarrhea, constipation, or both - without any visible damage to the digestive tract. It’s diagnosed using symptom-based criteria (the Rome IV criteria) after ruling out other conditions like inflammatory bowel disease or celiac disease.
IBS affects roughly 10-15% of the population worldwide, and while the exact cause remains unclear, factors such as altered gut motility, visceral hypersensitivity, gut-brain axis dysfunction, and changes in the gut microbiome are thought to play a role. Conventional treatment focuses on managing symptoms rather than addressing a root cause.

### การรักษาหลัก (Treatments)
Standard Western management includes dietary modifications (low-FODMAP diet, increased fiber), stress reduction, and medications targeted at predominant symptoms: antispasmodics for pain, loperamide for diarrhea, laxatives or lubiprostone for constipation, and low-dose antidepressants to modulate gut-brain signaling. Psychological therapies like cognitive behavioral therapy and gut-directed hypnotherapy are sometimes recommended.

## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนจีน (Traditional Chinese Medicine View)
### ความเข้าใจเกี่ยวกับการแพทย์แผนจีน (TCM Understanding)
In TCM, IBS is understood as a disruption in the harmonious movement of Qi through the digestive system, primarily involving the Liver, Spleen, and Large Intestine. The Liver is responsible for the smooth flow of Qi throughout the body, and when emotional stress, frustration, or anxiety cause the Liver Qi to stagnate, it can overact on the Spleen - the organ that transforms food into energy and fluids. This is the classic “Liver invading the Spleen” pattern, where cramping and urgent diarrhea strike after a tense meeting or argument.
But the Spleen can also be weak on its own, unable to manage fluids, leading to a buildup of internal Dampness. That Dampness makes stools loose and heavy, and leaves you feeling bloated and fatigued. If Dampness combines with Heat - often from rich, spicy foods or long-standing emotional heat - it settles in the Large Intestine, causing urgent, burning, foul-smelling diarrhea. On the other hand, if the body’s fluids are depleted (through Yin deficiency or excessive dryness), the Large Intestine loses its lubrication, leading to dry, pellet-like constipation.
Each of these patterns - and the deeper deficiency patterns involving Kidney Yang - represents a different root cause. That’s why two people with the same IBS diagnosis might need completely opposite treatments: one needs cooling and drying, another needs warming and moistening. TCM doesn’t just chase symptoms; it reads the body’s signals to restore the balance that keeps the gut calm.

### การวินิจฉัยโดยแพทย์แผนจีน (Practitioner Diagnosis)
A TCM practitioner begins by asking about bowel habits, pain, and emotional triggers. If stress or frustration brings on cramping, alternating loose stools and constipation, and relief after a bowel movement, the pattern is Rebellious Liver Qi invading the Spleen (肝气乘脾, gān qì chéng pí). The tongue often looks pale with a thin white coat, and the pulse may feel wiry on the left and relaxed on the right.
When the main issue is persistent loose stools, a heavy sensation in the limbs, and fatigue after eating, the diagnosis leans toward Spleen Deficiency with Dampness (脾虚湿盛, pí xū shī shèng). Here the Spleen cannot transform fluids, so dampness builds up. The tongue appears pale, swollen, and coated with a greasy white fur, and the pulse is soft and slow.
Sudden, urgent diarrhea that is foul-smelling, possibly with mucus and a burning anus, points to Damp-Heat in the Large Intestine (大肠湿热, dà cháng shī rè). This often flares after a rich meal or an infection. The tongue is red with a thick, yellow, greasy coating, and the pulse feels rapid and slippery.
When constipation dominates and stools are dry, hard, and pellet-like, the pattern is Large Intestine Dryness (大肠燥热, dà cháng zào rè). This stems from fluid depletion or Yin deficiency. The tongue may be dry and red with little coating, and the pulse is thready.
Long-standing emotional tension can lead to Liver Qi Stagnation that transforms into Heat (肝郁化热, gān yù huà rè). Here constipation comes with irritability, a dry mouth, a bitter taste, and a bloated abdomen that feels warm or burning. The tongue is red with a yellow coating, and the pulse is wiry and rapid.
In chronic cases where the body’s warming energy is depleted, Kidney and Spleen Yang Deficiency (脾肾阳虚, pí shèn yáng xū) sets in. The hallmark is early-morning diarrhea, accompanied by cold limbs, a pale complexion, and deep exhaustion. The tongue is pale, swollen, and wet, and the pulse is deep and weak.

### Rebellious Liver Qi invading the Spleen
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/rebellious-liver-qi-invading-the-spleen)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Abdominal pain that leads to diarrhea, Diarrhea triggered by emotional upset, Pain relief after bowel movement, Rib-side distension and fullness, Alternating constipation and loose stools, Irritability and frequent sighing
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Emotional stress, Anger or frustration, Cold or raw foods, Irregular eating, Overeating
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Stress reduction, Warm, cooked meals, Light exercise like walking, Regular meal times
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  The Liver is responsible for keeping Qi moving smoothly. When stress or frustration builds up, Liver Qi stagnates and then surges sideways, attacking the Spleen. This disrupts the Spleen's ability to transform food and fluids, so the intestines react with cramping and urgent diarrhea-often right after an emotional upset. Once the bowel empties, the pressure eases and the pain fades, but the cycle returns whenever tension rises.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: normal or slightly pale body, possible teeth marks at the edges, thin white coating that may be slightly greasy. Pulse: wiry (弦 xián) overall, distinctly wiry at the left middle (Liver) position, and softer at the right middle (Spleen) position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Emotional stress and anger directly surge Liver Qi, which then invades the Spleen and triggers intestinal cramping and diarrhea-the very mechanism of this pattern. Calming the mind and soothing the Liver removes the trigger. Warm, regular meals support the Spleen's digestive function, while cold and raw foods weaken it further, making the gut more reactive to emotional swings.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat warm, cooked meals at regular times; avoid cold, raw, and greasy foods that burden the Spleen. Gentle daily movement like walking helps keep Qi flowing. Build in small stress breaks-deep breathing or a short walk-especially before meals.

### Spleen Deficiency with Dampness
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/spleen-deficiency-with-dampness)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Loose or unformed stools, Abdominal bloating worse after eating, Heaviness in the body and limbs, Poor appetite or lack of taste, Fatigue and lack of energy, Sticky or greasy sensation in the mouth
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Cold or raw foods, Dairy and greasy foods, Overeating, Damp or humid weather, Prolonged mental fatigue
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warm, cooked meals, Light exercise like walking, Rest after eating, Dry, warm environment
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When the Spleen’s Qi is too weak to transform and transport fluids, water and moisture build up inside the body as internal Dampness. This Dampness sits heavily in the digestive system, clogging the normal movement of Qi and making the intestines sluggish and waterlogged. The result is loose, unformed stools, a feeling of bloating that gets worse after eating, and a heavy, tired sensation in the body and limbs.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale, swollen body with teeth marks and a white greasy coating that looks moist. Pulse: soggy (Ru) and slow (Huan), may feel slippery; deep and weak if deficiency is pronounced.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Dampness is a heavy, yin pathogen that thrives on cold and moisture. Warm, cooked foods and a dry environment help the Spleen function better and dry out excess Dampness. Overeating or eating hard-to-digest foods overwhelms the weak Spleen, creating more stagnation and loose stools. Gentle movement circulates Qi and helps shift fluid buildup, while rest allows the Spleen to recover its energy.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat warm, cooked, easily digestible foods like soups, congee, and steamed vegetables. Avoid cold, raw, greasy, and dairy-heavy foods that create more Dampness. Gentle daily movement, such as walking or stretching, helps keep Qi and fluids moving. Try not to overeat and allow time for rest after meals.

### Damp-Heat in the Large Intestine
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/damp-heat-in-the-large-intestine)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Urgent, explosive diarrhea with foul smell, Mucus or blood in stools, Burning sensation at the anus, Abdominal cramping and pain, often relieved after a bowel movement, Feeling of heaviness in the body and limbs, Incomplete evacuation after bowel movements
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Greasy, fried, or spicy foods, Alcohol and coffee, Hot, humid weather, Emotional stress, Overeating
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Light, bland diet, Cooling foods and drinks, Drinking warm water, Rest during acute flare-ups, Light exercise like walking
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When Dampness and Heat combine in the body, they can settle in the Large Intestine and disrupt its normal function. This often happens after eating too many greasy, spicy, or rich foods, or when emotional stress generates Heat that mixes with internal Dampness. The Damp-Heat irritates the intestinal lining, causing urgent, foul-smelling diarrhea with mucus or blood, a burning sensation at the anus, and a feeling of incomplete evacuation. In intestinal dysautonomia, this pattern typically appears as acute flare-ups that alternate with periods of relative calm.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red body with a thick, yellow, greasy coating, thickest at the centre and root. Pulse: slippery (Hua) and rapid (Shu).
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Greasy, spicy, and rich foods create Dampness and Heat in the body, which then lodge in the Large Intestine, aggravating the symptoms. Alcohol and coffee are heating and can directly irritate the gut. Emotional stress can cause Liver Qi to stagnate and generate Heat, which combines with Dampness. Cooling, bland foods and hydration help clear Heat and drain Dampness, while rest allows the body to recover and reduces internal friction.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat a light, bland diet with plenty of cooked vegetables and congee. Avoid greasy, fried, spicy, and sugary foods, as well as alcohol and coffee. Drink plenty of warm water or barley tea to help flush out Damp-Heat. Gentle exercise like walking can help move Qi, but avoid overheating.

### Large Intestine Dryness
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/large-intestine-dryness)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Dry, hard, pellet-like stools, Dry mouth and throat, Sensation of incomplete evacuation, Constipation with long intervals, no diarrhea
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Greasy, fried, or spicy foods, Alcohol and coffee, Stress and overwork, Dry, hot weather, Irregular eating
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Drinking warm water, Eating moistening foods like pears, Gentle abdominal massage, Light exercise like walking
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When body fluids are depleted or Yin is deficient, the Large Intestine loses its natural lubrication. Stools become dry, hard, and pellet-like, and the bowel's peristalsis slows, causing constipation with long intervals between movements. This dryness pattern often underlies constipation-predominant intestinal dysautonomia.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red, thin, dry body with cracks; thin dry slightly yellow coat. Pulse: fine (thin) and choppy, weaker in the right Guan position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Large Intestine Dryness stems from a lack of body fluids. Drinking water and eating moistening foods directly replenish Yin and fluids, lubricating the bowel. Spicy and fried foods, alcohol, and coffee are heating and drying, consuming the very fluids the body lacks. Stress and overwork further deplete Yin, while regular meals and gentle movement support the Spleen's ability to produce fluids.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Drink plenty of warm water and eat moistening foods such as pears, honey, and sesame. Avoid spicy, fried, and dry foods that deplete fluids. Gentle abdominal massage and regular light exercise can help stimulate bowel movements.

### Liver Qi Stagnation that transforms into Heat
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/liver-qi-stagnation-that-transforms-into-heat)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Distending or burning pain along the ribs, Constipation with dry, hard stools, Irritability and explosive anger, Bitter taste and dry mouth, Abdominal distension with a burning sensation
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Greasy, fried, or spicy foods, Emotional stress and frustration, Alcohol and coffee, Irregular eating
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Cooling foods and drinks, Stress reduction, Light exercise like walking, Regular meal times
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When frustration and emotional stress are held in for too long, Liver Qi stagnates. Over time this stuck energy generates heat, like friction creating a spark. That heat dries up the fluids that normally keep the intestines moist and moving, leading to constipation with dry, hard stools. The heat also rises, causing irritability, a bitter taste, and a burning sensation in the abdomen.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red body, especially red at the sides, with a yellow coating. Pulse: wiry and rapid (弦数 xián shuò), forceful at the left middle position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: This pattern is driven by heat that has built up from long-standing stagnation. Spicy and greasy foods, alcohol, and emotional upset all add more heat and keep the Qi stuck, making the constipation and irritability worse. Cooling foods and stress reduction help clear the heat and allow the Qi to flow smoothly again, which naturally moistens the intestines and calms the mind.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat plenty of cooling, moistening foods like cucumber, celery, pear, and spinach. Avoid spicy, greasy, and fried foods that add heat. Cut back on alcohol and coffee. Practice daily stress management through meditation, gentle walking, or tai chi, and keep regular meal times to help the Liver and Spleen work in harmony.

### Kidney and Spleen Yang Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/kidney-and-spleen-yang-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Diarrhea at dawn (around 5 AM), Cold hands and feet, especially below knees, Sore and cold lower back and knees, Undigested food in stools, Abdominal cold pain that improves with warmth, Frequent urination with clear, pale urine
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Cold or raw foods, Cold weather exposure, Overwork and exhaustion, Stress and anxiety, Iced drinks
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warmth on the abdomen, Warm, cooked meals, Rest and adequate sleep, Light exercise like walking, Moxibustion
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Kidney Yang is the body's root fire, responsible for warming all organs. When it weakens, it fails to warm the Spleen, causing Spleen Yang deficiency. The digestive fire cannot transform food and fluids, leading to chronic loose stools, undigested food, and cold pain in the abdomen. The Kidney's gate of life fire is weakest at dawn, so diarrhea often strikes early in the morning, leaving the intestines sluggish and cold.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale, puffy, and swollen with teeth marks on the sides and a white, slippery coating. Pulse: deep, weak, and slow; both rear (Chi) positions are notably feeble, and the right middle (Guan) position feels soft and lacks root.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Kidney and Spleen Yang deficiency means the body's warming fire is low. Cold and raw foods, cold weather, and overwork further extinguish that fire, worsening diarrhea and coldness. Warmth, rest, and warm foods help conserve and gently replenish Yang, allowing the digestive system to function.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat only warm, cooked foods like soups, stews, and congees; avoid all raw, cold, and iced items. Keep the abdomen and lower back warm with a belly band or hot water bottle. Gentle daily movement like walking or tai chi helps circulation without depleting energy. Moxibustion on the lower abdomen and lower back can be very helpful.

## คำถามที่พบบ่อย (Frequently Asked Questions)
### How does acupuncture help IBS?
Acupuncture helps regulate the nervous system, reduce visceral hypersensitivity, and restore the smooth flow of Qi through the digestive tract. Points like Zusanli (ST-36) and Tianshu (ST-25) directly calm the intestines and strengthen the Spleen, while Taichong (LR-3) eases Liver Qi stagnation - the root of stress-triggered symptoms. Most patients feel deeply relaxed during treatment, and many notice their gut “settle” after just a few sessions.

### Can TCM treat constipation-predominant IBS?
Yes. TCM treats constipation not with a single laxative but by identifying why the bowel is dry or sluggish. If it’s due to Liver Qi stagnation generating Heat, we clear the Heat and move the Qi. If it’s from fluid deficiency (Large Intestine Dryness), we nourish Yin and moisten the intestines. Acupuncture points like Zhigou (SJ-6) and Sanyinjiao (SP-6) are especially effective for promoting bowel movements without causing dependency.

### What herbs are commonly used for IBS with diarrhea?
The classic formula is Tong Xie Yao Fang (Pain and Diarrhea Formula), which contains Bai Shao (white peony root) to soothe the Liver, Bai Zhu (Atractylodes) to strengthen the Spleen, Chen Pi (tangerine peel) to regulate Qi, and Fang Feng (Saposhnikovia) to harmonize the Liver and Spleen. It’s specifically designed for stress-related cramping and loose stools. For Damp-Heat diarrhea, Ge Gen Qin Lian Tang is used to clear Heat and dry Dampness.

### Will I need to change my diet completely?
You don’t need a drastic overhaul, but some adjustments help the herbs work better. Generally, we recommend eating warm, cooked meals and avoiding raw, cold, and greasy foods - these tax the Spleen. As your digestion strengthens, you’ll likely be able to tolerate a wider variety of foods again. The goal is to restore your digestive resilience, not to keep you on a restrictive diet forever.

### How long does it take to see results with TCM?
Many people notice less bloating and pain within 2-4 weeks, with bowel movements becoming more regular. For stress-triggered patterns, improvement can be quite rapid once the Liver is soothed. Deeper deficiency patterns, like Kidney Yang Deficiency with early-morning diarrhea, take longer - often 3-6 months of consistent treatment to rebuild the body’s core warmth. Your practitioner will track your tongue and pulse to monitor progress even before symptoms fully resolve.

### Is TCM safe to use alongside my IBS medications?
Yes, in most cases. Herbs and acupuncture can complement antispasmodics, fiber supplements, and low-dose antidepressants. However, always tell both your TCM practitioner and your medical doctor about everything you’re taking. If you’re on strong prescription medications for diarrhea or constipation, your TCM practitioner will likely adjust the herbal formula to avoid over-treatment and may suggest a gradual reduction of pharmaceuticals as your gut function stabilizes - but only under your doctor’s guidance.

### Can TCM help with the anxiety that triggers my IBS?
Absolutely. TCM doesn’t separate the mind and body - the Liver is the organ most affected by stress, and when its Qi stagnates, it directly impacts the intestines. Acupuncture points like Yintang (between the eyebrows) and Shenmen (HT-7) calm the spirit, while herbs in formulas like Xiao Yao San smooth the Liver and relieve emotional tension. As your Liver Qi flows more freely, you may find that stressful situations no longer send you running to the bathroom.
