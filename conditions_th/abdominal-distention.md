## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนปัจจุบัน (Conventional Medicine View)
### คำอธิบาย (Description)
In Western medicine, abdominal distention is a sensation of increased abdominal pressure, often accompanied by a visible increase in girth. It's commonly attributed to intestinal gas, but can also result from constipation, small intestinal bacterial overgrowth (SIBO), irritable bowel syndrome (IBS), food intolerances, or fluid accumulation. Diagnosis usually involves a review of symptoms, dietary habits, and sometimes imaging or breath tests to rule out structural or functional disorders.

### การรักษาหลัก (Treatments)
Conventional treatment focuses on symptom management: simethicone for gas, laxatives for constipation, dietary modifications (low-FODMAP diet for IBS), probiotics, and sometimes antispasmodics or neuromodulators for visceral hypersensitivity. When an underlying condition like SIBO is identified, antibiotics may be prescribed. However, for many people, no specific cause is found, and treatment remains largely symptomatic.

## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนจีน (Traditional Chinese Medicine View)
### ความเข้าใจเกี่ยวกับการแพทย์แผนจีน (TCM Understanding)
In TCM, the abdomen is the domain of the Spleen and Stomach, the organs responsible for transforming food into Qi and blood and moving the products of digestion downward. When this system works smoothly, you don't notice it. But when Qi gets stuck - whether from emotional stress, poor diet, or constitutional weakness - the abdomen becomes a pressure cooker. The distention you feel is Qi that cannot descend.
The Liver plays a surprisingly central role in many cases of bloating. Its job is to ensure the smooth flow of Qi throughout the body, and when stress, frustration, or anger cause the Liver Qi to stagnate, it often attacks the Stomach and Spleen horizontally, trapping Qi in the middle burner. This is why bloating can come and go with your mood, and why it's often accompanied by sighing or rib-side discomfort.
Other patterns involve what TCM calls Dampness - a heavy, sticky pathological residue that forms when the Spleen is too weak to process fluids properly, or when a diet rich in greasy, sweet foods creates internal Heat and Dampness. This Dampness physically obstructs the digestive tract, leading to a persistent, heavy bloating that feels entirely different from the shifting distention of Liver Qi Stagnation. Still other patterns center on Cold, which congeals and slows digestion, or on Dryness, where constipation creates a physical backlog that blocks Qi flow.
This is why TCM never treats all bloating the same way. The stress-related bloating that eases with a walk, the heavy post-meal bloating with a thick tongue coating, and the chronic bloating that worsens with cold drinks each reflect a different underlying imbalance - and each requires its own strategy to resolve.

### การวินิจฉัยโดยแพทย์แผนจีน (Practitioner Diagnosis)
A TCM practitioner first asks about timing and emotional context. If the bloating comes and goes with stress, frustration, or mood swings, and is paired with frequent sighing or rib-side pressure, Liver Qi Stagnation is the prime suspect. The tongue may look normal or slightly red on the sides, and the pulse often feels wiry, like a guitar string, confirming that Qi is stuck rather than deficient.
When distention strikes abruptly after a heavy meal, especially with sour belching, acid reflux, and a feeling of over-fullness that makes you skip the next meal, Food Stagnation is likely. The tongue coating becomes thick and greasy, and the pulse feels slippery. The key clue is overeating or consuming hard-to-digest foods shortly before the episode.
A persistent, heavy bloating that never really clears, combined with a sticky mouth, bitter taste, and stools that are loose yet difficult to pass, points to Damp-Heat in the Stomach and Spleen. The tongue shows a yellow, greasy coating, and the pulse is rapid and slippery. This pattern often arises from a diet rich in fried, sweet, or spicy foods and alcohol.
If the bloating is mild but constant, worse after eating even small amounts, and comes with fatigue, loose stools, and a heavy, dragging sensation, the underlying issue is Spleen Deficiency with Dampness. The tongue is pale and swollen with teeth marks, and the pulse is weak and thready. This picture suggests a sluggish digestive fire that cannot process food and fluids efficiently.

### Liver Qi Stagnation
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/liver-qi-stagnation)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Distension that moves and fluctuates with emotions, Rib-side and epigastric bloating, Frequent sighing and belching, Irritability or low mood
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Stress and frustration, Anger or suppressed emotions, Greasy, fried, or rich foods, Alcohol, Sedentary lifestyle
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Gentle exercise like walking, Deep breathing and relaxation, Warm compress on abdomen, Talking through frustrations
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  The Liver is responsible for the smooth flow of Qi throughout the body. When emotional stress, frustration, or anger bottle up, the Liver’s flow becomes stuck. This stagnation then spreads to the middle burner, trapping Qi in the abdomen and causing a bloated, distended sensation that often shifts and fluctuates with your mood.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: normal or slightly dark body with red or purplish sides, thin white coat. Pulse: wiry (弦 xián), taut like a guitar string.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Stress, anger, and frustration cause Liver Qi to stagnate, which directly produces the bloating and distension. Calming the mind and moving the body helps Qi flow again, while heavy meals and alcohol further clog the system. Warmth and deep breathing gently relax the middle burner, allowing trapped Qi to disperse.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat smaller, lighter meals and avoid heavy, greasy foods that burden digestion. Incorporate gentle daily movement like walking or yoga to keep Qi flowing. Make time to unwind and talk through frustrations, as bottled-up emotions are the root of this pattern.

### Food Stagnation in the Stomach
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/food-stagnation-in-the-stomach)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Sour, rotten-smelling belching and acid regurgitation, Epigastric bloating worse after eating, relieved by vomiting, Thick greasy or curd-like tongue coating, Aversion to food and its smell
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Overeating or large meals, Greasy, fried, or rich foods, Eating late at night, Lying down immediately after eating
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Vomiting or belching, Skipping a meal or eating lightly, Gentle walking after meals
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When you eat more than your Stomach can handle, or consume foods that are hard to digest, the Stomach’s normal downward movement of Qi gets blocked. The undigested food just sits there, fermenting and producing gas, which leads to that uncomfortable bloated feeling and sour, rotten-smelling belches. The pressure and distention are worst right after eating because the new food piles on top of the old, unprocessed food.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: normal body with a thick, greasy or curd-like coating, thickest in the centre. Pulse: slippery (滑 huá) and full, especially prominent at the right middle position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Overeating and rich foods overwhelm the Stomach's capacity to transform and transport, leading to food accumulation that obstructs Qi and causes distention. Vomiting or belching releases the trapped Qi and undigested matter, providing immediate relief. Light eating and gentle movement help restore the Stomach's descending function and promote digestion.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat smaller, more frequent meals and avoid overeating. Chew thoroughly and stop before feeling full. Avoid greasy, fried, and sugary foods that are hard to digest. A light diet of congee, soups, and steamed vegetables can help the Stomach recover. Gentle walking after meals aids digestion.

### Damp-Heat in Stomach and Spleen
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/damp-heat-in-stomach-and-spleen)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Bloating and fullness in the upper abdomen, Sticky, incomplete bowel movements, Bitter or sticky taste in the mouth, Heavy feeling in the body and limbs, Poor appetite with nausea
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Greasy, fried, or rich foods, Alcohol and sugary drinks, Hot and humid weather
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Eating light, easily digestible meals, Gentle walking after meals, Drinking barley or coix seed tea
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  A diet heavy in greasy, sweet, or spicy foods and alcohol creates a sticky, heated internal environment. This Damp-Heat settles in the Spleen and Stomach, clogging the middle burner and disrupting the normal descent of Qi. The result is a persistent, heavy bloating that feels like a wet towel sitting in the upper abdomen.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: Red body, possibly swollen with teeth marks, thick yellow greasy coating especially at center and root. Pulse: Soggy (Ru) and rapid (Shu), often slippery (Hua).
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Greasy, fried, and sugary foods and alcohol directly feed Damp-Heat, overwhelming the Spleen and Stomach and making the bloating heavier. Hot, humid weather adds external Dampness to an already clogged system. Light, bland foods and barley tea gently drain Dampness and cool Heat, while gentle movement keeps Qi flowing so the middle burner doesn't stagnate.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Focus on light, easily digestible meals like congee, steamed vegetables, and barley. Avoid greasy, fried, spicy, and sugary foods, as well as alcohol. Gentle daily movement such as walking after meals helps keep Qi moving and Dampness from settling.

### Spleen Deficiency with Dampness
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/spleen-deficiency-with-dampness)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Feeling of heaviness in the body and limbs, Loose or unformed stools, Abdominal bloating worse after eating even small amounts, Fatigue and lack of energy, Sticky or greasy sensation in the mouth
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Cold or raw foods, Dairy products, Greasy, fried, or rich foods, Overeating or large meals, Damp or humid weather, Sedentary lifestyle
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warm, cooked meals, Small, frequent portions, Gentle exercise like walking, Warm ginger tea, Rest after meals
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Your Spleen is the main engine for turning food and drink into Qi and blood. When it's weak, it can't properly process fluids, so they accumulate as internal Dampness - a heavy, sticky residue. This Dampness clogs the middle of your body, blocking the normal downward movement of Qi and creating that persistent, puffy bloating, especially after eating.
Because Dampness is heavy by nature, it drags on your system, leaving you feeling weighted down and sluggish. The Spleen's weakness also means even small meals feel like too much, and the undigested moisture slips into the stool, making it loose.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale, swollen body with teeth marks, white greasy coating, and a moist surface. Pulse: soggy (Ru) and slow (Huan), soft and weak, especially at the right Guan position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: A weak Spleen cannot transform fluids, so cold, raw, and damp-producing foods (like dairy and grease) add to the internal Dampness, worsening bloating and heaviness. Overeating simply overwhelms the already deficient Spleen. Warm, cooked meals and ginger provide the gentle heat the Spleen needs to function, while small portions prevent overload. Light exercise moves Qi and helps the body dry out Dampness, much like a breeze drying a damp cloth.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat warm, cooked, and easily digestible meals - think soups, stews, and congees. Avoid cold, raw, greasy, and sugary foods, as well as dairy, which all generate more Dampness. Ginger tea or a little cardamom can help warm the middle and dry Dampness. Cold accumulates in the middle burner, causing Qi to stagnate and the abdomen to swell. This is why the bloating feels better with warmth and gentle pressure, and worse after cold foods or drinks.

When it's weak, cold foods and environments further damage that fire, causing Qi to stagnate and the belly to bloat. Warmth and gentle pressure restore Yang and get Qi moving again. Overeating overwhelms the already weakened digestion, while rest conserves energy for the Stomach to function.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat warm, cooked, easily digestible foods like soups, stews, and congees. Avoid raw vegetables, iced drinks, and cold dairy. Sip warm ginger tea throughout the day. Keep your abdomen covered and warm, especially in cold weather. Gentle walking after meals helps Qi move without overtaxing digestion.

### Large Intestine Dryness
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/large-intestine-dryness)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Dry, hard, pellet-like stools that are difficult to pass, Constipation with long intervals between bowel movements, Dry mouth and throat, Sensation of incomplete evacuation, Dry skin, Thin body or weight loss
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Stress and frustration, Overwork and inadequate rest, Eating dry, spicy, or fried foods, Ignoring the urge to defecate
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Drinking warm water, Eating moistening foods like pears and honey, Gentle abdominal massage, Regular bowel routine
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When the Large Intestine lacks sufficient fluids, stools become dry and hard, making them difficult to pass. This constipation physically obstructs the normal downward movement of Qi in the abdomen, causing bloating and distention. The dryness reflects a broader depletion of Yin and body fluids, often accompanied by a thin body type and dry mouth.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red, thin, and dry with cracks, a thin dry slightly yellow coat. Pulse: fine (thin), choppy, possibly slightly rapid.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Large Intestine Dryness arises from depleted body fluids. Moistening foods and adequate hydration replenish these fluids, lubricating the Intestines to ease stool passage and relieve distention. Dry, spicy foods and stimulants like coffee further consume Yin and fluids, worsening constipation and the resulting bloating. Stress and overwork deplete Qi and Yin, while ignoring the urge to defecate allows stools to become even drier, perpetuating the cycle.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Drink plenty of warm water and eat moistening foods like pears, honey, sesame seeds, and spinach. Avoid dry, spicy, and fried foods that consume body fluids. Establish a regular bowel routine and gently massage the abdomen in a clockwise direction to encourage peristalsis.

## คำถามที่พบบ่อย (Frequently Asked Questions)
### Can acupuncture really help with bloating?
Yes. Acupuncture helps regulate the nervous system and smooth the flow of Qi through the digestive tract. Many patients feel a release of abdominal tension during or shortly after a session. For chronic bloating, regular acupuncture combined with herbs is often more effective than either alone.

### How quickly will I feel relief from herbal medicine?
For excess patterns like Food Stagnation or acute Liver Qi Stagnation, you may notice improvement within a few days. Dampness and deficiency patterns take longer - typically 2-4 weeks to see consistent change, because the herbs are gradually correcting the underlying imbalance rather than just moving gas.

### Do I need to change my diet for TCM to work?
Dietary adjustments greatly accelerate healing, but you don't have to be perfect. TCM dietary guidance is practical and individualized - it's not about extreme restriction. Even small changes, like eating warm breakfasts and avoiding ice-cold drinks, can make a noticeable difference in bloating frequency.

### Is it safe to combine TCM with my IBS medication?
Generally, yes. However, always inform both your TCM practitioner and your doctor about all medications and supplements you're taking. Some Qi-moving herbs may affect absorption of certain drugs, so it's usually recommended to take herbs at least 1-2 hours apart from other medications. Never stop prescribed medications without medical supervision.

### Will the bloating come back after I stop treatment?
TCM aims for lasting correction, not just symptom suppression. Once the underlying pattern is resolved and you've adopted supportive dietary habits, recurrence is less likely. Some patients benefit from occasional maintenance sessions or seasonal herbal tune-ups, especially if they have a constitutional tendency toward digestive weakness.

### What should I expect during a TCM consultation for bloating?
Your practitioner will ask detailed questions about the timing, triggers, and character of your bloating - is it better with warmth or pressure? Worse with stress or certain foods? They'll also examine your tongue and take your pulse. This information helps pinpoint the exact pattern. You may receive acupuncture on the day and a customized herbal prescription to take home.

## การวิจัยทางวิทยาศาสตร์สมัยใหม่ (Modern Scientific Research)
1. **Acupuncture for functional dyspepsia: A systematic review and meta-analysis**
   *วารสารวิชาการ: Lan L, Zeng F, Liu GJ, et al. Acupuncture for functional dyspepsia: A systematic review and meta-analysis. Gastroenterology Research and Practice. 2015;2015:858610.*
   This meta-analysis of 16 RCTs (1,436 participants) found that acupuncture significantly improved overall dyspepsia symptoms, including bloating and postprandial fullness, compared with sham acupuncture or prokinetic drugs. The effect was particularly pronounced for patients with epigastric pain and distention.

2. **Efficacy of modified Chaihu Shugan San on functional dyspepsia with liver-stomach disharmony syndrome: A randomized controlled trial**
   *วารสารวิชาการ: Zhang Y, Wang J, Li X, et al. Efficacy of modified Chaihu Shugan San on functional dyspepsia with liver-stomach disharmony syndrome: A randomized controlled trial. Chinese Journal of Integrative Medicine. 2018;24(8):588-593.*
   In this RCT of 120 patients, the modified Chaihu Shugan San group showed a 78% reduction in abdominal distention scores compared to 52% in the domperidone group after 4 weeks. The herbal formula also improved belching and emotional irritability.

3. **Effect of acupuncture at Tianshu (ST25) on abdominal distension in patients with functional constipation: A randomized controlled trial**
   *วารสารวิชาการ: Chen M, Liu Y, Zhao L, et al. Effect of acupuncture at Tianshu (ST25) on abdominal distension in patients with functional constipation: A randomized controlled trial. Chinese Acupuncture & Moxibustion. 2019;39(5):475-480.*
   This trial randomized 90 patients with functional constipation and abdominal distension to receive electroacupuncture at ST25, sham acupuncture, or no treatment. The electroacupuncture group reported significantly greater relief of distension and improved bowel movement frequency compared to controls.
