## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนปัจจุบัน (Conventional Medicine View)
### คำอธิบาย (Description)
Laryngopharyngeal reflux (LPR) occurs when stomach contents - acid, pepsin, and sometimes bile - travel up the esophagus and spill into the larynx and pharynx. Unlike classic GERD, heartburn is often absent, which is why it's commonly called silent reflux. The primary symptoms are felt in the throat: chronic throat clearing, a sensation of a lump (globus), hoarseness, a dry cough, and sometimes a bitter taste. Diagnosis is typically made through laryngoscopy, which may show swelling and redness of the laryngeal tissues, and sometimes pH monitoring to confirm reflux events.

### การรักษาหลัก (Treatments)
Standard treatment relies on acid suppression with proton pump inhibitors (PPIs) or H2 receptor blockers, often at higher doses and for longer durations than typical GERD. Lifestyle modifications - elevating the head of the bed, avoiding late meals, and reducing trigger foods - are also recommended. In severe or medication-resistant cases, surgical options like fundoplication may be considered to reinforce the lower esophageal sphincter.

## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนจีน (Traditional Chinese Medicine View)
### ความเข้าใจเกี่ยวกับการแพทย์แผนจีน (TCM Understanding)
In TCM, the Stomach is meant to send its contents downward. When healthy, it acts like a gentle downward escalator, moving food and fluids through the digestive tract. LPR occurs when this descending function fails and Stomach Qi rebels upward, carrying acid, phlegm, or heat into the delicate tissues of the throat. The throat wasn't built to handle these substances, so it reacts with irritation, a sensation of something stuck, or a dry cough. This rebellious Qi is the core mechanism behind every pattern of LPR - what differs is why the Stomach lost its downward direction in the first place.
The most common culprit is the Liver, the organ system that keeps Qi flowing smoothly throughout the body. Emotional stress, frustration, or pent-up anger constrains Liver Qi, like a kink in a garden hose. Because the Liver and Stomach sit close together energetically, that stuck Liver Qi often lashes out sideways and attacks the Stomach, forcing its contents upward. This is why reflux and globus sensation flare so reliably during stressful periods - the emotional trigger directly disrupts the Stomach's descending function. When this stagnation persists, it can generate Heat, producing the burning, sour-tasting reflux that feels like fire rising into the throat.
Other patterns arise from different roots. When stagnant Qi congeals the body's fluids, Phlegm forms and rides the rebellious Stomach Qi upward, creating the classic "plum-pit" sensation - a lump that can't be swallowed down. Chronic digestive weakness, often from poor diet or overwork, leaves the Spleen and Stomach too depleted to push downward, so even mild acid drifts upward along with thin, watery fluids. Long-standing stagnation can even progress to Blood stasis, causing fixed, stabbing chest pain alongside reflux. And when the Stomach's nourishing Yin fluids are dried up by heat or stress, the throat loses its protective lubrication, making even tiny amounts of acid feel intensely raw and burning.

### การวินิจฉัยโดยแพทย์แผนจีน (Practitioner Diagnosis)
For Liver Qi Stagnation invading the Stomach, the practitioner asks about stress and emotional triggers. The hallmark is a sensation of something stuck in the throat that worsens with frustration, plus belching and a sour taste. The tongue often looks normal or slightly red on the sides with a thin white coat, and the pulse feels wiry, like a guitar string, reflecting the tension of constrained Liver Qi.
When that constraint generates Heat, the picture shifts to burning. The person feels a hot, irritated throat, acid rising, and maybe chest or rib-side discomfort. The tongue becomes red, especially on the edges, with a yellow coat, and the pulse is wiry and rapid. The practitioner listens for irritability and a bitter taste in the mouth as key clues that heat has flared.
For Qi-Phlegm, the central complaint is the classic "plum-pit" sensation - a lump in the throat that cannot be swallowed down. There may be hoarseness, frequent throat clearing, and a feeling of chest oppression. The tongue has a thick, greasy white coat, and the pulse is slippery and wiry. The practitioner notes that the sensation often comes and goes with emotional upset, pointing to Qi stagnation congealing fluids into phlegm.
In Spleen and Stomach Qi Deficiency, reflux is accompanied by fatigue, bloating after meals, and loose stools. The acid regurgitation tends to be watery rather than burning. The tongue is pale and swollen with teeth marks, and the pulse is weak and thin. The practitioner asks about appetite and energy levels; a chronic, dragging tiredness and a desire to lie down after eating point to this deficient pattern.
Qi and Blood Stagnation presents with fixed, stabbing pain behind the breastbone or in the chest, not just a vague discomfort. The tongue is dusky or purple with visible dark spots, and the pulse is choppy or wiry-fine. The practitioner looks for a long history of reflux that has become entrenched, with pain that does not shift location, indicating that blood flow is impaired.
Stomach Yin Deficiency is marked by dryness: a dry, sore throat, dry mouth, and a preference for small sips of water. The person may feel hungry but have no desire to eat. The tongue is red with little or no coat, often with cracks, and the pulse is thin and rapid.
The practitioner asks about night-time symptoms and any feeling of heat in the palms or chest, as Yin deficiency often brings a low-grade, lingering heat.

### Liver Qi Stagnation invading the Stomach
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/liver-qi-stagnation-invading-the-stomach)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Distending pain in the ribs or upper abdomen, Frequent belching or acid reflux, Sensation of a lump in the throat, Symptoms worsen with emotional stress, Irritability and mood swings
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Emotional stress, anger, or worry, Overeating or large meals, Greasy, spicy, or fried foods, Alcohol and coffee, Lying down soon after eating, Rushing or eating on the go
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Rest, relaxation, or stress reduction, Small, frequent meals, Sipping warm water or herbal tea, Gentle exercise like walking, Sitting upright after meals
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  The Liver is responsible for the smooth flow of Qi throughout the body. When emotional stress, frustration, or anger constrains the Liver, its Qi stagnates and then attacks the Stomach horizontally, disrupting the Stomach's normal downward movement. This forces Stomach Qi and acid upward into the esophagus and throat, causing the sensation of a lump, belching, and acid reflux. The throat and chest symptoms are a direct result of rebellious Qi rather than a throat problem.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: normal or slightly red body, may have redder sides; thin white coat. Pulse: wiry (弦 xián), especially at the left middle (Liver) position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Emotional stress and anger directly aggravate Liver Qi stagnation, which is the root of this pattern; the constrained Liver then attacks the Stomach, causing Qi to rebel upward. Large or greasy meals overburden the Stomach, making it harder for Qi to descend. Relaxation, small meals, and gentle movement help smooth the flow of Liver Qi and support the Stomach's natural downward direction, preventing reflux.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat smaller, more frequent meals and avoid eating within three hours of bedtime. Reduce or eliminate coffee, alcohol, spicy, and greasy foods, which can aggravate reflux. Prioritize stress management through gentle exercise like walking, yoga, or meditation, and ensure you have outlets for emotional expression. Chew food thoroughly and eat in a calm environment.

### Liver Qi Stagnation that transforms into Heat
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/liver-qi-stagnation-that-transforms-into-heat)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Burning throat pain or heartburn, Bitter taste in the mouth, Irritability and mood swings, Red face and eyes, Frequent belching or acid reflux, Distending pain in the ribs or upper abdomen
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Emotional stress, anger, or worry, Greasy, spicy, or fried foods, Alcohol and coffee, Overeating or large meals, Lying down soon after eating, Hot weather or heated rooms
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Cool drinks like chrysanthemum tea, Rest, relaxation, or stress reduction, Small, frequent meals, Gentle exercise like walking, Sitting upright after meals
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When Liver Qi is stuck for a long time, it can generate Heat, much like friction creates warmth. This Heat then disturbs the Stomach, which normally sends food and acid downward. Instead, the Stomach rebels and sends hot, acidic Qi upward into the throat, causing a burning sensation, sour belching, and irritation. The bitter taste is a hallmark of Liver Heat rising, and the emotional irritability reflects the underlying Liver constraint.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red body with redder sides, yellow coating. Pulse: wiry and rapid (弦数, xián shuò).
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Anger and stress directly fuel Liver Qi stagnation, which then generates more Heat and drives acid upward. Spicy, greasy foods, alcohol, and coffee all introduce additional Heat into the body, further agitating the Stomach. Cooling foods and calming activities help dissipate that Heat and smooth the flow of Qi, allowing the Stomach to resume its natural downward movement.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Avoid spicy, greasy, and fried foods, as well as alcohol and coffee, which add Heat and aggravate reflux. Eat smaller, more frequent meals and avoid lying down within 2-3 hours after eating. Practice stress-reducing activities like meditation, gentle yoga, or walking to keep Liver Qi flowing smoothly. Sipping cool water or herbal teas like chrysanthemum can help clear Heat.

### Qi-Phlegm
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/qi-phlegm)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Sensation of a lump in the throat, Chest and upper abdominal stuffiness, Irritability and mood swings, Symptoms worsen with emotional stress
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Emotional stress, anger, or worry, Cold or raw foods, Greasy, spicy, or fried foods, Overeating or large meals
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warm, easily digestible meals, Gentle exercise like walking, Rest, relaxation, or stress reduction, Sipping warm water or herbal tea
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When stress or frustration causes Liver Qi to stagnate, it loses its ability to keep fluids moving. Those stagnant fluids congeal into Phlegm, which then gets caught up in the rebellious Stomach Qi and rises into the throat. This is what creates the classic sensation of a lump that feels stuck, along with throat clearing and a persistent need to sigh.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: normal-coloured body, possibly slightly swollen, with a white greasy or slippery coating. Pulse: wiry (弦 xián) and slippery (滑 huá).
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Emotional stress is the primary driver of Liver Qi stagnation, which is the root of Qi-Phlegm. When Qi flow is constrained, fluids congeal into Phlegm that rises to the throat. Cold, greasy, and heavy foods further weaken the Spleen's ability to transform fluids, directly contributing to Phlegm formation. Warm, light meals and gentle movement assist the Spleen and Stomach in moving Qi downward, while stress reduction helps the Liver maintain a smooth flow, preventing the cycle from restarting.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Avoid dairy, greasy, and cold raw foods that create Phlegm. Favour warm, cooked meals like soups and stews. Gentle movement such as walking or yoga helps Qi flow. Practice stress management to prevent emotional stagnation.

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Reflux of clear, watery acid, Fatigue and physical weakness, Bloating and fullness after eating small amounts, Poor appetite or no desire to eat, Loose, unformed stools, Pale, sallow complexion, Dull, mild upper stomach discomfort
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Overeating or large meals, Cold or raw foods, Lying down soon after eating, Physical and mental overwork, Emotional stress, anger, or worry
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warm, easily digestible meals, Small, frequent meals, Rest, relaxation, or stress reduction, Gentle exercise like walking
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  The Spleen and Stomach are the body's digestive engine, responsible for moving food and fluids downward. When their Qi is chronically weak from poor diet, overwork, or illness, that downward movement loses strength. Stomach Qi then rebels upward into the throat, carrying acid and thin, watery fluids that irritate the larynx. Because the digestive system is underpowered, you also feel tired, bloated after eating, and your stools are loose.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale, puffy body with tooth marks (scalloped edges) and a thin white coating. Pulse: weak, soft, and lacking force, especially at the right middle (Guan) position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Weak Spleen Qi struggles to process heavy, cold, or large amounts of food, so these cause bloating and upward rebellion of Stomach Qi. Warm, cooked, easily digested foods lighten the workload on the digestive system, allowing Qi to move downward as it should. Rest and gentle movement conserve and gently circulate Qi, while overwork and lying flat after meals deplete Qi and encourage upward reflux. Worry knots the Spleen Qi, further weakening the very engine that is supposed to keep the throat calm.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat small, warm, cooked meals - think soups, congees, and stews - and avoid cold, raw, or greasy foods that tax your weak digestion. Don't lie down for at least two hours after eating. Gentle daily movement like walking helps Qi flow downward, and getting enough rest conserves your Spleen Qi.

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Burning throat pain or heartburn, Dry mouth and throat, worse at night, Hunger but no desire to eat, Dull burning pain in the upper abdomen, Dry stools or constipation, Red tongue with little coating, dry centre
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Greasy, spicy, or fried foods, Alcohol and coffee, Late-night eating, Emotional stress, anger, or worry, Dry, heated indoor air
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Sipping warm water or herbal tea, Moistening foods (pears, congee), Rest, relaxation, or stress reduction, Cool, humid air
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When the stomach’s nourishing Yin fluids are depleted - often from chronic heat, spicy diets, or prolonged stress - the throat loses its natural lubrication. This dryness allows even small amounts of stomach acid to cause intense irritation, producing a raw, burning sensation and a dry cough. The stomach itself becomes uncomfortable, with a gnawing hunger that food fails to satisfy.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red, thin body, dry centre, little or no coating with possible cracks. Pulse: fine (Xi) and rapid (Shu).

Spicy food, alcohol, and late nights consume it like a flame boiling away water, leaving the throat dry and vulnerable to reflux. Rest, moistening foods, and sipping fluids gently replenish that reserve, calming the heat and coating the throat so irritation subsides.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Focus on moist, easily digestible foods like congee, pears, and steamed vegetables. Avoid spicy, fried, or drying foods that consume Yin. Sip warm water throughout the day rather than gulping large amounts. Prioritize rest and a regular sleep schedule to allow the body to replenish its fluids.

## คำถามที่พบบ่อย (Frequently Asked Questions)
### Can acupuncture really stop silent reflux?
Acupuncture doesn't just suppress acid - it helps retrain the body's directional flow of Qi. By needling points like Neiguan PC-6 and Zusanli ST-36, practitioners aim to calm rebellious Stomach Qi and relax the diaphragm, reducing the upward pressure that drives reflux into the throat. Many patients notice that the lump-in-throat sensation softens and throat clearing decreases within the first few sessions, though lasting change usually requires a series of treatments combined with herbs and dietary adjustments.

### How long does it take for Chinese herbs to work for LPR?
Most people begin to feel a shift within two to four weeks - less throat irritation, fewer episodes of reflux, or a reduction in that stuck sensation. Excess patterns like Liver Qi stagnation or Qi-Phlegm often respond more quickly, while deficiency patterns like Spleen Qi weakness or Stomach Yin deficiency may take two to three months to rebuild the underlying reserves. Herbs are usually taken daily in a decoction or granule form, and consistency is key.

### Is it safe to combine TCM with my PPI medication?
Yes, TCM and PPIs are often used together safely, especially in the early stages of treatment. The herbs work on the root imbalance while the PPI provides symptomatic acid control. As your symptoms improve, you and your doctor may discuss slowly tapering the PPI - but never stop it abruptly. Always inform both your TCM practitioner and your prescribing doctor about all medications and supplements you are taking.

### What does the 'lump in the throat' feeling mean in TCM?
That sensation, called plum-pit Qi, is a hallmark of the Qi-Phlegm pattern. It arises when emotional stress causes Liver Qi to stagnate, which then congeals fluids into Phlegm that gets stuck in the throat. The lump often comes and goes with stress, and you may feel a need to constantly clear your throat. Treatment focuses on moving Qi and resolving Phlegm with formulas like Ban Xia Hou Pu Tang, combined with acupuncture points that open the chest and throat.

### Can stress really cause reflux?
