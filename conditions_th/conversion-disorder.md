## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนปัจจุบัน (Conventional Medicine View)
### คำอธิบาย (Description)
Conversion disorder, also called functional neurological symptom disorder, is a condition where psychological stress or conflict manifests as physical symptoms that cannot be explained by a medical condition. These symptoms - such as paralysis, tremors, blindness, or seizures - are not intentionally produced and cause significant distress.
Diagnosis typically involves ruling out neurological or other medical causes through clinical examination and testing. The symptoms are real and can be debilitating, but they do not match known anatomical pathways, which is a hallmark of the disorder.

### การรักษาหลัก (Treatments)
Standard treatment often includes psychotherapy, particularly cognitive behavioral therapy (CBT), to address underlying psychological stressors. Physical therapy and occupational therapy may help manage motor symptoms. Medications like antidepressants or anti-anxiety drugs are sometimes used for co-occurring conditions, but there is no specific medication to 'cure' conversion disorder itself.

## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนจีน (Traditional Chinese Medicine View)
### ความเข้าใจเกี่ยวกับการแพทย์แผนจีน (TCM Understanding)
In TCM, conversion disorder is understood through the lens of emotional and energetic imbalance. The Liver is central - it governs the smooth flow of Qi and emotions. When stress, frustration, or suppressed feelings disrupt this flow, Qi stagnates. Over time, this stagnation can generate Heat, which rises to disturb the Heart and mind.
That Heat-driven agitation triggers dramatic emotional outbursts and sudden physical symptoms like numbness or paralysis. This is the Liver Qi Stagnation turning to Heat pattern.
But the mind’s clarity also depends on the Spleen’s ability to transform fluids. If Qi stagnation weakens the Spleen, Phlegm can form - a heavy, turbid substance that clouds the Heart orifice. This Phlegm Misting the Heart pattern produces mental fog, confusion, and sensory distortions, often with a thick tongue coating.
In other cases, chronic worry and overwork drain the Heart and Spleen of Qi and Blood, leaving the mind ungrounded and prone to converting emotional distress into weakness or numbness. And when the deepest reserves - Kidney Essence - are depleted, the brain loses its anchor, leading to fatigue, memory loss, and a breakdown of the mind-body connection. Each pattern calls for a different treatment strategy.

### การวินิจฉัยโดยแพทย์แผนจีน (Practitioner Diagnosis)
A practitioner begins by listening closely to the emotional and physical story. Conversion disorder symptoms can look very different from person to person, so the first step is to understand what triggers the episode, how it feels in the body, and whether the person tends toward agitation, foggy-headedness, or exhaustion. These clues point toward one of the underlying patterns.
When the dominant feeling is irritability, chest tightness, a hot sensation, and a bitter taste in the mouth, the picture suggests Liver Qi Stagnation that has transformed into Heat. The tongue tends to be red with a yellow coating, and the pulse feels wiry and rapid. The person often describes their symptoms as flaring up with stress or suppressed anger.
If the person reports a heavy, cloudy sensation in the head, dizziness, chest oppression, and a sense of mental fog or confusion, Phlegm Misting the Heart is more likely. The tongue coating is thick and greasy, and the pulse is slippery. This pattern often produces sensory distortions or a feeling of being disconnected from one's surroundings.
A third common presentation is one of depletion: profound fatigue, poor appetite, palpitations, and broken sleep. These signs point to Heart and Spleen Deficiency, where worry and overwork have drained the Qi and Blood that nourish the mind. The tongue looks pale with a thin white coating, and the pulse is weak and thready.
In more chronic or long-standing cases, Kidney Essence Deficiency may be at the root. Here the person complains of memory loss, low back and knee soreness, and a deep, unrefreshing tiredness. The tongue is pale with little coating, and the pulse is thin and slow. This pattern reflects a deeper, constitutional drain that fails to support the brain and marrow.
When the hallmark symptom is a persistent sensation of a lump in the throat that cannot be swallowed down (plum-pit qi, 梅核气), the pattern is Qi-Phlegm. This arises when Liver stagnation and Spleen weakness combine to generate Phlegm that obstructs the throat. The tongue coating is white and greasy, and the pulse is often slippery or wiry. The practitioner checks for this specific sensation alongside emotional tension.

### Liver Qi Stagnation that transforms into Heat
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/liver-qi-stagnation-that-transforms-into-heat)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Irritability and explosive anger, Bitter taste in the mouth, Distending pain in the ribcage, Red face and eyes, Headache at the temples, Restlessness and agitation
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Stress and frustration, Greasy, heavy, or spicy foods, Alcohol and caffeine, Hot weather, Overwork and lack of sleep
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Cooling foods and drinks, Gentle exercise or movement, Stress reduction and emotional support, Cool environment, Deep breathing
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When frustration and emotional repression are held in, the Liver's Qi flow becomes stuck. Over time this stagnation generates Heat, which rises to disturb the Heart and mind. This heat-driven agitation produces the dramatic emotional outbursts, chest tightness, and sudden physical symptoms-like numbness or paralysis-that characterize conversion disorder.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red body, especially redder at the sides, with a yellow coating. Pulse: wiry and rapid (弦数, xián shuò), forceful at the left middle position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Emotional stress and frustration are the root cause of Liver Qi stagnation. When Qi is stuck, it generates Heat, which flares upward to disturb the mind and trigger the explosive symptoms of conversion disorder. Cooling foods and a calm environment help clear that Heat, while gentle movement and relaxation techniques keep Qi flowing smoothly, preventing stagnation from building up again.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Steer clear of spicy, fried, and greasy foods, as well as alcohol and coffee-these add Heat. Embrace cooling choices like chrysanthemum tea, mung bean soup, and plenty of leafy greens. Gentle daily movement like walking or tai chi helps keep Liver Qi flowing, and a regular wind-down routine before bed calms the mind.

### Phlegm Misting the Heart
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/phlegm-misting-the-heart)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Mental confusion or clouded consciousness, Gurgling throat, excessive saliva, Thick white greasy tongue coating, Chest stuffiness and heaviness
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Greasy, heavy, or spicy foods, Damp weather, Worry and overthinking, Sedentary lifestyle
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warm, easily digested meals and drinks, Gentle exercise or movement, Warm, dry environment, Mental stimulation
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When Qi stagnates and the Spleen fails to transform fluids, Phlegm accumulates. This thick, turbid substance then rises and clouds the Heart orifice - the seat of the mind - leading to mental confusion, sensory distortions, and the physical symptoms of conversion disorder such as slurred speech, blank staring, or the sensation of a lump in the throat.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale and swollen with a thick, white, greasy coating; may appear stiff. Pulse: slippery (滑 huá), especially strong at the Guan positions.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Phlegm is a thick, sticky substance formed when Qi stagnates and the Spleen fails to transform fluids. Heavy, greasy foods and damp environments overload the Spleen and create more Phlegm, while overthinking knots Qi and worsens stagnation. Light meals, gentle movement, and a dry environment help the Spleen function and transform Phlegm, clearing the mind.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Avoid heavy, greasy, and cold foods that generate Dampness and Phlegm. Favor warm, lightly cooked meals like congee with ginger. Gentle daily movement such as walking helps Qi flow and transforms Phlegm. Keep the mind active but calm - avoid excessive rumination.

### Heart and Spleen Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/heart-and-spleen-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Fatigue and lack of energy, Poor appetite and bloating, Palpitations, Insomnia with vivid dreams, Pale complexion, Loose stools
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Worry and overthinking, Irregular eating or skipping meals, Raw and cold foods, Overwork and lack of sleep
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Rest and relaxation, Warm, easily digested meals and drinks, Gentle exercise or movement
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Excessive worry and mental strain weaken the Spleen's ability to transform food into Qi and Blood. Without enough Blood, the Heart cannot properly house the mind (Shen), leaving it ungrounded and prone to converting emotional distress into physical symptoms like weakness, numbness, or sensory loss. The Spleen's deficiency also causes poor appetite, bloating, and loose stools, which further deplete the body's resources and deepen the fatigue.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale, tender body with teeth marks and a thin white coating. Pulse: fine and weak, especially weak in the left cun (Heart) and right guan (Spleen) positions.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: The Spleen is easily damaged by irregular eating and mental strain, so warm, regular meals and rest directly rebuild Qi and Blood. The Heart houses the mind; calming the mind through relaxation and sleep allows the Shen to settle, reducing palpitations and insomnia. Overwork and worry further deplete the very resources that are already lacking, which is why they worsen symptoms.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat warm, cooked meals at regular times, focusing on easily digestible foods like soups, congee, and stewed vegetables. Avoid raw, cold, and greasy foods that tax the Spleen. Gentle daily movement like walking or Qi Gong helps build Qi without draining it. Prioritize rest and calming activities before bed to support the Heart.

### Kidney Essence Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/kidney-essence-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Soreness and weakness of the lower back and knees, Poor memory and difficulty concentrating, Tinnitus or gradual hearing loss, Premature greying or hair loss, Deep fatigue and lack of vitality
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Overwork and lack of sleep, Excessive sexual activity, Raw and cold foods, Prolonged standing or physical strain
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Rest and relaxation, Warm, easily digested meals and drinks, Gentle exercise or movement, Warm environment
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Kidney Essence is the body's deepest reserve, responsible for nourishing the brain, marrow, and the very foundation of the mind-body connection. When this essence is chronically depleted through overwork, aging, or constitutional weakness, the brain loses its anchor, leading to mental fog, memory loss, and a fragile nervous system. This profound exhaustion can manifest as the weakness, numbness, or pseudo-seizures seen in conversion disorder, as the physical body mirrors the deep emptiness of the spirit.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale, thin body with little or no coating, possible fine cracks. Pulse: fine and deep, especially weak at the Chi (rear) positions.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Kidney Essence is a deep, slow-to-replenish resource. Overwork, insufficient sleep, and excessive sexual activity directly drain it, while warm, nourishing foods and adequate rest allow the body to slowly rebuild its reserves. Gentle movement helps circulate Qi without further depletion, unlike strenuous exercise which can exhaust the essence.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Prioritize deep rest and avoid overwork. Include bone broths, black sesame seeds, walnuts, and goji berries in your diet to gently nourish essence. Gentle practices like Tai Chi or Qi Gong can help conserve and circulate Qi without depleting reserves.

### Qi-Phlegm
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/qi-phlegm)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Sensation of a lump in the throat that cannot be swallowed or coughed out, Chest and upper abdominal oppression or stuffiness, Symptoms worsen with emotional stress, Frequent sighing and moodiness, Nausea or queasiness
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Stress and frustration, Worry and overthinking, Greasy, heavy, or spicy foods, Raw and cold foods, Sedentary lifestyle
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warm, easily digested meals and drinks, Deep breathing, Gentle exercise or movement, Stress reduction and emotional support
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Emotional stress, worry, and frustration cause the Liver Qi to stagnate. This stagnation weakens the Spleen's ability to transform fluids, which then accumulate into Phlegm. The Phlegm rises and obstructs the throat, producing the classic sensation of a lump that cannot be swallowed or coughed out. When Phlegm further clouds the Heart orifice or blocks the channels, it can trigger the numbness, paralysis, or sudden emotional outbursts seen in conversion disorder.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: normal pink-red body, possibly slightly swollen, with a white greasy or slippery coating. Pulse: wiry (Xian) and slippery (Hua).
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Emotional stress tightens Liver Qi, causing it to stagnate and then produce Phlegm. Damp, heavy foods weaken the Spleen, adding more Phlegm. Warmth, movement, and deep breathing help Qi flow and dissolve stagnation, while emotional calm prevents the cycle from restarting.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Avoid damp-producing foods like dairy, greasy fried dishes, and excessive sweets. Eat warm, light, easily digestible meals. Gentle daily movement such as walking or yoga helps keep Qi flowing. Prioritize stress reduction and emotional expression, as bottled-up feelings directly worsen this pattern.

## คำถามที่พบบ่อย (Frequently Asked Questions)
### Can acupuncture really help with paralysis or seizures from conversion disorder?
Yes, many patients experience significant improvement. In TCM, these symptoms are seen as manifestations of Qi stagnation, Phlegm obstruction, or deficiency - not as permanent damage. Acupuncture works by unblocking channels and calming the mind, which can restore normal function. It's common to see gradual reduction in the intensity and frequency of episodes after several sessions.

### How does TCM treat the emotional side of conversion disorder?
TCM doesn't separate emotions from the body. Herbs and acupuncture that soothe the Liver, clear Heat, or nourish the Heart directly calm the mind. For example, Liver Qi Stagnation with Heat is treated with formulas that cool and move, which reduces irritability and outbursts. As the physical symptoms ease, emotional stability often follows because the same underlying imbalance is being addressed.

### Is it safe to combine TCM herbs with my antidepressants?
Generally yes, but you must tell both your TCM practitioner and your doctor about all medications you're taking. Some herbs, like Dang Gui or Chai Hu, can have mild blood-moving or sedative effects that may interact with certain drugs. A qualified TCM practitioner will adjust the formula to avoid interactions. Never stop or reduce your prescribed medication without your doctor's guidance.

### How long until I see results?
Many patients notice some improvement within the first 2-4 weeks - perhaps better sleep, less chest tightness, or milder episodes. Full stabilization can take several months, especially for deficiency patterns that require rebuilding the body's energy reserves. Consistency is key: weekly acupuncture and daily herbs work together to create lasting change.

### Will the symptoms come back after treatment?
When the underlying pattern is fully resolved, recurrence is unlikely. TCM aims to correct the root imbalance, not just suppress symptoms. However, if the original emotional or lifestyle triggers return, some patients may need periodic tune-ups. Your practitioner will teach you self-care strategies - like acupressure, dietary tips, and stress management - to maintain balance.

### Do I need to change my diet?
Dietary adjustments support your treatment. In general, avoid greasy, fried, or overly spicy foods that create Phlegm and Heat. Warm, cooked meals like soups and rice porridge are easier to digest and help the Spleen generate Qi. Reducing caffeine and alcohol can calm the Liver. Your practitioner may give you more specific guidance based on your pattern.

### Can TCM help if I've had these symptoms for years?
Yes, chronic conditions often respond well to TCM because it addresses deep-seated imbalances. Long-standing conversion symptoms are usually rooted in deficiency or stubborn Phlegm, which take time to reverse. With patience and consistent treatment, many patients experience a significant reduction in symptoms and an improved quality of life, even after years of struggle.
