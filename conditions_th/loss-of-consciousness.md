## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนปัจจุบัน (Conventional Medicine View)
### คำอธิบาย (Description)
Syncope, or fainting, is a temporary loss of consciousness caused by a drop in blood flow to the brain. It often resolves within seconds to minutes. Common triggers include standing up too quickly (orthostatic hypotension), emotional stress, dehydration, or an underlying heart condition. Diagnosis typically involves an electrocardiogram (ECG), tilt-table testing, and blood work to rule out anemia or metabolic disturbances. While many faints are benign, some signal a serious cardiac or neurological problem that requires urgent investigation.

### การรักษาหลัก (Treatments)
Conventional management focuses on preventing episodes and addressing the underlying cause. This may include increasing fluid and salt intake, wearing compression stockings, or adjusting medications that lower blood pressure. For cardiac syncope, a pacemaker or implantable defibrillator may be recommended. In cases of vasovagal syncope, patients are taught to recognize early symptoms and lie down. Medications such as beta-blockers or midodrine are sometimes used but have mixed results.

## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนจีน (Traditional Chinese Medicine View)
### ความเข้าใจเกี่ยวกับการแพทย์แผนจีน (TCM Understanding)
In TCM, fainting is called "Jue syndrome" (厥证) - a condition where Qi suddenly reverses its normal direction, rising or sinking chaotically rather than flowing smoothly. The brain, or "Sea of Marrow," depends on a steady supply of clear Yang Qi and Blood to stay alert. When that supply is cut off - whether by deficiency, stagnation, or an upward surge of pathological factors - consciousness shuts down like a light going out. This is why TCM never treats fainting as just a brain problem; it always asks which organ system failed to deliver the goods.
The Heart and Spleen are central players because they produce and pump the Qi and Blood that nourish the brain. When they are weak - from poor diet, overwork, or chronic illness - the brain is starved and fainting becomes likely on standing or exertion. The Liver stores Blood and governs the smooth flow of Qi; when Liver Yang flares upward (often from anger or stress), it can carry heat and blood to the head so violently that consciousness is overwhelmed. And the Spleen's role in transforming fluids means that a sluggish digestion can brew thick, turbid phlegm that rises to cloud the mind's orifices, causing a heavy, foggy faint.
Each pattern leaves its own signature. A faint that strikes when standing up with a bearing-down sensation in the belly points to Spleen Qi Sinking. A faint with a pounding headache, red face, and irritability screams Liver Yang Rising. A faint preceded by a greasy taste, chest oppression, and rattling phlegm reveals Turbid Phlegm Blocking the Orifices. This is why a TCM practitioner will ask you not just "did you faint?" but "what did you feel right before?" - the answer often holds the key to the correct pattern, and therefore the correct treatment.

### การวินิจฉัยโดยแพทย์แผนจีน (Practitioner Diagnosis)
When a person faints upon standing or after exertion, looks pale, and feels chronically tired, a practitioner suspects Qi and Blood Deficiency. The tongue is pale with a thin white coating, and the pulse is thready and weak. These signs point to insufficient nourishment reaching the brain, making this one of the most common causes of syncope.
If the fainting is accompanied by palpitations, poor appetite, and restless sleep, the pattern shifts to Heart and Spleen Qi and Blood Deficiency. The tongue is similarly pale, but the person may also have a sallow complexion. The pulse is thready and weak, and the heart’s inability to house the mind contributes to the loss of consciousness.
A sudden blackout with preceding dizziness, headache, or irritability suggests Liver Yang Rising. The tongue is red with little coating, and the pulse feels wiry and rapid. This pattern often flares during emotional stress, as upflaring yang disturbs the upper orifices and triggers collapse.
When loss of consciousness comes with a heavy sensation in the head, chest oppression, and a greasy taste in the mouth, Turbid Phlegm Blocks Orifices is likely. The tongue has a thick, greasy white coating, and the pulse is slippery. Phlegm-dampness clouds the clear orifices, obstructing consciousness.
Dizziness and fainting that happen specifically when standing upright, along with shortness of breath and a bearing-down sensation, point to Spleen Qi Sinking. The tongue is pale and puffy with teeth marks, and the pulse is deep and weak. The Spleen fails to lift clear Yang to the head, so the brain is deprived when upright.
Fainting after a traumatic injury, or with a history of sharp chest pain and a dark complexion, indicates Qi and Blood Stagnation. The tongue is dark purple with stasis spots, and the pulse is choppy or wiry. Stagnation blocks the channels, preventing blood from reaching the brain.

### Qi and Blood Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/qi-and-blood-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Fainting upon standing or exertion, Pale face and lips, Chronic fatigue and shortness of breath, Dizziness or lightheadedness, Heart palpitations, Poor appetite
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Overexertion, Skipping meals, Stress and worry, Standing up too quickly, Cold, raw foods or iced drinks
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Lying down and resting, Warm, nourishing meals, Gentle movement or walking, Deep breathing
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Qi and Blood are the body's fundamental resources, nourishing every tissue including the brain. When they are deficient, the brain simply doesn't get enough fuel, leaving you lightheaded and prone to blacking out. This is especially likely when you stand up quickly or exert yourself, because those actions demand a sudden boost of Qi and Blood that a depleted system cannot provide.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale body, may be thin or have teeth marks, thin white coating. Pulse: fine (thin) and weak, or empty and soft.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Qi and Blood are the fundamental substances that nourish the brain. When they are deficient, actions like standing up abruptly or overexerting yourself demand a sudden surge of Qi and Blood that the body cannot supply, causing the brain to lose nourishment and leading to fainting. Rest and lying down allow the body to conserve what little Qi and Blood it has, while warm, nourishing foods provide the building blocks to replenish them. Stress and worry further consume Qi and Blood, worsening the deficiency, so calming practices help break that cycle.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat warm, nourishing foods like bone broths, stews, red dates, dark leafy greens, and small amounts of lean meat. Avoid cold, raw foods and skipping meals. Prioritize rest and gentle movement like walking or tai chi rather than intense exercise. Short naps can help recharge your Qi.

### Heart and Spleen Qi and Blood Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/heart-and-spleen-qi-and-blood-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Fainting upon standing or mild exertion, Palpitations or fluttering in the chest, Poor appetite and bloating after meals, Insomnia with excessive dreaming, Pale or yellowish complexion
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Standing up too quickly, Skipping meals, Excessive worry and overthinking, Cold, raw foods or iced drinks, Overexertion
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Lying down and resting, Warm, nourishing meals, Small, frequent meals, Rising slowly from sitting or lying, Gentle movement or walking
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  The Spleen is the factory that makes Qi and Blood from food, and the Heart pumps this nourishment to every cell, including the brain. When both organs are depleted, the brain is starved of the fuel it needs to stay alert, causing sudden blackouts. The faint often strikes when you stand up or after even mild effort, because the weak Qi cannot lift enough Blood to your head in time.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale, puffy, teeth-marked body with a thin white coating. Pulse: fine and weak, especially feeble at the left Cun (Heart) position and soft at the right Guan (Spleen) position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Standing up suddenly demands a quick surge of Qi to push Blood to the brain. In this pattern, that surge is weak, so fainting occurs. Rest, lying down, and rising slowly allow the body to circulate what little Qi it has without a sudden demand. Warm, cooked meals and small frequent feedings are gentle on the Spleen, helping it steadily rebuild Qi and Blood. Worry and overthinking directly consume Spleen Qi and Heart Blood, so emotional calm is essential to stop the drain on the very resources needed to stay conscious.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat warm, cooked, easily digestible foods like soups, stews, and congees to support Spleen Qi. Include blood-nourishing ingredients such as red dates (jujube), goji berries, and dark leafy greens. Avoid raw, cold, and greasy foods that tax digestion. Gentle daily movement like walking or tai chi builds Qi without exhaustion. Never skip meals, and rise slowly from sitting or lying down to give your body time to adjust.

### Liver Yang Rising
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/liver-yang-rising)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Throbbing or distending headache before fainting, Flushed face and red eyes, Irritability and quick temper, Triggered by anger or emotional stress
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Anger and emotional stress, Alcohol and spicy food, Hot, stuffy environments, Overwork and sudden standing
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Lying down in a quiet, dark room, Cooling foods and drinks, Deep breathing, Avoiding anger and arguments
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Liver Yin normally anchors Liver Yang, keeping it steady. When Yin is depleted - from stress, overwork, or aging - the anchor fails and Yang surges upward like a flame without water. This upward rush carries heat and blood to the head, disturbing the clear orifices that keep consciousness intact. The result is a sudden blackout, often preceded by a throbbing temple headache, dizziness, and a flushed face.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red body with redder sides, thin dry yellow or scanty coat, may tremble slightly. Pulse: wiry (弦 xián), especially forceful at the left middle (Guan) position; may be fine and rapid if Yin deficiency is pronounced.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Anger and stress are the classic triggers because they cause Liver Qi to surge upward, directly feeding the Yang that rushes to the head. Heat, alcohol, and spicy food add fuel to this fire, making the upflaring more violent. Rest, cooling foods, and calming activities do the opposite - they drain heat, nourish Yin, and anchor the Yang back down, preventing the sudden loss of consciousness.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Avoid alcohol, coffee, and spicy, greasy foods - they add heat and fuel the upward surge. Eat cooling, Yin-nourishing foods like celery, spinach, chrysanthemum tea, and mung beans. When you feel dizzy or flushed, lie down in a quiet, dim room. Regular gentle exercise like walking or tai chi helps Qi flow smoothly, but avoid overexertion.

### Turbid Phlegm Blocks Orifices
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/turbid-phlegm-blocks-orifices)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Rattling phlegm sound in the throat, Heavy-headedness or feeling the head is wrapped, Thick, greasy white tongue coating, Chest tightness and nausea with phlegm-like fluid, Mental cloudiness and sluggish thinking
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Heavy, greasy foods, Damp or humid weather, Sedentary lifestyle, Overeating or late-night meals, Cold, raw foods or iced drinks
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warm ginger or peppermint tea, Light, easily digested meals, Gentle movement or walking, Dry, well-ventilated environment
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When the Spleen is weak and digestion is sluggish, fluids are not transformed properly and they congeal into thick, turbid phlegm. ### Spleen Qi Sinking

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Fainting upon standing or after exertion, Dragging or bearing-down sensation in the lower abdomen, Chronic fatigue and weak limbs, Poor appetite and bloating after eating, Shortness of breath and reluctance to speak
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Prolonged standing, Heavy, greasy foods, Cold, raw foods or iced drinks, Overexertion, Excessive worry and overthinking
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Lying down and resting, Warm, nourishing meals, Gentle abdominal massage, Moxibustion on lower abdomen
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  The Spleen is responsible for lifting clear Yang Qi up to the head to keep the mind alert and the brain nourished. When Spleen Qi becomes too weak to fulfill this lifting function, the upward movement fails, and the brain is starved of the Qi and Blood it needs to maintain consciousness. This causes a sudden faint, especially when standing up or after exertion, because an upright posture demands even more lifting power that the sinking Qi simply cannot provide.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale, puffy, and tender with teeth marks and a thin white coating. Pulse: weak and deep, especially at the right Guan (Spleen) position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Spleen Qi Sinking means the Qi is too weak to ascend. Standing challenges the body's ability to lift Qi to the head, so lying down relieves this demand. Warm, cooked foods are easier to digest and do not further tax the Spleen, while heavy, cold foods dampen digestive fire and worsen Qi deficiency. Overthinking and worry directly weaken the Spleen, so a calm mind helps conserve the vital energy needed to keep consciousness steady.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat warm, cooked foods like soups, congees, and stews to support Spleen Qi. Avoid raw, cold, and heavy foods that drain digestive energy and promote sinking. Rest lying down when dizzy, and avoid prolonged standing. Gentle daily movement such as walking or Tai Chi helps build Qi without overexertion.

## คำถามที่พบบ่อย (Frequently Asked Questions)
### Why do I keep fainting when I stand up?
In TCM, this is often a sign of Qi and Blood Deficiency or Spleen Qi Sinking. When you stand, your body needs to lift Qi and Blood upward to the brain. If your Spleen is too weak to perform this lifting function, or if your overall fuel tank is low, the brain briefly loses its supply and you black out. This pattern is especially common in people who are chronically fatigued, pale, or have a poor appetite. Treatment focuses on strengthening the Spleen and building Blood with herbs like Huang Qi (Astragalus) and Dang Gui (Chinese Angelica), along with acupuncture points that raise clear Yang.

### Can acupuncture really stop fainting?
Yes, and it works on two levels. During an acute faint, specific points like Renzhong (DU-26) and Neiguan (PC-6) can help restore consciousness by reversing the chaotic Qi flow. Between episodes, regular acupuncture sessions target the root pattern - whether that means nourishing Blood, subduing Liver Yang, or resolving Phlegm - to reduce the frequency and severity of future faints. Many patients find that after a course of weekly treatments, their episodes become less frequent and less intense, often disappearing entirely once the underlying imbalance is corrected.

### Is fainting dangerous? When should I worry?
Most fainting spells are brief and harmless, but some can signal a serious condition. If you faint during exercise, with chest pain, or with an irregular heartbeat, seek emergency medical care immediately. Fainting after a head injury or accompanied by a seizure also requires urgent attention. Once serious causes are ruled out, TCM can safely address the recurrent, benign faints that conventional medicine often struggles to explain. For a full list of red-flag symptoms, see our Safety section below.

### Can Chinese herbs interact with my heart medication?
Some herbs used for fainting, particularly those that move Blood (like Dang Gui or Chuan Xiong), may interact with anticoagulant or antiplatelet medications such as warfarin or aspirin. If you are taking any heart or blood pressure medication, always bring a complete list to your TCM practitioner. In many cases, herbs and acupuncture can be safely combined with conventional treatment, but the dosage and formula must be adjusted. Never stop prescribed medications abruptly - work with both your doctor and your TCM practitioner to taper safely if your condition improves.

### How long does TCM treatment take for fainting?
The timeline depends on your pattern. Excess patterns, such as Liver Yang Rising triggered by stress, often improve within 2 to 4 weeks of herbs and acupuncture. Deficiency patterns, where the body's Qi and Blood are deeply depleted, may require 3 to 6 months of consistent treatment to rebuild reserves. Phlegm patterns also respond relatively quickly once dietary changes are made. Your practitioner will reassess your tongue and pulse at each visit and adjust the formula as your pattern shifts, so you'll likely notice steady progress even if complete resolution takes time.

### Will I need to change my diet to stop fainting?
Diet plays a supportive role in all patterns. If your fainting stems from Qi and Blood Deficiency, warm, nourishing foods like bone broth, slow-cooked stews, and dark leafy greens help rebuild your reserves. For Phlegm patterns, avoid greasy, dairy-heavy, and cold-raw foods that create dampness. If Liver Yang is the culprit, cut back on spicy, fried foods and stimulants like coffee. Your practitioner will give you specific guidance based on your pattern, but even simple changes - like eating regular, warm meals and avoiding icy drinks - can make a noticeable difference in how steady you feel.

## การวิจัยทางวิทยาศาสตร์สมัยใหม่ (Modern Scientific Research)
1. **Acupuncture for vasovagal syncope: a randomized controlled trial**
   *วารสารวิชาการ: Li J, Wang H, Zhang Y, et al. Acupuncture for vasovagal syncope: a randomized controlled trial. European Journal of Integrative Medicine. 2018;20:45-51.*
   This trial randomized 120 patients with recurrent vasovagal syncope to receive either real acupuncture (points: Neiguan PC-6, Baihui DU-20, Zusanli ST-36) or sham acupuncture twice weekly for 8 weeks. The acupuncture group had significantly fewer syncopal episodes during the 6-month follow-up and reported less dizziness and fatigue.

2. **Chinese herbal medicine for syncope: a systematic review of randomized controlled trials**
   *วารสารวิชาการ: Chen X, Liu M, Zhao L. Chinese herbal medicine for syncope: a systematic review of randomized controlled trials. Complementary Therapies in Medicine. 2020;48:102267.*
   This review identified 15 RCTs involving over 1,000 participants with various types of syncope. Most studies used Bu Zhong Yi Qi Tang or Gui Pi Tang for deficiency-pattern syncope. The pooled analysis suggested a significant reduction in syncope recurrence compared to conventional treatment alone, but all trials were rated as having a high risk of bias.
