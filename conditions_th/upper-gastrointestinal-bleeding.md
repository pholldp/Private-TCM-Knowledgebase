## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนปัจจุบัน (Conventional Medicine View)
### คำอธิบาย (Description)
Upper gastrointestinal bleeding refers to bleeding that originates from the esophagus, stomach, or duodenum. It is a medical emergency that can present as vomiting bright red blood, dark 'coffee-ground' material, or passing black, tarry stools. Causes include peptic ulcers, gastritis, esophageal varices, and Mallory-Weiss tears. Prompt diagnosis and treatment are essential to prevent shock and other complications.

### การรักษาหลัก (Treatments)
Emergency care focuses on stabilizing the patient with intravenous fluids and blood transfusions if needed. Medications such as proton pump inhibitors are given to reduce stomach acid and promote clot formation. Endoscopy is both diagnostic and therapeutic - it can inject medications, apply clips, or cauterize the bleeding vessel. In severe or recurrent cases, interventional radiology or surgery may be required. Long-term management targets the underlying cause, such as antibiotics for H. pylori or acid-suppressing drugs.

## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนจีน (Traditional Chinese Medicine View)
### ความเข้าใจเกี่ยวกับการแพทย์แผนจีน (TCM Understanding)
TCM sees vomiting blood as a rebellion of Stomach Qi - the normal downward movement of the digestive tract reverses, and blood follows the upward surge. The Stomach is the central pivot, but it rarely acts alone. Heat is the most common culprit: either direct Stomach Fire from spicy food and alcohol, or Liver Fire that rages upward after emotional frustration and invades the Stomach, scorching its blood vessels.
In both cases, the heat acts like a fire that boils over, forcing blood out of its channels.
But not all bleeding is driven by heat. Sometimes the blood simply leaks out because the container is too weak. The Spleen is responsible for holding blood inside the vessels, and when Spleen Qi is depleted - from overwork, chronic illness, or poor diet - it can no longer contain it. This produces a pale, thin bleeding with profound fatigue. Another deficiency pattern arises when the body's cooling Yin fluids are exhausted, creating a false, empty heat that flares up and damages the stomach lining, causing scanty bright-red bleeding with night sweats and a dry mouth.
Even the quality of the blood itself matters. Chronic stagnation can make the vessels brittle, leading to dark, clotted blood that pools in the stomach before being vomited. This is a pattern of Stomach Blood Stagnation, where the blood doesn't flow smoothly and instead congeals and leaks. So the same symptom - vomiting blood - can be caused by a raging fire, a weak container, a dry internal climate, or sluggish circulation. Each requires a different treatment, which is why TCM practitioners pay close attention to the color of the blood, the pain, the thirst, and the emotional state before deciding on a strategy.

### การวินิจฉัยโดยแพทย์แผนจีน (Practitioner Diagnosis)
A TCM practitioner begins by carefully observing the blood itself-its color, volume, and consistency-and asking about any pain, thirst, emotional state, and energy levels. These clues point toward the underlying imbalance driving the bleeding.
If the blood is bright red or dark purple, with a burning pain in the stomach, intense thirst, bad breath, and constipation, the pattern is likely Stomach Fire. The tongue will be red with a thick yellow coating, and the pulse will feel rapid and slippery. This points to excessive heat forcing blood upward.
When the blood is dark or fresh red and comes with sharp pain under the ribs, a bitter taste in the mouth, and a short temper, Liver Fire attacking the Stomach is suspected. The tongue is red with a yellow coat, and the pulse is wiry and rapid, reflecting the upward surge of angry heat.
If the vomiting is recurrent, the blood pale and thin, and the person feels exhausted, with a pale face and weak limbs, the root is Spleen not controlling Blood. The tongue is pale with a thin white coat, and the pulse is weak and thready. This is a deficiency pattern where the Spleen lacks the strength to hold blood in the vessels.
Scanty, bright-red blood accompanied by a sensation of heat in the palms, soles, and chest, night sweats, and a dry mouth suggests Empty-Heat from Yin Deficiency. The tongue is red with little or no coating, and the pulse is thready and rapid. This is a deeper, chronic imbalance where the body's cooling fluids are depleted.
Dark, clotted blood with a fixed, stabbing pain in the stomach area points to Stomach Blood Stagnation. The tongue may be purplish with dark spots, and the pulse feels choppy. This pattern indicates that blood has been pooling and congealing in the stomach vessels for some time.

### Stomach Fire (Stomach Heat)
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/stomach-fire-stomach-heat)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Burning pain in the upper stomach area, Vomiting bright red or dark purple blood, Foul breath and strong hunger or appetite, Thirst with desire for cold drinks, Constipation with dry, hard stools
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Spicy, greasy, or fried foods, Alcohol and coffee, Anger and emotional stress, Overeating or eating too fast, Hot weather or overheated rooms
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Cool drinks, Bland, easy-to-digest foods, Rest and lying down, Avoiding alcohol and spices
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When excess heat builds up in the stomach - often from rich, spicy food, alcohol, or intense emotions - it acts like a fire inside. That fire can damage the tiny blood vessels in the stomach wall, forcing blood to rush upward instead of staying in its proper channels. The result is vomiting of blood that is typically bright red or dark purple, often with a burning sensation in the upper belly.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: Red body with possible central prickles and a thick, dry yellow coating. Pulse: Rapid, slippery, and forceful - especially strong at the right Guan position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Stomach Fire is fueled by heat - from food, drink, and emotions - that rises upward and damages blood vessels. Cooling foods and drinks directly counter that heat, while rest and calm prevent the Qi from rushing upward. Spicy, fatty foods and alcohol add more fuel to the fire, and anger stirs Liver Qi to attack the Stomach, making bleeding worse.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Avoid alcohol, coffee, fried foods, chili, and lamb - anything that adds heat. Eat cooling, moistening foods like cucumber, watermelon, pear, and congee. Eat small, calm meals and never skip them. Gentle walks after eating help Qi descend.

### Liver Fire Blazing
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/liver-fire-blazing)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Bitter taste in the mouth, Rib pain or distension, Irritability and short temper, Vomiting blood triggered by anger or stress, Throbbing headache at temples
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Anger and emotional stress, Spicy, greasy, or fried foods, Alcohol and coffee, Hot weather or overheated rooms, Overwork and late nights
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Cool drinks, Calm, quiet environment, Bitter vegetables like dandelion greens, Gentle meditation or breathing exercises, Applying a cool compress to the forehead
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Emotional frustration and suppressed anger cause Liver Qi to stagnate and transform into Fire. This Liver Fire surges upward, invading the Stomach and scorching its blood vessels. The heat forces blood to rebel upward, resulting in vomiting of fresh red or dark purple blood. The characteristic rib pain, bitter taste, and irritability reflect the Liver's involvement.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red body with redder sides, dry yellow coating, possible red prickles. Pulse: wiry (弦 xián) and rapid (数 shuò), forceful in the left middle position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Anger and emotional stress cause Liver Qi to stagnate and transform into Fire, which then attacks the Stomach and forces blood upward. Cooling foods and drinks directly counteract the excess Heat, while bitter foods specifically drain Liver Fire. Calm environments and relaxation techniques help smooth the flow of Liver Qi, preventing it from surging. Spicy and greasy foods, alcohol, and hot environments add more Heat to the body, worsening the Fire. Overwork and lack of sleep deplete Yin, making it harder to anchor the Yang, so rest is essential to restore balance.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Avoid spicy, greasy, and deep-fried foods, as well as alcohol and coffee. Eat cooling, bitter foods like dandelion greens, chrysanthemum tea, and mung beans. Practice stress management techniques such as meditation or gentle yoga. Ensure adequate rest and avoid late nights, which deplete Yin and fuel Fire.

### Spleen not controlling Blood
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/spleen-not-controlling-blood)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Pale, thin, watery vomited blood, Vomiting recurs after exertion or prolonged illness, Profound fatigue and physical exhaustion, Pale or sallow complexion, Poor appetite and loose stools
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Overwork and late nights, Worry and overthinking, Raw, cold foods and drinks, Irregular eating or skipping meals
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Deep rest and sleep, Warm, cooked, bland meals, Gentle, regular meals
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  The Spleen is responsible for holding blood inside the vessels. When Spleen Qi becomes weak-often from overwork, prolonged illness, or poor diet-it can no longer contain the blood. This allows blood to leak out, and in the upper digestive tract it rises upward, producing vomiting of pale, thin blood that recurs when energy is low.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale, puffy, tender body with teeth marks, thin white coating. Pulse: fine and weak, especially at the right Guan (Spleen) position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Overwork and mental strain deplete Spleen Qi, the very root of this pattern. Raw and cold foods directly damage the Spleen's warming, transforming function, worsening Qi deficiency. Rest and warm, easily digested meals allow the Spleen to rebuild its strength, restoring its ability to hold blood where it belongs.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat warm, easily digested foods like congee, stewed root vegetables, and small amounts of well-cooked meat. Avoid raw, cold, greasy, or spicy foods that further weaken Spleen Qi. Rest deeply and avoid overwork; stress and exhaustion drain the Spleen. Gentle, consistent daily movement supports Qi production without depleting it.

### Empty-Heat caused by Yin Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/empty-heat-caused-by-yin-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Scanty bright-red blood in vomit, Night sweats, Five-center heat (palms, soles, chest), Dry mouth and throat, worse at night, Red tongue with little or no coating
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Spicy, greasy, or fried foods, Alcohol and coffee, Overwork and late nights, Anger and emotional stress, Hot weather or overheated rooms
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Cooling, moistening foods, Early, restful sleep, Calm, quiet environment, Sipping water, Gentle meditation or breathing exercises
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When the body's cooling, nourishing Yin fluids are chronically depleted, an empty, flaring heat arises from within. This false heat rises and damages the delicate blood vessels of the stomach, causing them to leak. The result is a scanty amount of bright-red blood in the vomit, often recurrent rather than a single massive episode.

### Stomach Blood Stagnation
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/stomach-blood-stagnation)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Sharp stabbing epigastric pain, fixed in location, Vomiting dark or coffee-ground blood, Black tarry stools, Pain worsens with pressure and after eating, Purple or dark dusky tongue with stasis spots
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Raw, cold foods and drinks, Anger and emotional stress, Overeating or eating too fast, Spicy, greasy, or fried foods, Prolonged standing or overwork
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warmth on the abdomen, Gentle walking, Small, warm meals, Rest and lying down
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When blood flow in the stomach collaterals becomes sluggish and congealed over time, the vessels become brittle and prone to rupture. This chronic stasis causes blood to leak out slowly, pooling in the stomach before being vomited. Because the blood has stagnated and clotted, it appears dark, coffee-ground-like, or clotted rather than fresh red.
The stabbing, fixed pain is a hallmark of blood stasis - it signals that Qi and blood are stuck in one place, unable to move smoothly. This pattern often arises after an acute stomach heat or bleeding episode that was not fully resolved, leaving behind congealed blood that blocks the collaterals.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: dark purple body with purple stasis spots, distended sublingual veins, thin white coat. Pulse: choppy (Se) or wiry (Xian), tight at the right middle position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Cold congeals and slows blood, making stasis worse, so warmth and warm foods help dissolve clots and restore flow. Emotional stress stagnates Liver Qi, which in turn worsens blood stasis in the stomach; calm and rest allow Qi to move freely. Gentle movement prevents stagnation without overburdening the weakened vessels, while heavy meals and overwork tax the Stomach and aggravate the blockage.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat warm, easily digested meals - soft porridges, stewed vegetables, and small amounts of lean protein. Avoid cold, raw, and greasy foods that congeal blood. Gentle daily walking helps Qi and blood circulate. Emotional calm is important, as anger and frustration worsen stagnation.

## คำถามที่พบบ่อย (Frequently Asked Questions)
### Can TCM stop active vomiting of blood?
TCM has a long history of using cooling, astringent herbs to help arrest acute bleeding, but vomiting blood is a medical emergency. In any active, significant bleed, you must go to the emergency room immediately. TCM can be used alongside conventional care under close medical supervision, but it is not a substitute for endoscopy, IV fluids, or blood transfusion. Once the acute bleeding is stabilized, herbal formulas can support healing and address the root imbalance that caused the bleed.

### What does the color of the blood mean in TCM?
The color is a key diagnostic clue. Bright red blood usually indicates heat - either Stomach Fire or Liver Fire - where the heat is fresh and forceful, pushing blood out rapidly. Dark red or purple blood suggests the blood has been stagnant or that heat has been smoldering longer. Dark brown, coffee-ground material points to blood that has pooled in the stomach and been partially digested, often seen in Stomach Blood Stagnation. Pale, thin blood with little color suggests a deficiency pattern where the Spleen is too weak to hold blood. Always tell your practitioner the exact color and consistency.

### Will I need to change my diet?
Yes, diet is a cornerstone of TCM treatment for this condition. During and after a bleeding episode, the stomach lining needs to heal. You'll be advised to eat only bland, easy-to-digest, cooked foods - think congee, steamed vegetables, and soups - and to avoid anything that generates heat, such as alcohol, coffee, spicy foods, fried foods, and rich meats. Cold and raw foods should also be avoided because they tax the Spleen's digestive fire. Once the acute phase passes, your practitioner will tailor dietary recommendations to your specific pattern.

### How do you treat vomiting blood caused by anger or stress?
This is a classic Liver Fire invading the Stomach pattern. Treatment focuses on clearing Liver Fire, soothing the Liver Qi, and cooling the blood. Herbal formulas like Long Dan Xie Gan Tang are used to drain the fire, while acupuncture points such as Taichong (LR-3) and Xingjian (LR-2) help calm the Liver and redirect its energy downward. Patients are also taught stress-management techniques because emotional regulation is part of the cure - if the anger keeps flaring, the bleeding may recur.

### Is it safe to use TCM herbs if I'm already on acid-reducing medication?
Generally yes, but it must be coordinated. TCM herbs and proton pump inhibitors (PPIs) like omeprazole can often be taken together, but timing may need to be adjusted to avoid interference with absorption. More importantly, some cooling herbs that stop bleeding, such as Da Huang (rhubarb root), can have a mild laxative effect, which might be intensified if you are also taking other medications. Always provide your TCM practitioner with a full list of your medications, and do not stop prescribed medication without consulting your doctor.

### Can vomiting blood be a sign of a chronic deficiency?
Yes, if the bleeding is recurrent, small in volume, and the blood is pale or thin, it often points to Spleen not controlling Blood. This is a chronic weakness pattern where the body's Qi is too depleted to hold blood inside the vessels. Treatment uses formulas like Gui Pi Tang to tonify the Spleen and build blood, and recovery is gradual - usually several months. You'll also need to prioritize rest, warm cooked meals, and avoid overwork, as exhaustion directly weakens the Spleen further.

### What should I expect during a TCM consultation for this problem?
Your practitioner will ask detailed questions about the bleeding episode: the color, amount, frequency, and what you felt before it happened. They'll look at your tongue and feel your pulse, which reveal the internal heat or deficiency state. They'll also ask about your emotions, diet, bowel habits, and energy levels to pinpoint the underlying pattern. Based on this, they'll prescribe a custom herbal formula and possibly acupuncture. If you are currently bleeding or have black stools, you'll be referred for emergency medical evaluation before any TCM treatment begins.
