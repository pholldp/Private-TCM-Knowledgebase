## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนปัจจุบัน (Conventional Medicine View)
### คำอธิบาย (Description)
Uterine prolapse occurs when the pelvic floor muscles and ligaments weaken, allowing the uterus to descend into the vaginal canal. Common symptoms include a feeling of heaviness or pressure in the pelvis, a visible bulge at the vaginal opening, lower back discomfort, and sometimes urinary or bowel difficulties. It is most common after vaginal childbirth, with aging, chronic heavy lifting, or conditions that increase abdominal pressure. Diagnosis is typically made through a pelvic examination, often with the patient bearing down to assess the degree of descent.

### การรักษาหลัก (Treatments)
Mild prolapse may be managed with pelvic floor exercises (Kegels) and lifestyle changes. A vaginal pessary can provide support for moderate prolapse. Severe or symptomatic cases are often treated surgically, with options ranging from uterine suspension to hysterectomy, sometimes with repair of the vaginal walls.

## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนจีน (Traditional Chinese Medicine View)
### ความเข้าใจเกี่ยวกับการแพทย์แผนจีน (TCM Understanding)
TCM views the uterus as held in place not just by ligaments and muscles, but by a vital upward force called Qi. The Spleen organ system is primarily responsible for this 'lifting' function. After childbirth, with chronic overwork, or as we age, Spleen Qi can become depleted and sink downward, losing its ability to hold the uterus. This is why the classic symptom is a heavy, bearing-down sensation that worsens with standing and fatigue - gravity overcomes the weakened Qi.
The Kidneys also play a crucial anchoring role. They store the body's deepest Qi and govern the lower back and pelvic floor. When Kidney Qi is weak, often from multiple pregnancies, heavy lifting, or constitutional deficiency, the lower burner loses its grip. The prolapse then comes with lower back soreness, weak knees, frequent nighttime urination, and a feeling of cold or emptiness in the lower abdomen.
In some women, both Spleen and Kidney Qi are deficient, and the prolapse is further complicated by a lack of nourishing Blood, which weakens the tissues. Additionally, when the uterus descends and is exposed, local irritation and infection can create a pattern of Damp-Heat, marked by foul-smelling yellow discharge and burning. So the same Western diagnosis of uterine prolapse can reflect very different underlying TCM patterns, each with its own characteristic sensations and treatment approach.

### การวินิจฉัยโดยแพทย์แผนจีน (Practitioner Diagnosis)
A TCM practitioner begins by listening carefully to how the prolapse feels and what makes it worse. The nature of the bearing-down sensation, the presence of fatigue or back soreness, and any changes in vaginal discharge are the first clues that steer the diagnosis toward one pattern or another.
When the main complaints are a heavy dragging feeling that worsens after standing or exertion, along with profound tiredness, poor appetite, and loose stools, the picture points to Spleen Qi Sinking. The tongue is often pale and slightly swollen with a thin white coat, and the pulse feels weak and forceless, confirming the Qi is too depleted to hold organs in place.
If the prolapse comes with a deep lower back ache, weak knees, frequent nighttime urination, and a sensation of coldness or emptiness in the lower abdomen, the root is likely Kidney Qi Deficiency. The tongue may be pale, and the pulse is deep and weak, especially at the rear position. The practitioner will ask about hearing, dizziness, and bladder habits to confirm the kidney’s anchoring role is failing.
In more chronic or severe cases, the body’s resources become even more drained, leading to a combined Qi and Blood Deficiency pattern. Here, alongside the sinking sensation, the person looks pale, feels dizzy, has dry skin and hair, and may experience heart palpitations. The tongue is pale and thin, and the pulse is thin and weak, reflecting a deeper depletion that needs both Qi and blood nourishment.
When the prolapsed tissue becomes irritated or infected, signs of Damp-Heat in the Lower Burner appear. The practitioner will notice yellow, sticky discharge with a foul odor, local burning or itching, and sometimes urinary discomfort. The tongue becomes red with a yellow greasy coating, and the pulse turns slippery and rapid. This pattern is a local complication layered on top of an underlying deficiency, so the practitioner will treat the heat and dampness while still supporting the body’s holding power.

## กลุ่มอาการการแพทย์แผนจีนและการรักษา (TCM Patterns and Treatment)

### Spleen Qi Sinking
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/spleen-qi-sinking)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Bearing-down sensation in the pelvis, Worsens with prolonged standing or exertion, Chronic fatigue and weak limbs, Poor appetite, Loose stools, Pale complexion, Reluctance to speak
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Prolonged standing, Heavy lifting, Chronic coughing, Postpartum weakness, Cold, raw foods, Overwork and fatigue
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Lying down to rest, Warm, nourishing meals, Gentle abdominal support, Avoiding heavy lifting
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  The Spleen is responsible for holding organs in their proper place. When Spleen Qi is weakened-often after childbirth, from overwork, or from chronic illness-it loses its lifting power and sinks downward. This sinking Qi can no longer support the uterus, so it gradually descends, causing a heavy, bearing-down sensation.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale, puffy, and tender with teeth marks on the edges; thin white coating. Pulse: weak and forceless, especially at the right Guan (Spleen) position, and may feel deep.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Spleen Qi is naturally ascending and holds organs up. Standing, lifting, and straining pull Qi downward, making the sinking worse. Cold and raw foods further weaken the Spleen's digestive fire, reducing its lifting ability. Resting flat allows Qi to rise back up, while warm, cooked foods gently nourish the Spleen, helping it regain the strength to support the uterus.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Avoid prolonged standing, heavy lifting, and intense exercise that push Qi further downward. Rest lying down with hips slightly elevated when you feel the heaviness. Eat warm, cooked, easily digestible foods like rice congee, sweet potato, and well-cooked vegetables. Steer clear of raw, cold, and greasy foods that weaken the Spleen.

### Kidney Qi Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/kidney-qi-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Soreness and weakness of the lower back and knees, Frequent urination, especially at night, Dizziness and ringing in the ears, Shortness of breath on exertion, Feeling of heaviness or bearing-down in the pelvis, Poor memory and general fatigue
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Heavy lifting, Prolonged standing, Overwork and fatigue, Cold weather or cold foods, Multiple childbirths
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Lying down to rest, Warmth on the lower back, Gentle Kegel exercises, Warm, nourishing meals
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  The Kidneys store the body's deepest Qi and anchor the uterus. When Kidney Qi is weakened, the lower burner loses its grip, and the uterus begins to slip downward. This often follows multiple childbirths, chronic heavy lifting, or aging. The same weakness causes the lower back and knees to feel sore and unsteady, and the bladder to lose its ability to hold urine, especially at night.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale, slightly puffy body with possible tooth marks, thin white coating. Pulse: deep and weak, especially at the chi (Kidney) positions.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Kidney Qi is depleted by overwork, heavy lifting, and prolonged standing, which further weakens the lower burner's ability to hold the uterus. Cold constricts and saps Yang Qi, so warmth and cooked foods help conserve and restore Kidney strength. Rest and lying down take pressure off the pelvic floor, allowing the Qi to return upward rather than sinking further.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Prioritize rest and avoid heavy lifting or prolonged standing. Gentle pelvic floor exercises and walking help tone the area without straining. Eat warm, cooked foods like soups and stews; include kidney beans, walnuts, and black sesame seeds. Avoid raw, cold foods and icy drinks, which further weaken Kidney Qi.

### Qi and Blood Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/qi-and-blood-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Pale or sallow complexion, Dizziness or lightheadedness, Heart palpitations, Dry skin and pale lips, Numbness or tingling in limbs, Prolapse worse with exertion
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Prolonged standing, Heavy lifting, Skipping meals, Excessive worry, Cold, raw foods
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Lying down to rest, Warm, nourishing meals, Gentle walking, Small, frequent meals
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Qi provides the lifting force that holds the uterus in place, while Blood nourishes and strengthens the tissues. When both are deficient, the supportive structures become slack and undernourished, and the body lacks the upward energy to counteract gravity. This dual depletion often develops after prolonged illness, heavy blood loss, or chronic overwork, causing the uterus to gradually descend.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale body, may be thin or have teeth marks on the edges, with a thin white coating. Pulse: fine (thin) and weak, lacking force.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Qi and Blood are the body's building blocks and lifting force. Standing or straining depletes what little Qi remains, while rest and lying down conserve it. Warm, nourishing foods directly supply the raw materials for Blood and Qi, and small frequent meals prevent overtaxing a weak Spleen. Worry knots the Spleen Qi, further impairing its ability to generate Blood and provide lift.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat warm, cooked meals rich in iron and protein, such as bone broths, red dates (大枣, dà zǎo), goji berries (枸杞子, gǒu qǐ zǐ), and dark leafy greens. Avoid raw, cold foods that weaken Spleen Qi. Prioritize lying down for short rests during the day to take pressure off the pelvic floor, and avoid heavy lifting or prolonged standing.

### Damp-Heat in the Lower Burner
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/damp-heat-in-the-lower-burner)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Foul-smelling yellow vaginal discharge, Burning sensation in the vulva or lower abdomen, Feeling of heaviness and heat in the lower body, Dark, scanty, or burning urination
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Spicy, greasy, or sweet foods, Hot and humid weather, Prolonged standing or heavy lifting, Emotional stress or anger
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Cool environment, Avoiding greasy/spicy food, Resting with legs elevated, Gentle walking
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When the uterus descends, the exposed tissue can become irritated and inflamed, leading to local Damp-Heat accumulation. This produces the foul-smelling yellow discharge, burning sensation, and redness. The underlying Spleen Qi Sinking or Kidney Qi Deficiency may still be present, but Damp-Heat complicates the picture and must be cleared before or alongside lifting treatment.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red body with a yellow greasy coating, thickest at the root. Pulse: slippery (滑 huá) and rapid (数 shuò), often more pronounced at the rear (Chi) position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Spicy, greasy, and sweet foods create internal Dampness and Heat, directly fueling this pattern. Rest and cool environments reduce Heat, while avoiding heavy lifting prevents further irritation and prolapse. Emotional stress can stir up Liver Qi, which transforms into Heat and worsens burning sensations.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Avoid spicy, greasy, and sweet foods that generate Dampness and Heat. Drink plenty of water and eat cooling, damp-draining foods like mung beans, celery, and cucumber. Keep the genital area clean and dry. Gentle exercise like walking can help, but avoid heavy lifting.

## คำถามที่พบบ่อย (Frequently Asked Questions)
### Can acupuncture really lift a prolapsed uterus?
Acupuncture alone cannot physically reposition a severely prolapsed uterus, but it strengthens the muscles and Qi that support the pelvic floor. When combined with herbal medicine that lifts Qi and moxibustion on points like Baihui (the top of the head), many women experience a noticeable reduction in heaviness and improved support. This is most effective for mild to moderate prolapse and as a complement to pelvic floor exercises.

### Will I still need surgery if I try TCM?
That depends on the severity. For mild to moderate prolapse, TCM often improves symptoms enough that surgery can be delayed or avoided. For severe prolapse that significantly affects quality of life, TCM can still be very helpful - it can strengthen the body before surgery to improve recovery, and reduce the risk of recurrence by addressing the underlying Qi weakness. It is not an either/or choice; many women use both approaches.

### Can I use a pessary while taking Chinese herbs?
Yes. A pessary provides mechanical support while the herbs and acupuncture work on strengthening your internal lifting Qi. There are no known interactions between the materials of a pessary and Chinese herbs. Just be sure to follow your doctor’s instructions for pessary cleaning and follow-up, and inform both your TCM practitioner and gynecologist about all treatments you are using.

### Is it safe to take Chinese herbs after childbirth for prolapse?
Absolutely, and the postpartum period is actually an ideal time to address prolapse with TCM. Many of the classic formulas for raising Qi were developed specifically for postpartum women. The herbs are gentle and nourishing, and they can be taken while breastfeeding. Always work with a qualified TCM practitioner who can tailor the formula to your postpartum needs and any other health concerns.

### How soon will I feel better?
Most women feel a reduction in the heavy, dragging sensation within the first 3-4 weeks of treatment. You may also notice improved energy and less back discomfort. The prolapse itself may take longer to show measurable improvement, especially if it is moderate or severe. Consistency is key: daily herbs and weekly acupuncture give the best results. Your practitioner will monitor your progress and adjust the treatment as needed.

### What foods should I avoid?
In general, avoid cold and raw foods, which can weaken the Spleen Qi and worsen sinking. Dairy, greasy foods, and sugar can create Dampness that complicates the prolapse. Also steer clear of very spicy foods if you have any discharge or burning. Instead, favor warm, cooked meals like soups and congees that are easy to digest and support Qi. We provide more detailed dietary guidance in the Treatment section.

### Can I do Kegels and TCM at the same time?
Yes, and they work beautifully together. Kegels strengthen the pelvic floor muscles directly, while TCM strengthens the internal Qi that holds the organs up. Many women find that the herbs and acupuncture give them the energy and awareness to perform Kegels more effectively. Just be sure not to overdo it - gentle, consistent practice is better than intense straining.
