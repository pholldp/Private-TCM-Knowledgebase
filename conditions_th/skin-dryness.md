## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนปัจจุบัน (Conventional Medicine View)
### คำอธิบาย (Description)
Western medicine defines dry skin (xerosis) as a common condition where the skin lacks moisture, often feeling rough, tight, flaky, or itchy. It can result from environmental factors like low humidity, excessive washing, or aging, and is sometimes linked to underlying conditions such as eczema, psoriasis, or hypothyroidism. Diagnosis is typically based on visual examination and a review of the patient's history.

### การรักษาหลัก (Treatments)
Conventional treatment focuses on restoring the skin's barrier with emollients and moisturizers, avoiding harsh soaps, and using humidifiers. For itching, topical corticosteroids or antihistamines may be prescribed. If an underlying condition is present, that is treated separately.

## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนจีน (Traditional Chinese Medicine View)
### ความเข้าใจเกี่ยวกับการแพทย์แผนจีน (TCM Understanding)
TCM sees the skin as a mirror of your internal organs. The Lungs govern the skin and are responsible for spreading fluids to the surface. The Spleen transforms food into Qi and moisture, while the Liver stores Blood that nourishes and lubricates. The Kidneys are the root of all Yin and Body Fluids. When any of these systems are out of balance, the skin loses its natural hydration.
The type of dryness tells a story. Flaky, itchy skin that worsens in wind often points to Blood Deficiency generating internal Wind. Hot, red dryness that flares at night suggests Yin Deficiency with Empty-Heat simmering away moisture. Deep, parched dryness with unquenchable thirst indicates a simple depletion of Body Fluids. Persistent dry skin paired with exhaustion signals both Qi and Yin have fallen low.
Even Damp-Phlegm can cause dry skin. If the Spleen is weak, fluids aren't transformed properly and instead become thick, sticky Phlegm that blocks the channels. Moisture can't reach the skin, so it feels dry while the body underneath feels heavy and sluggish. This is why simply applying moisturizer may not work - the root is inside, not on the surface.

### การวินิจฉัยโดยแพทย์แผนจีน (Practitioner Diagnosis)
A TCM practitioner starts by asking about the quality of the dryness: is it flaky and itchy, or just rough and tight? They also want to know when it worsens-at night, in winter, after bathing, or regardless of season. Associated symptoms like fatigue, thirst, or digestive issues are key clues that point toward one pattern over another.
If the dryness is accompanied by intense itching, pale skin, and flaking that resembles dandruff, Blood Deficiency with External Wind is likely. The tongue is pale with a thin white coat, and the pulse feels thin and weak. This pattern often appears in people who are anemic or have poor circulation, and the skin may worsen in dry, windy weather.
When the skin feels dry and hot, and the person notices more dryness at night, along with night sweats, a warm sensation in the palms and soles, and a red tongue with little coating, Empty-Heat caused by Yin Deficiency is the picture. This internal heat consumes fluids, leaving the skin parched.
Qi and Yin Deficiency adds pronounced fatigue and shortness of breath to the dryness, reflecting a deeper depletion of both energy and moisture.
Body Fluids Deficiency produces a simple, straightforward dryness without much heat or itching-just rough, tight skin that lacks moisture. The tongue may be dry and the pulse thin.
In contrast, Damp-Phlegm is a paradoxical pattern where the skin is dry yet the tongue has a thick, greasy coat and the pulse is slippery. Here, dampness blocks fluid distribution, so the skin becomes dry despite internal dampness.

## กลุ่มอาการการแพทย์แผนจีนและการรักษา (TCM Patterns and Treatment)

### Blood Deficiency with External Wind
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/blood-deficiency-with-external-wind)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Wandering itch that comes and goes, Dry, rough, or flaky skin, Pale or sallow complexion, Aversion to wind or drafts, Dizziness or light-headedness, Dull, brittle nails, Itching worse at night
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Stress and worry, Exposure to wind or drafts, Dry, cold weather, Overwork and lack of sleep, Spicy, fried, or greasy foods
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Rest and adequate sleep, Warm, wind-protected environment, Nourishing foods like bone broth, eggs, dark greens, Gentle exercise or movement, Applying natural moisturizers
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Your Blood nourishes and moistens your skin. When Blood is deficient, the skin loses its natural lubrication, becoming dry, rough, and flaky. The deficiency also creates an internal "Wind" - a TCM concept for movement and sudden change - which stirs up itching that wanders or comes and goes. At the same time, your body's protective layer weakens, allowing external Wind to invade and trigger sudden rashes, sensitivity to drafts, and itching that often gets worse at night.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale, thin body with a thin white and slightly dry coat. Pulse: fine (thin), possibly wiry at the left middle position and floating at the superficial level.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Blood Deficiency weakens the body's protective Qi, making it easy for external Wind to invade and stir up internal Wind, which causes the wandering itch. Stress, overwork, and poor diet further drain Blood and Qi, worsening dryness and sensitivity. Rest, nourishing foods, and a wind-protected environment help rebuild Blood and strengthen the surface, while gentle movement keeps Qi flowing without depleting it.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat blood-nourishing foods like dark leafy greens, beets, black sesame, eggs, and bone broth. Avoid spicy, fried, or overly drying foods. Protect your skin from wind and cold with gentle clothing and natural moisturizers. Prioritize rest and keep stress low-stress consumes Blood and Qi.

### Empty-Heat caused by Yin Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/empty-heat-caused-by-yin-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Skin feels hot to the touch, especially in the evening, Night sweats, Five-palm heat (hot palms, soles, and chest), Dry mouth and throat, worse at night, Flushed cheekbones, Restlessness and trouble sleeping
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Spicy, fried, or greasy foods, Overwork and lack of sleep, Hot, dry weather, Stress and anger, Alcohol and coffee
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Cool, moist environment, Rest and adequate sleep, Eating pears or moistening foods, Gentle exercise or movement
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When the body's cooling Yin fluids run low, they can no longer anchor the warming Yang. This creates an internal 'empty' heat that rises and slowly simmers away moisture, much like a low flame under a pot. The skin loses its natural lubrication and becomes dry, often feeling hot to the touch, especially as the day turns to evening.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red, thin, dry body with cracks, little or no coating. Pulse: fine and rapid.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Spicy and fried foods, alcohol, and emotional stress all generate heat, which further consumes the already depleted Yin fluids, making the skin even drier and hotter. Late nights and overwork drain the Kidney Yin that is essential for cooling and moistening the body. On the other hand, rest, cooling foods, and a calm environment help rebuild Yin and gently clear empty heat, allowing moisture to return to the skin naturally.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat more moistening, cooling foods like pears, snow fungus, lily bulb, and cucumber. Avoid spicy, fried, or barbecued foods, as well as alcohol and coffee, which generate heat. Go to bed before 11 p.m. and prioritize rest to protect Yin. Gentle, non-sweaty movement like tai chi or yin yoga helps settle the mind without depleting fluids.

### Qi and Yin Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/qi-and-yin-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Persistent tiredness and physical weakness, Dry mouth with little desire to drink, Shortness of breath worsened by exertion, Five-palm heat (hot palms, soles, and chest), Night sweats
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Overwork and lack of sleep, Spicy, fried, or greasy foods, Dry, heated indoor air, Stress and overthinking, Prolonged speaking
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Rest and adequate sleep, Warm, moistening drinks, Cool, moist environment, Gentle exercise or movement, Eating pears or moistening foods
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Qi is the force that moves moisture to the skin, and Yin provides the cooling, moist foundation. When both are depleted, the body cannot generate enough fluids nor push them to the surface. The skin then becomes persistently dry, often feeling rough or papery, while the person feels drained and parched.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red or pale-red body with a thin or absent coating, possibly cracked and dry. Pulse: fine (thin) and weak, often rapid, reflecting the insufficiency of both Qi and Yin fluids.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Rest and sleep preserve Qi and allow Yin to recover, while overwork and stress deplete them further. Moistening foods and a humid environment directly replenish the fluids that are lacking, whereas spicy, fried, or dry conditions consume those fluids. Gentle movement encourages Qi circulation without exhausting it, helping to distribute moisture to the skin.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat moistening foods like pears, honey, and congee. Avoid spicy, fried, and drying foods. Get plenty of rest and avoid overexertion. A humidifier at night can help prevent fluid loss.

### Damp-Phlegm
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/damp-phlegm)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Feeling of heaviness in the body and limbs, Thick, white, greasy tongue coating, Chest and upper abdominal stuffiness or bloating, Poor appetite, nausea, or loose sticky stools, Dizziness, brain fog, or muzzy-headedness
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Spicy, fried, or greasy foods, Cold, raw foods and iced drinks, Damp or humid weather, Sedentary lifestyle or overeating
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warm cooked meals, Gentle exercise or movement, Dry, airy environment
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When the Spleen is weak, it fails to transform and transport fluids properly. These fluids accumulate into Dampness and then condense into Phlegm, which is thick and sticky. This Phlegm-Dampness blocks the body's channels, preventing moisture from reaching the skin. The result is a paradoxical dryness on the surface while a heavy, sluggish, and greasy sensation lingers inside.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale, swollen body with scalloped teeth marks and a thick, white, greasy coat that looks oily or pasty. Pulse: slippery (滑, huá), like a bead gliding through oil, and may feel soggy in the right middle position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Damp-Phlegm is like sticky internal fog that blocks waterways. Warm, cooked foods and moderate exercise stoke the Spleen's digestive fire, helping to burn off the fog and move fluids to the skin. Greasy, cold, or raw foods add more dampness, while a sedentary lifestyle allows the sludge to settle and thicken. A dry environment reduces external dampness that can tip the balance inward.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat warm, cooked foods like soups and stews, and avoid cold, raw, greasy, or dairy-heavy meals that create more Dampness. Gentle daily movement-walking, yoga, or stretching-helps Qi circulate and moves the sludge. Keep your living space dry and well-ventilated.

### Body Fluids Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/body-fluids-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Thirst with desire to drink water, Dry mouth and cracked lips, Dry, rough, or flaky skin, Scanty dark urine, Dry hard stools, Absence of itching or feeling of heat
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Spicy, fried, or greasy foods, Alcohol and coffee, Dry, heated indoor air, Excessive sweating, Prolonged illness or dehydration
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Drinking warm water, Eating pears or moistening foods, Humidifier use, Rest and adequate sleep
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Body Fluids are the body's natural moisture, nourishing and lubricating the skin. When they become depleted - often after a prolonged illness, excessive sweating, or chronic dehydration - the skin loses its suppleness and becomes dry, rough, and lacking lustre. Unlike other dry-skin patterns, this one is a straightforward lack of moisture without prominent heat or itching.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red, dry, thin body with cracks, little or no coating. Pulse: fine (thin) and rapid.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Body Fluids Deficiency means your body's moisture reserves are low. Drinking water and eating moistening foods directly replenish those fluids, while spicy, fried, or drying substances deplete them further. A humid environment reduces moisture loss from the skin, and rest conserves the body's resources to rebuild fluids.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Drink warm water regularly throughout the day and include moistening foods like pear, honey, cucumber, and congee. Avoid spicy, fried, and overly dry foods, as well as alcohol and coffee, which can further deplete fluids. Use a humidifier in dry environments to protect your skin's moisture.

## คำถามที่พบบ่อย (Frequently Asked Questions)
### Can acupuncture really help with dry skin?
Yes. Acupuncture works by stimulating points that regulate the organs responsible for fluid production and distribution - such as the Spleen, Lungs, and Kidneys. It can improve circulation to the skin and help the body retain moisture. Many patients find their skin feels less tight and itchy after just a few sessions, especially when combined with herbal medicine.

### Will I need to change my diet?
Diet plays a supporting role. In general, you'll be encouraged to eat more moistening foods like pears, bone broth, and dark leafy greens, and to cut back on drying items like spicy foods and alcohol. If your pattern involves Damp-Phlegm, you may need to avoid heavy, greasy foods. Your practitioner will give you specific guidance tailored to your pattern.

### How long until I see results?
Most people notice some improvement within 2-4 weeks. Itching and tightness usually ease first, followed by a gradual return of smoother, more hydrated skin. Deeper deficiency patterns may take 1-3 months for lasting change. Consistency with herbs and acupuncture is key.

### Does the dryness come back after treatment?
TCM aims to correct the underlying imbalance, so results are meant to be lasting. However, if the same lifestyle or dietary habits that caused the problem return, the dryness can recur. Your practitioner will help you understand what to maintain long-term to keep your skin healthy.

### Can TCM help with dry skin caused by aging?
Yes. Aging often involves a natural decline in Yin and Blood, which TCM can support. Herbs and acupuncture can help replenish these reserves and improve the skin's moisture and elasticity, though the process may be slower than in younger people. Many find it a gentle and effective way to manage age-related dryness.

### Is it safe to combine TCM herbs with my moisturizers?
Absolutely. Herbal formulas work internally to address the root cause, while moisturizers continue to protect the skin externally. There is no conflict, and using both together often yields the best short-term comfort while the herbs take effect. Just inform your practitioner of any medicated creams you use.

### What if my dry skin is also very itchy?
Itchiness usually points to Blood Deficiency with internal Wind. TCM treats this by nourishing the Blood and dispelling Wind, which calms the itch from within. Herbs like Dang Gui and He Shou Wu are commonly used, and acupuncture can provide fast relief. It's one of the patterns that tends to respond well and relatively quickly.
