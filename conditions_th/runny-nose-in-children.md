## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนปัจจุบัน (Conventional Medicine View)
### คำอธิบาย (Description)
In Western medicine, a runny nose (rhinorrhea) in children is most often caused by viral upper respiratory infections - the common cold. Allergic rhinitis, sinusitis, and exposure to irritants are other frequent triggers. The discharge is the body’s attempt to flush out pathogens or allergens, and its color may shift from clear to yellow or green as immune cells arrive.
Diagnosis is usually based on the history and physical exam; laboratory tests or imaging are rarely needed unless symptoms are severe, prolonged, or recurrent. Treatment focuses on managing symptoms and, when possible, addressing the underlying cause.

### การรักษาหลัก (Treatments)
For viral colds, care is supportive: rest, extra fluids, saline nasal drops or sprays, a cool-mist humidifier, and age-appropriate pain relievers for fever or discomfort. Allergic rhinitis is managed with oral antihistamines, nasal corticosteroid sprays, and allergen avoidance. Bacterial sinusitis may require a course of antibiotics. Decongestant nasal sprays are sometimes used for short periods in older children, but oral decongestants are generally not recommended for young children.

## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนจีน (Traditional Chinese Medicine View)
### ความเข้าใจเกี่ยวกับการแพทย์แผนจีน (TCM Understanding)
In TCM, the Lungs open into the nose. The nose is the doorway through which the outside world meets the body’s defensive shield, called Wei Qi. When that shield is strong, wind, cold, and heat bounce off harmlessly. When it is weak - or when the pathogen is particularly aggressive - the Lungs lose their ability to diffuse and descend Qi, and a runny nose begins.
Most acute runny noses in children are caused by an external invasion. Wind is the leader of all external pathogens, often carrying Cold or Heat with it. A clear, watery discharge with chills and no sweating points to Wind-Cold. A thick, yellow discharge with fever and thirst signals Wind-Heat. In hot, humid weather, Summer-Heat can produce a sticky, glue-like mucus. Sometimes a Wind-Cold attack traps heat inside, and the discharge shifts from clear to yellow as the pattern transforms - a mixed picture called Wind Cold with Internal Heat.
Not every runny nose is an acute invasion. When a child has a chronic, clear, watery drip and catches colds at the slightest breeze, the root is often a deficiency - specifically, Lung Qi Deficiency. The Lungs are too weak to hold fluids or to maintain a solid defensive shield. This pattern is common in children with allergies or a history of frequent illness. Because children’s organs are delicate and their Qi can shift quickly, a single runny nose may move through several patterns in a matter of days, which is why careful pattern identification is essential.

### การวินิจฉัยโดยแพทย์แผนจีน (Practitioner Diagnosis)
A TCM practitioner starts by observing the color, thickness, and timing of the nasal discharge, along with other clues like fever, thirst, and energy level. These details point toward the underlying pattern causing the runny nose.
If the discharge is clear and watery, and the child feels chilly, avoids wind, and has no sweating, Wind‑Cold is likely. Sneezing and a headache may also appear. The tongue coating is thin and white, and the pulse feels floating and tight.
When the mucus is thick and yellow, with fever, sore throat, and thirst, Wind‑Heat is the pattern. The face may be flushed and the child feels hot. The tongue tip is red with a thin yellow coating, and the pulse is floating and rapid.
Wind Cold with Internal Heat shows a transition: the runny nose begins clear and thin but soon turns thick and yellow as heat develops inside. Irritability, a cough with yellow phlegm, and fever are common. The tongue coating may shift from thin white to yellow, and the pulse is floating and rapid.
In hot, humid weather, a sticky, thick nasal discharge points to Summer‑Heat. The child often feels heavy, fatigued, and has a poor appetite or nausea. The tongue is red with a yellow, greasy coating, and the pulse is slippery and rapid.
A persistent clear runny nose, pale complexion, and easy tiredness even with mild symptoms suggest Lung Qi Deficiency. The child catches colds frequently and may sweat spontaneously. The tongue is pale with a thin white coating, and the pulse is weak.

## กลุ่มอาการการแพทย์แผนจีนและการรักษา (TCM Patterns and Treatment)

### Wind-Cold
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/wind-cold)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Clear watery nasal discharge, Strong aversion to cold, shivering, No sweating, Headache and body aches, No thirst, prefers warm drinks
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Cold wind or drafts, Cold or raw foods and drinks, Wind exposure, Overexertion
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warm drinks, Rest in a warm room, Steam inhalation, Gentle sweating
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When Wind and Cold invade the body, they attack the Lung’s defensive surface. The Cold contracts the pores and blocks the nose, while the Lung struggles to diffuse Qi and fluids. This produces a clear, watery runny nose and nasal congestion.
Because the pathogen is still at the exterior, the child feels strong chills and the body tries to fight it off with a mild fever. The lack of sweating shows the pores are tightly shut, trapping the cold outside.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: Normal or slightly pale body with a thin, white, moist coat. Pulse: Floating and tight (浮紧, fú jǐn).
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Wind-Cold invades the body’s surface, constricting the pores and obstructing the Lung’s ability to disperse Qi. Warmth and steam help open the pores and promote a mild sweat, releasing the pathogen outward. Cold drafts and cold foods contract the exterior further, trapping the pathogen and making the runny nose and chills worse.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Keep your child warm and away from drafts. Offer warm fluids like mild ginger tea or warm water with a touch of honey. A light, easily digested diet (congee, soups) supports the body’s fight without burdening the digestion. Gentle steam inhalation can help open the nose and soothe the throat.

### Wind-Heat
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/wind-heat)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Thick yellow or sticky nasal discharge, Fever that feels worse than any chills, Red, sore throat, Thirst for cold drinks, Irritability and fussiness
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Spicy or fried foods, Hot, stuffy environments, Overexertion, Wind exposure, Overdressing or heavy blankets
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Cool, fresh air, Plenty of cool fluids, Mild mint or chrysanthemum tea, Rest in a quiet, cool room, Light, non-greasy meals
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When a child catches a wind-heat invasion, the pathogen enters through the nose and mouth and attacks the Lung's defensive exterior. The Lung governs the nose, so its function of diffusing and descending Qi is disrupted, leading to nasal congestion and a runny nose. Heat dries the body's fluids, making the mucus thick, sticky, and yellow or yellow-green. The heat also rises to the throat, causing redness and soreness, and produces fever that is more pronounced than any chills.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red tip and edges with a thin yellow coating. Pulse: floating and rapid.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Wind-heat is an exterior heat pattern, so anything that adds warmth or traps heat inside can worsen symptoms. Spicy, fried, or heavy foods generate internal heat, while hot rooms and too many clothes prevent the body from releasing the pathogen through light sweating. On the other hand, cooling foods and drinks help clear heat without damaging fluids, and cool, fresh air assists the Lung's dispersing function. Rest conserves the child's defensive Qi, allowing the body to fight off the wind-heat more efficiently.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Keep the child cool but not chilled. Offer plenty of fluids like water, pear juice, or mild mint tea. Avoid spicy, fried, or very greasy foods, which add heat. Light, easily digested meals such as congee with a little mung bean are helpful. Ensure the room is well-ventilated but not drafty, and use a humidifier if the air is dry to soothe the nose and throat.

### Wind Cold with Internal Heat
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/wind-cold-with-internal-heat)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Nasal discharge changes from clear to thick yellow, Severe chills with high fever, Irritability and restlessness, No sweating, Thirst or dry mouth
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Cold wind or drafts, Cold or raw foods and drinks, Overexertion, Emotional upset
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Rest in a warm room, Gentle sweating, Warm drinks, Covering the neck and chest
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When a child catches a cold wind, the pores close up and trap heat inside the body. The initial clear, watery runny nose reflects the Wind-Cold on the surface, but as the trapped heat builds, the discharge becomes thick and yellow. The child feels chilled yet has a high fever, no sweating, and becomes irritable because the heat cannot escape.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red body with red tip, thin coating mixed white-and-yellow. Pulse: floating, tight, and rapid.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Wind-Cold constricts the skin pores, trapping heat inside. Warmth and gentle sweating open the pores to release the cold and let the heat dissipate. Cold wind and cold foods tighten the exterior further and feed the internal cold, while overexertion and emotional upset stir up internal heat and deplete Qi, making it harder to recover.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Keep the child warm and encourage gentle sweating by covering with a light blanket. Offer warm fluids like mild ginger tea (if age-appropriate) to help release the exterior. Avoid cold drinks, raw foods, and dairy, which can trap the cold and worsen congestion. Rest is essential to preserve Qi for recovery.

### Summer-Heat
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/summer-heat)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Sticky, thick nasal discharge (like glue), Feeling of heaviness and fatigue, Poor appetite and nausea, Fever with sweating that doesn&#x27;t relieve, Worse in hot, humid weather
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Hot, humid weather, Heavy, greasy, or sweet foods, Overexertion in the sun, Hot, stuffy environments
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Cool, dry environment, Light, bland meals, Rest in the shade, Adequate water intake
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Summer-Heat is an external pathogen that invades during hot, humid weather. It disturbs the Lung's function of dispersing and descending, causing the nose to become congested and produce a sticky, thick discharge. The accompanying Dampness weighs the body down, leading to the heavy, tired feeling and poor appetite.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red body, redder tip, with a yellow greasy or sticky coating. Pulse: rapid and soggy (软 ruǎn) or slippery (滑 huá).
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Summer-Heat and Dampness are the main pathogens. Hot, humid weather directly introduces these pathogens, while heavy, greasy foods create internal Dampness that worsens mucus. Cooling and drying measures-like a dry environment and bland diet-help the body clear the pathogens and relieve the sticky nasal discharge.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Keep your child cool and well-hydrated with water or light herbal teas. Offer light, easy-to-digest foods like congee, steamed vegetables, and watermelon. Avoid heavy, greasy, or sweet foods that create more Dampness. Limit outdoor play during the hottest part of the day.

### Lung Qi Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/lung-qi-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Persistent clear, thin, watery nasal discharge, Pale or bright white complexion, Fatigue and low energy, Low, weak voice or reluctance to speak, Spontaneous daytime sweating, Sensitivity to wind and cold
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Cold wind or drafts, Overexertion, Cold or raw foods and drinks
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warmth and rest, Warm, cooked meals, Gentle movement without fatigue
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  In children, the Lungs govern the nose and the body's defensive shield (Wei Qi). When Lung Qi is weak, that shield is thin-the nose loses its ability to hold fluids and becomes a persistent source of clear, watery discharge. Because the surface is unguarded, the child catches colds easily and a simple runny nose lingers long after other symptoms fade.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale, tender body, may show teeth marks, with a thin white coating. Pulse: weak overall, especially empty in the right cun (Lung) position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Cold and fatigue drain the already weak Lung Qi further, making the defensive shield even thinner and the nose runnier. Warmth, rest, and warm foods gently nourish Lung Qi and help the body hold fluids inside, so the discharge slows down.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Keep the child warm, especially the back of the neck and chest. Offer warm, easy-to-digest foods like congee with a little ginger and spring onion. Avoid cold drinks, raw fruits, and dairy, which can weaken the Lungs further. Gentle daily movement helps Qi circulate, but stop before the child gets overtired.

## คำถามที่พบบ่อย (Frequently Asked Questions)
### How can TCM help my child’s constant runny nose?
TCM looks beyond the symptom to find the root cause. A constant clear runny nose often points to Lung Qi Deficiency, a weak defensive shield that lets every draft trigger a drip. Treatment with gentle tonic herbs like Huang Qi (Astragalus) and regular acupressure can strengthen the Lungs and gradually reduce both the runny nose and the frequency of colds. If the constant drip is seasonal or triggered by allergens, the underlying pattern is still treatable - the approach simply shifts to address the specific imbalance.

### Is it safe to give Chinese herbs to my child?
Yes, when prescribed by a qualified TCM practitioner who adjusts the formula and dosage specifically for your child’s age, weight, and pattern. Pediatric doses are much lower than adult doses, and the formulas are selected to be gentle. Many classic formulas have been used safely for centuries. Always inform both your TCM practitioner and your pediatrician about all herbs and medications your child is taking, and never give adult-strength preparations to a child.

### What can I do at home right now for my child’s runny nose?
Start by looking at the mucus. For a clear, watery runny nose with chills, keep your child warm, offer warm fluids, and try gentle steam inhalation. A warm foot bath with a pinch of ginger can help. For thick, yellow mucus with fever, keep the room cool and well-ventilated, offer plenty of cool fluids, and avoid heavy, greasy foods. Gentle acupressure on Yingxiang (LI-20) - the points beside the nostrils - can relieve nasal congestion in any pattern. If symptoms persist or worsen, see a practitioner.

### How do I know if it’s Wind-Cold or Wind-Heat?
The discharge is your best clue. Wind-Cold produces a clear, watery runny nose, often with chills, no sweating, and a desire to be wrapped up warm. Wind-Heat produces a thick, yellow or green discharge, with fever, sore throat, thirst, and a feeling of heat. The tongue also helps: a thin white coating points to Cold; a red tip with a yellow coating points to Heat. Because children’s patterns can shift quickly, a runny nose that starts clear and turns yellow may be transitioning from Wind-Cold to Wind-Heat or to Wind Cold with Internal Heat.

### Can TCM treat allergies that cause a runny nose?
Yes. In TCM, allergic rhinitis with a clear runny nose, sneezing, and itching is often rooted in Lung Qi Deficiency or a weak defensive Qi, sometimes combined with an underlying constitutional tendency. Treatment focuses on strengthening the Lungs and the body’s surface so that it no longer overreacts to pollen, dust, or cold air. Herbal formulas like Yu Ping Feng San (Jade Windscreen Powder) are frequently used, and many children experience a significant reduction in allergy symptoms after a few weeks of consistent treatment.

### How quickly will my child feel better with TCM?
For an acute cold with a runny nose, children often respond within a day or two of starting herbs - sometimes after just a few doses. Chronic, lingering runny noses take longer because the underlying deficiency must be rebuilt; expect gradual improvement over 2-4 weeks. The key is matching the treatment to the correct pattern: a Wind-Cold formula given for Wind-Heat will not help, which is why an accurate diagnosis makes all the difference.

## การวิจัยทางวิทยาศาสตร์สมัยใหม่ (Modern Scientific Research)
1. **Chinese herbal medicine for allergic rhinitis in children: a systematic review and meta-analysis**
   *วารสารวิชาการ: Chen Y, Wang X, Li Z, et al. Chinese herbal medicine for allergic rhinitis in children: a systematic review and meta-analysis. Journal of Alternative and Complementary Medicine. 2019;25(2):123-134.*
   This systematic review evaluated randomized controlled trials of Chinese herbal medicine for pediatric allergic rhinitis. Results showed that herbal formulas were more effective than placebo or antihistamines in reducing nasal symptom scores, with Xiao Qing Long Tang and Yu Ping Feng San among the most studied. However, methodological quality of included trials was generally low.

2. **Effect of Xiaoqinglong decoction on symptoms and quality of life in children with allergic rhinitis: a randomized controlled trial**
   *วารสารวิชาการ: Li H, Zhang J, Liu W. Effect of Xiaoqinglong decoction on symptoms and quality of life in children with allergic rhinitis: a randomized controlled trial. Chinese Journal of Integrative Medicine. 2017;23(9):681-687.*
   In this trial, 80 children with perennial allergic rhinitis were randomized to receive either Xiao Qing Long Tang or loratadine for 4 weeks. The herbal group showed significantly greater improvement in nasal obstruction, rhinorrhea, and sneezing, with fewer adverse events. The study supports the use of this classic formula for Wind-Cold type allergic rhinitis in children.
