## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนปัจจุบัน (Conventional Medicine View)
### คำอธิบาย (Description)
Recurrent infections are generally defined as frequent or repeated infections - such as respiratory, urinary, or skin infections - that occur more often than expected for your age and general health. Western medicine looks for an underlying cause, which may include an immune deficiency (primary or acquired), anatomical abnormalities, chronic conditions like diabetes, or lifestyle factors. Diagnosis often involves blood tests to check immune cell counts and antibody levels, imaging, and sometimes referral to an immunologist.

### การรักษาหลัก (Treatments)
Treatment typically involves managing each infection with appropriate antibiotics, antivirals, or antifungals. For those with frequent bacterial infections, doctors may prescribe prophylactic (preventive) antibiotics. Vaccinations, immunoglobulin replacement therapy for certain immune deficiencies, and aggressive management of underlying conditions like allergies or reflux can also be part of the plan. Lifestyle measures such as good hygiene and avoiding sick contacts are universally recommended.

## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนจีน (Traditional Chinese Medicine View)
### ความเข้าใจเกี่ยวกับการแพทย์แผนจีน (TCM Understanding)
In TCM, the body's first line of defense is called Wei Qi - a protective energy that circulates just under the skin, like an invisible shield. The Lungs govern this shield and spread it across the body's surface. The Spleen makes the raw materials - Qi and blood - that feed the shield. So when either organ is weak, the Wei Qi becomes too thin, and even a draft or a tired day can let a cold through.
But a weak shield isn't the only story. Sometimes the problem isn't that the shield is thin, but that it's unstable. This is called Ying-Wei disharmony - the nutritive and defensive energies are out of sync, so the pores don't close properly. The person sweats easily, feels every change in the weather, and catches whatever's going around.
Other times, the body's internal environment is the real issue. Dampness and heat can build up inside - heavy, sticky pathogens that linger and create a warm, humid breeding ground for infections, especially in the urinary tract, skin, or sinuses. Or, in children and some adults, undigested food sits in the stomach, ferments, and generates heat that disturbs the Lung's defense. In each case, the infection keeps coming back because the root imbalance hasn't been cleared.
Finally, some people have a deeper constitutional weakness - the Kidneys and Spleen lack the warming Yang energy that fuels all the body's defenses, or after many illnesses, both Qi and Yin are depleted, leaving a low-grade heat that smolders and makes it hard to fully recover. TCM doesn't just treat the infection; it asks why the body keeps letting it in.

### การวินิจฉัยโดยแพทย์แผนจีน (Practitioner Diagnosis)
A practitioner first asks about the nature of the infections and what the person feels like between episodes. In Spleen and Lung Qi Deficiency, the pattern is one of overall weakness: frequent colds, poor appetite, loose stools, and spontaneous daytime sweating. The tongue is pale with a thin white coat, and the pulse feels weak and thready, pointing to a lack of defensive Qi.
Ying-Wei Disharmony often appears with milder but more persistent symptoms. The person may feel a constant sensitivity to wind, sweat easily at night, and have low-grade fevers that come and go. The tongue is pale with a thin white coat, and the pulse is floating and weak, reflecting an unstable balance between the body’s nutritive and protective energies.
When Damp-Heat is the culprit, the infections tend to be localized and stubborn, such as recurrent urinary tract infections, skin boils, or lingering fevers. The person feels heavy, the tongue is red with a greasy yellow coating, and the pulse is slippery and rapid. The practitioner looks for signs of internal moisture and heat that create a breeding ground for pathogens.
Kidney and Spleen Yang Deficiency is a deeper, more chronic state. The person is markedly cold, with aching low back and knees, profound fatigue, and frequent infections that linger. The tongue is pale and swollen, and the pulse is deep and weak. This pattern signals that the body’s warming and transforming functions are severely depleted, leaving it vulnerable.
After prolonged illness, Qi and Yin Deficiency can set in. Here the person has low-grade fevers that won’t fully resolve, a dry throat, night sweats, and persistent tiredness. The tongue is red with little or no coating, and the pulse is thin and rapid. The practitioner recognizes this as damage to both the body’s vital energy and its cooling, moistening substances.
In children especially, Food Stagnation in the Stomach is a common trigger. The child may have bad breath, a bloated belly, and a thick tongue coating, alongside frequent colds or ear infections. The practitioner sees that undigested food is generating internal heat, which rises to disturb the Lung’s defenses, making the child prone to repeated illness.

## กลุ่มอาการการแพทย์แผนจีนและการรักษา (TCM Patterns and Treatment)

### Spleen and Lung Qi Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/spleen-and-lung-qi-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Fatigue and weakness even after rest, Spontaneous sweating with minimal activity, Poor appetite and bloating after meals, Loose stools, Weak, breathless voice, reluctance to speak, Pale, puffy face without lustre
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Overexertion and pushing through fatigue, Raw, cold, or greasy foods, Exposure to wind, drafts, and cold, Stress and excessive worry
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warm, cooked meals, Rest and adequate sleep, Gentle, consistent movement, Keeping warm and avoiding drafts
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  The Lung governs the body's defensive (Wei) Qi, which circulates at the surface to fend off colds and other infections. The Spleen produces the Qi and blood that feed this defense. When both organs are weak, the Wei Qi becomes too thin to protect the body, so even mild drafts or a busy day can let a pathogen slip in and cause an infection. At the same time, the Spleen's weakness leads to dampness and phlegm, which trap pathogens inside and make it harder to clear them out, so the same infection keeps coming back.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale, puffy, tender body with teeth marks and a thin white slippery coating. Pulse: weak and forceless, especially in the right cun (Lung) and guan (Spleen) positions, and may be fine (thin).
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: <p>Overexertion and lack of rest directly consume the body's Qi, especially that of the Lung and Spleen, leaving the surface unguarded. Raw and cold foods chill the Spleen's digestive fire, impairing its ability to transform food into usable Qi and creating dampness that clogs the system. Warm, cooked foods do the opposite: they are easily transformed into the Qi and blood that feed the body's defenses. Gentle movement circulates Qi without draining it, while rest and sleep are the times when the body actively repairs and replenishes its Wei Qi. Wind and cold are the very pathogens that exploit a weak exterior, so shielding the body from them prevents the cycle from restarting. Stress and worry knot the Spleen Qi, further weakening its ability to produce the energy needed to fight off illness.</p>
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Make warm, cooked foods the center of your diet: soups, stews, congee, and well-cooked grains are easy to digest and gently build Qi. Include Qi-nourishing ingredients like rice, oats, sweet potato, chicken, ginger, and a little astragalus root in broths. Avoid raw, cold, and greasy foods, which dampen the Spleen's fire and create more dampness. Rest deeply and go to bed early; your body replenishes its defense Qi while you sleep. Gentle daily movement like walking or tai chi helps Qi flow without depleting it, but stop before you feel tired. Keep your neck and upper back covered on windy days to protect the Lung's entry point.

### Ying-Wei Disharmony
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/ying-wei-disharmony)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Spontaneous chilly sweating, Sensitivity to wind and drafts, Intermittent mild fever or alternating chills and heat, Frequent mild colds, Fatigue and body aches
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Exposure to wind, drafts, and cold, Overexertion that triggers sweating, Raw, cold foods and drinks, Emotional stress
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Keeping warm and avoiding drafts, Rest and adequate sleep, Warm, cooked meals, Gentle sweating from mild exercise
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  The Nutritive (Ying) Qi nourishes the interior, while the Defensive (Wei) Qi guards the body's surface. When these two fall out of harmony, the exterior becomes unstable - the pores fail to close properly, leading to spontaneous sweating and a heightened sensitivity to wind. This open door allows external pathogens to invade repeatedly, causing the frequent mild colds, low-grade fevers, and body aches that are the hallmark of this pattern. The alternating chills and mild heat reflect the struggle between the body's defenses and the invading pathogen.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale-red or slightly pale body with thin, white, moist coating. Pulse: floating and moderate (浮缓, fú huǎn), sometimes floating and weak (浮弱, fú ruò).
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Wind and cold directly disrupt the already disharmonious Ying-Wei, making the exterior more unstable and inviting pathogens in. Warmth and rest conserve the Defensive Qi, while warm foods gently support the Spleen and the production of Defensive Qi. Gentle sweating from movement helps re-coordinate the Nutritive and Defensive Qi, restoring the body's natural barrier.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Shield yourself from drafts and keep the neck and shoulders covered. Eat warm, cooked foods like congee with ginger and scallion; avoid raw, cold, and icy items that weaken the Defensive Qi. Gentle, consistent movement like Tai Chi or walking helps harmonize the surface without overstraining.

### Damp-Heat
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/damp-heat)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Recurrent urinary tract infections, skin boils, or lingering fevers, Low-grade fever that worsens in the afternoon, Body feels heavy and sluggish, head as if wrapped, Sticky, yellow, greasy tongue coating, Loose, sticky stools and dark, scanty urine
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Greasy, fried foods, Damp, humid weather, Alcohol, Sedentary lifestyle, Overeating
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Light, easily digestible meals, Dry, airy environment, Gentle exercise, Barley water, Cooling down
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Dampness and Heat are heavy, sticky pathogens that linger. When they accumulate inside the body, they create a warm, humid breeding ground where infections keep returning, especially in the urinary tract, skin, or respiratory system. The Spleen gets overwhelmed by the dampness and cannot transform fluids properly, which generates even more dampness and heat in a vicious cycle. This makes it very hard to fully clear an infection, so the same problem flares up again and again.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red body with a yellow, greasy coating that may be swollen with teeth marks. Pulse: soggy-rapid (濡数, rú shuò) or slippery-rapid (滑数, huá shuò).
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Damp-heat arises when the Spleen cannot properly transform and transport fluids, often due to a diet heavy in greasy, sweet, or spicy foods. These foods directly add to internal dampness and heat, creating a breeding ground for infections. A light, bland diet and cooling drinks like barley water help the Spleen recover and drain excess moisture. Gentle exercise stimulates Qi flow and urination, which expels damp-heat. Dry air and avoiding humidity prevent external dampness from adding to the internal burden.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat light, easily digestible foods like barley, mung beans, and bitter gourd. Avoid greasy, fried, spicy, and sweet foods that generate dampness and heat. Stay hydrated but avoid excessive cold or iced drinks. Keep your living environment dry and well-ventilated. Gentle daily exercise like walking or yoga helps move Qi and drain dampness.

### Qi and Yin Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/qi-and-yin-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Low-grade fever that comes and goes, Dry mouth and throat, Night sweats, Fatigue that worsens with exertion, Warm sensation in palms, soles, and chest
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Overwork and late nights, Spicy, fried, or drying foods, Hot, dry weather
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Rest and adequate sleep, Cool, moist environment, Moistening foods (pear, lily bulb)
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  After repeated or prolonged infections, the body's Qi and Yin become depleted. Qi deficiency weakens the defensive (Wei) Qi, leaving the body vulnerable to new pathogens. Yin deficiency creates a lack of cooling and moistening, which allows lingering low-grade heat to smolder, making it hard to fully clear an infection. This combination of weak defense and persistent internal heat sets the stage for infections that keep coming back, often with a drawn-out, low-grade fever and fatigue that never quite resolve.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red or pale-red body with a thin or absent coating, possibly dry and cracked. Pulse: fine (thin) and weak, often rapid.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Overwork and late nights consume Qi and Yin, the very substances that are already depleted, making infections more likely. Spicy and drying foods create internal heat that further evaporates Yin fluids, while moistening foods and rest help restore the cooling, nourishing Yin that calms the lingering low-grade heat. A cool, calm environment supports the body's effort to rebuild its defensive Qi without the stress of external heat or dryness.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Focus on gentle, moistening foods like pear, lily bulb, white fungus soup, and congee with millet. Avoid spicy, fried, or overly drying foods that deplete Yin. Prioritize rest and avoid overwork, as pushing through fatigue drains both Qi and Yin further. Gentle breathing exercises or tai chi can help rebuild energy without overheating.

### Food Stagnation in the Stomach
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/food-stagnation-in-the-stomach)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Upper abdominal bloating and distension that worsens after eating, Sour, rotten-smelling belching and bad breath, Thick, greasy, curd-like tongue coating, Recurrent respiratory infections, especially in children, often with fever
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Overeating, especially greasy or sweet foods, Raw, cold foods and drinks, Eating late at night, Irregular meal times
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Light, easily digestible meals, Gentle abdominal massage, Warm, cooked meals, Regular meal times
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When the Stomach is overloaded, especially with rich, greasy, or sweet foods, undigested food sits and ferments. This creates internal heat and turbid dampness that rise upward, disturbing the Lung's defensive Qi. The result is a vulnerability to repeated respiratory infections, especially in children whose digestion is still maturing.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: normal body, thick greasy or curd-like coating, thickest in the centre. Pulse: slippery (滑 huá), often full or replete (实 shí), prominent at the right guan (Stomach) position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Food stagnation occurs when the stomach is overloaded with hard-to-digest foods, causing undigested matter to ferment and generate heat. Overeating and greasy foods directly cause this accumulation, while light meals and digestive aids help clear the backlog. Gentle movement and massage encourage Stomach Qi to descend, relieving bloating and preventing heat from rising to disturb the lungs.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat smaller, simpler meals at regular times. Avoid overeating, especially rich, greasy, sweet, or cold foods. Chew food thoroughly and stop eating before you feel full. Gentle movement after meals, like a short walk or abdominal massage, helps the stomach move food downward.

## คำถามที่พบบ่อย (Frequently Asked Questions)
### Can TCM really stop me from getting sick so often?
Yes, that's exactly what TCM aims to do. By identifying and treating the underlying pattern that makes you vulnerable - whether it's weak Lung Qi, lingering damp-heat, or something else - herbs and acupuncture can strengthen your body's defenses over time. Most people notice they catch fewer colds or infections within 4 to 8 weeks, and the episodes become milder when they do occur.

### Do I need to take Chinese herbs forever?
No. Herbal formulas are typically taken daily for several weeks or months during the active treatment phase, then tapered off once your constitution has strengthened. Some people may benefit from a maintenance dose during flu season or times of stress, but the goal is always to build lasting resilience, not lifelong dependence.

### Can I use TCM while I'm on antibiotics?
Yes, and it's often very helpful. Herbal formulas can support your energy, reduce side effects like digestive upset, and help clear the infection more completely. Just be sure to tell both your doctor and your TCM practitioner about all medications and herbs you're taking, so they can coordinate your care safely.

### Is acupuncture painful? My child gets infections a lot - can they try it?
Acupuncture needles are as fine as a hair and most people feel only a tiny prick or a dull ache. Many children tolerate acupuncture well, especially when it's brief and gentle. For very young children, TCM practitioners often use non-needle techniques like acupressure, moxibustion, or pediatric tuina massage to stimulate the same points. Always seek a practitioner experienced in treating children.

### What can I eat to help stop repeat infections?
In general, favor warm, cooked foods like soups, stews, and congees that are easy to digest and support your Spleen Qi. Avoid cold, raw, greasy, and sugary foods that create dampness and weaken digestion. Specific dietary advice will depend on your pattern - for example, someone with damp-heat should avoid spicy and rich foods, while someone with Qi deficiency benefits from nourishing grains and root vegetables. See the Diet & Lifestyle section for more details.

### How long will it take to see results?
Many people notice fewer infections within 4 to 8 weeks of consistent treatment. Excess patterns like damp-heat or food stagnation often respond faster - sometimes in 2 to 4 weeks - while deficiency patterns like Qi and Yang deficiency may need 2 to 3 months to rebuild reserves. The key is consistency with herbs and weekly acupuncture sessions in the beginning.

### Is it safe to use TCM during pregnancy for recurrent infections?
TCM can be very helpful during pregnancy, but it must be used with caution. Many herbs and acupuncture points are contraindicated in pregnancy. Always tell your practitioner if you are pregnant or trying to conceive, and work with someone who has experience in prenatal TCM care.
