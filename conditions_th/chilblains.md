## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนปัจจุบัน (Conventional Medicine View)
### คำอธิบาย (Description)
Chilblains (pernio) are itchy, painful, red or purple swellings that appear on fingers, toes, ears, or nose after exposure to cold but not freezing temperatures. They result from abnormal blood vessel constriction and inflammation when skin is rewarmed too quickly. Most episodes heal on their own within a few weeks, but some people suffer recurrent, stubborn lesions every winter. Diagnosis is usually based on appearance and history of cold exposure.

### การรักษาหลัก (Treatments)
Standard care focuses on gentle rewarming, protective clothing, and corticosteroid creams to calm inflammation. For severe or persistent cases, doctors may prescribe nifedipine, a calcium-channel blocker that improves circulation. However, these treatments manage symptoms rather than preventing future outbreaks, and side effects can limit long-term use.

## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนจีน (Traditional Chinese Medicine View)
### ความเข้าใจเกี่ยวกับการแพทย์แผนจีน (TCM Understanding)
In TCM, chilblains are seen as a local manifestation of cold invading the body and causing blood to congeal in the tiny vessels of the hands and feet. The primary culprit is a deficiency of Yang - the body’s inner fire - which fails to warm the limbs and repel external cold. When Qi, the force that moves blood, is also weak, circulation becomes sluggish, making the extremities even more vulnerable to cold-induced stasis. That’s why the same chilly day affects people so differently: it’s your internal weather that matters.
The Spleen and Kidney systems are central here. The Spleen produces Qi and blood, while the Kidney houses the body’s fundamental Yang. If either is depleted, the fingers and toes become a cold, stagnant environment where blood and fluids pool, swell, and eventually turn purple. Over time, this stuck blood can transform into heat, much like a log jam that begins to smolder - turning a quiet chilblain into a hot, red, pus-filled sore.
This means a Western diagnosis of chilblains can have several different TCM roots. A fit young person with purple swelling after a ski trip might have simple Blood Stagnation. An exhausted office worker with slow-healing sores and shortness of breath likely has Qi Deficiency causing Blood Stagnation. Someone who feels bone-chilling cold all winter may have deep Yang Deficiency with Cold-Damp. And when a lesion becomes hot and infected, it signals Blood Stagnation with Heat. Each pattern requires a completely different herbal and acupuncture approach.

### การวินิจฉัยโดยแพทย์แผนจีน (Practitioner Diagnosis)
A TCM practitioner begins by asking what the affected skin looks and feels like. If the area is purplish or bluish, feels cold to the touch, and the pain is sharp and stabbing, simple Blood Stagnation is likely. The tongue may appear pale or show purple spots, and the pulse feels deep and wiry.
When the same purple swelling and numbness come with persistent fatigue, shortness of breath, and a pale face, the root is Qi Deficiency causing Blood Stagnation. The practitioner will ask about your energy levels and how long lesions take to heal. A weak, thready pulse and a pale, slightly dusky tongue confirm the body lacks the Qi to move blood properly.
If the cold extends beyond the fingers and toes and the whole body feels icy, with deep chills and perhaps mental fogginess, the pattern shifts to Yang Deficiency with Cold-Damp. The practitioner checks for a puffy, pale tongue and a pulse that is deep, slow, and faint. This systemic cold indicates the body’s internal fire is too weak to warm the limbs.
When a chilblain becomes red, hot, swollen, and starts to ooze pus, and you also feel feverish and thirsty, the stasis has transformed into Blood Stagnation with Heat. The tongue turns red with a yellow coating, and the pulse becomes rapid. The practitioner will inspect for signs of infection and ask about any sensation of heat to distinguish this inflammatory stage from the earlier cold patterns.

## กลุ่มอาการการแพทย์แผนจีนและการรักษา (TCM Patterns and Treatment)

### Blood Stagnation
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/blood-stagnation)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Fixed stabbing pain in the affected area, Purple or dusky skin patches with swelling, Coldness and numbness in the extremities, Worse with cold exposure, better with warmth, No ulceration or pus (unlike the heat pattern)
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Cold weather or cold water, Prolonged immobility, Tight clothing or shoes, Damp conditions, Cold foods and drinks
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warmth, Gentle exercise, Warm, cooked foods, Rest in a warm environment, Ginger or cinnamon tea
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When cold invades the body, it constricts the blood vessels and slows circulation. In the extremities, where blood flow is already weaker, this leads to blood stasis - the blood congeals and fails to nourish the tissues, causing the characteristic purple swelling, cold pain, and numbness of chilblains.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale or dusky purple body, possible stasis spots, thin white coat. Pulse: deep, thin, and choppy (涩 sè), or wiry (弦 xián).
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Cold causes blood vessels to constrict, worsening stasis; warmth relaxes vessels and promotes blood flow. Movement helps Qi and blood circulate, while inactivity allows stasis to settle. Warming foods and herbs introduce yang energy to dispel cold and move blood, whereas cold foods further chill the body and congeal blood.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Keep hands and feet warm with gloves and thick socks. Soak affected areas in warm (not hot) water with ginger slices to invigorate blood. Eat warming, blood-nourishing foods like lamb stew with dang gui and ginger. Avoid cold drinks and raw foods that can further chill the body. Gentle daily exercise like walking helps keep blood moving.

### Qi Deficiency causing Blood Stagnation
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/qi-deficiency-causing-blood-stagnation)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Aching, cold pain in the lesions, Purple or dusky red lesions that heal slowly, Cold and numb fingers or toes, Marked fatigue and weakness, Shortness of breath with mild activity, Pale or dusky facial complexion
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Cold, damp weather, Overwork and fatigue, Prolonged standing or sitting, Cold foods and drinks, Excessive worry or anxiety
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warmth, Gentle exercise, Warm, cooked foods, Rest and sleep, Moxibustion
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Qi is the force that moves blood. When your Qi is deficient, blood circulation becomes sluggish, especially in the hands and feet. Cold weather easily congeals the already stagnant blood, causing the purple, swollen, and painful lesions of chilblains. Because Qi is too weak to repair the tissue, the sores heal slowly and keep coming back, while you feel tired and breathless.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale-dark or pale-purple body with possible purple spots, teeth marks, and a thin white coat. Pulse: deep (沉 chén), fine (细 xì) or weak, and choppy (涩 sè).
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Qi deficiency means the body lacks the energy to move blood effectively. Cold weather and raw foods further congeal the blood, worsening stasis, while overwork drains the already weak Qi. Warmth, rest, and nourishing foods build Qi and blood, gently moving stagnation. Moxibustion adds therapeutic heat to the channels, directly countering cold and invigorating blood flow.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat warm, cooked foods that build Qi and blood: slow-cooked stews with beef or chicken, root vegetables, ginger, cinnamon, and small amounts of dates or goji berries. Avoid raw, cold, and iced foods that chill the body and weaken digestion. Gentle daily movement like walking or tai chi helps circulate Qi without exhausting you. Keep your hands and feet warm with gloves and thick socks, and soak them in warm water with a slice of ginger.

### Yang Deficiency with Cold-Damp
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/yang-deficiency-with-cold-damp)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Icy cold hands and feet that are hard to warm, Deep, heavy aching in bones and joints, Low back feels cold and sore, Pale, puffy face and swollen tongue with teeth marks, Mental fog or confusion in severe cases
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Cold, damp weather, Cold foods and drinks, Overwork and fatigue, Prolonged standing in cold water
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warmth, Warm, cooked foods, Rest and sleep, Moxibustion, Gentle exercise
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When the body's inner fire (Yang) is deeply depleted, it can no longer warm the limbs or repel external cold and dampness. Cold invades and congeals the blood in the tiny vessels of the fingers and toes, while dampness adds heaviness and swelling. This leads to intensely cold, dusky-purple chilblains that are slow to heal, accompanied by systemic chilliness, deep aching, and in severe cases mental fog.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale, swollen body with teeth marks, white greasy moist coating. Pulse: deep (沉 chén), slow (迟 chí), and fine (微 wēi) or soft (濡 rú).
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: When Yang is deficient, the body cannot generate enough heat to warm the extremities or repel cold-damp. Cold and dampness further congeal blood and fluids, worsening chilblains and causing heavy aching. Warming foods and external heat directly supplement Yang, while rest conserves energy so it can be used for healing. Moxibustion at Mingmen and Dazhui specifically ignites the body's core fire, driving out deep cold.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat warming, cooked foods like stews with ginger, cinnamon, and lamb. Avoid cold raw salads, iced drinks, and dairy. Keep the lower back and feet warm, and consider using a moxa stick on the low back (Mingmen) daily. Gentle movement like walking can help circulate Yang, but avoid exhaustion.

### Blood Stagnation with Heat
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/blood-stagnation-with-heat)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Red, hot, swollen lesion that may ulcerate and discharge pus, Throbbing or stabbing pain in the affected area, Fever, thirst, and irritability, Dark or purplish-red tongue with stasis spots, Rapid, choppy pulse
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Spicy, greasy, or fried foods, Alcohol and coffee, Prolonged heat (hot bath, heating pad), Scratching or rubbing the lesion, Stress and frustration
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Cool compresses, Rest and elevation of the affected part, Cooling foods (cucumber, watermelon), Keeping the lesion clean and dry
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When the initial cold invasion causes blood to congeal in the small vessels of the fingers and toes, that stagnant blood can eventually transform into heat, much like a log jam that begins to smolder. This internal heat inflames the chilblain, turning it red, hot, and swollen.
As the heat intensifies, the tissue may break down and produce pus, and the heat rises through the body, causing fever, thirst, and a restless, irritable feeling.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: reddish-purple body with dark stasis spots, distended veins under the tongue, and a thin dry yellow coat. Pulse: choppy (涩 sè) and rapid (数 shuò), often with a wiry (弦 xián) quality.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: This pattern is driven by heat trapped in the blood. Cooling measures directly calm that fire, while heating foods and activities add fuel. Rest and elevation help blood flow away from the inflamed area, reducing the local stasis. Spicy and greasy foods, alcohol, and emotional stress all stir up internal heat, which can worsen the redness, swelling, and pain.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Keep the affected area clean and dry, and avoid scratching or rubbing it, which can worsen inflammation and invite infection. Apply cool (not ice-cold) compresses if it feels soothing. Eat plenty of cooling, non-greasy foods like cucumber, watermelon, mung beans, and leafy greens, and steer clear of spicy, fried, or overly rich meals. Alcohol and coffee can add heat, so it is best to avoid them while the lesion is inflamed. Gentle rest with the affected limb elevated helps reduce swelling and pain.

## คำถามที่พบบ่อย (Frequently Asked Questions)
### Can acupuncture really help chilblains?
Yes. Acupuncture and moxibustion (warming herbs burned near the skin) are core TCM tools for chilblains. They work by invigorating blood circulation, dispersing cold, and stimulating the body’s own healing response. Many patients notice the itching and swelling calm down after just a few sessions.

### Is moxibustion safe to use on chilblains?
Moxibustion is very safe for non-infected chilblains and is one of the most effective ways to warm the channels and move stagnant blood. However, it should never be applied directly on broken skin, open sores, or infected lesions. Always have a trained practitioner guide you.

### Can I use TCM alongside my usual corticosteroid cream or nifedipine?
Yes, TCM treatments generally complement conventional care. Herbs and acupuncture can be used together with topical creams or oral medications. It’s important to tell both your TCM practitioner and your doctor about everything you’re taking, especially if you use blood-moving herbs alongside anticoagulants.

### How long does it take to see results?
For a new chilblain, you may feel relief within days. Chronic, recurrent chilblains take longer - usually 4 to 8 weeks to see fewer outbreaks, and a full winter season of preventive treatment to stop them coming back year after year.

### Will my chilblains come back after TCM treatment?
The goal of TCM is to correct the underlying imbalance so you stop getting chilblains every winter. Once your Qi and Yang are rebuilt and blood moves freely, the tendency to develop new lesions should greatly diminish. Some patients may need a short seasonal tune-up each autumn to stay protected.

### Is it safe to use TCM herbs if I’m pregnant?
Many herbs are safe during pregnancy, but some blood-moving herbs (like Dang Gui or Hong Hua) must be used with caution or avoided. Always inform your TCM practitioner if you are pregnant or nursing so they can adjust the formula for safety.

### What foods should I avoid if I get chilblains?
In general, avoid cold, raw foods and icy drinks, which can dampen your internal fire. Steer clear of heavy, greasy, or dairy-rich foods that create dampness. Instead, favor warm, cooked meals with ginger, cinnamon, lamb, and root vegetables to stoke your Yang and keep blood moving.
