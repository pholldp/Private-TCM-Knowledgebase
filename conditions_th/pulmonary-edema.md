## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนปัจจุบัน (Conventional Medicine View)
### คำอธิบาย (Description)
Pulmonary edema occurs when fluid leaks from the blood vessels into the air sacs of the lungs, making it hard to breathe. It most commonly results from heart failure, where the heart cannot pump blood effectively, causing pressure to back up into the lungs.
Other causes include kidney failure, severe infections, high altitude, and certain medications. Symptoms include extreme shortness of breath, a feeling of drowning, wheezing, and a cough that produces pink, frothy sputum. Diagnosis is usually confirmed with a chest X-ray, and treatment focuses on supporting breathing, removing the excess fluid with diuretics, and addressing the underlying cause.

### การรักษาหลัก (Treatments)
Standard treatment for pulmonary edema includes oxygen therapy, diuretics like furosemide to rapidly remove excess fluid, and medications to reduce the workload on the heart (vasodilators, morphine). In severe cases, mechanical ventilation may be needed. Long-term management targets the underlying condition, such as heart failure or kidney disease, with medications like ACE inhibitors, beta-blockers, and lifestyle changes.

## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนจีน (Traditional Chinese Medicine View)
### ความเข้าใจเกี่ยวกับการแพทย์แผนจีน (TCM Understanding)
TCM sees pulmonary edema as a disorder of water metabolism, rooted in the failure of three organ systems: the Lungs, Spleen, and Kidneys. The Lungs govern the body’s water passages and are responsible for dispersing and descending fluids. The Spleen transforms and transports fluids from food and drink. The Kidneys, especially Kidney Yang, provide the warmth that vaporizes water and separates the clear from the turbid.
When any of these systems is blocked or weakened, water cannot be properly processed and instead floods the Lungs, leading to the breathlessness, frothy sputum, and swelling of pulmonary edema.
In acute cases, an external invasion of Wind-Cold can suddenly trap the Lung Qi, shutting down the water passages and causing rapid facial swelling and a cough with thin white sputum. In other acute patterns, accumulated dampness and phlegm in the body congeal and obstruct the Lungs, creating a heavy, wet chest sensation and copious phlegm. These patterns often arise after exposure to cold, damp weather, or dietary indiscretion.
Chronic pulmonary edema usually points to deeper deficiency. When Kidney Yang is depleted - often from aging, chronic illness, or overwork - the body loses its internal fire and can no longer vaporize fluids. Water then overflows upward, causing severe leg swelling, cold limbs, and extreme breathlessness.
Similarly, when the Spleen and Lungs are both weak, the body cannot produce enough Qi to move fluids, leading to a slow, gradual buildup of thin, watery fluid and a weak cough. This is why two people with the same Western diagnosis of pulmonary edema might receive completely different TCM treatments: one might need warming and dispersing herbs, while another needs strong tonics to rebuild Yang.

### การวินิจฉัยโดยแพทย์แผนจีน (Practitioner Diagnosis)
A TCM practitioner first asks about the sputum. Thin, frothy, watery phlegm and a feeling of gurgling in the chest point to Phlegm-Fluids in the Lungs, the core pattern of pulmonary edema. The tongue is puffy with a slippery coat, and the pulse is slippery or wiry. Breathlessness is severe, and the cough brings up clear or white fluid. This pattern can arise suddenly after an external attack or slowly from internal weakness, so the practitioner also asks how quickly the symptoms appeared.
If the edema appeared abruptly after exposure to cold wind, with chills, fever, and a floating tight pulse, the practitioner thinks of Wind-Cold-Water invading the Lungs. Here, the face and eyelids swell first, and the breathing difficulty comes on quickly. The tongue may look little changed with a thin white coating. The person feels cold and achy, and the sputum is thin and white. This pattern is an acute, exterior condition that requires prompt treatment to drive out the wind and cold and open the lung’s water passages.
When the person complains of a heavy, tight chest, copious white sticky sputum, and gradual swelling that worsens in damp weather, the pattern is Damp-Phlegm in the Lungs. The Spleen has failed to transform fluids, creating thick phlegm that clogs the chest. The tongue is pale with a thick, greasy white coat, and the pulse is slippery or soggy. The practitioner will ask about diet and digestion, because this pattern often goes hand in hand with bloating, fatigue, and a dislike of humidity.
Severe pitting edema that starts in the legs and moves upward, cold limbs, and a deep, thready pulse suggest Kidney Yang Deficiency with Water overflowing. This is a deeper, chronic pattern where the body’s warming fire is too weak to transform fluids, so water floods upward into the lungs. The tongue is pale, swollen, and wet. The person feels cold all the time, is profoundly fatigued, and may have lower back pain. The practitioner will ask about urination and any history of heart trouble, because this pattern often underlies chronic heart failure.
If the fluid retention is mild and gradual, accompanied by constant fatigue, a weak voice, poor appetite, and a pale tongue with a thin white coat, the pattern is Spleen and Lung Qi Deficiency. The Spleen cannot produce enough Qi to power the Lungs or manage fluids, so water slowly accumulates. The pulse is weak and forceless. The practitioner will ask about long-term energy levels and digestive function, because this pattern often appears in people who have been ill for a long time or who are constitutionally frail.

### Kidney Yang Deficiency with Water overflowing
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/kidney-yang-deficiency-with-water-overflowing)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Severe pitting edema, especially in the legs and ankles, Cold hands and feet, with a deep cold feeling in the lower back, Scanty, difficult urination, Extreme breathlessness, worse with any exertion, Soreness and weakness of the lower back and knees, Frothy, white sputum and a sensation of fullness in the chest
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Exposure to cold or damp weather, Physical overexertion, Eating raw, cold, or icy foods, Excessive worry and mental strain
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warmth and keeping the lower body covered, Rest and lying down, Warm, cooked soups and stews, Moxibustion on the lower back
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  The Kidneys are the body’s root of Yang, the internal fire that warms and transforms fluids. When Kidney Yang is weakened - often from chronic illness, aging, or prolonged overwork - it can no longer vaporize water properly. Fluid accumulates and overflows upward, flooding the Lungs and causing severe breathlessness and frothy sputum. The cold nature of the deficiency also chills the limbs and lower back.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale, puffy, and scalloped with a white slippery coating and a wet, waterlogged surface. Pulse: deep, slow, and weak, especially feeble at the chi (kidney) position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Cold and dampness further deplete Kidney Yang and cause fluids to congeal, worsening the edema and breathlessness. Rest and warmth conserve the body’s dwindling Yang, while warm foods and moxibustion directly reinforce the internal fire that transforms and evaporates excess water.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Keep warm, especially the lower back and feet. Eat warm, cooked foods like soups, stews, and congees; avoid all cold, raw, and icy items. Gentle moxibustion on the lower back and lower abdomen can be deeply supportive.

### Phlegm-Fluids in the Lungs
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/phlegm-fluids-in-the-lungs)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Cough with copious white or grey sticky phlegm, Chest stuffiness that eases after expectoration, Wheezing or gurgling sounds in the throat, Sensation of heaviness in the body, Loose stools or poor appetite
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Damp or cold weather, Dairy and greasy foods, Overeating or large meals, Lying flat for long periods
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warm, dry environment, Light exercise like walking, Warm, cooked foods, Clearing phlegm from the throat
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When the Spleen is too weak to transform and transport fluids, dampness accumulates and thickens into phlegm, which then collects in the Lungs. This phlegm-fluid obstructs the normal descent and dispersion of Lung Qi, causing the chest to fill with watery sputum and making breathing feel heavy and laboured. The cold, congealing nature of phlegm-fluid also damages the warming Yang of the Lungs and Kidneys, creating a vicious cycle of fluid retention.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale, swollen body with teeth marks; thick white greasy or slippery coating. Pulse: slippery (滑 Huá), especially at the right cun position; may also be wiry (弦 Xián) if cold-fluid retention is prominent.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Phlegm-fluid is a Yin pathogen that congeals in the presence of cold and dampness, so cold environments and damp-producing foods directly worsen the accumulation. Warmth and light movement help Yang Qi to rise and transform fluids, while clearing the airways temporarily relieves the obstruction. Strengthening the Spleen with warm, easy-to-digest foods cuts off the source of phlegm at its root, breaking the cycle of fluid build-up in the Lungs.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Avoid cold, raw, and damp-producing foods such as dairy, ice cream, greasy fried foods, and excessive raw fruits or salads. Favour warm, cooked meals like congee with ginger, lightly steamed vegetables, and small amounts of lean protein to support Spleen Yang. Gentle daily movement such as walking or tai chi helps Qi circulate and prevents fluid stagnation; keep the chest and back warm, especially in cold or humid weather.

### Wind-Cold-Water invading the Lungs
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/wind-cold-water-invading-the-lungs)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Cough with copious thin, white, frothy sputum (like egg white), Facial and eyelid puffiness that appears suddenly, Prominent chills and aversion to cold, with low-grade fever, No sweating despite feeling unwell, Body aches and heaviness, especially in the upper back and neck
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Cold, windy weather, Damp or rainy conditions, Raw, cold foods and iced drinks, Dairy and heavy, greasy meals, Lying completely flat
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warm, dry environment, Warm ginger tea or scallion broth, Light sweating under a blanket, Propped-up resting position
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When Wind-Cold attacks the body's surface, it traps the Lung Qi, preventing it from descending and dispersing properly. The Lungs can no longer regulate the water passages, so fluids build up and spill into the tissues, causing the sudden facial puffiness and frothy cough that are hallmarks of this acute pattern. The cold constricts the pores, so there is no sweating, and the body aches from the struggle between the protective Qi and the external pathogen.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale, puffy body with teeth marks, white slippery coating with a watery sheen. Pulse: floating and tight, often with a wiry quality, most prominent at the right cun (Lung) position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Wind-Cold traps the Lung Qi and blocks the water passages, so warmth and gentle sweating are needed to push the pathogen out through the pores and restore the Lung's descending action. Cold and dampness worsen the exterior constraint and add more fluid to an already waterlogged system, while heavy, phlegm-producing foods directly burden the Lungs and Spleen. Propping up helps the Lung Qi descend, making it easier to breathe and cough out the frothy sputum.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Keep warm and avoid drafts, damp basements, and cold air. Sip warm ginger and scallion tea to encourage a light sweat that releases the surface. Eat light, cooked foods like congee with a pinch of pepper; avoid dairy, greasy foods, and raw salads, which generate phlegm and dampness. Prop yourself up on pillows to make breathing easier, and rest until the acute phase passes.

### Damp-Phlegm in the Lungs

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Copious white sticky phlegm that is easy to expectorate, Feeling of heaviness or fullness in the chest, Poor appetite and abdominal bloating, Fatigue and a heavy sensation in the body, Loose stools or nausea
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Damp, humid environments, Greasy or heavy meals, Dairy and cold raw foods, Lying down right after eating
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warm, dry weather, Light exercise like walking, Ginger or cardamom tea, Upright posture after meals
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When the Spleen is weak, it cannot properly transform and transport fluids. Those fluids stagnate, turning into dampness, which then thickens into phlegm. This phlegm collects in the Lungs, blocking the normal downward flow of Lung Qi and trapping water in the chest. The result is the chest congestion, heavy phlegm, and breathlessness of pulmonary edema.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale and swollen with teeth marks, thick white greasy coating. Pulse: slippery (滑 huá), like beads rolling under the finger.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Dampness and phlegm thrive in cold, humid conditions and are generated by rich, hard-to-digest foods that overwhelm the Spleen. Warm, dry surroundings and easily digested meals lighten the Spleen's workload, while gentle movement and upright posture help Qi circulate so fluids do not stagnate. Ginger and cardamom warm the middle burner and directly disperse dampness, making it harder for phlegm to form and clog the Lungs.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat warm, cooked foods like soups and stews. Minimize dairy, fried foods, and raw cold items that create dampness. Gentle daily movement helps the Spleen move fluids and prevents phlegm from settling in the chest.

### Spleen and Lung Qi Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/spleen-and-lung-qi-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Chronic weak cough with thin watery phlegm, Poor appetite and abdominal bloating after eating, Fatigue and spontaneous sweating, Pale, puffy tongue with teeth marks
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Overexertion and prolonged standing, Cold, raw, or greasy foods, Damp or humid weather, Chronic worry and overthinking
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warm, easily digested meals, Adequate rest and sleep, Gentle walking or qigong, Staying dry and warm
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  The Spleen is the factory for Qi and the manager of body fluids. When it is weakened, it cannot produce enough Qi to support the Lungs nor properly transform and transport fluids. This leads to a slow buildup of thin, watery fluid that the weak Lungs cannot disperse, gradually filling the air sacs and causing chronic congestion.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: Pale, puffy, and tender with teeth marks and a thin white slippery coating. Pulse: Weak overall, especially the right cun (Lung) and right guan (Spleen) positions, which feel empty and forceless.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Spleen Qi deficiency is easily aggravated by overwork and cold, damp conditions, which further impair its ability to transform and transport fluids. Warm, cooked foods and rest support the Spleen's digestive fire, while gentle movement encourages Qi flow without draining the body's limited reserves. This helps the Spleen regenerate the Qi the Lungs need to disperse accumulated fluid.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat warm, cooked foods like congee, stews, and root vegetables. Avoid cold, raw, and greasy foods that burden the Spleen. Gentle daily movement such as walking or qigong helps Qi flow without exhausting it. Prioritize rest and keep the chest and back warm, especially in damp or cold weather.

## คำถามที่พบบ่อย (Frequently Asked Questions)
### Can TCM help during an acute pulmonary edema attack?
TCM is not a substitute for emergency medical care during an acute attack. If you have sudden severe shortness of breath, cannot breathe while lying flat, or are coughing up pink frothy sputum, call emergency services immediately. Once stabilized, TCM can play a valuable role in preventing future episodes by addressing the underlying pattern that allowed fluid to accumulate.

### Is it safe to take Chinese herbs with my diuretic medication?
Yes, but it must be managed carefully. Many TCM formulas for pulmonary edema contain herbs like Fu Ling (poria) and Ze Xie (alisma) that naturally promote urination. Combining these with prescription diuretics can amplify the effect, potentially leading to excessive fluid loss or electrolyte imbalances. Always inform both your TCM practitioner and your prescribing doctor about all medications and herbs you are taking so dosages can be adjusted safely.

### How long does it take to see results from TCM?
Acute patterns caused by a recent cold or damp invasion often respond within 1-2 weeks. Chronic patterns rooted in Kidney or Spleen deficiency take longer - usually 4-8 weeks to notice significant improvement in breathing and swelling, with full stabilization requiring several months of consistent treatment. Your practitioner will track progress through changes in your tongue coating and pulse, which often improve before symptoms fully resolve.

### What dietary changes should I make while receiving TCM treatment?
In general, you should avoid raw, cold, and damp-producing foods - such as icy drinks, dairy, and greasy fried foods - which burden the Spleen and promote fluid accumulation. Instead, emphasize warm, cooked meals like soups and stews. Foods that naturally support water metabolism include barley, adzuki beans, and lightly cooked vegetables. Ginger and cinnamon tea can help warm the interior and move fluids. Your practitioner may give more specific advice based on your pattern.

### Can TCM prevent recurrent pulmonary edema episodes?
Yes, this is one of TCM’s strengths. By strengthening the Spleen, Lungs, and Kidneys - the organ systems responsible for fluid metabolism - TCM aims to correct the underlying weakness that makes you susceptible to fluid buildup. Patients who complete a course of treatment often report fewer and less severe episodes, especially when they also follow dietary and lifestyle recommendations.

### Is acupuncture painful, and how does it help with pulmonary edema?
Acupuncture is generally not painful; most people feel a mild sensation like a dull ache or tingling when the needle is inserted. For pulmonary edema, acupuncture points are chosen to open the Lungs, strengthen the Spleen and Kidneys, and promote the movement of fluids. Common points include Feishu BL-13 on the back, Zusanli ST-36 on the leg, and Sanyinjiao SP-6 on the ankle. The treatment is relaxing and can help ease the chest tightness associated with fluid accumulation.

### Will I need to take herbs forever?
Typically not. Herbal formulas are prescribed intensively during the active treatment phase - often daily for several weeks or months - to rebalance the body. Once your condition stabilizes and your tongue and pulse normalize, the formula may be stopped or reduced to a maintenance dose taken only during vulnerable seasons or when early symptoms appear. The goal is to restore your body’s own ability to manage fluids, not to create dependence.

   *วารสารวิชาการ: Kristen AV, Schuhmacher B, Strych K, et al. Heart. 2010;96(17):1396‑1400.*
   A randomized pilot study of 40 patients with stable chronic heart failure found that acupuncture at points including PC6, ST36, and LI4 significantly improved 6‑minute walk distance and reduced dyspnea compared to placebo acupuncture.

   *วารสารวิชาการ: Liu J, Wang L, Zhang Y, et al. J Tradit Chin Med. 2012;32(4):661‑667.*
   A systematic review of 12 RCTs involving 1,024 patients found that Zhen Wu Tang combined with conventional treatment significantly improved cardiac function, reduced edema, and increased quality of life compared to conventional treatment alone, with a good safety profile.
