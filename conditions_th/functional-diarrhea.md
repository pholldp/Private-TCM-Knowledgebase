## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนปัจจุบัน (Conventional Medicine View)
### คำอธิบาย (Description)
Functional diarrhea is defined as chronic loose or watery stools without an identifiable structural or biochemical cause. In Western medicine, it is considered a functional gastrointestinal disorder, meaning the digestive system looks normal on tests like colonoscopy but doesn't work quite right. Diagnosis typically comes after ruling out infections, inflammatory bowel disease, celiac disease, and other organic causes. Patients usually experience loose stools for at least three months, often accompanied by urgency, bloating, or abdominal discomfort, but without blood in the stool or unexplained weight loss.

### การรักษาหลัก (Treatments)
Conventional management typically focuses on symptom control through dietary adjustments, stress management, and anti-diarrheal medications like loperamide. Fiber supplements may be recommended to add bulk to stools, while probiotics are sometimes suggested to improve gut flora. For some patients, bile acid binders or low-dose tricyclic antidepressants are prescribed to slow gut motility. Treatment is largely one-size-fits-all: the same anti-diarrheal medication is offered regardless of whether your diarrhea strikes after eating, after stress, or first thing in the morning.

## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนจีน (Traditional Chinese Medicine View)
### ความเข้าใจเกี่ยวกับการแพทย์แผนจีน (TCM Understanding)
TCM understands functional diarrhea primarily as a failure of the Spleen to properly transform and transport fluids. In this system, the Spleen is not just a single organ but a functional network responsible for digesting food, separating clear nutrients from turbid waste, and holding everything in its proper place. When Spleen Qi is weak - from poor diet, overwork, or constitutional tendency - fluids accumulate as dampness and descend into the intestines as loose stool.
But Spleen weakness is rarely the full story. The Liver governs the smooth flow of Qi throughout the body, and when emotional stress or frustration constrains that flow, Liver Qi can surge sideways to attack the Spleen. This is why many people experience sudden diarrhea triggered by arguments, deadlines, or anxiety. The root is Liver constraint, but the battlefield is the Spleen.
The Kidneys provide the deep warmth that powers all digestion. When Kidney Yang runs low - often from aging, chronic illness, or constitutional weakness - the entire digestive system loses its fire. Food is not properly cooked, fluids are not held, and diarrhea often strikes in the early morning when Yang energy should be rising but is too depleted to do so. Each of these mechanisms produces a different type of diarrhea, and TCM treatment targets the specific cause rather than just the symptom.

### การวินิจฉัยโดยแพทย์แผนจีน (Practitioner Diagnosis)
A practitioner first looks at the character of the diarrhea and what brings it on. Spleen and Stomach Qi Deficiency typically shows as chronic loose stools that are not particularly urgent or foul, accompanied by poor appetite, bloating after eating, and a general sense of fatigue. The tongue is pale with a thin white coat and the pulse feels thin and weak, confirming that the digestive engine lacks the energy to properly transform food and fluids.
Damp-Heat in the Large Intestine presents a very different picture. The diarrhea is urgent, frequent, and foul-smelling, often sticky or with a burning sensation around the anus. The tongue is red with a thick, yellow, greasy coat, and the pulse is rapid and slippery. This pattern often arises when dietary excess or lingering Spleen weakness allows heat and dampness to brew in the bowels.
When emotional stress is a clear trigger, Rebellious Liver Qi invading the Spleen becomes the prime suspect. The hallmark is abdominal pain that strikes just before a bowel movement and eases afterward. The tongue may look relatively normal or slightly pale, but the pulse feels wiry, especially on the left side, reflecting the constrained Liver energy that is lashing out at the digestive system.
Kidney and Spleen Yang Deficiency is suspected when diarrhea strikes in the very early morning, often around dawn, and is accompanied by a deep coldness in the limbs and soreness in the lower back. The tongue is pale, swollen, and often has tooth marks, while the pulse is deep and weak. This pattern indicates that the body’s internal fire has dimmed, failing to warm and hold the fluids.
Cold-Damp invading the Spleen produces watery, relatively painless diarrhea that feels cold and heavy. A person may describe a sensation of cold in the abdomen and an aversion to cold drinks. The tongue coat is white and slippery, and the pulse is deep and slow. This pattern is often acute, following exposure to cold or excessive raw foods, and is distinct from the chronic deficiency patterns.
In rare prolonged cases, Spleen Yin Deficiency can cause chronic diarrhea with very subtle signs. Unlike the cold or weak patterns, the tongue may appear red and thin with little coating, and the pulse is thin and rapid. There is often a dry mouth or a vague sense of internal heat that is not as intense as full-blown Damp-Heat, pointing to a lack of nourishing fluids rather than an excess of dampness.

## กลุ่มอาการการแพทย์แผนจีนและการรักษา (TCM Patterns and Treatment)

### Spleen and Stomach Qi Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/spleen-and-stomach-qi-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Loose stools after meals, Poor appetite and early fullness, Bloating after eating, Fatigue and heavy limbs, Sallow or pale complexion
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Cold raw foods and iced drinks, Heavy, greasy meals, Excessive worry and mental strain, Overwork and physical exhaustion, Damp or humid environments, Irregular eating habits
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warm, cooked, easily digestible meals, Rest and adequate sleep, Abdominal warmth (warm compress or covering), Gentle walking or movement, Regular meal times
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Your Spleen is the organ that transforms food into Qi and separates clear from turbid fluids. When Spleen Qi is weak, this process falters: food is not properly digested and fluids accumulate as dampness. The result is chronic loose, unformed stools that often appear after meals, along with poor appetite and a bloated, heavy feeling in the abdomen because the digestive engine simply lacks the power to keep things moving efficiently.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Pale, puffy tongue with tooth marks and a thin white coating. Pulse is weak, soft, and forceless, especially at the right Guan (middle) position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: A deficient Spleen is like a low fire that cannot cook food properly if you keep throwing cold water on it. Cold and raw foods, overwork, and worry all dampen that fire further, allowing dampness to accumulate and leak downward as loose stools. Warmth, rest, and easily digestible foods take the burden off the Spleen, giving it the gentle, steady support it needs to transform food into energy and hold the stools together.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Make warm, cooked, easily digestible foods the center of your meals: congee, soups, stewed root vegetables, and small portions of well-cooked grains. Avoid raw salads, icy drinks, dairy, and greasy fried foods, which overtax a weak Spleen. Eat at regular times, chew thoroughly, and stop before you feel completely full. Gentle daily movement like walking supports Qi flow, but rest when you are tired - pushing through fatigue further depletes your Spleen.

### Damp-Heat in the Large Intestine
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/damp-heat-in-the-large-intestine)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Urgent, explosive diarrhea, Foul-smelling stools with mucus or blood, Burning anus during and after defecation, Incomplete evacuation feeling, Thirst with body heat, Heavy, tired limbs
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Spicy, greasy, or fried foods, Alcohol and coffee, Hot, humid weather, Emotional stress and frustration, Overeating or eating late at night
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warm, cooked, easily digestible meals, Drinking warm water, Rest and avoiding overexertion, Cool, dry weather
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When the Spleen fails to manage fluids, Dampness builds up inside the body. If this Dampness combines with Heat - often from a rich diet, alcohol, or hot weather - it sinks into the Large Intestine. There it disrupts the normal separation of fluids and wastes, producing urgent, foul-smelling, sticky diarrhea that burns on the way out and leaves you feeling like you haven't finished.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red body with a thick, yellow, greasy coating (thickest at the centre and root). Pulse: slippery (滑 huá) and rapid (数 shuò).
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Damp-Heat is a combination of two heavy, sticky, and warm pathogenic factors. Spicy, greasy, and hot-natured foods and drinks add more Heat and Dampness, making the diarrhea more urgent and foul. Alcohol and coffee are particularly heating and dampening. Hot, humid weather mirrors the internal environment and aggravates the condition. Emotional stress can stir Heat and disrupt the Spleen's function, worsening the pattern. On the other hand, light, bland foods and warm water help the Spleen recover without adding more burden, while rest allows the body to drain the Damp-Heat naturally. Cool, dry environments counteract the external influences that feed the pattern.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat light, easily digested meals like rice congee, steamed vegetables, and small amounts of lean protein. Avoid all greasy, fried, spicy, and heavily sweetened foods, as well as alcohol and coffee, which directly feed Dampness and Heat. Drink warm water throughout the day, but not ice-cold drinks. Gentle walking after meals can help the Spleen move fluids, but rest when tired.

### Rebellious Liver Qi invading the Spleen
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/rebellious-liver-qi-invading-the-spleen)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Abdominal pain before diarrhea, Pain relief after bowel movement, Diarrhea triggered by emotions, Rib-side distension and fullness, Irritability and frequent sighing, Alternating constipation and loose stools
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Emotional stress, Anger or frustration, Cold raw foods and iced drinks, Irregular eating habits
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Calm, stress-free environment, Warm, cooked, easily digestible meals, Regular meal times, Gentle abdominal massage
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  The Liver is responsible for the smooth flow of Qi throughout the body. When emotional stress, frustration, or anger build up, Liver Qi stagnates and then surges sideways to attack the Spleen. This disrupts the Spleen's ability to transform and transport fluids, causing sudden diarrhea that is typically preceded by cramping abdominal pain. The pain often eases once the bowel movement passes, because the stagnant Qi is temporarily released.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: normal or slightly pale body with possible teeth marks, thin white coating that may be slightly greasy. Pulse: wiry (弦 xián) overall, especially at the left Guan position; the right Guan position may be softer, reflecting Spleen deficiency.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Emotional stress directly causes Liver Qi to stagnate and then invade the Spleen, which is the core mechanism of this pattern. Calm and relaxation allow the Liver to regain its smooth flow, while warm, regular meals support the Spleen's digestive function. Cold and raw foods further weaken the Spleen, making it even more vulnerable to the Liver's attack, so avoiding them helps break the cycle of pain and diarrhea.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Make stress management a priority: gentle walking, meditation, or deep breathing can help keep Liver Qi flowing smoothly. Eat warm, cooked meals at regular times and avoid cold, raw, or greasy foods that burden the Spleen. Limit alcohol and coffee, which can heat the Liver and worsen irritability.

### Kidney and Spleen Yang Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/kidney-and-spleen-yang-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Early-morning diarrhea (5 AM), Undigested food in stool, Aching cold in lower back and knees, Cold hands and feet
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Cold raw foods and iced drinks, Cold damp weather, Overwork and physical exhaustion, Emotional stress
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warm, cooked, easily digestible meals, Abdominal warmth (warm compress or covering), Gentle walking or movement
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Your digestive fire depends on the warmth of your body's deep reserves, called Kidney Yang. When that deep warmth runs low, the Spleen cannot cook food properly or hold fluids. The result is watery, undigested stool that often strikes in the early morning, when Yang energy should be rising but is too weak to do so.
This pattern often develops from long-standing Spleen Qi deficiency that has gradually exhausted the body's core warmth. Without that foundational heat, fluids accumulate as internal Dampness and spill downward, causing chronic loose stools.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale, swollen and puffy with teeth marks on the sides, and a white slippery coating. Pulse: deep, weak, and slow, especially feeble at the rear positions.

Overwork depletes Qi, which is the foundation of Yang, while stress stagnates Liver Qi, which can then attack the weakened Spleen. Warmth, rest, and gentle movement preserve Yang, helping your body's digestive fire gradually rebuild.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat only warm, cooked foods like soups, stews, and congees; raw salads and cold drinks extinguish your digestive fire. Ginger tea and cinnamon are your friends, while dairy and greasy foods worsen Dampness.
Keep your lower back and feet warm - a belly warmer or moxibustion on the lower back can make a big difference. Gentle movement like walking or Tai Chi helps move Qi without draining your reserves, but avoid over exercising to exhaustion.

### Cold-Damp invading the Spleen
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/cold-damp-invading-the-spleen)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Watery stools, no odor or urgency, Cold dull abdominal sensation, Heavy limbs and head, as if wrapped, Thick white greasy tongue coating, Poor appetite, nausea, sticky taste
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Cold raw foods and iced drinks, Heavy, greasy meals, Cold damp weather, Prolonged sitting or lying in a damp place
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Abdominal warmth (warm compress or covering), Warm ginger tea, Dry, warm environment, Warm, cooked, easily digestible meals
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When external Cold and Dampness invade the body - often from exposure to cold, damp weather or from eating too many cold, raw foods - they lodge in the Spleen and Stomach. The Spleen's ability to transform and transport fluids is blocked by the cold, heavy nature of Dampness. This forces water downward into the intestines, producing watery, painless diarrhea, while the cold congeals and slows everything down, leaving you feeling heavy and chilled.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale, swollen body with teeth marks and a thick, white, greasy coating that looks excessively moist. Pulse: soggy (Ru) or slippery (Hua) and slow (Chi), especially soft and weak at the right Guan position (Spleen/Stomach).
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Cold and Dampness are the direct cause of this pattern; they congeal and weigh down the Spleen's function. Warming the abdomen and drinking warm fluids counteract the Cold, while dry, easily digested foods and a dry environment help the Spleen shed excess Dampness. Avoiding cold and damp inputs prevents the pattern from deepening, giving the Spleen a chance to restore its transforming and transporting power.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Keep the abdomen warm with a scarf or hot water bottle. Eat warm, cooked foods like rice congee with a little ginger and avoid all raw, cold, or greasy items until the Dampness clears. A dry, warm environment helps the Spleen recover its function.

### Spleen Yin Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/spleen-yin-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Chronic loose or thin stools, Dry mouth and throat in afternoon, Hunger without desire to eat, Thin body or unintended weight loss, Warm palms and soles, afternoon heat, Red thin tongue with cracks, little coating
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Spicy, greasy, or fried foods, Overwork and physical exhaustion, Excessive worry and mental strain
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warm, cooked, easily digestible meals, Rest and adequate sleep, Cool, calm environments
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  The Spleen needs a balance of nourishing Yin fluids to keep the digestive lining moist and to gently transport wastes. When Spleen Yin is depleted over a long illness, the intestines lose their lubrication and the Spleen's holding function weakens. This creates a paradoxical situation: the body is dry, yet loose stools persist because the Spleen can no longer properly form or hold bowel movements.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red, thin body with small transversal cracks on the sides (Spleen area), little or no coating, dry surface. Pulse: fine (thin) and possibly slightly rapid, weak at the right Guan (middle) position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Spleen Yin is like the body's digestive moisture. Spicy foods and overwork burn through this moisture, making the intestines dry and irritable, which worsens the loose stool pattern. Rest and moistening foods gently replenish these fluids, helping the Spleen regain its ability to form and hold normal bowel movements.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat small, frequent meals of moistening, easy-to-digest foods like congee made with Chinese yam and lotus seeds. Avoid spicy, fried, and overly dry foods that consume Yin fluids. Gentle rest and a regular sleep schedule help the body rebuild Yin over time.

## คำถามที่พบบ่อย (Frequently Asked Questions)
### Can TCM cure my functional diarrhea permanently?
Many patients achieve long-term remission when the underlying pattern is fully corrected. For Spleen Qi deficiency, this often means 2-4 months of herbs plus dietary changes to rebuild digestive strength. The goal is not just stopping the diarrhea but strengthening your system so it no longer produces loose stools. Some patients may need periodic maintenance treatment, especially if they return to poor dietary habits or face prolonged stress.

### What should I eat while undergoing TCM treatment for diarrhea?
Across all patterns, warm, cooked, easily digestible foods are essential. Think congee, soups, steamed vegetables, and well-cooked grains rather than raw salads, cold drinks, or greasy foods. Your practitioner will give you specific guidance based on your pattern - someone with Damp-Heat will need cooling foods, while someone with Spleen Yang deficiency needs warming foods. The general rule: if it's cold, raw, or straight from the fridge, it's probably not helping your digestion right now.

### How quickly will I see results from acupuncture and herbs?
Most patients notice firmer stools within 2-4 weeks of starting treatment. Excess patterns like Damp-Heat typically respond faster - sometimes within days. Deficiency patterns take longer because the body needs time to rebuild Qi and Yang. We recommend committing to at least 4-6 weeks of consistent treatment before evaluating results fully.

### Can stress really cause diarrhea?
Yes, and TCM has a clear explanation for this. The Liver is responsible for the smooth flow of Qi, and emotional stress directly constrains this flow. When Liver Qi stagnates, it often attacks the Spleen horizontally - this is the Liver-Spleen disharmony pattern. This is why your diarrhea may flare before important meetings, during arguments, or when you're under pressure. Treatment focuses on smoothing Liver Qi while strengthening the Spleen so it can withstand the attack.

### Is acupuncture alone enough to treat functional diarrhea?
Acupuncture can be very effective for regulating bowel function, especially for Liver-Spleen disharmony where stress is a major trigger. However, most functional diarrhea patterns involve some degree of Spleen Qi deficiency, which typically responds best to herbal medicine combined with dietary therapy. Your practitioner will recommend the right combination for your specific pattern. Some patients do well with acupuncture alone; others need herbs to rebuild digestive strength.

### Can I take anti-diarrheal medication while doing TCM treatment?
Many patients begin TCM treatment while still using loperamide or similar medications as needed. As your digestion improves with herbs and acupuncture, you will likely find you need the medication less often. Work with both your doctor and TCM practitioner on a plan to reduce medication gradually. Do not stop any prescribed medications abruptly without consulting your prescribing physician.
