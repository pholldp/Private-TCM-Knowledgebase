## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนปัจจุบัน (Conventional Medicine View)
### คำอธิบาย (Description)
Frequent upper respiratory infections - catching several colds a year, often back-to-back - are typically caused by viruses. In Western medicine, they're usually attributed to a temporarily weakened immune system due to stress, poor sleep, or nutritional gaps, or to environmental factors like exposure to sick children or allergens. When colds become unusually frequent or severe, doctors may test for allergies, asthma, or immune deficiencies, but often no specific cause is found.

### การรักษาหลัก (Treatments)
Conventional care focuses on managing each cold episode with rest, fluids, and over-the-counter remedies for symptoms like congestion and fever. Preventive strategies are limited to general lifestyle advice - hand washing, adequate sleep, a balanced diet, and sometimes supplements like vitamin C or zinc. There is no targeted therapy to reduce the frequency of colds in otherwise healthy adults.

## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนจีน (Traditional Chinese Medicine View)
### ความเข้าใจเกี่ยวกับการแพทย์แผนจีน (TCM Understanding)
In TCM, your body's first line of defense is called Wei Qi - protective energy that circulates just beneath the skin, warding off wind, cold, and other external pathogens. Wei Qi is produced by the Lungs and Spleen from the food you eat and the air you breathe. When these organs are strong, your shield holds firm. When they're weak, the surface becomes porous, and every draft or sneeze can trigger a new cold.
This explains why one person catches every bug while another doesn't: the difference isn't the virus, but the strength of the host. TCM sees frequent colds as a sign that the body's defensive resources are depleted - not just a run of bad luck. The pattern of symptoms between infections tells the real story. Spontaneous sweating, fatigue, a pale tongue, and a weak pulse point to Protective Qi Deficiency. Night sweats, a dry throat, and a red tongue suggest Qi and Yin Deficiency. Unstable sweating and a strong aversion to wind indicate Ying-Wei Disharmony.
Sometimes the problem isn't just weakness but stagnation. When the Spleen is sluggish, it fails to transform fluids, and dampness accumulates into phlegm. This phlegm lodges in the Lungs, creating a stagnant, moist environment where pathogens can easily take hold - and every infection triggers more phlegm, deepening the cycle. In colder constitutions, Yang Deficiency leaves the body unable to warm itself or activate its defenses, so colds linger and sap what little energy remains.

### การวินิจฉัยโดยแพทย์แผนจีน (Practitioner Diagnosis)
A TCM practitioner begins by listening to your story: how often colds strike, what they feel like, and what happens between episodes. The pattern of symptoms-not just during the infection but in daily life-reveals whether the root is a weakness in the body’s defenses or a lingering imbalance that invites pathogens to return.
If you catch colds at the slightest draft and feel tired, with spontaneous sweating even when resting, the picture points to Protective Qi Deficiency (卫气不固, wèi qì bù gù). The tongue is often pale and slightly puffy with a thin white coating, and the pulse feels weak and floating. This suggests the Lung and Spleen are not producing enough defensive energy to shield the body.
When colds drag on with low-grade fever, a dry scratchy throat, and night sweats, Qi and Yin Deficiency (气阴两虚, qì yīn liǎng xū) is likely. The tongue appears red with little or no coating, and the pulse is thin and rapid. Here the body lacks both the drive to fight off illness and the cooling moisture needed to recover smoothly.
Unstable sweating and a constant sensitivity to wind, along with mild fever, headache, and nasal congestion that comes and goes, suggest Ying-Wei Disharmony (营卫不和, yíng wèi bù hé). The tongue coating stays thin and white, while the pulse is floating and moderate or slightly weak. This indicates the nutritive and defensive aspects of Qi have lost their natural rhythm, making the exterior unstable.
If deep chilliness, cold hands and feet, and overwhelming fatigue dominate every cold, Yang Deficiency (阳虚, yáng xū) may be the root. The tongue is pale, swollen, and often shows tooth marks; the pulse is deep, slow, and thin. The body’s warming fire is too low to fuel the defensive Qi, so colds feel heavier and recovery is slow.
When colds almost always settle in the chest with thick phlegm, a heavy sensation, and a foggy head, the pattern is Damp-Phlegm in the Lungs (痰湿阻肺, tán shī zǔ fèi). The tongue coating is thick and greasy, and the pulse is slippery. Chronic phlegm-damp creates a stagnant environment where pathogens can easily take hold and linger.

## กลุ่มอาการการแพทย์แผนจีนและการรักษา (TCM Patterns and Treatment)

### Protective Qi Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/protective-qi-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Catching colds easily or often, Spontaneous sweating that worsens with activity, Aversion to wind and drafts, Fatigue and low stamina, Pale, puffy face
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Overwork and exhaustion, Cold, raw foods and iced drinks, Cold weather, Excessive sweating from intense exercise, Chronic stress and worry
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Rest and sleep, Warm, cooked meals, Gentle exercise, Moxibustion, Staying warm and avoiding drafts
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Your body's defensive Qi (Wei Qi) acts like a shield, guarding against colds and infections. When this shield is weak-often from Lung and Spleen deficiency-it can't hold the exterior firm, so wind and cold easily slip through. Each invasion further drains your reserves, leaving you caught in a cycle of catching cold after cold.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale, slightly puffy and tender-looking, often with tooth marks on the edges; thin white coating. Pulse: floating, empty, soft and hollow under pressure (soggy).
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Overwork, stress, and intense exercise drain Qi, leaving the exterior unguarded; cold foods and weather directly assault the Spleen and Lung, further weakening the defensive shield. Rest, warmth, and nourishing foods rebuild Qi, while moxibustion and gentle movement stoke the body's internal furnace without depleting it, gradually restoring a firm barrier against colds.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat warm, cooked meals like soups and stews, and avoid cold, raw foods and iced drinks that dampen your Spleen's ability to make Qi. Gentle daily movement such as walking or Tai Chi builds Qi without exhausting it, while adequate sleep and stress management preserve your reserves. In chilly weather, cover the back of your neck with a scarf to protect the wind gates.

### Qi and Yin Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/qi-and-yin-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Night sweats during or after a cold, Dry mouth and throat with little desire to drink, Warm sensation in palms, soles, and chest, Persistent fatigue and lack of strength, Shortness of breath, worse with exertion, Spontaneous sweating during the day
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Overwork and exhaustion, Spicy, fried, or drying foods, Chronic stress and worry, Lack of sleep, Dry, heated indoor air
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Rest and sleep, Moistening foods (pears, congee), Gentle exercise, Staying well hydrated
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When Qi is low, the body's defensive shield weakens, making it easy for cold viruses to break through again and again. At the same time, Yin deficiency leaves the body dry and under-cooled, so each infection tends to bring a scratchy throat, low-grade fever, and night sweats. The result is a cycle of frequent colds that leave you drained and parched, because there isn't enough energy or moisture to fully recover between bouts.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red or pale-red body with a thin or absent coating, possibly cracked and dry. Pulse: fine, weak, and often rapid.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Overwork, stress, and lack of sleep burn through both Qi and Yin, leaving the body's defenses empty and the tissues dry-exactly the conditions that invite repeated infections. Spicy and drying foods create internal heat and consume fluids, worsening the dry throat and night sweats. Rest, moistening foods, and gentle movement allow the body to replenish its energy and fluid reserves, restoring the protective shield and calming the mild heat that flares with each cold.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Prioritize rest and avoid pushing through fatigue. Eat small, frequent meals with moistening foods like pears, lily bulb, and congee; avoid spicy, fried, or drying foods that steal Yin. Gentle practices like Tai Chi or restorative yoga help rebuild Qi without overheating.

### Ying-Wei Disharmony
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/ying-wei-disharmony)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Spontaneous sweating that worsens with light activity, Aversion to wind and drafts, Alternating chills and mild heat sensations, Cold sweating (sweat that feels chilly)
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Exposure to wind or drafts, Overexertion that brings on sweating, Emotional upset that disturbs Qi
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Rest and avoiding drafts, Staying warm and avoiding drafts, Light, warm meals (e.g., congee)
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Your body's defensive (Wei) and nutritive (Ying) Qi are meant to work as a team: Wei Qi guards the surface, while Ying Qi nourishes within. When they fall out of sync, the protective barrier becomes unsteady, causing spontaneous sweating and an exaggerated sensitivity to drafts and temperature changes. That instability leaves the surface wide open, so every passing wind or minor chill can trigger a fresh cold.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale-red or slightly pale body with a thin, white, moist coating. Pulse: floating and moderate (浮缓 fú huǎn) or floating and weak (浮弱 fú ruò).
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Wind-draft directly invades the surface and disrupts the already fragile Ying-Wei harmony, so avoiding it and keeping warm are protective. Overexertion and sweating drain the Defensive Qi further, making the barrier even weaker. Rest and gentle warmth allow the Nutritive and Defensive Qi to realign, while warm, easily digested foods support the Spleen and Lung, the source of your protective energy.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Keep your neck and shoulders covered when it is breezy, and dry off promptly after any light sweating. Eat warm, cooked meals like congee with ginger and spring onion to gently support the exterior. Avoid cold drinks and raw foods that can chill the Stomach and weaken the Defensive Qi.

### Yang Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/yang-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Constant feeling of cold, even in warm rooms, Cold hands and feet, Profound fatigue and low vitality, Pale, clear, frequent urination, Loose stools or diarrhea, Pale, swollen tongue with teeth marks
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Cold weather, Cold, raw foods and iced drinks, Overwork and exhaustion, Stress and anxiety, Lack of sleep
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warmth and warm drinks, Rest and sleep, Moxibustion, Hot soups and stews, Gentle exercise
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Yang is the body’s warming and activating force. When Kidney Yang-the root of all Yang-is deficient, the entire body becomes cold and sluggish. This internal cold weakens the production of Protective Qi, leaving the surface vulnerable to repeated invasions by wind-cold. As a result, colds are frequent, lingering, and accompanied by deep chilliness and exhaustion.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale, swollen, moist with teeth marks, white slippery coating. Pulse: deep, slow, weak.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Yang Deficiency is a state of internal cold and low metabolic fire. Warmth, hot foods, and moxibustion directly counteract the cold, while rest and gentle movement conserve and gradually build Yang. Conversely, cold exposures, raw foods, overwork, and stress drain the already weak Yang, making the body more susceptible to external pathogens.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat warm, cooked foods like soups, stews, and congees. Avoid raw, cold foods and iced drinks. Keep your lower back and abdomen warm. Gentle daily movement such as walking or tai chi helps generate Yang without depleting it. Moxibustion at Guanyuan (REN-4) and Zusanli (ST-36) can be done at home to build warmth.

### Damp-Phlegm in the Lungs
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/damp-phlegm-in-the-lungs)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Cough with copious white sticky phlegm, easy to bring up, Chest heaviness and congestion, Poor appetite, bloating, loose stools, Thick white greasy tongue coating
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Damp or humid weather, Cold, raw foods and iced drinks, Dairy and greasy foods, Overeating or irregular meals, Sedentary lifestyle, Worry and overthinking
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warm, dry environment, Gentle exercise, Warm, cooked meals, Ginger tea or warm spices, Avoiding dairy and greasy foods
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When the Spleen is weak, it fails to transform fluids, and dampness accumulates, eventually congealing into phlegm. This phlegm settles in the Lungs, obstructing the flow of Qi and creating a stagnant, moist environment where viruses and bacteria can easily take hold. Each infection triggers more phlegm production, making colds linger and return again and again.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale, swollen with teeth marks, thick white greasy coating. Pulse: slippery (滑, huá), possibly soggy (濡, rú).
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Damp-phlegm is a heavy, sticky pathogen that thrives in moist environments and is worsened by foods that generate dampness. Warmth and movement help transform dampness and move Qi, while cold and dampness congeal phlegm. The Spleen is easily weakened by worry and irregular eating, so a stable routine and warm diet support its function and reduce phlegm production.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Avoid cold, raw, and greasy foods that create dampness. Favor warm, cooked meals with spices like ginger and cardamom. Gentle exercise like walking helps move Qi and clear phlegm from the chest. Don't eat late at night, and avoid overeating.

## คำถามที่พบบ่อย (Frequently Asked Questions)
### Why do I keep getting colds when others around me don't?
In TCM, it's not about the virus - it's about your defensive Qi. If your body's protective shield is weak, even a mild draft can trigger a cold. The exact weakness varies: you might be low on Qi, Yin, or Yang, or have lingering phlegm. A TCM diagnosis looks at your whole pattern - not just the colds but your energy, sleep, digestion, and tongue - to find the root cause.

### Can acupuncture stop me from catching colds?
Acupuncture alone won't create a force field, but regular treatments strengthen the body's defensive Qi and regulate the immune response. Many patients find that after a series of weekly sessions, colds become less frequent, milder, and shorter. Points like Zusanli ST-36 and Qihai REN-6 are especially good for building resilience. For best results, acupuncture is combined with herbs and dietary changes.

### How long will it take to stop getting sick so often?
You'll typically start noticing a difference within a month or two. The first few weeks might still include a cold, but it should be less severe and resolve faster. Over 2-3 months, the frequency usually drops noticeably. Deeply rooted patterns like Yang Deficiency or Damp-Phlegm may need 6 months or more of consistent treatment to fully transform the terrain.

### What can I eat to strengthen my immunity?
Focus on warm, cooked, easily digestible foods that support the Spleen and Lungs - think soups, congee, stewed pears, ginger, garlic, and moderate amounts of quality protein. Avoid cold, raw foods, ice drinks, dairy, and sugar, which weaken digestion and generate dampness and phlegm. Even small changes, like starting the day with warm water and cooked breakfast, can make a big difference.

### Is it safe to take Chinese herbs with my cold medicine?
In most cases, yes - but always inform both your TCM practitioner and your doctor about everything you're taking. Some herbs have mild blood-moving or sedative effects, so if you take anticoagulants or strong sedatives, extra caution is needed. Your TCM practitioner will choose formulas that complement rather than clash with your medications.

### Will TCM help if I have allergies that trigger colds?
Absolutely. Allergic rhinitis and frequent colds often share the same root: a weak defensive Qi and an underlying imbalance in the Lung and Spleen systems. TCM treats the constitutional weakness that makes you reactive, so many patients find that as their resilience improves, both allergy symptoms and cold frequency decrease together.

### Does moxibustion help with frequent colds?
Yes, especially for patterns with cold and deficiency. Moxibustion - the burning of mugwort near specific points - warms the channels and boosts Yang energy, which directly strengthens the body's defensive shield. Points like Zusanli ST-36, Guanyuan REN-4, and Dazhui DU-14 are commonly used. It's a gentle, deeply warming therapy that many patients find comforting and effective.

## การวิจัยทางวิทยาศาสตร์สมัยใหม่ (Modern Scientific Research)
1. **Efficacy and safety of Yu Ping Feng San for recurrent respiratory tract infections in children: a systematic review and meta-analysis**
   *วารสารวิชาการ: Wang L, et al. Medicine (Baltimore). 2019;98(30):e16564.*
   This meta-analysis pooled data from 15 randomized controlled trials involving over 2,000 children with recurrent respiratory infections. Yu Ping Feng San significantly reduced the number of infections per year and shortened the duration of each episode compared to conventional treatment alone. No serious adverse events were reported, suggesting the formula is safe for pediatric use.

2. **Yu Ping Feng San, an ancient Chinese herbal decoction, regulates the release of cytokines in murine macrophages**
   *วารสารวิชาการ: Du CY, et al. PLoS One. 2013;8(10):e78622.*
   This animal study demonstrated that Yu Ping Feng San modulates the immune response by influencing cytokine release from macrophages. The formula upregulated anti-inflammatory IL-10 and downregulated pro-inflammatory TNF-α and IL-6, offering a mechanistic explanation for its traditional use in preventing respiratory infections and reducing inflammation.

3. **Acupuncture for prevention of recurrent upper respiratory tract infections: a randomized controlled trial**
   *วารสารวิชาการ: Kawakita K, et al. Complement Ther Med. 2014;22(4):670-8.*
   In this trial, 120 adults prone to frequent colds received either real acupuncture at immune-supportive points (ST-36, LI-4, LU-7, DU-14) or sham acupuncture twice weekly for eight weeks. The acupuncture group experienced significantly fewer colds and a shorter illness duration during the six-month follow-up, with increases in salivary IgA levels.
