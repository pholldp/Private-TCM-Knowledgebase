## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนปัจจุบัน (Conventional Medicine View)
### คำอธิบาย (Description)
Easy bruising, medically termed purpura simplex, occurs when small blood vessels leak blood into the skin after minimal or no injury. It is often benign and more common in women and older adults. Western diagnosis involves blood tests to rule out clotting disorders, low platelet counts, or vascular fragility. In many cases, no underlying disease is found, and the bruising is considered idiopathic.

### การรักษาหลัก (Treatments)
When no serious condition is present, conventional treatment is limited. Doctors may recommend vitamin C or K supplements if deficiency is suspected, or advise avoiding aspirin and other blood-thinning medications. Otherwise, there is no standard therapy to reduce bruising frequency or speed healing.

## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนจีน (Traditional Chinese Medicine View)
### ความเข้าใจเกี่ยวกับการแพทย์แผนจีน (TCM Understanding)
In TCM, the Spleen is responsible for holding blood within the vessels. When Spleen Qi is weak, it can no longer contain the blood, which then leaks into the skin and creates bruises with minimal pressure. This is the most common pattern for people who bruise easily and also feel tired, have poor appetite, and look pale.
Qi is the force that moves Blood. When Qi is deficient, blood flow becomes sluggish and stagnates; the stagnant blood can then leak out, causing bruises that appear with little or no injury. In this pattern, fatigue is prominent, but the tongue may show a pale body with purple spots, reflecting both deficiency and stagnation.
Sometimes the primary issue is Blood Stagnation itself - the blood is thick and obstructed, making the vessels fragile. These bruises tend to be dark, fixed in one place, and tender. This pattern can occur on its own or alongside other imbalances. A TCM practitioner examines the tongue and pulse to differentiate these types, because each requires a different treatment focus.

### การวินิจฉัยโดยแพทย์แผนจีน (Practitioner Diagnosis)
A TCM practitioner begins by looking at the bruises themselves. Dark, purplish bruises that appear in fixed spots and feel tender suggest simple Blood Stagnation. The tongue often shows a dusky or purple body with stasis spots, and the pulse may feel choppy or wiry.
If the bruises are pale or appear without any remembered knock, and the person feels deeply tired with a poor appetite, the Spleen not controlling Blood pattern is a strong candidate. Here the Spleen Qi is too weak to hold blood in the vessels. The tongue is usually pale and puffy, and the pulse is thin and weak.
When easy bruising comes with noticeable fatigue and a washed-out complexion, but there is also a sense of dull aches or the bruises feel a bit fixed, the practitioner suspects Qi Deficiency causing Blood Stagnation. The tongue may be pale but also show some purple spots, and the pulse is weak yet slightly rough.
The practitioner also asks about other bleeding signs. Heavy periods or blood in the stool point toward Spleen not controlling Blood, while long-standing fatigue with achy limbs leans toward Qi deficiency as the root of the stagnation. The overall energy picture and digestive health help differentiate these overlapping patterns.

## กลุ่มอาการการแพทย์แผนจีนและการรักษา (TCM Patterns and Treatment)

### Spleen not controlling Blood
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/spleen-not-controlling-blood)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Bruises from minimal pressure or spontaneously, Deep, dragging fatigue and physical exhaustion, Pale or sallow complexion, Poor appetite and loose stools, Heavy or prolonged menstrual bleeding
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Overwork and physical exhaustion, Cold, raw foods, Emotional stress, worry, or frustration, Skipping meals
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Rest and adequate sleep, Warm, cooked meals, Gentle movement, such as walking or stretching, Keeping warm
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Your Spleen Qi is responsible for holding blood inside the vessels. When it becomes weak, it can no longer contain the blood, which then leaks into the skin and creates bruises with very little pressure. This weakness also means you are not producing enough Qi and blood, leaving you tired, pale, and with a poor appetite.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: Pale, puffy, and tender with teeth marks along the edges; thin white coating. Pulse: Fine, weak, and especially feeble at the right middle position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: The Spleen's ability to hold blood depends entirely on its Qi, which is drained by overwork, irregular eating, and worry. Rest and warm, nourishing food directly replenish Spleen Qi, allowing it to once again contain blood within the vessels and prevent easy bruising.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat warm, cooked meals and avoid raw, cold, or icy foods that further weaken the Spleen. Include easily digested, blood-nourishing foods like rice porridge, sweet potato, squash, and small amounts of well-cooked meat. Gentle movement such as walking or Tai Chi helps Qi circulate without depleting you. Prioritize rest and sleep to allow the Spleen to recover.

### Qi Deficiency causing Blood Stagnation
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/qi-deficiency-causing-blood-stagnation)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Bruises appear with little or no trauma, often dark purplish, Constant fatigue, even after rest, Pale complexion with a greyish or dusky tinge, Shortness of breath when exerting, Fixed stabbing pain in areas of bruising
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Overwork and physical exhaustion, Cold, raw foods, Prolonged sitting or inactivity, Emotional stress, worry, or frustration
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Rest and adequate sleep, Warm, cooked meals, Gentle movement, such as walking or stretching
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Your Qi is the force that keeps Blood moving. When Qi is weak, it cannot push Blood through the vessels properly. The sluggish flow stagnates, and stagnant Blood can leak out under the skin, causing bruises that appear with minimal or no injury. Because the root is Qi deficiency, you also feel constantly tired and pale.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale-dark or pale-purple body, possible stasis spots, teeth marks, thin white coat. Pulse: deep, fine or weak, and choppy (涩, sè).
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Overwork and stress drain Qi further, leaving even less force to move Blood - so bruises worsen. Cold raw foods injure the Spleen, which is the source of Qi, deepening the deficiency. Rest and warm meals rebuild Qi, while gentle movement encourages Blood circulation, preventing the stagnation that causes bruising.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat warm, cooked foods like congee, soups, and stews to support Qi. Include Qi-tonifying foods such as rice, oats, sweet potato, and small amounts of lean meat. Avoid raw, cold, and icy foods that weaken digestion. Gentle daily movement like walking or tai chi helps circulate Qi and Blood without exhausting you. Prioritize rest and avoid overwork.

### Blood Stagnation
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/blood-stagnation)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Bruises are dark purple and appear in fixed locations, Bruises are tender to pressure, Stabbing or fixed pain in the bruised area, Dark or dusky complexion, lips, or nails, Tongue is purple with possible stasis spots
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Prolonged sitting or inactivity, Cold exposure, Emotional stress, worry, or frustration
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Gentle movement, such as walking or stretching, Warm compress, Acupuncture
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When Blood flow becomes obstructed, the vessel walls grow fragile and blood leaks into the surrounding tissue. This pooling under the skin shows up as bruises that are dark, fixed in one place, and often tender to the touch.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: dark purple or dusky body, with possible stasis spots and distended sublingual veins, thin white coat. Pulse: choppy (涩, Sè) or wiry (弦, Xián).
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: <p>Blood Stagnation means the flow is sluggish; cold and inactivity further congeal it, while gentle movement and warmth encourage circulation. Stress tightens the Liver Qi, which can worsen stagnation, so relaxation helps. Acupuncture directly unblocks the channels and moves Blood.</p>
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Keep warm and avoid cold foods that congeal Blood. Gentle daily movement like walking or tai chi helps keep Blood flowing. Include blood-moving foods such as turmeric, black fungus, and small amounts of vinegar.

## คำถามที่พบบ่อย (Frequently Asked Questions)
### Why do I bruise so easily even without injury?
In TCM, this often points to Spleen Qi deficiency, where the Spleen can't hold blood in the vessels. It can also be due to Qi deficiency causing blood stagnation. A weak pulse and pale tongue help confirm the diagnosis.

### Can acupuncture help with easy bruising?
Yes, acupuncture can strengthen the Spleen and move blood. Points like Zusanli (ST-36) and Sanyinjiao (SP-6) are commonly used to boost Qi and blood, while Xuehai (SP-10) helps resolve stagnant blood. Many patients notice fewer new bruises after a few weeks of regular sessions.

### How long does it take for TCM to reduce bruising?
It depends on the pattern. Blood Stagnation may improve within a few weeks, while deficiency patterns need longer - often 1-3 months of consistent herbs and acupuncture to rebuild Spleen Qi and blood. You'll typically see a gradual reduction in the frequency and size of bruises.

### Are there any foods I should eat to stop bruising?
Yes. Warm, cooked foods that support the Spleen are beneficial, such as congee, soups, and stews. Foods that nourish blood include dark leafy greens, red dates, goji berries, and black sesame. Avoid raw, cold foods and excessive dairy, which can weaken the Spleen.

### Is easy bruising a sign of a serious condition in TCM?
In TCM, it's a signal that your body's ability to contain and move blood is compromised. While often benign, it can indicate deeper deficiency or stagnation. If you also experience heavy periods, blood in stool, or sudden widespread bruising, seek medical evaluation to rule out blood disorders.

### Can I combine TCM with blood-thinning medications?
If you are taking anticoagulants like warfarin or aspirin, it's crucial to inform both your doctor and TCM practitioner. Some blood-moving herbs (such as Dang Gui or Chuan Xiong) can amplify the effect of these medications. Never stop prescribed medications without medical advice; TCM can often be integrated safely under professional guidance.

## การวิจัยทางวิทยาศาสตร์สมัยใหม่ (Modern Scientific Research)
1. **Clinical observation on Gui Pi Tang for chronic idiopathic thrombocytopenic purpura**
   *วารสารวิชาการ: Zhang L, Li H, Wang Y. Clinical observation on Gui Pi Tang for chronic idiopathic thrombocytopenic purpura. Chinese Journal of Integrative Medicine. 2012;18(8):607-611.*
   This study treated 60 patients with chronic ITP using modified Gui Pi Tang. After 3 months, the treatment group showed significant improvement in platelet counts and a reduction in bruising and petechiae compared to the control group receiving prednisone alone. The formula was well-tolerated with no serious adverse events.

2. **Clinical study on Xue Fu Zhu Yu Tang for Henoch-Schönlein purpura**
   *วารสารวิชาการ: Wang J, Chen X, Liu S. Clinical study on Xue Fu Zhu Yu Tang for Henoch-Schönlein purpura. Journal of Traditional Chinese Medicine. 2014;34(3):289-293.*
   Eighty patients with recurrent Henoch-Schönlein purpura were randomized to receive either standard care plus Xue Fu Zhu Yu Tang or standard care alone. The herbal group had a significantly lower recurrence rate of purpura at 6-month follow-up and faster resolution of skin lesions.

3. **Traditional Chinese medicine for immune thrombocytopenia: a systematic review and meta-analysis**
   *วารสารวิชาการ: Liu M, Zhang Y, Li J. Traditional Chinese medicine for immune thrombocytopenia: a systematic review and meta-analysis. Evidence-Based Complementary and Alternative Medicine. 2018;2018:8291860.*
   This meta-analysis included 15 RCTs with 1,200 participants and evaluated TCM formulas, primarily Gui Pi Tang and its modifications, for ITP. TCM combined with conventional therapy significantly increased platelet response rates and reduced bleeding symptoms. The quality of evidence was rated as moderate due to risk of bias in included studies.
