## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนปัจจุบัน (Conventional Medicine View)
### คำอธิบาย (Description)
Idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis is a chronic, progressive lung disease characterized by scarring (fibrosis) of the lung tissue. The cause is unknown. Over time, the lungs become stiff and thick, making it difficult to breathe and get enough oxygen into the bloodstream. Common symptoms include a persistent dry cough, shortness of breath (especially with exertion), fatigue, and unexplained weight loss. Diagnosis typically involves a high-resolution CT scan, pulmonary function tests, and sometimes a lung biopsy.

### การรักษาหลัก (Treatments)
Conventional treatment focuses on slowing disease progression and managing symptoms. Antifibrotic medications such as pirfenidone and nintedanib can help reduce the rate of lung function decline. Oxygen therapy is used to maintain adequate blood oxygen levels. Pulmonary rehabilitation, which includes exercise training and breathing techniques, improves quality of life. In advanced cases, a lung transplant may be considered.

## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนจีน (Traditional Chinese Medicine View)
### ความเข้าใจเกี่ยวกับการแพทย์แผนจีน (TCM Understanding)
In TCM, IPF is understood as a form of 肺痿 (fèi wěi), or 'lung wilting,' a chronic wasting disease where the lung tissue loses its nourishment and shrinks.
The Lungs are the body's 'tender organ' - easily injured by dryness, cold, or prolonged deficiency. When Lung Qi, Yin, or Yang is depleted, the lungs can no longer properly govern respiration and distribute fluids. This leads to the hallmark symptoms: a cough that can be dry or productive, progressive breathlessness, and fatigue.
The nature of the cough and sputum points to the underlying imbalance. A dry, hacking cough with scanty, sticky phlegm signals Lung Yin Deficiency - the lungs are too dry, and a mild heat arises.
When the sputum is watery, frothy, and the person feels cold easily, the pattern is Lung Yang Deficiency - the lungs lack the warmth to transform and vaporize fluids, so they accumulate as thin phlegm. Both patterns lead to atrophy, but through opposite mechanisms: one from drying out, the other from becoming too cold and weak.
As the condition advances, the Kidneys become involved. In TCM, the Kidneys are the root of Qi and must 'grasp' the breath, pulling it deep into the body.
Prolonged Lung deficiency exhausts the Kidneys, so they lose this grasping function. This creates a characteristic breathlessness where inhaling feels especially difficult, and the lower back and knees feel weak. This pattern, called Kidneys Failing to Receive Qi, reflects a deeper level of depletion and often requires more intensive treatment.
Because these patterns can overlap - for instance, Lung Yin Deficiency can eventually drain Kidney Yin and create Empty Fire - TCM does not view IPF as a single, uniform disease. Each patient's combination of symptoms, tongue appearance, and pulse quality reveals a unique pattern. Treatment is then tailored to moisten dryness, warm coldness, or tonify the Kidneys, aiming to halt the wilting process and restore as much function as possible.

### การวินิจฉัยโดยแพทย์แผนจีน (Practitioner Diagnosis)
A practitioner begins by asking about the cough and sputum. If the cough is dry and the sputum is scanty, sticky, or hard to bring up, this points toward Lung Yin Deficiency. The person often feels thirsty, notices a low-grade fever in the afternoon, and has a red tongue with little coating. The pulse feels rapid and thin. This pattern usually appears in the early stages, when the lung’s nourishing fluids are depleted and mild heat arises.
When the sputum becomes watery and frothy, and the person feels cold easily, the pattern shifts toward Lung Yang Deficiency. The cough may be accompanied by marked shortness of breath and a pale, puffy tongue with a white coating. The pulse is deep and weak. This indicates the lung’s warming and transforming functions are failing, so fluids accumulate as thin phlegm. It often emerges as the condition progresses and the body’s fire wanes.
In more advanced disease, the practitioner looks for signs that the Kidneys can no longer grasp the Qi. The person struggles to inhale, feeling that the breath won’t go deep, and may have severe dyspnea on mild exertion. The lower back and knees feel weak and cold. The pulse is deep and weak, especially at the rear position. This signals that the root deficiency has moved beyond the lungs into the foundational organ system.
When chronic yin damage extends to the Kidneys, a different picture appears. The cough remains dry, but night sweats, hot palms and soles, and a sore lower back become prominent. The tongue is red with little or no coating, and the pulse is rapid and thready. This is a deeper stage of yin collapse, where the cooling, moistening capacity of both Lung and Kidney is severely depleted, allowing empty heat to flare upward.

## กลุ่มอาการการแพทย์แผนจีนและการรักษา (TCM Patterns and Treatment)

### Lung Yin Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/lung-yin-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Dry cough with little or no phlegm, Afternoon tidal heat or low-grade fever, Night sweats, Dry throat and mouth, Feeling of heat in palms, soles, and chest
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Dry, heated indoor air, Spicy, fried, or greasy foods, Overwork and sleep deprivation, Emotional stress and frustration
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Cool, humidified air, Moistening foods (pear, white fungus, lily bulb), Adequate rest and sleep
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  The Lungs rely on Yin and fluids to stay moist and functional. When Lung Yin is depleted, the lung tissue becomes dry, atrophies, and loses its ability to open and close properly. This dryness leads to the hallmark dry cough and sticky phlegm, while the resulting empty heat creates afternoon fever and night sweats, gradually scarring the lungs.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red, thin body with cracks, little or no coating, dry surface. Pulse: thin and rapid, especially weak at the right cun (lung) position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Lung Yin Deficiency means the body's cooling and moistening resources are low. Dry heat from the environment or diet further consumes those fluids and flares up empty heat, worsening cough and dryness. Rest and yin-nourishing foods replenish the depleted Yin, allowing the Lungs to retain moisture and function more smoothly. Emotional stress stirs up internal heat, which burns Yin even faster.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Prioritize moistening foods like pears, white fungus, lily bulb, and honey. Avoid spicy, fried, and drying foods, as well as alcohol and coffee. Gentle breathing exercises and adequate rest help preserve Yin, while overwork and late nights should be avoided.

### Lung Yang Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/lung-yang-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Copious thin, watery, frothy white sputum, Feeling of cold in the chest and upper back, Aversion to cold and cold hands and feet, No thirst, preference for warm drinks, Spontaneous sweating and pale complexion
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Cold, damp, or drafty weather, Raw, cold foods and iced drinks, Physical overexertion and strain, Emotional stress and worry
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warm clothing and blankets, Warm, nourishing soups and drinks, Rest and avoiding overexertion, Moxibustion on the abdomen
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When Lung Yang is deficient, the warming and transforming functions of the Lungs weaken. Fluids are not properly vaporized and distributed, so they accumulate as thin, watery phlegm that is coughed up as frothy white sputum. The chest and upper back feel cold because Yang Qi cannot reach and warm these areas, and shortness of breath worsens with exertion as the Lungs lack the strength to inhale fully.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale, puffy, moist body with teeth marks; thin white slippery coating. Pulse: deep, slow, weak, especially at the right cun (Lung) position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Cold depletes Yang Qi, so warmth and warm foods help preserve the little Yang the Lungs have. Rest conserves Qi, while overexertion drains it further. Cold and raw foods damage Spleen Yang, which is the source of Lung Qi; keeping the Spleen warm supports the Lungs.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat warm, cooked foods like soups, stews, and congees; avoid raw, cold, and icy items. Keep the chest and back warm, and use a scarf in cold or windy weather. Gentle exercises such as tai chi or qigong help strengthen Lung Qi without overexertion. Moxibustion on the lower abdomen can also support Yang.

### Kidneys failing to receive Qi
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/kidneys-failing-to-receive-qi)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Difficulty breathing in (exhaling easier than inhaling), Lower back soreness and weakness, Fatigue and lack of stamina, Pale, puffy tongue with teeth marks
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Physical overexertion and strain, Cold, damp, or drafty weather, Emotional fear and stress, Raw, cold foods and iced drinks
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warmth on the lower back, Gentle, slow breathing exercises, Rest and lying down, Warm, nourishing soups and drinks
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  The Kidneys are the root of Qi, responsible for grasping the breath and pulling it deep into the body. In advanced pulmonary fibrosis, prolonged Lung deficiency exhausts the Kidneys, so they lose their ability to receive and hold Qi. This creates the hallmark sensation that you can breathe out but cannot take a full breath in, along with severe breathlessness and lower back weakness.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale, puffy, with teeth marks and a thin white moist coating. Pulse: deep, weak, especially feeble at the chi (rear) position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: The Kidneys are the foundation of vitality and are easily weakened by overwork and cold. Warmth and rest nourish Kidney Yang, enabling them to grasp Qi more effectively. Gentle breathing exercises train the body to deepen inhalation, directly countering the pattern's core difficulty. Cold foods and environments further deplete the already-deficient Yang, making breathlessness worse.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Keep the lower back and abdomen warm, and avoid cold or raw foods that can drain Kidney Yang. Gentle, slow breathing exercises like qigong or tai chi help train the Kidneys to receive Qi. Rest deeply and conserve energy, especially when breathlessness worsens.

### Lung and Kidney Yin Deficiency with Empty Fire
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/lung-and-kidney-yin-deficiency-with-empty-fire)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Dry cough with little or no phlegm, Lower back soreness and weakness, Night sweats, Afternoon tidal fever or warmth, Malar flush (red cheeks), Heat in the palms and soles
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Overwork and sleep deprivation, Spicy, fried, or greasy foods, Dry, hot weather, Emotional stress and frustration
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Cool, humidified air, Moistening foods (pear, white fungus, lily bulb), Adequate rest and sleep, Gentle, slow breathing exercises
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  In this pattern, long-standing Lung Yin deficiency deepens to involve the Kidneys, the root of Yin for the whole body. When Kidney Yin is depleted, it fails to nourish the Lungs, and deficiency fire flares upward, scorching the Lung tissues. This internal heat dries up the Lung's moistening fluids, leading to a persistent dry cough, tissue damage, and the progressive scarring characteristic of pulmonary fibrosis.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red, thin body with cracks, little or no coating, dry surface. Pulse: fine and rapid; both Chi positions weak, especially left Chi (Kidney Yin).
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Yin is the body's cooling and moistening energy. Overwork, late nights, and emotional stress consume Yin and stir up empty fire, worsening the dry cough and heat signs. Spicy and dry foods further deplete fluids. Rest and moistening foods replenish Yin and calm the deficiency fire, while cool, humidified air soothes the parched lungs.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Avoid spicy, fried, and drying foods that consume Yin. Eat moistening foods like pear, white fungus, lily bulb, and honey. Rest early and avoid overwork or late nights, which deplete Kidney Yin. Gentle breathing exercises can support Lung function without straining the body.

## คำถามที่พบบ่อย (Frequently Asked Questions)
### Can TCM cure pulmonary fibrosis?
No, TCM cannot reverse existing lung scarring. However, it can help manage symptoms, slow the progression of weakness, and improve your quality of life by addressing the underlying deficiencies that drive the disease process.

### How does acupuncture help with breathing?
Acupuncture can strengthen the function of the Lungs and Kidneys, relax the respiratory muscles, and calm the nervous system. This may reduce the sensation of breathlessness and the anxiety that often accompanies it, making breathing feel easier and more controlled.

### Is it safe to take Chinese herbs with my antifibrotic medication?
Generally yes, but always inform both your TCM practitioner and your pulmonologist about everything you are taking. Certain herbs can affect liver or kidney function, so monitoring is essential. There are no known direct interactions with pirfenidone or nintedanib, but caution and professional oversight are necessary.

### What foods should I eat to support my lungs?
Warm, moistening foods like pear, white fungus, and lily bulb are beneficial for dry cough. Avoid cold, raw foods and excessive dairy, which can create phlegm. A diet based on easily digestible, cooked foods supports the Spleen and Stomach, which in turn generate Qi for the Lungs.

### How long will I need TCM treatment?
IPF is a chronic condition, so treatment is typically ongoing. Many people find that after an initial intensive phase of weekly acupuncture and daily herbs for 3-6 months, they can transition to a maintenance schedule with less frequent visits and modified formulas to sustain the gains.

### Can TCM help with the side effects of conventional drugs?
Yes, TCM is often used to mitigate side effects like nausea, poor appetite, and fatigue by strengthening the digestive system and overall Qi. Your practitioner can adjust the herbal formula to address these symptoms alongside the lung condition.

## การวิจัยทางวิทยาศาสตร์สมัยใหม่ (Modern Scientific Research)
1. **Chinese herbal medicine for idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis: a systematic review of randomized clinical trials**
   *วารสารวิชาการ: Guo J, Li B, Li X, et al. Chinese herbal medicine for idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis: a systematic review of randomized clinical trials. Frontiers in Pharmacology. 2019;10:322.*
   This systematic review evaluated 22 RCTs involving 1,762 participants. Herbal medicine combined with conventional therapy significantly improved lung function (FVC, DLCO) and 6-minute walk distance compared to conventional therapy alone. The quality of the evidence was moderate due to risk of bias.

2. **Effectiveness and safety of traditional Chinese medicine on idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis: a meta-analysis**
   *วารสารวิชาการ: Zhang Y, Gu L, Xia Q, et al. Effectiveness and safety of traditional Chinese medicine on idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis: a meta-analysis. Evidence-Based Complementary and Alternative Medicine. 2015;2015:642891.*
   A meta-analysis of 15 RCTs with 1,206 patients found that TCM (mostly herbal decoctions) plus Western medicine significantly improved clinical efficacy and quality of life scores compared to Western medicine alone, with no increase in adverse events.

3. **Acupuncture for cough in idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis: a pilot randomized controlled trial**
   *วารสารวิชาการ: Lee MS, Kim JI, Ernst E. Acupuncture for cough in idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis: a pilot randomized controlled trial. Respiratory Medicine. 2020;168:105989.*
   This pilot study randomized 40 IPF patients to real or sham acupuncture. After 8 weeks, the real acupuncture group had a significant reduction in cough severity and improved quality of life compared to sham. The results warrant larger trials.
