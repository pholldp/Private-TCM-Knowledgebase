## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนปัจจุบัน (Conventional Medicine View)
### คำอธิบาย (Description)
Swollen gums, medically referred to as gingival swelling, occur when the gum tissue becomes inflamed, puffy, and often tender or red. The most common cause is plaque buildup along the gumline, leading to gingivitis - the earliest stage of gum disease. If left untreated, it can progress to periodontitis, which involves deeper infection and can cause gum recession and tooth loss. Other triggers include hormonal changes (pregnancy, puberty), certain medications, nutritional deficiencies (especially vitamin C), and systemic conditions like diabetes.
Diagnosis is usually made through a visual dental exam, sometimes with X-rays to check for bone loss. Symptoms range from mild puffiness and bleeding when brushing to severe pain, redness, and swelling that makes eating uncomfortable. Conventional treatment focuses on removing plaque and tartar, controlling infection, and improving oral hygiene habits.

### การรักษาหลัก (Treatments)
Conventional care for swollen gums typically begins with a professional dental cleaning to remove plaque and tartar. Patients are advised to improve brushing and flossing techniques and may be prescribed an antiseptic mouthwash. If an infection is present, antibiotics may be given. For more advanced gum disease, scaling and root planing (deep cleaning) or even surgical procedures may be necessary. Addressing underlying factors, such as quitting smoking or managing diabetes, is also recommended.

## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนจีน (Traditional Chinese Medicine View)
### ความเข้าใจเกี่ยวกับการแพทย์แผนจีน (TCM Understanding)
In TCM, the gums are directly connected to several major organ channels. The Stomach channel runs through the upper and lower gums, so when excessive heat builds up in the Stomach - often from spicy food, alcohol, or emotional stress - it blazes upward along this pathway, causing intense redness, swelling, and throbbing pain. This is the classic 'Stomach Fire' pattern, and it explains why a fiery gum flare-up often comes with bad breath, thirst, and a craving for cold drinks.
The Kidneys also play a central role because they govern the bones, and teeth are considered the surplus of the bones. When Kidney Yin is depleted, the body's cooling and anchoring force weakens, allowing Empty-Heat to rise and irritate the gums. This produces a dull, chronic swelling that worsens at night, often with loose teeth and a dry mouth. Unlike the angry red of Stomach Fire, the gums here are a darker, duller red.
Other patterns show how different triggers lead to the same symptom. A sudden, severe swelling with fever and sore throat points to an external Wind-Heat invasion. Emotional stress and frustration can cause Liver Qi to stagnate and transform into Fire, which then flares up to the gums - this comes with a bitter taste, red eyes, and irritability. And when Qi and Blood are deficient, the gums lose their nourishment, becoming pale, soft, swollen, and prone to bleeding without much redness. This is why TCM doesn't treat all swollen gums the same way - the root cause must be identified.

### การวินิจฉัยโดยแพทย์แผนจีน (Practitioner Diagnosis)
A practitioner starts by asking what the swelling feels like and when it began. If the gums are intensely red, swollen, and throbbing with a burning pain that worsens with hot foods, Stomach Fire is the first suspect. You may also notice thirst, bad breath, and constipation. The tongue is red with a thick yellow coating, and the pulse feels rapid and forceful, confirming blazing heat rising along the Stomach channel.
If the swelling erupted suddenly after exposure to wind or a change in weather, and the pain sharpens with warmth, a Wind‑Heat invasion is likely. You might also have a sore throat, mild fever, or a floating sensation. Here the tongue is red with a thin yellow coat, and the pulse is floating and rapid. The practitioner traces the external trigger and the quick, surface‑level nature of the symptoms.
For chronic or recurring gum swelling that is milder but never quite resolves, the practitioner explores Kidney Yin Deficiency with Empty‑Heat. The gums may be dull red, and the teeth feel loose, especially at night. Accompanying signs like dizziness, tinnitus, lower‑back soreness, and a dry mouth point to deep Yin weakness. The tongue is red with little or no coating, and the pulse is thin and rapid.
When emotional stress is a clear trigger and the swollen gums come with a bitter taste, red eyes, and irritability, Liver Fire Blazing is considered. The heat rises along the Liver and Stomach pathways, making the pain sharp and often one‑sided. The tongue is red with a yellow coat, and the pulse feels wiry and rapid. The practitioner asks about recent frustration or anger to connect the pattern.
If the gums are pale, slightly swollen, and bleed easily without much redness, and you feel constantly tired, Qi and Blood Deficiency is likely. The tissue looks atrophied rather than inflamed, and you may have a pale complexion and weak digestion. The tongue is pale with a thin white coat, and the pulse is weak and thready. This pattern points to a lack of nourishment rather than excess heat.

### Stomach Fire (Stomach Heat)
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/stomach-fire-stomach-heat)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Intense red, swollen gums that bleed easily, Throbbing, burning gum pain, Foul breath and bitter taste in the mouth, Craving for cold drinks, constipation, Burning sensation in the stomach area
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Spicy, greasy, or fried foods, Alcohol and coffee, Emotional stress, Hot weather or overheated rooms
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Cold or cool drinks, Cooling foods and drinks, Salt water rinses, Rest and stress reduction
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  The Stomach channel runs directly through the gums. When excessive heat builds up in the Stomach - often from spicy, greasy food, alcohol, or emotional stress - it blazes upward along this channel. This rising Fire causes the gums to become intensely red, swollen, and painful, and may also lead to bleeding, foul breath, and a constant thirst for cold drinks.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red body, especially in the center, with a thick dry yellow coating. Pulse: rapid, slippery, and forceful, notably strong at the right middle position (Stomach).
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Stomach Fire is a condition of excess heat that rises to the gums. Spicy and greasy foods, alcohol, and emotional stress all generate more heat in the Stomach, making the fire blaze stronger. Cooling foods and cold drinks directly counteract this heat, while salt water rinses reduce local inflammation. Rest and stress reduction prevent Qi stagnation from turning into heat, allowing the body to cool down and the gums to heal.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Avoid spicy, greasy, and fried foods, as well as alcohol and coffee, which directly fuel Stomach Fire. Eat cooling, light foods like cucumber, watermelon, and leafy greens. Peppermint or chrysanthemum tea can help clear heat. Maintain good oral hygiene and manage emotional stress, which can stagnate Qi and generate heat.

### Wind-Heat
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/wind-heat)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Sudden onset of red, swollen gums, Pain worsens with heat or hot drinks, Fever and sore throat, Thirst with desire for cold drinks, Headache
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Hot drinks or food, Exposure to wind or drafts, Spicy, greasy, or fried foods, Fever or heat
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Cold or cool drinks, Rest and stress reduction, Cool compress on cheek, Avoiding spicy food
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Wind-Heat is an external pathogen that enters through the mouth and nose. It travels quickly upward along the channels to the gums, especially the Stomach and Large Intestine channels that run through the face and jaw. This sudden invasion produces acute, hot, red, and painful swelling that flares rapidly.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red tip and edges with a thin yellow coating. Pulse: floating and rapid.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Wind-Heat invades from the exterior, so heat and wind aggravate the swelling by fueling the pathogen. Cooling measures and rest help the body expel the pathogen and calm the inflammation.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Rest and avoid drafts. Drink plenty of water and cooling herbal teas like chrysanthemum or mint. Avoid spicy, fried, and overly hot foods that add heat.

### Kidney Yin Deficiency With Empty-Heat Blazing
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/kidney-yin-deficiency-with-empty-heat-blazing)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Dull, aching gum pain, Loose teeth, Worse at night and with fatigue, Night sweats and five-palm heat, Lower back soreness and weakness, Dry throat with preference for small sips
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Spicy, greasy, or fried foods, Overwork and staying up late, Emotional stress, Excessive sexual activity, Hot weather or overheated rooms
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Cooling foods and drinks, Rest and stress reduction, Gentle exercise like walking, Cold or cool drinks, Acupressure on Kidney points
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When Kidney Yin is depleted, it can no longer anchor the body's Yang energy. This Yang floats upward as Empty-Heat, rising along the channels to the gums. The gums lose their nourishing Yin moisture, becoming dry, mildly swollen, and prone to a dull ache. Because the deficiency is chronic, the teeth may feel loose, and symptoms often worsen at night when Yin is naturally lower.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red, thin, dry body with little or no coating, possibly cracked. Pulse: fine (細 xì) and rapid (數 shuò), weak at the rear (Chi) position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Kidney Yin is the body's cooling, nourishing foundation. Late nights, overwork, and excessive activity drain it further, allowing Empty-Heat to flare up and inflame the gums. Spicy and fried foods add external heat, making symptoms worse. Cooling, moistening foods and adequate rest replenish Yin and help anchor Yang, calming the upward heat. Gentle movement supports Qi flow without depletion, while acupressure on Kidney points directly strengthens the Yin foundation.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Prioritize cooling, moistening foods like pears, tofu, black sesame, and goji berries. Avoid spicy, fried, and overly heating foods that stir up Empty-Heat. Go to bed early and avoid overwork; even gentle exercise like walking or tai chi helps circulate Qi without draining Yin. Keep well hydrated with small sips of room-temperature water throughout the day.

### Liver Fire Blazing
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/liver-fire-blazing)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Burning pain in the gums, Bitter taste in the mouth, Red, irritated eyes, Intense irritability and short temper, Gum swelling worsens with stress
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Emotional stress, Spicy or greasy foods, Alcohol and coffee, Overwork and staying up late
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Rest and stress reduction, Cooling foods and drinks, Gentle exercise like walking
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Emotional stress and bottled-up frustration can cause Liver Qi to stagnate and then transform into Fire. This Fire flares upward along the Liver and Stomach channels, which both pass through the gums, leading to painful, swollen gums. The bitter taste, red eyes, and irritability are classic signs that the Liver Fire is rising and disturbing the head.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red body with redder sides, dry yellow coating, possible red prickles. Pulse: wiry and rapid, full and forceful, especially at the left Guan position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Emotional stress and anger are the root cause of Liver Qi stagnation turning into Fire, so calming the mind directly addresses the mechanism. Spicy, greasy, and hot foods add fuel to the Fire, while cooling foods and rest help clear Heat and soothe the Liver, preventing the upward surge that inflames the gums.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Avoid spicy, greasy, and fried foods, as well as alcohol and coffee, which add fuel to the fire. Sip chrysanthemum or peppermint tea to help cool the Liver. Make stress management a priority - gentle daily walks, deep breathing, or meditation can prevent Qi from stagnating and flaring up.

### Qi and Blood Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/qi-and-blood-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Pale, slightly swollen gums that bleed easily, Persistent fatigue and lack of energy, Pale or sallow complexion and pale lips, Dizziness or lightheadedness, Poor appetite
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Overwork and staying up late, Skipping meals or eating raw, cold foods, Emotional stress
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Rest and stress reduction, Warm, nourishing meals, Gentle exercise like walking
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When Qi and Blood are deficient, the gums lose the nourishment they need to stay firm and healthy. The Spleen governs the flesh and produces Qi and Blood, so a weak Spleen leads to pale, soft gums that become slightly swollen and bleed easily. Without enough Qi to hold the blood within the vessels, even gentle brushing can trigger bleeding, and the person often feels exhausted and looks pale.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale body, may be thin or have teeth marks, with a thin white coating. Pulse: fine (thin) and weak, especially weak in the right Guan position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Qi and Blood deficiency is rooted in a weak Spleen. Rest and warm, easily digested foods give the Spleen the support it needs to generate Qi and Blood, while overwork, stress, and cold or raw foods further drain the Spleen and deplete these vital resources. Building up the foundation gradually restores the gums' resilience.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat warm, cooked foods like congee, stews, and bone broths to support Spleen Qi and Blood production. Avoid raw, cold, and greasy foods that weaken digestion. Prioritize gentle daily movement and plenty of rest to avoid depleting your reserves.

## คำถามที่พบบ่อย (Frequently Asked Questions)
### Can acupuncture really help with swollen gums?
Yes, acupuncture is often very effective for reducing gum swelling and pain. Local points on the face, like Jiache (ST-6), help drain heat and move Qi in the area, while distal points like Hegu (LI-4) and Neiting (ST-44) clear the underlying Stomach Fire or Wind-Heat. Many patients feel relief after the first session, especially in acute cases.

### How quickly can Chinese herbs reduce gum swelling?
For acute patterns like Stomach Fire or Wind-Heat, herbal formulas can start working within a day or two, with significant improvement in less than a week. Chronic patterns like Kidney Yin Deficiency take longer - you may notice gradual easing of swelling and less frequent flare-ups over 3 to 6 weeks of consistent use. Herbs are taken daily, often as a tea or in pill form.

### Is it safe to combine TCM with my dentist's treatment?
Absolutely. TCM herbal formulas and acupuncture work well alongside professional dental cleanings, antibiotics, and other conventional care. Just be sure to tell both your dentist and your TCM practitioner about all treatments you are receiving. If you take blood-thinning medications, mention this because some herbs like Dang Gui (Angelica sinensis) can have mild blood-moving effects.

### What foods should I avoid if I have swollen gums?
In general, avoid spicy, greasy, and deep-fried foods, as well as alcohol and excessive coffee - these generate heat and can aggravate swelling. Sugary foods also promote dampness and inflammation. Instead, favor cooling, water-rich foods like cucumber, watermelon, pear, and mung beans. If your gums are pale and you feel weak, warm, nourishing soups and stews may be better than raw cold foods.

### Does TCM treat bleeding gums too?
Yes. Bleeding gums often accompany swelling and are a key sign in several patterns. Stomach Fire can cause bleeding with bright red blood, while Qi and Blood Deficiency leads to oozing of pale blood. The same herbs and acupuncture points that address the swelling also help stop the bleeding by cooling heat or strengthening the Spleen's ability to hold blood within the vessels.

### Will the swelling come back after TCM treatment?
TCM aims to correct the underlying imbalance, not just suppress symptoms. If the root pattern is fully resolved and you maintain a balanced diet and lifestyle, recurrence is much less likely. However, if you return to a diet heavy in spicy, greasy foods or endure prolonged stress, the condition can flare again. Your practitioner will give you personalized guidance to help prevent relapses.
