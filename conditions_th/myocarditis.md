## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนปัจจุบัน (Conventional Medicine View)
### คำอธิบาย (Description)
Myocarditis is inflammation of the heart muscle, often triggered by a viral infection. The immune system's response can damage heart cells, leading to symptoms like chest pain, shortness of breath, fatigue, and palpitations. In severe cases, it can weaken the heart's pumping ability and cause heart failure. Diagnosis typically involves blood tests, ECG, echocardiogram, and sometimes cardiac MRI or biopsy.

### การรักษาหลัก (Treatments)
Conventional treatment focuses on supporting the heart and managing complications. This may include rest, medications to reduce inflammation (NSAIDs, corticosteroids), treat heart failure (beta-blockers, ACE inhibitors), or control arrhythmias. In severe cases, hospitalization for monitoring and intravenous medications is needed. The underlying infection is usually viral, so antibiotics are not effective unless a bacterial cause is identified.

## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนจีน (Traditional Chinese Medicine View)
### ความเข้าใจเกี่ยวกับการแพทย์แผนจีน (TCM Understanding)
TCM sees myocarditis as an invasion of an external heat toxin - often from a viral illness - that bypasses the body's outer defenses and directly attacks the Heart and its protective envelope, the Pericardium. In the early acute phase, this heat can be so intense that it disturbs the mind, causing fever, delirium, and chest pain. This is the Heat in Pericardium pattern, a medical emergency even in TCM terms.
If the heat isn't fully cleared, it can combine with body fluids to create a sticky, obstructive Phlegm-Fire that harasses the Heart. This leads to a sensation of chest oppression, irritability, and a thick yellow tongue coating. The mind becomes restless and agitated because Phlegm and Fire together cloud the Heart's ability to house the spirit.
Once the acute heat subsides, the real work of recovery begins. The toxin has burned up the heart's Qi and Yin - its energy and cooling fluids. This leaves a pattern of Qi and Yin Deficiency, characterized by crushing fatigue, palpitations on slight exertion, and night sweats.
Some people tip more toward Heart Yin Deficiency, with insomnia, anxiety, and a dry mouth, while others develop a deeper Heart Yang Deficiency, feeling cold and weak with a slow pulse.
In chronic cases, the weakened Qi fails to push blood effectively, leading to Blood Stagnation. This causes fixed, stabbing chest pain and a dusky complexion. So the same Western diagnosis of myocarditis can unfold through a spectrum of TCM patterns - from acute heat to chronic deficiency - each requiring a different treatment strategy. This is why TCM doesn't just treat 'myocarditis' but the specific pattern presenting in each person.

### การวินิจฉัยโดยแพทย์แผนจีน (Practitioner Diagnosis)
A practitioner first asks about the onset and what the chest discomfort feels like. In acute myocarditis, a sudden fever, sharp chest pain, and a racing heartbeat point toward Heat in Pericardium - an invasion of heat toxin that directly disturbs the heart. The tongue is red with a thin or yellow coating, and the pulse is rapid, confirming the acute heat picture.
If the heat lingers and condenses fluids, Phlegm‑Fire harassing the Heart develops. Here the chest feels oppressed rather than sharply painful, and the person is irritable, restless, and may have a sensation of fullness. The tongue shows a thick, greasy yellow coating, and the pulse becomes slippery and rapid. Asking about mucus or a heavy sensation helps separate this pattern from pure heat invasion.
After the toxin damages the heart’s resources, deficiency patterns emerge. Qi and Yin Deficiency brings crushing fatigue, palpitations that worsen with even mild exertion, and night sweats. The tongue is red with a scanty coat, and the pulse is weak and rapid. A closely related picture, Heart Yin Deficiency, presents more restlessness, insomnia, and a dry mouth, with a tongue that is redder and almost peel‑coated. The practitioner distinguishes them by whether fatigue or mental agitation dominates.
In the chronic phase, Qi Deficiency causing Blood Stagnation produces a stabbing, fixed pain in the chest. The tongue is dusky with purple spots, and the pulse is weak and choppy. When the condition deepens, Heart Yang Deficiency appears: the person feels cold, the limbs are chilly, and severe palpitations are accompanied by a deep, slow pulse and a pale, swollen tongue. These later patterns tell the story of a heart struggling to move blood and warm the body.

### Heat in Pericardium
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/heat-in-pericardium)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Burning chest pain, High fever that worsens at night, Delirium, confusion, or loss of consciousness, Cold hands and feet despite high fever, Crimson, stiff tongue with dry yellow coating
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Spicy, fried, or greasy foods, Overexertion or physical strain, Hot weather or stuffy rooms, Emotional stress or anger
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Complete bed rest, Cool drinks and cooling fruits, Cool, quiet environment
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  In myocarditis, an external heat toxin invades the body and pushes straight past the outer defenses into the Nutritive level, where it settles in the Pericardium - the protective envelope of the Heart. This intense heat disrupts the Heart’s ability to house the mind, causing fever, delirium, and chest pain. The pathogen consumes body fluids and yin, leading to a crimson stiff tongue and cold limbs despite a burning trunk.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: fresh crimson, stiff and shortened, with a dry yellow coating (or little coating) and possible prickles on the tip. Pulse: fine (xi) and rapid (shu).
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Heat in the Pericardium is a deep, intense heat that damages yin and disturbs the spirit. Anything that adds heat - spicy food, hot surroundings, or physical strain - fans the flames and pushes the pathogen deeper. Rest and cooling foods or drinks help the body clear the heat without further injury, while a calm environment prevents the mind from being overstimulated.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Absolute rest is essential to avoid overstimulating the heart. Sip cool water or mild herbal teas like chrysanthemum frequently. Eat light, cooling foods such as pear, watermelon, and cucumber; strictly avoid alcohol, coffee, spicy, fried, or greasy dishes that add internal heat.

### Qi and Yin Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/qi-and-yin-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Persistent tiredness and lack of strength, Shortness of breath that worsens with any exertion, Night sweats, Heart palpitations, especially when resting, Dry mouth and throat with little desire to drink, Warm sensation in the palms, soles, and chest
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Overexertion or physical strain, Emotional stress and worry, Spicy, fried, or greasy foods, Overwork, late nights, or insufficient sleep, Excessive talking or singing
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Rest and sleep, Nourishing, easy-to-digest foods, Gentle walking or movement, Cool, quiet environment, Deep breathing and meditation
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  In myocarditis, a strong heat toxin invasion during the acute phase burns away the heart's Qi and Yin like a fever depletes a battery. Once the fever subsides, the heart is left underpowered and undernourished-Qi deficiency means there is not enough drive to pump blood, causing fatigue and breathlessness, while Yin deficiency leads to a lack of cooling fluids, so the heart beats faster and feels unsettled, and night sweats and dry mouth appear.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red or pale-red body, thin or absent coating, possibly cracked, dry surface. Pulse: fine, weak, and often rapid.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Qi and Yin are easily depleted by overwork, emotional strain, and poor sleep-the very things that drain a recovering body. Rest and nourishing foods directly rebuild these substances, while a cool, quiet environment prevents further Yin loss. Spicy and drying foods consume Yin like adding fuel to a dying fire, so avoiding them preserves the body's remaining fluids. Gentle movement promotes Qi flow without exhausting it, whereas pushing too hard quickly exhausts the already depleted Qi, worsening fatigue and palpitations.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Rest is the most powerful medicine right now-sleep deeply and avoid pushing through fatigue. Eat small, frequent meals of warm, easy-to-digest foods like rice congee with a little ginseng, lily bulb, or goji berries. Avoid spicy, fried, or drying foods that consume Yin, and steer clear of coffee and alcohol. Gentle movement like tai chi or a slow walk can help Qi flow without draining it.

### Phlegm-Fire harassing the Heart
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/phlegm-fire-harassing-the-heart)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Copious thick yellow sticky phlegm, Chest oppression and fullness, Mental restlessness, agitation, and insomnia, Flushed red face and red eyes, Bitter taste in the mouth and thirst, Heart palpitations
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Spicy, fried, or greasy foods, Emotional stress or anger, Overwork, late nights, or insufficient sleep, Hot, humid weather
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Rest and mental calm, Light, non-greasy meals, Cool, quiet environment, Gentle walking or movement
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  In myocarditis, an initial heat toxin invasion (often a viral infection) can burn body fluids and condense them into sticky Phlegm. This Phlegm then combines with the lingering Heat, creating a hot, obstructive mass that rises to disturb the Heart. When the Heart is muddled by Phlegm-Fire, its ability to house the mind is compromised, leading to mental restlessness and palpitations. The Phlegm blocks the chest, causing oppression and a sensation of fullness, while the Heat generates thirst, bitter taste, and a flushed face.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: Red body, swollen with a red prickly tip, and a thick yellow greasy coating. Pulse: Slippery and rapid; the left Cun (Heart) position is full and overflowing, the right Guan (Spleen/Stomach) is slippery.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Spicy and greasy foods directly fuel the production of Phlegm and Heat, which are the core pathological factors in this pattern. Emotional stress and anger stir up Heart Fire, making the mind more restless and the chest tighter. Rest and a calm mind allow the Heart to settle; light meals stop adding more Phlegm and Heat to the system. Gentle movement helps the Qi circulate and transforms Phlegm, while a cool environment counteracts the internal Heat and eases irritability.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Avoid greasy, spicy, and sweet foods that generate Phlegm and Heat. Favor light, cooling foods like pears, bamboo shoots, and mung beans. Steer clear of alcohol and coffee. Keep a calm mind-emotional stress fans the Fire-and incorporate gentle daily movement like walking to keep Qi flowing and Phlegm from settling.

### Heart Yin Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/heart-yin-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Palpitations worse at rest or at night, Night sweats, Mental restlessness and anxiety, Feeling of heat in palms, soles, and chest, Red tongue with little or no coating
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Overwork, late nights, or insufficient sleep, Spicy, fried, or greasy foods, Emotional stress and worry
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Cool, quiet environment, Regular sleep schedule, Moistening foods (pear, millet)
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  In myocarditis, an initial heat toxin invades the heart and burns up the body's cooling, nourishing fluids. This leaves the heart muscle undernourished and overheated, like an engine running without enough coolant. The depleted Yin fails to anchor the mind at night, so restlessness, vivid dreams, and nighttime palpitations become prominent.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red body, especially at the tip, with little or no coating, dry, possibly a midline crack. Pulse: thin and rapid (细数 xì shù), weak at the deep level in the left cun position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Heart Yin Deficiency means the body's cooling, nourishing reserves are low. Overwork, late nights, and emotional stress consume Yin further, while spicy and fried foods add heat. ### Qi Deficiency causing Blood Stagnation
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/qi-deficiency-causing-blood-stagnation)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Fixed, stabbing chest pain that worsens with pressure, Profound fatigue and lack of strength, Shortness of breath with reluctance to speak, Dusky or pale-grey facial complexion
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Overexertion or physical strain, Cold weather or drafts, Raw, cold, or greasy foods, Emotional stress
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Rest and sleep, Warmth and warmth on chest, Warm nourishing meals, Gentle walking or movement
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  In the chronic phase of myocarditis, the heart muscle is left weakened and its Qi is depleted. Qi is the force that pumps blood through the vessels; when it is too weak to push effectively, the blood slows down and begins to stagnate. This stagnation creates fixed, stabbing pain in the chest, while the underlying Qi deficiency causes profound fatigue, shortness of breath, and a dull complexion.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale-dark or pale-purple body, with possible stasis spots, teeth marks, and distended sublingual veins; thin white coat. Pulse: deep, fine/weak, and choppy (涩, sè).
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: <p>Qi deficiency means the body's vital force is low; rest and sleep allow Qi to rebuild, while overexertion further depletes it. Cold environments and raw foods congeal blood and worsen stasis, whereas warmth and warm cooked meals promote smooth circulation. Gentle movement helps move Qi and blood without draining energy, while emotional stress stagnates Qi and worsens the underlying deficiency.</p>
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Prioritize rest and avoid overexertion to conserve Qi. Eat warm, cooked, easily digestible foods like soups and stews with mild spices such as ginger to support Qi and blood circulation. Gentle movement such as walking or Tai Chi can help move blood without depleting energy. Avoid cold, raw foods and icy drinks, which congeal blood and weaken Qi.

### Heart Yang Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/heart-yang-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Cold hands and feet, Bright pale or white face, Palpitations that worsen with exertion, Fatigue and mental listlessness, Chest stuffiness or mild pain, Spontaneous sweating
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Cold weather or drafts, Overexertion and fatigue, Raw, cold, or greasy foods, Emotional stress and fear
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warmth and covering up, Rest and lying down, Warm drinks and soups, Gentle warmth like moxibustion
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Myocarditis often begins with a heat toxin that damages the heart's Qi and Yin. When the illness is severe or prolonged, or in someone with a constitutional weakness, the heart's Yang becomes depleted. The heart's warming and propelling force fails, leaving the chest cold and the pulse weak and slow. This leads to severe palpitations that worsen with any effort, cold limbs, and a bright pale face.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale, puffy, tender body with teeth marks and a white slippery coating. Pulse: deep, weak, and slow; may be knotted (irregular pauses).
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Heart Yang Deficiency means the body's internal fire is low. Cold and raw foods further chill the body, while overexertion depletes remaining Yang. Warmth, rest, and warm nourishment gently stoke the heart's fire and conserve energy, helping to restore normal circulation and warmth.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Keep warm, especially the chest and feet. Eat warm, cooked foods like soups, stews, and porridges. Use warming spices such as ginger, cinnamon, and cloves. Avoid raw, cold, and icy foods and drinks. Gentle movement like walking or tai chi is beneficial, but avoid overexertion. Moxibustion on the lower abdomen can be very supportive.

## คำถามที่พบบ่อย (Frequently Asked Questions)
### Can TCM help with myocarditis recovery after the infection is gone?
Yes, TCM excels at post-infectious recovery. Once the acute virus has passed, many people are left with lingering fatigue, palpitations, and exercise intolerance. TCM identifies the specific deficiency pattern (such as Qi and Yin Deficiency or Heart Yang Deficiency) and uses herbs and acupuncture to rebuild the heart's energy. This can shorten the convalescent period and help you return to normal activities sooner.

### Is it safe to combine Chinese herbs with my heart medications?
In most cases, yes, but coordination with both your cardiologist and TCM practitioner is essential. Some herbs that invigorate blood (like Tao Ren or Hong Hua) may increase the effect of anticoagulants or antiplatelet drugs. Certain herbs can also influence blood pressure or potassium levels. Always bring a complete list of your medications to your TCM consultation, and never stop prescribed drugs without medical guidance.

### How long until I can exercise again after myocarditis?
Exercise must be reintroduced very gradually and only after your heart has recovered enough. In TCM terms, you need sufficient Qi and Yin before you can safely exert yourself. We typically advise waiting until the tongue coating returns to normal and the pulse regains strength - usually at least 4-8 weeks into treatment. Even then, start with gentle walking or qigong, and avoid strenuous activity until your practitioner gives the green light.

### Does acupuncture work for chest pain from myocarditis?
Acupuncture can help relieve chest pain, especially when it stems from Blood Stagnation or Qi stagnation. Points like Neiguan PC-6 and Xinshu BL-15 are commonly used to calm the heart and move blood. However, acute, severe chest pain should always be evaluated by a doctor first to rule out serious complications.

### What can I eat to support heart healing?
Focus on warm, easily digestible foods that nourish Qi and Yin, such as congee, steamed vegetables, and small amounts of lean protein. Avoid greasy, fried, or spicy foods that generate phlegm and heat. Cold and raw foods weaken the Spleen and should be limited. Alcohol and caffeine can overstimulate the heart and are best avoided during recovery.

### I had myocarditis months ago and still feel tired - can TCM help?
Absolutely. Persistent fatigue is one of the most common post-myocarditis complaints and often corresponds to Qi and Yin Deficiency or Heart Yang Deficiency. Herbal formulas like Sheng Mai San can be tailored to your exact presentation and have been shown in studies to improve energy and cardiac function. Many patients notice a lift in fatigue within 4-6 weeks of starting treatment.

### Can children with myocarditis use TCM?
Yes, TCM can be adapted for children using gentler herbal dosages and acupuncture techniques like acupressure or low-level laser acupuncture. It is particularly helpful during the recovery phase to prevent lingering weakness. Always work with a pediatric-trained TCM practitioner and coordinate with your child's cardiologist.
