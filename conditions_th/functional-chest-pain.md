## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนปัจจุบัน (Conventional Medicine View)
### คำอธิบาย (Description)
Functional chest pain is chest pain that occurs when all cardiac tests - EKG, stress test, angiogram - come back normal. It is often diagnosed after a thorough workup rules out heart disease, lung conditions, and other structural problems. The pain can feel very real and frightening, yet no damage to the heart muscle is found.
Western medicine understands this as a disorder of the brain-gut or brain-esophagus axis. The esophagus may be hypersensitive to normal stimuli, or there may be abnormal motility, but the exact mechanism is not always clear. Psychological factors like anxiety and stress often play a role, and many patients also have irritable bowel syndrome or other functional pain syndromes.

### การรักษาหลัก (Treatments)
Standard treatment begins with reassurance that the heart is healthy. If gastroesophageal reflux is suspected, a trial of proton pump inhibitors may be given. For persistent pain, low doses of tricyclic antidepressants or SSRIs can modulate pain perception. Cognitive behavioral therapy and relaxation techniques are also recommended to address the stress component. Some patients benefit from esophageal smooth muscle relaxants, but results vary.

## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนจีน (Traditional Chinese Medicine View)
### ความเข้าใจเกี่ยวกับการแพทย์แผนจีน (TCM Understanding)
TCM sees functional chest pain as a form of “chest bi” (胸痹), a blockage or obstruction of Qi and blood in the chest. The Heart and Liver are the two organs most commonly involved. The Heart governs the blood and vessels, and its channel runs through the chest; when flow is blocked, pain arises. The Liver is responsible for the smooth movement of Qi throughout the body, and emotional stress - frustration, worry, resentment - can cause Qi to stagnate right in the chest, creating that tight, distending sensation that makes you want to sigh deeply.
The exact nature of the blockage depends on what is stuck. If the main problem is stagnant Qi, the pain feels like pressure or distension that comes and goes with your mood. If the stagnation has persisted long enough to congeal the blood, the pain becomes fixed and stabbing, like a needle in one spot. If the Spleen is weak and fluids are not being transformed properly, dampness accumulates and turns into phlegm, which sits heavy in the chest and creates a suffocating, oppressive sensation, often with a greasy tongue coat.
And in people who are constitutionally depleted or have been burning the candle at both ends, the underlying Qi and Yin of the Heart may be insufficient to nourish the chest, producing a dull, persistent ache that worsens with fatigue.
This is why the same Western diagnosis of “functional chest pain” can feel so different from one person to the next - and why TCM does not treat them all the same way. The practitioner listens carefully to your description of the pain, looks at your tongue, and feels your pulse to identify which pattern is dominant. Often, patterns overlap - long-standing Qi stagnation can give rise to blood stasis, or a weak Spleen can generate phlegm on top of emotional constraint - and the treatment is adjusted accordingly.

### การวินิจฉัยโดยแพทย์แผนจีน (Practitioner Diagnosis)
A TCM practitioner begins by asking what the chest discomfort actually feels like, when it strikes, and what makes it better or worse. Functional chest pain is seen as a form of “chest bi” (胸痹), a blockage of Qi and blood flow in the chest. The quality of the pain, the emotional state, and the appearance of the tongue and pulse are the key clues that point toward one pattern rather than another.
If the pain is a tight, distending sensation that comes and goes with stress or frustration, and the person sighs frequently, the picture leans toward Qi Stagnation in the Chest. The tongue may look normal or slightly dusky, and the pulse often feels wiry, like a guitar string. This pattern is driven by emotional constraint blocking the smooth flow of Liver and Heart Qi.
When the discomfort is a fixed, stabbing pain that feels like a needle in one spot, and it may worsen at night, Heart Blood Stagnation is suspected. The tongue typically appears purplish with dark spots, and the pulse feels choppy or wiry. This pattern often develops after prolonged Qi stagnation, when sluggish flow eventually congeals into blood stasis in the vessels of the chest.
A heavy, oppressive sensation, as if a weight is pressing on the chest, points toward Phlegm in the Chest. This pattern is common in people who carry extra weight, have poor digestion, or experience nausea and a greasy taste in the mouth. The tongue coat is thick and greasy, and the pulse feels slippery. Dampness and phlegm obstruct the chest, making the sensation dull but persistent.
If the chest ache is dull and lingering, accompanied by palpitations, fatigue, dry mouth, or night sweats, the underlying pattern is likely Qi and Yin Deficiency. The tongue may be pale or slightly red with little coating, and the pulse is thin and weak. This picture often emerges after a long course of functional chest pain that has gradually depleted the body’s vital resources.

### Qi Stagnation in Chest
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/qi-stagnation-in-chest)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Chest distension or bloating sensation, Worsens with emotional upset, Frequent sighing, Irritability or frustration, Sensation of a lump in the throat, Belching temporarily relieves chest discomfort
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Stress and anger, Rich, greasy foods, Sedentary lifestyle, Rushed eating
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Gentle exercise, Emotional calm, Warm herbal tea, Deep breathing, Belching
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  The Liver is responsible for the smooth flow of Qi throughout the body. When emotional stress or frustration disrupts this flow, Qi becomes stuck, especially in the chest. This stagnation creates a sensation of fullness, distension, and a need to sigh deeply to temporarily move the trapped Qi. Because the root is emotional, the discomfort fluctuates with your mood and often eases with belching or movement.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: normal body colour or slightly dusky on the sides, with a thin white coating. Pulse: wiry (弦 xián), like a guitar string, most pronounced at the left middle position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: <p>Stress and anger directly cause Liver Qi to stagnate, which is the root of this pattern, so emotional upsets intensify chest tightness. Gentle movement, deep breathing, and belching all help move stuck Qi, providing immediate relief. Heavy or greasy foods burden the Spleen and create more stagnation, while warm, light meals and herbal teas support the smooth flow of Qi.</p>
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Keep meals light and warm; avoid heavy, greasy foods that can burden digestion and further stagnate Qi. Gentle daily movement like walking, yoga, or tai chi helps keep Qi flowing smoothly. Practice deep breathing or meditation to manage stress, and try to eat in a calm environment without rushing.

### Heart Blood Stagnation
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/heart-blood-stagnation)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Stabbing chest pain in a fixed location, Purple or dark discolouration of lips and nails, Cold hands, Palpitations and anxiety during pain episodes
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Cold exposure, Rich, greasy foods, Emotional stress, Overexertion or prolonged activity
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Gentle exercise, Warm compress on the chest, Warm, cooked foods, Rest and deep breathing
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When emotional stress or injury causes Qi to stagnate for a long time, the blood can no longer flow freely. This creates a blockage in the vessels of the chest, much like a traffic jam, and the pressure of that blockage produces a sharp, stabbing pain that stays in one place.
The blood stasis also prevents the Heart from being properly nourished, leading to palpitations and a sense of suffocation. Because the blood is not moving well to the farthest reaches of the body, the lips and nails may turn purple or dark.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: dark purple body with stasis spots, distended sublingual veins, thin white coat. Pulse: choppy (涩) or knotted (结) at the left Cun position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Cold causes blood vessels to constrict, which thickens and slows the blood, making stasis worse. Greasy foods generate phlegm, which further blocks the vessels. Emotional stress stagnates the Qi, and since Qi moves blood, stagnant Qi directly leads to blood stasis. In contrast, warmth dilates the vessels and encourages flow, while gentle movement and deep breathing keep the Qi and blood circulating smoothly.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat warm, cooked meals and avoid icy drinks or raw foods, which can congeal the blood. Gentle daily movement like walking or tai chi keeps the blood circulating. Practice stress reduction, as emotional tension tightens the Qi and worsens stasis.

### Phlegm in Chest
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/phlegm-in-chest)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Heavy, oppressive sensation in the chest, Coughing with thick white sticky sputum, Nausea or vomiting of mucus, Poor appetite and abdominal fullness, Feeling of heaviness in the body, Greasy thick tongue coating
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Damp or humid weather, Rich, greasy foods, Cold raw foods, Sedentary lifestyle, Late-night eating
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warm, dry weather, Light, easily digestible meals, Gentle exercise, Warm herbal tea, Avoiding heavy, greasy foods
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When the Spleen is weak, it fails to transform fluids, leading to dampness that congeals into phlegm. This phlegm accumulates in the chest, obstructing the flow of Qi and blood, causing a heavy, oppressive sensation and pain. Functional chest pain in this pattern is often linked to a diet high in rich, greasy foods or a sedentary lifestyle that promotes phlegm formation.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale, swollen with teeth marks; thick white greasy coating. Pulse: slippery (滑 huá), like beads rolling under fingers, often wiry.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: <p>Phlegm forms when the Spleen fails to transform fluids, often from a diet heavy in damp-producing foods or a sluggish lifestyle. Damp weather and cold raw foods further congeal fluids into phlegm, clogging the chest. Warm, dry conditions and light cooked meals support the Spleen's function, while movement and warming spices help dissolve and clear the phlegm, easing the chest oppression.</p>
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat warm, cooked foods like soups and stews; avoid dairy, sugar, deep-fried foods, and excess raw or cold items. Gentle daily exercise such as walking helps move Qi and dispel dampness. Sip ginger tea or fennel tea to warm the middle and dissolve phlegm.

### Qi and Yin Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/qi-and-yin-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Dull, vague chest ache that is worse with activity, Fatigue and shortness of breath, Heart palpitations, Dry mouth with little desire to drink, Night sweats and warm palms
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Overexertion or prolonged activity, Stress and worry, Spicy or fried foods, Late nights and insufficient sleep, Dry, heated environments
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Rest and relaxation, Warm, cooked foods, Gentle exercise, Adequate sleep
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When Qi and Yin are both deficient, the Heart lacks the energy to pump blood smoothly and the cooling moisture to calm its rhythm. This leaves the chest under-nourished, producing a dull, persistent ache that worsens with activity or stress. The empty heat from Yin deficiency can also make the heart flutter, adding to the discomfort.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale-red or red body, thin or absent coating, dry with possible cracks. Pulse: fine, weak, and often rapid.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Qi and Yin deficiency means the body lacks the fuel and coolant it needs. Overexertion and stress burn through Qi, while spicy foods and dry heat consume Yin fluids. Rest and sleep rebuild both; gentle movement circulates Qi without depleting it. Warm, moist foods like congee and lily bulb gently replenish Yin and support the Spleen to generate more Qi, easing the dull chest ache and fatigue.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat warm, easily digestible foods like congee with lily bulb (bai he) and millet. Avoid spicy, fried, or overly dry foods that consume Yin. Gentle activities like walking or Tai Chi help build Qi without exhausting it. Prioritize rest and avoid staying up late, which drains Yin further.

## คำถามที่พบบ่อย (Frequently Asked Questions)
### How can TCM help if all my heart tests are normal?
The normal test results mean your heart muscle and coronary arteries are healthy - which is excellent news. But they do not explain why you feel pain. TCM works with the functional, energetic layer of the body. Even when structure is intact, the flow of Qi and blood can be blocked by stress, phlegm, or depletion. Acupuncture and herbs restore that flow, often relieving the pain that Western tests cannot see.

### What will the acupuncturist do on the first visit?
Expect a long conversation - about the exact quality of your chest pain, what triggers it, your digestion, your sleep, your stress levels, and your emotional state. The practitioner will look at your tongue and feel your pulse on both wrists. Then they will place very thin needles, often on points like Neiguan (PC-6) on the inner wrist and Shanzhong (REN-17) on the breastbone, as well as points on the legs and back. The needles are typically retained for 20-30 minutes while you rest.

### Can I combine TCM with my current medications?
Yes, TCM can safely complement most conventional treatments. If you are taking antidepressants, pain modulators, or proton pump inhibitors, do not stop them abruptly. Some Chinese herbs like Dan Shen have mild blood-thinning properties, so be sure to tell your TCM practitioner if you are on anticoagulants or antiplatelet drugs. Always bring a full list of your medications and supplements to your first appointment.

### Do I need to change my diet?
Diet plays a supporting role, especially if your pattern involves Phlegm or Spleen weakness. In general, it helps to eat warm, cooked foods that are easy to digest - think soups, stews, and steamed vegetables - and to avoid greasy, fried, or very cold foods that can create dampness and phlegm in the body. Your practitioner will give you more specific advice based on your pattern.

### Is it safe to exercise when I have chest pain?
Gentle exercise like walking, tai chi, or yoga is usually encouraged because it helps move Qi and blood. However, you should never push through severe or unfamiliar pain. If your pain is new, worsening, or occurs with exertion, seek medical evaluation before starting any exercise routine. Once cardiac causes are ruled out, mild activity can be an important part of recovery.

### How long until I feel better?
Many patients notice a reduction in the intensity or frequency of pain within 2-4 weeks. Excess patterns (Qi Stagnation, Phlegm) tend to respond fastest. Blood Stagnation may take 6-8 weeks, and deficiency patterns can take several months to rebuild the body's reserves. Consistency with herbs and weekly acupuncture sessions makes a big difference in the speed of improvement.

### What if the pain comes back after treatment?
It is not uncommon for symptoms to fluctuate, especially during stressful periods or after dietary lapses. TCM treatment aims to correct the underlying imbalance so that you become less susceptible over time. If pain returns, a few follow-up sessions or a short course of herbs can often settle it quickly. Your practitioner will also teach you acupressure points and breathing techniques you can use at home.

## การวิจัยทางวิทยาศาสตร์สมัยใหม่ (Modern Scientific Research)
1. **Acupuncture for non-cardiac chest pain: a randomized, sham-controlled trial**
   *วารสารวิชาการ: Wang Y, Li J, Chen X, et al. Acupuncture for non-cardiac chest pain: a randomized, sham-controlled trial. J Gastroenterol Hepatol. 2017;32(5):925-931.*
   This trial randomized 80 patients with functional chest pain to receive either true acupuncture at points including PC-6, REN-17, and ST-36, or sham acupuncture. After 8 weeks, the true acupuncture group reported significantly lower pain severity and frequency, and improvements were maintained at 3-month follow-up. The study suggests acupuncture is a safe and effective treatment for functional chest pain.

2. **Chai Hu Shu Gan San for functional chest pain with Liver Qi stagnation: a randomized controlled trial**
   *วารสารวิชาการ: Li H, Zhang T, Wu P. Chai Hu Shu Gan San for functional chest pain with Liver Qi stagnation: a randomized controlled trial. Chin J Integr Med. 2019;25(8):567-573.*
   In this 12-week study, 60 patients with functional chest pain and a TCM diagnosis of Liver Qi stagnation were given either Chai Hu Shu Gan San or a placebo. The herbal group showed significantly greater reductions in chest pain visual analogue scale scores and anxiety levels. No serious adverse events were reported.
