## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนปัจจุบัน (Conventional Medicine View)
### คำอธิบาย (Description)
Obsessive Compulsive Disorder (OCD) is a mental health condition characterized by persistent, unwanted intrusive thoughts (obsessions) and repetitive behaviors or mental acts (compulsions) performed to neutralize the anxiety those thoughts cause. Common obsessions include fears of contamination, harm, or symmetry, while compulsions often involve excessive washing, checking, or counting. Diagnosis is clinical, based on the presence of obsessions and/or compulsions that are time-consuming and cause significant distress or impairment in daily functioning.

### การรักษาหลัก (Treatments)
First-line treatment typically includes selective serotonin reuptake inhibitors (SSRIs) such as fluoxetine or sertraline, and cognitive behavioral therapy (CBT), specifically exposure and response prevention (ERP). Some patients benefit from clomipramine, a tricyclic antidepressant. Treatment often combines medication and therapy, and may require higher doses of SSRIs than those used for depression.

## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนจีน (Traditional Chinese Medicine View)
### ความเข้าใจเกี่ยวกับการแพทย์แผนจีน (TCM Understanding)
TCM understands Obsessive Compulsive Disorder primarily through the relationship between the Liver, Heart, and Spleen. The Liver is responsible for the smooth flow of Qi throughout the body and mind. When emotional stress, frustration, or suppressed feelings block this flow, Qi stagnates. This stagnation can press upward and disturb the Heart, which houses the Shen - the mind and consciousness. The result is a sense of being mentally 'stuck,' with thoughts that loop endlessly and a compulsive need to perform rituals to temporarily relieve the inner pressure.
The Heart is directly involved because it anchors the Shen. When excess Heat or Fire blazes in the Heart - often from long-standing stagnation - it agitates the mind, producing relentless intrusive thoughts and a feeling of inner heat that demands immediate action. Alternatively, when the Spleen is weak and fails to transform fluids properly, Dampness congeals into Phlegm. This turbid Phlegm can rise and cloud the Heart orifice, much like a fog obscuring a clear window. The mind becomes dull and heavy, unable to let go of thoughts.
Over time, chronic stress and mental strain can deplete the body's deeper resources. Blood deficiency leaves the Heart and Liver without enough nourishment to anchor the spirit, creating a timid, anxious disposition. Kidney Yin deficiency leads to a hollow, restless mind that worsens in the evening. This is why the same Western diagnosis of OCD can have multiple TCM causes - the specific pattern of imbalance determines the treatment strategy, not just the symptom label.

### การวินิจฉัยโดยแพทย์แผนจีน (Practitioner Diagnosis)
A TCM practitioner begins by tracing the emotional and physical landscape of the intrusive thoughts. The quality of the mental restlessness, the presence of chest or rib-side distension, and the nature of any irritability all provide the first clues. Liver Qi Stagnation (肝郁气滞, gān yù qì zhì) is the earliest and most common pattern, marked by a sense of emotional constraint, frequent sighing, and a wiry pulse that feels like a taut guitar string.
When that stuck Qi smolders into Heat, the picture shifts toward Liver Qi Stagnation transforming into Heat. The person becomes noticeably more irritable and impulsive, with a bitter taste in the mouth, a redder tongue with a thin yellow coating, and a rapid, wiry pulse. The compulsive cycle feels more urgent and fiery, distinguishing it from the simple tightness of plain stagnation.
Heart Fire blazing is one of the patterns that directly disturbs the mind. It produces a red tongue tip, mouth ulcers, palpitations, and a rapid pulse; the thoughts feel scorching and agitating.
Phlegm Misting the Heart, by contrast, brings a heavy, foggy rumination, a thick greasy tongue coating, and a slippery pulse. The person feels mentally stuck in a loop but without the burning intensity of pure Fire.
When the condition drains the body’s resources over time, deficiency patterns emerge. Liver and Heart Blood Deficiency leaves the person pale, easily startled, and fatigued, with a thin pale tongue and a weak pulse-obsessive worry worsens after exertion.
Kidney and Liver Yin Deficiency brings a red tongue with little coating, night sweats, dizziness, and a fine rapid pulse; the mind feels restless and unanchored, especially at night, due to empty Heat flaring upward.

### Liver Qi Stagnation
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/liver-qi-stagnation)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Feeling stuck in repetitive thought loops, Tension or distension along the ribs, Frequent sighing, Irritability and mood swings, Worsens with emotional stress
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Stress and frustration, Suppressing anger, Heavy, greasy foods, Lack of physical activity
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Gentle exercise or movement, Deep breathing and meditation, Warm compress on ribs, Expressing feelings, Rest and adequate sleep
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When emotional stress, frustration, or suppressed feelings build up, the Liver's ability to keep Qi flowing smoothly becomes impaired. The stagnant Qi then presses upward and disturbs the Heart, which houses the mind (Shen). This creates a sense of being mentally 'stuck'-the mind loops on the same thoughts and can't move forward, just like the Qi itself is stuck. The physical tension in the rib area and frequent sighing are direct signs of this stagnation.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: normal or slightly dark body with red or slightly purple sides, thin white coat. Pulse: wiry (弦 xián), like a taut guitar string, strongest at the left middle position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Stress and emotional suppression directly cause Liver Qi to stagnate, which is the root mechanism of this pattern. Expressing feelings and reducing stress help release the stagnation. Gentle movement and deep breathing physically move Qi, while warmth relaxes the Liver channel and eases constriction.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Gentle daily exercise like walking, yoga, or tai chi helps keep Qi flowing and prevents stagnation. Avoid excessive alcohol and greasy, heavy foods that burden the Liver. Practice mindful breathing or meditation to release emotional tension. Engage in joyful activities and express feelings rather than bottling them up.

### Liver Qi Stagnation that transforms into Heat
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/liver-qi-stagnation-that-transforms-into-heat)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Explosive irritability and anger, Restlessness and agitation, Bitter taste in the mouth, Throbbing headache at the temples, Red face and eyes, Distending pain in the ribcage
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Stress and frustration, Spicy, greasy, or fried foods, Alcohol and coffee, Hot, stuffy environments or overheating, Suppressing anger
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Cooling foods and drinks, Gentle exercise or movement, Quiet, dark environment, Cool cloth on forehead
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When the Liver Qi stagnates over time, it generates Heat. This Heat rises and disturbs the Heart, which houses the Shen (mind). The restless Shen then produces obsessive thoughts, intense irritability, and a compulsive need to perform rituals to temporarily relieve the inner agitation. The explosive anger and restlessness are the outward signs of this internal fire.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red body with redder sides, yellow coating. Pulse: wiry (弦 xián) and rapid (数 shuò), forceful.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Stress and frustration directly worsen Liver Qi stagnation, which is the root of this pattern, generating more Heat that disturbs the mind. Cooling foods and environments help clear Heat, while gentle exercise moves stagnant Qi, preventing it from turning into fire. Deep breathing and calm environments soothe the Liver and allow the Shen to settle, breaking the cycle of obsessive thoughts and compulsive behaviors.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Avoid spicy, greasy, and fried foods, as well as alcohol and coffee, which add Heat. Emphasize cooling foods like cucumber, celery, chrysanthemum tea, and pear. Regular gentle exercise like walking or yoga helps move stagnant Qi. Practice deep breathing or meditation to calm the mind and prevent frustration from building up.

### Heart Fire blazing
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/heart-fire-blazing)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Palpitations and a fluttering sensation in the chest, Mental restlessness and agitation, Mouth or tongue ulcers with red, painful edges, Red flushed face and a feeling of heat, Insomnia with vivid, disturbing dreams, Thirst for cold drinks
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Anger and emotional stress, Spicy, greasy, or fried foods, Alcohol and coffee, Hot, stuffy environments or overheating, Overwork and lack of sleep
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Cooling foods and drinks, Quiet, dark environment, Cool ambient temperature, Deep breathing and meditation
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  The Heart houses the Shen (mind and consciousness). When excess Fire blazes in the Heart, it directly disturbs the Shen, producing relentless intrusive thoughts, anxiety, and a feeling of inner heat that demands action. The mental restlessness and agitation drive compulsive behaviors as a desperate attempt to calm the turmoil, but the underlying Fire remains, creating a vicious cycle. Insomnia and vivid dreams worsen the condition, leaving the mind exhausted and more susceptible to obsessive loops.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red body with a markedly redder tip, red prickles, and a dry yellow coating. Pulse: rapid, forceful, and overflowing, especially at the left front position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Heart Fire blazing is an excess heat pattern that disturbs the mind. Cooling foods and environments directly counteract the heat, reducing mental agitation. Emotional stress and anger add fuel to the fire, while spicy foods and alcohol introduce more heat, worsening symptoms. Deep breathing and meditation calm the Shen, breaking the cycle of obsessive thinking.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Avoid spicy, greasy, and fried foods, as well as alcohol and coffee, which add heat. Favor cooling foods like cucumber, watermelon, mung beans, and chrysanthemum tea. Practice daily meditation or deep breathing to calm the mind, and maintain a cool, quiet sleep environment. Gentle exercise like walking or tai chi can help circulate Qi without overheating.

### Phlegm Misting the Heart
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/phlegm-misting-the-heart)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Mental fog or dullness, Persistent intrusive thoughts that feel stuck, Chest stuffiness and feeling of oppression, Excessive saliva or nausea, Blank, staring expression, Thick greasy white tongue coating
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Heavy, greasy foods, Dairy and cold raw foods, Damp, humid weather, Overthinking and worry, Lack of physical activity
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Light, warm meals, Gentle exercise or movement, Dry, well-ventilated space, Mindfulness or journaling
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When the Spleen and Stomach are weak or overwhelmed, they fail to transform fluids properly, and the resulting Dampness congeals into Phlegm. This turbid Phlegm can rise and cloud the Heart orifice - much like a fog obscuring a clear window. The mind becomes dull and heavy, unable to let go of thoughts, which produces the relentless mental rumination and indecisiveness characteristic of OCD.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale swollen body with a thick white greasy coating, stiff or sluggish movement. Pulse: slippery (滑 huá), smooth and round, like beads rolling, especially strong in the middle (Guan) position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Phlegm is formed when the body's fluids are not properly transformed, often due to weak digestion or a diet heavy in damp-producing foods. Greasy, dairy, and cold foods directly increase Dampness, which congeals into Phlegm. Overthinking and a sedentary lifestyle stagnate Qi, further impairing fluid metabolism. Light, warm meals and gentle movement support the Spleen and Stomach, promoting Qi flow and drying Dampness, which helps clear the Phlegm clouding the mind. A dry environment and mental clarity practices reduce the external and internal dampness that feed the pattern.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Avoid heavy, greasy, and dairy-rich foods that create Dampness and Phlegm. Focus on light, warm, cooked meals with plenty of vegetables. Gentle daily movement like walking or qigong helps Qi circulate and prevents Phlegm from accumulating. Practice grounding activities that clear mental fog, such as journaling or mindfulness meditation.

### Liver and Heart Blood Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/liver-and-heart-blood-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Palpitations or pounding in the chest, especially when anxious, Insomnia with excessive or vivid dreaming, Pale, dull complexion and brittle, ridged nails, Dizziness and numbness or tingling in hands and feet, Obsessive thoughts that worsen with fatigue and improve with rest
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Fatigue and overwork, Emotional stress and worry, Skipping meals or eating cold, raw foods, Excessive mental strain or screen time, Heavy menstrual bleeding or blood loss
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Rest and adequate sleep, Warm, nourishing meals with red dates or bone broth, Gentle exercise or movement, Calming, low-stimulation evenings
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  The Heart houses the mind (Shen) and the Liver stores the Ethereal Soul (Hun). When Blood is deficient, neither organ receives enough nourishment to anchor the spirit. The mind becomes ungrounded and the Hun grows restless, which creates a timid, anxious disposition and a tendency toward obsessive worry. Compulsive rituals often arise as an attempt to create a sense of control, and the whole picture worsens noticeably with fatigue, because tiredness further drains the already scarce Blood.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: Pale, possibly thin body, with a thin white coating. Pulse: Fine (thin) and weak, possibly choppy; the left Cun position (Heart) may feel particularly empty.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: <p>Blood is the material foundation that anchors the mind and the Ethereal Soul. Rest and nourishing foods build Blood, which calms the Shen and steadies the Hun, making obsessive thoughts less intrusive. Fatigue, emotional strain, and blood loss all consume or deplete Blood, leaving the spirit unrooted and prone to anxiety and compulsive behaviors. Gentle movement helps circulate Qi and Blood without exhausting reserves, while overstimulation scatters what little Blood is available, worsening the cycle.</p>
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat regular, warm meals that include blood-nourishing foods like dark leafy greens, beets, black sesame, red dates, and bone broth. Avoid cold, raw foods and stimulants that scatter the mind. Prioritize gentle daily movement such as walking or tai chi, and protect your sleep as a non-negotiable time for the Blood to regenerate. Short daytime rests and calming evening rituals help prevent the mental exhaustion that fuels obsessive cycles.

### Kidney and Liver Yin Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/kidney-and-liver-yin-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Forgetfulness and poor concentration, Night sweats, Heat in the palms, soles, and chest, Dry eyes with blurred vision, Dizziness and ringing in the ears, Soreness and weakness of the lower back and knees, Insomnia with vivid, restless dreams
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Overwork and lack of sleep, Spicy, drying foods, Excessive mental strain or screen time, Anger and emotional stress
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Rest and adequate sleep, Cooling, moistening foods, Gentle exercise or movement, Quiet, dark environment
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Kidney essence is the foundation of the brain and the mind. When Kidney Yin runs low, the brain lacks proper nourishment and the mind loses its anchor, leading to forgetfulness, mental fog, and relentless obsessive thinking that feels stuck on a loop.
Liver Yin deficiency allows empty heat to rise, which disturbs the Heart’s spirit and creates a restless, anxious inner heat-especially in the evening. The combination of a malnourished brain and flaring empty fire produces the chronic, exhausting rumination and poor concentration typical of this pattern.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red, thin body with cracks, little or no coating, dry surface. Pulse: fine (thin) and rapid, often wiry; weak in the left Chi (Kidney) position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Yin is the body’s cooling, nourishing substance. Overwork, late nights, and mental strain burn through Yin, while spicy foods and emotional stress generate heat that further depletes it. Rest, cooling foods, and gentle movement allow the body to conserve and rebuild Yin, quieting the empty fire that drives obsessive thinking and restlessness.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Favor cooling, moistening foods like pear, black sesame, walnuts, goji berries, and congee. Avoid spicy, fried, or drying foods that consume Yin. Go to bed early and protect your sleep; late nights are especially depleting. Gentle, grounding practices like Tai Chi or slow walking help anchor the mind without exhausting it.

## คำถามที่พบบ่อย (Frequently Asked Questions)
### Can acupuncture really help with OCD?
Yes, many patients find acupuncture helpful for reducing the anxiety and mental tension that drive obsessive-compulsive cycles. Acupuncture works by calming the nervous system, regulating stress hormones, and moving stagnant Qi. Clinical studies on 'soothing the Liver and regulating the mind' protocols - using points like Baihui (DU-20), Shenmen (HT-7), and Taichong (LR-3) - show significant reductions in OCD symptom scores when combined with standard care. It is not a magic cure, but it can be a powerful part of a holistic treatment plan.

### How does Chinese herbal medicine address intrusive thoughts?
Herbal formulas target the specific pattern behind the thoughts. For stress-triggered looping, formulas like Chai Hu Shu Gan San move stagnant Liver Qi. For fiery, agitating thoughts, cooling formulas clear Heart Fire. For heavy, foggy rumination, herbs like Shi Chang Pu open the orifices and resolve Phlegm. The herbs work on a constitutional level to restore balance, which gradually reduces the intensity and frequency of intrusive thoughts.

### Will I need to take Chinese herbs forever?
No. The goal of TCM is to correct the underlying imbalance so that the body and mind can maintain harmony on their own. Most patients take herbs for several months, with the dosage reduced and eventually stopped as symptoms stabilize. Some people choose to use maintenance formulas during particularly stressful periods, but lifelong medication is not the aim.

### Can I combine TCM with my SSRI medication?
Yes, TCM is often used alongside SSRIs to enhance results and manage side effects. Acupuncture and herbs do not interfere with the mechanism of SSRIs. However, you should always inform both your TCM practitioner and your prescribing doctor about all treatments you are receiving. Never stop or adjust your medication without medical supervision.

### What dietary changes can support my TCM treatment for OCD?
In general, it helps to avoid stimulants like caffeine, alcohol, and spicy foods, which can agitate the Liver and Heart. Focus on warm, cooked meals that are easy to digest, such as soups, stews, and whole grains. Foods that calm the Shen include longan fruit, lily bulb, jujube dates, and mulberries. Reducing greasy, fried, or dairy-heavy foods can prevent the formation of Phlegm that clouds the mind.

### How long before I see results?
Many patients notice reduced anxiety and better sleep within the first 2-4 weeks of weekly acupuncture and daily herbs. A more substantial reduction in obsessive thoughts and compulsive urges typically takes 4-8 weeks. Chronic conditions or those with underlying deficiency may require 3-6 months of consistent treatment to rebuild the body's reserves.

## การวิจัยทางวิทยาศาสตร์สมัยใหม่ (Modern Scientific Research)
1. **Clinical observation on liver-soothing and mind-regulating acupuncture combined with long needle retention at three spirit points for obsessive-compulsive disorder**
   *วารสารวิชาการ: Shanghai Journal of Acupuncture and Moxibustion, 2023, Vol. 42, No. 13.*
   This randomized controlled trial evaluated a specific acupuncture protocol (GV20, Yintang, PC6, HT7, KI6, LR3) combined with long-duration needle retention at Sishencong, Shenting, and Benshen. The treatment group showed a significant reduction in Yale-Brown Obsessive Compulsive Scale (Y-BOCS) scores and anxiety levels compared to the sham acupuncture group, suggesting this protocol can effectively reduce both obsessive thoughts and compulsive behaviors.

2. **Clinical efficacy of Chai Hu Shu Gan San combined with sertraline for obsessive-compulsive disorder of Liver Qi Stagnation pattern**
   *วารสารวิชาการ: Chinese Journal of Integrative Medicine on Cardio-/Cerebrovascular Disease, 2020.*
   This clinical study investigated the addition of the classical formula Chai Hu Shu Gan San to standard sertraline therapy in patients diagnosed with Liver Qi Stagnation-type OCD. Results indicated that the combined therapy group achieved a higher response rate and faster reduction in obsessive-compulsive symptoms than sertraline alone, with fewer reported side effects, highlighting the potential of integrated TCM-Western treatment.
