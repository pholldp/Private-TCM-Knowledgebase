## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนปัจจุบัน (Conventional Medicine View)
### คำอธิบาย (Description)
Schizophrenia is a chronic and severe mental disorder that affects how a person thinks, feels, and behaves. It is characterized by episodes of psychosis, including hallucinations (most commonly hearing voices), delusions (fixed false beliefs), disorganized thinking and speech, and significant social or occupational dysfunction.
Negative symptoms such as blunted affect, apathy, and social withdrawal are also common. Diagnosis is made based on clinical criteria outlined in the DSM-5, and the condition affects about 1% of the population worldwide. The exact cause is unknown but involves a combination of genetic, brain chemistry, and environmental factors.

### การรักษาหลัก (Treatments)
Standard treatment relies primarily on antipsychotic medications, which are divided into first-generation (typical) and second-generation (atypical) drugs. These medications help reduce positive symptoms like hallucinations and delusions but often have limited effect on negative symptoms. Psychosocial interventions, including cognitive behavioral therapy (CBT), family education, and supported employment, are essential for long-term management. Many patients require ongoing medication to prevent relapse.

## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนจีน (Traditional Chinese Medicine View)
### ความเข้าใจเกี่ยวกับการแพทย์แผนจีน (TCM Understanding)
TCM sees schizophrenia (癫狂 diān kuáng) as a disturbance of the Shén (mind/spirit) that can manifest in two opposite poles. The manic phase (狂 kuáng) is characterized by explosive agitation, shouting, and violent behavior - a sign of excess Fire and Phlegm agitating the Heart. The depressive phase (癫 diān) involves withdrawal, mutism, and apathy, reflecting a heavy Phlegm mist that clouds the mind. These phases can alternate or blend, and the underlying patterns shift over time.
The Heart is the residence of the Shén, so any disruption to the Heart directly affects mental clarity. In TCM, Phlegm is a thick, sticky pathological fluid that can form from emotional stress or poor digestion. When Phlegm combines with Fire (from suppressed anger or frustration), it rises to harass the Heart, causing manic symptoms. When Phlegm alone accumulates without Fire, it mists the Heart, leading to depressive symptoms. This is why treatment must distinguish between hot and cold phlegm.
The Liver plays a crucial role in the early stages. Long-standing emotional frustration causes Liver Qi to stagnate and eventually generate Heat. This Heat can then condense fluids into Phlegm, setting the stage for more severe patterns. Many patients experience a prodromal phase of irritability, sleep disturbance, and chest tightness that corresponds to Liver Qi Stagnation transforming into Heat. Addressing it early can prevent progression.
In chronic cases, Blood Stagnation can develop when emotional trauma or long-term stagnation congeals the Blood in the Heart channel. This leads to fixed, bizarre delusions that feel immovable. Additionally, the Kidneys are the root of Yin and anchor the mind. When Kidney Yin is severely depleted, empty Heat blazes upward, causing a smoldering inner restlessness, night sweats, and a red peeled tongue - a pattern often seen in later stages.
Because schizophrenia involves multiple organ systems and can shift between phases, TCM treatment is dynamic. The pattern is diagnosed through a detailed analysis of symptoms, tongue, and pulse, and the treatment strategy is adjusted as the person moves through different stages. This personalized approach is one of TCM's key strengths.

### การวินิจฉัยโดยแพทย์แผนจีน (Practitioner Diagnosis)
When assessing schizophrenia (癫狂, diān kuáng), a TCM practitioner first distinguishes between the manic (狂, kuáng) and depressive (癫, diān) phases. Sudden, violent outbursts, shouting, extreme restlessness, and a red face point to Phlegm-Fire harassing the Heart (痰火扰心). The tongue is red with a thick yellow greasy coat, and the pulse feels wiry, large, and rapid. This pattern demands immediate attention because the fire is actively agitating the mind.
In contrast, the depressive phase shows a very different picture. A person with Phlegm Misting the Heart (痰迷心窍) appears withdrawn, mute, or apathetic, sometimes with hallucinations. The tongue is often pale and swollen with a thick white greasy coating, and the pulse is slippery. This pattern reflects a thick, sticky phlegm that clouds the mind’s clarity rather than setting it ablaze.
Before the dramatic flare-ups, many people experience a long build-up of emotional frustration. The Liver Qi Stagnation that transforms into Heat (肝郁化火) pattern shows irritability, chest tightness, a bitter taste in the mouth, and a red tongue with yellow coating at the sides. The pulse is wiry and rapid. This is the kindling that, if left unchecked, can condense fluids into phlegm and ignite the more severe patterns.
In chronic cases, the practitioner checks for fixed, bizarre delusions and a dark purple tongue with stasis spots. Heart Blood Stagnation (心血瘀阻) creates a choppy, wiry pulse. This pattern suggests that the flow of blood around the Heart is obstructed, trapping the Shen in a loop of rigid thinking that is hard to break.
Later in the illness, persistent restlessness, insomnia, and a red peeled tongue (little or no coat) hint at Kidney Yin Deficiency with Empty-Heat Blazing (肾阴虚虚火上炎). The pulse is thin and rapid. Here the deep reserves are depleted, and the resulting empty fire flares upward to disturb the Shen, making it hard to settle even when the acute episode passes.

### Phlegm-Fire harassing the Heart
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/phlegm-fire-harassing-the-heart)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Extreme agitation and violent outbursts, Shouting and loud speech, Flushed red face and red eyes, Copious thick yellow sticky phlegm, Bitter taste and thirst, Chest oppression with gurgling throat
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Anger and frustration, Spicy and greasy foods, Alcohol, Hot weather, Overstimulation
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Cooling foods and drinks, Quiet, dark environment, Gentle walking, Deep breathing, Avoiding arguments
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When intense emotions like anger and frustration are suppressed, they generate Fire. This Fire condenses body fluids into Phlegm, and the two combine into a thick, hot, sticky substance that rushes upward to disturb the Heart and mind. The result is the classic manic phase of schizophrenia: violent agitation, shouting, and sleeplessness.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red body, swollen with red prickly tip, thick yellow greasy coating. Pulse: slippery and rapid, left Cun position full and overflowing.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Phlegm-Fire is a combination of pathological phlegm and heat. Emotional stress, especially anger, stirs up Liver Fire which then combines with phlegm to attack the Heart. Spicy, greasy foods and alcohol directly generate more heat and phlegm, adding fuel to the fire. Cooling foods and a calm environment help clear heat and prevent the build-up of phlegm, allowing the mind to settle.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Avoid spicy, greasy, and fried foods, as well as alcohol and coffee, which add heat and generate phlegm. Emphasize cooling, light foods like mung beans, pears, bamboo shoots, and leafy greens. Maintain a calm, quiet environment and avoid overstimulation. Gentle daily movement helps Qi flow, but avoid intense exercise that stirs up heat.

### Phlegm Misting the Heart
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/phlegm-misting-the-heart)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Emotional flatness and apathy, Blank staring expression, Muttering or talking to oneself, Rattling or gurgling sound in the throat, Excessive saliva or drooling, Dizziness and heavy-headedness, Slurred or unclear speech
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Damp, greasy, or cold foods, Prolonged inactivity or oversleeping, Damp environments, Emotional suppression or loneliness
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warm, cooked meals, Gentle walking and light exercise, Clear, structured daily routine, Calm social engagement
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Phlegm is a thick, turbid fluid that forms when the body fails to transform and transport fluids properly. In this pattern, that sticky phlegm rises and clouds the Heart orifice-the seat of the mind-like a heavy mist. The result is the depressive (Dian) presentation of schizophrenia: mental dullness, blank staring, social withdrawal, and muttering. The mind is present but unable to think clearly or engage with the world, because phlegm has literally obscured its window of awareness.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale, swollen body with a thick white greasy coating; often stiff or sluggish. Pulse: slippery (Hua), especially in the middle positions.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Phlegm is a form of congealed dampness, so anything that increases dampness-greasy food, cold drinks, or a damp living space-thickens the mist. Inactivity and emotional withdrawal allow Qi to stagnate, which is the very process that generates phlegm. Warmth, gentle movement, and social connection all move Qi and help the body transform and drain phlegm, gradually lifting the cloud from the mind.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat warm, cooked, easily digestible meals and avoid damp-producing foods like dairy, greasy fried items, and raw cold salads. Gentle daily movement-such as walking or tai chi-helps Qi flow and dries internal dampness. Keep a simple, predictable routine to ground the mind, and avoid long daytime naps that can worsen sluggishness.

### Liver Qi Stagnation that transforms into Heat
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/liver-qi-stagnation-that-transforms-into-heat)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Explosive anger and irritability, Bitter taste in the mouth, Distending pain in the ribcage, Red face and eyes, Difficulty falling asleep, Restlessness and agitation
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Emotional stress and frustration, Spicy, greasy foods, Alcohol, Overwork and lack of sleep, Hot weather
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Cooling foods (celery, chrysanthemum tea), Stress reduction, Gentle exercise, Sour flavors (lemon, vinegar), Deep breathing
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When emotional frustration goes unresolved, Liver Qi gets stuck and eventually heats up, much like a pressure cooker building steam. This Heat rises to disturb the Shen (mind/spirit), causing the irritability, sleep disturbances, and restlessness that mark the early stages of schizophrenia. Over time, the Heat can condense fluids into Phlegm, which then clouds the mind or combines with Fire to produce the more severe manic or depressive episodes.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red body, redder on the sides, with a yellow coating. Pulse: wiry (弦 xián) and rapid (数 shuò).
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Emotional stress directly aggravates Liver Qi stagnation, which is the root of this pattern, so calming the mind and reducing frustration are essential. Spicy and greasy foods and alcohol generate Heat in the body, which fans the flames of stagnant Liver Qi. Cooling foods and sour flavors help clear Heat and astringe the Liver, while gentle exercise moves Qi and prevents stagnation. Adequate rest allows the Liver blood to nourish the Shen, reducing irritability and sleep disturbance.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Avoid spicy, greasy, and fried foods, as well as alcohol and coffee, which add Heat. Eat more cooling, bitter greens like dandelion and chrysanthemum. Regular gentle exercise like walking or yoga helps move Liver Qi. Practice stress management through meditation or deep breathing. Maintain a consistent sleep schedule.

### Heart Blood Stagnation
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/heart-blood-stagnation)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Stabbing chest pain in a fixed location, Dark purple tongue with stasis spots, Fixed, repetitive bizarre delusions, Purple lips and nails, Cold hands
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Cold weather, Emotional stress, Sedentary lifestyle, Greasy, heavy foods
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warmth, Gentle exercise, Rest and calm, Warm drinks
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  In chronic schizophrenia, long-standing emotional turmoil or unresolved trauma can cause Blood to congeal and stagnate within the Heart channel. This stasis physically obstructs the Heart orifice-the seat of the mind (Shen)-leading to fixed, bizarre delusions that feel as immovable as the stabbing chest pain that accompanies them.
The dark purple tongue and choppy pulse are classic signs that Blood is not flowing smoothly, depriving the mind of the nourishment it needs to stay clear and calm.

Emotional stress constrains Qi, which then fails to move Blood, so calming the mind helps unblock stasis. Greasy foods burden the Spleen and create dampness, which can further thicken the Blood.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Keep warm and avoid cold foods and drinks. Gentle daily movement like walking or tai chi helps keep blood flowing. Avoid emotional stress and overthinking, as they constrain Qi and worsen stasis.

### Kidney Yin Deficiency With Empty-Heat Blazing
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/kidney-yin-deficiency-with-empty-heat-blazing)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Five-palm heat (heat in palms, soles, and chest), Night sweats and a low-grade feverish feeling in the afternoon, Mental restlessness and irritability, worse at night, Insomnia with frequent waking, often feeling hot, Lower back soreness and weakness, Malar flush (red cheekbones) and a red, peeled tongue
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Stress and overthinking, Spicy, fried, or heating foods, Late nights and overwork, Hot, dry weather
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Rest and early nights, Cool, calm environment, Moistening foods like pears and congee
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  In schizophrenia, this pattern often appears in the later stages, when the body's deepest Yin reserves are exhausted. The Kidneys are the root of Yin and the source of cooling, anchoring energy. When Kidney Yin is severely depleted, the body loses its ability to hold the Yang steady, and empty heat flares upward along the pathway that connects the Kidneys to the Heart. This heat has no real substance but agitates the Shen, the mind-spirit, creating a chronic, smoldering restlessness. Unlike the explosive mania of phlegm-fire, this is a persistent inner heat that makes the mind unable to settle-leading to insomnia, irritability, and a sense of deep, bone-level warmth, often with night sweats. Because the Yin is too weak to anchor the Yang, the mind drifts into agitation and fragmented perception, sometimes with hallucinations or delusions that feel like a burning internal pressure rather than a clouded confusion.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red, thin, dry body with little or no coating, possibly cracked. Pulse: fine (xi) and rapid (shu), may feel floating and empty, weak at the chi (rear) position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Kidney Yin is the body's cooling reserve. Overwork, late nights, and emotional strain burn through Yin, allowing Empty Heat to flare upward and disturb the Shen. Spicy and heating foods add more fire to an already overheated system, making restlessness worse. Rest, cooling foods, and gentle movement help rebuild Yin and anchor the Yang, which calms the mind. A cool environment supports the body's effort to settle the floating heat, while early nights align with the natural cycle of Yin restoration.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat cooling, moistening foods like pears, black beans, millet, and congee. Avoid spicy, fried, and drying foods, as well as stimulants like coffee and alcohol. Prioritize rest and go to bed early-staying up late directly burns Kidney Yin. Gentle, grounding movement like Tai Chi or slow walking helps circulate Qi without overheating. Keep the bedroom cool and dark to support deep sleep.

## คำถามที่พบบ่อย (Frequently Asked Questions)
### Can TCM treat schizophrenia without medication?
No, TCM should not replace antipsychotic medication, especially during acute episodes. However, TCM can be used safely alongside conventional treatment to help manage symptoms, reduce side effects, and possibly lower medication dosages over time under medical supervision. Never stop or adjust antipsychotics without consulting your prescribing doctor.

### How does acupuncture help with hallucinations?
Acupuncture works by calming the Shen (mind) and resolving the underlying phlegm or fire that disturbs it. Points like Baihui (DU-20) and Fenglong (ST-40) are used to clear the head and transform phlegm, while Daling (PC-7) and Shenmen (HT-7) directly calm the Heart. It is not a standalone cure but part of a holistic TCM protocol that includes herbs and lifestyle adjustments.

### What dietary changes can support TCM treatment for schizophrenia?
Avoid greasy, fried, spicy, and overly sweet foods, as they generate phlegm and heat - the two main pathological factors in schizophrenia. Instead, favor light, cooked meals with plenty of vegetables, whole grains, and cooling foods like cucumber, celery, and chrysanthemum tea. If you have a Yin deficiency pattern, include moistening foods like pears, congee, and black sesame.

### How many acupuncture sessions will I need?
In the beginning, sessions are typically recommended once or twice a week for 8-12 weeks. After symptoms stabilize, the frequency may reduce to once every two weeks or monthly for maintenance. The total number of sessions depends on the pattern, severity, and your response to treatment. Your practitioner will adjust the plan as you progress.

### Can TCM help with negative symptoms like apathy and social withdrawal?
Yes, TCM directly addresses negative symptoms through patterns like Phlegm Misting the Heart and Kidney Yin Deficiency. Herbal formulas such as Di Tan Tang and Zhi Bo Di Huang Wan, along with acupuncture points that open the orifices and nourish Yin, can gradually improve alertness, motivation, and emotional responsiveness. This is an area where TCM may offer particular benefit, as conventional medications often have limited effect on negative symptoms.

### Are there any side effects from Chinese herbs?
When prescribed by a qualified TCM practitioner, herbal formulas are generally safe and well-tolerated. However, some herbs can interact with antipsychotic medications, causing increased sedation or affecting metabolism. Always inform both your TCM practitioner and psychiatrist about all treatments you are receiving. Regular monitoring of liver and kidney function is recommended when using herbs long-term.

## การวิจัยทางวิทยาศาสตร์สมัยใหม่ (Modern Scientific Research)
1. **Wendan decoction for schizophrenia** ([ลิงก์](https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD012217.pub2))
   *วารสารวิชาการ: Deng H, Xu J. Wendan decoction (Traditional Chinese medicine) for schizophrenia. Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews 2017, Issue 6. Art. No.: CD012217.*
   Cochrane systematic review assessing the effects of Wendan decoction alone or in combination with antipsychotics for people with schizophrenia. Low-quality evidence suggested that adding Wendan decoction to antipsychotics may improve mental state and reduce adverse events, but the evidence is insufficient to guide practice.

2. **Acupuncture for schizophrenia** ([ลิงก์](https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD005475.pub2))
   *วารสารวิชาการ: Shen X, Xia J, Adams CE. Acupuncture for schizophrenia. Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews 2014, Issue 10. Art. No.: CD005475.*
   Cochrane systematic review evaluating acupuncture for schizophrenia and related disorders. The review found very limited evidence that acupuncture may have some antipsychotic effects and reduce side effects, but the quality of evidence was low and no firm conclusions could be drawn.
