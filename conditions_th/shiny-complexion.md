## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนปัจจุบัน (Conventional Medicine View)
### คำอธิบาย (Description)
Western medicine views a shiny complexion as a result of excessive sebum production by the sebaceous glands, often driven by hormonal fluctuations, genetics, or environmental factors. It is commonly associated with oily skin types, acne-prone skin, and conditions like seborrheic dermatitis. Diagnosis is typically visual, and treatment focuses on topical products that reduce oil, exfoliate, or regulate hormones.

### การรักษาหลัก (Treatments)
Conventional management usually involves topical agents such as salicylic acid, benzoyl peroxide, or retinoids to reduce oil and unclog pores. Oil-free moisturizers and mattifying products are recommended. For hormonally driven oiliness, oral contraceptives or spironolactone may be prescribed. In severe cases, isotretinoin can permanently reduce sebaceous gland activity, though it carries significant side effects.

## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนจีน (Traditional Chinese Medicine View)
### ความเข้าใจเกี่ยวกับการแพทย์แผนจีน (TCM Understanding)
In TCM, the face is a mirror of the internal organs, and a shiny complexion is rarely just a skin problem. It usually signals that excessive Heat and Dampness are trapped inside and rising upward, much like steam carrying oil to the surface. The organs most often involved are the Spleen and Stomach (digestion), the Liver (stress and emotions), and the Lungs (external influences). Where the heat and dampness come from determines the pattern and the treatment.
The Spleen and Stomach are responsible for transforming food into energy and fluids. When they are overwhelmed by rich, greasy, or sweet foods, they produce Dampness and Heat, which can steam up to the face, causing a greasy, yellowish shine. This is why a heavy meal or alcohol binge can make the face look oily the next day. The same digestive weakness may also cause bloating, loose stools, and a heavy body sensation.
The Liver is closely tied to emotions. Stress, frustration, and late nights can cause Liver Qi to stagnate and generate Fire. That Fire flares upward along the Liver channel to the head and face, reddening the skin and pushing out oil. This explains why anger or a sleepless night can trigger a sudden oily shine, often with a bitter taste and irritability.
The Lungs govern the skin and body hair. When an external Wind-Heat invades (like a mild cold), it can disrupt the Lung's function and cause heat to rise to the face, producing a shiny complexion alongside a sore throat or cough. This pattern is usually acute and resolves when the cold is cleared.

### การวินิจฉัยโดยแพทย์แผนจีน (Practitioner Diagnosis)
A TCM practitioner begins by looking beyond the shine itself, asking about diet, emotions, and other bodily signs. The quality of the oiliness, accompanying symptoms, and tongue and pulse findings together reveal which internal imbalance is steaming upward to the face.
When damp-heat brews in the stomach and spleen, the complexion looks greasy, dull, and sometimes yellowish. The person often craves rich or fried foods and alcohol, and may feel heavy, bloated, or have loose stools. The tongue is red with a thick, yellow, greasy coat, and the pulse feels slippery and rapid - a classic picture of digestive damp-heat rising.
If damp-heat lodges in the liver and gallbladder, the face is oily and shiny, but the clues shift to irritability, a bitter taste in the mouth, and perhaps discomfort under the ribs. The tongue remains red with a yellow greasy coating, yet the pulse takes on a wiry quality alongside its rapid beat, pointing to the liver system rather than the stomach.
Liver fire blazing, often triggered by stress or staying up late, produces a flushed, oily shine with red eyes, a quick temper, and headaches. Here the tongue is red with a thinner yellow coat, and the pulse is wiry and rapid - more heat, less dampness. Unlike the damp-heat patterns, the greasiness may be less pronounced, but the emotional heat is unmistakable.
Wind-heat invading the lungs is a less common, more acute cause. The shiny complexion appears alongside acne, a mild sore throat, or nasal congestion. The tongue tip is red with a thin yellow coating, and the pulse feels floating and rapid. This pattern often follows a recent exposure to wind and heat, distinguishing it from the chronic internal patterns above.

## กลุ่มอาการการแพทย์แผนจีนและการรักษา (TCM Patterns and Treatment)

### Damp-Heat in Stomach and Spleen
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/damp-heat-in-stomach-and-spleen)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Oily, shiny face that looks unclean, Bloating and fullness in upper abdomen, Sticky or incomplete bowel movements, Bitter or sticky taste in mouth, Heavy feeling in body and limbs
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Greasy, fried, or spicy foods, Alcohol, Overeating or irregular meals, Damp, humid weather, Stress and worry
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Light, bland foods, Drinking barley or mung bean soup, Moderate exercise that induces light sweating
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When the Spleen and Stomach are overwhelmed by rich, greasy foods and alcohol, they fail to transport and transform fluids properly. This creates internal Dampness and Heat, which steam upward to the face like steam rising from a boiling pot. The result is a persistently oily, shiny complexion that looks unclean or greasy.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red body, possibly swollen with teeth marks, thick yellow greasy coating especially at centre and root. Pulse: soggy (Ru) and rapid (Shu), often slippery (Hua).
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Rich, greasy foods and alcohol directly generate dampness and heat in the Stomach and Spleen. Stress and worry further weaken the Spleen's ability to transform fluids, allowing dampness to accumulate. A light, bland diet and moderate sweating help the body drain excess dampness and cool internal heat, so the upward steaming that causes a shiny complexion subsides.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat a light, bland diet rich in vegetables and whole grains. Avoid greasy, fried, and spicy foods, as well as alcohol and excessive sweets. Incorporate barley, mung beans, and coix seed into meals to help drain dampness. Gentle daily exercise that makes you sweat lightly can help clear damp-heat.

### Liver and Gallbladder Damp-Heat
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/liver-and-gallbladder-damp-heat)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Bitter taste in the mouth, Pain or distension below the ribs, especially on the right side, Irritability and restlessness, Dark yellow or scanty urine
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Anger and frustration, Alcohol and greasy food, Late nights and insufficient sleep
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Cooling, light meals, Stress reduction, Regular sleep
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When damp-heat builds up in the liver and gallbladder, the turbid heat rises along the channels to the face, much like steam carrying grease. This makes the complexion look oily, shiny, and sometimes yellowish. Emotional stress, rich food, and alcohol all fuel this upward surge, leaving the skin feeling greasy and unclean.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red body, especially red at the sides, with a thick yellow greasy coating. Pulse: wiry (弦 xián), slippery (滑 huá), and rapid (数 shuò).
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Anger and emotional stress directly stir up liver Qi, which turns into fire and combines with dampness to steam upward to the face. Alcohol and greasy, spicy foods create more damp-heat in the digestive system, which then travels to the liver and gallbladder. Rest, cooling foods, and stress relief calm the liver and clear heat, preventing that greasy shine.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Cut back on alcohol, fried foods, and spicy dishes-they pour fuel on the fire. Favor cooling, dampness-clearing foods like barley, mung beans, and cucumber. Gentle exercise and stress management help keep liver Qi flowing smoothly.

### Liver Fire Blazing
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/liver-fire-blazing)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Flushed red face with oily sheen, Throbbing headache, especially at temples, Intense irritability and short temper, Bitter taste in the mouth, Red, burning, or painful eyes
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Anger and frustration, Greasy, fried, or spicy foods, Alcohol, Late nights and insufficient sleep, Hot or windy weather
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Cooling foods (mung beans, cucumber), Stress reduction, Cool environment, Regular sleep, Gentle exercise (yoga, walking)
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When emotional stress, anger, or late nights push the Liver into a state of excess Heat, that Fire flares upward along the Liver channel to the head and face. This rush of Heat reddens the skin, opens the pores, and forces out oil, creating a flushed, shiny complexion. The same Heat disturbs the mind, causing the irritability and restlessness that often accompany the breakout.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red body with redder sides, dry yellow coating, possibly with red prickles. Pulse: wiry (弦 xián), rapid, and forceful, especially in the left Guan position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Emotional stress and anger cause Liver Qi to stagnate and then surge upward as Fire, directly reddening and oiling the face. Spicy, fried, and greasy foods introduce excess Heat into the body, while alcohol is pure Heat that easily inflames the Liver. Late nights deplete the Yin that normally anchors Liver Yang, allowing Fire to flare. Cooling foods, a calm mind, and adequate sleep help clear Heat and restore balance, preventing the upward rush of Fire that causes the shiny complexion.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Avoid spicy, fried, and greasy foods, as well as alcohol and coffee, which fan the flames of Liver Fire. Prioritize cooling, light meals with plenty of green vegetables, mung beans, and chrysanthemum tea. Establish a regular sleep schedule, aiming to be asleep before 11 p.m., as the Liver detoxifies and regenerates during the night hours. Manage stress through gentle exercise like walking or yoga, and practice deep breathing when anger arises.

### Wind-Heat invading the Lungs
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/wind-heat-invading-the-lungs)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Shiny complexion with a feeling of warmth, Recent-onset cough with yellow phlegm, Sore or scratchy throat, Fever stronger than any chills, Nasal congestion with yellow mucus, Thirst and slight sweating
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Greasy, fried, or spicy foods, Hot or windy weather, Overexertion or lack of sleep
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Rest and staying indoors, Cool drinks like peppermint tea, Light, bland foods
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  The Lungs govern the skin and body hair. When Wind-Heat invades from outside, it disrupts the Lung's ability to diffuse and descend Qi. The trapped heat rises upward to the face, making the complexion appear shiny and oily.
This pattern often comes on suddenly alongside mild respiratory symptoms. The facial shine is usually accompanied by a feeling of warmth and may be worse when the person has a fever or is in a stuffy room.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red tip, thin yellow coating, body slightly red. Pulse: floating (fú) and rapid (shuò).
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Wind-Heat is an exterior pathogen that thrives on heat and agitation. Spicy food and hot environments feed the heat, while wind can drive the pathogen deeper. Rest and cooling fluids help the body's defenses push the pathogen out through the skin's surface, calming the facial shine.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Rest and stay hydrated. Drink cooling teas like chrysanthemum or peppermint. Eat light, moistening foods such as pear and congee. Avoid spicy, fried, or greasy food until the wind-heat clears.

## คำถามที่พบบ่อย (Frequently Asked Questions)
### Why does my face get so oily after eating greasy food?
In TCM, greasy, fried, or rich foods overwhelm the Spleen and Stomach, creating internal Dampness and Heat. This heat steams upward to the face, much like steam rising from a pot, leaving the skin shiny and oily. The effect often appears the next day or even a few hours after a heavy meal, and it's a clear sign that your digestion is struggling to process the fats and sugars.

### Can Chinese herbs really reduce oily skin?
Yes. Herbal formulas like Yin Chen Hao Tang and Long Dan Xie Gan Tang are designed to clear the internal Heat and Dampness that cause facial oiliness. Rather than just drying the skin, they address the root - by cooling the Liver, strengthening the Spleen, or dispelling external Wind-Heat. Many people see their skin become less greasy and more balanced within a few weeks, though results depend on the underlying pattern and consistency of treatment.

### I'm stressed and my skin breaks out - can acupuncture help?
Absolutely. Stress is a major trigger for Liver Qi stagnation, which can quickly turn into Liver Fire and cause an oily, flushed complexion. Acupuncture points like Taichong (LR-3) and Xingjian (LR-2) help soothe the Liver, reduce irritability, and calm the upward rush of heat. Regular sessions can break the cycle of stress-related breakouts and shine.

### Do I have to change my diet completely?
You don't have to be perfect, but dietary adjustments are key to lasting results. The most important step is to reduce greasy, fried, spicy, and sugary foods, as well as alcohol - these are the main culprits that generate Dampness and Heat. Adding cooling, bland foods like mung beans, cucumber, barley, and leafy greens can make a big difference. Even small, consistent changes help your herbs and acupuncture work better.

### Is it safe to combine TCM with my current skincare routine?
Generally, yes. Topical skincare products rarely interact with internal herbal medicine. However, always tell your TCM practitioner about any oral medications you take, including birth control pills or isotretinoin, as some herbs may have overlapping effects. A collaborative approach between your dermatologist and TCM practitioner is ideal for both safety and results.

### What does the tongue say about my shiny complexion?
The tongue is a key diagnostic tool. A red tongue body with a thick, yellow, greasy coating points to Damp-Heat in the Stomach or Liver. A red tip with a thin yellow coat suggests Lung Wind-Heat. A red body with redder sides and a dry yellow coat indicates Liver Fire. Your practitioner will use these signs, along with your pulse and symptoms, to pinpoint the exact pattern and prescribe the right formula.

## การวิจัยทางวิทยาศาสตร์สมัยใหม่ (Modern Scientific Research)
1. **Complementary therapies for acne vulgaris** ([ลิงก์](https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD009436.pub2))
   *วารสารวิชาการ: Cao H, Yang G, Wang Y, Liu JP, Smith CA, Luo H, Liu Y. Complementary therapies for acne vulgaris. Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews 2015, Issue 1. Art. No.: CD009436.*
   This Cochrane systematic review evaluated the effectiveness of complementary therapies, including Chinese herbal medicine, for acne vulgaris. The review found low- to moderate-quality evidence that Chinese herbal medicine may reduce inflammatory and non-inflammatory lesion counts compared to placebo or conventional treatments. The authors noted that many included studies had a high risk of bias and called for larger, well-designed RCTs. The findings indirectly support the TCM approach to treating shiny complexion, as acne and facial oiliness often share the same Damp-Heat and Fire patterns.

2. **Efficacy and safety of Chinese herbal medicine Long Dan Xie Gan Tang in insomnia: a systematic review and meta-analysis** ([ลิงก์](https://doi.org/10.1097/MD.0000000000020382))
   *วารสารวิชาการ: Li Y, Zhang J, Li L, et al. Efficacy and safety of Chinese herbal medicine Long Dan Xie Gan Tang in insomnia: a systematic review and meta-analysis. Medicine (Baltimore). 2020;99(22):e20382.*
   Although this study focused on insomnia, it provides safety and efficacy data for Long Dan Xie Gan Tang, a key formula for the Liver Fire and Liver Damp-Heat patterns that cause shiny complexion. The meta-analysis found that Long Dan Xie Gan Tang was significantly more effective than placebo or benzodiazepines for improving sleep quality, with a favourable safety profile. Its demonstrated ability to clear excess heat from the Liver system supports its traditional use for heat-related facial symptoms.
