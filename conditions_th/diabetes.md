## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนปัจจุบัน (Conventional Medicine View)
### คำอธิบาย (Description)
Diabetes mellitus is a chronic metabolic condition in which the body either doesn’t produce enough insulin or can’t use it effectively, leading to high blood sugar. The most common symptoms are excessive thirst, frequent urination, unexplained weight loss, and fatigue. Diagnosis is made through blood tests measuring fasting glucose, HbA1c, or oral glucose tolerance.
Type 2 diabetes accounts for the vast majority of cases and is strongly linked to diet, lifestyle, and genetics. Over time, poorly controlled blood sugar can damage blood vessels, nerves, kidneys, and eyes, making management a lifelong priority.

### การรักษาหลัก (Treatments)
Standard care begins with dietary changes, exercise, and weight management. When these aren’t enough, oral medications like metformin, sulfonylureas, or SGLT2 inhibitors are added to lower blood sugar. Many people eventually require insulin injections. The goal is to keep glucose levels within a target range and prevent complications.

## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนจีน (Traditional Chinese Medicine View)
### ความเข้าใจเกี่ยวกับการแพทย์แผนจีน (TCM Understanding)
TCM sees diabetes as a progressive loss of the body’s ability to manage fluids and energy. The classic name ‘wasting-thirst’ (消渴) captures the three hallmark symptoms: excessive thirst (upper wasting), ravenous hunger (middle wasting), and profuse urination (lower wasting). Each maps to a different organ system - Lung, Stomach/Spleen, and Kidney - and reflects a specific imbalance between Yin (cooling, moistening) and Yang (warming, activating).
At its core, diabetes is a disease of Yin deficiency and dry heat. Imagine a pot of water simmering on a stove; if the water (Yin) runs low, the fire (Yang) overheats the pot, creating steam and dryness. In the body, this dryness first hits the Lungs, causing unquenchable thirst. It can then scorch the Stomach, sparking insatiable hunger, or settle deep into the Kidneys, where it disrupts water metabolism and leads to frequent urination, especially at night.
Not everyone follows the same path. Some people develop diabetes from a constitutionally weak Spleen that generates dampness rather than heat, leading to bloating, fatigue, and sticky mouth rather than intense thirst. Others have long-standing Kidney Yin deficiency that eventually chills into Yang deficiency, leaving them cold and depleted. This is why the same blood sugar reading can look so different from person to person - and why TCM tailors treatment to the pattern, not just the diagnosis.

### การวินิจฉัยโดยแพทย์แผนจีน (Practitioner Diagnosis)
A TCM practitioner begins by mapping the “three polys” - excessive thirst, hunger, and urination - onto the three burners of the body. The location and quality of these symptoms, along with energy levels and temperature sensations, quickly point toward which organ system is most out of balance and whether the root is heat, deficiency, or dampness.
If intense, unrelenting thirst with a dry mouth and throat is the standout complaint, especially accompanied by a dry cough, the focus is the Lung. This upper wasting pattern reflects Lung Dryness-Heat. The tongue is red with a thin yellow coat, and the pulse feels rapid and forceful.
When extreme hunger and a burning sensation in the stomach dominate, often with bad breath or constipation, Stomach Fire is driving the middle wasting. The tongue appears red with a thick, dry, yellow coating, and the pulse is slippery and rapid - signs of heat consuming fluids in the digestive system.
For people with fatigue, bloating, heaviness, and a poor appetite - common in pre-diabetes or early type 2 diabetes - Spleen Qi deficiency with dampness is the underlying pattern. The tongue is pale and swollen, with a greasy white coating, and the pulse feels weak and soft, indicating the body is struggling to transform fluids and food.
Night thirst, frequent urination (especially at night), and lower back soreness point to the lower burner and Kidney Yin deficiency with empty-heat blazing upward. The tongue is red with little or no coating, and the pulse is thin and rapid. This is the classic lower wasting picture.
As diabetes progresses and the initial heat begins to wear down the body, Qi and Yin deficiency may emerge. The thirst is milder, but fatigue becomes profound, the mouth remains dry, and the pulse turns weak and thready. The tongue may be pale or slightly red with a scant coating.
In long-standing or severe cases, if the person feels cold, has cold limbs, and passes frequent clear urine, Kidney Yang has weakened. The tongue is pale and swollen with a white coating, and the pulse is deep and slow. This rare pattern signals a deeper collapse of the body’s warming energy.

## กลุ่มอาการการแพทย์แผนจีนและการรักษา (TCM Patterns and Treatment)

### Lung Heat
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/lung-heat)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Intense thirst with desire for cold drinks, Dry mouth and throat, Restlessness and irritability, Dark, scanty urine, Constipation or dry stools, Red face or feeling of body heat
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Spicy or greasy food, Alcohol and smoking, Hot weather, Anger and frustration
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Cooling foods and drinks, Rest and a cool environment, Gentle deep breathing
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  In the early stages of diabetes, Heat can lodge in the Lungs, the organ that distributes fluids throughout the body. This Heat acts like a fire under a kettle, boiling away the body's Yin fluids, which leaves the mouth and throat parched. The intense thirst is the body's desperate call for water to cool the Heat, and the frequent urination is a sign that the fluids are being lost before they can be used.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red body, especially at the tip and edges, with a yellow, dry coating; small red prickles may appear on the front third. Pulse: rapid (数 shu) and may be slippery (滑 hua) or overflowing (洪 hong), notably forceful at the right cun (inch) position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Lung Heat is an excess pattern driven by internal fire that consumes Yin fluids. Cooling foods and a calm environment directly counter that fire, while spicy food, alcohol, and emotional stress add more fuel. Gentle breathing helps the Lung Qi descend and spread moisture, whereas overexertion and heat trap the fire inside.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Focus on cooling, moistening foods like pear, cucumber, watermelon, and tofu. Avoid spicy, greasy, or deep-fried foods, as well as alcohol and smoking, which all add Heat. Drink small amounts of room-temperature water frequently throughout the day rather than large icy drinks. Gentle deep-breathing exercises can help the Lung Qi descend and cool the body.

### Stomach Fire (Stomach Heat)
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/stomach-fire-stomach-heat)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Ravenous hunger that returns quickly after eating, Burning sensation in the stomach area, Bad breath and swollen, bleeding gums, Thirst with desire for cold drinks, Constipation with dry, hard stools, Acid reflux or sour belching
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Spicy and fried foods, Alcohol, Emotional stress or anger, Overeating, Late-night eating
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Cooling foods (cucumber, watermelon), Drinking cold water, Small, frequent meals, Avoiding spicy and greasy foods, Rest and stress reduction
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When Stomach Fire blazes, it speeds up digestion like a furnace burning too hot, leading to ravenous hunger that returns quickly after eating. This intense heat consumes the body's fluids, causing intense thirst for cold drinks and dry mouth. The heat also dries out the intestines, resulting in constipation with hard, dry stools, and pushes foul-smelling breath upward as the Stomach's energy rises chaotically.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red body, especially in the center, with a thick dry yellow coat; possible central prickles. Pulse: rapid, slippery, and forceful, especially at the right Guan position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: <p>Stomach Fire is a pattern of excess heat. Hot, spicy, and greasy foods directly add heat to the Stomach, making the fire blaze higher. Alcohol is also heating and drying. Emotional stress and anger stir up Liver Qi, which can easily transform into fire and attack the Stomach, worsening the burning sensation and hunger. Cooling foods and cold drinks help counter the internal heat, while small meals prevent overburdening the Stomach. Rest and calm help the body's Qi flow smoothly, preventing the buildup of heat.</p>
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat cooling, easy-to-digest foods like cucumber, celery, and bitter melon. Avoid spicy, greasy, and fried foods that add heat. Eat smaller, more frequent meals to avoid overloading the Stomach. Manage stress with gentle exercise and relaxation, as emotional upset can stoke the fire.

### Spleen Deficiency with Dampness
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/spleen-deficiency-with-dampness)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Abdominal bloating worse after eating, Feeling of heaviness in the body and limbs, Loose or unformed stools, Thirst with no desire to drink, Sticky or greasy sensation in the mouth, Drowsiness, especially after meals
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Cold, raw foods, Overeating, Greasy or sweet foods, Damp weather, Sedentary lifestyle
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warm, cooked meals, Light exercise like walking, Rest after meals, Ginger tea, Dry, warm environment
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When the Spleen's Qi is weak, it cannot properly transform food into usable energy or transport fluids around the body. This leads to an accumulation of dampness that clogs the middle burner, causing the bloating, heaviness, and fatigue that often appear in pre-diabetes and early type 2 diabetes.
Because dampness obstructs the normal distribution of fluids, a person may feel thirsty but have no real desire to drink. The Spleen's failure to separate clear from turbid also sends unrefined essence downward, contributing to sweet urine and unstable blood sugar.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale, swollen body with teeth marks, white greasy coating, moist surface. Pulse: soggy (Ru) and slowed-down (Huan), soft and lacking force, especially at the right Guan position; may be slippery (Hua) if dampness is pronounced.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Spleen Qi thrives on warmth and gentle movement. Cold, raw, and greasy foods demand extra digestive fire, weakening the Spleen and encouraging dampness to accumulate. Warm, cooked foods and light exercise support the Spleen's transforming and transporting functions, helping to dry out internal dampness. Rest after meals preserves Qi for digestion, while a dry environment prevents external dampness from invading and adding to the internal load.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat warm, cooked, and easily digestible foods-think soups, congees, and stews-and avoid cold, raw, or greasy items that create dampness. Incorporate barley, adzuki beans, winter melon, and a little fresh ginger. Gentle daily movement like walking helps invigorate the Spleen and move stagnant fluids; avoid sitting for long periods, especially after meals.

### Kidney Yin Deficiency With Empty-Heat Blazing
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/kidney-yin-deficiency-with-empty-heat-blazing)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Frequent urination, especially at night, Intense thirst that worsens at night, Lower back soreness and weakness, Five-palm heat (palms, soles, chest), Tinnitus, Dry throat, especially at night, Scanty dark urine
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Overwork and late nights, Emotional stress and frustration, Spicy, fried, or greasy foods, Alcohol and coffee, Excessive sexual activity
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Rest and adequate sleep, Cooling, moistening foods, Gentle movement like tai chi, Quiet, dark environment
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  In diabetes, this pattern marks the 'lower wasting' stage. Long-standing Yin deficiency has depleted the Kidney's ability to govern water, so urine is not properly held and becomes frequent, especially at night. At the same time, the lack of Yin allows empty heat to flare upward, consuming the body's remaining fluids and driving the intense thirst and dry throat. The lower back soreness and a bone-deep sensation of heat are classic signs that the Kidney essence is weakened.

### Qi and Yin Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/qi-and-yin-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Persistent fatigue and lack of strength, Dry mouth with little desire to drink, Night sweats, Warm sensation in palms, soles, and chest, Shortness of breath on exertion, Poor appetite
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Overwork and exhaustion, Skipping meals, Spicy, fried, or drying foods, Emotional stress, Excessive sweating (e.g., sauna)
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Rest and adequate sleep, Gentle, regular exercise, Warm, easy-to-digest foods, Moistening foods like pear and congee, Stress reduction
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  In diabetes, the initial stages of Heat and Dryness consume the body's fluids and eventually weaken the Qi. As the disease progresses, the Spleen and Stomach Qi become deficient, leading to profound fatigue and poor appetite. The Yin fluids are also depleted, causing a dry mouth and throat, but the Qi deficiency weakens the urge to drink, so the thirst is not as intense as in pure Heat patterns. This dual deficiency leaves the body unable to generate enough energy or moisture, resulting in a lingering state of exhaustion and dryness.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: Red or pale-red body with a thin or absent coating; may appear cracked and dry. Pulse: Fine (thin) and weak, often rapid.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Qi and Yin are already depleted, so overwork and stress further drain them. Rest and nourishing foods help rebuild Qi and fluids. Drying foods worsen Yin deficiency, while gentle movement supports Qi without exhausting it.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat warm, cooked, and easily digestible foods like congee, steamed vegetables, and soups. Include moistening foods such as pear, lily bulb, and yam to support Yin. Avoid overwork and get plenty of rest. Gentle, consistent exercise like walking or tai chi helps build Qi without exhausting it.

### Kidney Yang Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/kidney-yang-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Cold and aching lower back and knees, Frequent clear urination, worse at night, Feeling cold all over, especially in the lower body, Deep fatigue and low spirits, Loose stools or early morning diarrhea, Pale or dark complexion
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Cold weather or drafts, Raw, cold foods and icy drinks, Overwork and staying up late, Excessive sexual activity, Prolonged standing
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warmth on the lower back, Rest and early nights, Warm cooked foods and soups, Gentle movement like tai chi, Moxibustion on the lower abdomen
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  In long-standing diabetes, the underlying Yin deficiency can eventually deplete Kidney Yang, or a person may have a constitutional weakness of the Kidney's warming power. When Kidney Yang is insufficient, the body's pilot light dims. The Kidneys can no longer properly transform and distribute fluids, so water passes straight through, causing large volumes of clear, frequent urination - especially at night. At the same time, the lack of warming Yang leaves the lower back, knees, and whole body feeling cold and achy, and the person becomes deeply fatigued.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale, puffy, and tender with teeth marks on the edges, and a wet white coating. Pulse: deep, slow, and weak, especially feeble at the rear (Chi) position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Kidney Yang is the body's warming and transforming fire. Cold, overwork, and raw foods dampen that fire, making fluid metabolism sluggish and causing cold to settle in the lower body. Warmth, rest, and gentle movement stoke the fire, helping the Kidneys transform fluids properly, reducing frequent urination and easing cold, aching limbs.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat warm, cooked foods like soups, stews, and congees. Include small amounts of warming spices such as cinnamon, ginger, and fennel. Avoid raw, cold, and frozen foods, as well as excessive cold drinks. Keep the lower back and abdomen warm, and consider applying a warm compress or moxibustion to the lower back. Gentle daily movement like walking or tai chi helps circulate Yang without exhausting it. Protect your energy by going to bed early and avoiding overwork.

## คำถามที่พบบ่อย (Frequently Asked Questions)
### Can TCM cure diabetes?
TCM does not claim to ‘cure’ diabetes in the sense of making it disappear forever, but it can help manage symptoms, improve energy, and support blood sugar control. Many people find they need less medication over time and feel significantly better. The goal is to restore balance so the body can regulate itself more effectively.

### Is it safe to take Chinese herbs with my diabetes medication?
Yes, many herbs can be safely combined with conventional drugs, but this must be done under professional guidance. Some herbs, like Coptis (Huáng Lián), have blood-sugar-lowering effects and could cause hypoglycemia if your medication dose isn’t adjusted. Always inform both your TCM practitioner and your prescribing doctor about everything you are taking.

### How quickly will I see results?
Symptom relief - less thirst, fewer hunger pangs, better energy - often appears within 2-4 weeks. Blood sugar changes may take 6-12 weeks to become evident and depend on diet, exercise, and consistency with herbs. Deep-seated patterns require months of steady treatment for lasting change.

### What foods should I avoid?
In general, avoid or minimize sweet, greasy, and spicy foods, as these generate heat and dampness. Alcohol and raw, cold foods can weaken the Spleen. Focus on warm, cooked meals with plenty of non-starchy vegetables. Bitter foods like bitter gourd and celery are especially helpful for clearing heat.

### Does acupuncture help with blood sugar control?
Research suggests acupuncture can help improve insulin sensitivity and reduce fasting blood glucose, especially when combined with lifestyle changes. It also addresses the fatigue, neuropathy, and digestive issues that often accompany diabetes. Most patients receive weekly sessions for several months.

### Can TCM prevent diabetes if I’m prediabetic?
Yes. TCM is particularly effective in the early stages, when patterns like Spleen Deficiency with Dampness or mild Lung Heat are present. Herbal formulas, acupuncture, and dietary adjustments can strengthen the Spleen, clear dampness, and nourish Yin, potentially reversing the slide toward full diabetes.

## การวิจัยทางวิทยาศาสตร์สมัยใหม่ (Modern Scientific Research)
1. **Acupuncture for diabetic peripheral neuropathy: a systematic review and meta-analysis**
   *วารสารวิชาการ: Zhang C, et al. Medicine (Baltimore). 2017;96(4):e5956.*
   This meta-analysis of 12 RCTs found that acupuncture significantly improved nerve conduction velocity and reduced pain scores compared to conventional medication, with no serious adverse events.

2. **Liuwei Dihuang Pill for diabetic nephropathy: a systematic review and meta-analysis**
   *วารสารวิชาการ: Wang X, et al. J Ethnopharmacol. 2013;149(2):513-521.*
   A meta-analysis of 15 RCTs indicated that adding Liu Wei Di Huang Wan to standard care reduced proteinuria and slowed the decline of renal function in patients with early diabetic nephropathy.

3. **Chinese herbal medicine for type 2 diabetes mellitus** ([ลิงก์](https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD006442.pub2))
   *วารสารวิชาการ: Liu JP, et al. Cochrane Database Syst Rev. 2009;(3):CD006442.*
   This Cochrane review included 16 RCTs and found that some Chinese herbal medicines may have a hypoglycemic effect, but the evidence was insufficient to recommend any specific formula due to methodological flaws.
