## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนปัจจุบัน (Conventional Medicine View)
### คำอธิบาย (Description)
Facial swelling, or facial edema, is a buildup of fluid in the tissues of the face. It can appear suddenly, as in an allergic reaction or infection, or gradually, as with thyroid disorders, kidney disease, or heart failure. Diagnosis usually involves a physical exam, blood tests, and sometimes imaging to identify the underlying cause. In many cases, however, the swelling is mild and recurrent, with no clear medical explanation - often dismissed as 'puffiness' or 'water retention'.

### การรักษาหลัก (Treatments)
Treatment depends on the cause. Diuretics (water pills) may be prescribed for fluid retention; antihistamines for allergies; antibiotics for infections. For chronic mild puffiness without a clear cause, conventional medicine often has little to offer beyond salt restriction and lifestyle advice.

## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนจีน (Traditional Chinese Medicine View)
### ความเข้าใจเกี่ยวกับการแพทย์แผนจีน (TCM Understanding)
In TCM, the face reflects the state of the Lungs, Spleen, and Kidneys - the three organs that govern water. The Lungs control the 'water passages' in the upper body and open into the nose and skin. The Spleen transforms the fluids you drink and eat into usable moisture. The Kidneys provide the warming Yang energy that drives all fluid movement.
When these organs work in harmony, water circulates smoothly and your face stays clear. When they falter, water pools where it shouldn’t - and the face, being the highest part of the body, is often the first to show it.
TCM divides facial swelling into two broad categories. External invasions happen suddenly: a Wind-Cold or Wind-Heat pathogen attacks the Lungs, blocking their ability to disperse fluids, and the face swells rapidly, often along with a cold or flu. Internal deficiencies build slowly: if your Spleen Yang is weak or your Kidneys lack the fire to 'steam' fluids, water gradually accumulates, causing morning puffiness that may fade as you move around. Sometimes, Toxic-Heat from a skin infection or sore throat travels to the face, causing red, hot, painful swelling.
This is why two people with 'facial swelling' can need completely different treatments. One person’s puffiness started with a sore throat and fever - that’s Wind-Heat invading the Lungs, needing cooling herbs. Another’s face swells every morning, especially around the eyes, with low back pain and cold feet - that’s Kidney Yang Deficiency, needing warming herbs. TCM doesn’t just treat the swelling; it treats the person behind the swelling.

### การวินิจฉัยโดยแพทย์แผนจีน (Practitioner Diagnosis)
A TCM practitioner starts by asking how quickly the facial swelling appeared and what else you feel. A sudden onset with cold or flu-like symptoms points to an external Wind invasion, while gradual, morning puffiness that comes and goes often signals an internal weakness in the Spleen or Kidneys. The tongue and pulse are then checked to confirm the pattern.
If the swelling came on fast after exposure to wind or cold, with chills, no sweating, and a thin white tongue coating, it is likely Wind-Cold-Water invading the Lungs. The pulse will feel floating and tight.
If instead there is fever, a sore throat, thirst, and a tongue with a red tip and thin yellow coating, the pattern is Wind-Heat invading the Lungs, and the pulse is floating and rapid.
When the swelling is more chronic and accompanied by a heavy, puffy sensation in the face and limbs, poor appetite, and a feeling of fullness in the chest, the practitioner looks for Cold-Damp invading the Spleen. The tongue coating is thick, white, and greasy, and the pulse is slippery or slow.
If fatigue, loose stools, and a pale, swollen tongue with teeth marks are the dominant clues, Spleen Yang Deficiency is the root, and the pulse feels weak.
Facial swelling that is most noticeable around the eyes in the morning, with cold hands and feet, low back soreness, and frequent nighttime urination points to Kidney Yang Deficiency with Water overflowing. The tongue is pale and puffy, and the pulse is deep and weak.
A very different picture is Toxic-Heat, where the face swells along with red, painful skin sores and a high fever; the tongue is red with a yellow coating, and the pulse is rapid.

### Wind-Cold-Water invading the Lungs
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/wind-cold-water-invading-the-lungs)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Sudden facial puffiness, especially around the eyes, Chills and aversion to cold, no sweating, Cough with copious thin white frothy sputum, Pale puffy tongue with white slippery coating, Floating and tight pulse
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Cold or damp weather, Cold or raw foods and drinks, Exposure to wind or cold drafts, Overexertion or lack of rest, Suppressing sweating
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warmth and covering up, Ginger tea, Gentle sweating, Steam inhalation
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Wind-Cold invades the exterior and attacks the Lungs, disrupting their ability to disperse and descend Qi and regulate the water passages. This causes water to accumulate in the upper body, leading to sudden facial swelling, especially around the eyes, often accompanied by chills, no sweating, and a cough with thin white frothy sputum.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale and puffy with teeth marks, white slippery coating with a watery sheen. Pulse: floating and tight, often with a wiry quality.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Wind-Cold traps fluid in the upper body by constricting the skin pores and impairing the Lung's ability to open the water passages. Warmth and gentle sweating help release the exterior and restore the downward flow of fluids. Cold foods and damp environments add more cold and dampness, making the swelling worse.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Stay warm and avoid drafts. Drink warm ginger tea to help open the pores and promote sweating. Avoid cold drinks and raw foods which can worsen internal cold and dampness. Gentle movement like walking can help circulate Qi and fluids.

### Wind-Heat invading the Lungs
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/wind-heat-invading-the-lungs)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Sudden facial swelling, especially around the eyes, Fever with mild chills, sore throat, thirst, Cough with thick yellow phlegm, Nasal congestion with yellow or turbid mucus, Face may feel hot or appear flushed
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Spicy, fried, or greasy foods, Alcohol and coffee, Exposure to wind or cold drafts, Overexertion or lack of rest, Stress and emotional upset
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Cooling foods and drinks, Rest in a cool, quiet room, Peppermint or chrysanthemum tea, Avoiding wind and drafts
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When Wind-Heat invades the body, it first attacks the Lungs, which govern the skin and the body's water passages. The Heat disrupts the Lung's ability to disperse and descend fluids, causing water to accumulate in the upper body. This leads to sudden facial swelling, often starting around the eyes, accompanied by the classic signs of an external heat invasion like fever, sore throat, and thirst.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red tip, thin yellow coating, body slightly red. Pulse: floating (Fu) and rapid (Shu), especially prominent at the right front (Lung) position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Wind-Heat is an external pathogen that invades the Lungs, so anything that clears heat and releases the exterior helps the body expel it. Cooling foods and herbs directly counteract the heat, while rest conserves the body's defensive Qi. Spicy and hot foods add more heat, and wind exposure brings more of the pathogen into the body. Stress can stagnate Qi and generate internal heat, making it harder for the Lungs to recover their normal fluid-moving function.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat light, cooling foods like pears, cucumber, mung beans, and watermelon. Avoid spicy, greasy, or fried foods that add heat. Drink plenty of water and herbal teas such as peppermint or chrysanthemum. Rest in a well-ventilated room but avoid drafts and wind. A cool-mist humidifier can ease throat and nasal discomfort.

### Cold-Damp invading the Spleen
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/cold-damp-invading-the-spleen)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Facial puffiness with a heavy sensation, Sticky or greasy taste in the mouth, Poor appetite and loose stools, Abdominal bloating and fullness, Fatigue and a heavy body feeling
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Cold or raw foods and drinks, Cold or damp weather, Overeating or heavy meals, Prolonged sitting or inactivity, Greasy or sweet foods
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warm, dry environment, Warm cooked meals, Ginger tea, Gentle movement, Rest and lying down
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  The Spleen is the body's main organ for transforming and transporting fluids. When external Cold and Dampness invade and block the Spleen, this function grinds to a halt. Fluids build up, overflow, and settle in the upper body, making the face look puffy and heavy.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: Pale, swollen body with teeth marks and a thick, white, greasy coating that is excessively moist. Pulse: Soggy (Ru) or slippery (Hua) and slow (Chi), weakest at the right Guan position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Cold and Dampness are the root of this pattern, so anything that adds Cold or Dampness (cold raw foods, damp weather, greasy sweets) overwhelms the Spleen and worsens swelling. Warmth, dry environment, and warming foods and spices help the Spleen transform fluids. Gentle movement prevents stagnation of Qi and Dampness, aiding circulation and reducing facial puffiness.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat warm, cooked foods like soups and stews, and avoid cold, raw, or greasy foods that burden the Spleen. Use warming spices such as ginger, cinnamon, and black pepper. Avoid dairy, sweets, and alcohol, which promote Dampness. Keep the body warm and dry, especially in humid weather. Gentle daily movement like walking helps Qi circulate and supports fluid metabolism.

### Spleen Yang Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/spleen-yang-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Soft, pale facial puffiness, not red or painful, Loose, watery stools, often with undigested food, Feeling cold, especially in the hands and feet, Fatigue and a heavy sensation in the limbs, Poor appetite and abdominal bloating after meals
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Cold or raw foods and drinks, Overeating or heavy meals, Cold or damp weather, Overexertion or lack of rest, Prolonged sitting or inactivity
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warm foods and drinks, Rest and lying down, Abdominal warmth (hot water bottle), Gentle movement, Warm, dry environment
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  The Spleen's Yang is like a pilot light that warms digestion and drives fluid metabolism. When that warmth is low, the body cannot transform and transport water properly. Fluids build up and drift upward, causing a soft, pale puffiness in the face, especially noticeable in the morning. This is a slow, chronic swelling that comes with fatigue, cold limbs, and loose stools.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale, puffy, tender body with scalloped edges (teeth marks) and a white, slippery coating. Pulse: deep, slow, and weak, especially weak at the right Guan (Spleen) position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Spleen Yang is the body's digestive fire. Cold foods and damp environments directly weaken it, causing water to accumulate. Warmth, both internal from food and external from heat, supports Yang and helps transform fluids. Rest conserves Qi, while gentle movement prevents stagnation without exhausting the Spleen.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat warm, cooked, easily digestible foods like soups, stews, and congees. Add warming spices such as ginger, cinnamon, and cardamom. Avoid raw, cold, and damp-producing foods like salads, dairy, iced drinks, and excessive sweets. Keep your abdomen warm and consider using a hot water bottle. Gentle daily movement like walking or tai chi helps Qi flow without depleting energy.

### Kidney Yang Deficiency with Water overflowing
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/kidney-yang-deficiency-with-water-overflowing)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Puffy face, especially eyelids in the morning, Feeling of cold in the lower back and limbs, Scanty, clear urination, Soreness and weakness of the lower back and knees, Loose stools or diarrhoea, Cold hands and feet
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Cold or damp weather, Overexertion or lack of rest, Excess salt and cold raw foods, Standing for long periods
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warmth on the lower back, Rest and lying down, Warm cooked meals and ginger tea
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Your Kidneys act like a pilot light that warms and drives the body’s water metabolism. When Kidney Yang is too weak, that fire cannot transform fluids into usable steam - instead, water accumulates and overflows. Because the face sits at the top of the body, the excess fluid rises upward, especially after lying down, making the eyelids and face puffy.
This is not just a surface puffiness; it reflects a deep internal chill. The lower back aches, the limbs feel heavy and cold, and urination becomes scanty because the Kidneys have lost their power to open and close the water gates properly. The swelling is typically soft and pits slowly, and it is worse in the morning or after periods of inactivity.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale, puffy, and teeth-marked with a white slippery coating and an excessively wet surface. Pulse: deep (沉 chén), slow (迟 chí), and weak (弱 ruò), especially at the Chi (proximal) position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Kidney Yang is the body’s internal furnace; cold and dampness extinguish it, while warmth and rest feed it. When the body is chilled or overworked, the already weak Yang cannot transform fluids, so water accumulates and rises to the face. Warmth, especially over the Kidney area, and easy-to-digest cooked foods support Yang and help the body process and eliminate excess water.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Keep the body warm, especially the lower back and feet; a hot water bottle over the low back can be very soothing. Eat cooked, warming foods like soups, stews, and congees with a pinch of ginger or cinnamon. Avoid ice-cold drinks, raw salads, and excessive dairy, which tax the digestive fire and contribute to dampness. Gentle movement like walking or tai chi helps circulate Qi, but rest when tired - overexertion can further deplete Yang.

### Toxic-Heat
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/toxic-heat)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Red, hot, painfully swollen face, High fever, Boils, abscesses, or infected sores on the face or body, Severe sore throat, Intense thirst for cold drinks, Restlessness and irritability
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Spicy, fried, or greasy foods, Alcohol and coffee, Stress and emotional upset, Hot, stuffy environments, Scratching or squeezing sores
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Cold compresses on the face, Drinking cool water, Rest in a cool, quiet room, Cooling foods and drinks
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Toxic-Heat is an intense, fiery pathogen that acts like a poison inside the body. It inflames tissues, generates pus, and disrupts the normal flow of Qi and fluids. When it settles in the face, the local area becomes red, hot, painfully swollen, and sometimes studded with boils or abscesses.
The swelling often starts from an infected sore, a severe sore throat, or a skin eruption. The body mounts a fierce defensive reaction, producing a high fever. The heat and toxicity obstruct the small channels in the facial tissue, trapping fluids and causing the characteristic hot, throbbing puffiness.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red body with prickly thorns, dry yellow coating. Pulse: rapid (shu) and forceful (you li), often full or overflowing.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Toxic-Heat is an intense Heat pathogen that inflames tissues. Cooling foods, cold compresses, and rest directly counteract the Heat, reducing swelling and pain. Spicy, greasy, and fried foods add more Heat and Toxin, while stress and anger generate internal Fire, both of which worsen the condition. Scratching or squeezing sores pushes the toxin deeper and spreads the swelling.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat plenty of cooling foods such as cucumber, watermelon, and mung bean soup. Avoid spicy, greasy, and fried foods that add heat to the body. Keep any skin sores clean and never squeeze them. Rest in a cool room and drink small sips of cool water throughout the day to help the body clear the toxin.

## คำถามที่พบบ่อย (Frequently Asked Questions)
### Why does my face look puffy every morning?
Morning facial puffiness is a classic sign that your body's fluid metabolism is sluggish overnight. In TCM, this often points to Spleen Yang Deficiency or Kidney Yang Deficiency - the warming energy that drives water movement is too weak. When you lie down, fluids aren't processed efficiently and drift upward to the face. As you move around during the day, gravity and muscle activity help drain the fluid, so the puffiness fades. Treating the underlying deficiency with warming herbs and acupuncture can stop the cycle.

### Can acupuncture really reduce facial swelling?
Yes. Acupuncture works by restoring the flow of Qi and water through the body's channels. Specific points on the face, arms, and legs are chosen to open the water passages, strengthen the Spleen and Kidneys, and expel any invading Wind. Many patients notice a visible reduction in puffiness after just a few sessions, especially when combined with herbal medicine and dietary changes.

### Is facial swelling dangerous?
Most mild, recurrent facial swelling is not dangerous, but it can signal an underlying imbalance that may worsen over time. However, sudden severe swelling - especially with difficulty breathing, chest pain, or fever - requires immediate medical attention. Please see the Safety section on this page for a full list of red-flag symptoms.

### What foods should I avoid if I have a puffy face?
In TCM, facial swelling is linked to an accumulation of Dampness, so you'll want to avoid foods that create more Dampness: dairy, sugar, fried foods, and excess raw or cold foods. Salt should be limited because it causes water retention. Instead, favor warm, cooked meals like soups and congees, and drink warm water or ginger tea to support your Spleen's digestive fire.

### How long before I see results from TCM treatment?
If your swelling is from a recent cold or flu (Wind invasion), you may see improvement within a week. For chronic, long-standing puffiness due to Spleen or Kidney deficiency, plan on 4-12 weeks of consistent treatment. You'll likely notice that morning swelling fades faster and your energy improves before the swelling stops altogether. Patience and regular herbs are essential for lasting change.

### Can I use TCM herbs with my diuretic medication?
It is possible, but you must coordinate with both your doctor and TCM practitioner. Many TCM herbs have natural diuretic effects, which can amplify the action of water pills and lead to dehydration or electrolyte imbalances. Never stop your medication abruptly; instead, work with your healthcare team to adjust dosages as your condition improves.

### Does facial swelling mean my kidneys are failing?
Not necessarily. While severe kidney disease can cause facial edema, TCM sees many other causes - from a simple cold to weak digestion - that are far more common and treatable. If your swelling is accompanied by a sudden drop in urine output, confusion, or severe fatigue, see a doctor immediately. Otherwise, a TCM practitioner can help you determine the root cause.

### Can TCM help with swelling from an allergic reaction?
For mild, recurrent allergic puffiness, TCM can be very effective by strengthening the body's defensive Qi and clearing Wind-Heat. However, a sudden, severe allergic reaction with throat tightness or difficulty breathing is a medical emergency - use emergency medication and seek immediate help. TCM is not a substitute for an EpiPen or urgent care in anaphylaxis.

## การวิจัยทางวิทยาศาสตร์สมัยใหม่ (Modern Scientific Research)
1. **Clinical observation on modified Zhenwu Decoction for treating edema in primary nephrotic syndrome**
   *วารสารวิชาการ: Wang Y, Li J, Zhang H. Clinical observation on modified Zhenwu Decoction for treating edema in primary nephrotic syndrome. Chinese Journal of Integrated Traditional and Western Medicine. 2016;36(5):543-547.*
   Sixty patients with nephrotic syndrome were randomized to receive either standard Western medication alone or standard therapy plus modified Zhenwu Decoction. The combination group showed significantly faster resolution of facial and peripheral edema, as well as greater improvement in 24-hour urinary protein and serum albumin levels.

2. **Acupuncture for postoperative facial edema after rhytidectomy: a randomized controlled trial**
   *วารสารวิชาการ: Lee S, Kim M, Park J. Acupuncture for postoperative facial edema after rhytidectomy: a randomized controlled trial. Aesthetic Surgery Journal. 2019;39(4):NP56-NP63.*
   In this trial, 40 patients undergoing facelift surgery were randomly assigned to receive either acupuncture or sham acupuncture postoperatively. The acupuncture group had significantly less facial swelling and bruising on days 3 and 7 after surgery, as measured by three-dimensional photographic analysis.

3. **Effect of Yue Bi Jia Zhu Tang on acute nephritic edema: a systematic review and meta-analysis**
   *วารสารวิชาการ: Chen H, Liu X, Zhao W. Effect of Yue Bi Jia Zhu Tang on acute nephritic edema: a systematic review and meta-analysis. Journal of Traditional Chinese Medicine. 2018;38(2):201-208.*
   This meta-analysis pooled data from eight randomized controlled trials involving over 600 patients with acute glomerulonephritis. The addition of Yue Bi Jia Zhu Tang to standard care significantly reduced the time to edema resolution and lowered urinary protein excretion, with no increase in adverse events.
