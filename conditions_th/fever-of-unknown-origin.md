## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนปัจจุบัน (Conventional Medicine View)
### คำอธิบาย (Description)
In conventional medicine, fever of unknown origin is defined as a temperature above 38.3°C (101°F) on several occasions, lasting for at least three weeks, with no clear cause after one week of intensive hospital investigation. It is a diagnostic challenge that often involves extensive blood work, imaging, and sometimes invasive biopsies. Common underlying causes include hidden infections, autoimmune conditions, and malignancies, but in a significant number of cases no cause is ever found.
When all tests come back negative, patients are often left with the label of "idiopathic" fever and few treatment options beyond antipyretics or watchful waiting. The fever itself can be debilitating, causing fatigue, night sweats, and anxiety, yet the conventional framework offers little guidance when the root cause remains elusive.

### การรักษาหลัก (Treatments)
When a specific cause is identified, treatment targets that underlying condition - antibiotics for infection, immunosuppressants for autoimmune disease, or cancer-directed therapy. For undiagnosed cases, management is often limited to antipyretic medications like acetaminophen or ibuprofen to reduce fever. In some instances, a trial of corticosteroids may be used if an inflammatory process is suspected, but this carries risks and can mask the true diagnosis.

## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนจีน (Traditional Chinese Medicine View)
### ความเข้าใจเกี่ยวกับการแพทย์แผนจีน (TCM Understanding)
In TCM, a fever is not just a number on a thermometer - it is a sign that the body's internal balance has been disturbed. Instead of looking for a single pathogen, TCM examines the quality, timing, and triggers of the fever to understand which organ systems are out of harmony. A fever that rises in the afternoon and brings night sweats points to a very different root than one that flares with exhaustion or emotional stress.
One of the most common patterns in FUO is Empty-Heat caused by Yin Deficiency. Think of Yin as the body's cooling, moistening fluid. When it runs low - from chronic overwork, prolonged illness, or simply aging - the relative cooling is lost, and a low-grade heat emerges from within. This heat is not a raging fire but a subtle, persistent warmth that worsens in the afternoon and evening, often accompanied by a dry throat and a feeling of heat in the palms and soles.
Another major cause is Qi Deficiency Fever. When the body's vital energy, especially the Spleen's ability to generate clear Yang, is weak, the Yang Qi can sink and become trapped, creating a low-grade fever that flares up after physical or mental exertion. This is a fever of depletion, not of excess - the body is simply too tired to keep its heat in check.
Emotional stress creates a different kind of heat. Unresolved frustration or anger can cause Liver Qi to stagnate, and over time that stuck energy generates heat, much like friction. This pattern produces an intermittent fever that comes and goes with stress, often with irritability and a bitter taste in the mouth.
When a pathogen gets stuck between the exterior and interior - the Lesser Yang stage - the body's defensive Qi and the pathogen wrestle back and forth, causing the classic alternating chills and fever. This half-exterior, half-interior struggle often also brings a bitter taste and a sense of fullness in the ribs.
Sometimes, after an illness that never fully resolved, the protective shield (Wei Qi) becomes uncoordinated with the deeper nutritive Qi, leading to a lingering low-grade fever with spontaneous sweating and an aversion to drafts - a pattern called Ying-Wei Disharmony.
When both Qi and Yin are depleted, the body loses its ability to anchor heat and stay balanced. Qi deficiency fails to hold the Yang energy in its proper place, while Yin deficiency leaves the body dry and overheated. The result is a low-grade fever that flares with exertion, often in the afternoon, with night sweats, fatigue, and a weak, thin pulse.

### การวินิจฉัยโดยแพทย์แผนจีน (Practitioner Diagnosis)
A TCM practitioner begins by asking about the timing and character of the fever. An afternoon or evening rise that feels like heat in the palms and soles, with night sweats and a dry throat, points strongly toward Empty-Heat from Yin Deficiency. The tongue appears red with little coating, and the pulse feels thin and rapid.
If the fever is low-grade and clearly worsens after physical exertion or long days, Qi Deficiency Fever is suspect. The person often looks pale, feels exhausted, and has a poor appetite. The tongue is pale and puffy, and the pulse is weak and forceless, reflecting the body’s inability to anchor heat due to depleted Qi.
When emotional stress is a clear trigger, and the fever comes and goes with irritability, a sense of chest tightness, and frequent sighing, the pattern is Liver Qi Stagnation turning into Heat. The tongue may be slightly red on the sides, and the pulse feels wiry like a guitar string, indicating constrained Liver energy.
A very distinctive clue is alternating chills and fever - feeling hot one moment and chilled the next - often with a bitter taste in the mouth and a dry throat. This is the Lesser Yang stage, a half-exterior, half-interior pattern. The pulse is typically wiry, and the tongue coating is thin and white, showing the struggle between the body’s defensive energy and a lingering pathogen.
If the fever lingers with spontaneous sweating that doesn’t break the fever, and the person is unusually sensitive to drafts, Ying-Wei Disharmony is likely. This pattern often follows an illness that was never fully resolved. The tongue may look normal or slightly pale, and the pulse is often floating and weak, reflecting the disharmony between the nutritive and defensive layers.
During recovery from a long illness, when both energy and fluids are depleted, a low-grade fever may appear alongside profound fatigue and a dry mouth. This is Qi and Yin Deficiency. The tongue is often red with a thin or no coating, and the pulse is weak and thin. The fever is usually mild but persistent, and the person feels drained rather than simply hot.

### Empty-Heat caused by Yin Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/empty-heat-caused-by-yin-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Low-grade afternoon or evening fever, Night sweats, Heat sensation in palms, soles, and chest, Dry mouth and throat, thirst for small sips, Flushed cheekbones (malar flush), Restlessness and difficulty sleeping, Red, thin, dry tongue with little or no coating
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Stress and emotional upset, Spicy, greasy, or fried foods, Overwork and overexertion, Hot, dry weather, Excessive sweating (sauna, intense exercise)
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Rest and sleep, Cool environment, Moistening foods (pear, lily bulb, black sesame), Small sips of water throughout the day, Gentle movement (tai chi, walking), Humidifier in dry air
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  In TCM, your body's cooling, moistening Yin fluids act like a radiator fluid. When those fluids are depleted - often from chronic stress, overwork, or prolonged illness - the relative cooling is lost, and a subtle, low-level heat emerges from within. This heat is not a raging fire from an infection; it's a simmering, empty heat that tends to surface in the afternoon and evening, when Yin is naturally meant to be dominant but can't keep the heat down. That's why you feel hot in the palms, soles, and chest, and why the heat disrupts sleep and causes night sweats.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red, thin, dry with cracks, little or no coating. Pulse: fine and rapid (thin, fast), weak on deep pressure.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Yin is the body's cooling, moistening energy. When it's depleted, the relative heat flares up, especially in the afternoon and evening. Activities that consume Yin - like staying up late, overworking, or eating spicy foods - make the heat worse. Rest, cooling foods, and gentle movement replenish Yin and help the body's internal thermostat settle down.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat moistening, cooling foods like pear, lily bulb, black sesame, and tofu. Avoid spicy, greasy, and drying foods. Prioritize rest and avoid late nights, which consume Yin. Gentle exercise like tai chi or walking can help, but avoid overexertion. Keep your environment cool and use a humidifier if the air is dry.

### Qi Deficiency Fever
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/qi-deficiency-fever)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Low-grade fever that worsens after physical or mental exertion, Profound fatigue and lack of strength, Pale or sallow complexion, Poor appetite and loose stools, Spontaneous sweating without exertion, Reluctance to speak due to lack of energy
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Stress and emotional upset, Cold, raw, or greasy foods, Overwork and overexertion, Chronic illness or prolonged stress
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Rest and sleep, Warm, easily digestible foods, Gentle movement (tai chi, walking), Warmth and avoiding chills
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When the body's Qi is weak, especially the Spleen's ability to generate clear Yang Qi, the Yang Qi can sink downward and become trapped. This stagnation creates a low-grade fever that flares up whenever you push yourself physically or mentally. It is a sign that your body is struggling to lift and distribute energy, not a sign of an external infection.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale, puffy body with teeth marks and a thin white coating. Pulse: weak (虚 ruò) or fine (细 xì), and may feel large superficially but collapses under pressure (a hallmark of Qi deficiency fever).
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Qi deficiency means the body's energy is low, and overexertion depletes it further, causing the fever to flare. Rest and nourishing foods rebuild Qi, stabilizing the body's temperature. Warmth supports Yang Qi, while cold foods and overwork drain it. Gentle movement helps Qi flow without exhausting the reserves.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat warm, easily digestible foods like congee, stews, and root vegetables. Avoid raw, cold, and greasy foods that damage Spleen Qi. Prioritize rest and avoid overexertion. Gentle movement like walking or Qi Gong supports Qi circulation without draining it further.

### Liver Qi Stagnation that transforms into Heat
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/liver-qi-stagnation-that-transforms-into-heat)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Intermittent low-grade fever, often triggered by stress, Irritability and explosive anger, Bitter taste in the mouth, Distending pain in the ribcage area, Red face and eyes
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Stress and emotional upset, Spicy, greasy, or fried foods, Alcohol, Overwork and overexertion
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Stress management and relaxation, Cooling foods and drinks (e.g., chrysanthemum tea), Gentle movement (tai chi, walking), Expressing emotions openly, Cool environment
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Unresolved emotional stress causes the Liver Qi to become stuck and sluggish. Over time, this trapped energy generates heat, much like friction creates warmth. That internal heat rises and disturbs the mind, producing an intermittent low-grade fever that often flares during periods of frustration or tension.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red body, redder at the sides, with a yellow coating. Pulse: wiry and rapid (弦数 xián shuò), especially forceful at the left middle (Guan) position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Emotional stress and anger are the primary triggers because they directly cause Liver Qi to stagnate, which then transforms into heat. Spicy, greasy foods and alcohol add more heat to the body, worsening the fever and irritability. Stress management, gentle movement, and cooling foods help to smooth Liver Qi, clear heat, and calm the mind, directly counteracting the underlying mechanism.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Avoid spicy, greasy, and fried foods, as well as alcohol, which add heat. Sip chrysanthemum or peppermint tea to gently cool the Liver. Make time for gentle, rhythmic movement like walking or Tai Chi to keep Qi flowing. Most importantly, find a healthy outlet for pent-up emotions-journaling, talking with a friend, or deep breathing can prevent stagnation from building into heat.

### Lesser Yang stage
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/lesser-yang-stage)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Alternating chills and fever, Bitter taste in the mouth, Fullness and tightness along the ribs, Irritability and restlessness, Nausea or loss of appetite
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Stress and emotional upset, Spicy, greasy, or fried foods, Exposure to wind and drafts, Overexertion or irregular eating
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Rest and sleep, Warm, easily digestible foods, Warm compress on the ribs, Gentle movement (tai chi, walking), Stress management and relaxation
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When a pathogen gets stuck between the exterior and interior of the body, it lodges in the Lesser Yang channels of the Gallbladder and Triple Burner. The body’s defensive Qi and the pathogen wrestle back and forth, so the fever cannot break free or settle - it rises when the struggle intensifies and falls when the pathogen retreats, producing the classic alternating chills and fever. Because the Gallbladder channel governs the sides of the body and influences digestion, the heat also disturbs the mouth, throat, and rib area, leaving a bitter taste and a sense of fullness under the ribs.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: normal or slightly red body, sometimes with red sides; thin white or thin yellow coating. Pulse: wiry (弦 xián) and fine.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Emotional stress, especially anger and frustration, stagnates Liver Qi, which directly worsens the Gallbladder-related constraint of the Lesser Yang stage. Greasy and spicy foods generate damp-heat that further clogs the half-exterior half-interior pathways. Rest, gentle movement, and warm light meals support the Spleen and help Qi flow smoothly, allowing the body to push the pathogen out without exhausting itself. A stable, calm environment and protection from drafts prevent the external factor from deepening, while avoiding heavy exertion preserves the Qi needed to resolve the alternating fever.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat light, warm, and easily digested foods like congee and steamed vegetables. Avoid greasy, fried, or spicy dishes that can create dampness and heat. Keep your body comfortably warm and shield yourself from drafts, especially around the neck and ribs. Gentle daily movement like walking or stretching helps Qi flow, but avoid heavy exertion. Emotional steadiness is key - short breathing exercises or quiet time can prevent frustration from aggravating the pattern.

### Ying-Wei Disharmony
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/ying-wei-disharmony)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Spontaneous sweating that worsens with light activity, Aversion to wind and drafts, Intermittent mild fever or alternating chills and mild heat, Fatigue and feeling easily tired, Slight thirst without desire to drink much
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Exposure to wind and drafts, Overwork and overexertion, Cold, raw, or greasy foods, Stress and emotional upset, Inadequate rest after an illness
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Rest in a warm, draft-free room, Warm, easily digestible foods, Gentle movement (tai chi, walking), Wearing a scarf or covering the neck, Moxibustion on the lower abdomen or back
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Your body's protective shield, called Defensive Qi (Wei Qi), normally stays on the surface to keep out wind and regulate sweating. When this shield is weakened by an illness that never fully resolved, it becomes uncoordinated with the deeper Nutritive Qi (Ying Qi). The Defensive Qi can no longer hold the pores closed, so you sweat spontaneously and feel chilly in drafts. At the same time, the Nutritive Qi cannot circulate smoothly, causing an intermittent low-grade fever that comes and goes as the body tries to rebalance itself.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale-red or slightly pale body with a thin, white, moist coating. Pulse: floating and moderate (浮缓 fú huǎn), sometimes floating and weak.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Wind and drafts directly challenge the already weak Defensive Qi, worsening the disharmony between the surface and the interior. Rest and warmth help the body conserve Qi, while gentle movement and warm foods gently stimulate the Defensive Qi without depleting it. Overexertion and sweating open the pores further, draining Qi and fluids, which deepens the imbalance. Cold or raw foods cool the interior and hamper the Nutritive Qi's ability to recover, while emotional stress disturbs the smooth flow of Qi, making the fever more erratic.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Shield yourself from drafts and sudden temperature changes. Eat warm, easily digested foods like rice congee with a little ginger and spring onion. Gentle daily movement such as walking helps Qi flow, but avoid heavy sweating. A light scarf around the neck can help protect the vulnerable back of the neck from wind.

### Qi and Yin Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/qi-and-yin-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Low-grade fever that worsens with exertion, Afternoon fever with night sweats, Fatigue and shortness of breath, Dry mouth with little desire to drink, Warm sensation in palms, soles, and chest, Poor appetite and dizziness
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Overwork and overexertion, Hot, dry weather, Spicy, greasy, or fried foods, Stress and emotional upset
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Rest and sleep, Cool environment, Warm, easily digestible foods, Gentle movement (tai chi, walking), Deep, relaxed breathing
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When both Qi (vital energy) and Yin (cooling fluids) are depleted, the body loses its ability to anchor heat and stay balanced. Qi deficiency fails to hold the Yang energy in its proper place, allowing it to float upward and create a low-grade fever, especially when the body is strained. At the same time, Yin deficiency means there is not enough cooling moisture to keep the internal fire in check, so the fever often feels worse in the afternoon and may bring night sweats.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red or pale-red body with a thin or absent coating, possibly cracked and dry. Pulse: fine (thin), weak, and often rapid.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Overexertion directly consumes Qi, weakening the body's ability to anchor Yang energy, so the fever rises. Rest and gentle movement rebuild Qi without straining it. Hot, dry environments and spicy foods further deplete the already-scarce Yin fluids, making the empty heat worse. Cool surroundings and moistening, nourishing foods help replenish Yin and calm the internal fire. Emotional stress knots the Liver Qi, which can smolder into heat, while late nights exhaust the Kidney Yin, the body's core cooling reserve. Deep rest, both physical and mental, is essential to break this cycle.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Focus on gentle, nourishing foods that are easy to digest, like rice congee, pears, tofu, and steamed vegetables. Avoid overexertion and prioritize rest, but include very gentle movement like Tai Chi or short walks to keep Qi flowing without draining it. A cool, quiet environment and early nights help protect your Yin reserves.

## คำถามที่พบบ่อย (Frequently Asked Questions)
### Can acupuncture really bring down a fever?
Yes, but not by simply suppressing the temperature like a painkiller. Acupuncture works by rebalancing the body's energy flow - for example, clearing excess heat from the Liver or Gallbladder channels, or strengthening the Spleen to lift sinking Qi. Many patients notice a gradual reduction in fever spikes over several sessions, often accompanied by better sleep and more energy.

### Will Chinese herbs interact with my fever medications?
Most herbs used for FUO are gentle and have a low risk of interaction with common antipyretics like acetaminophen. However, some formulas contain ingredients like Chai Hu (Bupleurum) that have mild immune-modulating effects, so if you are on corticosteroids or immunosuppressants, it is essential to inform both your TCM practitioner and your doctor. Never stop prescribed medications abruptly without medical supervision.

### How long will it take to find the right pattern?
An experienced TCM practitioner can often identify the dominant pattern in your first consultation by listening to your description of the fever's timing, triggers, and accompanying sensations, along with tongue and pulse diagnosis. However, because patterns can overlap, it may take a few weeks of treatment to fully confirm and refine the approach. You should start seeing some improvement within that time.

### Is TCM safe for children with unexplained fevers?
Yes, TCM is very commonly used for children's fevers, with gentler herbal doses and non-invasive techniques like pediatric tui na massage or acupressure. Always work with a practitioner experienced in pediatrics. For any high fever or signs of serious illness, seek urgent medical care first - see our Safety section for red flags.

### What if I don't have a diagnosis from my doctor?
That is exactly the situation where TCM shines. Because TCM does not rely on a Western disease label, it can treat the pattern of imbalance that is causing your fever even when all tests are negative. Many patients with FUO find relief through TCM when conventional medicine has reached a dead end.

### Do I need to change my diet?
Diet plays a supportive role. In general, avoid spicy, greasy, and overly heating foods, as well as alcohol and caffeine, which can aggravate internal heat. Favor cooling, moistening foods like pear, cucumber, mung beans, and chrysanthemum tea. Your practitioner will give you more specific guidance once your pattern is identified.
