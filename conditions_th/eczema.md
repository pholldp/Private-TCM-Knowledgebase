## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนปัจจุบัน (Conventional Medicine View)
### คำอธิบาย (Description)
Eczema (atopic dermatitis) is a chronic inflammatory skin condition that causes dry, itchy, red, and sometimes oozing patches. It often begins in childhood and may be associated with allergies, asthma, or a genetic defect in the skin barrier protein filaggrin. Diagnosis is based on clinical appearance and history, with triggers including irritants, allergens, stress, and climate. In Western medicine, eczema is understood as a combination of skin barrier dysfunction and immune dysregulation, leading to inflammation and intense itch.

### การรักษาหลัก (Treatments)
Conventional care focuses on moisturizing to repair the skin barrier, reducing inflammation with topical corticosteroids or calcineurin inhibitors, and controlling itch with antihistamines. For moderate to severe cases, doctors may prescribe systemic immunosuppressants, phototherapy, or biologic drugs like dupilumab. While these treatments can effectively control flares, they require ongoing use and may have side effects such as skin thinning or increased infection risk.

## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนจีน (Traditional Chinese Medicine View)
### ความเข้าใจเกี่ยวกับการแพทย์แผนจีน (TCM Understanding)
In TCM, the Spleen is the organ system responsible for transforming food and fluids into usable energy and moisture. When the Spleen is weakened - by poor diet, stress, or constitutional tendency - it fails to manage fluids properly, and dampness accumulates. This internal dampness can overflow into the skin, creating the moist, oozy, swollen patches of acute eczema. If heat mixes with the dampness, the skin becomes red, hot, and intensely itchy.
Itching is the hallmark of eczema, and in TCM, itching is almost always caused by Wind. Wind can arise externally (from weather or allergens) or internally (from Blood Deficiency or Heat). In acute eczema, Wind-Heat attacks the skin surface, causing sudden red papules and burning itch. In chronic eczema, long-standing dampness and heat consume the body's Blood and Yin, leaving the skin undernourished and dry. The resulting emptiness allows internal Wind to stir, producing the relentless, dry itch that worsens at night.
This is why a single Western diagnosis of eczema can correspond to several TCM patterns. A patient with weepy, red lesions after eating spicy food has Damp-Heat invading the Spleen. Another with dull, less oozy patches and fatigue has Spleen Deficiency with Dampness. A third with dry, thickened, lichenified skin that itches in winter has Blood Deficiency with External Wind. Each pattern requires a different herbal formula and acupuncture strategy - what works for one may worsen another.
Chronic eczema can also involve the Liver and Kidneys, especially in older adults. The Kidneys store the body's fundamental Yin, and the Liver stores Blood. When these reserves run low, deficiency heat dries the skin, leading to thin, cracked, and itchy patches. This pattern often accompanies other signs of aging, like dry eyes, sore back, or night sweats. By identifying the underlying organ system involved, TCM offers a personalized path to healing that goes beyond the skin.

### การวินิจฉัยโดยแพทย์แผนจีน (Practitioner Diagnosis)
A TCM practitioner first looks at the eczema’s stage and appearance. Acute, weepy, red, and hot skin points toward excess patterns like Damp-Heat or Wind-Heat, while chronic, dry, scaly, and thickened skin suggests deficiency patterns such as Blood Deficiency with External Wind or Kidney and Liver Yin Deficiency. The timing of itching and what makes it better or worse also help narrow the picture.
If the rash is bright red, swollen, and oozing with intense itching, Damp-Heat invading the Spleen is the most likely pattern. The tongue will be red with a thick, yellow, greasy coat, and the pulse feels slippery and rapid. The person may feel heavy, thirsty but not very thirsty, and have sticky stools.
When the eczema is dull, less oozy, and accompanied by fatigue, loose stools, and a pale, swollen tongue with a white greasy coat, the pattern is Spleen Deficiency with Dampness. The pulse is soft and slow. This pattern often follows a prolonged bout of Damp-Heat that has weakened the Spleen’s ability to manage fluids.
In chronic eczema with dry, flaky, lichenified skin and itching that worsens at night, Blood Deficiency with External Wind is the typical pattern. The tongue is pale with a thin white coat, and the pulse is thin. Long-standing damp-heat consumes yin and blood, leaving the skin undernourished and vulnerable to internal wind that stirs up itching.
Wind-Heat is less common and presents as a sudden outbreak of red papules with a burning sensation, often triggered by an external pathogen. The tongue tip is red with a thin yellow coat, and the pulse is floating and rapid. It can overlap with early Damp-Heat, but oozing is minimal and the heat sensation is more prominent.
In elderly people with chronic dry eczema, Kidney and Liver Yin Deficiency may be the root. The skin is dry and rough, and they often have a sore lower back, dizziness, tinnitus, and a red tongue with little or no coat. The pulse is thin and rapid. Here the focus is on nourishing yin rather than just clearing dampness.

### Damp-Heat invading the Spleen
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/damp-heat-invading-the-spleen)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Red, oozing papules and vesicles, Intense itching, worse with heat and humidity, Stuffiness and fullness in the upper abdomen, Sticky, loose stools or diarrhea, Heavy, tired sensation in the body and limbs, Sticky or greasy taste in the mouth
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Spicy, greasy, or sweet foods, Hot, humid weather, Alcohol and heavy meals, Emotional stress and overwork
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Light, bland diet, Cool, dry environment, Rest and stress reduction, Cool compresses on skin
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  The Spleen is responsible for transforming and transporting fluids. When it is overwhelmed by dampness and heat from a rich, greasy diet or a hot, humid environment, damp-heat accumulates internally. This internal damp-heat can then overflow into the skin, producing the red, oozing, intensely itchy vesicles of acute eczema. The digestive symptoms-bloating, sticky stools, and nausea-are the Spleen's cry for help, signalling that the root of the skin problem lies in the digestive system.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red body with a yellow, greasy coating, thickest at the centre and root; may be swollen with teeth marks. Pulse: soggy (Ru) and rapid (Shu) or slippery (Hua) and rapid.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Damp-Heat invading the Spleen arises when the digestive system is overloaded by damp and hot influences, mainly from diet and environment. Spicy, greasy, and sweet foods generate internal dampness and heat, while hot, humid weather adds external damp-heat. These factors overwhelm the Spleen, causing damp-heat to spill into the skin as oozing, itchy eczema. A light, bland diet and cool, dry surroundings reduce that burden, allowing the Spleen to recover and the skin to clear.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat light, easily digestible foods like congee, barley, and mung beans. Avoid greasy, fried, spicy, and sweet foods, as well as alcohol and dairy, which create dampness and heat. Keep the skin cool and dry; avoid hot showers and excessive sweating. Gentle exercise like walking can help move Qi, but avoid overexertion.

### Spleen Deficiency with Dampness
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/spleen-deficiency-with-dampness)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Dull, darkish skin lesions with mild oozing or scaling, Fatigue and a heavy, dragged-down feeling in the body, Poor appetite and bloating after eating, Loose or unformed stools, Thirst with no real desire to drink
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Hot, humid weather, Overeating or heavy, greasy foods, Cold, raw foods and iced drinks, Prolonged sitting or inactivity, Mental overwork and worry
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warm, dry weather, Eating small, warm meals, Gentle daily exercise, Adequate sleep
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Your Spleen is like a pump that moves fluids around your body. When it gets weak, that pump slows down and fluids start to pool into unwanted dampness. This dampness then seeps outward into the skin, creating the dull, slightly oozy patches of eczema that just won't dry up.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale, puffy body with scalloped (teeth-marked) edges and a white, greasy coating. Pulse: soggy (濡 ru) and slightly slow (缓 huan), soft and weak.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: This pattern is all about a weak Spleen drowning in dampness. Raw, cold, and greasy foods further bog down the Spleen, while damp weather saturates the body with external moisture. Warmth and movement, on the other hand, act like sunshine and a gentle breeze, helping the Spleen dry out and pump fluids again.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat warm, cooked foods like soups, stews, and congee; avoid cold, raw, greasy, and dairy-heavy meals that create more dampness. Gentle daily movement like walking helps your Spleen pump fluids more efficiently. Keep the skin clean and dry, but avoid harsh soaps that strip natural oils.

### Blood Deficiency with External Wind
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/blood-deficiency-with-external-wind)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Dry, scaly, thickened skin, Itching that comes and goes or moves around, Itching worse at night, Sensitivity or aversion to wind and drafts, Pale or dull complexion, Dizziness or light-headedness
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Windy or dry weather, Emotional stress and overwork, Spicy or fried foods, Lack of sleep
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Regular moisturizing, Warm, non-drying environment, Adequate sleep, Nourishing soups and stews
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Chronic eczema often develops when long-standing damp-heat gradually consumes the body's blood and yin. Without enough blood to moisten and nourish the skin, it becomes dry, rough, scaly, and thickened. The deficiency creates an emptiness that external wind can invade, stirring up itching that shifts location and worsens at night when blood circulation naturally quiets.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale, thin body with a thin white and slightly dry coating. Pulse: fine (thin) overall, wiry at the left middle position, and floating at the right front position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Blood deficiency leaves the skin dry and undernourished, so moisturizers and blood-building foods directly address the root. Wind easily invades when blood is weak, making drafts and dry weather aggravating, while wind-dispelling herbs and protected environments bring relief. Night-time itching worsens because yin and blood are quieter then, so rest and sleep help replenish them, while stress and spicy foods further consume blood and stir up wind.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat blood-nourishing foods like dark leafy greens, liver, red dates, goji berries, and black sesame. Keep skin well-moisturized with gentle, fragrance-free emollients. Avoid long exposure to wind and drafts, and get plenty of restful sleep to support blood regeneration.

### Wind-Heat
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/wind-heat)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Sudden outbreak of red papules, Burning sensation on the skin, Intense itching, Sore, red throat, Thirst with desire to drink, Fever with mild chills
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Spicy or fried foods, Windy or hot weather, Hot showers or baths, Stress and emotional upset
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Cool compresses on skin, Rest in a calm environment, Light, bland diet
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Eczema in this pattern begins when an external invasion of Wind and Heat strikes the surface of the body. Wind is light and mobile, so it opens the pores and carries Heat into the skin, where the two pathogens become lodged. This sudden clash produces an acute outbreak of red, burning papules that itch fiercely, because Wind stirs the skin and Heat inflames it. Because the attack is still at the exterior level, you may also feel feverish, with a sore throat and a floating, rapid pulse.

### Kidney and Liver Yin Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/kidney-and-liver-yin-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Dry, thin skin with fine scaling or mild redness, Itching worse at night, Soreness and weakness of lower back and knees, Dizziness, ringing in the ears, and blurred vision, Night sweats with heat in the palms, soles, and chest, Dry mouth and throat, insomnia or restless sleep
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Overwork and late nights, Spicy, fried, or drying foods, Dry, heated indoor air, Emotional stress and frustration
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Adequate sleep, Cool, humidified environment, Yin-nourishing foods (pear, sesame, tofu), Gentle daily exercise, Regular moisturizing
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Kidney and Liver Yin are the body's deep cooling and moistening reserves. When these reserves run low, deficiency heat flares up and dries out the skin, leading to chronic, dry, itchy eczema. This is especially common in older adults, whose yin naturally declines with age. The skin becomes thin, dry, and may show fine scaling or mild redness, while deeper signs like night sweats and sore back reveal the root deficiency.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red, thin body with cracks, little or no coating, dry surface. Pulse: fine, rapid, and wiry.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Yin is the body's coolant and moisture. Staying up late, overwork, and spicy foods generate heat and consume yin, worsening dryness and itching. Rest, cooling environments, and yin-nourishing foods replenish these reserves, calming deficiency heat and soothing the skin.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Prioritize rest and avoid staying up late, as sleep rebuilds yin. Eat moistening foods like pear, black sesame, tofu, and goji berries; avoid spicy, fried, or overly drying foods. Keep skin hydrated with gentle, fragrance-free moisturizers, and practice calming activities like tai chi or meditation to conserve yin.

## คำถามที่พบบ่อย (Frequently Asked Questions)
### Can Chinese medicine cure eczema?
TCM aims to resolve the underlying imbalance so that eczema no longer recurs. While we cannot guarantee a permanent cure, many patients achieve long-term remission - especially when they follow dietary and lifestyle guidance. Acute cases often resolve fully; chronic cases may see significant reduction in frequency and severity.

### How long does it take to see results with acupuncture and herbs?
Most people notice a decrease in itching and oozing within the first 2 to 4 weeks of treatment. For chronic, dry eczema, improvement may be slower, with visible skin healing over 2 to 3 months. Consistency is key - weekly acupuncture and daily herbs produce the best outcomes.

### Can I use Chinese medicine alongside my steroid creams?
Yes, TCM can be used together with topical corticosteroids. Many patients find that acupuncture and herbs reduce the need for steroids over time. Always tell both your TCM practitioner and your dermatologist about all treatments you are using. Do not stop prescribed medications abruptly without medical supervision.

### What foods should I avoid if I have eczema?
In TCM, foods that create dampness and heat are the main culprits. These include greasy or fried foods, spicy dishes, alcohol, dairy products, and excessive sugar. For dry eczema, overly drying or heating foods like coffee and roasted nuts may also worsen itching. A bland, cooling diet with plenty of cooked vegetables and grains is generally supportive.

### Is acupuncture effective for eczema?
Acupuncture helps reduce inflammation, calm the itch, and rebalance the organ systems involved. Points like Quchi (LI-11), Xuehai (SP-10), and Zusanli (ST-36) are commonly used to clear heat, cool blood, and strengthen the Spleen. For best results, acupuncture is combined with herbal medicine and dietary changes.

### Does eczema come back after TCM treatment?
If the underlying imbalance is fully corrected, recurrence is unlikely. However, if dietary or lifestyle habits that originally caused the imbalance are resumed, eczema may return. TCM emphasizes long-term prevention through diet, stress management, and occasional maintenance treatments. Many patients find that even if a small flare occurs, it is milder and resolves faster.
