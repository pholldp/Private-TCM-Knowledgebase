## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนปัจจุบัน (Conventional Medicine View)
### คำอธิบาย (Description)
In Western medicine, shoulder stiffness is often a symptom of an underlying condition like adhesive capsulitis (frozen shoulder), rotator cuff injury, osteoarthritis, or bursitis. The joint capsule thickens and tightens, or inflammation limits the range of motion. Diagnosis typically involves a physical exam, imaging such as X-rays or MRI, and sometimes blood tests to rule out inflammatory arthritis.

### การรักษาหลัก (Treatments)
Treatment usually starts with rest, ice or heat, and over-the-counter anti-inflammatories. Physical therapy is a cornerstone, with stretching and strengthening exercises. If these don't help, corticosteroid injections can reduce inflammation, and in persistent cases, manipulation under anesthesia or arthroscopic surgery may be considered.

## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนจีน (Traditional Chinese Medicine View)
### ความเข้าใจเกี่ยวกับการแพทย์แผนจีน (TCM Understanding)
Chinese medicine sees the shoulder as a meeting point of many channels, especially those of the Large Intestine, Small Intestine, and San Jiao. For the joint to move freely, Qi and Blood must flow smoothly through these pathways, and the sinews and bones must be well-lubricated. When something blocks that flow - whether it's stagnant Blood, cold, dampness, or heat - stiffness sets in. And when the body lacks the resources to keep the tissues supple, the shoulder becomes tight and weak.
The Liver plays a central role because it governs the sinews and ensures the smooth movement of Qi. If Liver Qi gets stuck, often from stress or frustration, the shoulder can seize up. The Kidneys nourish the bones and marrow, and as their essence declines with age, the joint can feel dry and stiff - a common story in frozen shoulder after fifty. The Spleen produces the Qi and Blood that fuel the muscles; if digestion is weak, the shoulder is undernourished and easily fatigued.
This is why two people with the same Western diagnosis can have completely different patterns in Chinese medicine. One may have a hot, swollen joint that hates humidity (Damp-Heat). Another may have a cold, rigid shoulder that loosens with a warm shower (Cold-Damp or Qi and Blood Stagnation). And a third may just feel a dull, persistent tightness that gets worse as the day wears on (Qi and Blood Deficiency). Each pattern points to a different root, and treatment follows that root, not just the symptom.

### การวินิจฉัยโดยแพทย์แผนจีน (Practitioner Diagnosis)
When a person describes a sharp, stabbing pain that stays in one spot and gets worse at night, a TCM practitioner suspects Qi and Blood Stagnation. The shoulder may feel very stiff and refuse to move. The tongue often looks dark or shows purple spots, and the pulse feels choppy or wiry. A history of an old injury or long-standing strain often points toward this pattern.
If the main complaint is a dull ache with a sense of weakness and heaviness that worsens after exertion or at the end of the day, the picture shifts to Qi and Blood Deficiency. The person may look pale and tired, and the tongue is usually pale with a thin coating. The pulse is thin and weak. This pattern is more common in those who are constitutionally frail or recovering from a long illness.
In middle-aged and older adults, gradual stiffness and loss of flexibility without a clear injury often points to declining Kidney and Liver Yin. The shoulder may feel tight and dry, and the person might also have lower back soreness, night sweats, or a dry mouth. The tongue appears red with little coating, and the pulse is thin and rapid. This reflects a deeper depletion of the body’s foundational resources.
Though less common, a shoulder that feels hot, swollen, and heavy suggests Damp-Heat blocking the channels. The pain may be accompanied by a sensation of burning, and the area might look slightly red. The tongue is typically red with a greasy yellow coating, and the pulse is slippery and rapid. This pattern can arise when dampness from diet or environment combines with internal heat.

### Qi And Blood Stagnation
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/qi-and-blood-stagnation)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Fixed, stabbing pain in the shoulder, Pain worsens at night, Pain worse with pressure, Dark purple tongue with stasis spots, Wiry and choppy pulse, Irritability or mood swings
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Cold and damp weather, Prolonged inactivity, Emotional stress or frustration, Cold, raw foods, Overuse or heavy lifting
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warm compress or heat, Gentle movement or stretching, Light massage, Rest, Warm, cooked foods
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When Qi and Blood become stuck in the shoulder’s channels and collaterals, the joint loses its smooth nourishment. The obstruction causes a fixed, stabbing pain that worsens at night, when Blood moves more slowly, and with any pressure. The stiffness reflects the inability of Qi to properly direct movement through the area.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: dark purple body with stasis spots, distended sublingual veins. Pulse: wiry (弦 xián) and choppy (涩 sè), like a taut guitar string with a rough, scraping edge.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Warmth, movement, and massage help to move stagnant Qi and Blood, relieving the obstruction that causes stabbing pain. Cold and inactivity cause further stagnation, while stress constricts Qi flow, making the stabbing pain worse.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Keep the shoulder warm and avoid cold, raw foods that congeal Blood. Eat warming, blood-moving foods like turmeric, ginger, black pepper, and dark leafy greens. Gentle daily shoulder circles and stretches improve circulation, but stop before sharp pain. A warm compress or a hot bath in the evening can ease night pain.

### Qi and Blood Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/qi-and-blood-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Dull, aching shoulder stiffness, Stiffness worsens with exertion and fatigue, Pale or sallow complexion, General weakness and fatigue, Dizziness upon standing
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Overexertion, Poor nutrition or skipping meals, Cold and damp weather, Excessive sweating
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Rest, Nourishing meals (e.g., bone broth), Gentle movement or stretching
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  The shoulder's sinews and muscles rely on a steady supply of Qi and Blood to stay flexible and strong. When Qi and Blood become deficient, the shoulder is undernourished, leading to a dull, persistent stiffness and weakness. This is especially noticeable after exertion, when the body's limited resources are further depleted.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale, may be thin or have teeth marks, thin white coating. Pulse: fine (thin) and weak.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: When Qi and Blood are deficient, the shoulder's sinews lack nourishment, so overexertion and fatigue worsen stiffness. Rest conserves Qi, and nourishing food and warmth help build Blood and promote circulation to the shoulder.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat warm, nourishing foods like congee with red dates and goji berries, bone broths, and lightly cooked vegetables. Avoid raw, cold, and greasy foods that tax the Spleen. Gentle movement such as tai chi or qigong helps circulation, but rest when fatigued.

### Kidney and Liver Yin Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/kidney-and-liver-yin-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Gradual loss of shoulder flexibility, Dull ache rather than sharp pain, Dry eyes and blurred vision, Night sweats, Heat in the palms, soles and chest, Dizziness and ringing in the ears, Soreness and weakness of the lower back and knees, Stiffness often worse after prolonged use
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Overwork and late nights, Spicy, drying foods, Prolonged stress, Dry, hot weather
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Rest, Gentle movement or stretching, Yin-nourishing foods, Warm, not hot, compresses
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Kidney and Liver Yin provides the moisture and nourishment that keep tendons supple and bones strong. When this deep Yin declines with age or overwork, the shoulder’s sinews lose their lubrication and flexibility. The result is a gradual, tight stiffness that feels like the joint is drying out, often accompanied by a dull ache and a sense of heat in the palms and soles.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red, thin body with cracks, little or no coating, dry surface. Pulse: fine and rapid, often wiry; the left Chi (Kidney) position is weak.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Kidney and Liver Yin is the body’s deep reserve of cooling, moistening energy. Overwork, late nights and stress drain this reserve, leaving tendons dry and stiff. Rest, yin-rich foods and gentle movement help rebuild that moisture, while spicy foods and excessive heat further deplete it, making the shoulder feel tighter.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Prioritise gentle, consistent movement like tai chi or qigong to keep the shoulder fluid without straining it. Eat yin-nourishing foods such as black sesame, goji berries, mulberries, bone broth and pears. Avoid late nights, overwork and spicy, drying foods that consume precious Yin fluids.

### Painful Obstruction due to Damp Heat in Channels
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/painful-obstruction-due-to-damp-heat-in-channels)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Shoulder feels hot and may look red, Swelling that is warm to the touch, Heavy, aching stiffness, Pain and stiffness ease with cold compresses, Worse in warm, humid weather, Thirst and irritability
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Warm compresses or heat, Humid or hot weather, Greasy, spicy food, Alcohol
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Cold compresses, Cool, dry weather, Light, cooling foods, Gentle movement or stretching
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Dampness and Heat combine and lodge in the shoulder channels, blocking the flow of Qi and Blood. Dampness is heavy and sticky, so it makes the joint feel swollen, stiff, and achy. Heat inflames the tissues, causing redness, a sensation of warmth, and sharper pain. This combination creates a swollen, hot, and immobile shoulder that feels worse with heat and better with cold.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red body, possibly slightly swollen, with a yellow greasy coating. Pulse: slippery and rapid.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Damp-Heat is a combination of pathogenic moisture and heat. Warmth and humidity feed the Heat and Dampness, making the joint more swollen, stiff, and painful. Cold and drying measures counteract these pathogens, calming inflammation and reducing the heavy, sticky obstruction. This is why a cool, dry environment and cooling foods bring relief, while heat and dampness make it worse.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Avoid greasy, fried, and spicy foods, as well as alcohol, which all create Dampness and Heat. Eat cooling, damp-draining foods like barley, mung beans, cucumber, and celery. Keep the shoulder gently mobile but avoid hot compresses or heating pads. A cool, dry environment helps reduce swelling and pain.

## คำถามที่พบบ่อย (Frequently Asked Questions)
### How does TCM treat shoulder stiffness differently from physical therapy?
Physical therapy focuses on stretching and strengthening the muscles and joint capsule locally. TCM adds a whole-body lens: we look at why the stiffness appeared - is it a circulation problem, a digestive weakness, or age-related decline? Acupuncture and herbs work on both the local shoulder channels and the underlying organ systems to improve flow and nourishment. Many patients combine both approaches for better results.

### Can acupuncture really help a frozen shoulder?
Yes, especially in the early and middle stages. Acupuncture points around the shoulder, like Jianyu (LI-15), and along the affected channels can relax tight muscles, reduce pain, and increase blood flow to the joint capsule. Combined with gentle movement and herbs, it often helps restore range of motion more quickly than waiting alone.

### What foods should I avoid if I have shoulder stiffness?
In general, avoid cold and raw foods, which can constrict the channels and worsen stagnation. Damp-forming foods like dairy, fried foods, and excess sugar can contribute to heaviness and swelling, especially in damp-heat or phlegm patterns. Greasy, spicy foods may aggravate heat. Warm, cooked meals with moderate amounts of ginger, turmeric, and black pepper are usually helpful.

### Is it safe to take TCM herbs with my anti-inflammatory medication?
Often yes, but you must tell both your doctor and your TCM practitioner about everything you're taking. Some blood-moving herbs (like Dang Gui or Chuan Xiong) can interact with blood thinners such as warfarin or even high-dose aspirin. Your practitioner can adjust the formula to be safe. Never stop prescribed medication abruptly without medical advice.

### How many acupuncture sessions will I need?
For acute stiffness, 4-6 weekly sessions are common. Chronic or frozen shoulder may need 8-12 sessions over two to three months. You should start to feel some improvement in pain and mobility within the first few visits. If there's no change after 4-5 sessions, the pattern may need re-evaluation.

### Can TCM help if my shoulder stiffness is from an old injury?
Yes, especially if the injury led to lingering Blood stasis. TCM excels at moving old stagnation that causes fixed, stabbing pain and restricted movement. Herbs like Tao Ren and Hong Hua in Tao Hong Si Wu Tang, along with local acupuncture, can help break up that stuck Blood and restore function.
