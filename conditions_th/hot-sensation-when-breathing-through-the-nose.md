## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนปัจจุบัน (Conventional Medicine View)
### คำอธิบาย (Description)
In Western medicine, a hot or burning sensation in the nose when breathing is often linked to dry air, allergies, sinusitis, or an upper respiratory infection. Inflammation of the nasal passages can make the air feel warmer than usual, especially when the mucosa is swollen or irritated. Diagnosis usually involves a physical exam, sometimes an allergy test or imaging if sinusitis is suspected.

### การรักษาหลัก (Treatments)
Typical treatments include saline nasal sprays, humidifiers, antihistamines for allergies, decongestant sprays or pills, and pain relievers if there is facial discomfort. If a bacterial sinus infection is confirmed, antibiotics may be prescribed. Steroid nasal sprays are sometimes used to reduce chronic inflammation.

## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนจีน (Traditional Chinese Medicine View)
### ความเข้าใจเกี่ยวกับการแพทย์แผนจีน (TCM Understanding)
In TCM, the nose is the opening of the Lungs, so any heat that builds up in the Lung system naturally rises to the nasal passages. This is why a hot sensation when breathing is almost always a sign of some kind of heat - but not all heat is the same. It can be an excess heat from an infection or emotional stress, a phlegm-heat that clogs the chest, or an empty heat from long-term Yin deficiency that leaves the nose dry and burning.
The Stomach and Spleen also play a role. The Stomach channel runs through the nose, so when rich, greasy foods create damp-heat in the digestive system, that turbid steam can travel upward and make the nose feel hot and stuffy. This explains why some people notice their symptoms flare after heavy meals or alcohol.
Because a single symptom like “hot nose breath” can arise from such different internal landscapes, TCM doesn’t have one standard treatment. Instead, a practitioner will look at the whole picture - your tongue, pulse, the type of nasal discharge, thirst, digestion, and even the time of day it worsens - to identify which pattern is active. That pattern then determines the exact herbal formula and acupuncture points, making the treatment deeply personal.

### การวินิจฉัยโดยแพทย์แผนจีน (Practitioner Diagnosis)
A practitioner begins by asking what the hot sensation feels like and when it started. The thickness, color, and amount of nasal discharge are among the first clues, because each pattern produces a different kind of mucus. The tongue and pulse are then checked to confirm which internal imbalance is causing heat to rise into the nose.
If the heat is constant and accompanied by yellow nasal discharge, congestion, and thirst, that points toward Lung Heat. This pattern reflects an internal excess of heat in the Lung system, and the tongue is typically red with a yellow coating, while the pulse feels rapid and full.
When the hot sensation arrives suddenly after exposure to wind, along with sneezing, an itchy nose, and a sore throat, Wind-Heat invading the Lungs is the likely picture. The discharge is yellow, the tongue is red with a thin yellow coat, and the pulse feels floating and rapid, indicating the fight is still at the surface of the body.
If a productive cough with thick yellow sputum and chest tightness accompanies the hot breath, Phlegm-Heat in the Lungs is often the root. Here the nasal discharge is also thick and yellow, the tongue coating is yellow and greasy, and the pulse feels slippery and rapid-a sign that phlegm and heat are tangled together deep in the Lungs.
A hot nose with thick yellow-green discharge and heavy congestion, plus digestive symptoms like bloating or a poor appetite, suggests Damp-Heat in the Stomach and Spleen. The tongue coating is distinctly yellow and greasy, and the pulse is slippery and rapid, reflecting dampness and heat brewing in the digestive system and steaming upward.
When the sensation is more dry than burning, with scant or no discharge, a dry throat, and perhaps night sweats, Empty-Heat from Yin Deficiency is the most likely pattern. The tongue appears red with little or no coating, and the pulse is thready and rapid. This chronic heat arises because the body’s cooling Yin fluids have run low.

### Lung Heat
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/lung-heat)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Hot, burning sensation when breathing through the nose, Thick yellow or green nasal discharge, Cough with thick yellow phlegm, Thirst with desire for cold drinks, Fever or feeling of body heat, No itching or sneezing (unlike Wind-Heat)
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Spicy, greasy, or fried foods, Hot, stuffy, or dry environments, Smoking or secondhand smoke, Emotional stress, anger, or overwork
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Cool, fresh air, Drinking cool water, Eating pears, Rest in a quiet, cool room
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  The Lungs open into the nose. When excess heat builds up inside the Lungs, it rises along the respiratory tract and escapes through the nostrils, making the air feel hot as you breathe. This internal heat also thickens fluids, leading to yellow nasal discharge, and dries out the mouth and throat.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red body with a yellow, dry coating; possible prickly papillae on the front third. Pulse: rapid (shu) and forceful, often slippery (hua) or overflowing (hong), especially in the right cun (Lung) position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Internal Heat rises and irritates the nose. Cooling foods and environments directly counter that Heat, while spicy and greasy foods fuel it. Stress and anger stir up Liver Qi which can turn into Heat and worsen Lung Heat, so calming down helps keep the fire in check.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Avoid spicy, greasy, and fried foods that add Heat. Drink plenty of room-temperature water and eat cooling foods like pear, cucumber, and watermelon. Keep indoor air moist with a humidifier and avoid smoking or smoky environments.

### Wind-Heat invading the Lungs
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/wind-heat-invading-the-lungs)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Sudden onset of hot, burning sensation in the nose, Yellow or turbid nasal discharge, Sore throat, Fever with mild chills, Sneezing, Thirst
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Spicy, greasy, or fried foods, Alcohol, Hot, stuffy, or dry environments, Smoking or secondhand smoke, Overexertion
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Rest and adequate sleep, Cool, fresh air, Peppermint tea, Steam inhalation
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When Wind-Heat invades from the outside, it attacks the body's defensive surface and lodges in the Lungs. Since the Lungs open into the nose, the trapped Heat rushes upward along the Lung channel, creating a sudden, distinct burning sensation when you breathe in. The body tries to expel the pathogen, leading to sneezing and yellow nasal discharge.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red tip, thin yellow coating, body slightly red. Pulse: floating (Fu) and rapid (Shu).
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Wind-Heat is an external pathogen that has lodged in the Lungs. Rest and sleep allow the body's defensive Qi to focus on expelling it. Spicy, greasy, and hot foods create more internal Heat, which fuels the burning sensation. Cool fresh air and mint tea help disperse the Heat, while steam inhalation moistens and opens the nasal passages. Acupressure on local points like LI20 directly relieves the hot, congested feeling.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Rest as much as possible and drink plenty of warm fluids like mint or chrysanthemum tea. Avoid spicy, greasy, or fried foods that generate internal heat. Use a cool-mist humidifier and gentle steam inhalation with a few drops of eucalyptus or peppermint oil to soothe and open the nasal passages.

### Phlegm-Heat in the Lungs
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/phlegm-heat-in-the-lungs)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Thick yellow phlegm that is hard to cough up, Breath feels hot, like a furnace, Chest feels tight and full, as if something is stuck, Wheezing or gurgling sounds when breathing, Intense thirst, desire for cold drinks, Restlessness and irritability
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Greasy, fried, or dairy foods, Spicy, greasy, or fried foods, Lying flat after eating, Humid or stuffy environments, Emotional stress, anger, or overwork
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Eating pears, Steam inhalation, Gentle exercise or movement, Cool, fresh air, Drinking warm water
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  The Lungs open into the nose, so when thick, sticky Phlegm and Heat clog the Lungs, the hot and turbid Qi has nowhere to go but out through the nostrils. This makes the exhaled breath feel noticeably hot. The Phlegm itself causes the deep cough, chest tightness, and rattling sounds, while the Heat adds the burning sensation, thirst, and restlessness.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red body, possibly slightly swollen at the front, with a yellow greasy coating; red prickles may appear on the front third. Pulse: slippery (滑 huá) and rapid (数 shuò), strongest at the right cun position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Phlegm-Heat in the Lungs is a condition of excess Heat and sticky Phlegm obstructing the Lung system. Greasy, fried, and dairy foods create more Phlegm, while spicy foods add Heat, worsening the hot breath and thick sputum. Lying flat allows Phlegm to pool in the chest, increasing congestion. Cooling foods like pears and radish help clear Heat and resolve Phlegm. Steam inhalation moistens and loosens the sticky Phlegm, making it easier to expel. Gentle movement stimulates Lung Qi flow, helping to break up Phlegm and cool the Heat naturally.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Avoid greasy, fried, dairy, and sweet foods that create Phlegm and Dampness. Spicy and heating foods like chili, garlic, and alcohol will worsen the Heat. Eat cooling, light foods like pears, radish, and mung beans. Steam inhalation with mint or eucalyptus can help clear the airways. Gentle exercise like walking or qi gong helps move Lung Qi and clear Phlegm.

### Damp-Heat in Stomach and Spleen
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/damp-heat-in-stomach-and-spleen)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Bloating and fullness in the upper abdomen, Sticky or incomplete bowel movements, Bitter or sticky taste in the mouth, Thick yellow-green nasal discharge, Heavy feeling in the body and limbs
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Spicy, greasy, or fried foods, Dairy and sweets, Alcohol, Hot, humid weather, Overeating
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Light, easily digestible meals, Avoiding greasy or sweet foods, Gentle exercise or movement, Dry, warm environment
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When the Spleen and Stomach are overwhelmed by rich, greasy foods and irregular eating, they fail to transform fluids properly. Dampness accumulates and combines with Heat, creating a turbid steam that rises along the Stomach channel to the nose. This is what produces the hot, stuffy sensation and thick, yellow-green nasal discharge.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red body, possibly swollen with teeth marks, thick yellow greasy coating (especially center and root). Pulse: soggy (濡 ru) and rapid (数 shu), often slippery (滑 huá).
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: This pattern stems from Spleen weakness combined with an overload of damp-producing foods. Greasy, sweet, and heavy foods directly create Dampness, while alcohol and spicy foods add Heat. Avoiding these removes the fuel for the steaming turbidity. Light meals and gentle exercise support the Spleen's function, helping to transform and drain Dampness, which in turn clears the heat that rises to the nose.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat light, cooked foods like congee and steamed vegetables. Avoid greasy, fried, sweet, and dairy-rich foods that create Dampness. Skip alcohol and spicy dishes, which add Heat. Gentle daily movement like walking helps the Spleen transform Dampness.

### Empty-Heat caused by Yin Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/empty-heat-caused-by-yin-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Dry, burning sensation in the nose, Little or no nasal discharge, Symptoms worsen in the evening or at night, Night sweats and feeling of heat in palms, soles, and chest, Flushed cheeks, Dry mouth and throat with desire to sip small amounts
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Staying up late, Spicy, greasy, or fried foods, Emotional stress, anger, or overwork, Hot, stuffy, or dry environments, Smoking or alcohol
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Cool, moist air, Eating pears, Rest and adequate sleep, Using a humidifier
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When the body's Yin fluids are chronically depleted, a relative excess of Yang creates 'empty heat' that rises upward. The Lungs, which open into the nose, become dry and undernourished, so the nose feels persistently hot and dry, especially when breathing in. Because the heat comes from deficiency rather than a true excess, the sensation is often worse in the evening or at night, and there is little to no thick discharge.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red, thin, dry body with cracks, little or no coating. Pulse: fine (thready) and rapid, often weak at the left rear (Kidney) position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Yin deficiency is worsened by anything that consumes fluids and generates heat, such as late nights, stress, and spicy foods. These habits deplete the body's cooling, moistening resources and allow empty heat to flare upward. Cool, moist environments and nourishing foods help replenish Yin and gently calm the heat, while rest and early sleep allow the body to restore its Yin reserves.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat moistening, cooling foods like pears, tofu, cucumber, lily bulb, and black sesame. Avoid spicy, fried, or overly heating foods. Stay well hydrated and use a humidifier if the air is dry. Go to bed early and avoid late nights, as staying up late depletes Yin. Gentle exercise such as yoga, tai chi, or walking is better than intense, sweat-drenching workouts.

## คำถามที่พบบ่อย (Frequently Asked Questions)
### Why does my nose feel hot when I breathe out?
In TCM, the Lungs govern the nose, so when there is excess heat in the Lungs, it escapes with the exhaled breath. This can happen after a respiratory infection, from chronic inflammation, or even from digestive heat rising upward. The specific pattern - whether it’s Lung Heat, Phlegm-Heat, or Yin Deficiency - will determine the right treatment.

### Can acupuncture really help a burning sensation in the nose?
Yes. Acupuncture points like Yingxiang (LI-20) beside the nose and Hegu (LI-4) on the hand are used to clear heat and open the nasal passages. Many patients feel a cooling effect in the nose during or shortly after a session. When combined with herbs, acupuncture addresses both the symptom and the underlying pattern.

### What herbs are used for hot nose sensation?
The choice depends on the pattern, but herbs like Huang Qin (Scutellaria) and Shi Gao (Gypsum) clear Lung Heat, while Xin Yi Hua (Magnolia flower) specifically opens the nasal passages. For Yin deficiency, cooling and moistening herbs like Mai Dong (Ophiopogon) and Zhi Mu (Anemarrhena) are used. Never self-prescribe; a TCM practitioner will craft a formula for your exact presentation.

### Is it safe to use TCM alongside my antihistamines or steroid sprays?
Generally yes. Herbal formulas and acupuncture can be used alongside conventional medications. However, always tell both your TCM practitioner and your doctor what you are taking. Some herbs can have mild blood-thinning effects, so if you are on anticoagulants, extra caution is needed. Do not stop prescribed medications abruptly without medical guidance.

### How long until I feel relief?
Most acute patterns start to improve within one to two weeks. Chronic or deficiency patterns take longer - you might notice subtle changes in the first month, with deeper improvement over two to three months. Acupuncture often gives quicker symptomatic relief, while herbs work more gradually to rebalance the system.

### What foods should I avoid if my nose feels hot?
Stay away from spicy, greasy, and deep-fried foods, as well as alcohol and excessive coffee - these all add heat to the body. Sugar and dairy can create dampness and phlegm, which worsen the sensation. Instead, favor cooling foods like pears, cucumber, mint tea, and plenty of room-temperature water.

### Could a hot sensation in the nose be a sign of something serious?
On its own, a hot sensation is usually not a sign of a serious illness. However, if you experience other worrying symptoms, please refer to the urgent medical care section above. For persistent but mild hot sensation, TCM is a safe and effective option.
