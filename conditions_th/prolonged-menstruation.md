## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนปัจจุบัน (Conventional Medicine View)
### คำอธิบาย (Description)
In Western medicine, prolonged menstruation (often called menorrhagia or prolonged menstrual bleeding) is typically defined as menstrual flow lasting longer than seven days. It can be caused by hormonal imbalances (such as anovulatory cycles or perimenopause), uterine fibroids, polyps, adenomyosis, pelvic inflammatory disease, or bleeding disorders. Diagnosis usually involves a pelvic exam, ultrasound, and sometimes endometrial biopsy to rule out serious conditions.

### การรักษาหลัก (Treatments)
Conventional management may include hormonal therapies (combined oral contraceptives, progestin-releasing IUDs, or cyclic progestins), nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs (NSAIDs) to reduce bleeding, tranexamic acid to promote clotting, or surgical interventions like endometrial ablation or hysterectomy for severe cases. Iron supplementation is often recommended if anemia is present.

## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนจีน (Traditional Chinese Medicine View)
### ความเข้าใจเกี่ยวกับการแพทย์แผนจีน (TCM Understanding)
In TCM, the uterus relies on the Chong (Penetrating) and Ren (Conception) vessels for a healthy menstrual cycle. These vessels need abundant Qi and Blood to function, but also a calm, cool environment. When the body's holding force - Spleen Qi - is weak, it cannot keep blood in the vessels, and the uterine lining sheds slowly, leading to prolonged, pale, thin bleeding with deep fatigue. This is the pattern of Qi not controlling Blood.
Sometimes, blood flow becomes sluggish and congeals in the uterus, blocking the vessels. Instead of shedding cleanly, old, dark, clotted blood lingers, causing the period to drag on with stabbing pain. This is Blood Stagnation, often linked to emotional stress or cold invasion. The tongue may be purplish with spots, and the pulse feels wiry or choppy.
When Kidney Yin is depleted - often from overwork, late nights, or chronic stress - a low-grade heat called Empty-Heat disturbs the uterus. This heat agitates the blood, making it seep out slowly but persistently. The bleeding is scanty, bright red, and accompanied by night sweats, hot palms, and a dry restless feeling. The tongue is red with little coating.
Damp-Heat, a sticky, hot pathogenic factor, can also invade the lower burner. It arises from heavy, greasy foods and humid environments, sinking to the uterus and causing dark, sticky bleeding with yellow discharge and a dragging sensation. The tongue has a thick, greasy yellow coat, and the pulse is slippery and rapid. Each pattern requires a completely different herbal and acupuncture strategy.

### การวินิจฉัยโดยแพทย์แผนจีน (Practitioner Diagnosis)
To understand why a period drags on, a TCM practitioner first listens carefully to the story of the bleeding. They ask about the color, consistency, and volume of the flow, whether there are clots or pain, and what else the body is feeling - energy levels, thirst, mood, and any other discharge. These details are the first clues that separate one underlying pattern from another.
If the blood is pale, thin, and maybe heavy but lacks clots, and the person feels wiped out, short of breath, or light-headed, the practitioner suspects that Qi is too weak to hold blood in the vessels. This pattern, Qi not controlling Blood, often shows a pale tongue with a thin white coating and a weak, thready pulse - signs that the body’s vital energy is depleted and cannot anchor the flow.
When the bleeding is dark, purplish, or filled with clots, and there is a stabbing or fixed pain in the lower abdomen that feels better after clots pass, the focus shifts to Blood Stagnation. Here blood is not flowing smoothly; it pools and obstructs the channels. The tongue may look purplish with dark spots, and the pulse feels wiry and rough, confirming that stagnation is the culprit.
If the period is scanty but persists, with bright-red blood that may be slightly sticky, and the person notices night sweats, a dry mouth, hot palms and soles, or a feeling of heat in the evening, the pattern is Kidney Yin Deficiency with Empty-Heat Blazing. The tongue is often red with little coating, and the pulse is thin and rapid - a sign that cooling Yin fluids are low and a low-grade heat is disturbing the uterus.
When the bleeding is dark red, sticky, and perhaps foul-smelling, accompanied by a heavy sensation in the pelvis, yellow vaginal discharge, or a feeling of heat and dampness, the practitioner thinks of Damp-Heat in the Lower Burner. The tongue appears red with a thick, greasy yellow coating, and the pulse is slippery and fast, reflecting the presence of both moisture and heat that are agitating the womb.

- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Qi is the body’s holding force. When Spleen Qi is weak, it cannot keep blood inside the vessels, so the uterine lining sheds slowly. The result is a period that drags on for many days with pale, thin blood and deep fatigue.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale, puffy body with teeth marks, thin white coating. Pulse: fine (thin) and weak, especially at the right Guan position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Qi is the body's holding force. When Spleen Qi is weak, it cannot keep blood in the vessels, so periods drag on. Rest and warm, nourishing foods replenish Qi, while overwork and cold foods drain it further. Moxibustion and gentle warmth directly support the Spleen's ability to contain blood.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat warm, cooked, easily digestible foods like soups, stews, and congees. Avoid raw, cold, and greasy items that weaken Spleen Qi. Prioritize rest and avoid overexertion, especially during menstruation. Gentle practices like walking and restorative yoga help Qi flow without depleting it.

### Blood Stagnation
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/blood-stagnation)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Stabbing, fixed lower abdominal pain worse with pressure, Pain worsens at night, Dark menstrual blood with clots, Dark purple tongue with stasis spots, Dark circles under eyes or dusky complexion
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Stress and frustration, Cold environments or icy drinks, Prolonged sitting or inactivity, Greasy, heavy foods
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warmth on the lower abdomen, Gentle exercise like walking, Warming foods and spices, Rest and relaxation
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When blood flow becomes sluggish and congeals in the uterus, it blocks the Chong and Ren vessels that govern menstruation. Instead of shedding cleanly, old blood lingers, causing the period to drag on with dark, clotted bleeding. The stabbing pain is a direct signal that blood is stuck and not moving smoothly.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: dark purple body with stasis spots, distended dark sublingual veins, thin white coat. Pulse: choppy (涩 sè) or wiry (弦 xián).
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Blood Stagnation means blood is congealed and not moving. Warmth and gentle movement help dissolve stasis and encourage smooth flow, while cold and inactivity make it worse. Emotional stress constrains the Liver, which governs the smooth flow of Qi and blood, so frustration and anger can directly aggravate the stagnation. Warming foods with spices like turmeric and ginger help invigorate blood, whereas heavy, greasy foods bog down digestion and create more stagnation.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat warming, blood-moving foods like turmeric, ginger, black sesame, and dark leafy greens. Avoid cold, raw foods and icy drinks that can congeal blood. Gentle daily exercise like walking or yoga helps keep Qi and blood flowing. Apply a warm compress to the lower abdomen during pain.

### Kidney Yin Deficiency With Empty-Heat Blazing
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/kidney-yin-deficiency-with-empty-heat-blazing)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Scanty but prolonged bright-red bleeding, Night sweats and five-palm heat, Dry throat and mouth, especially at night, Lower back soreness and weakness, Dizziness and tinnitus, Mental restlessness and irritability
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Staying up late, Spicy, fried, or barbecued foods, Alcohol and coffee, Overwork and chronic stress, Hot weather or saunas
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Early, consistent bedtime, Cooling, moist foods (pear, tofu, mung bean), Gentle, non-sweaty movement, Quiet, dim environment in the evening
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Kidney Yin is the body’s cooling and moistening reserve. When it runs low, a subtle but persistent heat-called Empty-Heat-flares up and disturbs the uterus. This heat agitates the blood and prevents it from settling, so the period becomes scanty yet stretches on for days.
The bleeding is typically bright red because of the heat, but the flow is light because the root Yin and blood are insufficient. Night sweats, hot palms and soles, and a dry throat are telltale signs that the Yin is failing to anchor the body’s warmth.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red, thin, and dry with little or no coating, possibly with cracks. Pulse: fine (xi) and rapid (shu), especially weak at the rear positions.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Kidney Yin is like the body's coolant. Late nights, overwork, and emotional strain drain it further, allowing empty heat to rise and disturb the uterus. Spicy and heating foods add fuel to that fire, making the bleeding more persistent. Cooling, moistening foods and adequate rest replenish Yin, while gentle movement keeps Qi flowing without overheating. A calm, dim environment in the evening helps the body anchor its warmth downward, so the blood can settle.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Go to bed by 10:30 p.m.-late nights are the fastest way to burn Kidney Yin. Eat cooling, moistening foods like pears, black beans, goji berries, spinach, and millet. Sip warm water throughout the day and avoid spicy, fried, or barbecued foods, as well as alcohol and coffee, which add heat and dry out Yin.
Gentle exercise like walking or yin yoga helps, but avoid drenching sweats. A calm, unrushed daily rhythm protects the Yin you are rebuilding.

### Damp-Heat in the Lower Burner
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/damp-heat-in-the-lower-burner)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Dark-red, sticky menstrual blood with a foul odor, Yellow, foul-smelling vaginal discharge, Lower abdominal fullness and burning sensation, Feeling of heaviness in the legs and lower body
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Spicy, greasy, or fried foods, Alcohol, Hot, humid weather, Stress and frustration, Overwork and insufficient rest
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Cooling, light meals, Staying in a cool, dry place, Gentle walking, Rest
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Damp-Heat accumulates when heavy, greasy foods and humid environments overload the body's ability to process fluids. This sticky, hot mixture sinks to the lower burner, invading the uterus and the Chong and Ren vessels. The Heat agitates the blood, making it move recklessly, while the Dampness makes it turbid and hard to stop-so periods become prolonged, with dark, sticky, foul-smelling flow and often a yellow, odorous discharge.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red body with a yellow greasy coating, thickest at the root. Pulse: slippery (滑 huá) and rapid (数 shuò), often soggy (濡 rú), more pronounced at the rear position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Damp-Heat thrives on rich, spicy, and greasy foods that generate internal Dampness and Heat, and it worsens in humid environments. Cooling, light foods and dry surroundings help clear Heat and drain Dampness, while rest and gentle movement support the Spleen's ability to transform and transport fluids.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Avoid greasy, spicy, sweet, and dairy-heavy foods that create Dampness and Heat. Eat light, cooling foods like mung beans, barley, cucumber, and celery. Stay hydrated but avoid cold drinks. Keep the genital area clean and dry, and avoid sitting on damp surfaces. Gentle exercise like walking helps circulate Qi and drain Dampness.

## คำถามที่พบบ่อย (Frequently Asked Questions)
### How long does it take for TCM to shorten my period?
Most women notice a difference within one to two menstrual cycles. Excess patterns like Blood Stagnation or Damp-Heat often respond faster - bleeding becomes less clotted and shorter within 4-6 weeks. Deficiency patterns, such as Qi not controlling Blood or Kidney Yin Deficiency, may need 2-3 months of consistent herbs and acupuncture to rebuild the body’s reserves and fully normalize the cycle.

### Can I still take my birth control pill while using Chinese herbs?
Yes, in most cases. Herbal formulas for prolonged bleeding do not generally interfere with hormonal contraceptives. To be safe, take your herbs at least two hours apart from your pill. Always inform your TCM practitioner about any medications you are using, including hormonal birth control, so they can choose the most appropriate formula.

### What foods should I avoid if my period drags on?
In general, avoid raw, cold, and greasy foods, which can weaken your digestive Qi and worsen Dampness. If your bleeding is bright red or you feel hot, also reduce spicy foods, alcohol, and coffee. Focus on warm, cooked meals that are easy to digest - soups, stews, and steamed vegetables are excellent choices.

### Is it safe to use acupuncture and herbs for prolonged bleeding if I'm trying to conceive?
Yes, TCM is often used to support fertility by regulating the menstrual cycle. Acupuncture and herbal formulas for prolonged bleeding can be safely used while trying to conceive, as they aim to restore balance and create a healthier uterine environment. However, it’s essential to work with a qualified practitioner who can adjust treatment according to your cycle phase and any pregnancy risk.

### My period is dark and clotted - does that mean I have a serious blockage?
In TCM, dark, clotted blood indicates Blood Stagnation, which means the flow is not moving smoothly. It’s not necessarily a 'blockage' in the Western sense, but rather a sign that the body’s circulation in the pelvic area needs support. This pattern is very treatable with herbs that move blood and acupuncture. However, if you have severe pain or very large clots, it’s wise to get a medical check-up to rule out fibroids or other structural issues.

### I feel exhausted and my period is pale and thin - what does that mean?
This combination strongly suggests Qi not controlling Blood, a pattern where your body’s vital energy is too weak to hold blood in the vessels. The fatigue, pale tongue, and thin, light-colored flow are classic signs. Treatment focuses on strengthening Spleen Qi with herbs like Huang Qi and Ren Shen, and acupuncture points such as Zusanli ST-36. Rest and warm, nourishing foods are also crucial.

## การวิจัยทางวิทยาศาสตร์สมัยใหม่ (Modern Scientific Research)
1. **Acupuncture for menorrhagia: a systematic review**
   *วารสารวิชาการ: Smith CA, Armour M, Zhu X, et al. Acupuncture for menorrhagia: a systematic review. BJOG. 2016;123(8):1294-1303.*
   This systematic review evaluated the effectiveness of acupuncture for heavy menstrual bleeding. It found that acupuncture was associated with a significant reduction in menstrual blood loss compared to no treatment or sham acupuncture, and was comparable to conventional medical treatments. The authors noted that acupuncture may be a useful option for women seeking non-pharmacological approaches.

2. **Treating Gynaecological Disorders with Traditional Chinese Medicine: A Review**
   *วารสารวิชาการ: Zhang X, et al. Treating Gynaecological Disorders with Traditional Chinese Medicine: A Review. Afr J Tradit Complement Altern Med. 2010;7(3):188-195.*
   This review discusses the TCM approach to common gynaecological conditions including abnormal uterine bleeding. It outlines the key patterns such as Qi deficiency and Blood stasis, and describes herbal formulas like Gui Pi Tang and Tao Hong Si Wu Tang. The authors emphasize the importance of pattern differentiation and the potential of TCM as an integrative therapy.
