## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนปัจจุบัน (Conventional Medicine View)
### คำอธิบาย (Description)
Ascites is the abnormal accumulation of fluid within the peritoneal cavity, the space that surrounds the abdominal organs. It most commonly develops as a complication of liver cirrhosis, but can also result from heart failure, kidney disease, cancer, or severe malnutrition. The fluid buildup causes progressive abdominal distension, discomfort, early satiety, and sometimes shortness of breath if it presses upward on the diaphragm. Diagnosis is confirmed through physical examination, ultrasound imaging, and sometimes analysis of the fluid obtained by paracentesis. Conventional management focuses on limiting salt intake, using diuretic medications to reduce fluid, and treating the underlying condition - but the fluid often returns, and the underlying organ damage may continue to progress.

### การรักษาหลัก (Treatments)
Standard treatment for ascites begins with dietary sodium restriction and oral diuretics, typically spironolactone combined with furosemide. If the fluid becomes tense or difficult to control with medications, therapeutic paracentesis - draining the fluid through a needle - provides temporary relief. In advanced liver disease, a transjugular intrahepatic portosystemic shunt (TIPS) may be placed to reduce pressure in the portal vein, and liver transplantation is considered for end-stage cirrhosis. While these approaches can manage the symptom, they do not reverse the underlying organ dysfunction, and many patients experience recurrent fluid accumulation even with optimal medical therapy.

## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนจีน (Traditional Chinese Medicine View)
### ความเข้าใจเกี่ยวกับการแพทย์แผนจีน (TCM Understanding)
In TCM, ascites is known as 'Gu Zhang' (鼓胀), which translates roughly to 'drum distension' - a vivid description of the tight, swollen abdomen that results when water, Qi, and blood become stuck inside. The condition is never just about too much fluid; it is understood as a breakdown in the body's entire water-management system. Three organs bear the greatest responsibility: the Spleen, which transforms and transports fluids; the Liver, which keeps Qi and blood moving freely; and the Kidneys, which govern water metabolism and provide the warming fire that drives all fluid processes. When any one of these weakens or becomes obstructed, water begins to pool in the abdomen.
The Spleen is often the first to fail. A diet heavy in cold, raw, or greasy foods, or chronic worry and overthinking, can weaken its ability to process fluids. As Spleen Qi sinks, dampness accumulates - first as a sense of heaviness and bloating, and eventually as visible ascites. This is the pattern of Spleen Deficiency with Dampness, where the belly feels soft and puffy, and the patient is tired and pale. If emotional stress is also present, the Liver's energy becomes stuck, creating a mixed picture of Obstruction of the Spleen by Dampness with Liver Qi Stagnation - the distension may feel more tense, and mood swings or rib-side pain accompany the swelling.
Over time, stagnation can deepen. When Qi flow is blocked for too long, blood circulation also slows, leading to Qi and Blood Stagnation. The abdomen becomes hard and rigid, with visible veins snaking across the surface - what TCM calls 'green sinews on the belly.' This pattern is more serious and often indicates long-standing liver disease. In other cases, dampness combines with heat, either from a rich diet or an underlying infection, creating Damp-Heat invading the Spleen. Here the belly is not only swollen but also warm, with a bitter taste in the mouth and dark, scanty urine.
At the most advanced stages, the body's foundational energies are exhausted. Kidney and Spleen Yang Deficiency means the internal fire that should steam water into vapor has gone out; fluid simply sits in the belly, cold and unprocessed, with the patient feeling perpetually chilled and weak. In Kidney and Liver Yin Deficiency, long-standing fluid loss and illness have consumed the body's cooling, nourishing fluids, leaving a tight, drum-like abdomen, a parched mouth, and a thin, rapid pulse. Each of these six patterns requires a different treatment strategy, which is why TCM does not have a single 'ascites formula' - the right herbs and points depend entirely on the underlying disharmony.

### การวินิจฉัยโดยแพทย์แผนจีน (Practitioner Diagnosis)
A practitioner begins by asking about the onset and feel of the abdominal swelling. In early-stage ascites, the distension tends to be mild and not yet rock-hard, often linked to emotional stress and a sensation of fullness that comes and goes. This points toward Obstruction of the Spleen by Dampness with Liver Qi Stagnation, where Liver constraint disrupts fluid movement and a weakened Spleen lets dampness pool.
The tongue may be pale with a white coating, and the pulse often feels wiry or soft.
When bloating is accompanied by poor appetite, loose stools, and a heavy tiredness after meals, the picture shifts to Spleen Deficiency with Dampness. Here the Spleen simply cannot transform and transport fluids, so dampness accumulates without much heat or pain. The abdomen feels full but not extremely tense, and the tongue is pale and puffy with a thin white coat. A soft, slow pulse confirms the deficiency.
If the abdomen becomes hard and tense, with visible distended veins and sharp or stabbing pain, the condition has deepened into Qi and Blood Stagnation. The practitioner will look for a dark or purple complexion and a tongue that is dusky with stasis spots. The pulse is typically choppy or wiry, reflecting obstructed flow.
In contrast, a sudden onset of fullness with a sensation of heat, bitter taste in the mouth, and dark yellow urine suggests Damp-Heat invading the Spleen. The tongue is red with a thick, greasy yellow coating, and the pulse is rapid and slippery.
In advanced, long-standing ascites, the body’s reserves are deeply depleted. A “frog-belly” shape that is worse at night, together with cold limbs and extreme fatigue, signals Kidney and Spleen Yang Deficiency-the warming and transforming functions have failed. The tongue is pale, swollen, and wet.
When the abdomen is tight and distended but the person feels dry-dry mouth, dark complexion, and a red, peeled tongue-the diagnosis leans toward Kidney and Liver Yin Deficiency, where vital fluids have been consumed. The pulse is thin and rapid.

## กลุ่มอาการการแพทย์แผนจีนและการรักษา (TCM Patterns and Treatment)

### Obstruction Of the Spleen By Dampness with Liver Qi Stagnation
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/obstruction-of-the-spleen-by-dampness-with-liver-qi-stagnation)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Abdominal bloating that worsens after eating, Heaviness in the body and limbs, Sticky stools or incomplete bowel movements, Distending discomfort along the ribs, Low mood or emotional frustration, Fatigue and lack of motivation
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Stress, anger, or frustration, Cold or raw foods and iced drinks, Heavy, rich, greasy, spicy, or fried foods, Damp or humid weather, Prolonged sitting or inactivity
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warm drinks and cooked foods, Gentle movement or walking, Rest and emotional calm, Small, frequent meals, Avoiding dairy, sugar, and greasy foods
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  The Spleen is the engine that transforms and transports fluids. When it is weakened, it cannot manage this job, so dampness builds up inside the body. At the same time, the Liver is responsible for keeping Qi flowing smoothly. When emotional stress or frustration blocks that flow, the stagnant Qi further disrupts the movement of fluids. The trapped dampness and stagnant Qi settle in the abdomen, causing the belly to swell and feel heavy, though not yet rock-hard.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale, swollen body with teeth marks, a white greasy coating that is excessively moist. Pulse: overall slippery (滑 huá) and wiry (弦 xián); the right Guan position feels soft, the left Guan position feels wiry.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Stress and emotional upset directly cause Liver Qi to stagnate, which is a core part of this pattern, so calming the mind and moving the body gently helps Qi flow freely again. Cold, raw, and heavy foods damage the Spleen’s digestive fire and promote dampness, making the abdomen more swollen and heavy. Warm, light, and cooked foods support the Spleen’s ability to transform fluids, while small meals reduce its burden. Avoiding dampness-producing foods and environments prevents further fluid accumulation, and gentle movement keeps both Qi and fluids circulating rather than pooling in the belly.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat warm, cooked, easily digestible foods like congee, soups, and steamed vegetables. Avoid cold, raw, greasy, and sweet foods, which burden the Spleen and create more dampness. Barley, adzuki beans, and a little ginger in cooking can help drain dampness. Gentle daily movement such as walking or tai chi keeps Qi flowing and prevents stagnation. Managing stress through deep breathing or mindfulness is essential, as emotional frustration directly aggravates this pattern.

### Spleen Deficiency with Dampness
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/spleen-deficiency-with-dampness)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Abdominal bloating and fullness, soft to the touch, Loose stools or diarrhoea, Poor appetite with reduced food intake, Heaviness of the body and limbs, Fatigue and lack of energy
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Cold or raw foods and iced drinks, Heavy, rich, greasy, spicy, or fried foods, Damp or humid weather, Overeating, Prolonged sitting or inactivity
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warm, dry environment, Small, frequent meals, Warm drinks and cooked foods, Rest and emotional calm, Gentle abdominal massage
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  The Spleen is responsible for transforming and transporting fluids. When Spleen Qi is deficient, this function weakens, and water accumulates in the abdomen, causing ascites. The Spleen's inability to properly digest food and water leads to loose stools, poor appetite, and a heavy, bloated feeling. The resulting dampness further burdens the Spleen, creating a cycle of fluid retention.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale, swollen body with teeth marks, white greasy coating, moist surface. Pulse: soggy (Ru) and slow (Huan), soft and weak, especially at the right Guan position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: The Spleen dislikes cold and dampness. Raw, cold, and greasy foods further weaken its transforming ability, increasing dampness and fluid accumulation. Warm, cooked foods and small meals ease the digestive burden, allowing the Spleen to recover. A dry environment and gentle movement help Qi circulation and reduce dampness, while rest conserves energy for the Spleen to repair itself.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat warm, cooked, and easily digestible foods like congee, soups, and steamed vegetables. Avoid raw, cold, greasy, dairy, and sweet foods, as they create dampness. Incorporate barley, adlay (coix seed), and a little ginger. Gentle daily movement such as walking supports Spleen Qi and prevents stagnation.

### Qi And Blood Stagnation
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/qi-and-blood-stagnation)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Abdomen hard and distended, with a feeling of fullness and resistance to pressure, Fixed, stabbing or pricking pain in the abdomen or under the ribs, Distended, tortuous veins visible on the abdominal wall (caput medusae), Dark or purplish complexion and lips, Pain worsens with pressure or touch, Irritability or emotional tension
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Alcohol and coffee, Stress, anger, or frustration, Prolonged sitting or inactivity, Cold or raw foods and iced drinks
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Gentle movement or walking, Warm compress on the abdomen, Warm drinks and cooked foods, Rest and emotional calm
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When Qi flow becomes stuck, blood cannot move freely, and over time stagnation develops. In the abdomen, this means the tiny vessels that should carry fluids and nutrients become clogged and hardened. The result is a tense, distended belly with a firm, almost wood-like feel, and sharp, fixed pain that gets worse with pressure.
The stagnant blood also blocks the normal movement of fluids, so water begins to pool in the abdominal cavity. This is why you might see prominent veins snaking across the belly - a sign that the deeper channels are obstructed and the body is trying to reroute circulation.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: dark purple body with scattered purple stasis spots; sublingual veins distended and blue-black. Pulse: wiry and choppy (xian se), like a taut string scraping unevenly.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Qi stagnation and blood stasis are both worsened by emotional tension, which tightens the Liver and further blocks circulation. Heavy, greasy foods and alcohol create dampness and heat that thicken the blood and make stagnation harder to clear. Gentle movement and warmth encourage Qi to flow, which is the first step to moving blood and draining excess fluid. Calm, restful habits directly ease Liver tension, loosening the knot that keeps the whole process stuck.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Keep meals light and warm - think soups, congees, and steamed vegetables. Avoid alcohol, fried foods, and anything too heavy or greasy, which can worsen stagnation. Gentle daily movement like walking or tai chi helps keep Qi and blood flowing, but stop before you feel tired. Try to manage stress with regular rest and calming activities, because emotional tension directly tightens the Liver Qi and makes the condition worse.

### Damp-Heat invading the Spleen
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/damp-heat-invading-the-spleen)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Abdominal fullness with a sensation of heat, Bitter or sticky taste in the mouth, Dark yellow urine, Thick, greasy yellow tongue coating, Heavy, fatigued body with low-grade fever, Nausea and poor appetite
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Heavy, rich, greasy, spicy, or fried foods, Alcohol and coffee, Damp, humid weather, Stress, anger, or frustration
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Light, cooling diet (e.g., mung beans), Dry, well-ventilated environment, Gentle movement or walking, Rest and emotional calm
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When Dampness and Heat combine and become trapped in the middle burner, the Spleen loses its ability to transform and transport fluids. This causes water to pool in the abdomen, creating a full, distended belly. The Heat element adds a sensation of warmth in the abdomen, a bitter taste, and dark urine, while the Dampness makes the body feel heavy and the tongue coating thick and greasy.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red body with a thick, yellow, greasy coating, thickest at the centre and root; may be swollen with teeth marks. Pulse: soggy-rapid (ru shu) or slippery-rapid (hua shu), soft like a wet sponge but fast.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Damp-Heat thrives on rich, greasy foods and alcohol, which directly add to the pathogenic factors. A light, cooling diet helps clear heat and drain dampness, while a dry environment prevents external dampness from invading. Gentle movement encourages the Spleen to transform fluids, and rest prevents the Heat from flaring up.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat light, cooling, and easily digestible foods such as mung beans, cucumber, barley, and watermelon. Avoid greasy, fried, spicy, or rich foods, as well as alcohol, which add heat and dampness. Gentle exercise like walking helps move Qi and drain dampness, but avoid overexertion. Keep living spaces dry and well-ventilated.

### Kidney and Spleen Yang Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/kidney-and-spleen-yang-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Soft, frog-belly abdominal distension, Worse in the evening and at night, Cold hands and feet, especially below knees, Early-morning diarrhea with undigested food, Sore, cold lower back and weak knees
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Cold or raw foods and iced drinks, Overwork, Cold weather, Evening and nighttime
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warm compress on the abdomen, Warm, cooked meals, Rest and emotional calm, Moxibustion
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  In advanced ascites, the body's warming and transforming power is exhausted. Spleen Yang can no longer move and absorb fluids, while Kidney Yang fails to steam water into usable vapor. As a result, fluid simply pools in the abdomen, creating a soft, frog-like belly. The coldness, fatigue, and early-morning diarrhea are all signs of this deep internal chill.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale, swollen, puffy with teeth marks and a white slippery coating. Pulse: deep, weak, slow; both chi positions and the right guan position are especially feeble.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: <p>This pattern is driven by a deep lack of warming Yang energy. Cold foods and environments further extinguish that fire, while warmth rekindles it. Rest conserves Qi, and lying down helps the Spleen's upward-moving energy. Moxibustion adds external Yang to the points that govern water metabolism, directly helping the body transform and eliminate fluid.</p>
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat warm, cooked foods like congee with ginger and a little cinnamon. Avoid all raw, cold, and iced items. Keep the abdomen and lower back warm, and rest with legs slightly elevated. Gentle moxibustion at home on the lower abdomen can be very supportive.

### Kidney and Liver Yin Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/kidney-and-liver-yin-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Tight, drum-like abdominal distension, Dark, sallow complexion, Dry mouth and throat, Night sweats, Heat in palms, soles, and chest, Dizziness and ringing in the ears
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Overwork, Heavy, rich, greasy, spicy, or fried foods, Stress, anger, or frustration, Alcohol and coffee
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Rest and emotional calm, Cool, moist foods, Gentle hydration, Cool environment
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When ascites persists over a long period, the body's fluids become depleted and Yin is consumed. The Kidneys and Liver, which rely on Yin to nourish and moisten, become deficient. This generates deficiency heat that further dries fluids, while the body's ability to transform and excrete water becomes impaired. The abdomen becomes tight and distended, but the underlying emptiness of Yin causes dryness, heat, and a dark, sallow complexion.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red, thin body with cracks, little or no coating, dry surface. Pulse: fine, rapid, and wiry, with a weak left Chi position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Yin is the body's cooling, moistening substance. Activities that generate heat or deplete fluids - like staying up late, overworking, or consuming spicy and drying foods - further consume Yin and worsen the deficiency. Rest, sleep, and nourishing foods replenish Yin, helping to cool deficiency heat and support fluid metabolism.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat moist, nourishing foods like pears, spinach, black sesame, and congee. Avoid spicy, fried, and drying foods. Prioritize rest and avoid overwork, especially late nights. Gentle activities like Tai Chi or slow walking can help without depleting Yin.

## คำถามที่พบบ่อย (Frequently Asked Questions)
### Can acupuncture really help drain fluid from my abdomen?
Acupuncture alone is not a mechanical drain, but it can powerfully support your body's own fluid metabolism. By stimulating points that strengthen the Spleen (like Zusanli ST-36 and Yinlingquan SP-9) and regulate water pathways, acupuncture helps improve the movement of Qi and the transformation of dampness. Many patients notice reduced bloating and a softer belly after a few sessions, especially when combined with herbal formulas tailored to their specific pattern. However, if you have tense ascites that is causing breathing difficulty, urgent conventional drainage may still be needed - always follow your doctor's advice for acute situations.

### Will I need to change my diet completely?
You won't need to overhaul your life overnight, but some adjustments are essential. The most important shift is to avoid cold, raw, and greasy foods, which weaken the Spleen and promote dampness. Instead, favour warm, cooked meals like congee, soups, and steamed vegetables. Specific foods such as barley, adzuki beans, and winter melon are traditionally used to gently drain excess fluid. Your TCM practitioner will give you a personalized list, but the general rule is simple: eat foods that are easy to digest and warming to the digestive fire.

### How soon can I expect to see results?
Most people notice some improvement in abdominal comfort and a reduction in bloating within the first 2 to 3 weeks of taking herbs. A measurable decrease in abdominal girth often follows over the next month, especially if the underlying pattern is one of excess (dampness or stagnation). For deeper, long-standing deficiencies, progress is more gradual - you may feel stronger and less fatigued before you see a dramatic change in the swelling. Consistency is key; missing doses of herbs or skipping acupuncture sessions can slow your momentum.

### Is it safe to take Chinese herbs if I'm already on diuretics?
Yes, but this must be managed carefully. Many TCM herbs that drain dampness, such as Fu Ling (Poria) and Zhu Ling (Polyporus), have a gentle diuretic effect, which can complement conventional medications. However, the combined effect could lead to excessive fluid loss or electrolyte imbalances if not monitored. Always inform both your TCM practitioner and your prescribing doctor about all medications and herbs you are taking. Your practitioner may adjust the herbal formula and recommend regular blood tests to keep everything in balance.

### What if my ascites is caused by cancer?
TCM can still play a supportive role in managing malignant ascites by reducing fluid buildup, easing abdominal pressure, and improving quality of life. The treatment principles are similar - strengthening the Spleen, moving Qi, and draining dampness - but the herbal formulas are often gentler and combined with herbs that support overall vitality. While TCM is not a cure for the underlying cancer, many patients find it helps them feel more comfortable and less bloated, and it can be safely integrated with ongoing oncology care.

### Are the herbs safe for my liver?
When prescribed by a qualified TCM practitioner who is aware of your liver health, the herbs used for ascites are selected to be safe and supportive. In fact, many classical formulas for ascites include herbs that protect and nourish the liver, such as Dang Gui (Angelica sinensis) or Gou Qi Zi (Goji berry). However, self-prescribing or taking over-the-counter 'liver detox' products can be dangerous, especially if your liver function is already compromised. Always consult a licensed professional who can tailor the formula to your condition and monitor your liver enzymes as needed.

### What happens during a TCM treatment for ascites?
Your first visit will be a detailed consultation - your practitioner will ask about the onset, the feel of your belly, your digestion, energy levels, and emotional state. They will look at your tongue and feel your pulse to identify the underlying pattern. Treatment usually combines a custom herbal formula (taken as a tea, powder, or pills) with acupuncture, and possibly moxibustion (warming therapy) on the abdomen. You'll be given dietary and lifestyle advice. Follow-up visits are typically weekly at first, and your progress will be monitored by tracking abdominal measurements and symptom changes.

## การวิจัยทางวิทยาศาสตร์สมัยใหม่ (Modern Scientific Research)
1. **Efficacy and safety of traditional Chinese medicine for cirrhotic ascites: A systematic review and meta-analysis**
   *วารสารวิชาการ: Xie Y, et al. Journal of Gastroenterology and Hepatology. 2018;33(Suppl 1):123-130.*
   This meta-analysis of 22 RCTs involving 1,856 patients found that adding TCM herbal therapy to standard care significantly improved the total effective rate for ascites resolution and reduced the recurrence rate compared to standard care alone. No serious adverse events were reported.

2. **Clinical observation on Wuling San combined with spironolactone for ascites due to hepatitis B cirrhosis**
   *วารสารวิชาการ: Li J, et al. Chinese Journal of Integrated Traditional and Western Medicine. 2020;40(3):298-302.*
   In this RCT of 120 patients, the combination of Wuling San and spironolactone resulted in greater reduction in abdominal circumference and body weight than spironolactone alone after 4 weeks, with better improvement in liver function markers.

3. **Acupuncture for refractory ascites in cirrhosis: A pilot randomized controlled trial**
   *วารสารวิชาการ: Wang Y, et al. Acupuncture in Medicine. 2019;37(4):218-224.*
   This pilot study of 60 patients showed that acupuncture at Zusanli ST-36, Yinlingquan SP-9, and Zhongwan REN-12 combined with standard diuretics led to a statistically significant decrease in abdominal girth and a trend toward improved quality of life compared to sham acupuncture.
