## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนปัจจุบัน (Conventional Medicine View)
### คำอธิบาย (Description)
Western medicine defines vertigo as a false sensation of movement - often a spinning or whirling feeling - that arises from disturbances in the vestibular system of the inner ear or its connections to the brain. It is not a disease itself but a symptom with many possible causes, including benign paroxysmal positional vertigo (BPPV), vestibular neuritis, Meniere's disease, and vestibular migraine.
Diagnosis typically involves a detailed history, a physical exam that may include positional testing like the Dix-Hallpike maneuver, and sometimes hearing tests or imaging to rule out central causes such as stroke or tumor.

### การรักษาหลัก (Treatments)
Conventional treatment depends on the cause. For BPPV, canalith repositioning maneuvers (like the Epley maneuver) are highly effective. Acute attacks of vertigo are often managed with vestibular suppressants such as antihistamines (meclizine), benzodiazepines, or antiemetics.
For chronic conditions like Meniere's disease, a low-salt diet and diuretics may be prescribed, while vestibular rehabilitation exercises help the brain compensate for inner ear damage. In some cases, medications like betahistine or prophylactic drugs for vestibular migraine are used.

## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนจีน (Traditional Chinese Medicine View)
### ความเข้าใจเกี่ยวกับการแพทย์แผนจีน (TCM Understanding)
In TCM, the head is called the 'palace of clear Yang' - it should be light, clear, and well-nourished. Vertigo happens when something disturbs that clarity.
The most common culprit is the Liver. The Liver governs the smooth flow of Qi throughout the body, and when it becomes overactive - often from chronic stress, anger, or frustration - its Yang energy can surge upward like a pressure cooker, bringing heat and wind to the head. This produces the classic spinning vertigo with a pounding headache, red face, and irritability that many people experience during stressful periods.
But the Liver is only part of the story. The Spleen and Stomach are responsible for transforming food and drink into clear energy and managing moisture. When they are weakened - by poor diet, overwork, or worry - they fail to process fluids properly. These fluids turn into a heavy, turbid substance called Phlegm-Dampness, which rises and clouds the head's orifices.
The result is a very different kind of vertigo: a heavy, wrapped-up sensation in the head, nausea, brain fog, and a greasy tongue coating. This pattern is common in people who feel dizzy after rich meals or in damp weather.
Two deficiency patterns round out the picture. When Qi and Blood are depleted - from chronic illness, poor nutrition, or overwork - the brain simply doesn't get enough nourishment. The dizziness is mild but worsens with any exertion, and it comes with profound fatigue, a pale face, and a desire to lie down.
When Kidney Essence, the foundation of the brain's marrow, runs low - often with aging or constitutional weakness - the vertigo feels hollow and chronic, accompanied by tinnitus, lower back soreness, and poor memory.
A less common but important pattern involves Liver Qi stagnation that has turned into heat, where the vertigo is triggered specifically by emotional upset and comes with chest tightness, a bitter taste, and a red tongue.

### การวินิจฉัยโดยแพทย์แผนจีน (Practitioner Diagnosis)
A TCM practitioner begins by asking what the dizziness feels like and what triggers it. The quality of the spinning, the time of day it appears, and the sensations that accompany it - like nausea, ringing in the ears, or a heavy head - all act as signposts. These clues, together with tongue and pulse diagnosis, guide the practitioner toward one of several distinct patterns.
If the vertigo comes on suddenly and is accompanied by irritability, a bitter taste in the mouth, and a flushed face, the likely culprit is Liver Yang Rising. The tongue is often red with a yellow coating, and the pulse feels wiry, rapid, and forceful. This pattern is like a pressure cooker - too much heat and upward-driving energy in the Liver system that storms the head.
When vertigo worsens with emotional upset, and the person also feels tightness in the chest, frequent sighing, and a dry or bitter mouth, the picture shifts to Liver Qi Stagnation transforming into Heat. Here the tongue is red with a thin yellow coating, and the pulse is wiry and rapid. The stagnation creates heat that rises, but the emotional trigger is the key differentiator from the more explosive Liver Yang pattern.
A heavy, wrapped-up sensation in the head - as if a wet towel were draped over it - points toward Phlegm-Dampness in the Middle-Burner. The dizziness often comes with nausea, a foggy mind, and a greasy white coating on a swollen tongue. The pulse feels slippery like pearls rolling on a plate. This pattern arises when the digestive system fails to transform fluids, generating a murky dampness that clouds the head.
In contrast, vertigo that worsens with tiredness and physical effort suggests Qi and Blood Deficiency. The person looks pale, feels weak, and may have heart palpitations. The tongue is pale with a thin white coat, and the pulse is thin and weak. The brain simply isn’t receiving enough nourishment, so any exertion makes the spinning worse.
Chronic, low-grade vertigo accompanied by tinnitus, sore lower back, and poor memory is typical of Kidney Essence Deficiency. The tongue is pale with a thin coating, and the pulse is deep and thready. This pattern reflects a deeper depletion of the body’s foundational reserves, leaving the “sea of marrow” (the brain) underfilled.

### Liver Yang Rising
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/liver-yang-rising)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Throbbing or distending headache, Irritability and quick temper, Flushed face and red eyes, Tinnitus (ringing in the ears), Dizziness worse with emotional stress, Lower back and knee soreness
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Anger and frustration, Stress and emotional upset, Alcohol and spicy food, Overwork and lack of rest, Hot weather or stuffy environments
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Rest and sleep, Cool, quiet, dark room, Cooling foods and drinks, Stress reduction and relaxation, Applying a cool compress to the head
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Liver Yang Rising happens when the Liver’s cooling Yin and Blood are too depleted to anchor its active Yang. The unanchored Yang surges upward to the head, disturbing the clear orifices and causing the sensation of spinning vertigo. This upward rush of heat and pressure also produces the pounding temple headache, flushed face, and quick temper that often accompany the dizziness.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red body, redder on the sides, with a thin dry yellow or scanty coat, possibly trembling. Pulse: wiry, especially forceful at the left middle position, and may be fine and rapid if Yin deficiency is prominent.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Anger, frustration, and emotional stress directly stir Liver Qi, causing it to stagnate and then surge upward as rising Yang, which disturbs the head and triggers vertigo. Overwork and lack of sleep deplete Liver Yin, weakening the anchor that holds Yang down. Alcohol, spicy foods, and hot environments add heat, further fueling the upward rush. Rest, a cool dark room, and cooling foods help calm the Liver, drain heat, and allow Yang to settle back down, relieving the spinning sensation.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Avoid spicy, fried, and greasy foods, alcohol, and coffee, which can fuel heat and stir Liver Yang. Focus on cooling, light meals with plenty of vegetables. Sip chrysanthemum tea to gently clear the head. Prioritize stress management and calming activities like gentle walking or Tai Chi, especially in the evening.

### Qi and Blood Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/qi-and-blood-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Vertigo worse with exertion or standing, Pale face, lips, and nail beds, Persistent fatigue and weakness, Heart palpitations
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Overwork and lack of rest, Skipping meals, Standing up suddenly, Stress and emotional upset
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Rest and sleep, Warm, nourishing foods, Gentle exercise like Tai Chi, Regular mealtimes
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Qi and Blood are the fundamental substances that nourish the brain and keep the senses clear. When they are deficient, the 'sea of marrow' (the brain) does not receive enough nourishment, causing dizziness and a sensation of emptiness in the head. Because the body's energy is already low, any exertion-even standing up-further drains Qi, making the vertigo worse. The Spleen, which produces Qi and Blood, is often weak, leading to fatigue, poor appetite, and a pale complexion.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale body, may be thin or teeth-marked, thin white coating. Pulse: fine and weak (细弱 xì ruò).
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Overexertion and skipping meals further drain the already depleted Qi and Blood, so rest and regular, warm meals directly rebuild these vital substances. Gentle movement encourages the flow of Qi without consuming it, while sudden standing or heavy exertion demands a surge of Qi that the body cannot supply, triggering dizziness. Stress and worry knot the Spleen Qi, impairing its ability to produce Blood, which is why emotional calm and adequate sleep are essential.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat warm, nourishing meals like soups and stews with easily digestible proteins (chicken, beef, lentils) and cooked root vegetables. Avoid raw, cold, or heavy foods that tax the Spleen. Prioritize regular sleep and avoid overexertion; gentle daily movement such as walking or tai chi helps Qi circulate without depleting it.

### Phlegm-Dampness in the Middle-Burner
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/phlegm-dampness-in-the-middle-burner)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Head feels heavy, as if wrapped in cloth, Nausea or vomiting, Poor appetite and bloating after eating, Greasy, sticky white tongue coating, Drowsiness and heavy limbs
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Heavy, greasy meals, Damp or humid weather, Overeating, Cold raw foods, Sedentary lifestyle
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Eating warm, dry foods, Gentle exercise like Tai Chi, Sipping warm ginger tea, Avoiding dairy and greasy foods
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  The Spleen is responsible for transforming food and fluids into clear Qi. When it is weakened-often by poor diet or overthinking-it fails to manage moisture, which then congeals into Phlegm-Dampness. This turbid, heavy substance blocks the Middle Burner and rises to cloud the head's clear orifices, resulting in a heavy, foggy vertigo that often comes with nausea and a sensation as if the head is wrapped in cloth.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale and swollen with teeth marks, thick white greasy coating, excessively moist. Pulse: slippery (Hua), slow or moderate, soggy (Ru).
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Phlegm-Dampness forms when the Spleen is too weak to process moisture, often from a diet rich in cold, raw, or greasy foods. Warm, dry, and lightly spiced foods help the Spleen transform and transport fluids, while movement activates Qi to disperse Dampness. Cold and heavy foods, along with inactivity, further bog down the Spleen, causing more turbid phlegm to rise and cloud the head.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat warm, cooked meals like soups and stews. Avoid cold, raw, and greasy foods that create more Dampness. Gentle daily movement like walking helps the Spleen move Qi and transform moisture.

### Kidney Essence Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/kidney-essence-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Dizziness with a hollow, empty feeling in the head, Lower back and knee soreness and weakness, Poor memory and difficulty concentrating, Frequent urination at night
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Overwork and lack of rest, Excessive sexual activity, Ageing and long-term illness
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Rest and sleep, Warm, nourishing foods, Gentle exercise like Tai Chi
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Kidney Essence is the foundation of the brain's marrow. When it becomes depleted through ageing, overwork, or chronic illness, the sea of marrow is no longer adequately filled. This leaves the head poorly nourished, producing a chronic, hollow-feeling vertigo that often comes with tinnitus and a heavy, weak sensation in the lower back and knees.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale, thin body with little or no coating, possible fine cracks. Pulse: fine, deep, and weak at both rear (Kidney) positions.

- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Avoid overwork and chronic sleep deprivation, which drain essence. Eat warm, nourishing foods like black sesame, walnuts, goji berries, and bone broth. Gentle daily movement such as Tai Chi or walking supports Kidney Qi without exhausting it.

### Liver Qi Stagnation that transforms into Heat
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/liver-qi-stagnation-that-transforms-into-heat)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Vertigo triggered or worsened by emotional stress, Bitter taste in the mouth, Distending or burning pain in the ribcage, Irritability and explosive anger, Chest tightness, Red tongue body with yellow coating
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Anger and frustration, Spicy, greasy, or fried foods, Alcohol and coffee, Overwork and lack of rest
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Stress reduction and relaxation, Cooling foods and drinks, Gentle exercise like Tai Chi, Time alone to decompress
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When emotional stress, especially frustration and unexpressed anger, becomes chronic, the Liver's Qi flow stagnates. Over time this stuck energy generates heat, which rises along the Gallbladder channel to disturb the head's clear orifices, producing vertigo that often feels like a spinning or light-headed sensation triggered by tension.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red body, especially redder along the sides, with a yellow coating. Pulse: wiry and rapid (xian shu), most pronounced at the left middle (Guan) position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Anger and frustration cause Liver Qi to stagnate, which generates heat that rises to the head, directly triggering vertigo. Cooling foods and stress relief help clear heat and smooth Qi flow, preventing the upward disturbance.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat cooling, light foods like celery, cucumber, and chrysanthemum tea; avoid spicy, greasy, and alcohol. Practice stress management with meditation, deep breathing, or gentle exercise like walking or Tai Chi to keep Qi flowing smoothly.

## คำถามที่พบบ่อย (Frequently Asked Questions)
### How does acupuncture help with vertigo?
Acupuncture helps by rebalancing the flow of Qi and Blood to the head. Specific points on the head, neck, and body are chosen based on your pattern - for example, to subdue rising Liver Yang, clear Phlegm, or nourish deficient Qi and Blood. Many patients feel a sense of grounding and reduced spinning even during the first session, though lasting change requires a series of treatments.

### Can Chinese herbs stop the spinning sensation?
Yes, but the formula must match your pattern. A formula like Tian Ma Gou Teng Yin (Gastrodia-Uncaria Decoction) is designed to anchor rising Liver Yang and stop pounding vertigo, while Ban Xia Bai Zhu Tian Ma Tang (Pinellia-Atractylodes-Gastrodia Decoction) transforms Phlegm-Dampness and clears the heavy, foggy dizziness. Herbs are typically taken daily and work gradually to correct the root imbalance, so consistent use is key.

### How many treatments will I need?
For excess patterns like Liver Yang Rising or Phlegm-Dampness, many patients see noticeable improvement within 2-4 weeks of weekly acupuncture combined with daily herbs. Deficiency patterns, where the body's reserves need rebuilding, often require 3-6 months for lasting change. Your practitioner will reassess your progress regularly and adjust the treatment plan as needed.

### Is vertigo in TCM always related to the Liver?
Not always. While the Liver is a very common source - especially when stress and anger are triggers - vertigo can also arise from a weak Spleen generating Phlegm-Dampness, from deficient Qi and Blood failing to nourish the brain, or from depleted Kidney Essence. The quality of the dizziness and your other symptoms help your practitioner determine which organ system is primarily involved.

### Can I combine TCM with my vertigo medications?
In most cases, yes. Acupuncture is generally safe alongside conventional vertigo medications. If you are taking herbal formulas, it's important to inform both your TCM practitioner and your prescribing doctor, as some herbs can interact with sedatives, blood pressure medications, or anticoagulants. Never stop prescribed medications abruptly - work with your doctor to adjust them as your condition improves.

### What should I eat to reduce dizziness?
General dietary advice for vertigo includes avoiding greasy, heavy, and very spicy foods, which can create dampness or stir up internal wind. Eat warm, easily digestible meals like soups and congees. If your pattern is Phlegm-Dampness, ginger tea can be especially helpful; if you have a Liver pattern, reducing alcohol and caffeine often makes a big difference. Your practitioner will give you more specific guidance based on your pattern.
