## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนปัจจุบัน (Conventional Medicine View)
### คำอธิบาย (Description)
Contact dermatitis is an inflammatory skin reaction caused by direct contact with an irritant or allergen. It typically presents as a red, itchy rash that may blister, weep, or become dry and cracked. Diagnosis is usually based on the appearance and location of the rash and a history of exposure to a known trigger, such as nickel, poison ivy, or harsh chemicals. Patch testing can identify specific allergens in persistent cases.

### การรักษาหลัก (Treatments)
Standard treatment involves identifying and avoiding the trigger, along with topical corticosteroids to reduce inflammation and itching. For severe or widespread cases, oral corticosteroids or antihistamines may be prescribed. Emollients and barrier creams are used to protect and repair the skin barrier.

## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนจีน (Traditional Chinese Medicine View)
### ความเข้าใจเกี่ยวกับการแพทย์แผนจีน (TCM Understanding)
In TCM, contact dermatitis is seen as an invasion of external pathogens - usually Damp-Heat or toxic Heat - that penetrate the skin when the body's defensive Qi is weak or the constitution is intolerant. The skin is the first line of defense, and when it is overwhelmed, redness, swelling, and oozing erupt. This acute reaction is the body's attempt to expel the pathogen, but if the underlying imbalance isn't corrected, the condition can become chronic.
The most common acute pattern is Damp-Heat. Dampness makes the skin weep and swell, while Heat brings redness, burning, and intense itching. The tongue is red with a thick yellow greasy coating, and the pulse is rapid and slippery. This pattern often appears shortly after contact with an irritant and responds well to herbs that clear heat and drain dampness.
When the rash persists for weeks or months and becomes dry, thickened, and scaly, TCM recognizes a shift to Blood Deficiency with External Wind. The initial Damp-Heat or repeated inflammation damages the body's Blood and fluids, leaving the skin undernourished. This dryness stirs up internal Wind, which causes the characteristic itching that moves around or worsens at night. The tongue is pale with a thin coat, and the pulse is thin and wiry.
In some people, especially children or those with weak digestion, a deeper pattern of Spleen Deficiency with Dampness underlies the skin problem. When the Spleen fails to transform fluids, internal dampness accumulates and overflows to the skin as lingering papules, vesicles, and mild eczema. This pattern often comes with bloating, loose stools, and fatigue, and requires strengthening the digestion to clear dampness from the root.

### การวินิจฉัยโดยแพทย์แผนจีน (Practitioner Diagnosis)
A TCM practitioner begins by looking at the skin itself - its color, moisture, and texture - and asking about the timing of the outbreak. Acute rashes that appear shortly after contact with an irritant and look red, swollen, and weepy point toward one pattern, while a chronic, dry, thickened rash that flares repeatedly suggests a very different underlying imbalance.
When the skin is bright red, hot to the touch, and studded with tiny blisters that ooze fluid, the practitioner suspects Damp-Heat. The itching is often intense and burning. The tongue is usually red with a greasy yellow coating, and the pulse feels rapid and slippery. These signs confirm that dampness and heat toxins are stuck in the skin and need to be cleared.
If the rash has been around for a long time and the skin is dry, scaly, or even leathery, the focus shifts to Blood Deficiency with External Wind. The itch may be worse at night, and the person may look pale or feel tired. The tongue tends to be pale with a thin coat, and the pulse is thin and wiry. This picture tells the practitioner that the blood is not nourishing the skin, allowing dry wind to stir up itching.
In children or people with a history of digestive weakness, a Spleen Deficiency with Dampness pattern may show up as scattered, lingering papules that are less fiery but still itchy, often alongside loose stools and a poor appetite. The tongue is pale and puffy, possibly with a white coat, and the pulse is soft or slow. Here the root is an internal dampness that seeps out to the skin, and treatment must strengthen digestion as well as drain dampness.

## กลุ่มอาการการแพทย์แผนจีนและการรักษา (TCM Patterns and Treatment)

### Damp-Heat
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/damp-heat)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Bright red, swollen skin with weeping vesicles, Intense burning and itching, Rash appears quickly after contact, Feeling of heaviness in the body and head, Thirst with little desire to drink, Dark, scanty urine, Yellow greasy tongue coating
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Spicy, greasy, or fried foods, Hot, humid weather, Alcohol, Scratching or friction, Re-exposure to the irritant
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Cool compresses, Light, cooling foods, Keeping skin dry and clean, Rest and low stress
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When your constitution is intolerant to external irritants, damp-heat toxins can invade the skin. Dampness makes the skin weep, swell, and feel heavy, while heat brings redness, burning, and rapid inflammation. Together they block the channels at the surface, causing the classic acute rash with oozing vesicles and intense itching.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red body with a thick, yellow, greasy coating that is difficult to scrape off; may be swollen with teeth marks. Pulse: soggy-rapid (ru shu) or slippery-rapid (hua shu).
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Damp-Heat thrives on warmth and moisture, so spicy, greasy, and humid influences add fuel to the fire. Cooling compresses and foods directly counteract the Heat, while drying measures clear the Dampness. Scratching generates friction-heat and damages the skin, making the rash worse. Avoiding the trigger stops the external toxin from re-entering, giving the body a chance to clear the Damp-Heat already lodged in the skin.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Avoid spicy, greasy, fried, and sugary foods - they generate internal Dampness and Heat that feed the rash. Eat more cooling, damp-draining foods like mung beans, cucumber, watermelon, and lightly cooked leafy greens. Keep the affected skin clean and dry, and use cool (not hot) water for washing. Identify and strictly avoid the triggering substance. A cool compress of green tea or diluted apple cider vinegar can soothe acute burning.

### Blood Deficiency with External Wind
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/blood-deficiency-with-external-wind)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Dry, flaky, or thickened skin, Itching that worsens at night, Pale or sallow complexion, Aversion to wind or drafts, Dull, brittle nails and dry hair, Poor sleep or dream-disturbed sleep
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Dry, windy weather, Stress and overwork, Spicy, greasy, or fried foods, Hot showers and harsh soaps, Poor sleep
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Cool, humid air, Regular, gentle moisturizing, Adequate, restful sleep, Warm, nourishing meals
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When the body's Blood becomes deficient and can no longer nourish the skin, the skin turns dry, scaly, and fragile. This dryness stirs up internal Wind, which manifests as intense itching that comes and goes or shifts around the body. In chronic contact dermatitis, the initial Damp-Heat or toxin attack has drained the Blood over time, leaving the skin vulnerable to Wind and unable to repair itself, so the rash becomes thick, cracked, and persistently itchy rather than red and oozing.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale, thin body with a thin white and slightly dry coating. Pulse: thin (fine) and wiry, often with a floating quality at the superficial level.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Blood Deficiency leaves the skin undernourished and dry, so Wind easily penetrates and triggers itching. Wind itself is dry and moves erratically, which is why the itch shifts and worsens in dry, windy conditions. Stress and lack of sleep further deplete Blood and stir up Liver Wind, intensifying the rash. By nourishing Blood with food and rest, moisturizing to seal the skin, and avoiding drying triggers, you rebuild the skin's barrier and calm the internal Wind that drives the itch.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Moisturize the skin daily with gentle, fragrance-free creams and avoid hot showers or harsh soaps that strip natural oils. Eat blood-building foods like dark leafy greens, beets, red meat, bone broth, and black sesame seeds, while cutting back on spicy, fried, or drying foods and alcohol. Protect the skin from wind and sudden temperature changes, and prioritize restful sleep to allow the Blood to regenerate.

### Spleen Deficiency with Dampness
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/spleen-deficiency-with-dampness)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Loose stools or diarrhoea, Abdominal bloating after eating, Poor appetite and fatigue, Heaviness of the body and limbs, Dull, lingering papules or eczema (less red, more oozy)
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Cold, raw foods, Dairy and greasy foods, Damp or humid weather, Overwork and fatigue
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warm cooked meals, Rest and adequate sleep, Dry, warm environment, Gentle exercise like walking
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When the Spleen is constitutionally weak, it fails to transform and transport fluids properly. This leads to an internal buildup of dampness, which can overflow to the skin as lingering papules, vesicles, and damp eczema. Because the root is digestive weakness, the skin symptoms are almost always accompanied by loose stools, bloating, and poor appetite.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale, swollen body with teeth marks, white greasy coating, moist surface. Pulse: soggy (Ru) and slow (Huan), possibly slippery.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: The Spleen is the engine of digestion and fluid metabolism. Cold, raw, and greasy foods impair its function, generating more dampness that can surface on the skin. Warm, cooked foods and rest support the Spleen's transformative power, reducing internal dampness and thereby calming skin eruptions.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat warm, cooked foods like soups and congees; avoid raw, cold, and greasy foods that burden the Spleen. Keep the skin clean and dry, but avoid harsh soaps. Gentle daily movement helps Qi flow and supports digestion.

## คำถามที่พบบ่อย (Frequently Asked Questions)
### Can TCM help with acute contact dermatitis?
Yes. For acute Damp-Heat rashes that are red, weepy, and burning, TCM uses cooling and drying herbs internally and as topical washes. Many patients notice reduced redness and oozing within a few days. Acupuncture can also be used to quickly relieve itching and inflammation.

### How long does it take for the rash to go away with herbs?
Acute rashes typically improve within 1-2 weeks, and often clear completely in 2-4 weeks. Chronic dry, scaly rashes take longer - usually 4-8 weeks - because the underlying Blood deficiency needs to be rebuilt. If Spleen weakness is the root, expect 2-3 months of consistent treatment to strengthen digestion and prevent recurrence.

### Can I use TCM alongside steroid creams?
Yes. TCM can complement conventional treatments. If you are using prescription corticosteroid creams, do not stop them suddenly. Work with your doctor to taper as the rash improves. There are no known serious interactions, but always tell both your TCM practitioner and dermatologist about all treatments you are using.

### What foods should I avoid if I get contact dermatitis?
Generally, avoid spicy, greasy, and sugary foods that generate dampness and heat. Alcohol and caffeine can aggravate itching. For acute Damp-Heat rashes, favor cooling foods like cucumber, mung beans, and leafy greens. For chronic dry rashes, include blood-nourishing foods such as dark leafy greens, beets, and moderate amounts of lean protein.

### Is acupuncture effective for itching?
Yes, acupuncture can significantly reduce itching by calming the nervous system and moving stagnant Qi and Blood. Points like Quchi (LI-11) and Xuehai (SP-10) are commonly used to clear heat and cool the blood. Many patients feel immediate relief after a session, though repeated treatments are usually needed for lasting results.

### Can TCM prevent future flare-ups?
TCM aims to correct the underlying imbalance that makes you susceptible to contact dermatitis. By strengthening the Spleen, nourishing Blood, or clearing lingering dampness, the body becomes less reactive to triggers over time. Many patients find that after a course of treatment, they either stop reacting to the same triggers or have much milder reactions.

### Are there any side effects of Chinese herbs for skin?
When prescribed by a qualified practitioner, Chinese herbs are generally safe and well-tolerated. Some cooling herbs can be slightly hard on the digestion if taken on an empty stomach, so they are usually taken after meals. Rarely, allergic reactions to herbs can occur - inform your practitioner if you notice any new symptoms. Always use herbs under professional guidance.

### Is TCM safe for contact dermatitis during pregnancy?
Many TCM treatments can be adapted for pregnancy, but certain herbs that move Blood or strongly drain dampness are avoided. Always tell your practitioner if you are pregnant or trying to conceive. Topical herbal washes are generally safe, but internal formulas must be carefully selected. Acupuncture is also safe when performed by a trained professional.
