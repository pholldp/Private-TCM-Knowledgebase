## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนปัจจุบัน (Conventional Medicine View)
### คำอธิบาย (Description)
Stomach cancer, also called gastric cancer, begins when cells in the lining of the stomach grow out of control. It often develops slowly over many years, and early symptoms - such as indigestion, bloating, or mild nausea - can be easy to overlook. As the disease advances, more noticeable signs like unintended weight loss, persistent stomach pain, difficulty swallowing, or vomiting may appear.
Diagnosis usually involves an upper endoscopy with biopsy, along with imaging like CT scans to determine the stage. Risk factors include chronic Helicobacter pylori infection, a diet high in smoked or salted foods, smoking, and certain genetic conditions.

### การรักษาหลัก (Treatments)
Standard treatment depends on the stage and may include surgery to remove part or all of the stomach, chemotherapy, radiation therapy, targeted drug therapy, and immunotherapy. For advanced disease, the focus often shifts to palliative care aimed at relieving symptoms and maintaining quality of life.

## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนจีน (Traditional Chinese Medicine View)
### ความเข้าใจเกี่ยวกับการแพทย์แผนจีน (TCM Understanding)
In TCM, the Stomach is the central cooking pot of the body, where food and drink are broken down and transformed into Qi and Blood. When the Spleen and Stomach are weak - perhaps from years of poor diet, overwork, or emotional stress - they lose their ability to process fluids and food properly. This leads to a buildup of dampness and phlegm, which can stagnate, generate heat, and over time congeal into masses. TCM sees cancer not as a sudden invader but as the end stage of a long process of internal disharmony.
The Liver also plays a crucial role. Chronic stress, frustration, or unexpressed anger can cause Liver Qi to stagnate. Since the Liver energy pathway runs through the stomach area, this stuck Qi can invade the Stomach, disrupting its downward-moving function and causing bloating, acid reflux, and pain. Over time, this stagnation can contribute to blood stasis and the formation of lumps.
As the disease progresses, it drains the body's most fundamental resources - Qi and Blood. This is why many patients experience profound fatigue, pallor, and weight loss. TCM therefore categorizes stomach cancer into patterns based on whether the dominant problem is deficiency (weakness of the Spleen and Stomach, or lack of Qi and Blood) or excess (damp-heat, phlegm, blood stasis, or Liver Qi stagnation). Often, a person will show a mixture of both, which guides the herbal formula selection.

### การวินิจฉัยโดยแพทย์แผนจีน (Practitioner Diagnosis)
When fatigue, poor appetite, and loose stools dominate, and the tongue is pale with a weak pulse, the root is Spleen and Stomach Qi Deficiency. This is the core weakness that often underlies the disease, where the digestive engine lacks the energy to function properly.
If stress or anger triggers epigastric distension, belching, and acid reflux, and the pain shifts with mood, Liver Qi Stagnation invading the Stomach is suspected. The tongue may be slightly red with a thin coating, and the pulse often feels wiry, reflecting constrained Qi flow.
A feeling of fullness and nausea with a bitter, sticky taste points to Damp-Heat in the Stomach and Spleen. The tongue coating is typically yellow and greasy, and the pulse may be slippery and rapid - signs of an internal environment that is both wet and overheated.
Fixed, stabbing pain that does not move, along with a dark complexion and a purple tongue with possible spots, signals Stomach Blood Stagnation. The pulse is often choppy or hesitant, indicating that blood is not flowing smoothly through the stomach’s collaterals.
As the condition advances, profound fatigue, pallor, emaciation, and dizziness emerge, with a very pale tongue and a thin, weak pulse. This is Qi and Blood Deficiency, reflecting severe depletion when the body’s vital substances are consumed by the disease.
A sensation of a lump or fullness, persistent nausea, and a thick, greasy tongue coating - even if the tongue body is pale - suggest Phlegm-Dampness accumulating in the middle burner. The pulse is often slippery, indicating pathological dampness that contributes to tumor formation.

### Spleen and Stomach Qi Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/spleen-and-stomach-qi-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Poor appetite and early satiety, Abdominal bloating that worsens after eating, Loose, unformed stools, Profound fatigue and physical weakness, Pale, sallow complexion, Weak, heavy limbs and reluctance to speak
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Raw, cold, or greasy foods, Overeating or large meals, Physical overexertion, Emotional stress and worry, Damp, cold weather
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warm, cooked meals, Small, frequent meals, Rest and adequate sleep, Gentle walking or movement, Warm compress on the abdomen
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When the Spleen and Stomach Qi are weak, the body's ability to transform food into usable energy and blood falters. This leads to a buildup of dampness, phlegm, and undigested matter in the middle burner. Over time, this stagnation can congeal into masses, while the underlying deficiency leaves the body without the strength to resist or contain them.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale, puffy body with tooth marks and a thin white coating. Pulse: weak and soft, especially in the right Guan (middle) position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: The Spleen is easily damaged by cold, raw, and heavy foods, which require more Qi to digest. Overeating overwhelms its already weak transforming ability, leading to bloating and fatigue. Rest conserves Qi, while worry knots the Spleen Qi, so managing stress and avoiding overexertion are crucial. Warmth and gentle movement help activate the Spleen's function without draining it.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat warm, well-cooked, easily digestible foods like congee, soups, and steamed vegetables. Avoid raw, cold, and greasy foods that tax the Spleen. Eat small, frequent meals and chew thoroughly. Prioritize rest and gentle movement such as walking or Qi Gong to support Qi without depleting it.

### Liver Qi Stagnation invading the Stomach
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/liver-qi-stagnation-invading-the-stomach)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Distending pain in the upper abdomen that radiates to the ribs, Frequent belching or acid reflux, Symptoms worsen with emotional stress or frustration, Irritability, moodiness, or frequent sighing, Sensation of a lump in the throat
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Emotional stress or anger, Irregular eating or skipping meals, Spicy, greasy, or fried foods, Overwork or prolonged mental strain
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Emotional calm and relaxation, Warm compress on the abdomen, Small, frequent meals, Gentle walking or movement
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When the Liver's Qi flow becomes stuck-often from chronic stress, frustration, or emotional upheaval-it can surge sideways and attack the Stomach. This disrupts the Stomach's natural downward-moving rhythm, causing Qi to rebel upwards. The result is bloating, belching, acid reflux, and a distending pain that radiates from the upper abdomen toward the ribs.
Over time, this Liver-Stomach disharmony weakens digestion, leading to poor appetite and nausea. Because the Liver's smooth flow is so closely tied to emotional state, the symptoms reliably flare up whenever you feel tense, angry, or upset.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: normal or slightly red body with redder sides, thin white coat. Pulse: wiry (弦 xián), like a taut string, especially strong at the left middle position (left guan).
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Emotional stress and anger are the primary engines of Liver Qi stagnation; when they build up, the Liver lashes out at the Stomach, causing pain and reflux. Soothing the mind and keeping a steady routine directly reduces this attack. Warmth and gentle movement help unblock stuck Qi, while small, warm meals ask less of the weakened Stomach, preventing further rebellion of Qi upward.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat small, warm, cooked meals at regular times-avoid skipping meals or eating on the run. Steer clear of spicy, greasy, or overly sour foods that can further agitate the Liver. Gentle daily movement like walking, tai chi, or stretching helps keep Qi flowing and eases emotional tension. Prioritize emotional well-being: journaling, breathing exercises, or speaking with a counselor can reduce the stress that fuels this pattern.

### Damp-Heat in Stomach and Spleen
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/damp-heat-in-stomach-and-spleen)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Epigastric bloating and fullness, Nausea or vomiting, Sticky, incomplete bowel movements, Bitter, sticky taste in the mouth, Heavy sensation in the limbs, Thick yellow greasy tongue coating
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Spicy, greasy, or fried foods, Alcohol, Overeating or large meals, Damp, humid weather, Emotional stress and worry
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Light, bland meals, Warm water with lemon, Gentle walking or movement, Rest after meals
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When greasy, spicy, or sweet foods overwhelm the digestive system, dampness and heat build up in the stomach and spleen. This simmering internal environment can, over time, damage the stomach lining, create stagnation, and generate toxic heat that contributes to cancerous growth. The typical symptoms of bloating, nausea, and a sticky bitter taste reflect this damp-heat burden.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red body, possibly swollen with teeth marks, thick yellow greasy coating especially at the center and root. Pulse: soggy (ru) and rapid (shu), often slippery (hua).
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Greasy, spicy, and sweet foods directly generate dampness and heat in the digestive system, which is the root of this pattern. Alcohol and overeating further burden the spleen and stomach, making symptoms worse. Light, bland meals and gentle movement support the spleen's ability to transform and transport fluids, while rest after meals prevents qi stagnation.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Focus on light, easily digestible foods like congee, steamed vegetables, and moderate amounts of lean protein. Avoid greasy, fried, spicy, and very sweet foods that create dampness and heat. Small, frequent meals are better than large ones. Gentle daily movement like walking helps the spleen transform dampness.

### Stomach Blood Stagnation
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/stomach-blood-stagnation)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Sharp stabbing or cutting pain in the upper abdomen, Pain is fixed in one spot and does not move, Pain worsens with pressure on the abdomen, Pain worse at night, Vomiting of dark or coffee-ground material, Black, tarry stools, Dark or purplish lips and dull complexion
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Cold or raw foods and iced drinks, Overeating or heavy, greasy meals, Pressure on the abdomen, Emotional stress and bottled-up anger, Lying down immediately after eating
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warm compress on the abdomen, Gentle walking or movement, Small, frequent meals, Rest in a quiet, dark room
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  In stomach cancer, long-standing Qi stagnation, Heat, or Deficiency can damage the tiny blood vessels (collaterals) inside the stomach wall. When Blood flow becomes sluggish and congeals, it creates stabbing pain that stays in one spot and often feels worse at night. Over time, this static Blood can clump together, contributing to the formation of a palpable mass or tumor.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: purple or dark dusky body, possible purple stasis spots, distended sublingual veins, thin white coat. Pulse: choppy (涩, Sè) or wiry (弦, Xián).
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Cold constricts and slows down Blood flow, which worsens stasis, so warmth and gentle movement help open the collaterals and ease pain. Stress and anger cause Qi to stagnate, and since Qi moves Blood, this directly aggravates the stuck, stabbing sensation. Small warm meals require less digestive effort, preventing further injury to the already compromised Stomach lining.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat small, warm, easy-to-digest meals like congee or stewed vegetables. Avoid cold, raw, and greasy foods that slow down circulation. Gentle movement like walking or Tai Chi keeps Qi and Blood flowing, but rest when pain is severe. A warm compress on the upper abdomen can soothe stabbing pain.

*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/qi-and-blood-deficiency)*

- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Physical overexertion, Irregular eating or skipping meals, Raw, cold, or greasy foods, Emotional stress and worry, Chronic illness or prolonged recovery
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Rest and adequate sleep, Warm, cooked meals, Gentle walking or movement, Small, frequent meals, Emotional calm and relaxation
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Stomach cancer is a deeply consumptive illness. As it advances, it drains the body's most fundamental resources - Qi and Blood. The Spleen, which normally transforms food into Qi and Blood, becomes too weak to do its job. Without enough Qi to power the body and enough Blood to nourish the tissues, a person becomes profoundly exhausted, pale, and thin. This is the stage where the cancer's presence has led to a systemic state of emptiness and malnourishment.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale body, may be thin or show teeth marks, thin white coating. Pulse: fine (thin) and weak, especially in the right Guan position (Spleen and Stomach).

Rest and proper nutrition allow the Spleen to slowly rebuild these resources, while overexertion, cold foods, and emotional strain further deplete them. By protecting the Spleen's transforming function and avoiding unnecessary drains on energy, the body can begin to recover its vitality.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat warm, easily digestible, nutrient-dense foods such as congee with a little lean protein, bone broth, and well-cooked vegetables. Avoid raw, cold, and greasy foods that tax the Spleen. Prioritize rest and avoid overexertion; gentle movement like short walks or qigong can support Qi flow without depleting energy. Small, frequent meals are easier on a weakened digestive system.

### Phlegm-Dampness in the Middle-Burner
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/phlegm-dampness-in-the-middle-burner)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Thick white greasy tongue coating, especially in the center, Persistent fullness and stuffiness in the upper abdomen, Heavy, sluggish sensation in the body and limbs, Nausea and queasiness, worse after eating, Sticky or bland taste in the mouth, no thirst, Loose, sticky stools that feel incomplete
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Raw, cold, or greasy foods, Dairy and greasy fried foods, Damp, humid weather, Overeating or eating late at night, Sedentary lifestyle
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warm, cooked meals, Ginger or cardamom tea, Gentle walking or movement, Dry, sunny weather
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  The Spleen is meant to transform food and fluids into usable energy. When it weakens, fluids stagnate and slowly congeal into a thick, sticky pathogen called phlegm-dampness. This sludge-like substance settles in the stomach and middle burner, obstructing the flow of Qi and creating a congested environment where masses can eventually form. The blockage produces the persistent sensation of fullness, heaviness, and nausea - as if the digestion is clogged and nothing can move through properly.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale, swollen body with scalloped edges, thick white greasy coating that looks wet or oily, especially in the center. Pulse: slippery (滑 huá) and soggy (濡 rú), most noticeable at the right middle position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: <p>Phlegm-dampness is a cold, heavy pathogen that forms when the Spleen is too weak to transform fluids. Cold and raw foods further damage the Spleen's digestive fire, while greasy and dairy foods directly add to the dampness burden. Damp weather overwhelms the body's ability to stay dry.</p><p>Warmth - from cooked foods and spices like ginger - supports the Spleen's transformative function and helps evaporate dampness. Gentle movement stimulates Qi circulation, which is essential because Qi moves fluids; when Qi stagnates, dampness pools and thickens into phlegm.</p>
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Avoid cold, raw, and greasy foods that burden the Spleen. Eat warm, well-cooked meals with aromatic spices like fresh ginger, cardamom, and a little black pepper. Gentle daily walking helps circulate Qi and dry dampness. Avoid eating late at night and never overeat.

## คำถามที่พบบ่อย (Frequently Asked Questions)
### Can TCM cure stomach cancer?
TCM is not a standalone cure for stomach cancer and should never replace conventional medical treatment. However, it is a powerful complementary therapy that can strengthen your body, reduce the side effects of chemotherapy and radiation, improve appetite and energy, and support your overall recovery. Many patients find that TCM helps them tolerate conventional treatments better and maintain a higher quality of life.

### Is acupuncture safe during chemotherapy?
Yes, acupuncture is generally safe and well-tolerated during chemotherapy when performed by a qualified practitioner. It can help relieve nausea, fatigue, pain, and neuropathy. Always inform your acupuncturist about your full treatment plan, and make sure they use sterile, single-use needles, as your immune system may be compromised.

### Can I take Chinese herbs with my cancer medications?
Many Chinese herbs can be taken alongside conventional cancer treatments, but it is critical that both your oncologist and your TCM practitioner know exactly what you are taking. Some herbs may interact with chemotherapy drugs or affect liver enzymes. Blood-moving herbs like Dang Gui or Chuan Xiong should be used cautiously if you are on blood thinners. Never stop or change your conventional medications without medical advice.

### What foods should I eat if I have stomach cancer?
In general, TCM recommends warm, cooked, easily digestible foods that support the Spleen and Stomach. Think congee, soups, steamed vegetables, and lean proteins. Avoid raw, cold, greasy, spicy, or overly sweet foods, as they can create dampness and phlegm. Ginger and cardamom tea can help settle the stomach. Your TCM practitioner can give you more specific dietary advice based on your individual pattern.

### How often do I need acupuncture treatments?
For active support during cancer treatment, weekly acupuncture sessions are common initially. As your condition stabilizes, the frequency may be reduced to every other week or monthly. Your practitioner will adjust the schedule based on your energy levels, symptoms, and how you respond.

### Will TCM help with the pain from stomach cancer?
Yes, acupuncture and herbal formulas can be very effective for managing cancer-related pain, especially when the pain is due to Qi stagnation or blood stasis. Many patients find they need less pain medication when they incorporate TCM. Acupuncture points like Zusanli ST-36 and Zhongwan REN-12 are commonly used to relieve stomach pain and regulate digestion.
