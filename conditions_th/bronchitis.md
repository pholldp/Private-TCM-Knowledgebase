## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนปัจจุบัน (Conventional Medicine View)
### คำอธิบาย (Description)
Bronchitis is an inflammation of the bronchial tubes that carry air to and from the lungs. It often develops after a cold or respiratory infection and can be acute (short-term, lasting a few weeks) or chronic (a persistent, often recurring condition). Typical symptoms include a cough that brings up mucus, chest discomfort, mild fever, and fatigue. Diagnosis is usually based on a physical exam and listening to the lungs, sometimes with a chest X-ray to rule out pneumonia.

### การรักษาหลัก (Treatments)
Most acute bronchitis is viral and does not require antibiotics. Treatment focuses on rest, increased fluids, and over-the-counter remedies like cough suppressants, expectorants, and pain relievers. A doctor may prescribe an inhaler (bronchodilator) to open airways or, if a bacterial infection is suspected, antibiotics. Chronic bronchitis management often includes inhaled steroids, bronchodilators, pulmonary rehabilitation, and smoking cessation.

## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนจีน (Traditional Chinese Medicine View)
### ความเข้าใจเกี่ยวกับการแพทย์แผนจีน (TCM Understanding)
In TCM, a cough is always a sign that the Lung Qi is rebelling upward instead of descending smoothly. The Lungs are considered the "tender organ" - the most exterior of the internal organs - and are easily affected by external climatic factors like Wind, Cold, Heat, and Dryness. When these pathogens invade, they block the Lung's ability to diffuse and descend Qi, forcing it upward as a cough.
But not all coughs come from the outside. Internal imbalances also play a major role. The Spleen, for example, transforms fluids in the body; if it is weak, fluids accumulate into Phlegm, which then settles in the Lungs and triggers a productive cough. Emotional stress can cause the Liver to generate Heat that flares up to attack the Lungs. And when the body's deeper reserves are depleted - such as Lung Qi or Yin Deficiency - the Lungs lack the strength or moisture to keep Qi descending, leading to a chronic, weak cough.
The sound, timing, and sputum of the cough reveal which pattern is active. A loud, hacking cough with thin white phlegm points to Wind-Cold. A frequent cough with sticky yellow phlegm and a sore throat suggests Wind-Heat. Copious yellow-green phlegm with chest tightness signals Phlegm-Heat. A dry, tickling cough that worsens at night is often Lung Yin Deficiency.
This is why one Western diagnosis of bronchitis can have several TCM causes - and why the treatment must be tailored to the individual pattern, not just the symptom name.

### การวินิจฉัยโดยแพทย์แผนจีน (Practitioner Diagnosis)
A TCM practitioner begins by listening to the cough itself. The sound, timing, and the type of sputum offer the first big clues. A loud, hacking cough with thin white sputum points toward an external invasion, while a weak, chronic cough with little sputum suggests an internal deficiency. The tongue and pulse then confirm the pattern.
If the cough came on suddenly after a chill and brings up thin, white sputum, with an aversion to cold and a floating tight pulse, the pattern is Wind-Cold invading the Lungs (风寒束肺, fēng hán shù fèi). The tongue coating is thin and white. This is the classic early-stage acute bronchitis.
When the cough is frequent, the sputum is yellow and sticky, and the person has a sore throat and a floating rapid pulse, the diagnosis shifts to Wind-Heat invading the Lungs (风热犯肺, fēng rè fàn fèi). The tongue may be red with a thin yellow coating. This pattern often follows a wind-cold invasion that has turned hot.
As bronchitis deepens, Phlegm-Heat in the Lungs (痰热壅肺, tán rè yōng fèi) may develop. The cough produces copious yellow or green purulent sputum, the chest feels tight, and the pulse is slippery and rapid. The tongue is red with a thick, greasy yellow coating. This is a more severe inflammatory picture.
In chronic or recurrent bronchitis, the cough becomes weak and the person feels short of breath. This is Lung Qi Deficiency (肺气虚, fèi qì xū). The sputum is thin and white, the voice is low, and the pulse is weak and thready. The tongue is pale with a thin white coating. The body lacks the strength to clear the lungs.
A dry, hacking cough that worsens at night, with scant sticky sputum and a dry throat, suggests Lung Yin Deficiency (肺阴虚, fèi yīn xū). The tongue is red with little or no coating, and the pulse is thin and rapid. This pattern is common in lingering coughs after an infection or in smokers.
Finally, if the cough is dry and irritating, with an itchy throat and little to no sputum, and it occurs in a dry season or climate, the pattern is Exterior Dry Cold invading the Lungs (凉燥犯肺, liáng zào fàn fèi). The tongue may be dry with a thin white coating, and the pulse is floating. This is a dryness attack that injures the lung’s moisture.

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Cough with thin white watery phlegm, Chills and aversion to cold, Nasal congestion with clear runny discharge, Itchy throat, Headache and body aches
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Cold weather or exposure, Cold or raw foods and iced drinks, Overexertion or overwork
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warmth, Hot ginger tea, Rest, Steam inhalation
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When Wind-Cold attacks the body, it constricts the Lung’s ability to diffuse and descend Qi. This trapped Qi rebels upward, producing a cough with thin, white, watery phlegm. The exterior cold also blocks the pores, causing chills, an absence of sweating, and the characteristic floating tight pulse.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: normal or slightly pale body with a thin, white, moist coating. Pulse: floating (fu) and tight (jin).
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Wind-Cold constricts the Lung’s dispersing and descending functions, leading to cough and chills. Warmth and hot drinks help open the pores and dispel the external pathogen, while rest conserves Qi to fight the invasion. Cold exposure and drafts introduce more pathogenic cold, and overexertion weakens the defensive Qi, allowing the pathogen to linger.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Drink warm fluids like ginger tea with a little brown sugar to help dispel cold. Avoid cold drinks and raw foods. Keep the chest and neck covered, especially in windy weather. Rest and stay warm to support the body’s exterior defenses.

### Wind-Heat invading the Lungs
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/wind-heat-invading-the-lungs)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Cough with thick yellow phlegm, Fever stronger than chills, Sore or scratchy throat, Thirst and dry mouth, Nasal congestion with yellow mucus
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Spicy, greasy, or fried foods, Windy, dry weather, Smoke, dust, or polluted air, Overexertion or overwork
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Light, non-greasy meals, Sipping warm chrysanthemum tea, Rest, Using a humidifier
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When a wind‑heat pathogen enters the body, it attacks the Lungs first because the Lungs are the most exterior of the internal organs. The heat component inflames the airways and forces the Lung Qi to rebel upward, producing a cough. At the same time, heat dries body fluids, turning the phlegm thick and yellow and causing a sore, dry throat.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red tip, thin yellow coating, body slightly red. Pulse: floating (fú) and rapid (shuò).
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Wind‑heat is an exterior hot pathogen that inflames the Lungs and dries fluids. Spicy and fried foods add internal heat, while wind and smoke drive the pathogen deeper and irritate the airways. Rest conserves the body’s defensive Qi, and cooling, moistening foods and drinks help clear heat and restore the Lung’s natural moisture, making it easier to expel phlegm and calm the cough.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat light, cooling foods like pear, apple, and cucumber; sip warm chrysanthemum or mint tea throughout the day. Avoid spicy, greasy, and deep‑fried foods that add heat. Rest in a calm, draft‑free room and use a humidifier to keep the throat moist.

### Phlegm-Heat in the Lungs
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/phlegm-heat-in-the-lungs)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Cough with thick yellow or green sticky phlegm that is hard to bring up, Chest tightness and feeling of heat or fever, Rapid breathing or wheezing, Thirst and flushed face, Restlessness and irritability
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Spicy, greasy, or fried foods, Dairy and sweets, Hot, stuffy environments, Overexertion or overwork, Emotional stress
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Cool, fresh air, Warm drinks, Eating pear or radish, Rest and avoiding exertion, Light, non-greasy meals
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When the Lungs are invaded by an external pathogen like Wind-Heat and the body fails to clear it, or when rich, greasy food and emotional stress create internal Heat, fluids can condense into sticky Phlegm. This Phlegm combines with Heat and blocks the Lungs, preventing them from descending Qi properly. The result is a deep, productive cough with thick yellow phlegm that feels stuck in the chest.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red body, possibly swollen at the front, with a yellow greasy coating; red prickles may appear on the front third. Pulse: slippery and rapid (滑数 huá shuò).
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Phlegm-Heat forms when Dampness and Heat accumulate in the body. Rich, greasy, and spicy foods directly create Dampness and Heat, while dairy and sweets generate Phlegm. Hot surroundings and overexertion further agitate internal Heat. Cooling foods like pear and radish clear Heat and resolve Phlegm, while rest conserves Qi so the body can clear the Lungs. Fresh air and warm fluids help the Lungs descend Qi and thin the sticky phlegm, making it easier to expel.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Avoid dairy, sweets, fried foods, and spicy dishes, as they generate Phlegm and Heat. Eat cooling, moistening foods like Asian pear, radish, water chestnut, and loofah. Sip warm water throughout the day to help thin the phlegm. Keep the room air fresh but not too dry; a cool-mist humidifier can soothe the airways. Rest is essential - exertion stirs up Heat and makes the cough worse.

### Lung Qi Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/lung-qi-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Weak, low-force cough that worsens with effort, Spontaneous daytime sweating, Shortness of breath and fatigue, Pale face and catching colds easily
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Overexertion or overwork, Cold or raw foods and iced drinks, Cold weather or exposure
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Rest, Warmth, Gentle exercise or deep breathing, Warm, nourishing foods
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  The Lungs govern Qi and respiration, keeping the breath descending smoothly. When Lung Qi is deficient, the organ lacks the strength to keep Qi moving downward, so Qi rebels upward as a weak, low-force cough. The weakened defensive Qi (Wei Qi) also fails to protect the body, making it easy for wind-cold to invade and trigger repeated bouts of bronchitis. The cough is weak because there isn't enough Qi to produce a strong expulsion, and the thin, watery phlegm reflects the body's inability to transform fluids properly.

Rest and warmth conserve the little Qi you have, and gentle movement encourages circulation without draining your reserves. Warm, nourishing foods provide the building blocks to slowly replenish Lung Qi.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat warm, cooked foods that are easy to digest, such as congee, soups, and stews. Add a little fresh ginger or a slice of astragalus root to broths to gently support Qi. Avoid raw, cold, and greasy foods that can further weaken digestion. Gentle, consistent movement like Tai Chi or walking helps Qi flow without draining it, but avoid pushing through exhaustion.

### Lung Yin Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/lung-yin-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Dry, hacking cough with little or no phlegm, Scanty, sticky phlegm that is hard to expectorate, Dry throat and mouth, hoarse voice, Night sweats and afternoon tidal heat sensation, Flushed cheeks, especially in the afternoon, Restlessness and irritability from internal heat
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Overexertion or overwork, Spicy, greasy, or fried foods, Smoke, dust, or polluted air, Emotional stress
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Rest, Cool, fresh air, Pear juice or honey water, Gentle exercise or deep breathing
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Your Lungs need a moist, cool environment to function smoothly. When Lung Yin-the nourishing, cooling aspect-becomes depleted, the Lung tissues dry out and lose their ability to properly descend Qi. This creates a pattern of empty heat that rises upward, triggering a dry, hacking cough that is worse at night when Yin energy should be dominant but is lacking. Without enough moisture, any phlegm that forms is scanty, sticky, and hard to bring up.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red, thin body with cracks, little or no coating, dry surface. Pulse: fine (thin) and rapid, especially weak at the right cun (Lung) position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Yin represents the body's cooling, moistening reserves. Late nights, overwork, and stress consume Yin, while spicy or drying foods generate internal heat that further depletes it. Rest, early sleep, and moistening foods replenish Yin and help the Lungs regain their ability to descend Qi smoothly. Cool, humid air soothes the dry throat and reduces coughing fits.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Focus on moistening, cooling foods: pear, white fungus, lily bulb, honey, and almond milk. Avoid spicy, fried, or drying foods like chips, coffee, and alcohol. Sip warm water throughout the day and use a humidifier in dry rooms. Prioritize rest and go to bed early-late nights are especially damaging to Yin. Gentle breathing exercises or restorative yoga can support Lung function without overexertion.

## คำถามที่พบบ่อย (Frequently Asked Questions)
### Can TCM help with a wet, productive cough?
Yes, especially when the cough is caused by Phlegm-Heat or Phlegm-Dampness. Herbal formulas like Qing Qi Hua Tan Wan are designed to transform and expel sticky phlegm from the Lungs, while acupuncture points such as Fenglong ST-40 help resolve Phlegm at its source. The goal is not to suppress the cough but to clear the phlegm so the Lungs can heal.

### How quickly does acupuncture work for bronchitis?
Many patients feel some relief after the first session, particularly if the cough is acute and caused by an external pathogen. For chronic bronchitis, it may take 4-6 weekly sessions to notice a lasting change in cough frequency and severity. Acupuncture is often combined with herbs for faster and more complete results.

### Is it safe to take Chinese herbs with my antibiotics or inhalers?
In most cases, yes. Herbal formulas for bronchitis are generally compatible with antibiotics, bronchodilators, and inhaled steroids. However, you should always inform both your TCM practitioner and your medical doctor about all medications you are taking. Some herbs that move Blood (like Dang Gui) may interact with anticoagulants, so full disclosure is essential.

### Can TCM prevent recurrent bronchitis?
Absolutely. While acute treatment focuses on clearing the current infection, TCM also addresses the underlying weakness that makes you susceptible. For example, if you tend to catch every cold that goes around, your practitioner may work to strengthen Lung Qi with herbs like Huang Qi and points like Zusanli ST-36. Over time, this can reduce the frequency and severity of bronchitis episodes.

### What's the difference between a Wind-Cold and Wind-Heat cough?
A Wind-Cold cough usually starts suddenly after exposure to cold, with thin white phlegm, chills, and no sweating. A Wind-Heat cough often follows a sore throat, with sticky yellow phlegm, a fever that feels stronger than the chill, and a red tongue with a yellow coating. The treatment is very different - warming herbs for Wind-Cold, cooling herbs for Wind-Heat - so getting the pattern right is crucial.

### Do I need to change my diet while being treated for bronchitis?
Yes, diet can support or undermine your recovery. In general, avoid cold, raw, greasy, and dairy-heavy foods, which can create more phlegm. Warm, cooked foods like congee and soups are ideal. Specific foods like pear and radish help clear Phlegm-Heat, while ginger tea is excellent for Wind-Cold. Your practitioner will give you personalized advice based on your pattern.

### Can TCM treat chronic bronchitis even if I'm a smoker?
Yes, but the outcome depends heavily on smoking cessation. TCM can reduce cough, clear phlegm, and improve lung function, but continued smoking will constantly reintroduce Heat and Phlegm, undermining the treatment. The best results come when TCM is combined with a genuine effort to quit or cut down significantly.
