## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนปัจจุบัน (Conventional Medicine View)
### คำอธิบาย (Description)
Irritable Bowel Syndrome is a chronic functional gastrointestinal disorder defined by recurrent abdominal pain associated with a change in stool frequency or form. The pain is often relieved by defecation, and the altered bowel habit may be diarrhea-predominant (IBS-D), constipation-predominant (IBS-C), or mixed. Diagnosis is made using the Rome IV criteria, which require symptoms to be present for at least six months, with no alarm features such as blood in the stool or unintended weight loss.
Conventional testing - colonoscopy, blood work, imaging - typically shows no structural or biochemical abnormality. The condition affects roughly 10-15% of the global population, with women diagnosed more often than men, and it significantly impacts quality of life through pain, urgency, bloating, and unpredictable bowel habits.

### การรักษาหลัก (Treatments)
First-line management usually involves dietary adjustments such as a low-FODMAP diet, increased soluble fiber, and avoiding trigger foods. Medications target the predominant symptom: antispasmodics for cramping, loperamide for diarrhea, laxatives or lubiprostone for constipation. When pain is prominent, low-dose tricyclic antidepressants or SSRIs are used for their neuromodulating effects. Psychological therapies like cognitive behavioral therapy and gut-directed hypnotherapy are also recommended for moderate to severe cases.

## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนจีน (Traditional Chinese Medicine View)
### ความเข้าใจเกี่ยวกับการแพทย์แผนจีน (TCM Understanding)
In TCM, IBS is understood primarily as a disorder of the Liver and Spleen. The Liver is responsible for the smooth, free flow of Qi throughout the body, and it is especially sensitive to emotional strain. When stress, frustration, or worry build up, the Liver Qi stagnates and then surges sideways to attack the Spleen. The Spleen governs the transformation of food into energy and the transportation of fluids - so when it is bullied by the Liver, its work is disrupted.
This is why so many people with IBS notice their symptoms flare with emotional upset: the Liver-Spleen axis is directly wired to your mood.
But the story doesn't end there. Chronic Liver Qi stagnation can generate Heat, which dries fluids and causes constipation, or it can combine with Dampness to create a sticky, burning diarrhea. On the other hand, if the Spleen's yang energy becomes weak from overwork, cold foods, or long-standing illness, it fails to warm the intestines, leading to chronic loose stools and a feeling of cold.
In some cases, the Kidney yang - the body's foundational fire - becomes depleted, producing a classic early-morning diarrhea that leaves you exhausted. This is why one Western diagnosis of IBS can have so many different TCM faces.
A TCM practitioner listens carefully to the quality of your symptoms. Is the diarrhea urgent and burning, or painless and watery? Does the pain move around or stay fixed? Are you more irritable or more fatigued? The tongue and pulse offer further clues - a red tongue with yellow coat points to Heat, while a pale, puffy tongue with teeth marks signals deficiency.
By matching your unique picture to one of the six core patterns, treatment can be precisely targeted, not just to calm the bowel, but to restore the organ relationships that allowed the problem to develop in the first place.

### การวินิจฉัยโดยแพทย์แผนจีน (Practitioner Diagnosis)
A practitioner begins by asking about your bowel rhythm, pain character, and emotional triggers. Irritable Bowel Syndrome in Chinese medicine is seen as a disharmony between the Liver and Spleen, so the first clues come from whether stress, frustration, or anxiety sets off your symptoms. The quality of the stool and the type of discomfort help narrow down which pattern is active.
If your main complaint is cramping pain and loose stools that strike suddenly when you are upset, and your tongue is pale with a thin coating and your pulse feels wiry, that points to Rebellious Liver Qi invading the Spleen. This is the classic root pattern, where emotional tension directly disrupts the digestive rhythm, often creating an alternating diarrhea-and-constipation picture.
When stools are urgent, burning, and foul-smelling with a sensation of incomplete emptying, and the tongue appears red with a yellow greasy coat, Damp-Heat in the Large Intestine is likely. This pattern flares during acute episodes, often after rich or spicy food, and the pulse will feel slippery and rapid. It represents a buildup of pathogenic moisture and heat in the bowels.
Other common patterns shift the picture toward cold or deficiency. Spleen Yang Deficiency brings chronic loose stools, fatigue, and a craving for warmth, with a pale puffy tongue and a slow weak pulse.
If Heat arises from long-standing stagnation, you may see constipation with hard stools, thirst, and a bitter taste. In deeper or older cases, Kidney Yang Deficiency causes early-morning diarrhea and deep coldness in the lower back and limbs.

- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Emotional stress, Irregular eating habits, Cold raw foods, Overwork, Frustration and anger
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Calming activities, Warm compress on abdomen, Small, regular meals, Warm cooked foods, Stress reduction
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  The Liver is responsible for the smooth flow of Qi throughout the body. When emotional stress, frustration, or worry builds up, the Liver Qi stagnates and then surges sideways to attack the Spleen. This disrupts the Spleen's ability to transform food and transport fluids, causing sudden abdominal cramping, diarrhea, and alternating bowel habits.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: normal or slightly pale body with possible teeth marks, thin white coating that may be slightly greasy. Pulse: wiry (弦 xián), especially at the left middle position, with the right middle position softer.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Emotional stress and frustration cause Liver Qi to stagnate and then attack the Spleen, directly triggering abdominal pain and diarrhea. Cold and raw foods further weaken the Spleen's digestive fire, while irregular eating disrupts the Spleen's rhythm. Calming activities and stress reduction soothe the Liver, and warm cooked foods support the Spleen, breaking the cycle of disharmony.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat warm, cooked foods and avoid cold, raw, or greasy items that burden the Spleen. Small, regular meals are better than large ones. Manage stress with gentle exercise like walking, qigong, or meditation. A warm compress on the abdomen can ease cramping.

### Liver Qi Stagnation with Spleen Qi Deficiency that transforms into Heat
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/liver-qi-stagnation-with-spleen-qi-deficiency-that-transforms-into-heat)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Distending rib pain that worsens with stress, Urgent, burning diarrhea after meals or stress, Irritability and quick temper, Bitter taste in the mouth
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Emotional stress and frustration, Spicy, greasy foods, Alcohol and caffeine, Irregular eating habits
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Stress reduction, Cooling foods (cucumber, mung bean), Gentle exercise, Regular meal times
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Chronic emotional stress and frustration cause the Liver Qi to stagnate. This stagnant Qi then attacks the Spleen, weakening its ability to transform food and fluids, which leads to bloating and loose stools. Over time, the trapped Qi generates Heat, and this Heat aggravates the intestines, producing the urgent, burning diarrhea and bitter taste characteristic of this pattern.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red body with redder sides, teeth marks, and a thin to moderately thick yellow, slightly greasy coating. Pulse: wiry and rapid, especially taut at the left Guan (Liver) position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Emotional stress directly causes Liver Qi to stagnate and surge, which is the engine of this pattern. Spicy and greasy foods add Heat and Dampness to an already overheated system, while alcohol and caffeine further agitate Liver Yang. Cooling foods and stress reduction help drain the accumulated Heat, soothe the Liver, and allow the Spleen to recover its digestive function.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Focus on cooling, easy-to-digest foods like cucumber, mung beans, and lightly cooked leafy greens. Avoid spicy, greasy, and deep-fried foods that fuel Heat and Dampness. Eat smaller, regular meals and chew thoroughly. Daily stress management-such as walking, gentle yoga, or meditation-is essential to prevent Liver Qi from stagnating.

### Damp-Heat in the Large Intestine
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/damp-heat-in-the-large-intestine)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Abdominal cramping with urgent need to go, Burning sensation at the anus during or after bowel movements, Foul-smelling, yellow-brown, sticky stools, sometimes with mucus or blood, Incomplete evacuation - the feeling of not being done, Feeling of heaviness in the body and limbs, Thirst and a sensation of body heat
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Spicy, greasy, or fried meals, Alcohol and rich desserts, Hot, humid weather, Emotional stress or anger, Overeating or irregular meals
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Light, bland, cooling foods, Warm water and barley tea, Rest and a calm environment, Passing a full bowel movement
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When Dampness and Heat combine and settle in the Large Intestine, they disrupt the bowel's normal function. The Heat creates an urgent, burning need to go, while the sticky Dampness makes stools foul-smelling, slimy, and hard to pass completely. This toxic combination irritates the intestinal lining, producing cramping pain, mucus, and the frustrating sensation that you are never quite finished.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red body with a thick, yellow, greasy coating, thickest in the centre and root. Pulse: slippery (滑 huá) and rapid (数 shuò).
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Damp-Heat is sticky and inflammatory by nature. Rich, greasy, and spicy foods add more Dampness and Heat to the system, directly fueling the intestinal irritation. Alcohol and emotional stress stir up internal Heat, making the urgency and burning worse. Cooling, bland foods and rest help drain the Dampness and clear the Heat, allowing the bowel to settle and function smoothly again.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Avoid greasy, fried, spicy, and sugary foods, as well as alcohol and dairy, which all generate Dampness and Heat. Eat cooling, bland foods like barley, mung beans, cucumber, and lightly cooked vegetables. Drink warm water throughout the day, but avoid ice-cold drinks. Gentle exercise like walking helps move Qi and drain Dampness, but rest during acute flare-ups.

### Spleen Yang Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/spleen-yang-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Dull abdominal pain that improves with warmth and pressure, Loose, watery stools with undigested food, Feeling cold easily, with cold hands and feet, Poor appetite and bloating after eating, Deep fatigue and heaviness in the limbs
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Cold raw foods, Cold weather, Overeating, Fatigue and overwork, Prolonged stress
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warmth on the abdomen, Warm cooked meals, Rest and lying down, Ginger tea, Gentle walking
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Your Spleen is like a gentle flame that transforms the food you eat into usable energy and warmth. When that flame grows too weak-from long-standing illness, overwork, or too much cold food-it can no longer cook and move things properly. This leaves food half-digested, so stools become loose, watery, or filled with undigested bits. Without enough warmth, the abdomen turns cold and achy, and the pain feels better when you press a hot water bottle against it or rest, because heat and stillness help the Spleen’s flame burn a little brighter.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale, puffy, and tender with scalloped edges (teeth marks), and a white slippery coating. Pulse: deep, slow, and weak, especially at the right guan (Spleen) position.

Cold foods and environments directly weaken that fire, leading to pain and loose stools. Warmth, both external and internal, rekindles the Spleen’s Yang, restoring digestion and easing pain. Rest conserves Qi that would otherwise be spent on activity, allowing the body to direct energy toward healing the digestive system.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat warm, easily digestible foods like congee, soups, and well-cooked vegetables. Avoid raw, cold, and greasy foods that further chill the Spleen. Add warming spices such as fresh ginger and a pinch of cinnamon to your meals. Keep your abdomen warm with a hot water bottle or gentle moxibustion, and practice gentle daily movement like walking to support Spleen Qi without exhausting it.

- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:

- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red body with redder sides, yellow coating. Pulse: wiry and rapid.

Spicy food and alcohol introduce external Heat that further dries the intestines. Cooling foods and calming practices help soothe the Liver, clear Heat, and restore moisture to the bowels, easing constipation and irritability.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Avoid spicy, greasy, and fried foods, as well as alcohol, which add Heat to the body. Emphasize cooling, high-fiber foods like cucumber, celery, and leafy greens. Drink peppermint or chrysanthemum tea to help clear Liver Heat. Practice stress-reducing activities like meditation, yoga, or deep breathing, as emotional tension directly fuels this pattern.

- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Cold or raw foods and iced drinks, Cold, damp weather, Overwork and exhaustion, Late nights and insufficient sleep, Standing or sitting on cold surfaces
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warm drinks and soups, Heating pad on the lower back or belly, Rest and staying warm, Moxibustion, Cooked ginger and cinnamon
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  The Kidneys house the body's foundational Yang, the fire that warms the Spleen and intestines. When Kidney Yang becomes weak, that fire dims and the Spleen loses its source of warmth, leading to poor digestion and loose stools. The early morning diarrhoea happens because Yang energy is at its lowest point just before dawn, and the cold, weak intestines cannot hold fluids.

Cold foods and overwork drain this fire, allowing internal Cold to settle in the intestines and cause loose stools. Warmth, rest, and moxibustion gently stoke the Gate of Life, restoring the fire that warms the Spleen and secures the bowels.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat only warm, cooked foods; avoid all raw, cold, or frozen items. Add warming spices like ginger, cinnamon, and cloves to your meals. Keep your lower back and abdomen warm, especially at night. Gentle morning walks help coax Yang energy upward, and a warm foot soak before bed can ease the chill.

## คำถามที่พบบ่อย (Frequently Asked Questions)
### Can acupuncture really help with IBS diarrhea?
Yes, acupuncture can be very effective for IBS diarrhea, especially when the diarrhea is triggered by stress or anxiety. Points like Taichong (LR-3) and Zusanli (ST-36) are used to smooth the Liver Qi and strengthen the Spleen, directly addressing the Liver-Spleen disharmony that drives many cases. Research supports its benefit in reducing stool frequency and abdominal pain. Most patients notice a calmer gut within a few sessions, though a full course of 8-12 weekly treatments is typical for lasting results.

### How long does it take for Chinese herbs to work for IBS?
You may feel some relief within the first 1-2 weeks - less cramping, more formed stools, or a drop in urgency. However, the full effect of a herbal formula builds over 4-8 weeks as it rebalances the underlying pattern. For chronic, deep-seated deficiency patterns like Kidney Yang Deficiency, expect to take herbs for 3-6 months to rebuild your body's reserves. Your practitioner will adjust the formula as your symptoms evolve, so it's not a static prescription.

### Is IBS caused by stress in Chinese medicine?
Emotional stress is a major trigger, but it's not the only cause. In TCM, stress causes Liver Qi to stagnate, which then disrupts the Spleen's digestive function - hence the gut-brain connection. However, dietary factors (too much cold, raw, or greasy food), overwork, and constitutional weakness can also directly damage the Spleen and Kidney yang, creating IBS patterns even in the absence of obvious emotional stress. The key is identifying which factor is primary in your case.

### What foods should I avoid with IBS according to TCM?
Across all patterns, TCM recommends avoiding cold and raw foods (iced drinks, salads, raw fruit in excess), greasy or fried foods, dairy, and spicy-hot foods, as these tend to create Dampness and irritate the gut. Alcohol and caffeine are also generally discouraged because they stoke Heat and stir up Liver Qi. Instead, favor warm, cooked, easily digested meals like rice congee, steamed vegetables, and small amounts of lean protein. Specific dietary advice will be tailored to your pattern - for example, someone with Damp-Heat might need to avoid rich, sweet foods, while someone with Spleen Yang Deficiency should emphasize warming ingredients like ginger and cinnamon.

### Can I take TCM herbs alongside my IBS medication?
In most cases, yes, but it's essential to coordinate with both your prescribing doctor and your TCM practitioner. Herbs are generally compatible with antispasmodics, fiber supplements, and low-dose neuromodulators. However, some herbs that move Blood or clear Heat may interact with anticoagulants or antiplatelet drugs. Always bring a complete list of your medications and supplements to your TCM consultation so potential interactions can be safely managed. Never stop prescribed medication abruptly without medical supervision.

### Can TCM cure IBS permanently?
TCM aims to bring the body back to a state of balance where symptoms no longer arise spontaneously. Many people achieve long-term remission - months or years without significant IBS episodes - after a thorough course of treatment and lifestyle adjustments. However, because the underlying constitution remains somewhat susceptible, major stressors or dietary lapses can trigger a recurrence. In that sense, TCM offers lasting control rather than a one-time "cure," and periodic tune-ups can help maintain your gut stability.
