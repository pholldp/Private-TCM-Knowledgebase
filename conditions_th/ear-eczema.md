## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนปัจจุบัน (Conventional Medicine View)
### คำอธิบาย (Description)
Ear eczema, also called otitis externa eczematosa, is an inflammatory skin condition affecting the external ear and ear canal. It typically causes itching, redness, scaling, and sometimes weeping or crusting. It can be part of a broader atopic dermatitis, a contact reaction to products like earrings or shampoos, or seborrheic dermatitis. Diagnosis is usually made by looking at the skin, and treatment focuses on controlling inflammation and repairing the skin barrier.

### การรักษาหลัก (Treatments)
Conventional care usually involves topical corticosteroids to reduce inflammation and itching, along with emollients to moisturize and protect the skin. If a secondary bacterial or fungal infection is present, antibiotic or antifungal creams may be prescribed. Avoiding known irritants-such as certain metals, soaps, or hair products-is also a key part of management.

## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนจีน (Traditional Chinese Medicine View)
### ความเข้าใจเกี่ยวกับการแพทย์แผนจีน (TCM Understanding)
TCM views ear eczema through the lens of moisture. The skin around the ear is delicate, and when the body’s fluids get out of balance, that dampness rises and gets trapped in the ear’s channels, causing the skin to react. Whether the rash is wet and oozing or dry and scaly tells the practitioner exactly which internal imbalance is driving the problem.
Two patterns are dominated by Dampness. In Damp-Heat, which often follows a period of rich, greasy, or spicy food, or exposure to a hot, humid climate, Heat and Dampness combine into a sticky, inflammatory mix. The ear becomes red, swollen, and weepy, with a yellow discharge and an intense itch. The tongue shows a greasy yellow coating, and the pulse feels slippery and rapid. This is an acute, angry flare-up.
When the Spleen is constitutionally weak, it fails to transform and transport fluids properly, leading to a chronic, low-grade accumulation of Dampness. The ear skin thickens, itches persistently, and may ooze a little, but the inflammation is less fierce than in Damp-Heat. The person often feels bloated, tired, and has loose stools. The tongue is pale and puffy with tooth marks, and the pulse is soft. This pattern often underlies eczema that never quite goes away.
The third pattern, Blood Deficiency with External Wind, is different: the ear skin is dry, scaly, and cracked, with a maddening itch that worsens at night. This happens when the body’s Blood is too depleted to nourish and moisten the skin. The resulting emptiness stirs up internal Wind, which combines with external Wind to trigger intense itching. The tongue is pale and thin, and the pulse is fine. This is the pattern seen in chronic, long-standing ear eczema that has damaged the skin’s integrity.

### การวินิจฉัยโดยแพทย์แผนจีน (Practitioner Diagnosis)
A TCM practitioner begins by looking at the ear rash itself and asking about its feel and timeline. An acute flare-up that appears red, hot, and wet with yellow oozing points toward a Damp-Heat pattern. They will check the tongue, expecting a red body with a yellow, greasy coating, and feel the pulse for a slippery, rapid quality that signals heat and dampness trapped in the skin.
If the outbreak is less angry but keeps returning, with thickened skin and a duller itch, the practitioner suspects Spleen Deficiency with Dampness. They will ask about digestion, energy, and bowel habits, because a weak Spleen struggles to manage fluids. The tongue often looks pale and puffy with a thin white coating, and the pulse feels soft or weak, reflecting the body’s inability to clear lingering dampness.
When ear eczema has dragged on for months or years and the skin is now dry, scaly, cracked, and intensely itchy-especially at night-the diagnosis shifts to Blood Deficiency with External Wind. The tongue appears pale with little coating, and the pulse is thin. The practitioner will also look for signs like a dull complexion or dry hair, because when blood is too depleted to nourish the skin, invisible “wind” stirs up itching.

## กลุ่มอาการการแพทย์แผนจีนและการรักษา (TCM Patterns and Treatment)

### Damp-Heat
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/damp-heat)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Red, swollen, weeping ear, Yellow, sticky discharge, Intense itching, worse with heat, Feeling of heaviness or sluggishness, Bitter taste and thirst with little desire to drink
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Greasy, fried, or spicy foods, Alcohol, Hot and humid weather, Scratching, Overwork, fatigue, and stress
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Cool, dry air, Light, bland, easily digestible meals, Gentle cleaning of discharge, Rest and adequate sleep
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When Dampness and Heat combine, they create a sticky, inflammatory environment. This Damp-Heat can arise from a diet rich in greasy, spicy, or sweet foods, or from living in a hot, humid climate. The heavy, turbid Damp-Heat rises to the ear, causing the skin to become red, swollen, and weepy, with a characteristic yellow discharge and fierce itching.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Red tongue with a yellow, greasy coating. Pulse: slippery and rapid (滑数 huá shuò).
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Spicy, greasy foods and alcohol directly generate Dampness and Heat in the body, worsening the ear inflammation. Hot, humid weather introduces external Damp-Heat, which combines with internal conditions. Scratching irritates the skin and spreads the Damp-Heat locally. Cooling, bland foods and a dry environment help to clear Damp-Heat and calm the skin.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Avoid greasy, fried, spicy, and sweet foods that create Dampness and Heat. Eat more cooling, light foods like cucumber, mung beans, and barley. Keep the ear dry and clean, avoid scratching, and stay in a cool, dry environment.

### Spleen Deficiency with Dampness
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/spleen-deficiency-with-dampness)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Persistent itching around the ear, Less oozing than acute cases, Thickened, rough skin, Abdominal bloating and loose stools, Fatigue and heaviness of the body
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Damp or humid weather, Overeating or large meals, Greasy, fried, or spicy foods, Cold, raw foods, Overwork, fatigue, and stress
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warm drinks and soups, Rest and adequate sleep, Dry, well-ventilated environment, Light, bland, easily digestible meals, Gentle daily movement
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When the Spleen is weak, it fails to transform and transport fluids, allowing Dampness to accumulate internally. This Dampness rises and stagnates in the skin around the ear, causing persistent itching, mild oozing, and gradual skin thickening. Unlike acute Damp-Heat cases, the inflammation is less intense but more chronic, reflecting the underlying deficiency.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale, swollen with teeth marks, white greasy coating, moist surface. Pulse: soggy (Ru) and slow (Huan), may feel slippery.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Cold and raw foods, overeating, and greasy foods weaken the Spleen and generate more Dampness, worsening ear eczema. Damp weather adds external Dampness. Warmth, rest, and light meals support Spleen Qi, helping it transform Dampness and dry the skin lesions.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat warm, cooked foods like soups and stews. Avoid raw, cold, greasy, and dairy products that burden the Spleen and generate Dampness. Gentle exercise like walking helps move Qi and Dampness. Keep the ear clean and dry but not overly dry.

### Blood Deficiency with External Wind
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/blood-deficiency-with-external-wind)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Dry, flaky, cracked skin around the ear, Itching that comes and goes or shifts location, Itching worse at night, Pale or sallow complexion, Dizziness or light-headedness, Brittle nails and dry hair, Aversion to wind or drafts
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Dry, windy weather, Greasy, fried, or spicy foods, Overwork, fatigue, and stress, Exposure to drafts, Scratching
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Moisturizing the skin, Eating blood-nourishing foods, Warm, wind-free environment, Rest and adequate sleep
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When the body's Blood is insufficient, it cannot properly nourish and moisten the skin around the ear. This leaves the skin dry, flaky, and vulnerable. The deficiency creates an internal emptiness that stirs up Wind, a TCM concept for movement and itching, which then combines with external Wind entering through the weakened skin. The result is the chronic, dry, cracked ear eczema with itching that comes and goes.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale, thin body with a thin white and slightly dry coat. Pulse: fine (thin), wiry at the left middle position, and floating at the right front position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Blood deficiency leaves the skin dry and vulnerable to Wind, so anything that further dries or depletes the body-like spicy foods, overwork, or dry weather-worsens the itching and cracking. Wind exposure directly triggers the external Wind factor. Blood-nourishing foods and rest replenish the root deficiency, while moisturizing and protecting the skin shield it from Wind, allowing the skin to heal.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Focus on moistening, blood-nourishing foods like black sesame, goji berries, red dates, and bone broths. Avoid spicy, fried, and drying foods that consume Blood and fluids. Protect the ears from wind and cold drafts with a soft scarf or hat. Keep the skin gently moisturized with a natural, fragrance-free balm. Prioritize rest and manage stress to prevent further Blood depletion.

## คำถามที่พบบ่อย (Frequently Asked Questions)
### Can Chinese herbs cure ear eczema permanently?
Herbs can correct the internal imbalance that allows ear eczema to keep returning, which often leads to long-term remission. For Damp-Heat patterns, clearing the heat and dampness can stop the cycle of acute flares. For Spleen deficiency, strengthening the digestive system prevents dampness from accumulating again. For Blood deficiency, nourishing the blood rebuilds the skin’s resilience. However, if dietary and lifestyle triggers return, the eczema can come back-so lasting freedom usually requires maintaining some of the habits you learn during treatment.

### How does acupuncture help ear eczema?
Acupuncture works on several levels. Locally, points around the ear like Ermen (SJ-21) and Tinggong (SI-19) reduce inflammation and itching right where you need it. Distal points like Hegu (LI-4) and Sanyinjiao (SP-6) address the root pattern-clearing damp-heat, strengthening the Spleen, or nourishing blood. Most people find that the intense itch subsides within a few sessions, and the skin begins to look calmer.

### Is ear eczema caused by my diet?
Diet plays a big role in the two dampness-driven patterns. Greasy, fried, spicy, and sugary foods, as well as alcohol, can generate Damp-Heat and directly trigger a flare. Dairy and cold, raw foods can weaken the Spleen and worsen Spleen Deficiency with Dampness. For Blood Deficiency patterns, a nutrient-poor diet that fails to build blood can slowly deplete the skin’s moisture. Your practitioner will help you identify which foods are your personal triggers.

### Can I use TCM alongside my steroid cream?
Yes, and many people do exactly that at the start of treatment. The herbs and acupuncture work to heal the skin from the inside, while the cream can give immediate topical relief. Over time, as the internal pattern shifts, most people find they need the cream less and less. Always let both your TCM practitioner and your doctor know what you’re using, and never stop a prescribed medication abruptly without medical advice.

### Is it safe to put herbal ointments inside the ear canal?
Only if specifically prescribed by a qualified TCM practitioner who has examined your ear. The ear canal is sensitive, and the eardrum can be damaged by the wrong substance. Many herbal ear treatments are designed for the external ear and the skin just inside the opening, not deep in the canal. If you have pain, discharge, or hearing loss, see the safety section for urgent signs.

### How long does it take to see results?
Acute Damp-Heat flares often calm down within 2-4 weeks of herbal treatment and weekly acupuncture. Spleen Deficiency with Dampness, being more chronic, typically takes 6-8 weeks to show lasting improvement. Blood Deficiency with External Wind can take 3-6 months, because rebuilding blood is a slower process. Even in long-standing cases, the itching usually lessens much sooner than the skin fully heals.

## การวิจัยทางวิทยาศาสตร์สมัยใหม่ (Modern Scientific Research)
1. **Efficacy and safety of Chinese herbal medicine for atopic dermatitis: a systematic review**
   *วารสารวิชาการ: Gu S, Yang AW, Xue CC, et al. Br J Dermatol. 2013;169(4):794-803.*
   This systematic review evaluated randomized controlled trials of Chinese herbal medicine for atopic dermatitis. It found that herbal medicine improved clinical symptoms and quality of life compared to placebo or conventional treatments, though the quality of included studies was variable and heterogeneity limited firm conclusions.

2. **Acupuncture in patients with atopic eczema: a randomized controlled trial** ([ลิงก์](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1398-9995.2011.02772.x))
   *วารสารวิชาการ: Pfab F, Huss-Marp J, Gatti R, et al. Allergy. 2012;67(4):566-73.*
   In this randomized trial, acupuncture significantly reduced itch intensity and improved quality of life in patients with atopic eczema compared to sham acupuncture. The effects were noticeable after a few sessions and persisted during follow-up, supporting acupuncture as a useful adjunctive therapy.
