## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนปัจจุบัน (Conventional Medicine View)
### คำอธิบาย (Description)
From a Western medical perspective, armpit pain can originate from several structures. Musculoskeletal causes include muscle strains, rotator cuff injuries, or thoracic outlet syndrome, where nerves or blood vessels are compressed. Lymph nodes in the axilla can become tender due to infection, inflammation, or, less commonly, malignancy. Skin conditions like hidradenitis suppurativa or shingles can also produce local pain. Diagnosis typically involves a physical exam, and may include imaging such as ultrasound or MRI, blood tests, or a biopsy to rule out serious underlying disease.

### การรักษาหลัก (Treatments)
Standard care depends on the suspected cause. Musculoskeletal pain is often managed with rest, ice or heat, nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs (NSAIDs), and physical therapy. Infections may require antibiotics or drainage. Chronic nerve compression might be treated with nerve blocks, medications like gabapentin, or surgery. For inflammatory skin conditions, topical or systemic medications are used. When no clear structural cause is found, pain management and lifestyle adjustments are the mainstay.

## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนจีน (Traditional Chinese Medicine View)
### ความเข้าใจเกี่ยวกับการแพทย์แผนจีน (TCM Understanding)
In TCM, the armpit is a crossroads for several vital channels, most notably the Liver channel, which runs through the ribcage and up into the axilla. The Liver governs the smooth flow of Qi throughout the body, and when emotional stress, frustration, or suppressed feelings disrupt this flow, Qi can stagnate right in the armpit, creating a distending, wandering pain. This is why armpit pain so often flares with stress and eases with relaxation - it's a direct reflection of Liver Qi stagnation.
But the Liver isn't the only player. The Heart, Pericardium, and Gallbladder channels also traverse the area, and they can be affected by other pathogenic factors. Dampness and Heat can accumulate in the Liver channel, causing burning, swollen pain with a bitter taste and yellow urine.
Long-standing Qi stagnation can deepen into Blood Stagnation, producing a fixed, stabbing pain that worsens at night. When the body's Qi and Blood are deficient, the channels are undernourished, leading to a dull, lingering ache that feels worse with fatigue. Even Cold and Dampness can settle in the area if the body's Yang energy is weak, creating a cold, heavy pain that improves with warmth.
This is why TCM doesn't have one treatment for armpit pain. Each pattern represents a different internal imbalance, and the treatment - whether it's moving Qi, clearing Damp-Heat, invigorating Blood, or nourishing deficiency - must match that specific root. The same armpit pain that one person experiences as a stress-related distension is an entirely different condition from another person's burning Damp-Heat pain, even though they share the same Western diagnosis.

### การวินิจฉัยโดยแพทย์แผนจีน (Practitioner Diagnosis)
A practitioner starts by asking what the pain actually feels like and what makes it better or worse. The armpit is a crossroads for several channels, most notably the Liver channel, which is deeply affected by emotional stress. The quality, timing, and associated signs are the first clues that point toward one pattern rather than another.
If the pain feels distending and wandering, and it clearly rises and falls with your mood or stress levels, Liver Qi Stagnation is the likely picture. The tongue often looks pale red with a thin white coating, and the pulse feels wiry. This pattern is the most common trigger for armpit pain.
When the pain is burning and the area feels swollen or hot, with a bitter taste in the mouth and dark yellow urine, Damp-Heat in the Liver Channel is suspected. The tongue coating becomes thick, greasy, and yellow, while the pulse feels rapid and slippery. Rich or spicy foods often make this pattern flare up.
A fixed, stabbing pain that worsens at night and feels like a deep bruise points to Blood Stagnation. The tongue may show dark or purplish spots, and the pulse can feel choppy. This often develops after a long period of Qi stagnation or a direct injury to the area.
If the ache is dull and lingering, accompanied by overall fatigue, a pale face, and a pale tongue with a weak pulse, the underlying issue is likely Qi and Blood Deficiency. Here the sinews are undernourished, causing a vague discomfort rather than a sharp attack.
Cold, heavy pain that feels better with a hot compress and worse in cold weather indicates Yang Deficiency with Cold-Damp. The tongue appears pale and swollen, and the pulse is deep and slow. This pattern reflects an internal lack of warmth that allows dampness to settle in the channel.
An acute, red, swollen, and throbbing armpit that may be accompanied by fever signals Toxic-Heat, often in the form of an abscess. The tongue is red with a yellow coating, and the pulse is rapid. This is a more urgent presentation that needs prompt professional attention.

### Liver Qi Stagnation
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/liver-qi-stagnation)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Distending, wandering pain in the armpit that moves around, Pain worsens with stress or frustration, eases with relaxation, Irritability, mood swings, or frequent sighing, Tightness or distension along the ribcage
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Emotional stress and frustration, Greasy, fried, or spicy foods, Alcohol and caffeine, Sedentary lifestyle, Suppressing emotions
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Gentle stretching or yoga, Deep breathing and relaxation, Warm, light meals with leafy greens, Talking through emotions, Applying warmth to the armpit
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  The Liver is responsible for the smooth flow of Qi throughout the body. When emotional stress, frustration, or suppressed feelings build up, the Liver Qi gets stuck. The Liver channel runs through the ribcage and up into the armpit, so this stagnation creates a sensation of distending, wandering pain that moves around and fluctuates with your mood.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: normal or slightly dark body with red or slightly purple sides, thin white coat. Pulse: wiry (弦 xián), especially at the left middle position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Liver Qi Stagnation is directly triggered by emotional stress and frustration, which cause the Liver's Qi to become stuck and then surge, creating distending pain along the Liver channel that runs through the armpit. Greasy and spicy foods can further obstruct Qi flow. Conversely, relaxation, gentle movement, and warmth help smooth the flow of Qi, while expressing emotions prevents stagnation from building up.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Incorporate stress-reducing practices like meditation, tai chi, or gentle yoga. Eat warm, cooked meals with plenty of leafy greens, and avoid greasy, fried, or spicy foods that can aggravate Liver Qi. Sip on peppermint or chrysanthemum tea to help move Qi. Regular gentle exercise helps keep Qi flowing smoothly.

### Damp-Heat in the Liver Channel
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/damp-heat-in-the-liver-channel)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Burning pain in the armpit, Swollen, red armpit, Bitter taste in the mouth, Dark yellow, scanty urine, Feeling of heaviness in the body, Genital itching or dampness
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Spicy, greasy food, Alcohol, Humid weather, Emotional frustration, Prolonged sitting in dampness, Overwork
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Cool, dry weather, Light, bland meals, Bitter vegetables, Cooling showers, Gentle stretching
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  The Liver channel runs through the rib-side and into the armpit. When Dampness and Heat accumulate in this channel, the Heat causes burning and swelling, while the Dampness makes the pain feel heavy and sticky. This combination of Heat and Dampness obstructs the flow of Qi and Blood, resulting in painful, swollen armpit discomfort that often flares up in humid weather or after rich meals.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red body with redder sides, yellow greasy coating thicker at centre and root. Pulse: wiry, slippery, and rapid.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Spicy, greasy foods and alcohol directly create Damp-Heat inside the body, while humid weather adds external Dampness, both of which worsen the burning, heavy pain. Emotional frustration stirs Liver Qi, generating more Heat. Cooling, bland foods and bitter vegetables clear Heat and drain Dampness, while a dry environment prevents external Damp from invading. Gentle movement prevents Qi stagnation, which helps the Liver channel stay open and reduces pain.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Avoid greasy, fried, and spicy foods, as well as alcohol and sugary drinks, which generate Damp-Heat. Focus on cooling, damp-draining foods like mung beans, bitter melon, cucumber, and celery. Stay in a dry, well-ventilated environment, and avoid prolonged sitting in damp or humid conditions. Gentle exercise like walking or qigong helps keep Qi moving and prevents stagnation.

### Blood Stagnation
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/blood-stagnation)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Fixed, stabbing pain, Pain worsens at night, Pain worsens with pressure, Dark purple tongue with stasis spots, Palpable lump or bruising in the armpit
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Cold weather, Prolonged inactivity, Pressure on the armpit, Stress, Cold or raw foods
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warm compress, Gentle stretching, Rest, Light massage
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When Qi flow is blocked for a long time, or after a physical injury, the blood in the armpit area can become stagnant. This creates a fixed, knife-like pain because the blood is not moving freely through the channels that pass through the axilla, such as the Heart and Liver meridians. The pain is especially sharp at night because nighttime is Yin, which tends to slow circulation further.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: dark purple or dusky body, stasis spots, distended dark sublingual veins, thin white coat. Pulse: choppy (涩, sè) or wiry (弦, xián).
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Cold congeals blood and worsens stagnation, while warmth and movement promote circulation. Pressure physically obstructs blood flow, aggravating fixed pain. Stress causes Liver Qi stagnation, which is often the root of blood stasis, so calming the mind helps. Gentle exercise and massage disperse stasis and encourage blood to flow through the armpit channels.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat warming and blood-moving foods like turmeric, black fungus, vinegar, and cherries. Avoid cold, raw foods and icy drinks, which can congeal blood. Gentle daily exercise such as walking or yoga helps keep circulation moving. Avoid tight clothing that puts pressure on the armpit.

### Qi and Blood Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/qi-and-blood-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Dull, lingering ache, Worsens with fatigue and overwork, Pale face and lips, General weakness and shortness of breath
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Overwork, Stress, Cold environment, Poor sleep
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Rest, Warmth, Gentle movement
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  The armpit is traversed by multiple sinew channels that depend on ample Qi and Blood to stay nourished and pain-free. When Qi and Blood are deficient, these tissues are undernourished, leading to a dull, lingering ache that feels worse when the body is tired. Unlike the sharp or burning pains of excess patterns, this pain reflects a lack of vital resources, so it improves with rest and worsens with overwork.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: Pale body, may be thin or have teeth marks, thin white coating. Pulse: Fine, weak, and empty.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Qi and Blood are the body's fundamental nourishment. Rest and warmth conserve and gently stimulate them, easing the undernourished armpit tissues. Overwork, stress, and cold deplete Qi or congeal Blood, further starving the sinews and worsening the dull ache.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat warm, cooked foods like soups and stews with dark leafy greens, moderate amounts of red meat, and bone broth to build Blood. Avoid raw, cold foods that weaken the Spleen. Prioritize rest and gentle movement like tai chi or walking to conserve Qi.

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Cold, heavy, aching pain in the armpit, Pain improves with warmth and worsens with cold, Cold hands and feet, aversion to cold, Fatigue and desire to lie down, Loose stools
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Cold weather or drafts, Eating cold or raw foods, Prolonged sitting or inactivity, Overwork and exhaustion, Damp living conditions
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Applying warmth (hot pack, warm compress), Warm drinks and soups, Gentle movement that warms the body, Rest in a warm environment
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When the body's Yang energy is weak, it fails to warm the channels and transform fluids. Internal Cold accumulates and combines with Dampness, which then settles in the armpit area along the sinew channels. This cold-damp obstruction blocks the flow of Qi and blood, producing a deep, cold, heavy pain that feels better with heat and worse in cold or damp weather.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale, swollen body with teeth marks and a white greasy moist coating. Pulse: deep (Chen) and slow (Chi), possibly fine (Wei) or soggy (Ru).
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Cold and dampness are the main aggravating factors in this pattern because they further deplete Yang and congeal the channels, intensifying the pain. Warmth, on the other hand, restores Yang's ability to warm and move, dispersing the cold-damp obstruction and easing pain. Gentle movement also encourages circulation, while rest in a warm setting prevents further Yang drain.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat warm, cooked foods like soups, stews, and congees. Avoid raw, cold, and iced foods that dampen Yang. Keep the body warm, especially the armpit and back, and use a hot pack or moxibustion for comfort. Gentle exercise like walking or tai chi can help move Qi and warm the channels.

### Toxic-Heat
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/toxic-heat)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Throbbing, burning pain, Visible redness, swelling, and heat in the armpit, Possible pus formation or abscess, Fever and intense thirst, Restlessness and irritability
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Spicy, greasy, or fried foods, Alcohol and coffee, Emotional stress and anger, Heat and humidity, Tight clothing or friction
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Cool compresses, Rest and reduced activity, Drinking plenty of water
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When a powerful external pathogen or intense internal Heat condenses into a localized Toxin, it can settle in the armpit and create an acute abscess or carbuncle. This Toxic-Heat blocks the channels, causing intense redness, swelling, heat, and a throbbing pain that feels like a hot coal under the skin. The body may also react with fever and restlessness as it tries to fight off the concentrated infection.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red body with prickly thorns, dry yellow coating. Pulse: rapid (shu), forceful, and often slippery (hua).
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Toxic-Heat is an intense, localized Fire pattern. Cooling applications and rest help the body contain and extinguish the fire, while spicy foods, alcohol, and emotional heat add more fuel, making the abscess angrier and more painful.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Avoid all spicy, greasy, and deep-fried foods, alcohol, and coffee - they add fuel to the fire. Drink plenty of water and cooling teas like dandelion or chrysanthemum. Keep the armpit clean and dry, wear loose cotton clothing, and avoid antiperspirants that block pores. Rest is essential to let the body's defenses fight the Toxin.

## คำถามที่พบบ่อย (Frequently Asked Questions)
### Does acupuncture in the armpit hurt?
Acupuncture points near the armpit, such as Jiquan HT-1, are needled with very fine, sterile needles and most people feel only a mild sensation of pressure or a dull ache - not sharp pain. The area is sensitive, so practitioners use gentle technique and often combine local points with distal points on the arms and legs to reduce discomfort. Many patients find the treatment deeply relaxing once the needles are in place.

### Can TCM help if my armpit pain is from swollen lymph nodes?
Yes, TCM can often address the underlying cause of lymph node swelling and pain, especially when it stems from patterns like Damp-Heat or Toxic-Heat. Herbal formulas and acupuncture work to clear the pathogenic factors, reduce inflammation, and restore normal flow. However, any persistent, hard, or rapidly growing lump should be evaluated by a medical doctor first to rule out serious conditions. TCM can then be used safely alongside conventional care.

### Is armpit pain related to stress?
Absolutely. In TCM, the Liver is highly sensitive to emotional stress, and its channel passes directly through the armpit. When stress causes Liver Qi to stagnate, the pain often feels distending, comes and goes, and is clearly linked to your mood. Many people notice their armpit pain flares during periods of frustration or anxiety and eases when they relax. This pattern is one of the most common causes of armpit pain in TCM practice.

### How long until I feel better with TCM?
Most patients begin to notice a reduction in pain intensity or frequency within the first 2-4 weeks of weekly acupuncture and daily herbal medicine. Excess patterns, like stress-related Qi stagnation, often respond faster than deficiency patterns, which require more time to rebuild the body's resources. Your practitioner will adjust the treatment plan as you progress, and many people continue with less frequent maintenance sessions after the pain resolves to prevent recurrence.

### Can I treat armpit pain at home with acupressure?
Gentle acupressure on points like Taichong LR-3 on the foot and Yanglingquan GB-34 on the lower leg can help move Liver Qi and relieve mild distending pain. However, self-treatment is tricky because pressing the wrong point or using too much force can worsen the pain, and it won't address the root pattern. It's safest to consult a qualified TCM practitioner who can give you a tailored acupressure protocol. If your pain is severe, burning, or accompanied by a lump, see a professional rather than self-treating.

### Is TCM safe for armpit pain during pregnancy?
Yes, acupuncture is generally safe during pregnancy when performed by a licensed practitioner who knows which points to avoid. Many herbal formulas, however, are contraindicated in pregnancy, so always inform your practitioner if you are pregnant or trying to conceive. They can select safe herbs and acupuncture points to address your armpit pain without risking your pregnancy. Always coordinate care with your obstetrician.

1. **Acupuncture for Shoulder Pain: A Systematic Review** ([ลิงก์](https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD005319))
   *วารสารวิชาการ: Green S, Buchbinder R, Hetrick S. Acupuncture for shoulder pain. Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews 2005, Issue 2. Art. No.: CD005319.*
   This Cochrane review assessed the evidence for acupuncture in treating shoulder pain. It found moderate evidence that acupuncture is superior to sham acupuncture and physiotherapy for pain relief and functional improvement, though the quality of included trials was variable.

   *วารสารวิชาการ: Hur MH, Kim DH, Lee MS, et al. Acupuncture for cyclic mastalgia: a randomized controlled trial. Obstetrics & Gynecology. 2008;111(5):1055-1061.*
