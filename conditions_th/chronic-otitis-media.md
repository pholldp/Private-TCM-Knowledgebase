## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนปัจจุบัน (Conventional Medicine View)
### คำอธิบาย (Description)
Chronic otitis media is a persistent inflammation or infection of the middle ear, often accompanied by a perforated eardrum and recurrent discharge. It may follow an acute ear infection that never fully healed, or it can develop gradually due to eustachian tube dysfunction. Symptoms include hearing loss, a feeling of fullness, and intermittent or constant drainage from the ear. Diagnosis is typically made by otoscopic examination.

### การรักษาหลัก (Treatments)
Conventional treatment includes antibiotic eardrops or oral antibiotics during active infections, regular cleaning of the ear canal (aural toilet), and keeping the ear dry. For persistent perforations, surgical repair (tympanoplasty) may be recommended. However, even with surgery, some patients experience ongoing issues due to underlying eustachian tube problems or immune factors.

## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนจีน (Traditional Chinese Medicine View)
### ความเข้าใจเกี่ยวกับการแพทย์แผนจีน (TCM Understanding)
In TCM, the ear is closely linked to the Kidney, Liver, and Spleen organ systems. The Kidney "opens into the ear," meaning that the ear's health depends on Kidney essence and Yin. When Kidney Yin is deficient, the ear becomes dry, hearing slowly fades, and a high-pitched ringing may appear. The Liver and Gallbladder channels travel around the ear, so emotional stress or dietary indiscretions can send Heat and Dampness rising along these pathways, causing redness, swelling, and thick yellow discharge.
The Spleen is responsible for transforming fluids; when it is weak, dampness accumulates and can overflow upward to the ear.
This is why one Western diagnosis of chronic otitis media can look so different from person to person - and why TCM distinguishes several patterns. A dry, quiet ear with gradual hearing loss and night sweats points to Kidney Yin Deficiency.
A pale, tired person with a watery, lingering discharge often has Qi and Blood Deficiency. Thick, yellow, sticky discharge with a bitter taste and itching indicates Damp-Heat in the Liver Channel. Sharp pain and loud tinnitus that flare with anger suggest Liver Fire Blazing. And a sticky discharge that worsens after heavy meals, with bloating, signals Damp-Heat in the Stomach and Spleen.
Crucially, TCM sees the ear discharge not as a local infection alone, but as a manifestation of a deeper imbalance. The oozing fluid is a form of "dampness" that the body is failing to manage - either because it is producing too much (from dietary or emotional factors) or because it is too weak to transform and excrete it (from deficiency).
Treatment therefore aims to both clear the ear and correct the underlying imbalance, so the environment that allows infections to thrive is no longer present.

### การวินิจฉัยโดยแพทย์แผนจีน (Practitioner Diagnosis)
A TCM practitioner starts by asking about the ear discharge - its color, thickness, and smell - and the quality of any pain or itching. The ear is linked to the Kidney, Liver, and Spleen systems, so whole-body clues like energy level, digestion, and emotional triggers are just as important. The tongue and pulse then confirm which pattern is driving the chronic otitis media.
If the ear feels dry and the discharge is thin or scanty, with gradual hearing loss and a soft, low tinnitus, the root is often Kidney Yin Deficiency. This pattern is more common after a long illness or in older adults. The person may also have a sore lower back, night sweats, and a red tongue with little coating; the pulse feels thin and rapid, reflecting emptiness and empty heat flaring upward.
When the ear problem has dragged on and the whole body feels drained, Qi and Blood Deficiency is likely. The pain is mild and dull, the discharge may be watery, and hearing fades slowly. The face looks pale, fatigue is constant, and appetite is poor. The tongue appears pale with a thin white coat, and the pulse is weak and thready - signs that the body lacks the resources to repair the ear tissue.
A thick, yellow, sticky discharge with a strong smell, along with itching and swelling, points to Damp-Heat in the Liver Channel. This pattern often flares up acutely. The person may notice a bitter taste in the mouth, a feeling of fullness under the ribs, and a tongue with a thick, yellow, greasy coating. The pulse feels slippery and rapid, showing that both dampness and heat are rising to the ear.
Sharp, stabbing ear pain, loud tinnitus, and redness that appear suddenly - especially after anger or stress - suggest Liver Fire Blazing. The discharge may be yellow and purulent, and the symptoms are intense and fiery. The tongue is red with a yellow coating, and the pulse is wiry and rapid. Irritability and a bitter taste in the mouth are common companions of this heat-driven pattern.
When the ear discharge is sticky and persistent, and it comes with digestive troubles like bloating, a heavy body sensation, and a greasy taste in the mouth, the culprit may be Damp-Heat in the Stomach and Spleen. The tongue coating is thick, yellow, and greasy, and the pulse is slippery. This pattern shows that the digestive system is generating dampness that rises and settles in the ear.

### Kidney Yin Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/kidney-yin-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Dry sensation in the ear, Gradual hearing loss, High-pitched tinnitus like cicadas, Night sweats, Soreness and weakness of lower back and knees, Heat in the palms, soles, and chest (five-centre heat)
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Overwork and staying up late, Spicy, greasy, or fried foods, Emotional stress and frustration, Excessive heat or dry environments
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Adequate rest and sleep, Cool, quiet environment, Nourishing Yin foods (black sesame, walnuts, goji berries)
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  The Kidney opens into the ear, and Kidney Yin is the body's cooling and nourishing foundation. When Kidney Yin is depleted, empty heat rises upward, drying the ear and causing a persistent low-grade inflammation. This leads to a dry ear, gradual hearing loss, and a high-pitched tinnitus like cicadas. The condition often comes with other signs of Yin deficiency such as night sweats, low back soreness, and heat in the palms and soles.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red, thin body with little or no coating, dry surface, possible cracks. Pulse: fine (xi) and rapid (shu), especially weak at the left rear position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Kidney Yin is the body's cooling and moistening resource. Rest and nourishing foods replenish Yin, calming empty heat and reducing ear dryness. Overwork, late nights, and spicy foods consume Yin and fan empty fire upward, worsening the inflammation and tinnitus. Stress stirs Liver Yang, which rises and aggravates the ear, while heat and dryness further deplete fluids.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Prioritise rest and avoid staying up late, which depletes Yin. Eat nourishing Yin foods like black sesame, walnuts, goji berries, and pears. Avoid spicy, greasy, and fried foods that create internal heat, and reduce alcohol and coffee. Gentle exercise like tai chi or walking is helpful, but avoid overexertion that can further drain Yin.

### Qi and Blood Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/qi-and-blood-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Mild, dull ear pain or fullness, Gradual, progressive hearing loss, Pale or sallow complexion, Fatigue and lack of energy, Poor appetite, Thin, clear or watery ear discharge, Dizziness or lightheadedness
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Overwork and exhaustion, Skipping meals or irregular eating, Emotional stress and worry, Prolonged illness
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Adequate rest, Warm, easy-to-digest meals, Gentle walking, Stress reduction
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Chronic ear infections and persistent discharge gradually drain the body's Qi and Blood, much like a slow leak saps a battery. Without enough Qi and Blood, the ear tissues become weak and unable to repair themselves, so low-grade inflammation lingers. The Spleen, which produces Qi and Blood from food, is often weak as well, leading to the fatigue and pale complexion that accompany this pattern. The result is a dull, nagging ear discomfort and a slow, progressive loss of hearing because the sensory structures are undernourished.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale body, may be thin or have teeth marks, thin white coating. Pulse: fine (thin) and weak, lacking force.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Overwork, poor diet, and chronic stress drain the Spleen's ability to produce Qi and Blood, leaving the ear undernourished and unable to heal. Rest and warm, nourishing meals directly support the Spleen and help rebuild Qi and Blood. Gentle movement keeps Qi flowing without exhausting reserves. By conserving and replenishing these vital substances, the ear finally receives the resources it needs to repair and recover.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat regular, warm, cooked meals like congee, soups, and stews with small amounts of quality protein and leafy greens. Avoid raw, cold, and greasy foods that tax the Spleen. Prioritize rest and gentle movement like walking-overexertion depletes Qi and Blood. A short afternoon rest can be especially restorative.

### Damp-Heat in the Liver Channel
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/damp-heat-in-the-liver-channel)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Thick yellow sticky ear discharge, Ear itching and swelling, Bitter taste in the mouth, Irritability or short temper, Dark scanty urine, Yellow greasy tongue coating
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Spicy and greasy foods, Alcohol, Anger and frustration, Humid weather
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Cooling, bland foods, Stress reduction, Avoiding alcohol
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  The Liver and Gallbladder channels travel up to the ear. When Damp-Heat accumulates in these channels, it can rise upward, causing inflammation, thick yellow sticky discharge, itching, and swelling in the ear. The bitter taste in the mouth and irritability are signs that the Liver is affected.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red body with redder sides, yellow greasy coating thicker at centre and root. Pulse: wiry, slippery, and rapid.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Spicy, greasy foods and alcohol generate Damp-Heat internally, directly fueling this pattern. Emotional stress, especially anger, causes Liver Qi stagnation that turns into Fire, which then combines with Dampness and rises to the ear. Cooling, bland foods help clear Heat and drain Dampness, while stress management prevents the Liver from overheating.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Avoid greasy, spicy, and deep-fried foods, as well as alcohol and coffee, which create Damp-Heat. Eat light, cooling foods like mung beans, bitter greens, and cucumber. Manage stress with gentle exercise and relaxation, as anger and frustration can worsen the condition.

### Liver Fire Blazing
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/liver-fire-blazing)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Sharp, stabbing ear pain, Loud tinnitus like rushing water, Redness and swelling around the ear, Bitter taste in the mouth, Intense irritability and quick temper, Throbbing headache at the temples, Red, burning eyes, Constipation and dark yellow urine
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Anger and frustration, Spicy, fried, or greasy food, Alcohol and coffee, Hot, stuffy weather, Overwork and late nights
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Cooling foods like cucumber and chrysanthemum tea, Calm, quiet environment, Gentle exercise to release tension
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  The Liver stores blood and ensures the smooth flow of Qi. When intense anger, frustration, or prolonged stress builds, Liver Qi stagnates and then bursts upward as Fire. This Fire rushes along the Liver and Gallbladder channels, which loop around the ear, igniting acute sharp pain, redness, and loud tinnitus. The ear becomes a vent for the body's overheated emotional charge.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: Red body with redder sides, dry yellow coating, possible red prickles. Pulse: Wiry (弦 xián) and rapid (数 shuò), forceful at the left Guan position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Anger and frustration are the primary sparks that ignite Liver Fire - they cause Qi to surge upward, carrying heat to the ears. Spicy, greasy foods, alcohol, and coffee add heat and dampness that further fuel the fire. Cooling foods and drinks, like chrysanthemum tea, gently drain Liver Fire and soothe the head. Calm environments and gentle movement help the Liver Qi flow smoothly, preventing stagnation from turning into fire. Adequate rest, especially before midnight, allows the Liver to store blood and cool the body, reducing the risk of flare-ups.

### Damp-Heat in Stomach and Spleen
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/damp-heat-in-stomach-and-spleen)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Chronic sticky or thick ear discharge, Bloating and upper abdominal fullness, Sticky or slimy taste in the mouth, Loose, sticky stools that feel incomplete, Heavy, foggy feeling in the head and body, Thick, yellow, greasy tongue coating
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Heavy, greasy meals, Dairy and rich foods, Overeating, Humid weather, Sedentary lifestyle
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Light, bland meals, Bitter greens (dandelion, endive), Avoiding dairy and sugar, Warm, cooked foods, Gentle daily exercise
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When the Spleen and Stomach struggle to process food and fluids, dampness and heat build up in the middle burner. This stagnant, heavy mixture can rise along the channels to the ear, producing a persistent sticky discharge and a feeling of fullness. Because the root lies in digestion, ear symptoms often flare alongside bloating, a pasty taste, and sluggish bowels.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: Red body, possibly swollen with teeth marks, with a thick yellow greasy coating especially at the centre and root. Pulse: soggy (ru) and rapid (shu), often also slippery (hua).
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Damp-Heat in the Spleen and Stomach is largely driven by diet and sluggish digestion. Greasy, sweet, and heavy foods overwhelm the Spleen, generating dampness that turns into heat and rises to the ear. Light, warm, and bitter foods do the opposite-they help the Spleen drain dampness and clear heat. Exercise moves Qi and dries dampness, while humidity and inactivity let it accumulate, so a consistently active, clean-eating routine is the most effective way to quiet this pattern.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Avoid greasy, fried, sugary, and dairy-heavy foods that create dampness. Favor warm, cooked, lightly spiced meals with plenty of bitter greens, barley, and adzuki beans. Eat until only about 70% full, and take a short walk after meals to help the Spleen move Qi and dry dampness. A consistent gentle exercise routine is essential.

## คำถามที่พบบ่อย (Frequently Asked Questions)
### Can TCM cure chronic ear infections permanently?
TCM aims to resolve the underlying imbalance that makes you prone to ear infections. When the root pattern is fully corrected, it is possible to go years without a recurrence. However, like any chronic condition, lifestyle and dietary habits play a big role in maintaining results. Many people find that even if occasional mild symptoms return, they are much less severe and respond quickly to a short course of herbs.

### Is acupuncture safe if I have a perforated eardrum?
Yes, acupuncture is safe with a perforated eardrum. The needles are placed on the external ear or body points, never into the ear canal. In fact, acupuncture points around the ear (like Tinggong SI-19 and Tinghui GB-2) are commonly used to promote drainage and healing. Always inform your practitioner of your perforation so they can choose appropriate points.

### Can I use TCM alongside antibiotic eardrops?
Generally, yes, but coordination is important. Herbal formulas taken by mouth can complement topical antibiotics, as they work systemically. However, herbal ear drops should not be used at the same time as antibiotic drops without guidance. It is best to space them out and keep your ENT and TCM practitioner informed of all treatments.

### Do I need to change my diet?
Diet plays a key role in managing chronic ear problems. Dampness and heat are often fueled by rich, greasy, spicy, or sugary foods, as well as alcohol. Reducing dairy, fried foods, and sweets while increasing lightly cooked vegetables, whole grains, and bitter greens can significantly reduce discharge and inflammation. Your practitioner will give you specific advice based on your pattern.

### How soon will I see results?
Many patients notice less discharge and a feeling of lightness in the ear within the first few weeks of treatment. If your condition is primarily excess (heat/dampness), improvement can be rapid. Deficiency patterns take longer because the body needs time to rebuild. Consistency with herbs and weekly acupuncture sessions is key.

### Can children with chronic ear infections be treated with TCM?
Yes, children often respond very well to TCM. Herbal formulas can be adjusted to pediatric doses, and acupuncture can be replaced with acupressure or laser acupuncture for young children. Dietary changes are especially effective in kids. Always work with a practitioner experienced in pediatrics.
