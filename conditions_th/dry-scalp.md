## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนปัจจุบัน (Conventional Medicine View)
### คำอธิบาย (Description)
In Western medicine, a dry scalp is often linked to conditions like seborrheic dermatitis, psoriasis, eczema, or simply environmental dryness. The scalp may feel tight, itchy, and flaky, with white or yellowish scales. Diagnosis is usually made by a dermatologist based on the appearance of the scalp and sometimes a skin scraping to rule out fungal infections.

### การรักษาหลัก (Treatments)
Standard treatments include over-the-counter medicated shampoos containing zinc pyrithione, ketoconazole, selenium sulfide, or coal tar. For more severe cases, topical corticosteroids, salicylic acid preparations, or prescription antifungal shampoos may be used. Moisturizing conditioners and oils can help relieve tightness, but they don't address the underlying cause.

## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนจีน (Traditional Chinese Medicine View)
### ความเข้าใจเกี่ยวกับการแพทย์แผนจีน (TCM Understanding)
TCM understands dry scalp primarily through the body's nourishing substances - Blood and Yin. These fluids travel upward to moisten the skin and hair, and when they run low, the scalp is often the first place to show it. The scalp sits at the highest point of the body, so it relies on a steady supply of moisture to stay supple. When that supply is interrupted by deficiency, internal heat, or external wind, dryness, flaking, and itching follow.
Several organ systems are involved. The Liver stores Blood and ensures its smooth flow; the Spleen transforms food into the fluids that become Blood and Yin; the Kidneys store the body's foundational Yin. When any of these systems is out of balance, the scalp can become dry. Stress, for example, can cause Liver Qi to stagnate and generate heat that rises to the head and consumes moisture. A diet heavy in greasy, spicy foods can create Damp-Heat that steams the scalp, leaving it paradoxically dry yet oily.
This is why two people with the same Western diagnosis of dry scalp may need completely different TCM treatments. One might have fine, powdery flakes with a pale tongue - a sign of Blood Deficiency needing nourishment. Another might have yellow, oily flakes with a red tongue - a sign of Damp-Heat needing clearing. TCM doesn't just treat the symptom; it reads the patterns to correct the root cause, so the scalp can heal itself.

### การวินิจฉัยโดยแพทย์แผนจีน (Practitioner Diagnosis)
A TCM practitioner begins by asking about the scalp’s texture, the type of flakes, and any itching or redness. They also explore your overall energy, stress levels, sleep, and diet, because the scalp rarely tells the whole story alone. These early clues help point toward one of several distinct patterns that can all cause a dry scalp.
If the dryness comes with fine, powdery flakes, a pale face, and a sense of overall fatigue, the root is often Blood Deficiency with External Wind. The tongue tends to be pale with a thin white coat, and the pulse feels thready and weak. This pattern means the blood is too weak to nourish the scalp, allowing internal wind to stir up dryness and itching.
When the scalp feels tight and papery, and the tongue is red with very little coating, the picture shifts to Empty-Heat caused by Yin Deficiency. The pulse is typically thready and rapid. People with this pattern may also notice night sweats, a dry mouth, or warm palms. The body’s cooling, moistening yin is depleted, and the resulting heat dries out the scalp.
A strong link to stress points toward Liver Qi Stagnation that transforms into Heat. The scalp may feel irritated and itchy, and the tongue often shows red edges with a wiry pulse. This pattern flares up when emotions run high, as stagnant liver energy turns into heat that rises to the head and damages fluids.
If the dryness is accompanied by noticeable fatigue, a pale tongue with a thin coat, and a weak pulse, Qi and Yin Deficiency may be the cause. Here, both the body’s vital energy (Qi) and its nourishing yin are low, so there simply isn’t enough power to produce and send moisture up to the scalp.
When the scalp is red and itchy with yellowish flakes, and the tongue is red with a yellow coat, an external invasion of Wind-Heat is likely. This pattern often starts suddenly after exposure to wind and heat, and the pulse feels rapid. The invading heat dries the skin and stirs up itching.
A paradoxical picture-dry-looking scalp with oily, yellowish scales and a greasy feel-suggests Damp-Heat. The tongue is red with a yellow, greasy coat and the pulse is slippery. In this pattern, dampness and heat steam upward, causing the scalp to appear dry on the surface while underlying dampness creates a sticky, oily residue.

### Blood Deficiency with External Wind
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/blood-deficiency-with-external-wind)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Dry, flaky scalp, Itching that wanders or comes and goes, Worse at night, Pale or sallow complexion, Aversion to wind or drafts
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Cold, dry weather, Windy or drafty weather, Overwork, late nights, or fatigue, Spicy, fried, or greasy foods, Harsh shampoos
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warmth and humid air, Gentle scalp massage, Nourishing soups and stews, Rest, sleep, and relaxation
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Blood nourishes and moistens the scalp. When Blood is deficient, the scalp loses this nourishment, becoming dry and flaky. This dryness generates an internal Wind that causes itching, and the weakened surface allows external Wind to invade, making the itch wander or come and go.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale and thin with a thin white, slightly dry coating. Pulse: fine (thin) and wiry, may feel floating at the superficial level.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Blood Deficiency leaves the scalp undernourished and dry, while external Wind provokes itching that moves. Cold and dry weather worsen the dryness and invite Wind, so warmth and humidity soothe it. Nourishing foods and rest rebuild Blood, directly addressing the root cause. Gentle massage moves Blood locally without aggravating Wind, while harsh products strip natural oils and further dry the scalp.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat blood-nourishing foods like dark leafy greens, red meat, eggs, black sesame seeds, and goji berries. Avoid spicy, fried, or overly drying foods. Protect your head from cold wind and dry air; a silk scarf or hat can help. Gentle daily scalp massage with warm oil improves local circulation.

### Empty-Heat caused by Yin Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/empty-heat-caused-by-yin-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Scalp feels tight and dry, with fine white flakes, Worse in the evening or in warm environments, Night sweats, Dry mouth and throat, especially at night, Flushed cheeks (malar flush), Restlessness and trouble sleeping
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Spicy, fried, or greasy foods, Hot, dry environments or heated rooms, Overwork, late nights, or fatigue, Emotional stress, frustration, or anger
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Cool, humid environment, Cooling, hydrating foods like cucumber and pear, Rest, sleep, and relaxation, Gentle exercise or movement
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When the body's Yin - its cooling, nourishing fluids - runs low, a subtle heat stirs inside. This Empty-Heat behaves like a low flame that gradually dries up moisture, leaving the scalp parched and tight. The scalp is the uppermost reach of the body, so when fluids are scarce, it is often one of the first places to feel dry and flaky.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: Red, thin, dry body with cracks and little or no coating. Pulse: Fine (thin) and rapid.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Empty-Heat is a quiet fire that burns when Yin is low. Heat and dryness add fuel, while cooling, moistening foods and environments help put it out. Rest and calm allow the body to rebuild its Yin reserves, which is why sleep and stress management are so important. Spicy foods, alcohol, and late nights all consume Yin and stir up heat, making the scalp feel even more parched.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat plenty of moistening foods like pears, black sesame, walnuts, and cucumber. Avoid spicy, fried, and overly roasted foods, as well as alcohol and coffee, which can fan the empty heat. Go to bed before 11 p.m. to allow Yin to regenerate during the night, and keep your living space cool and humidified.

### Liver Qi Stagnation that transforms into Heat
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/liver-qi-stagnation-that-transforms-into-heat)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Scalp dryness worsens with stress or anger, Irritability and explosive anger, Bitter taste in the mouth, Distending or burning pain along the ribs, Red tongue body with redder sides and a yellow coating
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Emotional stress, frustration, or anger, Spicy, fried, or greasy foods, Alcohol and sugary drinks, Overwork, late nights, or fatigue
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Rest, sleep, and relaxation, Cooling, hydrating foods like cucumber and pear, Mint or chrysanthemum tea, Gentle exercise or movement, A cool compress on the scalp
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  The Liver is responsible for the smooth flow of Qi throughout the body. When emotional stress, frustration, or suppressed anger build up, Liver Qi stagnates and eventually generates internal Heat. This Heat rises naturally to the head, where it consumes the body's nourishing fluids, leaving the scalp dry, tight, and prone to itching. The more stress and frustration you experience, the more this Heat flares, making the dryness worse.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red body with redder sides, yellow coating. Pulse: wiry and rapid (弦数, xián shuò), most pronounced at the left Guan position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Emotional stress, especially frustration and anger, is the primary driver of this pattern. It causes Liver Qi to stagnate and generate Heat, which then rises to dry the scalp. Spicy foods and alcohol add extra Heat to the body, fueling the fire. Cooling foods, stress relief, and gentle movement calm the Liver, reduce Heat production, and allow the body's fluids to return to the scalp, relieving the dryness.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Avoid spicy, greasy, and fried foods, as well as alcohol and coffee, which add Heat to the body. Instead, favor cooling, moistening foods like cucumber, pear, mint tea, and chrysanthemum tea. Gentle daily exercise like walking or yoga helps Qi flow smoothly and reduces stress. Make time for relaxation and address emotional tension before it builds up.

### Qi and Yin Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/qi-and-yin-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Dry, tight scalp with fine white flakes, Persistent fatigue and weakness, Dry mouth with little desire to drink, Spontaneous sweating or night sweats, Pale or pale-red tongue with thin coat, Fine, weak pulse
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Overwork, late nights, or fatigue, Spicy, fried, or greasy foods, Emotional stress, frustration, or anger, Hot, dry environments or heated rooms
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Rest, sleep, and relaxation, Moistening foods (pears, honey), Gentle exercise or movement, Cool, humid environment
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Qi is the body's engine for distributing fluids; Yin is the moisture itself. When both are deficient, the body lacks both the energy and the substance to nourish the scalp. This leaves the scalp undernourished, tight, and flaky, while the whole person feels drained of vitality.
Unlike patterns driven by heat or wind, the dryness here is quiet and chronic-more like a garden that isn't being watered enough because the pump is weak and the water tank is low. The hallmark is that fatigue and a dry mouth with little thirst accompany the scalp symptoms.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale-red or red body, thin or absent coating, possibly dry and cracked. Pulse: fine (thin) and weak, may be rapid.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Qi and Yin are like the body's fuel and coolant. Rest and nourishing foods replenish both, allowing the body to generate and distribute moisture to the scalp. Overexertion, stress, and drying substances deplete Qi and Yin further, worsening dryness and fatigue.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Include moistening foods like pear, honey, black sesame, and eggs. Avoid spicy, fried, and overly dry foods. Gentle activities like walking or yoga are better than intense exercise. Prioritize rest and sleep to restore Qi and Yin.

### Wind-Heat
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/wind-heat)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Scalp redness and yellowish flakes, Itching, Sore throat, Mild fever or aversion to wind
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Windy or drafty weather, Spicy, fried, or greasy foods, Emotional stress, frustration, or anger, Overwork, late nights, or fatigue
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Mint or chrysanthemum tea, Rest, sleep, and relaxation, Cool environment, Gentle scalp massage
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  External Wind-Heat invades the body, often through the nose and mouth, and rushes upward to the head. There it damages the fluids that normally keep the scalp moist, creating dryness, redness, and irritation. This pattern tends to appear alongside a mild cold or flu, with symptoms like a sore throat and slight fever.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red tip and edges with a thin yellow (or white-to-yellow) coating. Pulse: floating and rapid.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Wind-Heat invades from the exterior, so windy weather and drafts worsen the condition. Spicy foods create internal Heat that rises to the head. Rest, cooling drinks, and a calm environment help the body clear the pathogen and restore moisture to the scalp.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Avoid spicy, greasy, and fried foods that generate internal Heat. Drink cooling herbal teas like chrysanthemum and mint. Keep the scalp clean with mild, natural shampoos and avoid excessive scratching. Protect from wind and drafts.

### Damp-Heat
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/damp-heat)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Scalp feels dry but flakes are oily and yellowish, Heavy, foggy sensation in the head, Sticky or bitter taste in the mouth, Loose, sticky stools or a sense of incomplete evacuation, Body feels heavy and sluggish, especially in humid weather
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Hot, humid weather, Spicy, fried, or greasy foods, Alcohol and sugary drinks, Sedentary lifestyle, Emotional stress, frustration, or anger
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Light, bland meals (e.g., congee, barley water), Cool, dry environment, Gentle exercise or movement, Avoiding greasy and sweet foods
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When the Spleen's ability to transform fluids is weakened, Dampness accumulates in the body and combines with Heat. This Damp-Heat rises to the head and steams the scalp. The paradoxical result is a scalp that feels dry and tight, yet produces sticky, yellowish, oily flakes because the underlying fluids are turbid rather than nourishing.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red body with a yellow, greasy coating that is difficult to scrape off, often swollen with teeth marks. Pulse: slippery and rapid (滑数 huá shuò).
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Damp-Heat thrives on dietary excess and sluggish circulation. Greasy, sweet, and spicy foods create more internal Dampness and Heat, while a sedentary lifestyle allows fluids to stagnate. Light, bland foods and exercise help the Spleen transform and transport fluids, while a cool, dry environment counteracts the external factors that aggravate Damp-Heat. Stress stirs up Liver Qi, which can transform into Heat and combine with Dampness, so emotional calm helps keep the pattern in check.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat light, bland foods that help drain Dampness, such as barley, mung beans, bitter gourd, and lightly cooked leafy greens. Strictly avoid greasy, fried, and sugary foods, as well as alcohol, which all generate more Damp-Heat. Gentle daily exercise that produces a light sweat helps the body clear turbid fluids. Keep the scalp clean with a mild, non-drying cleanser, but avoid harsh scrubbing that can irritate the skin.

## คำถามที่พบบ่อย (Frequently Asked Questions)
### Can TCM cure dry scalp permanently?
TCM aims to correct the underlying imbalance that causes dryness, so results can be long-lasting. If the pattern is fully resolved and you maintain a balanced diet and lifestyle, the scalp often stays healthy. However, if old habits return - chronic stress, poor diet, overwork - the imbalance can creep back. Think of it as retraining your body rather than a one-time fix.

### How long until I see results?
Most people notice less itching and flaking within 2-4 weeks of starting herbs and acupuncture. Patterns driven by excess heat or wind tend to respond faster, sometimes in just a few weeks. Deficiency patterns - where the body needs to rebuild Blood or Yin - require more patience, often 3-6 months for deep, lasting change. Consistency with herbs and dietary changes is key.

### Can I still use my regular shampoo?
Yes, you can use a gentle, fragrance-free shampoo. Avoid harsh anti-dandruff shampoos that strip oils, as they can worsen dryness. Your TCM practitioner may also recommend an external herbal rinse made from herbs like Ku Shen or Huang Bai to calm itching and reduce flakes between treatments.

### Is acupuncture for dry scalp painful?
Acupuncture points on the scalp are typically needled very shallowly, with a mild sensation rather than pain. Most people find the experience relaxing. The needles are hair-thin, and the treatment often includes points on the arms and legs as well to address the internal pattern, so it's a whole-body approach rather than just focusing on the head.

### What foods should I avoid to help my dry scalp?
In general, avoid spicy, greasy, and fried foods, which can generate heat and dampness in the body. Excess alcohol and coffee can also be drying. Instead, favor moistening foods like pears, cucumber, honey, and dark leafy greens. Black sesame seeds and walnuts are especially good for nourishing the scalp. Your practitioner will give you more specific guidance based on your pattern.

### Can stress really cause dry scalp?
From a TCM perspective, yes. Stress causes Liver Qi to stagnate, and over time that stagnation can turn into heat. Heat naturally rises to the head and consumes the fluids that keep the scalp moist. So a flare-up of itching and flaking during a stressful period is a real pattern, not just in your head - and it responds well to herbs and acupuncture that smooth the Liver and clear heat.
