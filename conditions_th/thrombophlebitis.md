## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนปัจจุบัน (Conventional Medicine View)
### คำอธิบาย (Description)
Thrombophlebitis is inflammation of a vein caused by a blood clot. It typically occurs in the legs, causing pain, swelling, redness, and warmth along the affected vein. Superficial thrombophlebitis affects veins near the skin's surface, while deep vein thrombosis (DVT) involves deeper veins and carries a risk of the clot breaking loose and traveling to the lungs (pulmonary embolism). Diagnosis is usually made with ultrasound imaging.

### การรักษาหลัก (Treatments)
Treatment depends on whether the clot is superficial or deep. Superficial thrombophlebitis is often managed with warm compresses, compression stockings, and nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs (NSAIDs). Deep vein thrombosis requires anticoagulant medications (blood thinners) like heparin or warfarin to prevent clot extension and embolism. In some cases, thrombolytic therapy or surgical removal may be necessary.

## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนจีน (Traditional Chinese Medicine View)
### ความเข้าใจเกี่ยวกับการแพทย์แผนจีน (TCM Understanding)
TCM understands thrombophlebitis as a condition of obstructed blood flow in the veins, but the obstruction is rarely just a simple clot. It's always a combination of Blood Stasis - the blood is not moving properly - and another factor that caused it to stagnate and congeal. That second factor might be Damp-Heat (heavy, hot moisture clogging the vessels), Cold-Dampness (cold and moisture slowing circulation), or even a deep deficiency of Yang energy that fails to warm and move the blood.
The Spleen plays a central role because it transforms fluids and keeps them moving. When the Spleen is weak, dampness accumulates, and this dampness can combine with heat from emotional stress or diet to create Damp-Heat. Damp-Heat is heavy and sticky; it sinks to the legs and obstructs the veins, causing the classic red, hot, swollen limb. If the heat intensifies, it can become toxic, damaging the vein wall and leading to ulceration - a pattern called Toxic-Heat.
Alternatively, if the body's Yang energy is deficient - often from chronic illness or aging - the blood vessels lose their warmth and propulsion. Cold and dampness congeal, leading to a pale, cold, heavy limb with chronic swelling. This is the Kidney and Spleen Yang Deficiency pattern. And after the acute inflammation resolves, the body may be left depleted of Qi and Blood, resulting in lingering weakness and a tendency for the clot to recur.
Because each pattern has a different root, TCM treatment is tailored. One person needs cooling and drying herbs to clear Damp-Heat, another needs warming and strengthening herbs to rebuild Yang. This is why a single Western diagnosis like thrombophlebitis maps to multiple TCM patterns - and why the same medication doesn't work for everyone.

### การวินิจฉัยโดยแพทย์แผนจีน (Practitioner Diagnosis)
A TCM practitioner first inspects the limb's color and temperature. A red, hot, swollen, and painful limb with a greasy yellow tongue coating and a slippery rapid pulse points to Damp-Heat-heat and moisture clogging the vessels.
If the limb is instead dark purple, feels warm but not intensely hot, and the pain is a deep ache with a purplish tongue and a wiry or choppy rapid pulse, Blood Stagnation with Heat is the core, where stagnant blood generates local heat.
When the inflammation escalates to intense redness, possible ulceration, and severe pain, accompanied by systemic fever and a deep-red tongue with a yellow greasy coating and a flooding rapid pulse, Toxic-Heat has developed. This dangerous intensification creates toxic fire from heat and stasis, demanding immediate recognition and strong heat-clearing, detoxifying herbs.
If the limb feels heavy, numb, and cold to the touch, with pale skin, aversion to cold, and a white greasy tongue coating with a deep thready slow pulse, Damp-Cold is obstructing the channels.
In contrast, when morning edema, a sore lower back, and cold intolerance dominate, with a pale tongue and thin white coating and a deep thready pulse, the root is Kidney and Spleen Yang Deficiency-the body lacks the warmth to move fluids.
In the recovery or chronic atrophic phase, the pain is mild, the limb muscles may waste, and the skin becomes dry. The tongue appears pale and swollen with a white slippery coating, and the pulse is deep, thready, and weak. This Qi and Blood Deficiency pattern reflects depleted resources after a long illness, calling for gentle nourishment rather than strong dispersing methods.

## กลุ่มอาการการแพทย์แผนจีนและการรักษา (TCM Patterns and Treatment)

### Damp-Heat
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/damp-heat)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Swollen, hot, painful limb, Red or dark red skin over the affected vein, Heavy, dragging sensation in the limb, Yellow greasy tongue coating, Slippery rapid pulse, Bitter taste or sticky sensation in the mouth
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Spicy, greasy, or fried foods, Alcohol, Hot or humid weather, Prolonged sitting or standing
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Elevating the leg, Cool compresses, Light, bland meals, Gentle walking
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Damp-Heat is a combination of excess moisture and heat that builds up inside the body, often from diet, environment, or impaired digestion. In thrombophlebitis, this heavy, hot, sticky pathogen sinks down into the lower limbs, obstructing the flow of Qi and Blood in the veins. The blockage causes swelling, redness, and a hot, painful sensation along the affected vein. The greasy nature of Dampness makes the inflammation linger and the limb feel heavy.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red body with a thick yellow greasy coating. Pulse: slippery and rapid (滑数 Huá Shuò).
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Damp-Heat thrives on heavy, greasy foods and stagnant, humid conditions, which add more moisture and heat to the body. Cooling, light foods and elevating the limb help drain excess fluid and reduce inflammation. Gentle movement encourages circulation, preventing further stasis, while rest and cool applications calm the local heat.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Avoid greasy, fried, spicy, and sweet foods that create Damp-Heat. Eat cooling, light foods like barley, mung beans, cucumber, and celery. Stay hydrated but avoid excessive cold drinks. Gentle walking and elevating the leg when resting help circulation and reduce swelling.

### Blood Stagnation with Heat
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/blood-stagnation-with-heat)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Fixed stabbing pain in the vein, Dark purplish discoloration of the skin, Sensation of local warmth, Pain worse at night and with pressure, Thirst with desire to rinse mouth but not swallow, Restlessness and irritability
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Stress and anger, Spicy, greasy, or fried foods, Hot or humid weather, Prolonged sitting or standing
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Elevating the leg, Cool compresses, Gentle walking, Drinking plenty of water
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  In thrombophlebitis, blood stasis is the root cause. When blood flow becomes sluggish and obstructed in the veins, it can stagnate for a long time, and this long-standing stasis generates heat. The heat further thickens and congeals the blood, creating a vicious cycle that results in a hard, dark, and painful vein with a distinct sensation of warmth.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: reddish-purple body with stasis spots, distended sublingual veins, and a thin dry yellow coating. Pulse: choppy (涩 sè) and rapid (数 shuò), often wiry (弦 xián) at the left Guan position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Spicy foods and hot weather introduce external or dietary heat that fans the flames of the blood stasis heat, making the vein more swollen and painful. Prolonged immobility allows blood to stagnate further, while stress stirs Liver Qi, which can lead to more stasis and heat. Cooling measures and gentle movement help clear heat and move blood, breaking the vicious cycle.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Choose cooling, blood-moving foods like celery, eggplant, mung beans, and plenty of water. Strictly avoid spicy, greasy, and fried foods that add heat. Gentle walking and keeping the legs elevated help circulation, but avoid prolonged standing or sitting. A cool compress can soothe local warmth.

### Toxic-Heat
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/toxic-heat)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Dark red, swollen limb that may ulcerate or form pus, Intense, burning pain that is worse with heat, High fever with restlessness and irritability, Intense thirst for cold drinks, dark scanty urine, constipation
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Spicy, greasy, or fried foods, Alcohol, Heat exposure (hot baths, sauna), Stress and anger
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Cool compresses, Elevating the leg, Cooling foods (mung beans, cucumber), Drinking plenty of water
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When stagnant blood and dampness in the vessels are not cleared, they can smolder and intensify into a toxic fire that scorches the blood and tissues. This toxic heat directly damages the vein walls, causing severe redness, swelling, and a tendency to ulcerate, with intense burning pain. The toxin also spreads beyond the local area, triggering systemic signs like high fever, extreme thirst, and restlessness as the body’s fluids are consumed.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: deep red body with prickly thorns and dry yellow or greasy coating. Pulse: flooding (hong) and rapid (shu), forceful.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Toxic heat is a fiery, aggressive pathogen that thrives on more heat and dampness. Spicy foods, alcohol, and external heat feed the fire and worsen inflammation, while stress and anger stir up liver fire that fans the toxin. Cooling foods and compresses directly counter the heat, rest prevents further irritation, and hydration helps replenish the fluids that the fever and toxin consume.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Avoid all spicy, greasy, and fried foods, as well as alcohol, which add heat and dampness. Eat cooling, detoxifying foods like mung beans, cucumber, watermelon, and dandelion greens. Rest the affected limb and keep it elevated; gentle movement is okay, but avoid overheating. Drink plenty of water to help cool the blood and flush toxins.

### Kidney and Spleen Yang Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/kidney-and-spleen-yang-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Limb edema worse in the morning, Cold intolerance and cold limbs, Sore and cold lower back and knees, Chronic loose stools or early-morning diarrhea, Tiredness and lack of energy
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Cold weather or drafts, Prolonged sitting or standing, Overexertion or overwork, Raw or cold foods
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warmth and warm compresses, Elevating the leg, Gentle walking, Moxibustion
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When the warming fire of the Kidneys and the transforming power of the Spleen are both weak, the body cannot move fluids or keep blood vessels warm. Cold and damp accumulate, slowing circulation and causing blood to congeal in the veins of the legs. The result is chronic, pitting edema and aching that is worse in the morning, because Yang is at its lowest after a night of rest.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale, swollen, and puffy with teeth marks; white slippery coating. Pulse: deep, weak, and slow.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Cold and overwork deplete the body's Yang, the very energy needed to warm the blood and move fluids. Warmth, rest, and gentle movement support Yang, encouraging fluid drainage and easing pain. Elevation assists the weakened Spleen's ability to return fluids upward, while moxibustion adds warming energy directly into the meridians.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat warm, cooked foods like soups and stews; avoid raw, cold, and greasy items. Keep the legs elevated when resting and wear warm socks. Gentle daily walking helps circulation, but avoid standing still for long periods.

### Damp-Cold
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/damp-cold)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Limb feels heavy, numb, and cold to the touch, Pale or slightly dusky skin, no redness or heat, Aversion to cold, preference for warmth, Pain or stiffness worse in cold, damp weather, Poor appetite, loose stools, or a bland taste in the mouth
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Cold weather or drafts, Prolonged sitting or standing, Raw or cold foods
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warmth and warm compresses, Gentle walking, Warm, cooked meals, Moxibustion
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When the body is exposed to prolonged cold and dampness, or when the Spleen's ability to transform fluids is weak, Cold and Dampness can combine and seep into the channels. This heavy, sticky obstruction slows the flow of Qi and blood, causing the limb to feel heavy, numb, and cold. The sluggish circulation allows blood to stagnate locally, forming a clot that feels like a hard cord under the skin, but without the redness and heat seen in hotter patterns.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale, swollen, often with teeth marks, and a thick white greasy coating that looks excessively moist. Pulse: deep, slow, and slippery or soggy.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Damp-Cold is heavy, sticky, and contracting by nature. Cold and moisture from the environment or diet directly feed the pattern, thickening the Dampness and further slowing circulation, which makes the limb feel colder and more painful. Warmth and drying methods do the opposite-they melt the Cold, evaporate the Dampness, and get Qi and blood moving again, which is why moxibustion, warm food, and gentle exercise bring relief.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Keep the affected limb warm with soft wool or cotton coverings, and avoid exposure to cold, damp environments. Eat warm, cooked foods like soups and stews, and include warming spices such as ginger, cinnamon, and black pepper. Gentle daily movement like walking helps keep Qi and blood moving, but avoid overexertion or prolonged sitting. Moxibustion at home on the lower abdomen or legs can also provide deep, penetrating warmth.

### Qi and Blood Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/qi-and-blood-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Mild, lingering dull ache, Muscle wasting or thinning of the limb, Dry, pale skin, Persistent fatigue and weakness, Dizziness or lightheadedness, Poor appetite
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Overexertion or overwork, Cold weather or drafts, Raw or cold foods, Stress and anger
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Rest and adequate sleep, Warm, cooked meals, Gentle walking, Elevating the leg
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  After the acute inflammation of thrombophlebitis subsides, the body's Qi and Blood are often left severely depleted. Without enough Qi to propel circulation and enough Blood to nourish the vessel walls, the veins become weak and fail to recover fully. This leads to lingering dull ache, muscle wasting, and a heavy sensation in the affected limb long after the redness and heat have gone.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale body, may be thin or have teeth marks, with a thin white coating. Pulse: deep, thready, and weak.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Qi and Blood Deficiency means the body's engine is running on empty. Overexertion and cold further drain the little Qi you have, while rest and warm, nutrient-dense foods provide the raw materials to rebuild. Gentle movement prevents blood from pooling without exhausting you, and elevating the limb uses gravity to assist the weakened veins.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat warm, easily digested, nourishing foods like chicken bone broth, congee with red dates, and gently cooked leafy greens. A small handful of goji berries daily can help build Blood. Avoid cold raw foods and iced drinks, which tax the Spleen. Gentle walking or tai chi is ideal-enough to move Qi and Blood without draining your reserves.

## คำถามที่พบบ่อย (Frequently Asked Questions)
### How does TCM treat thrombophlebitis differently from Western medicine?
Western medicine focuses on preventing clot extension and embolism with blood thinners, while TCM aims to resolve the root imbalance that caused the blood to stagnate in the first place. This means treatment is personalized: cooling and drying herbs for Damp-Heat, blood-moving herbs for stasis, or warming tonics for cold and deficiency. The goal is not just to manage the current clot but to restore healthy circulation so clots are less likely to form again.

### Can I take Chinese herbs while on blood thinners?
It is possible, but requires close coordination with both your TCM practitioner and your prescribing doctor. Many Chinese herbs have mild blood-moving or antiplatelet effects, which could theoretically increase bleeding risk when combined with warfarin, heparin, or other anticoagulants. Never stop or adjust your medication without medical supervision. Always bring a full list of your medications to your TCM consultation, and keep your doctor informed about any herbs you are taking.

### How long does it take to see results?
Most patients notice a reduction in pain and redness within the first 1-2 weeks of starting herbs. Swelling may take a bit longer to subside. Acupuncture typically provides some immediate relief of discomfort, but lasting improvement builds over several sessions. The total treatment duration depends on your pattern - acute heat patterns often resolve in 4-6 weeks, while chronic deficiency patterns may require 3-6 months to fully rebuild the body's strength and prevent recurrence.

### Does acupuncture help with the pain of thrombophlebitis?
Yes, acupuncture can be very effective for reducing the local pain, heaviness, and discomfort of thrombophlebitis. Points on the affected limb and along the Spleen and Liver channels are selected to move stagnant blood and clear heat or dampness. Many patients experience a noticeable easing of pain after a session. However, acupuncture alone is rarely sufficient to dissolve a clot - it works best as part of a comprehensive plan that includes herbal medicine.

### What dietary changes should I make?
Regardless of your pattern, avoid foods that create dampness and inflammation: greasy, fried, and heavily processed foods, excessive sugar, dairy, and alcohol. Eat light, easily digestible meals - steamed vegetables, lean proteins, and whole grains. Drink plenty of warm water to help keep the blood moving. If your pattern is hot, add cooling foods like cucumber and mung beans; if cold or deficient, favour warming spices like ginger and cooked rather than raw foods.

### Is it safe to exercise with thrombophlebitis?
Gentle walking is usually encouraged to promote circulation, but avoid strenuous activity or prolonged standing that increases leg pressure. Always follow your doctor's advice regarding activity level, especially if you have a deep vein thrombosis. In TCM, moderate movement helps prevent Qi stagnation, but overexertion can weaken the Spleen and worsen dampness. Listen to your body and rest when the limb feels heavy or painful.

### Can TCM prevent the clot from traveling to my lungs?
TCM is not a substitute for emergency medical care or anticoagulant therapy in preventing pulmonary embolism. If you have a diagnosed DVT, you must follow your doctor's treatment plan. TCM can support overall vascular health and reduce the underlying tendency toward clot formation, but it does not provide immediate protection against clot migration. Seek emergency care immediately if you experience sudden shortness of breath, chest pain, or coughing up blood.
