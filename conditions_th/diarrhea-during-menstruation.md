## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนปัจจุบัน (Conventional Medicine View)
### คำอธิบาย (Description)
Western medicine explains menstrual diarrhea primarily through prostaglandins - hormone-like substances that cause the uterus to contract and shed its lining. These prostaglandins can also affect the bowel, increasing its contractions and leading to loose stools or diarrhea. For some women, underlying conditions like irritable bowel syndrome (IBS) or endometriosis can flare during menstruation. Diagnosis is usually based on symptom history, and if severe, tests may rule out inflammatory bowel disease or infection. The focus is on managing the symptoms rather than the cyclical pattern itself.

### การรักษาหลัก (Treatments)
Standard approaches include over-the-counter anti-diarrheal medications like loperamide, nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs (NSAIDs) to reduce prostaglandin production, and hormonal contraceptives to regulate or suppress the menstrual cycle. Dietary adjustments, such as avoiding trigger foods and increasing fiber, are also recommended. For those with concurrent IBS, antispasmodics or low-dose antidepressants may be prescribed.

## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนจีน (Traditional Chinese Medicine View)
### ความเข้าใจเกี่ยวกับการแพทย์แผนจีน (TCM Understanding)
In TCM, your period is a time of natural downward movement - blood and Qi flow to the uterus. If your digestive system (ruled by the Spleen and Stomach) is already weak or congested, this downward pull can tip the balance, causing loose stools or diarrhea.
Think of it like a drain in a sink: if the pipes are clear, water flows smoothly; if there's a blockage or the water pressure is low, the downward force stirs up trouble. That's why the diarrhea often starts just before or during your period.
The Spleen is the main organ involved. It transforms food into energy and manages fluids. When it's deficient, Dampness accumulates - imagine a soggy, heavy sponge that can't do its job. The menstrual downward movement then sends that Dampness into the intestines.
But other organs play a role too: the Liver, which can become stagnant from stress and 'attack' the Spleen, disrupting digestion; the Kidneys, whose warming Yang fire supports the Spleen and holds fluids in; and the Large Intestine itself, which can be inflamed by Heat or frozen by Cold.
This is why two women with menstrual diarrhea can need completely different treatments. One might have a pale, puffy tongue and feel better with a hot water bottle - that's Spleen Deficiency with Dampness. Another might have a wiry pulse and cramping that eases after a bowel movement - that's Liver Qi invading the Spleen. A third might wake at dawn with watery stools and feel cold to the bone - that's Kidney and Spleen Yang Deficiency. TCM's strength is matching the pattern to the person, not just the symptom.

### การวินิจฉัยโดยแพทย์แผนจีน (Practitioner Diagnosis)
A practitioner begins by asking about the diarrhea’s timing, texture, and what it feels like alongside the period. Loose stools that start a few days before the flow and ease once bleeding begins point toward different imbalances than watery stools that strike in the early morning or urgent, foul-smelling diarrhea that flares during heavy flow days.
If the stools are mushy and pale, and you feel heavy-limbed, bloated, and easily tired with a poor appetite, that suggests Spleen Deficiency with Dampness. The tongue looks pale and puffy with a greasy white coating, and the pulse feels soft and slippery - signs the Spleen is too weak to manage fluids properly.
When diarrhea arrives with premenstrual cramping, abdominal distension, irritability, and breast tenderness, then eases after a bowel movement, the picture shifts to Rebellious Liver Qi invading the Spleen. The tongue may be pale-red with a thin white coat, and the pulse feels wiry, reflecting the stuck energy that disrupts digestion each cycle.
Watery, urgent diarrhea that happens at dawn or feels worse with cold, along with a sore lower back, chilly limbs, and fatigue, points to Kidney and Spleen Yang Deficiency. The tongue is pale and moist with a thin white coating, and the pulse is deep, slow, and weak - the body lacks the warming fire needed to hold stools firm during menstruation.
Less commonly, foul-smelling, burning stools with a yellow, greasy tongue coating and a rapid, slippery pulse suggest Damp-Heat in the Large Intestine. Alternatively, intense cramping pain that feels better with a hot water bottle, along with clear, watery stools and a pale tongue with a white coat, reveals Cold invading the Large Intestine. Each pattern’s unique tongue and pulse signature helps confirm the diagnosis.

## กลุ่มอาการการแพทย์แผนจีนและการรักษา (TCM Patterns and Treatment)

### Spleen Deficiency with Dampness
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/spleen-deficiency-with-dampness)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Loose stools before or during period without strong urgency, Bloating and fullness, worse after eating, Heaviness in body and limbs, Fatigue and lack of energy, Pale, puffy tongue with teeth marks and white greasy coating
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Cold or raw foods, Dairy and greasy foods, Overeating, Damp, cold weather, Excessive fatigue
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warmth on the abdomen, Rest and lying down, Warm, cooked meals, Ginger tea, Light walking
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When the Spleen is weak, it cannot properly transform food and fluids into usable Qi and blood. Dampness accumulates inside the body. During menstruation, blood and Qi move downward, further draining the already deficient Spleen. This allows the accumulated Dampness to pour into the intestines, producing loose stools or watery diarrhea during your period.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale, swollen body with teeth marks, white greasy coating, moist surface. Pulse: soggy (Ru) and slow (Huan), possibly slippery.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: The Spleen needs warmth and easily digestible food to function; cold, raw, and heavy foods overwhelm its weak Qi and generate more Dampness. Rest conserves Spleen Qi, while gentle movement helps Qi circulate without draining it. Warmth directly counteracts the Cold-Damp nature of this pattern, so keeping the abdomen warm and drinking ginger tea eases diarrhea.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Favor warm, cooked foods like congee, soups, and steamed vegetables. Avoid raw salads, cold drinks, dairy, and greasy foods that burden the Spleen. Light exercise like walking helps Qi move, but rest when fatigued. Keep your abdomen warm during menstruation.

### Rebellious Liver Qi invading the Spleen
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/rebellious-liver-qi-invading-the-spleen)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Abdominal pain before diarrhea, relieved after, Worse with stress and emotional upset, Distension along ribs and bloating, Irritability and frequent sighing, Loose stools alternating with constipation
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Stress and emotional upset, Anger or frustration, Cold, raw, or greasy foods, Irregular meal times, Overwork and lack of rest
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Stress reduction, Warm, cooked foods, Gentle walking or yoga, Avoiding cold and raw foods
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  In the days before your period, Liver Qi can easily become stagnant from stress or frustration. When the menstrual flow begins, that stuck Qi may surge and 'invade' the Spleen, which is responsible for digestion. The Spleen's function gets disrupted, so food and fluids are not properly transformed, leading to loose stools or diarrhea that often comes with cramping pain before the bowel movement and relief afterwards.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: Normal or slightly pale body, possible teeth marks, thin white coating that may be slightly greasy. Pulse: Wiry (弦 xián) overall, especially at the left middle position; softer at the right middle position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Emotional stress, anger, and frustration are the primary triggers because they directly cause Liver Qi to stagnate and then rebel against the Spleen. This is the core mechanism of this pattern. Cold, raw, and greasy foods further weaken the Spleen's digestive fire, making it more vulnerable to the Liver's attack. Gentle exercise and stress reduction help smooth the flow of Liver Qi, while warm, cooked meals provide the Spleen with the support it needs to function properly.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat warm, easily digestible meals like soups and congees to support the Spleen. Avoid cold, raw, and greasy foods, which create dampness and burden digestion. Gentle movement such as walking or yoga can help move stagnant Liver Qi, and finding ways to manage stress-like deep breathing or journaling-is essential to prevent the Liver from overacting on the Spleen.

### Kidney and Spleen Yang Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/kidney-and-spleen-yang-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Watery diarrhea around 5 AM (early morning), Stools contain undigested food, Cold hands and feet, especially below the knees, Sore, aching lower back and weak knees, Chronic tiredness and feeling cold easily
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Cold or raw foods and iced drinks, Cold weather or drafts, Overwork and staying up late, Eating late at night
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warmth on the abdomen, Hot cooked meals and ginger tea, Rest and lying down, Moxibustion on the lower back
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Your Kidney and Spleen Yang act like a warming fire that cooks food and manages fluids. When that fire is weak, the Spleen can't transform nutrients and the Kidneys can't hold fluids in place. During your period, the body's Qi and blood naturally move downward, pulling even more warmth away from the middle. This leaves cold, undigested fluids to slip straight into the intestines, producing watery diarrhea that often strikes in the early morning hours when Yang is at its lowest.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale, swollen and puffy with teeth marks on the edges, and a white slippery coating. Pulse: deep, weak and slow; both rear (Kidney) positions feel especially feeble.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: This pattern is driven by a lack of warming Yang. Cold and raw substances douse that fire further, while overwork and late nights exhaust the Kidneys and Spleen. Warmth, rest, and warm foods directly supplement the Yang, helping the body cook and hold onto what it needs instead of losing fluids as diarrhea.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Keep your abdomen and lower back warm, especially during your period. Eat only cooked, warm foods - soups, stews, congee with ginger and a pinch of cinnamon. Avoid all raw, cold, or iced foods and drinks. Gentle moxibustion on the lower abdomen and lower back a few days before your period can make a big difference. Rest early and avoid overworking, which drains Yang further.

### Damp-Heat in the Large Intestine
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/damp-heat-in-the-large-intestine)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Urgent, foul-smelling yellow-brown stools, Burning sensation at the anus and incomplete evacuation, Feeling of body heat, thirst, and scanty dark urine, Thick, yellow, greasy tongue coating, Abdominal cramping and heaviness in the limbs
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Spicy, fried, or greasy foods, Alcohol and coffee, Emotional stress or anger, Hot, humid weather, Overeating or heavy meals
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Light, bland diet (e.g., congee, steamed greens), Cooling foods like mung bean or barley soup, Rest and reduced stress, Gentle abdominal massage, Warm (not iced) water
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Damp-Heat brews in the Large Intestine when rich, spicy foods and irregular eating overwhelm the Spleen and create a warm, sticky environment. This leads to inflammation, urgent bowel movements, and foul-smelling stools. During menstruation, the body's natural downward movement of Qi and blood stirs the already irritated intestines, causing a flare of diarrhea that coincides with your period.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red body with a thick, yellow, greasy coating that is thickest at the centre and root. Pulse: slippery (滑 huá) and rapid (数 shuò), reflecting the combination of Dampness and Heat.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Damp-Heat thrives on rich, spicy, and greasy foods that create internal heat and stickiness, directly aggravating the intestines. Alcohol and emotional stress further stir up Heat, while hot weather adds external dampness. Cooling, bland foods and rest help drain Dampness and clear Heat, allowing the bowels to settle. Strictly avoid spicy, fried, greasy, or sugary foods, as well as alcohol and coffee, which fuel Damp-Heat. Gentle daily movement like walking helps Qi flow, but avoid heavy exercise during your period. Stay hydrated with warm or room-temperature water, never iced drinks.

### Cold invading the Large Intestine
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/cold-invading-the-large-intestine)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Sudden gripping or cramping pain in the lower abdomen, Watery diarrhea with little odor, Cold sensation in the abdomen, Pain improves with warmth or hot compresses, Prefers warm drinks, no thirst
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Eating cold or raw foods, Exposure to cold environments, Drinking iced beverages, Overexertion
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Applying a hot water bottle to the abdomen, Drinking warm ginger tea, Resting in a warm room, Eating warm, easily digestible foods
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When Cold invades the Large Intestine, it freezes the normal flow of Qi and causes the intestinal muscles to contract painfully. This obstructs the separation of fluids, so water rushes through without being absorbed, producing urgent, watery diarrhea with little odor. During menstruation, the body's natural downward flow of blood can stir the bowels, making this pattern more likely to flare. The cold sensation and relief from warmth are classic signs that the problem is Cold rather than Heat.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale body with a thick white slippery coat, moist. Pulse: deep (Chen) and tight (Jin), especially at the right middle position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Cold congeals and contracts, so warmth relaxes the intestines and promotes normal fluid absorption. Warmth also stimulates Yang Qi to counter the Cold, reducing pain and diarrhea. Cold foods and environments introduce more Cold, worsening the obstruction.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Avoid cold foods and iced drinks completely. Eat only warm, cooked, easily digestible meals and use warming spices like ginger and cinnamon. Keep your abdomen warm with a hot water bottle or a belly band, especially during your period. Rest in a warm environment and avoid drafts.

## คำถามที่พบบ่อย (Frequently Asked Questions)
### What causes diarrhea during my period in TCM?
In TCM, it's usually due to a weakness in your Spleen's ability to manage fluids, combined with the natural downward movement of Qi and blood during menstruation. This can be worsened by stress (Liver Qi stagnation), a lack of warming energy (Kidney Yang deficiency), or internal Dampness and Heat. Each pattern needs a different approach.

### Can acupuncture and herbs really stop menstrual diarrhea?
Yes, when the pattern is correctly identified. Acupuncture helps regulate the nervous system and organ function, while herbs directly strengthen the Spleen, move Liver Qi, or warm the Kidneys. Most women see a significant reduction in symptoms after a few cycles.

### How soon will I see results?
Many women notice that the first period after starting treatment is less intense - perhaps the diarrhea is less urgent or doesn't last as long. For excess patterns, complete relief may come in 2-3 cycles; for deficiency patterns, it may take longer as your body rebuilds its reserves.

### Is it safe to take Chinese herbs during my period?
Yes, the formulas are designed to be taken throughout your cycle, including during menstruation. In fact, they often work best when taken consistently. Always inform your practitioner if you are on any medications, especially blood thinners or hormonal contraceptives.

### Do I need to change my diet?
Diet is a key part of treatment. Generally, warm, cooked foods are best, and cold, raw, or greasy foods should be avoided, as they strain the Spleen. Specific recommendations depend on your pattern - for example, Damp-Heat patterns may need to avoid spicy foods, while Cold patterns benefit from ginger and cinnamon.

### Can TCM help if I also have IBS or endometriosis?
Absolutely. TCM treats the whole person, so the same pattern causing menstrual diarrhea often underlies IBS or endometriosis symptoms. By addressing the root imbalance, you may find relief across multiple conditions.

### Is there anything I can do at home?
Applying a warm compress to your lower abdomen, drinking ginger tea, and eating easily digestible foods like congee can soothe symptoms. Gentle exercise like walking or yoga can also help move stagnant Qi. But for lasting change, a professional diagnosis is essential.
