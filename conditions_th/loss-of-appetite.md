## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนปัจจุบัน (Conventional Medicine View)
### คำอธิบาย (Description)
Western medicine sees appetite loss as a nonspecific symptom with many possible causes - from infections and medications to emotional distress, digestive disorders, and chronic illness. It's often assessed through history, physical exam, and lab tests to rule out serious underlying conditions. Treatment typically targets the root cause when it can be identified, but in many cases, especially when stress or functional issues are involved, specific interventions are limited.

### การรักษาหลัก (Treatments)
When no serious disease is found, conventional management often includes dietary advice (small, frequent meals, nutritional supplements), stress reduction, and sometimes appetite stimulants like megestrol acetate or dronabinol for severe cases. For underlying depression or anxiety, antidepressants or therapy may be recommended. However, many patients are told simply to 'eat more' without a clear strategy for restoring a natural hunger drive.

## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนจีน (Traditional Chinese Medicine View)
### ความเข้าใจเกี่ยวกับการแพทย์แผนจีน (TCM Understanding)
In TCM, appetite is governed primarily by the Spleen and Stomach - the organs responsible for transforming food into Qi and blood. When Spleen Qi is strong, you feel hungry at mealtimes and digest food easily. When it's weak, the body's digestive fire sputters, and hunger fades. But the Spleen doesn't work alone. The Liver, which ensures the smooth flow of Qi, can overact on the Stomach when stressed, suppressing appetite.
Dampness can clog the system, making the very thought of food feel heavy. And when the Stomach's Yin fluids run low, a gnawing emptiness can appear without any real desire to eat. So the same symptom - 'I'm not hungry' - can arise from very different roots.
The Spleen is particularly vulnerable to dietary habits and emotional strain. Irregular eating, cold and raw foods, and overthinking all drain its Qi. That's why Spleen Qi Deficiency is the most common pattern behind chronic poor appetite: it's the classic picture of fatigue, bloating after even small meals, and loose stools. The tongue is pale and puffy, the pulse weak. Treatment focuses on warming and strengthening the Spleen's transformative power.
But not all appetite loss is a deficiency. Sometimes food simply sits undigested because you've overeaten, creating Food Stagnation - a temporary blockage that kills appetite and causes sour belching. Or the Liver, angered by stress, invades the Stomach, making the epigastric area distend and the appetite vanish until the mood lifts. These excess patterns need moving and dispersing, not tonifying.
And then there's the dry, burning type - Stomach Yin Deficiency - where the stomach lining is parched, creating a hunger that isn't truly satisfied. Or the pattern where chronic worry drains both Heart Blood and Spleen Qi, leaving you with little appetite, palpitations, and restless sleep. Each of these six patterns has its own tongue, pulse, and treatment strategy. TCM doesn't just try to make you eat; it restores the conditions that make eating natural again.

### การวินิจฉัยโดยแพทย์แผนจีน (Practitioner Diagnosis)
A TCM practitioner first asks about energy levels and digestion. When appetite is low alongside persistent fatigue, loose stools, and a feeling of bloating after even small meals, Spleen Qi Deficiency is likely. The tongue is often pale with a thin white coating, and the pulse feels weak and soft. This pattern tends to be chronic and worsens when a person is tired or eats cold, raw foods.
If the loss of appetite comes and goes with emotional stress, and is accompanied by epigastric distension, frequent belching, and sighing, the practitioner suspects the Liver is overacting on the Stomach. The tongue may look normal or have slightly red sides, and the pulse feels wiry. The key clue is that the appetite fluctuates directly with mood - worse when frustrated or anxious, better when relaxed.
When poor appetite is paired with a heavy, sluggish sensation in the body, nausea, and a sticky taste in the mouth, dampness is encumbering the Spleen. The tongue is often swollen with tooth marks and a thick, greasy white coating; the pulse is slippery or weak. The person typically has no thirst and feels worse in humid weather or after eating greasy or sweet foods.
An acute loss of appetite that follows overeating or irregular meals points to Food Stagnation. The person feels an uncomfortable fullness in the upper abdomen, may have sour regurgitation, bad breath, and an aversion to the sight or smell of food. The tongue coat is thick and greasy, and the pulse is slippery. This pattern is usually short-lived and directly tied to a recent dietary indiscretion.
If the lack of appetite is accompanied by a dry mouth, a slight burning sensation in the stomach area, and a desire to sip fluids but not to eat, Stomach Yin Deficiency may be present. The tongue appears red with little or no coating, and the pulse is thin and rapid. This pattern often develops after a prolonged fever, chronic stress, or late nights that deplete the body’s cooling, moistening resources.
When overthinking, worry, or mental strain are prominent, and appetite loss is coupled with palpitations, insomnia, and pale complexion, the practitioner considers a combined deficiency of the Heart and Spleen. The tongue is pale with a thin white coat, and the pulse is weak or thready. The appetite fades because the Spleen’s transforming function is weakened by excessive mental work.

### Spleen Qi Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/spleen-qi-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Poor appetite with early satiety, Abdominal bloating or fullness after eating, Loose stools, Fatigue and low energy, Pale complexion
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Cold, raw foods, Overeating or heavy meals, Worry and overthinking, Skipping meals, Damp living environment
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warm, cooked meals, Small, frequent meals, Rest and relaxation, Gentle walking or movement
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  The Spleen is the body’s main digestive organ in TCM, responsible for transforming food into Qi and blood. When Spleen Qi is deficient, this transformation weakens, so the body doesn’t generate the normal signal of hunger. Food sits in the stomach without being processed, causing bloating and a sense of fullness that further suppresses appetite.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale, puffy, and tender with tooth marks, thin white coating. Pulse: weak and soft, especially at the right middle position (Spleen).
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: The Spleen needs warmth and ease to transform food into Qi. Cold and raw foods require extra energy to warm and digest, draining Spleen Qi further. Overeating overwhelms the Spleen’s capacity, leading to stagnation and bloating. Rest and gentle movement support Qi circulation without depleting it, while regular, warm meals provide a steady supply of nourishment that a weak Spleen can handle.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat warm, cooked, easily digestible foods like congee, soups, and steamed vegetables. Avoid raw, cold, and greasy foods that tax the Spleen. Eat at regular times in a relaxed environment, and chew thoroughly. Gentle exercise like walking or tai chi helps Qi circulation without depleting energy.

### Liver Qi Stagnation invading the Stomach
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/liver-qi-stagnation-invading-the-stomach)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Distending pain in the upper abdomen that radiates to the ribs, Frequent belching or acid reflux, Symptoms worsen with stress or frustration, Irritability or emotional depression, Frequent sighing, Sensation of a lump or blockage in the throat
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Emotional stress, Anger or resentment, Overeating or heavy meals, Alcohol, Skipping meals
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Deep breathing or meditation, Warm compress on abdomen, Light, easily digestible meals, Gentle stretching or yoga, Time away from stress
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  The Liver is responsible for the smooth flow of Qi throughout the body. When emotional stress, frustration, or anger disrupt this flow, Liver Qi stagnates and then overacts horizontally on the Stomach. This invasion disrupts the Stomach's natural descending function and its ability to receive food, leading to a loss of appetite, upper abdominal distension, and frequent belching.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: normal or slightly red body with redder sides and a thin white coating. Pulse: wiry (弦 xián), taut like a guitar string, most pronounced at the left middle position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Stress and frustration cause Liver Qi to stagnate and invade the Stomach, directly triggering the pattern. Calming activities allow Liver Qi to flow smoothly, while warm, bland meals reduce the digestive burden and prevent further Qi stagnation. Gentle movement helps circulate Qi, and avoiding heavy or greasy foods prevents additional stagnation in the Middle Burner.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat smaller, regular meals and avoid skipping meals. Steer clear of heavy, greasy, or spicy foods that can further stagnate Qi. Gentle movement like walking or yoga helps keep Qi flowing. Peppermint tea can soothe the Stomach and relieve distension.

### Spleen Deficiency with Dampness
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/spleen-deficiency-with-dampness)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Heaviness in the body and limbs, Sticky or greasy sensation in the mouth, Abdominal bloating or fullness after eating, Loose stools, Drowsiness after meals
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Cold, raw foods, Dairy and greasy foods, Damp, humid weather, Sedentary lifestyle, Worry and overthinking
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warm, cooked meals, Ginger or cardamom tea, Gentle walking or movement, Warm compress on abdomen
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  The Spleen transforms food into Qi and nutrients. When Spleen Qi is weak and dampness builds up, the digestive system becomes sluggish and waterlogged. Dampness weighs down the Spleen, creating a heavy, full sensation even without eating much, and the appetite fades because the body cannot process food properly.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale, swollen body with teeth marks, white greasy coating, moist surface. Pulse: soggy (ru) and slow (huan), soft and weak, especially at the right Guan position; may be slippery (hua).
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Spleen Qi needs warmth and dryness to function. Cold and raw foods, dairy, and damp environments introduce external dampness that further burdens a weak Spleen. Overthinking knots the Qi, while sedentary habits cause Qi to stagnate, both allowing dampness to accumulate. Warm, cooked foods and gentle movement provide the gentle heat and motion needed to transform dampness and restore the appetite.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Avoid cold, raw, and greasy foods that create dampness. Eat warm, cooked meals like congee with ginger or cardamom to support Spleen Qi. Gentle daily movement, like walking, helps Qi flow and dries dampness.

### Food Stagnation in the Stomach
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/food-stagnation-in-the-stomach)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Upper abdominal bloating and distension, Sour, rotten-smelling belching, Aversion to food and its smell, Abdominal bloating or fullness after eating, Thick greasy tongue coating, Loose stools with sour, foul smell
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Overeating or heavy meals, Eating late at night, Lying down immediately after eating, Stressful or rushed eating
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Fasting or eating very lightly, Relief after vomiting, Gentle abdominal massage, Sipping warm water, Warm compress on abdomen
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When you eat more than your Stomach can handle, or eat irregularly, the food sits undigested and stagnates. This creates a sensation of fullness and pressure in the upper abdomen, making the idea of eating more repulsive. The Stomach's natural downward movement is blocked, so Qi rebels upward, causing sour belching and nausea. Because the digestive system is already overwhelmed, appetite disappears.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: normal body with a thick, greasy, curd-like coating, thickest in the centre. Pulse: slippery (Hua, 滑) and full, especially at the right guan (Stomach/Spleen) position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Food stagnation arises from overloading the Stomach, so giving it a rest by fasting or eating lightly allows the digestive system to clear the backlog. Chew food thoroughly and stop eating before you feel full. A warm compress on the abdomen or gentle clockwise massage can help move stagnation. Avoid eating late at night and do not lie down immediately after meals.

### Stomach Yin Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/stomach-yin-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Feeling hungry but not wanting to eat, Dry mouth and throat, Dull burning pain or gnawing discomfort in the upper abdomen, Thirst with a preference for small sips, Dry stools or constipation, Red tongue with little or no coating, dry centre
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Spicy and greasy foods, Dry, roasted, or baked foods, Late nights and overwork, Emotional stress, Alcohol and coffee
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Cool, moistening foods (pears, tofu), Sipping warm water, Rest and relaxation, Light, easily digestible meals
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  The Stomach relies on Yin fluids to moisten and break down food. When those fluids run low, the Stomach becomes dry and mildly overheated. That creates a gnawing, burning sensation and a peculiar mix of hunger with no real desire to eat - the Stomach is signaling for nourishment, but the lack of lubrication makes the thought of food unappealing. The dry mouth and small-sip thirst are direct signs that the body’s digestive moisture is depleted.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red, thin body, dry centre, little or no coating, possibly cracked. Pulse: fine (Xi) and rapid (Shu), weak at the right middle position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Stomach Yin is like the body’s digestive moisture. Dry, spicy, and fried foods further deplete these fluids, intensifying the burning sensation and aversion to eating. Rest and cooling, moistening foods help rebuild Yin and soothe the Stomach, making food more appealing. Small sips of fluid gently replenish without overwhelming the weakened digestive fire.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat small, frequent meals of moistening, easy-to-digest foods like congee, steamed pears, tofu, and spinach. Avoid spicy, fried, and dry foods that further deplete Yin. Sip warm water or herbal teas throughout the day rather than gulping large amounts. Prioritize rest and avoid late nights, as overwork and insufficient sleep consume Yin fluids.

*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/heart-and-spleen-qi-and-blood-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Palpitations or fluttering chest sensation, Insomnia with excessive dreaming, Poor appetite with tiredness after eating, Forgetfulness and poor concentration, Pale, yellowish complexion
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Worry and overthinking, Mental overwork, Cold, raw foods, Irregular meal times
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Rest and relaxation, Warm, cooked meals, Small, frequent meals, Gentle walking or movement
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  The Spleen transforms food into Qi and Blood. Chronic worry and overthinking drain both Spleen Qi and Heart Blood, so the Spleen lacks the energy to create a healthy appetite and the Heart loses its nourishment. This leaves you with little desire to eat, along with palpitations, restless sleep, and deep fatigue.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale, puffy, teeth-marked body with a thin white coating. Pulse: fine and weak overall; the left Cun (Heart) position feels empty, the right Guan (Spleen) position soft and forceless.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Overthinking and mental strain directly consume Heart and Spleen Qi and Blood, which is the root of this pattern. Rest and calm allow those resources to rebuild. Warm, cooked foods are easy for a weakened Spleen to transform, while raw and cold items demand extra energy and further exhaust it.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat small, warm, easily digestible meals - think soups, congees, and stews - and avoid raw, cold, or greasy foods that tax the Spleen. Protect your Heart and Spleen by setting aside quiet time each day; gentle movement like walking or qigong can help, but stop before you feel drained.

## คำถามที่พบบ่อย (Frequently Asked Questions)
### Will acupuncture cure my loss of appetite?
Acupuncture alone can be very effective, especially for stress-related or functional appetite loss. It works by regulating the Stomach and Spleen channels, calming the nervous system, and moving stagnant Qi. However, for deeper deficiency patterns, herbal medicine is usually needed alongside acupuncture to rebuild digestive strength. Most practitioners combine both for lasting results.

### Can Chinese herbs really make me feel hungry again?
Yes. Herbal formulas are designed to address the specific pattern causing your appetite loss. For example, Si Jun Zi Tang strengthens Spleen Qi, while Chai Hu Shu Gan San relieves stress-induced Stomach disharmony. As the underlying imbalance corrects, your body's natural hunger signal gradually returns - not as a forced stimulation, but as a genuine desire to eat.

### Is it safe to combine TCM treatment with my current medications?
Generally yes, but always inform both your doctor and TCM practitioner about all medications you're taking. Herbs that tonify the Spleen (like Dang Shen, Bai Zhu) are very safe and rarely interact with drugs. However, if you're on appetite stimulants or antidepressants, your practitioner may adjust the formula. Never stop prescribed medications without consulting your doctor.

### How long before I see results?
Many people notice less bloating and a slight return of appetite within the first 2 weeks. Full restoration of a healthy appetite typically takes 4-8 weeks, depending on the pattern and how long the imbalance has been present. Excess patterns resolve faster; long-standing deficiency takes longer because the body needs time to rebuild Qi and blood.

### Do I need to change my diet completely?
You don't need a drastic overhaul, but a few key shifts help tremendously. Focus on warm, cooked foods and regular meal times. Avoid icy drinks, raw salads, and greasy foods. Small, frequent meals are easier on a weak Spleen. Your TCM practitioner will give you specific guidance based on your pattern - for example, someone with Dampness may need to cut out dairy, while someone with Yin Deficiency benefits from moistening foods like pears.

### Can stress really cause me to lose my appetite?
Absolutely. In TCM, the Liver is responsible for the smooth flow of Qi. When you're stressed or frustrated, Liver Qi stagnates and then 'invades' the Stomach, disrupting its function. This directly suppresses appetite and causes bloating, belching, and a tight sensation in the upper abdomen. The good news is that this pattern often responds quickly to acupuncture and herbs that soothe the Liver.

   This review of 49 RCTs concluded that Chinese herbal medicine, including formulas like Liu Jun Zi Tang and Shen Ling Bai Zhu San, significantly improved overall symptoms, appetite, and quality of life in functional dyspepsia compared to placebo or prokinetics. However, the quality of evidence was rated low to moderate due to risk of bias.

   An RCT of 120 patients with Spleen Qi Deficiency found that Si Jun Zi Tang significantly increased appetite scores, reduced bloating, and improved gastric emptying time compared to placebo. Serum ghrelin levels also increased, suggesting a hormonal mechanism for the appetite improvement.
