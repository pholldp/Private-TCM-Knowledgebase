## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนปัจจุบัน (Conventional Medicine View)
### คำอธิบาย (Description)
A hemorrhagic stroke happens when a weakened blood vessel in the brain ruptures and bleeds into the surrounding tissue, damaging brain cells through pressure and reduced oxygen. The most common triggers are high blood pressure, aneurysms, or head injury. Symptoms strike suddenly and can include a thunderclap headache, weakness or numbness on one side of the body, trouble speaking or understanding speech, vision changes, and loss of balance or consciousness. Doctors confirm the diagnosis with a CT scan or MRI, and emergency treatment focuses on stopping the bleeding, lowering pressure inside the skull, and stabilizing vital functions.

### การรักษาหลัก (Treatments)
In the emergency room, treatment centers on rapidly controlling blood pressure, reversing any blood‑thinning medications, and sometimes surgically removing the blood clot if it is large or life‑threatening. After the acute phase, rehabilitation becomes the mainstay - physical therapy to regain movement, occupational therapy for daily skills, and speech therapy for communication and swallowing. Medications to manage risk factors (antihypertensives, statins, and sometimes antiplatelets) are prescribed long‑term to help prevent another stroke.

## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนจีน (Traditional Chinese Medicine View)
### ความเข้าใจเกี่ยวกับการแพทย์แผนจีน (TCM Understanding)
TCM understands a hemorrhagic stroke as a violent disruption of the body’s internal landscape. The most common trigger is Liver Yang rising and transforming into internal Wind - a sudden, forceful upward surge of Qi and Blood that can rupture vessels in the head. This pattern often builds over years of unaddressed Liver and Kidney imbalance, where Kidney Yin is too weak to anchor the Liver’s Yang, and emotional stress, anger, or overwork finally ignites the crisis.
Phlegm plays a different but equally dangerous role. When the Spleen is weak, it fails to transform fluids, and a heavy, turbid phlegm accumulates. This phlegm can rise to the brain and block the clear orifices, causing the deep confusion, slurred speech, and rattling breath sounds that sometimes accompany a severe stroke. Phlegm often combines with Wind, making the loss of consciousness deeper and recovery more challenging.
Heat in the Blood is another acute pattern. Here, intense internal heat makes the blood “reckless” and prone to extravasation - it boils over and escapes the vessels, directly causing bleeding. The person may have a high fever, a flushed face, and a tongue that is deep red with a dry yellow coat, signaling that the Heat must be cleared urgently to stop further damage.
After the acute event, Blood Stasis becomes a central concern. The escaped blood turns into a static clot that presses on brain tissue and blocks the flow of Qi through the channels, leading to paralysis, numbness, or speech loss.
At the same time, underlying deficiencies - often Qi deficiency or Yin deficiency - become more apparent. Qi deficiency means the body lacks the energy to clear stasis and repair tissue; Yin deficiency means the cooling, anchoring force is still missing, leaving the person vulnerable to another surge of Wind. These root deficiencies explain why the same Western diagnosis of hemorrhagic stroke can arise from such different TCM patterns, and why treatment must be highly individualized.

### การวินิจฉัยโดยแพทย์แผนจีน (Practitioner Diagnosis)
In the emergency setting, a TCM practitioner pieces together the pattern by observing the person’s level of consciousness, facial color, breathing sounds, and the appearance of the tongue. Family reports of a sudden collapse, severe headache, or dizziness also guide the diagnosis. Because hemorrhagic stroke is so abrupt, the tongue body and coating provide a quick window into the dominant internal imbalance.
Liver Wind agitating internally from Liver Yang rising is the core pattern, present in nearly every case. The person typically loses consciousness suddenly, with a red face, hot body, bitter taste in the mouth if awake, a red tongue with a thin yellow coating, and a wiry, rapid pulse. This picture tells the practitioner that Liver Yang has transformed into Wind and surged upward to disturb the brain.
When Turbid Phlegm blocks the orifices, the loss of consciousness is deeper and there is often a rattling sound of phlegm in the throat. The tongue coating becomes thick and greasy, and the pulse feels slippery. This pattern frequently combines with Wind, making it harder for the person to regain awareness and pointing to a strong phlegm component that must be addressed.
Heat in the Blood shows up as high fever, extreme restlessness, a dark red or purple-red tongue, and a very rapid pulse. This excess heat drives further bleeding and inflammation, adding urgency to the clinical picture. Blood Stagnation, while often a consequence of the hemorrhage, presents with a fixed, severe headache, a purplish tongue with stasis spots, and a choppy pulse.

### Liver Wind agitating Internally due to Liver Yang Rising
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/liver-wind-agitating-internally-due-to-liver-yang-rising)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Severe dizziness with a falling sensation, Throbbing, distending headache, Stiff neck and tremor of hands or head, Flushed face and red eyes, Irritability, easy anger, and vivid disturbing dreams, Tongue red, stiff or trembling, may deviate to one side
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Emotional stress, anger, or worry, Alcohol, spicy, greasy, or fried foods, Overexertion, overwork, or lack of sleep, Hot or stuffy environments
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Rest in a quiet, dark room, Cooling foods and drinks (e.g., mung bean, watermelon, cucumber), Gentle movement or exercise, Acupuncture, acupressure, or herbal therapy
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  In hemorrhagic stroke, the root is often a long-standing Kidney Yin deficiency that fails to anchor Liver Yang. When Liver Yang loses its mooring, it surges upward as turbulent internal Wind, carrying Blood and Qi violently to the head. This sudden upward rush can rupture cerebral vessels, causing bleeding into the brain tissue and the abrupt onset of severe neurological symptoms.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red body, stiff or trembling, may deviate to one side, with a thin yellow or scanty coating. Pulse: wiry (弦 xián), fine (细 xì), and rapid (数 shuò).
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Anger and emotional stress directly stir Liver Yang to surge upward, which is the core mechanism of this pattern. Alcohol and heating foods add fuel to the fire, making the Wind more violent. Rest, darkness, and quiet help anchor Yang back down, while cooling and Yin‑nourishing foods replenish the depleted Kidney and Liver Yin that normally holds Yang in check. Gentle movement like Tai Chi promotes smooth Qi flow without over‑stimulating the Liver, and acupuncture points like Taichong and Baihui actively redirect rebellious Qi downward. By consistently removing the triggers that fan the flames and strengthening the Yin foundation, the internal Wind gradually settles.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Focus on cooling, Yin‑nourishing foods such as pears, spinach, cucumber, black sesame, and mung beans. Avoid alcohol, coffee, spicy and greasy foods, which can fan Liver fire and stir up Wind. Keep a calm, low‑stress routine, and consider gentle practices like Tai Chi or meditation to help anchor Yang. Never skip rest, especially in the evening when Yin should be dominant.

### Turbid Phlegm Blocks Orifices
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/turbid-phlegm-blocks-orifices)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Clouded or confused consciousness, Rattling phlegm sound in the throat, Slurred or impaired speech, Heavy-headedness or feeling the head is wrapped, Nausea or vomiting of phlegm-like fluid
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Cold, raw, or heavy foods, Overeating, Cold or damp environments, Prolonged inactivity or bed rest
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warmth or warm compresses, Light, easily digestible meals, Gentle movement or exercise, Rest in a quiet, dark room
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When the Spleen is weak, it fails to transform fluids, which then congeal into phlegm-dampness. This turbid phlegm accumulates in the middle burner, causing chest tightness and nausea, and then rises to the head where it blocks the brain's clear orifices. The result is the heavy-headed confusion, slurred speech, and rattling throat sounds characteristic of this stroke presentation.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale, swollen body with teeth marks, thick white greasy-slippery coat. Pulse: slippery (滑 huá) and often wiry (弦 xián).
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Phlegm forms when the Spleen fails to transform fluids, a problem worsened by heavy, cold, or damp foods that further burden digestion. A warm, dry environment and light, warm meals support Spleen Yang and help it process fluids properly. Gentle movement keeps Qi circulating so phlegm cannot settle and stagnate, while rest conserves the Spleen's energy for recovery.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Avoid damp-producing foods: dairy, sugar, cold and raw items, and heavy greasy meals. Favor warm, cooked, easily digested dishes like congee with ginger. Small, regular meals support the Spleen. Gentle daily movement - even seated stretching - helps Qi flow and prevents phlegm from stagnating.

### Heat in the Blood
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/heat-in-the-blood)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Sudden severe headache, High fever and flushed red face, Intense thirst for cold drinks, Restlessness and irritability, Deep red tongue with red prickles, Rapid, forceful pulse
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Emotional stress, anger, or worry, Alcohol, spicy, greasy, or fried foods, Hot or stuffy environments, Overexertion, overwork, or lack of sleep
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Rest in a quiet, dark room, Cool environment or cold compresses, Cooling foods and drinks (e.g., mung bean, watermelon, cucumber)
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Heat in the Blood is an excess internal heat pattern where pathogenic heat invades the Blood level, making the blood 'reckless' and prone to extravasation. In the brain, this reckless blood movement can rupture vessels, causing a hemorrhagic stroke. The intense heat also disturbs the Heart and mind (Shen), leading to acute restlessness, confusion, or loss of consciousness, while consuming body fluids, causing thirst and a dry, red tongue.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: deep red with red spots or prickles, thin yellow dry coating. Pulse: rapid (Shu), wiry (Xian), and forceful.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Heat in the Blood is an excess heat pattern where any additional heat-from spicy food, alcohol, or emotional agitation-fans the internal fire and makes the blood even more reckless. Cooling foods, rest, and a calm environment directly counteract the heat, helping to settle the blood and prevent further bleeding.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: In the acute phase, emergency medical care is essential. After stabilization, avoid all heating foods and substances: spicy foods, alcohol, coffee, and red meat. Eat cooling, Yin-nourishing foods like cucumber, watermelon, pear, mung bean soup, and leafy greens. Keep the room cool and quiet, and practice gentle stress reduction like meditation once cleared by a doctor.

### Blood Stagnation
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/blood-stagnation)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Fixed, stabbing head pain (if conscious), Dark purplish or dusky facial complexion, Dark purple tongue with stasis spots, Dark lips and nails, Hemiplegia that feels fixed and unmoving
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Cold, raw, or heavy foods, Emotional stress, anger, or worry, Prolonged inactivity or bed rest, Cold or damp environments, Overexertion, overwork, or lack of sleep
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Gentle movement or exercise, Warmth or warm compresses, Light, easily digestible meals, Avoiding prolonged immobility, Rest in a quiet, dark room
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  In a hemorrhagic stroke, blood escapes from vessels inside the brain and becomes static blood (瘀血). This stagnant blood presses on and blocks the brain's delicate channels, cutting off the flow of Qi and nourishment. The result is sudden, fixed neurological deficits like paralysis or speech loss. The static blood also triggers local swelling and further impairs circulation, worsening the damage.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: dark purple body with stasis spots, distended dark sublingual veins. Pulse: choppy (涩, Se) or wiry (弦, Xian).
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Blood Stagnation is like a traffic jam in the vessels. Cold and inactivity thicken the blood and slow its flow, making the jam worse, while warmth and gentle movement encourage circulation and help clear the static blood. Emotional stress tightens the vessel walls and further obstructs flow, so calmness and relaxation are essential to allow blood to move freely again.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Avoid cold, raw foods and icy drinks, which can congeal blood and worsen stasis. Eat warming blood-nourishing foods like black fungus, hawthorn, turmeric, and ginger. Once stable, gentle daily movement such as walking or physiotherapy helps keep blood flowing. Avoid prolonged bed rest and keep the room comfortably warm.

### Qi Deficiency causing Blood Stagnation
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/qi-deficiency-causing-blood-stagnation)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Profound fatigue and weakness, Fixed, stabbing head pain worse with pressure, Shortness of breath with reluctance to speak, Dull, dusky facial complexion
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Emotional stress, anger, or worry, Cold, raw, or heavy foods, Overexertion, overwork, or lack of sleep, Cold or damp environments
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Rest in a quiet, dark room, Warmth or warm compresses, Warm, nourishing soups and congee, Gentle movement or exercise
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When Qi is weak, it cannot push blood through the vessels with enough force. Over time, the blood slows down and begins to pool, creating stasis in the brain's delicate network of vessels. That stagnant blood becomes a physical blockage that can trigger a rupture and bleed, or it lingers after the stroke and makes recovery slow and incomplete.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale-dark or pale-purple body with stasis spots, tooth marks, and a thin white coat. Pulse: deep (沉 chén), fine (细 xì) or weak (弱 ruò), and choppy (涩 sè).
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Qi deficiency means the body's vital energy is low. Rest and warmth conserve Qi, while cold and overexertion deplete it. Gentle movement helps circulate blood and build Qi gradually, whereas prolonged inactivity leads to further stasis. Nourishing foods support Qi production, while cold raw foods weaken the digestive system that produces Qi.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Focus on gentle, consistent movement like slow walking or tai chi to gradually rebuild Qi without exhausting it. Eat warm, easily digestible foods such as rice congee with a little ginger. Avoid cold, raw foods and iced drinks that further weaken the digestive Qi.

  When Liver Yin and Blood are chronically depleted, the Liver loses its ability to anchor and cool its Yang. This is not a sudden Wind attack but a slow, grinding process where deficiency allows Yang to surge, causing a vessel to rupture and bleed.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red, thin body with red sides; coating is scanty or peeled, and the surface is dry. Pulse: wiry (xian) and fine (xi), especially at the chi positions; the left guan position feels wiry and forceful.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Stress and anger surge Liver Yang upward, the very mechanism that can rupture a vessel, so calming the mind is essential. Overwork and insufficient sleep further consume Yin and Blood, leaving Yang even less anchored. Spicy and greasy foods create heat and dampness that can aggravate rising Yang, while cooling, Yin-nourishing foods replenish the deficient foundation and help tether Yang. A cool, quiet environment and gentle movement soothe the Liver and prevent the upward rush of Qi and blood.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Focus on deeply nourishing, cooling foods that build Yin and Blood: black sesame, mulberries, goji berries, pears, and spinach. Avoid spicy, greasy, or fried foods that generate heat. Gentle, consistent movement like Tai Chi or walking helps Qi flow without depleting Yin. Prioritize rest and sleep, especially before midnight, to allow the body to regenerate Yin.

## คำถามที่พบบ่อย (Frequently Asked Questions)
### Can acupuncture really help after a hemorrhagic stroke?
Yes, many patients find that acupuncture improves recovery of movement, speech, and daily function when it is started after the acute phase has stabilized. Points like Baihui (DU‑20), Fengchi (GB‑20), and points on the affected limbs are used to stimulate the brain’s repair mechanisms and unblock the meridians that have been affected by the bleed. Research and clinical experience suggest that earlier intervention - once medically safe - often yields better results, though even chronic stroke survivors can benefit.

### Is it safe to take Chinese herbs while I’m on blood pressure medication?
In most cases, yes, but it must be done under the supervision of both your TCM practitioner and your prescribing doctor. Some herbs, such as Tian Ma (Gastrodia) and Gou Teng (Uncaria), are actually used to help lower blood pressure, so your medication dose may need adjustment. Never stop or change your blood pressure medication on your own, and always bring a complete list of your herbs to every medical appointment.

### How soon after a hemorrhagic stroke can I start TCM treatment?
Acupuncture and herbal therapy can often begin while you are still in the hospital, as soon as the bleeding is controlled and you are medically stable. Many acute‑care hospitals in China integrate acupuncture within the first few days to support consciousness and reduce intracranial pressure. In Western settings, it is common to start once you are transferred to a rehabilitation unit. Your neurologist and TCM practitioner should coordinate the timing.

### Will TCM help with the fatigue and brain fog I feel months after the stroke?
Yes, post‑stroke fatigue and mental cloudiness are often signs of underlying Qi deficiency or lingering Phlegm‑dampness. Herbal formulas like Bu Yang Huan Wu Tang can be modified to boost Qi and lift the fog, while acupuncture points such as Zusanli (ST‑36) and Qihai (REN‑6) help rebuild energy. Many patients notice their stamina and mental clarity improve gradually over 4-8 weeks of consistent treatment.

### Can TCM prevent another hemorrhagic stroke?
While no approach can guarantee prevention, TCM aims to correct the root imbalances - such as Liver Yang rising, Phlegm accumulation, or Yin deficiency - that set the stage for the first stroke. By calming the Liver, strengthening the Spleen, and nourishing Kidney Yin, ongoing herbal and acupuncture care can reduce the internal tendencies that make another event more likely. Lifestyle and dietary changes are a crucial part of this long‑term prevention strategy.

### What foods should I eat or avoid after a hemorrhagic stroke?
In general, avoid heavy, greasy, spicy, and overly heating foods that can stir up Wind and Phlegm. Favor light, easily digestible meals like congee, steamed vegetables, and moderate amounts of lean protein. Foods that gently nourish Blood and Yin - such as black sesame, walnuts, goji berries, and mulberries - are often recommended. Keep salt intake low to support healthy blood pressure, and avoid alcohol and smoking.

### Do I have to take herbs forever?
Not necessarily. In the first few months after a stroke, daily herbs are usually needed to drive recovery. As function improves and the pattern stabilizes, many patients transition to a lower maintenance dose or switch to periodic “pulse” courses of herbs, especially during seasonal changes or times of stress. The goal is always to restore enough internal balance that the body can maintain health with minimal intervention, but some people choose to continue gentle herbal support long‑term for prevention.

## การวิจัยทางวิทยาศาสตร์สมัยใหม่ (Modern Scientific Research)
1. **Study of Chinese Medicine Pattern of Acute Stage Hemorrhagic-type Stroke in Taiwan** ([ลิงก์](https://www.airitilibrary.com/Article/Detail/10293507-200603-11-1-42-49-a))
   *วารสารวิชาการ: Lee YC, et al. Study of Chinese Medicine Pattern of Acute Stage Hemorrhagic-type Stroke in Taiwan. Journal of Chinese Medicine. 2006;11(1):42-49.*
   A prospective observational study of 120 acute hemorrhagic stroke patients in Taiwan identifying the most common TCM patterns: Wind (95%), Phlegm (40%), Fire-Heat (30%), Blood Stasis (26%), and Qi Deficiency (5%). The study confirmed that Wind-Phlegm and Wind-Fire-Heat are the dominant combined patterns, guiding targeted herbal and acupuncture interventions.

2. **The Pathological Complexity of Stroke and Rational Treatment with Tong Fu Tang** ([ลิงก์](https://www.thieme-connect.com/products/ejournals/pdf/10.1055/s-0045-1807257.pdf))
   *วารสารวิชาการ: Chen X, et al. The Pathological Complexity of Stroke and Rational Treatment with Tong Fu Tang. Chinese Medicine and Culture. 2025. doi:10.1055/s-0045-1807257.*
   A review article discussing the application of Tong Fu Tang (Open-the-Bowel Decoction) in acute hemorrhagic stroke. It highlights the formula’s ability to clear intense internal heat, resolve phlegm, and promote downward movement to relieve intracranial pressure and restore consciousness during the excess acute phase.

3. **Acupuncture for acute stroke** ([ลิงก์](https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD003317.pub3))
   *วารสารวิชาการ: Yang A, Wu HM, Tang JL, et al. Acupuncture for acute stroke. Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews. 2016;Issue 8. Art. No.: CD003317.*
   Cochrane systematic review of 33 RCTs (n=3946) evaluating acupuncture for acute stroke. Results suggested that acupuncture may improve neurological function and reduce the combined outcome of death or dependency compared with sham acupuncture or no acupuncture, but the evidence was of moderate quality due to methodological limitations.
