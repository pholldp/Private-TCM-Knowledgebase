## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนปัจจุบัน (Conventional Medicine View)
### คำอธิบาย (Description)
In Western medicine, irregular periods refer to menstrual cycles that fall outside the typical 21- to 35-day range, or cycles that vary significantly in length from month to month. They can involve missed periods, unusually heavy or light bleeding, spotting between periods, or cycles that are shorter or longer than normal. Common causes include hormonal imbalances (such as polycystic ovary syndrome or thyroid disorders), stress, significant weight changes, excessive exercise, and structural issues like fibroids or polyps. Diagnosis usually involves a medical history, blood tests, and sometimes a pelvic ultrasound.

### การรักษาหลัก (Treatments)
Treatment depends on the underlying cause. Hormonal contraceptives (pills, patches, rings) are frequently prescribed to regulate the cycle, while progestin therapy can induce a period in women who are not ovulating. For heavy bleeding, tranexamic acid or NSAIDs may be used. Structural problems like fibroids or polyps may require surgical removal. Lifestyle modifications - stress management, maintaining a healthy weight, and moderating exercise - are also recommended.

## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนจีน (Traditional Chinese Medicine View)
### ความเข้าใจเกี่ยวกับการแพทย์แผนจีน (TCM Understanding)
In TCM, the menstrual cycle is governed by the Kidney system, the Liver, and the Spleen, working together through two extraordinary vessels called the Chong (Penetrating Vessel) and Ren (Conception Vessel). The Kidneys store essence, the foundation for all reproductive function. The Liver stores Blood and keeps Qi moving smoothly, and the Spleen transforms food into the Qi and Blood that fill the uterus each month. When these organ systems fall out of balance, the Chong and Ren vessels can't fill and empty on time, and the cycle becomes irregular.
Emotional stress is one of the most common triggers. The Liver is easily affected by frustration, anger, or unexpressed feelings, causing its Qi to stagnate. This stuck Qi disrupts the rhythmic flow that drives the period, leading to unpredictable timing, premenstrual tension, and mood swings. Over time, stagnant Qi can also slow Blood circulation, creating dark clots and painful cramping - a Blood Stagnation pattern.
On the other hand, a woman with chronic fatigue, poor appetite, and a pale, scanty flow is often dealing with Spleen Qi Deficiency. Here, the body simply isn't manufacturing enough Blood to support a full, regular period. The Kidneys can also be deficient: when Kidney Yin runs low, the nourishing foundation dries up, and the resulting empty heat can make periods come early and light, with night sweats and a dry mouth.
Even Dampness and Heat can disturb the cycle. When these pathogens settle in the Liver channel, they flow downward and cause sticky, yellowish menses with a foul odor and a bitter taste. All of these scenarios fall under the Western umbrella of "irregular periods," but in TCM they are distinct imbalances requiring different treatments. The key is to match the pattern to the person, not just the symptom.

### การวินิจฉัยโดยแพทย์แผนจีน (Practitioner Diagnosis)
A practitioner begins by listening carefully to the rhythm of your cycle and the character of your flow. Is it early or late, heavy or scanty, dark or pale? These details, together with how you feel in the days before and during your period, are the first clues that point toward one pattern rather than another.
If your periods swing unpredictably and flare up with emotional stress, Liver Qi Stagnation is often the leading suspect. You might notice breast tenderness, irritability, and a tense feeling in the chest before each cycle. The tongue may be slightly red with a thin coat, and the pulse feels wiry and tight, like a guitar string.
When the blood is dark, clotted, and accompanied by sharp, stabbing cramps, Blood Stagnation is likely at play. The pain tends to be fixed in one spot and feels worse with pressure. The tongue often shows purple spots or a dusky hue, and the pulse has a rough, choppy quality that signals the flow is obstructed.
A period that is consistently light, pale, and delayed, along with persistent fatigue and a poor appetite, points toward Spleen Qi Deficiency. This pattern reflects a lack of the raw material needed to make healthy blood. The tongue is pale and puffy, and the pulse feels weak and thin, as if it’s running out of steam.
If your cycle comes early with scanty, light-colored blood and you feel overheated at night with dry mouth and night sweats, Kidney Yin Deficiency may be the root. Here the body’s cooling, nourishing reserves are low. The tongue is red with little or no coat, and the pulse is thin and rapid, like a small flame flickering.
When the menstrual blood is sticky, yellowish, and has a foul odor, and you notice a yellow vaginal discharge, Damp-Heat in the Liver Channel is often behind the chaos. The tongue coat looks greasy and yellow, and the pulse feels slippery and rapid, as if it’s sliding through a hot, humid mist.

### Liver Qi Stagnation
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/liver-qi-stagnation)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Premenstrual breast distension or tenderness, Irritability and mood swings before period, Frequent sighing, Abdominal bloating that shifts, Cycle unpredictability-early or late, often stress-related
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Emotional stress, frustration, or anger, Sedentary lifestyle, Greasy or heavy foods, Alcohol
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Stress reduction and emotional calm, Gentle exercise (walking, stretching), Peppermint or rose tea, Warmth (heating pad, warm bath on lower abdomen)
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  The Liver is in charge of keeping Qi flowing smoothly throughout the body, including the Chong and Ren channels that govern the menstrual cycle. When emotional stress, frustration, or unexpressed anger build up, Liver Qi gets stuck. This stagnation disrupts the rhythmic filling and emptying of the uterus, so your period may arrive early one month and late the next, often triggered by a stressful event. The blocked Qi also causes the breast tenderness and mood swings that many women feel in the days before bleeding begins.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: normal or slightly dark body, red or slightly purple sides, thin white coat. Pulse: wiry (弦 xián), like a guitar string.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Emotional stress directly causes Liver Qi to stagnate, which is the root of this pattern-so when stress spikes, your cycle becomes unpredictable. Calming activities and herbs that move Qi help unlock the stagnation, allowing the Chong and Ren channels to resume their natural rhythm. Gentle movement prevents Qi from getting stuck, while warmth helps relax the smooth flow of Blood.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Make stress release a non-negotiable part of your day-gentle walks, yoga, or simply deep breathing help keep Qi moving. Sip peppermint or rosebud tea, and add fennel, turmeric, and leafy greens to meals. Minimize greasy, heavy foods and alcohol, which can bog down the Liver.

### Blood Stagnation
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/blood-stagnation)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Stabbing, fixed pain in the lower abdomen, Dark menstrual blood with large clots, Pain worse at night and with pressure, Dark purple tongue with stasis spots, Choppy (Se) pulse, rough and uneven
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Cold exposure (cold weather, icy drinks), Sedentary lifestyle, Emotional stress, frustration, or anger, Raw, cold, or greasy foods
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warmth (heating pad, warm bath on lower abdomen), Gentle exercise (walking, stretching), Warm cooked foods and spices, Stress reduction and emotional calm
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Blood Stagnation means the flow of Blood through the uterus and the Chong and Ren vessels is physically blocked. Instead of a clean, full shedding each month, the lining breaks down unevenly around stuck clots, causing irregular timing, dark clotted bleeding, and sharp, fixed pain.
This often develops after prolonged Liver Qi Stagnation, where stuck Qi fails to push Blood, or after exposure to cold that congeals the Blood. The result is a cycle that feels unpredictable, heavy with clots, and painful in a very specific spot.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: dark purple body with stasis spots, distended sublingual veins. Pulse: choppy (涩 s&egrave;), rough and uneven like a knife scraping bamboo.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Cold congeals Blood and makes stasis worse, so warmth naturally helps Blood flow again. Movement circulates Qi and Blood, preventing stagnation. Stress and frustration tighten the Liver Qi, which is the main driver of Blood movement; when Qi gets stuck, Blood follows. So calming the mind and avoiding raw, cold foods - which chill the Spleen and Uterus - directly eases the root of this pattern.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Keep your abdomen warm, especially during your period, and avoid cold foods and icy drinks. Gentle daily movement like walking or yoga helps circulate Blood. Include warming, blood-moving spices like turmeric, ginger, and black pepper in your meals, and eat moderate amounts of red meat and dark leafy greens. Avoid raw salads and dairy when you feel heavy or painful.

### Spleen Qi Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/spleen-qi-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Scanty, pale, delayed periods, Poor appetite and bloating after eating, Loose stools, Fatigue and heaviness in limbs
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Raw, cold, or greasy foods, Overwork and stress, Skipping meals, Excessive worry
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warm, easily digestible foods, Regular meal times, Rest and naps, Gentle exercise (walking, stretching)
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  The Spleen is the engine that turns food into Qi and Blood. When Spleen Qi is deficient, the body cannot manufacture enough Blood to fill the Chong and Ren vessels-the two channels that govern menstruation. The result is a period that arrives late, is scanty, and looks pale, because the body lacks the vital resources to produce a full, timely flow.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale, puffy, tender body with tooth marks, thin white coating. Pulse: weak, soft, especially at the right guan (Spleen) position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: The Spleen transforms food into Qi and Blood. Cold, raw, or irregular eating depletes Spleen Qi, while warm, consistent meals support its function. Overwork and worry further drain Qi, so rest and calm restore the Spleen's ability to produce enough Blood for a regular cycle.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat warm, cooked foods like soups, stews, and congees. Avoid raw, cold, and greasy foods that weaken the Spleen. Regular, gentle exercise like walking helps Qi circulation but avoid overexertion.

### Kidney Yin Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/kidney-yin-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Scanty, light or bright red menstrual flow, Periods come early or are irregular, Night sweats, Heat in palms, soles, and chest, Lower back soreness, Dry mouth and throat
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Emotional stress, frustration, or anger, Late nights, Overwork and stress, Greasy, fried, or spicy foods, Alcohol
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Adequate sleep, Cooling foods and drinks (light, like cucumber, mung beans), Hydration, Gentle exercise (walking, stretching), Cool environment
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  The Kidneys store the essence that fuels the menstrual cycle. When Kidney Yin is deficient, the nourishing, cooling foundation for the Chong and Ren vessels runs dry, so the uterus isn't filled with enough quality blood.
The resulting empty heat can also push what little blood there is out too early, leading to scanty, often early or irregular periods. This is why the flow is light or bright red and accompanied by classic signs of Yin deficiency like night sweats and a dry mouth.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red, thin body with little or no coating, dry surface, possible cracks. Pulse: fine (xi) and rapid (shu), often weak at the left rear position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Kidney Yin is the body's cooling, moistening reserve. Overwork, late nights, and emotional stress consume this reserve, generating empty heat that further dries up fluids. Spicy, fried foods and alcohol add heat, worsening the imbalance. Rest, hydration, and cooling foods help preserve and replenish Yin, while gentle exercise keeps Qi flowing without depleting fluids.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Prioritize sleep and avoid staying up late, as nighttime is when Yin restores itself. Eat moistening, cooling foods like black sesame, goji berries, mulberries, pears, and bone broths. Avoid spicy, greasy, or overly heating foods and alcohol. Gentle movement like yoga or walking is better than intense workouts that deplete fluids.

### Damp-Heat in the Liver Channel
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/damp-heat-in-the-liver-channel)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Sticky, yellowish menstrual blood, Foul-smelling yellow vaginal discharge, Genital itching or burning, Bitter taste in the mouth, Feeling of heaviness in the body, Dark, scanty urine
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Greasy, fried, or spicy foods, Alcohol and sugar, Hot, humid weather, Emotional stress, frustration, or anger, Sedentary lifestyle
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Cooling foods and drinks (light, like cucumber, mung beans), Gentle exercise (walking, stretching), Keeping the genital area clean and dry, Avoiding greasy and spicy foods, Stress reduction and emotional calm
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  The Liver channel runs through the genitals, and the Liver stores Blood. When Dampness and Heat accumulate in this channel, they flow downward and disturb the uterus. The Heat can make periods arrive early or become heavy, while the Dampness makes the menstrual blood sticky, yellowish, and accompanied by a foul-smelling vaginal discharge.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red body with redder sides, yellow greasy coating thicker at centre and root. Pulse: wiry, slippery, and rapid.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: <p>Greasy, spicy, and sugary foods, along with alcohol, directly generate Dampness and Heat inside the body, feeding the very pathogen that disrupts the Liver channel and the uterus. Emotional stress causes Liver Qi to stagnate; when Qi gets stuck it can generate Heat, which then combines with Dampness and sinks downward. A sedentary lifestyle slows the movement of Qi and allows Dampness to accumulate.</p><p>On the other hand, light, cooling foods help clear Heat without adding more Dampness, while gentle exercise moves Qi and opens the pores to drain Dampness. Keeping the genital area clean and dry prevents external Dampness from invading the channel. Managing stress keeps Liver Qi flowing smoothly, cutting off the internal source of Heat.</p>
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Avoid greasy, fried, spicy, and sugary foods, as well as alcohol, which all create Dampness and Heat. Favour light, cooling foods like cucumber, celery, mung beans, and bitter greens. Drink plenty of room-temperature water, but avoid ice-cold drinks. Gentle daily exercise such as walking or yoga helps move Qi and drain Dampness. Keep the genital area clean and dry, and manage stress with calm breathing or meditation to prevent Liver Qi stagnation.

## คำถามที่พบบ่อย (Frequently Asked Questions)
### How does acupuncture help regulate my periods?
Acupuncture points like Sanyinjiao (SP-6), Taichong (LR-3), and Guanyuan (REN-4) are used to unblock Qi stagnation, nourish Blood, and strengthen the Chong and Ren vessels. The needles signal your body to restore the natural rhythm of the menstrual cycle. Most patients find sessions relaxing, and many notice a reduction in stress and cramping even after the first few treatments.

### Can Chinese herbs balance my hormones?
Yes, but TCM doesn't think in terms of isolated hormones like estrogen or progesterone. Instead, herbal formulas like Xiao Yao San (for Liver Qi stagnation) or Zuo Gui Wan (for Kidney Yin deficiency) work to restore the underlying organ functions that govern the cycle. As the Liver, Spleen, and Kidneys regain balance, the hormonal environment naturally stabilizes. This is why two women with irregular periods may receive completely different herbal formulas.

### Can I combine TCM with birth control pills or other hormonal treatments?
In most cases, yes. Many women start TCM while already on oral contraceptives or using an IUD. The herbs and acupuncture work on a different level - correcting the constitutional imbalance - and do not directly interfere with synthetic hormones. However, always inform both your TCM practitioner and your prescribing doctor about all treatments you are using. Some blood-moving herbs may interact with anticoagulant medications, so full disclosure is essential.

### How long does it take for TCM to regulate my cycle?
You may notice subtle improvements - like less breast tenderness or a lighter mood - within the first month. For the actual cycle length to become more predictable, expect three to six months of consistent treatment. Excess patterns (stagnation, heat) tend to respond faster than deficiency patterns, which require rebuilding the body's Qi and Blood. Patience is key; TCM treats the root, not just the symptom.

### I'm trying to conceive. Is TCM safe while trying to get pregnant?
Absolutely. In fact, regulating the menstrual cycle is often the first step in TCM fertility support. The herbs and acupuncture points used for irregular periods are generally safe when trying to conceive, and many of the same formulas that regulate menstruation also improve egg quality and uterine lining. Let your practitioner know you are trying to conceive so they can tailor the treatment accordingly.

### Will I need to change my diet?
Diet plays a supporting role. In general, TCM recommends warm, cooked foods to protect the Spleen and avoid raw, icy, or greasy foods that can create Dampness and stagnation. Your practitioner may give you more specific advice based on your pattern - for example, someone with Blood Deficiency might be encouraged to eat more iron-rich foods like dark leafy greens and dates, while someone with Damp-Heat might need to cut back on spicy and fried foods.

## การวิจัยทางวิทยาศาสตร์สมัยใหม่ (Modern Scientific Research)
1. **Acupuncture for menstrual irregularity in polycystic ovary syndrome: a randomized controlled trial**
   *วารสารวิชาการ: Zhang Y, et al. (2016). Acupuncture for menstrual irregularity in PCOS: a randomized controlled trial. Chinese Journal of Integrative Medicine, 22(8), 586-593.*
   This trial compared acupuncture with sham acupuncture in 120 women with PCOS-related irregular cycles. After 12 weeks, the acupuncture group showed a significant increase in regular ovulatory cycles and a reduction in cycle length variability compared to the sham group, with effects persisting at follow-up.

2. **Clinical efficacy of Xiao Yao San for irregular menstruation with Liver Qi stagnation: a randomized controlled trial**
   *วารสารวิชาการ: Li H, et al. (2018). Clinical efficacy of Xiao Yao San for irregular menstruation: a randomized controlled trial. Journal of Traditional Chinese Medicine, 38(4), 604-610.*
   In this 12-week RCT, 90 women with irregular cycles and Liver Qi stagnation pattern received either Xiao Yao San or placebo. The herbal group experienced significantly greater improvements in cycle regularity, premenstrual breast tenderness, and emotional irritability. No serious adverse events were reported.
