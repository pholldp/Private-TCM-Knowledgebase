## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนปัจจุบัน (Conventional Medicine View)
### คำอธิบาย (Description)
Sinus pain typically arises from sinusitis - inflammation of the mucous membranes lining the air-filled spaces around the nose, cheeks, and forehead. This inflammation can be triggered by viral infections, bacterial overgrowth, allergies, or structural issues like nasal polyps. The result is swelling that blocks normal drainage, leading to pressure, throbbing or aching pain, thick nasal discharge, reduced sense of smell, and sometimes fever. Diagnosis is usually based on symptom history and physical examination, with imaging reserved for recurrent or severe cases.

### การรักษาหลัก (Treatments)
Standard treatment includes saline nasal irrigation, decongestant sprays, corticosteroid nasal sprays to reduce inflammation, and over-the-counter pain relievers. When a bacterial infection is suspected, antibiotics may be prescribed. For chronic or recurrent sinusitis, doctors may recommend longer courses of antibiotics, allergy management, or surgery to widen the sinus openings and improve drainage.

## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนจีน (Traditional Chinese Medicine View)
### ความเข้าใจเกี่ยวกับการแพทย์แผนจีน (TCM Understanding)
TCM understands sinus pain primarily through the Lungs, because the Lungs open into the nose and govern the body's defensive Qi. When an external pathogen like Wind-Heat invades, it attacks the Lung channel first, disrupting the Lung's ability to diffuse and descend Qi. Fluids and heat accumulate in the nasal passages, causing the sudden congestion, thick yellow discharge, and facial pressure that mark the acute stage.
But the story doesn't end with the Lungs. If the initial invasion isn't fully cleared, heat can lodge deeper, turning into persistent Lung Heat with thicker, greenish mucus and more fixed pain. Alternatively, an underlying weakness of the Spleen - the organ in charge of transforming fluids - can generate internal Dampness. When this Dampness combines with dietary heat or emotional stress, it rises as turbid Damp-Heat, creating a heavy, foggy sinus pain with sticky discharge and digestive sluggishness.
The Liver and Gallbladder can also be involved. Emotional frustration and stress cause Liver Qi to stagnate and generate heat, which travels up the Gallbladder channel to the head, producing sinus pain often at the temples or forehead, with a bitter taste and irritability.
In chronic, lingering cases, the root may be a deficiency - either Spleen and Lung Qi that are too weak to keep the sinuses clear, or Kidney Yin that has been depleted by years of overwork, allowing empty heat to drift upward and dry the nasal passages. This is why two people with the same Western diagnosis of sinusitis can need entirely different TCM treatments.

### การวินิจฉัยโดยแพทย์แผนจีน (Practitioner Diagnosis)
A TCM practitioner begins by looking at the nature of your nasal discharge and the quality of the pain. The color, thickness, and smell of mucus, along with whether the discomfort is sharp, dull, or pressure-like, all act as early clues that point toward one pattern rather than another. Timing matters too - acute, sudden flare-ups suggest an external invasion, while slow, lingering symptoms point to an internal weakness.
If the sinus pain came on quickly after exposure to wind or a change in weather, and you have yellow or thick discharge with a mild fever and a floating, rapid pulse, the picture fits Wind-Heat invading the Lungs. The tongue may be slightly red at the tip with a thin yellow coating. This is the typical acute stage, where the body is fighting off an external pathogen that has lodged in the upper respiratory tract.
When the discharge becomes persistently thick, yellow, or even greenish, and the pain feels more fixed and intense over the cheeks or forehead, Lung Heat is often the culprit. Here the tongue is redder overall with a thicker yellow coat, and the pulse is rapid and forceful. This pattern develops when the initial Wind-Heat was not fully cleared, allowing heat to deepen and smolder in the Lung channel, continuously generating turbid mucus.
If you feel a heavy, foggy sensation in the head along with sinus pressure, and the nasal discharge is sticky, cloudy, and copious, the problem likely involves Damp-Heat invading the Spleen. A greasy yellow tongue coating and a slippery, rapid pulse confirm this. TCM sees this as a digestive weakness creating internal dampness that combines with heat and rises to clog the sinus passages - so you might also notice bloating, poor appetite, or a heavy feeling in the limbs.
Sinus pain accompanied by a bitter taste in the mouth, irritability, and pain that radiates to the temples or sides of the head suggests Liver and Gallbladder Damp-Heat. The tongue here is red, especially at the edges, with a yellow greasy coat, and the pulse is wiry and rapid. In TCM, the Gallbladder channel runs along the sides of the head, so stagnant heat in this organ system easily travels upward and disturbs the sinus area.
Chronic sinus discomfort with clear or white watery discharge, a pale complexion, and constant fatigue points to Spleen and Lung Qi Deficiency. The tongue is pale and swollen with a thin white coating, and the pulse is weak. This is a pattern of depletion - the body's Qi is too weak to properly transform fluids, so thin mucus accumulates over time, and sinus pain returns whenever you are run down.
Finally, when sinus pain is mild but nagging, with a dry nose, night sweats, and a sensation of heat in the evenings, the root is often Kidney Yin Deficiency with Empty-Heat Blazing. The tongue is red with little or no coating, and the pulse is thin and rapid. This pattern is more common in older adults or after prolonged illness, where the body's cooling, nourishing resources are depleted and a low-grade, upward-flaring heat irritates the nasal passages.

### Wind-Heat invading the Lungs
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/wind-heat-invading-the-lungs)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Sudden onset of sinus pain, Thick yellow nasal discharge, Fever with mild chills, Sore throat, Frontal headache or facial pressure, Red tip of tongue with thin yellow coating
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Exposure to wind and cold, Spicy or greasy foods, Stress, Overwork
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Rest, Warm fluids, Steam inhalation, Avoiding drafts
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  The Lungs open into the nose. When external Wind-Heat invades the body, it attacks the Lung channel first, disrupting the Lung's ability to diffuse and descend Qi. Heat and fluids accumulate in the nasal passages, causing congestion, thick yellow discharge, and painful pressure in the sinuses.
This is an acute exterior pattern, so sinus pain typically comes on suddenly with other signs of a fresh cold or flu, like fever, mild chills, and a sore throat.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red at the tip, body slightly red, with a thin yellow coating. Pulse: floating and rapid.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Wind-Heat is an exterior pathogen that invades through the skin and nose. Rest and warmth help the body's defensive Qi push it back out, while steam opens the pores and nasal passages, encouraging the pathogen to exit. Spicy foods add internal heat, and stress or overwork deplete Qi, making it harder to fight off the invasion. Avoiding cold and wind prevents further invasion and aggravation.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Rest and stay warm to support the body's defensive Qi. Drink plenty of warm fluids like chrysanthemum tea or light ginger tea. Steam inhalation with a few drops of peppermint oil can help open the sinuses. Avoid cold drinks, dairy, and greasy or spicy foods that can generate more Heat and mucus.

### Lung Heat
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/lung-heat)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Facial pain and pressure (forehead, cheeks), Thick yellow or greenish nasal discharge, Fever or feeling of body heat, Thirst with desire for cold drinks, Red tongue with yellow dry coating
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Spicy, fried, or greasy foods, Alcohol and smoking, Hot, stuffy environments, Emotional stress and anger
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Cool drinks and fresh fruit, Steam inhalation, Rest in a well-ventilated room
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When external Wind-Heat is not fully cleared, heat lodges in the Lungs. The Lung channel opens into the nose, so this internal heat rises and scorches sinus tissues, turning fluids into thick yellow or greenish mucus. The resulting congestion and inflammation create pressure and pain in the forehead and cheeks.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red body, yellow dry coating, possible prickly papillae on the front third. Pulse: rapid (shu), may be slippery (hua) or overflowing (hong) at the right cun position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Lung Heat is an internal excess heat pattern. Spicy foods, alcohol, and hot environments add more heat to the body, worsening the condition. Emotional stress can stir up internal fire. Cool drinks and steam help clear heat, moisten the passages, and soothe the inflamed sinus tissues. Rest allows the body to reduce internal heat production and supports the Lung's function of descending and dispersing Qi.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Avoid spicy, greasy, and fried foods that generate internal heat. Drink cooling fluids like chrysanthemum tea or pear juice. Use steam inhalation with a few drops of peppermint oil to moisten and open the sinuses. Keep indoor air clean and free of smoke or strong fragrances.

### Damp-Heat invading the Spleen
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/damp-heat-invading-the-spleen)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Heavy, foggy sensation in the head, Thick, sticky, yellow or turbid nasal discharge, Sinus pain and pressure that worsens in damp weather or after heavy meals, Poor appetite, nausea, or abdominal bloating, Greasy, sticky taste in the mouth, Fatigue and heavy, sluggish limbs
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Greasy, fried, or sweet foods, Damp or humid weather, Overeating or late-night meals, Dairy products, Sedentary lifestyle
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Light, easily digestible meals, Warm, dry weather, Gentle exercise like walking, Aromatic spices (e.g., ginger, cardamom), Avoiding heavy, rich foods
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When the Spleen is weak, it fails to transform and transport fluids properly, allowing Dampness to accumulate. Over time, this Dampness can combine with Heat-often from a diet rich in greasy, sweet, or spicy foods-creating a turbid Damp-Heat mixture. Because the Spleen’s channel connects to the nose, this internal Damp-Heat can rise upward and settle in the sinuses, causing thick, sticky discharge, a heavy sensation in the head, and persistent sinus pressure.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red body with a yellow, greasy coating thickest at the centre and root. Pulse: soggy-rapid (Ru-Shu) or slippery-rapid (Hua-Shu).
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Damp-Heat arises when a weak Spleen can no longer transform fluids, and rich, greasy, sweet foods add more dampness and heat to the system. This turbid mixture then rises to the sinuses. Humid weather worsens external dampness, which burdens the Spleen further. Light, easily digestible meals and warm, dry conditions reduce the load on the Spleen, allowing it to process dampness more efficiently. Gentle exercise moves Qi and helps the body dry out internal dampness, while aromatic spices like ginger and cardamom warm the Spleen and dispel dampness, cutting through the heavy, sticky quality of the discharge.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat light, easily digestible meals-think soups, steamed vegetables, and moderate amounts of whole grains. Avoid greasy, fried, sweet, and dairy-heavy foods that create more Dampness. Gentle daily movement like walking or stretching helps the Spleen move Qi and dry out internal dampness. Avoid damp, humid living environments when possible, and try to eat your last meal at least three hours before bed to give digestion a rest.

### Liver and Gallbladder Damp-Heat
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/liver-and-gallbladder-damp-heat)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Pain at the forehead or temples, Thick yellow sticky nasal discharge, Bitter taste in the mouth, Irritability, Fullness or discomfort below the ribs
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Greasy food, Alcohol, Stress/anger, Spicy food, Hot weather
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Bitter vegetables, Cool drinks, Rest, Gentle exercise, Stress reduction
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When stress and frustration cause Liver Qi to stagnate, it can generate heat. This heat, combined with Dampness from a rich diet or weak digestion, travels upward along the Gallbladder channel to the head. The result is sinus pain, often at the forehead or temples, with thick yellow discharge and a bitter taste in the mouth.

*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/spleen-and-lung-qi-deficiency)*

- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When Lung Qi is weak, it fails to properly descend and disperse fluids, allowing thin mucus to accumulate in the sinuses. At the same time, Spleen Qi deficiency impairs the transformation and transport of fluids, generating internal Dampness that rises to obstruct the nasal passages. The result is a chronic, dull sinus pressure and pain with clear or white discharge, made worse by fatigue and improved by rest.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale, puffy, tender body with teeth marks, thin white slippery coating. Pulse: weak, especially in the right cun (Lung) and guan (Spleen) positions.

Overwork and fatigue further deplete Qi, while cold and raw foods damage the Spleen's digestive fire, leading to more Dampness and sinus congestion. Rest and warm foods conserve and support Qi, while warm compresses help move local stagnation and ease pain.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat warm, cooked foods like soups and stews; avoid cold, raw, and greasy foods that burden the Spleen. Get plenty of rest and avoid overexertion. Gentle exercise such as walking or Tai Chi can help build Qi without draining it. Use a warm compress over the sinuses to ease pressure.

### Kidney Yin Deficiency With Empty-Heat Blazing
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/kidney-yin-deficiency-with-empty-heat-blazing)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Dry, burning sinus pain, Worse in the afternoon and evening, Night sweats, Feeling of heat in palms and soles, Red cheekbones (malar flush), Tinnitus or dizziness, Lower back soreness
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Spicy or fried foods, Late nights and overwork, Emotional stress, Hot, dry weather, Alcohol and coffee
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Cool, moist foods (pears, soups), Adequate sleep and rest, Cool environments, Gentle, restorative movement
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Kidney Yin is the body's deep reserve of cooling and moisture. When it runs low-often from years of overwork, late nights, or chronic illness-it can no longer anchor the body's warming Yang energy. Unchecked, this Yang transforms into a subtle but persistent empty heat that drifts upward, drying the delicate tissues of the nose and sinuses.
This empty heat causes a chronic, low-grade sinus pain that feels dry and burning rather than full or congested. The pain tends to flare in the afternoon or evening when the body's Yin is naturally at its lowest ebb. Unlike acute sinus infections, nasal discharge is scanty or dry, and the discomfort is accompanied by classic signs of Yin deficiency: night sweats, a feeling of heat in the palms and soles, and a dry throat.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red, thin, and dry with little or no coating, possibly with cracks. Pulse: fine (xi) and rapid (shu), especially weak at the rear (Kidney) position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Kidney Yin is like the body's cooling water reserve. Late nights, overwork, and emotional stress drain this reserve, allowing empty heat to rise and dry the sinuses. Spicy foods, alcohol, and hot environments add external heat, making the burning pain worse. Rest, cooling foods, and a calm environment help replenish Yin and settle the heat, while gentle movement keeps energy flowing without overheating. By nourishing the deep reserves, the body can naturally moisten and soothe the irritated nasal passages.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat moistening, cooling foods like pears, black sesame seeds, goji berries, and bone broths. Avoid spicy, fried, or overly heating foods that can further deplete Yin. Prioritize rest and avoid staying up late, as nighttime is when Yin restores itself. Gentle practices like tai chi or restorative yoga can help balance energy without overheating. Stay hydrated with room-temperature or warm water, not iced drinks.

## คำถามที่พบบ่อย (Frequently Asked Questions)
### How does TCM treat sinus pain differently from antibiotics?
Antibiotics target bacterial infection directly, but they don't address why the sinuses became a place where bacteria could thrive in the first place. TCM looks at the terrain - the internal environment of heat, dampness, or deficiency that allowed congestion to build. By clearing that terrain with herbs and acupuncture, the sinuses can drain naturally and become less hospitable to future infections.

### Can acupuncture help drain my sinuses?
Yes, many patients feel a noticeable opening of the nasal passages during or shortly after an acupuncture session. Points on the face, like Yingxiang LI-20 and Yintang EX-HN-3, work locally to reduce inflammation and encourage drainage, while distal points on the hands and legs address the underlying pattern that is driving the congestion.

### What should I eat to help my sinuses?
In general, avoid dairy products, greasy or deep-fried foods, and excessive sugar - these tend to create Dampness and Phlegm that thicken mucus. Favour warm, cooked foods like soups and congees, and include aromatics such as ginger, garlic, onion, and radish, which help disperse mucus. Pears and peppermint tea can gently cool and soothe the respiratory tract.

### Is it safe to take Chinese herbs with my allergy medication?
In most cases, yes, but always inform both your TCM practitioner and your prescribing doctor about everything you are taking. Some herbs used for sinus pain, such as Cang Er Zi or Xin Yi Hua, are generally safe alongside antihistamines or nasal sprays, but a professional should check for any rare interactions. Never stop prescribed medication abruptly without medical advice.

### How long until I feel relief?
For an acute sinus flare-up, many people notice improvement within 2-3 days of starting herbs and having one acupuncture session. Chronic, low-grade sinus pain that has been present for months will take longer - often 2-4 weeks for a clear reduction in pressure and discharge, with full resolution depending on the underlying pattern and how consistently you follow the treatment plan.

### Can TCM prevent my sinus infections from coming back?
Yes, this is one of TCM's strengths. Once the current episode is cleared, treatment shifts to correcting the underlying imbalance - whether that's strengthening the Spleen and Lung Qi, clearing residual Damp-Heat, or nourishing Kidney Yin - so that the sinuses become naturally more resilient. Many patients find their cycle of recurrent sinusitis gradually breaks over a few months of consistent care.
