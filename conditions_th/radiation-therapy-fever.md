## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนปัจจุบัน (Conventional Medicine View)
### คำอธิบาย (Description)
Radiation therapy can cause fever as a side effect, often due to the body's inflammatory response to tissue damage, tumor lysis, or in some cases infection. The fever may be low-grade and persistent or spike higher, and it is frequently accompanied by fatigue, loss of appetite, and local symptoms depending on the treatment area - such as dry cough or chest discomfort when the chest is irradiated. Doctors typically evaluate fever during cancer treatment by checking blood counts, imaging, and cultures to rule out infection or radiation pneumonitis.

### การรักษาหลัก (Treatments)
Conventional management includes antipyretics like acetaminophen or ibuprofen to lower the fever, antibiotics if a bacterial infection is confirmed, and corticosteroids to reduce inflammation in cases of radiation pneumonitis. Supportive care such as hydration and rest is always recommended. If the fever is caused by tumor lysis, treatment focuses on managing the underlying metabolic changes.

## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนจีน (Traditional Chinese Medicine View)
### ความเข้าใจเกี่ยวกับการแพทย์แผนจีน (TCM Understanding)
In Chinese medicine, radiation therapy is understood as an external invasion of fire-heat and dryness - a fierce pathogenic factor that enters the body and directly damages tissues, especially the Lungs and Stomach. This heat toxin consumes Yin, the body's cooling and moistening essence, much like a wildfire dries out a forest. When Yin is depleted, the body loses its ability to anchor Yang, and empty heat flares upward, producing a lingering low-grade fever. This is why many patients feel worse in the afternoon or evening - the Yin energy naturally wanes at that time.
But not every fever after radiation is from Yin deficiency. If the fire-heat is particularly intense, it can lodge in the Lungs and create a true excess heat pattern, with high fever, thick yellow phlegm, and a strong thirst - the body is fighting a toxin, not just lacking fluids. In other cases, the radiation disrupts the smooth flow of Qi and Blood, especially in the chest where the treatment is often focused. This stagnation generates its own trapped heat, causing a fever accompanied by fixed, stabbing pain.
And when the heat combines with fluids that have congealed into phlegm, a sticky phlegm-heat pattern emerges, with a heavy chest and greasy tongue coating.
These four patterns - Lung Yin Deficiency, Lung Heat, Qi and Blood Stagnation, and Phlegm-Heat in the Lungs - can appear alone or in combination. A TCM practitioner reads the quality of the fever, the accompanying symptoms, and the tongue and pulse to identify which pattern is dominant and design a treatment that clears the right kind of heat while supporting the body's recovery from radiation.

### การวินิจฉัยโดยแพทย์แผนจีน (Practitioner Diagnosis)
A TCM practitioner begins by understanding the nature of the fever and the symptoms that accompany it. Radiation is viewed as a “fire‑heat” and “dryness” evil that can damage Yin fluids, but the resulting fever can take several different forms. The quality of the heat, the state of the tongue and pulse, and the presence of phlegm or pain are the first clues that point toward one pattern rather than another.
If the fever is low‑grade and worse in the afternoon or evening, with a dry cough, thirst, night sweats, and a sensation of heat in the palms and soles, this points toward Lung Yin Deficiency. The tongue is typically red with little or no coating, and the pulse is thin and rapid. This pattern reflects a lack of cooling, moistening Yin, allowing empty heat to rise.
When the fever is high and accompanied by a strong cough with thick yellow phlegm, a sore throat, and a feeling of heat in the chest, Lung Heat is more likely. The tongue is red with a yellow coating, and the pulse is rapid and forceful. Here the radiation heat toxin has directly invaded the Lungs, creating an excess heat condition rather than a deficiency.
If chest pain or a fixed stabbing sensation is prominent, along with a dark or purplish tongue that may show stasis spots, and a wiry or choppy pulse, the pattern is Qi and Blood Stagnation. Radiation can damage tissues and block the flow of Qi and blood, and this stagnation itself can generate heat. The fever may be irregular and less responsive to simple cooling measures.
When the fever comes with a heavy sensation in the chest, a productive cough with copious yellow, sticky phlegm, and a greasy yellow tongue coating, the pattern is Phlegm‑Heat in the Lungs. The pulse is often slippery and rapid. This arises when radiation‑induced dampness and heat combine to form phlegm, which obstructs the Lung’s function and creates heat.

### Lung Yin Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/lung-yin-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Afternoon low-grade fever or tidal heat, Dry cough with little or no phlegm, Night sweats, Dry throat and mouth, Hoarse or weak voice, Flushed cheeks in the afternoon
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Spicy, greasy, or fried foods, Dry, heated indoor air, Overwork and lack of sleep, Emotional stress, Smoking or alcohol
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Cool, humid environment, Rest and adequate sleep, Moistening foods like pears and congee, Deep breathing exercises
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Radiation therapy is a powerful fire-heat and dryness evil in Chinese medicine. It directly scorches the delicate Lungs, consuming their Yin and Body Fluids. When Lung Yin is depleted, the cooling, moistening function of the body fails, allowing empty heat to rise and produce a lingering low-grade fever, especially in the afternoon and evening.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red, dry, thin body with cracks, little or no coating (may appear mirror-like). Pulse: fine (thin) and rapid, weak in the right cun position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Radiation is a fire-heat and dryness evil that consumes Lung Yin. Spicy and fried foods add more internal heat, further depleting Yin and worsening the fever. Rest and cool environments help preserve the body's remaining Yin, while moistening foods directly replenish lost fluids. Deep breathing gently exercises the Lungs without exhausting them, supporting recovery.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Focus on moistening, Yin-nourishing foods like pears, lily bulb, white fungus, and honey. Avoid spicy, fried, and drying foods. Stay well hydrated and rest in a cool, calm environment. Gentle breathing exercises can support Lung function without exhausting Yin.

### Lung Heat
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/lung-heat)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: High fever with a feeling of body heat, Thirst with a strong desire for cold drinks, Cough with thick yellow phlegm, Rapid, coarse breathing and chest tightness, Restlessness and irritability
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Spicy, greasy, or fried foods, Hot, dry, or smoky environments, Overexertion and physical strain, Emotional stress
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Cool drinks and cooling foods like pear, Resting in a cool, well-ventilated room, Deep breathing exercises, Avoiding spicy and greasy food
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Radiation therapy introduces a powerful fire-heat toxin that directly invades the Lungs. This excess heat scorches the Lung system, disrupting its ability to descend Qi and causing high fever, thick yellow phlegm, thirst, and chest tightness. The heat consumes body fluids, leading to a dry throat and a red tongue with a yellow dry coating.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red body with a yellow dry coating, possibly with prickly papillae at the front. Pulse: rapid (shu), slippery (hua), or overflowing (hong), forceful at the right cun position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Spicy and greasy foods introduce additional heat and dampness, which directly aggravate the excess Heat in the Lungs. Cool drinks and moistening foods help clear that Heat and replenish the fluids it has consumed. Rest in a cool environment prevents the body from generating more internal heat, while emotional stress can cause Qi stagnation that transforms into fire, further fueling the fever. Gentle breathing helps restore the Lung's natural descending function without taxing the system.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Keep the body cool and hydrated. Drink plenty of room-temperature or slightly cool water, and sip on chrysanthemum or pear tea. Eat cooling, moistening foods like pear, watermelon, cucumber, and water chestnut. Avoid spicy, greasy, fried, and grilled foods, as well as alcohol and coffee, which add more heat. Rest in a well-ventilated, cool room and avoid smoky or overly dry environments. Gentle deep breathing can help soothe the Lungs without overexertion.

### Qi And Blood Stagnation
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/qi-and-blood-stagnation)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Fixed, stabbing chest or rib pain that worsens with pressure, Fever that comes with a feeling of oppression or tightness in the chest, Dark purple tongue with stasis spots or distended sublingual veins, Irritability, mood swings, or emotional tension, Dark purplish complexion or lips
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Emotional stress, Spicy, greasy, or fried foods, Overexertion and physical strain, Sedentary lifestyle
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Gentle movement like walking, Deep breathing and relaxation, Warm compress on the painful area, Light, bland diet
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Radiation is a powerful heat and dryness evil that damages tissues and disrupts the smooth flow of Qi and Blood. This leads to local stagnation, especially in the chest and rib area, where the tumor or treatment field is often located. Over time, the trapped Qi and Blood generate their own heat, producing a low-grade or intermittent fever that does not feel like a typical infection, but rather like a smoldering internal fire.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: dark purple body with stasis spots, distended sublingual veins, thin white coat. Pulse: wiry (xian) and choppy (se), like a taut guitar string scraping bamboo.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Qi and Blood stagnation is the root of this pattern, so anything that further blocks their flow-like stress, which knots the Liver Qi, or heavy, greasy foods that create more internal heat and sluggishness-will worsen the fever and pain. Gentle movement and relaxation do the opposite: they encourage Qi and Blood to move, which gradually clears the stasis and the heat it produces. Warmth helps dilate vessels and relax tight muscles, making it easier for blood to flow through the chest.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Keep meals light and warm-think soups and steamed vegetables-and avoid greasy, spicy, or deep-fried foods that can create more heat and stagnation. Gentle daily movement like walking or tai chi helps Qi and Blood flow smoothly; even a short stroll after meals can make a difference. Manage emotional stress with deep breathing, meditation, or talking to someone you trust, because frustration and worry directly lock up the Liver Qi and worsen the pain and fever.

### Phlegm-Heat in the Lungs
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/phlegm-heat-in-the-lungs)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Cough with thick yellow phlegm, Chest fullness and oppression, Wheezing or gurgling sounds in the throat, Restlessness and irritability, Thirst with a desire to drink
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Spicy, greasy, or fried foods, Dairy products, Hot and humid weather, Smoking or pollution, Overexertion and physical strain
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Light, bland diet, Cool fresh air, Expectorating phlegm, Rest
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Radiation therapy acts like a fierce fire-toxin that invades the body and scorches the Lungs. The intense heat disrupts the normal movement of fluids, causing them to pool and thicken into phlegm. This phlegm then combines with the lingering heat to form sticky phlegm-heat, which clogs the Lungs and traps the fire inside. The result is a persistent fever, a chest that feels heavy and full, and a cough that brings up thick yellow phlegm.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: Red body, possibly swollen at the front, with a thick yellow greasy coating; red prickles may appear on the front third. Pulse: Slippery (滑, huá) and rapid (数, shuò), strongest at the right cun (inch) position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Greasy, spicy, and dairy foods create dampness and phlegm, which combine with heat to make this pattern worse. Cooling, light foods help clear heat and resolve phlegm. Expectorating phlegm relieves the obstruction in the chest, allowing Lung Qi to descend and trapped heat to dissipate.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Avoid greasy, fried, spicy, and dairy-heavy foods, which generate Phlegm and Heat. Eat light, cooling foods like pear, radish, and water chestnut. Drink plenty of warm water to help thin phlegm. Gentle breathing exercises can support Lung function.

## คำถามที่พบบ่อย (Frequently Asked Questions)
### Can Chinese medicine really help with a fever caused by radiation?
Yes. TCM doesn't just try to lower the thermometer - it identifies whether the heat is from Yin deficiency, trapped toxins, phlegm, or stagnation and then uses specific herbs and points to address that root. Many patients find their fever drops naturally and other side effects like dry mouth, fatigue, or chest pain also improve. It works best when started early in the radiation course or as soon as the fever appears.

### Is it safe to take Chinese herbs during radiation therapy?
In most cases, yes - but it's essential that your oncologist and TCM practitioner both know what you're taking. Some herbs can influence immune function or interact with treatment, so a qualified practitioner will choose formulas that support your body without interfering with radiation. Never self-prescribe herbs during cancer treatment; always work with a professional who has experience in integrative oncology.

### How quickly can acupuncture bring down a radiation fever?
Acupuncture can sometimes provide rapid relief, especially for excess heat patterns - some patients feel cooler within hours of a session. For Yin deficiency, the effect builds more gradually over several treatments. Typically, weekly acupuncture combined with daily herbs gives the best results, with noticeable improvement in fever within the first two weeks.

### What foods should I eat to help cool the fever?
Focus on cooling, moistening foods like pear, watermelon, cucumber, and congee. Avoid spicy, greasy, or fried foods, as well as alcohol and coffee, which add heat and dry out fluids. Sip warm or room-temperature water throughout the day rather than ice-cold drinks, which can shock the system. If you have a lot of phlegm, limit dairy and sweets, which can create more phlegm.

### Will the fever come back after I stop TCM treatment?
If the underlying pattern has been fully resolved, the fever usually does not return. However, radiation damage can be deep, and some patients need maintenance herbs or occasional acupuncture for a few months after treatment ends to keep Yin strong and prevent recurrence. Your practitioner will guide you on when it's safe to taper off.

### Can TCM be used alongside antibiotics or steroids for the fever?
Yes, TCM can complement these treatments. Herbs and acupuncture can reduce the side effects of steroids and support the body's recovery while the medication handles the acute issue. Always inform both your TCM practitioner and your medical doctor so they can coordinate care and avoid any potential interactions.
