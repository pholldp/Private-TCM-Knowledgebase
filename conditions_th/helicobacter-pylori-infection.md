## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนปัจจุบัน (Conventional Medicine View)
### คำอธิบาย (Description)
Helicobacter pylori is a spiral-shaped bacterium that infects the stomach lining, where it can cause chronic inflammation (gastritis) and peptic ulcers. It is extremely common - roughly half the world's population carries it, though many people never develop symptoms. When symptoms do appear, they often include upper abdominal pain, bloating, nausea, loss of appetite, and frequent burping. Diagnosis is usually made through a breath test, stool antigen test, or biopsy during endoscopy.

### การรักษาหลัก (Treatments)
Standard treatment is eradication therapy, most often a combination of two antibiotics (such as amoxicillin and clarithromycin) plus a proton-pump inhibitor (PPI) to reduce stomach acid, sometimes with bismuth added. This is known as triple or quadruple therapy. Treatment typically lasts 10 to 14 days. However, rising antibiotic resistance has made eradication more difficult, and some patients require multiple rounds of different drug combinations.

## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนจีน (Traditional Chinese Medicine View)
### ความเข้าใจเกี่ยวกับการแพทย์แผนจีน (TCM Understanding)
TCM sees H. pylori as an external pathogen - primarily a damp-heat evil - that invades the middle burner, which is the body's digestive center governed by the Stomach and Spleen. Whether the bug can settle in and cause trouble depends on the body's internal landscape. If the Spleen is weak from poor diet, overwork, or stress, it fails to transform and transport fluids properly, leading to a buildup of dampness. That warm, stagnant environment becomes a perfect breeding ground for the damp-heat pathogen.
But damp-heat is not the only pattern. When the body's digestive fire (Spleen Yang) is too low, cold and dampness accumulate, and the infection presents as dull pain relieved by warmth, fatigue, and loose stools. Emotional stress can also play a major role: frustration and anger knot the Liver Qi, which then attacks the Stomach, causing bloating, belching, and acid reflux that flare with mood. Over time, the chronic inflammation can damage the stomach lining, leading to Yin deficiency (dryness and burning) or even blood stasis (sharp, fixed pain).
This is why two people with the same positive breath test can have completely different symptom profiles - and why TCM treatment must be tailored to the individual pattern, not just the lab result. The tongue and pulse are the key diagnostic tools that reveal which pattern is dominant. A red tongue with a thick yellow greasy coating points to damp-heat; a pale puffy tongue with teeth marks suggests Spleen Yang deficiency; a dusky tongue with purple spots indicates blood stasis.

### การวินิจฉัยโดยแพทย์แผนจีน (Practitioner Diagnosis)
A TCM practitioner sees H. pylori as an invading damp-heat pathogen, but the pattern it creates depends heavily on your body’s inner landscape. The first step is to map your digestive symptoms-what the discomfort feels like, when it strikes, and what makes it better or worse. The tongue and pulse then confirm which pattern has taken hold.
In the very common Damp-Heat in Stomach and Spleen pattern, the pathogen’s damp-heat nature is front and center. You will likely feel a heavy, full sensation in the upper abdomen, a dry and bitter taste in the mouth, and a lack of thirst despite the dryness. The tongue is typically red with a thick, yellow, greasy coating, and the pulse feels slippery and rapid.
If the body’s warming fire is low, a Spleen Yang Deficiency pattern emerges. Here the pain is dull and aching, and it feels better after eating something warm or when you press on the area. You might regurgitate clear, watery fluid and feel deeply fatigued, with cold hands and feet. The tongue looks pale and puffy with tooth marks on the sides, and the pulse is deep and weak.
Emotional stress often triggers Liver Qi Stagnation invading the Stomach. The hallmark is distension and pain that moves around, especially in the rib sides, with frequent belching and sighing. Symptoms flare up with frustration or anxiety. The tongue may appear slightly red on the edges, and the pulse has a distinctive tight, wiry quality that feels like a guitar string.
When the Spleen’s transport function is weak, dampness accumulates internally, creating Spleen Deficiency with Dampness. You will notice persistent bloating, a heavy sensation in the limbs, and loose, sticky stools. The appetite is poor, and the mouth feels sticky rather than dry. The tongue coating is thick and greasy (white or slightly yellow), and the pulse is soft and slippery.
Long-standing heat or illness can consume the stomach’s fluids, leading to Stomach Yin Deficiency. This less common pattern causes a nagging burning pain, a dry mouth and throat, and a peculiar hunger that fades quickly when you start eating. The tongue is red with very little coating, sometimes with cracks, and the pulse is thin and rapid.
Finally, chronic Qi stagnation or cold can congeal into Stomach Blood Stagnation. The pain is sharp and fixed in one spot, like a knife, and it worsens at night. You might notice dark stools or even vomiting of blood in severe cases. The tongue is dark purple with possible stasis spots, and the pulse is choppy or wiry and hesitant.

### Damp-Heat in Stomach and Spleen
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/damp-heat-in-stomach-and-spleen)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Bitter or sticky taste in the mouth, Bloating and fullness in the upper abdomen, Thick, yellow, greasy tongue coating, Loose, sticky, or incomplete bowel movements, Heavy feeling in the body and limbs, Nausea or vomiting, Thirst with little desire to drink
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Greasy, fried, or rich foods, Dairy and sugar, Alcohol, Hot, humid weather, Stress and frustration
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Light, simple meals, Bitter greens and cooling vegetables, Barley or adzuki bean porridge, Gentle movement after meals, Dry, well-ventilated environment
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  In TCM, the H. pylori bacterium is seen as a damp-heat evil that invades the middle burner. When the Stomach and Spleen are already weakened by poor diet or stress, this pathogen can settle in, creating a stew of heat and moisture that congests the digestive system. The heat rises, causing a bitter, sticky taste and bad breath, while the dampness weighs everything down, leading to bloating, nausea, and a heavy, foggy feeling. The thick, greasy coating on the tongue is a classic sign that damp-heat has taken hold in the stomach and spleen.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red body, possibly swollen with teeth marks; thick, yellow, greasy coating, especially at the centre and root. Pulse: soggy (ru) and rapid (shu), often also slippery (hua).
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Damp-heat is a combination of moisture and heat that gunks up the digestive system. Greasy, sweet, and rich foods add more dampness, while alcohol and spicy food add more heat, making the environment even more hospitable to the pathogen. Cooling, bland foods and bitter greens help drain dampness and clear heat, essentially cleaning out the middle burner. Gentle movement after meals encourages the Spleen to transform and transport, preventing the stagnation that damp-heat loves. Emotional stress can generate heat and disrupt Qi flow, so staying calm keeps the internal climate stable.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Focus on light, easily digestible foods that drain dampness and clear heat. Cook with barley (yi yi ren), adzuki beans, mung beans, and plenty of leafy greens. Avoid greasy, fried, or very sweet foods, dairy, and alcohol, as they create more dampness and heat. Eat smaller meals and stop before you feel completely full. Gentle daily movement like walking or qigong helps keep Qi and fluids moving, preventing stagnation.

### Spleen Yang Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/spleen-yang-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Dull, persistent epigastric pain, Better with warmth and pressure, Cold hands and feet, Loose stools with undigested food, Fatigue and poor appetite, Clear-fluid regurgitation
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Cold, raw foods and iced drinks, Exposure to cold weather, Overwork and fatigue, Stress and anxiety, Large, heavy meals
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warm compress on abdomen, Eating warm, cooked meals, Rest and lying down, Warm ginger tea, Gentle abdominal massage
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When Spleen Yang is weak, the digestive fire that warms and transforms food becomes too low. This allows internal cold and dampness to accumulate in the stomach, creating an environment where H. pylori can persist. The dull, persistent pain and bloating come from cold congealing Qi, while the fatigue, loose stools, and cold limbs reflect the body’s lack of warming Yang energy.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale, puffy, tender body with teeth marks, white slippery coating. Pulse: deep (沉 chén), slow (迟 chí), weak (弱 ruò), especially at the right guan position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Spleen Yang is like a pilot light that keeps digestion warm and active. Cold foods, icy drinks, and chilly environments directly douse that fire, worsening pain and loose stools. Overwork and stress drain Qi, leaving even less Yang to warm the middle. Warmth, rest, and warm, cooked foods gently feed the pilot light, helping the Spleen transform food properly and push out lingering dampness and cold.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Focus on warm, cooked, easily digestible foods like soups, stews, and congees. Avoid raw, cold, and greasy foods, as well as iced drinks, which further drain Spleen Yang. Sip warm ginger or cinnamon tea throughout the day. Keep the abdomen warm with a scarf or heat pack, and avoid overwork or strenuous exercise; gentle walking after meals helps Qi move without exhausting it.

### Liver Qi Stagnation invading the Stomach
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/liver-qi-stagnation-invading-the-stomach)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Distending pain that radiates to the rib area, Symptoms worsen with emotional stress or frustration, Frequent belching or acid reflux, Irritability, moodiness, or frequent sighing, Sensation of a lump in the throat
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Emotional stress, anger, or frustration, Irregular meal times or eating in a rush, Greasy, spicy, or fried foods, Coffee and alcohol, Prolonged mental strain without breaks
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Deep breathing and meditation, Gentle movement like walking or yoga, Warm, cooked, easily digested meals, Talking through worries or journaling
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Emotional stress tightens the Liver's Qi flow, creating a kind of internal knot that presses sideways into the Stomach. This disrupts the Stomach's natural downward movement, trapping Qi and fluids so they rebel upward as belching, acid reflux, and bloating. In the presence of H. pylori, this stagnant, tense environment weakens the gut's defenses, allowing the pathogen to thrive and trigger more inflammation.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: normal or slightly red body, often with redder sides; thin white coat. Pulse: wiry (弦 xián), like a taut guitar string, most noticeable at the left guan (Liver) position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Emotional upset directly tightens the Liver Qi, which then jams into the Stomach and creates the hallmark bloating, belching, and rib-side pain. Calming the mind and moving the body gently unknots that tension, allowing Qi to flow downward again. Irregular, rushed eating and hard-to-digest foods further burden an already struggling Stomach, while warm, simple meals give it the least amount of work to do. Avoiding stimulants like coffee and alcohol prevents adding heat to the stagnation, which would otherwise intensify acid reflux and irritability.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat at regular, calm times and avoid rushing meals. Steamed greens, mild soups, and small portions of lean protein are easier on a tight Stomach. Cut back on coffee, alcohol, and spicy or fried foods, which can add heat to already stagnant Qi. Gentle daily movement like walking, tai chi, or yoga helps keep Qi flowing and eases emotional tension. A short breathing practice before meals can shift the body out of 'stress mode' and prepare the Stomach to receive food.

### Spleen Deficiency with Dampness

### Stomach Yin Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/stomach-yin-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Dull burning pain in the upper abdomen, Dry mouth and throat, thirst for small sips, Hunger but no real desire to eat, Dry retching or hiccups, Dry stools or constipation
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Spicy, fried, or dry foods, Alcohol and coffee, Late nights and overwork, Prolonged antibiotic courses
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Cool, bland, moistening foods, Small frequent meals, Sipping warm water, Gentle rest after eating
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  H. pylori is a damp-heat pathogen that can smolder in the stomach. Over time, that heat dries up the stomach's own lubricating fluids, leaving the lining parched and vulnerable. This is what produces the gnawing, burning discomfort, dry mouth, and the odd sensation of hunger that fades as soon as you start eating.
Long-term antibiotic use, irregular eating, or chronic stress can also drain stomach yin, making it harder for the stomach to repair itself and resist the infection.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red, thin body, dry especially in the centre, with little or no coating and possible cracks. Pulse: fine (Xi) and rapid (Shu), weak at the right middle position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Stomach yin is like the cooling, lubricating fluid that protects the stomach lining. Spicy, fried, and drying foods consume those fluids directly, while alcohol and late nights generate internal heat that further evaporates yin. Moistening, bland foods and warm liquids replenish fluids and soothe the parched lining, allowing the stomach to heal and better resist the pathogen.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat small, frequent, moistening meals like congee, steamed pears, or well-cooked oatmeal. Avoid spicy, fried, and dry foods that steal fluids. Sip warm water or herbal teas throughout the day, and go easy on coffee and alcohol. A regular, unhurried meal rhythm helps the stomach rebuild its lining.

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Sharp, stabbing pain fixed in one spot, Pain worsens with pressure and after eating, Pain worse at night, Black tarry stools or vomiting dark material, Dark or purplish tongue with stasis spots
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Stress and anger, Cold, raw foods and iced drinks, Overeating or heavy, greasy meals, Lying still for long periods
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Gentle movement and stretching, Warmth on the abdomen, Rest and emotional calm
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When Qi flow in the stomach has been stuck for a long time - often from chronic stress, unresolved damp-heat, or cold - the blood in the tiny vessels of the stomach lining begins to congeal. This creates a fixed obstruction, like a rock in a stream, which causes the sharp, stabbing pain that defines this pattern. The H. pylori infection acts as a persistent irritant that deepens the stasis over time, making the pain worse at night when circulation naturally slows.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: dark, dusky, or purplish body with possible purple stasis spots; distended sublingual veins; thin white coat. Pulse: choppy (涩, sè) like a knife scraping bamboo, or wiry (弦, xián) and tight.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: <p>Blood stasis is like a traffic jam in the stomach’s tiny vessels. Cold, heavy food, and stillness make the blood congeal further, while emotional stress tightens the Qi, adding to the blockage. Gentle movement and warmth act like a mild current that loosens the stagnation, and a calm mind prevents the Qi from knotting up, allowing blood to flow more freely.</p>
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat warm, cooked, and easily digestible foods like soups and congees. Avoid cold, raw, and greasy foods that congeal blood. Gentle daily movement like walking or tai chi helps keep Qi and blood flowing smoothly. Manage emotional stress, as frustration and anger tighten the chest and worsen stasis.

## คำถามที่พบบ่อย (Frequently Asked Questions)
### Can acupuncture and herbs actually kill H. pylori?
Several Chinese herbs, such as Huang Lian (Coptis), Huang Qin (Scutellaria), and Pu Gong Ying (Dandelion), have demonstrated direct antibacterial activity against H. pylori in laboratory studies. However, TCM's strength lies less in directly eradicating the bug and more in changing the stomach environment so the bacterium cannot thrive. When used alongside antibiotics, herbal formulas can improve eradication rates and reduce side effects.

### Do I still need antibiotics if I use TCM?
For most patients, antibiotics remain the standard of care for confirmed H. pylori infection, especially if you have an ulcer or a family history of stomach cancer. TCM can be a powerful complement - it may help your body respond better to the antibiotics, ease nausea and diarrhea, and address the underlying weakness that allowed the infection. Never stop prescribed antibiotics without consulting your doctor. A TCM practitioner can work with you to time herbs and acupuncture around your pharmaceutical treatment.

### Can TCM help if I've failed multiple rounds of antibiotics?
Yes, this is one of the most common scenarios where patients turn to TCM. ### How long does TCM treatment take for H. pylori?
You will likely notice digestive symptoms improving within 2-4 weeks of starting herbs and acupuncture. For damp-heat patterns, a course of 4-6 weeks is common. For Spleen Yang Deficiency or other weak-digestion patterns, expect 8-12 weeks or more to fully rebuild digestive function. ### Will the infection come back after TCM treatment?
TCM aims to correct the underlying imbalance that made you susceptible, which reduces the risk of reinfection. Your practitioner will give you dietary and lifestyle guidance to maintain the gains. Follow-up breath tests can confirm clearance, and occasional maintenance herbal formulas may be recommended if you are at high risk of recurrence.
