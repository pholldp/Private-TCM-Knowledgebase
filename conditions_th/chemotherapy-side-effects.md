## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนปัจจุบัน (Conventional Medicine View)
### คำอธิบาย (Description)
Chemotherapy drugs work by targeting rapidly dividing cells, which includes cancer cells but also healthy cells in the bone marrow, digestive tract, and hair follicles. This leads to common side effects such as fatigue, nausea, vomiting, low blood counts (anemia, neutropenia, thrombocytopenia), mouth sores, hair loss, and peripheral neuropathy. The severity and type depend on the specific drugs, dosage, and individual patient factors. These side effects are usually managed with antiemetics, growth factors, pain medications, and supportive care.

### การรักษาหลัก (Treatments)
Conventional management includes anti-nausea medications (5-HT3 antagonists, NK1 receptor antagonists), corticosteroids, growth factors like G-CSF to boost white blood cells, erythropoietin for anemia, and platelet transfusions for severe thrombocytopenia. Mouth sores are treated with topical anesthetics and mouthwashes; peripheral neuropathy may be addressed with gabapentin or pregabalin. Despite these measures, many patients still experience significant discomfort and reduced quality of life.

## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนจีน (Traditional Chinese Medicine View)
### ความเข้าใจเกี่ยวกับการแพทย์แผนจีน (TCM Understanding)
In TCM, chemotherapy drugs are seen as a fierce "toxic heat" pathogen that invades the body, burning and depleting its fundamental substances. This assault primarily damages the Spleen and Stomach, which govern digestion and energy production, and the Kidneys, which store the body's deepest reserves of vitality. When the Spleen is weakened, it fails to transform food into Qi and Blood, leading to profound fatigue, poor appetite, and low blood counts. When the Kidneys are injured, the body's cooling Yin or warming Yang may become depleted, causing night sweats, coldness, or fluid accumulation.
The same chemotherapy regimen can trigger very different patterns depending on a person's underlying constitution. Someone who was already prone to digestive weakness may develop Phlegm-Dampness with heavy nausea and a thick tongue coating. Another person with a robust constitution might instead manifest Toxic-Heat with mouth ulcers and a red, prickly tongue. This is why two patients on the same chemo can have completely different side effect profiles, and why TCM always treats the person, not just the disease.
Often, multiple patterns coexist because chemotherapy attacks the body on many fronts at once. For instance, the deep fatigue of Qi and Blood Deficiency frequently accompanies the nausea of Phlegm-Dampness in the Middle-Burner, or the mouth sores of Toxic-Heat may appear alongside the night sweats of Empty-Heat from Yin Deficiency. A skilled TCM practitioner will identify the dominant pattern while addressing the others with a customized formula that evolves as your symptoms change through each chemotherapy cycle.

### การวินิจฉัยโดยแพทย์แผนจีน (Practitioner Diagnosis)
A TCM practitioner starts by asking about your energy level and complexion. If you feel deeply drained, look pale, and struggle with dizziness or palpitations, that points toward Qi and Blood Deficiency. The tongue is typically pale with a thin white coating, and the pulse feels fine and weak. This pattern reflects the direct depletion of your body’s foundational resources by the toxic heat of chemotherapy.
When nausea, poor appetite, and a sensation of heaviness or chest oppression dominate, the practitioner suspects Phlegm-Dampness in the Middle-Burner. The key clue is a thick, greasy tongue coating and a slippery pulse. Questions focus on digestive comfort and whether there is a feeling of mucus or fullness after eating, which signals that the Spleen’s fluid metabolism has been impaired.
If mouth ulcers, skin rashes, or a feverish feeling are prominent, the picture shifts to Toxic-Heat. The tongue appears red with a yellow coating, and the pulse is rapid. The practitioner will ask about thirst, throat pain, and any burning sensations, because these signs indicate that the fiery toxicity of the drugs has accumulated internally and is flaring upward.
Sharp or fixed pain, along with numbness or tingling in the hands and feet, suggests Qi and Blood Stagnation. The tongue may look dark or have purple spots, and the pulse can feel wiry or choppy. The practitioner inquires about the exact location and nature of the discomfort, as well as emotional tension, since stagnation often follows the physical and emotional stress of treatment.
Night sweats, hot flashes in the palms and soles, and a dry mouth that isn’t relieved by drinking water point toward Empty-Heat caused by Yin Deficiency. The tongue is red with little or no coating, and the pulse is thready and rapid. The practitioner will ask about sleep quality and any afternoon feverishness, as these subtle heat signs emerge when the body’s cooling fluids have been consumed.
When swelling in the ankles or face, profound coldness, and a deep fatigue that rest doesn’t fix are the main complaints, the diagnosis leans toward Kidney Yang Deficiency with Water overflowing. The tongue is pale and puffy, often with tooth marks, and the pulse is deep and weak. The practitioner asks about urination and lower back soreness, because the Kidney’s warming and water-controlling functions have been undermined.

## กลุ่มอาการการแพทย์แผนจีนและการรักษา (TCM Patterns and Treatment)

### Qi and Blood Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/qi-and-blood-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Persistent, deep fatigue that rest alone doesn&#x27;t fix, Pale or sallow complexion, pale lips and nail beds, Dizziness or lightheadedness, especially on standing, Heart palpitations or a fluttery feeling in the chest, Poor appetite and reduced food intake, Numbness or tingling in the hands and feet
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Overexertion and pushing through fatigue, Emotional stress and worry, Cold, raw, or greasy foods, Skipping meals or irregular eating, Lack of sleep
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Rest and adequate sleep, Warm, nourishing soups and stews, Gentle exercise like walking, Acupuncture and moxibustion
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Chemotherapy drugs act as a powerful toxic heat pathogen that directly attacks the body's fundamental substances. This assault rapidly consumes Qi, the vital force that powers every organ, and Blood, the material foundation that nourishes tissues. When Qi and Blood are severely depleted, the body cannot sustain normal function, leading to the profound fatigue, pale complexion, dizziness, and immune suppression that are the hallmark of this pattern.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale body, may be thin or show teeth marks on the edges, with a thin white coating. Pulse: fine (细 xì), weak, and may feel empty under the finger.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Rest and warm, nourishing foods directly support the Spleen's ability to transform food into Qi and Blood, which is the core strategy for recovery. Overexertion, stress, and poor sleep further drain Qi and Blood, while cold or raw foods chill the digestive fire, making it even harder for the body to extract nutrients. Gentle movement helps Qi flow without wasting it, and acupuncture with moxibustion gives a targeted boost to the organs that produce Blood and energy.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Focus on warm, easily digestible foods like congee, bone broth, stewed root vegetables, and small amounts of high-quality protein. Eat small, frequent meals to avoid overwhelming a weak digestive system. Gentle daily movement such as walking or qigong can help Qi circulate without exhausting it, while adequate sleep is non-negotiable for rebuilding Blood.

### Phlegm-Dampness in the Middle-Burner
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/phlegm-dampness-in-the-middle-burner)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Feeling of fullness and stuffiness in the upper abdomen, Nausea or vomiting, Heavy sensation in the body and limbs, Thick, white, greasy tongue coating, Loose, sticky stools, No thirst or desire to drink
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Cold or raw foods, Greasy, fried foods, Dairy products and sweets, Overeating or large meals, Damp, humid weather, Sedentary lifestyle
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warm, cooked foods (e.g., congee), Ginger tea, Small, frequent meals, Gentle walking, Avoiding dairy and sweets
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Chemotherapy drugs are a form of toxic heat that directly injures the Spleen and Stomach. When these organs are weakened, they can no longer transform and transport fluids properly. Instead, fluids accumulate and congeal into phlegm-dampness, which then stagnates in the middle burner - the digestive center. This clogging of the digestive system leads to nausea, bloating, a heavy sensation in the body, and a thick greasy coating on the tongue.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale and swollen with teeth marks, covered by a thick, white, greasy coating that is especially prominent in the center. Pulse: slippery (滑, huá) and often soggy (濡, rú), particularly in the right guan position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Phlegm-dampness arises when the Spleen is too weak to transform fluids. Cold, raw, and greasy foods further damage Spleen Yang and directly add to the dampness burden, while dairy and sweets are notorious for generating phlegm. Warm, cooked foods and ginger tea gently support the Spleen's function and help transform dampness. Gentle movement stimulates Qi flow, which is essential to break up stagnation and clear phlegm-dampness from the body.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Favor warm, cooked, and easily digestible foods like congee, soups, and steamed vegetables. Avoid cold, raw, greasy, and dairy-rich foods, as well as sweets, which directly generate phlegm and dampness. Sip ginger tea or warm water throughout the day to support Spleen Yang. Gentle daily movement like walking helps Qi circulate and transform dampness.

### Toxic-Heat
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/toxic-heat)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: High fever or burning body sensation, Red, swollen, painful mouth ulcers or sore throat, Red, hot skin eruptions (boils, abscesses), Intense thirst with desire for cold drinks, Restlessness and irritability, Dark, scanty urine and constipation
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Spicy, fried, or greasy foods, Hot environments or overexertion, Emotional stress or anger
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Cooling foods (melon, cucumber), Cold or room-temperature drinks, Rest in a cool, quiet place
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Chemotherapy drugs are seen as a powerful “fire toxin” that invades the body directly. This pathogenic heat accumulates inside, burning the fluids and tissues, which erupts as mouth ulcers, sore throat, and red, swollen skin. The heat agitates the mind, causing restlessness, and dries up fluids, leading to intense thirst, dark urine, and constipation.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red body with prickly thorns, dry yellow coating. Pulse: rapid (shu) and forceful, often full (shi) or overflowing (hong).
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Toxic-Heat is an excess fire pattern, so anything that adds heat-spicy food, hot weather, emotional agitation-will stoke the flames and worsen symptoms. Cooling foods and drinks directly counter the pathogenic heat, while rest prevents further consumption of fluids and Qi. Acupuncture points like Quchi and Xuehai specifically drain heat from the blood and skin, helping the body clear the toxin.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Focus on cooling, moistening foods such as cucumber, watermelon, pear, mung beans, and chrysanthemum tea. Avoid spicy, greasy, or fried foods that add heat. Keep the body well hydrated with plain water or herbal teas, and rest in a cool, calm environment.

### Qi And Blood Stagnation
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/qi-and-blood-stagnation)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Fixed, stabbing pain (e.g., in limbs or chest), Numbness, tingling or burning in hands and feet, Irritability, mood swings or depression, Dark purplish complexion or lips, Pain worse with pressure
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Prolonged inactivity or bed rest, Cold exposure, Emotional stress and frustration, Greasy, heavy foods
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Gentle movement (walking, stretching), Warm compress on painful areas, Emotional calm and stress relief
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  The toxic heat of chemotherapy drugs can damage the flow of Qi and Blood directly. When this flow becomes sluggish or stuck, it causes stagnation - like a traffic jam in the body's energy and circulation. This stagnation produces the hallmark fixed, stabbing pain, numbness or tingling in the limbs, and a feeling of oppression in the chest.
Because the Liver is responsible for keeping Qi moving smoothly, the emotional toll of cancer treatment often adds Liver Qi stagnation on top of Blood stasis, making the cycle of pain and tension even worse.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: dark purple body with stasis spots, distended sublingual veins. Pulse: wiry (弦 xián) and choppy (涩 sè), like a taut guitar string scraping bamboo.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Qi and Blood stagnation is like a sluggish river - movement and warmth keep it flowing, while stillness and cold cause it to freeze up. Emotional stress directly tightens the Liver Qi, which is the main driver of circulation, so calming the mind literally unkinks the flow. ### Empty-Heat caused by Yin Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/empty-heat-caused-by-yin-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Night sweats, Dry mouth and throat, worse at night, Heat in palms, soles, and chest (five-palm heat), Flushed cheekbones, Restlessness and difficulty sleeping
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Spicy or fried foods, Alcohol and coffee, Overwork and late nights, Stress and frustration, Hot, dry weather
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Cool, quiet environment, Sipping warm water, Moistening foods like pear or congee, Rest and early nights
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Chemotherapy drugs act as a fierce toxic heat that initially batters Qi and Blood. Over time, this heat penetrates deeper, scorching the body's cooling Yin fluids - especially those of the Kidneys and Stomach. When Yin is too thin to anchor Yang, an empty, restless heat rises inside, much like a kettle boiling dry. That is why the telltale signs - night sweats, hot palms and soles, a dry mouth that no amount of water quite quenches - tend to emerge later in treatment rather than at the start.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red, thin, and dry, with cracks and little to no coating. Pulse: fine (thin) and rapid, weak at the deep level, especially in the left rear (Kidney) position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Spicy, fried, and heating foods and drinks act like adding fuel to the fire - they consume precious Yin and stir up the empty heat. Stress and overwork drain the Kidneys, the body's deepest reservoir of Yin, making the internal heat worse. Cooling, moistening foods and a calm, restful environment gently replenish Yin and give the body the time it needs to rebuild its cooling capacity.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Choose cooling, moistening foods like pear, lily bulb, black sesame, tofu, and congee. Sip warm water throughout the day rather than gulping cold drinks. Avoid spicy, fried, or barbecued foods, and limit alcohol and coffee. Gentle movement such as qigong or a slow walk helps Qi flow without draining your reserves. Protect your sleep - a cool, dark, quiet bedroom is medicine itself.

### Kidney Yang Deficiency with Water overflowing
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/kidney-yang-deficiency-with-water-overflowing)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Swelling of legs and ankles, Feeling of cold, especially in the lower body, Scanty or difficult urination, Profound fatigue and heaviness in the limbs, Pale, puffy, teeth-marked tongue with a wet white coating
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Cold environments or drafts, Overexertion and prolonged standing, Salty or raw cold foods
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warmth and warm clothing, Rest and lying with legs elevated, Warm, cooked meals, Gentle movement
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Chemotherapy drugs are a fierce toxic assault that can exhaust the body's deepest reserves of warmth. In someone whose Kidney Yang was already vulnerable, this pushes the system into a state of internal cold. The Kidneys lose their ability to transform and move fluids, so water builds up in the lower body and the whole system slows down, creating the heavy, cold, swollen picture of this pattern.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale, puffy, teeth-marked body with a white slippery coating; the surface looks excessively wet. Pulse: deep (Chen), slow (Chi), and weak (Ruo), especially faint at the Chi (proximal) position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Cold directly injures the already deficient Kidney Yang, slowing water metabolism further, while warmth helps the body's internal fire move fluids. Rest conserves Yang, and elevating the legs uses gravity to assist the weakened Kidney Qi. Warm, cooked foods are easier to digest and add gentle Yang, whereas raw, cold, or salty foods burden the Spleen and encourage water retention.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Keep the body warm, especially the lower back and feet. Eat warm, cooked foods like soups, stews, and congees with ginger and cinnamon. Avoid raw, cold, and icy foods and drinks. Gentle movement like slow walking or qigong can help circulate Yang without exhausting it. Rest with the legs slightly elevated when swelling is present.

## คำถามที่พบบ่อย (Frequently Asked Questions)
### Can acupuncture really help with chemo nausea when I'm already on anti-nausea meds?
Yes. Acupuncture, especially at point PC-6 (Neiguan), can enhance the anti-nausea effect and reduce breakthrough vomiting. It is safe to combine with medications like ondansetron. Many cancer centers now offer acupuncture as a complementary therapy for this purpose.

### Will Chinese herbs interfere with my chemotherapy?
When prescribed by a qualified TCM practitioner who understands oncology, herbs are selected to support your body without interfering with chemo. However, always inform your oncologist and TCM practitioner about all treatments. Certain herbs may affect liver enzymes or blood clotting, so coordination is essential. Never self-prescribe herbal formulas during chemotherapy.

### How soon can I expect to feel less tired?
Many patients notice a lift in energy within 2-3 weeks of starting herbs and acupuncture, though deeper fatigue from Qi and Blood deficiency may take 4-6 weeks to improve steadily. Consistency is key, and the effect is often cumulative, with better energy after each cycle of treatment.

### I have mouth sores - can TCM help?
Yes. Mouth sores from the Toxic-Heat pattern respond well to cooling herbs like Jin Yin Hua (honeysuckle) and acupuncture points like LI-4 (Hegu) and LI-11 (Quchi). Topical herbal mouthwashes can also soothe pain and speed healing. Your practitioner will tailor the formula to your specific pattern.

### Is it safe to use moxibustion during chemo?
Moxibustion (warming therapy) is generally safe and can help boost white blood cell counts when applied to points like ST-36 (Zusanli). However, it should be avoided directly over areas of active tumor or infection. Always work with a trained practitioner who can assess your individual situation.

### Can TCM help with chemo-induced neuropathy?
Yes, the Qi and Blood Stagnation pattern with numbness and tingling often responds to herbs like Chuan Xiong (Sichuan lovage root) and acupuncture that promote circulation. Improvement may take several weeks to months, but many patients experience reduced severity and better function.

### Do I need to change my diet?
Generally, TCM recommends warm, cooked, easy-to-digest foods like congee, soups, and stews. Avoid raw, cold, greasy, or spicy foods that can tax digestion. Specific dietary advice will depend on your pattern, but this baseline supports Spleen function and helps you maintain strength during treatment.
