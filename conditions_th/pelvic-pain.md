## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนปัจจุบัน (Conventional Medicine View)
### คำอธิบาย (Description)
Pelvic pain refers to discomfort in the lowest part of the abdomen and pelvis, and it can be acute (sudden and severe) or chronic (lasting six months or longer). Common causes include gynecological conditions such as endometriosis, pelvic inflammatory disease (PID), ovarian cysts, and uterine fibroids, as well as non-gynecological sources like irritable bowel syndrome, interstitial cystitis, or musculoskeletal strain. Diagnosis typically involves a pelvic exam, imaging (ultrasound, MRI), and sometimes laparoscopy to visualize internal structures.

### การรักษาหลัก (Treatments)
Conventional treatment depends on the underlying cause: antibiotics for infections, hormonal therapies (birth control pills, GnRH agonists) for endometriosis or menstrual-related pain, nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs (NSAIDs) for pain relief, and surgery to remove cysts, fibroids, or adhesions when necessary. Physical therapy, nerve blocks, and counseling may also be recommended for chronic pelvic pain syndromes.

## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนจีน (Traditional Chinese Medicine View)
### ความเข้าใจเกี่ยวกับการแพทย์แผนจีน (TCM Understanding)
In TCM, the pelvis is understood as the domain of the Lower Burner, governed by the Liver, Kidneys, Spleen, and the extraordinary vessels - the Chong (Penetrating Vessel) and Ren (Conception Vessel) meridians. These channels supply Qi and Blood to the uterus, ovaries, and surrounding tissues, and any disruption to their smooth flow can result in pain. Rather than seeing pelvic pain as one disease, TCM identifies the underlying imbalance that is causing the stagnation or malnourishment - and this imbalance can be an excess (like Damp-Heat or Blood stasis) or a deficiency (like Qi and Blood deficiency or Yin deficiency).
The Liver is especially important because it stores Blood and ensures the free flow of Qi throughout the body. Emotional stress, frustration, or prolonged sitting can cause Liver Qi to stagnate, which then fails to move Blood through the pelvic vessels, leading to stabbing, fixed pain that worsens before periods. The Spleen, meanwhile, transforms fluids; when it weakens, Dampness accumulates and can combine with Heat to create a heavy, burning pain with thick yellow discharge, or with Cold to produce a cold, dragging ache with clear watery discharge.
This is why one woman's pelvic pain feels completely different from another's - and why TCM tailors treatment not to the symptom label but to the pattern. A sharp, fixed pain that worsens at night points to Blood stasis, while a dull, persistent ache with night sweats suggests Kidney and Liver Yin deficiency. Even when Western tests find no clear cause, TCM can often identify a functional pattern and provide relief by restoring balance to the organs and channels involved.

### การวินิจฉัยโดยแพทย์แผนจีน (Practitioner Diagnosis)
When the pain comes with a heavy, dragging sensation and a yellow, thick, foul-smelling vaginal discharge, a TCM practitioner leans toward Damp-Heat in the Lower Burner. The pain often flares during menstruation or after exertion, and there may be a burning feeling. The tongue is typically red with a greasy yellow coating, and the pulse feels slippery and rapid - signs of heat and dampness trapped in the pelvis.
If the pain is more distending or stabbing, fixed in one spot, and clearly linked to emotional stress, Qi and Blood Stagnation is the likely pattern. Menstrual blood may be dark with clots, and breast tenderness before periods is common. The tongue looks dark red or purplish, sometimes with stasis spots, and the pulse has a wiry quality that reflects the stuck energy.
A cold, heavy pain that improves with a hot water bottle and worsens in cold or damp weather points to Cold-Dampness in the Lower Burner. The discharge is usually white and thin, and the whole body may feel chilly. On examination, the tongue is pale with a thick white coating, and the pulse feels deep and tight, as if the cold has congealed the flow.
Some women experience a sharp, fixed pain that feels like a knife, with local tenderness and dark menstrual clots. This is Blood Stagnation, a pattern that often weaves through others but can stand alone. The tongue is dark purple with distinct stasis spots, and the pulse is choppy or wiry. A history of surgery, injury, or long-standing emotional suppression often accompanies this picture.
A dull, persistent ache that feels better with rest but never fully disappears, along with night sweats, hot flushes, and scanty periods, suggests Kidney and Liver Yin Deficiency. The pelvis is undernourished because the body’s cooling, moistening resources have run low. The tongue is red with little or no coating, and the pulse is thin and rapid - a sign of emptiness with residual heat.
When the pain is mild and lingering, more like an ache that comes and goes, and the person looks pale, feels exhausted, and has a poor appetite, Qi and Blood Deficiency is the root. This often follows childbirth, prolonged illness, or heavy menstrual loss. The tongue is pale with a thin white coat, and the pulse is thready and weak, reflecting the body’s struggle to nourish the pelvic tissues.

### Damp-Heat in the Lower Burner
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/damp-heat-in-the-lower-burner)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Heavy, burning or dragging pelvic pain, Yellow, thick, foul-smelling vaginal discharge, Pain worsens with exertion, heat, or damp weather, Feeling of heaviness in the lower abdomen and legs, Thirst with no desire to drink much
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Hot weather or overheating, Greasy, spicy, or heavy foods, Overexertion or prolonged sitting
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Cool environment, Light, bland foods, Gentle movement or massage, Adequate urination
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When Dampness and Heat combine in the lower burner - the pelvic region - they create a heavy, sticky obstruction that blocks the smooth flow of Qi and Blood. This trapped energy generates inflammation and a characteristic burning, dragging pain that often radiates to the lower back or thighs. The Heat component stirs up foul-smelling, thick yellow vaginal discharge, while the Dampness adds a sense of heaviness and makes symptoms worse in humid weather or after exertion.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red body with a yellow greasy coating, thickest at the root. Pulse: slippery (滑 hua) and rapid (数 shuo), more pronounced at the rear (Chi) position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Damp-Heat is like a steamy, stagnant swamp in the pelvis. Hot, humid weather and heavy foods add more fuel and moisture, worsening the blockage and burning pain. Cooling, drying foods and gentle movement help drain the Dampness and clear the Heat, restoring free flow to the lower burner.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Favor light, bland, cooling foods like barley, mung beans, cucumber, and celery. Steer clear of greasy, fried, sugary, or spicy foods that create more Dampness and Heat. Keep the pelvic area dry and avoid sitting on cold, damp surfaces. Gentle daily movement like walking or yoga helps keep Qi and fluids moving so they don't stagnate in the pelvis.

### Qi And Blood Stagnation
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/qi-and-blood-stagnation)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Stabbing or distending pain in a fixed location, Pain worsens with pressure, Irritability and mood swings, Dark menstrual blood with clots, Dark purple tongue with stasis spots
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Emotional stress or frustration, Prolonged sitting or inactivity, Cold foods and drinks, Premenstrual phase
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Gentle movement or massage, Warmth on the lower abdomen, Rest and relaxation
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Emotional stress, frustration, or prolonged sitting cause the Liver Qi to stagnate. When Qi cannot move freely, it fails to push Blood through the pelvic vessels, leading to Blood stasis. This trapped, stagnant Blood then produces a fixed, stabbing pain in the lower abdomen.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: dark purple body with stasis spots, distended sublingual veins, thin white coat. Pulse: wiry (弦, xián) and choppy (涩, sè).
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Emotional stress directly stagnates Liver Qi, which in turn blocks Blood flow, so calming the mind and moving the body are essential. Warmth and gentle movement dilate vessels and encourage circulation, dissolving stasis, while cold and inactivity cause further congealing of Blood. Pressure on the fixed, stabbing area aggravates the trapped Blood, so avoiding direct pressure brings relief.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat warm, cooked foods and add gentle spices like turmeric and ginger. Avoid cold, raw foods and icy drinks, which congeal Blood. Daily gentle walking or yoga helps keep Qi and Blood moving. Practice stress-reducing activities like deep breathing or journaling to prevent Liver Qi stagnation.

### Cold-Dampness in the Lower Burner
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/cold-dampness-in-the-lower-burner)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Cold sensation in the lower abdomen, Heavy dragging feeling in the pelvis, Clear, white, watery vaginal discharge without odor, Dull aching lower back pain that worsens with cold or damp, Cold limbs, especially legs and feet
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Cold weather or drafts, Damp living conditions, Cold foods and drinks, Prolonged sitting or inactivity
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warmth on the lower abdomen, Cool environment, Warm foods and drinks, Rest and relaxation, Moxibustion
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When the body is exposed to cold and damp environments, especially during menstruation or after childbirth, these Yin pathogens invade the Lower Burner. They congeal Qi and Blood, blocking the free flow of energy in the pelvis. The result is a heavy, cold, dragging pain that feels deep and persistent, as if the pelvis is weighed down and frozen.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale, enlarged body with scalloped tooth marks; thick white greasy coating, excessively moist especially at the root. Pulse: deep, slow, and soggy, particularly weak in the Chi (rear) positions.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Cold and Dampness are Yin pathogens that contract and obstruct the flow of Qi and Blood in the pelvis. Warmth and dryness counteract them by promoting circulation and dispersing the obstruction, while cold and damp environments worsen the stagnation, increasing pain and heaviness.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Avoid cold, raw foods and icy drinks. Favor warm, cooked meals with spices like ginger, cinnamon, and black pepper. Keep the lower abdomen warm with a heating pad or warm baths. Avoid damp environments and prolonged sitting in cold places.

### Blood Stagnation
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/blood-stagnation)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Fixed, stabbing pelvic pain, Pain worsens at night, Dark menstrual blood with clots, Pain that feels worse with pressure, Dark circles under the eyes or dusky complexion
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Cold weather or cold foods, Prolonged sitting or inactivity, Emotional stress or frustration, Greasy, spicy, or heavy foods
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warmth on the lower abdomen, Gentle movement or massage, Warm, cooked meals with mild spices, Rest and relaxation
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When Qi fails to move Blood properly through the pelvic vessels, Blood slows and pools, eventually forming stasis. This stuck Blood creates a fixed, stabbing sensation - like a splinter lodged deep inside - that is worse with pressure and often intensifies at night, when circulation naturally slows.
In many cases, this stasis is driven by emotional frustration, prolonged sitting, or incomplete clearing after childbirth or surgery. The stagnant Blood can also manifest as dark menstrual clots, irregular cycles, or palpable lumps such as fibroids.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: dark purple or dusky body, often with stasis spots; distended, dark sublingual veins; thin white coat. Pulse: choppy (涩 sè) like a knife scraping bamboo, or wiry (弦 xián) if Qi stagnation is also prominent.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Cold causes Blood to congeal and vessels to constrict, so warmth is a natural antidote that opens the channels and encourages flow. Gentle movement actively pumps Qi, and in TCM, Qi is the commander of Blood - when Qi moves, Blood follows. Stress and frustration, on the other hand, knot up Liver Qi, which is the engine that drives Blood circulation; when that engine stalls, stasis deepens and pain intensifies.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Avoid iced drinks, raw salads, and cold foods, which congeal Blood and worsen stasis. Instead, eat warm, cooked meals with gentle spices like turmeric, ginger, and a little black pepper. Dark leafy greens, beets, and small amounts of high-quality red meat can help nourish and move Blood. Gentle daily movement - walking, yoga, or tai chi - is essential to keep Qi flowing; avoid prolonged sitting. Applying a warm compress or hot water bottle to the lower abdomen during painful episodes can bring immediate relief.

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Dull, persistent pelvic ache, Worse with fatigue and at night, Night sweats, heat in palms and soles, Scanty menstrual flow, vaginal dryness, Insomnia, restless sleep, Sore lower back and knees, dizziness
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Overwork or exhaustion, Greasy, spicy, or heavy foods, Emotional stress or frustration, Hot weather or overheating, Excessive sweating

- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  The Kidneys and Liver share the same source of Yin, which moistens and nourishes the pelvic organs. When long-term stress, overwork, or chronic illness depletes this Yin, the pelvic tissues become undernourished, leading to a dull, persistent ache. The lack of cooling Yin also allows deficiency heat to arise, which can make the pain feel worse at night or after exertion, and is often accompanied by night sweats and scanty menstrual flow.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red, thin body with cracks, little or no coating, dry surface. Pulse: fine, rapid, and often wiry; weak at the left Chi (Kidney) position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Yin is the body's cooling, nourishing, and moistening substance. Overwork, insufficient sleep, and excessive sweating consume Yin, worsening the deficiency and the resulting dull pain and heat sensations. Spicy foods and hot environments further deplete Yin by increasing internal heat. Conversely, rest, cooling foods, and gentle movement help to preserve and replenish Yin, calming the deficiency heat and allowing the pelvic tissues to be properly nourished again.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Prioritize rest and avoid overwork or staying up late, as these deplete Yin. Eat cooling, moistening foods like pears, black sesame, goji berries, walnuts, and tofu; avoid spicy, greasy, or drying foods. Gentle exercise such as yoga or walking can help, but avoid intense workouts that cause excessive sweating. Keep the bedroom cool and dark to support restful sleep.

### Qi and Blood Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/qi-and-blood-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Dull, lingering ache rather than sharp or stabbing pain, Pain improves with rest and gentle pressure, Pain worsens after exertion or long periods of standing, Profound fatigue and lack of energy, Pale or sallow complexion, pale lips, Poor appetite and bloating
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Overwork or exhaustion, Prolonged standing, Skipping meals or eating cold raw foods, Emotional stress or frustration
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Rest and relaxation, Warmth on the lower abdomen, Warm, nourishing meals, Gentle movement or massage
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  Qi and Blood are the body's fundamental energy and nourishment. After a prolonged illness, childbirth, or chronic overwork, both can become depleted. When Qi and Blood fail to adequately nourish the pelvic organs and channels, the result is a dull, lingering ache - not the sharp pain of stagnation, but a sense of emptiness and soreness. Because the discomfort stems from a lack of nourishment rather than a blockage, it typically eases with rest and gentle pressure, and worsens when energy is further drained by exertion.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale body, may be thin or have teeth marks, thin white coating. Pulse: fine (thin) and weak.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: In Qi and Blood Deficiency, the pelvic organs lack the nourishment they need to function without discomfort. Rest and gentle warmth conserve and slowly rebuild that energy, while gentle pressure and massage encourage local Blood flow without straining the system. Overwork, prolonged standing, and stress further deplete already-scarce Qi and Blood, leaving the pelvis undernourished and more painful. Cold, raw foods challenge the Spleen's ability to produce new Qi and Blood, so they tend to worsen the condition.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Eat warm, easily digestible foods that build Blood and Qi, such as bone broths, congee with red dates and goji berries, and gently cooked dark leafy greens. Avoid raw, cold foods and iced drinks, which further weaken the Spleen. Gentle, regular movement like walking or qigong is helpful, but avoid overexertion. Prioritize rest and consistent sleep.

## คำถามที่พบบ่อย (Frequently Asked Questions)
### How does acupuncture help pelvic pain?
Acupuncture stimulates specific points along the meridians that traverse the lower abdomen and pelvis, helping to unblock stagnant Qi and Blood, clear Damp-Heat, or nourish deficient tissues - depending on your pattern. Points like Sanyinjiao (SP-6), Guanyuan (REN-4), and Taichong (LR-3) are frequently used to regulate the uterus and relieve pain. Many women feel a dull ache or warmth in the pelvic area during treatment, and relief often builds over a series of sessions.

### Can Chinese herbs really stop pelvic pain?
Yes, when the correct formula matches your pattern. For example, Damp-Heat patterns respond to cooling, drying herbs that clear infection and reduce inflammation, while Blood stasis patterns need herbs that invigorate circulation and break up stuck blood. Herbs are taken daily and work cumulatively; most women notice a gradual lessening of pain intensity and frequency over several weeks, rather than instant relief.

### Is it safe to combine TCM with my pain medication?
In most cases, yes - acupuncture is generally safe alongside conventional medications. However, some Chinese herbs that move Blood (such as Dang Gui, Chuan Xiong, or Tao Ren) can interact with anticoagulants or antiplatelet drugs, so you must inform both your TCM practitioner and your doctor about all medications you are taking. Never stop prescribed pain medication abruptly; work with your prescribing physician to adjust dosages as your pain improves.

### How long until I feel better?
Excess patterns like Damp-Heat or Qi and Blood Stagnation often show improvement within 4-8 weeks of weekly acupuncture and daily herbs. Deficiency patterns, such as Kidney and Liver Yin deficiency, may take 3-6 months because the body needs time to rebuild depleted reserves. Pain typically reduces in intensity first, then frequency; full resolution depends on the chronicity and severity of the condition.

### Do I need to change my diet?
Dietary adjustments support your treatment and help prevent recurrence. Generally, avoid cold, raw, and greasy foods that promote Dampness, as well as excessive spicy or heating foods that can aggravate Heat patterns. Focus on warm, cooked meals like soups and stews that are easy to digest. Your practitioner will give specific advice based on your pattern - for instance, someone with Cold-Dampness should avoid icy drinks and salads, while someone with Damp-Heat should limit alcohol and rich foods.

### Can TCM help if the doctors can't find a cause for my pelvic pain?
Absolutely. TCM diagnoses patterns based on your symptoms, tongue, and pulse - not on lab tests or imaging. Even when Western medicine labels the pain as 'idiopathic' or 'functional', TCM can often identify an underlying imbalance (such as Qi stagnation or Blood deficiency) and treat it effectively. Many women with chronic pelvic pain of unknown origin find significant relief with TCM.

### Is acupuncture painful in the pelvic area?
Acupuncture needles are extremely thin - much finer than injection needles - and most people feel only a slight pinch or a dull ache when they are inserted. Points on the lower abdomen are needled gently, and any sensation is usually brief. Many women find the treatment deeply relaxing and even fall asleep during the session. If you are nervous, your practitioner can use fewer needles or gentler techniques.
