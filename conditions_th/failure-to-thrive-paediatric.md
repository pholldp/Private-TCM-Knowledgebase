## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนปัจจุบัน (Conventional Medicine View)
### คำอธิบาย (Description)
Failure to thrive is a term used when a child's weight or rate of weight gain falls significantly below that of other children of the same age and sex. It is usually defined as a weight below the 3rd or 5th percentile on standard growth charts, or a drop in weight that crosses two major percentile lines.
Common causes include inadequate calorie intake, malabsorption, chronic illnesses, or a combination of these. Diagnosis typically involves reviewing growth charts, dietary intake, and sometimes blood tests or stool studies to rule out underlying medical conditions.

### การรักษาหลัก (Treatments)
Treatment usually begins with nutritional counseling to increase calorie and nutrient intake. This may involve high-calorie supplements, feeding strategies, or, in severe cases, feeding tubes. If an underlying medical condition is found (such as reflux, food allergies, or an infection), that condition is treated directly. A multidisciplinary team including a pediatrician, dietitian, and sometimes a gastroenterologist or feeding therapist often manages the child's care.

## มุมมองทางการแพทย์แผนจีน (Traditional Chinese Medicine View)
### ความเข้าใจเกี่ยวกับการแพทย์แผนจีน (TCM Understanding)
TCM understands childhood failure to thrive - known as 疳证 (gān zhèng) - primarily as a disorder of the Spleen and Stomach. These organs are responsible for transforming food into Qi and Blood, the substances that fuel growth. When the Spleen and Stomach are weak, often due to an immature digestive system, improper feeding, or prolonged illness, they cannot extract enough nourishment from meals. The child's body then lacks the raw materials to grow, leading to thinness, fatigue, and a sallow complexion.
This weakness rarely stays simple. Over time, undigested food and fluids can accumulate, creating dampness that distends the belly even as the limbs stay thin. In advanced stages, the Spleen's failure to produce Blood and Qi drains the body's deeper reserves, affecting the Liver, Kidneys, and Heart. This is why a malnourished child may also develop dry eyes, mouth sores, or puffy limbs - each complication reflects a specific organ system being starved of nourishment or overheated by deficiency fire.
Because the condition is seen as a progressive spectrum, the same Western diagnosis of failure to thrive can correspond to several TCM patterns. A child who is only slightly underweight with a picky appetite and loose stools is in the early Spleen and Stomach Qi Deficiency stage. A child with extreme emaciation, dry wrinkled skin, and a low-grade fever has advanced to Qi and Blood Deficiency. A child with recurrent mouth ulcers and a red tongue tip has Heart Fire blazing. Each pattern requires a different herbal formula and acupuncture strategy.

### การวินิจฉัยโดยแพทย์แผนจีน (Practitioner Diagnosis)
A TCM practitioner begins by observing the child’s overall build, skin tone, and energy level. The degree of weight loss and the quality of the complexion offer the first clues. A child who is only slightly underweight with a sallow but not withered face points toward the early Spleen and Stomach Qi Deficiency stage (疳气证). In contrast, a child who is extremely emaciated with dry, wrinkled skin suggests the severe Qi and Blood Deficiency stage (干疳证).
The practitioner then asks about appetite and digestion. In the early stage, appetite may be poor or the child may eat but not gain weight, with loose stools. If the abdomen is visibly distended and the child is irritable, that signals Spleen Deficiency with Dampness (疳积证), where undigested food and fluids have accumulated. The tongue coating will be greasy, and the pulse feels deep and thin.
Specific complications direct the diagnosis to deeper organ systems. If the child has dry, itchy eyes, sensitivity to light, and redness at the corners, the practitioner looks for Kidney and Liver Yin Deficiency (眼疳证). The tongue will be red with little coating, and the pulse thin and rapid. For recurrent mouth ulcers, bad breath, and a red face with irritability, the pattern is Heart Fire blazing (口疳证), often with a red tongue tip and thin yellow coating.
Finally, the practitioner checks for swelling. Puffiness around the ankles or limbs, scanty urine, and a pale, lustreless face indicate Spleen Yang Deficiency (疳肿胀证), where the body’s warming and water-moving functions have weakened. The tongue will be pale and swollen with a white coating, and the pulse deep and thin. By matching the dominant physical signs with the tongue and pulse, the practitioner pinpoints the stage and directs treatment accordingly.

### Spleen and Stomach Qi Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/spleen-and-stomach-qi-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Slight weight loss, Pale or sallow complexion, Poor appetite or picky eating, Loose stools, Abdominal bloating after eating, Fatigue and low energy, Irritability and fussiness
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Overeating or large meals, Cold, raw, or greasy foods, Irregular meal times or skipping meals, Overexertion or lack of sleep, Prolonged illness
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warm, cooked meals, Small, frequent meals, Gentle daily routine, Adequate rest and sleep
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  In children, the Spleen and Stomach are the engines that turn food into the Qi and Blood needed for growth. When these organs are weak - often from an immature digestive system, poor feeding habits, or prolonged illness - they cannot extract enough nourishment from meals. The child’s body then lacks the raw materials to build muscle and tissue, leading to a gradual failure to gain weight and grow as expected.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale, slightly puffy with tooth marks on the edges, and a thin white coating. Pulse: weak and soft, especially at the right middle position (Spleen and Stomach).
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: A weak Spleen and Stomach struggle to process large amounts of food, especially if it is cold or greasy, which further drains their limited energy. Warm, cooked foods and small meals demand less digestive fire, allowing the organs to rebuild strength gradually. Consistent rest and a calm routine conserve Qi that the body can then redirect toward growth and repair.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Offer small, frequent meals of warm, easy-to-digest foods like congee with a little cooked carrot or sweet potato, and avoid cold drinks and raw foods that chill the Spleen. A predictable daily routine with enough rest and gentle play supports the child’s digestive recovery without overtaxing their energy.

### Spleen Deficiency with Dampness
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/spleen-deficiency-with-dampness)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Marked weight loss with a bloated belly, Abdominal bloating after eating, Loose stools, Irritability and fussiness, Heaviness of the limbs and body
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Cold, raw, or greasy foods, Greasy, fried, or spicy foods, Overeating or large meals, Damp or cold living environment, Overexertion or lack of sleep
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warm, cooked meals, Small, frequent meals, Gentle abdominal massage, Rest after eating
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When the Spleen is too weak to transform food and fluids, undigested material and moisture build up inside, creating dampness. This dampness clogs the digestive tract, causing the belly to swell even as the child loses weight. The trapped dampness also makes stools loose and unformed, and the child becomes irritable because the blocked Qi cannot flow smoothly.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale, swollen body with teeth marks, white greasy coating, moist surface. Pulse: soggy (Ru) and slowed (Huan), sometimes slippery.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: A weak Spleen struggles to process food and fluids, so dampness piles up. Warm, cooked foods are much easier to transform and absorb, giving the Spleen a chance to recover. Cold and raw items, on the other hand, directly damage Spleen Yang, making dampness even worse. Overeating overwhelms the already sluggish system, while rest and gentle massage help Qi move without draining limited reserves.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Offer small, frequent meals of warm, cooked, easily digested foods like congee, steamed vegetables, and well-cooked grains. Avoid all cold, raw, greasy, or sugary foods and dairy, which create more dampness. Gentle abdominal massage in a clockwise direction after meals can help move stagnation.

### Qi and Blood Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/qi-and-blood-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Extreme weight loss and muscle wasting, Dry, wrinkled, or scaly skin, Low-grade fever or feeling of heat, Pale lips and nail beds, Dizziness and heart palpitations, Fatigue and low energy
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Overexertion or lack of sleep, Irregular meal times or skipping meals, Cold, raw, or greasy foods, Emotional upset or crying
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warm, cooked meals, Small, frequent meals, Adequate rest and sleep, Gentle warmth on the abdomen
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  This is the late, severe wasting stage of paediatric malnutrition. The Spleen and Stomach have become so weakened that they can no longer transform food into Qi and Blood. Without enough Qi and Blood to nourish the body, extreme emaciation sets in, the skin becomes dry and wrinkled, and a low-grade fever may appear as the body's Yin fluids are also severely depleted.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: pale body, may be thin or have teeth marks, with a thin white coating. Pulse: deep, thready, and weak.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Qi and Blood are rebuilt through digestion and rest, so easily absorbed, warm foods and plenty of downtime allow the body to slowly replenish what has been lost. Overexertion, irregular eating, and cold foods further drain the Spleen's already weak fire, deepening the deficiency and delaying recovery.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Serve small, frequent meals of warm, easily digestible foods such as congee with a little meat broth, steamed egg, or well-cooked root vegetables. Avoid raw, cold, greasy, or sugary foods that burden the weak digestion. Gentle rest and a calm environment are essential; avoid overstimulation and physical overexertion.

### Kidney and Liver Yin Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/kidney-and-liver-yin-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Dry, gritty sensation in the eyes, Sensitivity to light (photophobia), Redness at the inner and outer corners of the eyes, Blurred vision, especially in dim light, Night sweats, Heat in the palms, soles, and chest, Restless sleep with vivid dreams, Irritability and fussiness
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Excessive screen time or reading, Greasy, fried, or spicy foods, Hot weather or stuffy rooms, Emotional upset or crying, Overexertion or lack of sleep
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Gentle eye rest and dim lighting, Moistening foods like congee and soups, Cool, quiet environment, Gentle outdoor time in fresh air
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  In prolonged paediatric malnutrition, the Spleen’s failure to generate Qi and Blood gradually empties the reserves of Liver Blood and Kidney Yin. The Liver opens into the eyes, and Kidney essence is the root of all Yin fluids. When these deep resources are depleted, the eyes become dry, sensitive to light, and prone to redness because they are no longer being moistened and nourished. The deficiency heat that arises from empty Yin further irritates the eyes and causes night sweats and restlessness.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red, thin body with cracks, little or no coating, dry surface. Pulse: fine, rapid, and wiry; weak in the left Chi and Guan positions.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Yin deficiency means the body’s cooling, moistening resources are low, so heat easily flares up. Spicy foods, emotional upset, and eye strain all consume Yin and stir up deficiency heat, worsening eye dryness and redness. Rest, nourishing foods, and a calm environment help rebuild Yin and soothe the eyes.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Include moistening, blood-nourishing foods like goji berry congee, black sesame paste, steamed egg, and bone broth. Avoid spicy, fried, or overly dry snacks. Limit screen time and ensure the child gets plenty of restful sleep to preserve Yin.

### Heart Fire blazing
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/heart-fire-blazing)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Recurrent mouth or tongue ulcers with red edges, Irritability and fussiness, Red flushed face and sensation of heat, Thirst for cold drinks, Bad breath (halitosis), Dark yellow urine
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Greasy, fried, or spicy foods, Emotional upset or crying, Hot weather or stuffy rooms
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Cooling foods and drinks, Quiet, calm environment
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When a child's chronic malnutrition (疳证) weakens the Spleen, the body fails to generate enough nourishing Yin and Blood. This deficiency allows empty heat to build up, which can flare upward along the Heart channel to the mouth and tongue. The result is the mouth complication (口疳证) - recurrent painful ulcers, a red flushed face, and intense irritability that sets this pattern apart from other stages of failure to thrive.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: red body with a distinctly redder tip, often with red points, and a yellow dry coating. Pulse: rapid and forceful, especially at the left front (Heart) position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Heart Fire blazing is an excess heat pattern that flares upward. Spicy foods, emotional stress, and external heat all stoke that fire, worsening mouth sores and irritability. Cooling foods and a calm environment help to clear heat and settle the mind, addressing both the symptoms and the underlying imbalance.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Avoid spicy, greasy, or fried foods that add internal heat. Offer cooling, bland foods like congee, cucumber, and pear, and ensure the child stays hydrated. A calm, quiet environment and consistent bedtime routine help soothe the Heart and reduce restlessness.

### Spleen Yang Deficiency
*ลิงก์รายละเอียดกลุ่มอาการ: [หน้าหลักข้อมูลกลุ่มอาการ (ภาษาอังกฤษ)](https://www.meandqi.com/knowledge-base/patterns/spleen-yang-deficiency)*

- **สัญญาณและอาการทางคลินิก (Clinical Signs)**: Swelling or puffiness of the ankles and limbs, Cold hands and feet, dislikes being cold, Loose stools, Poor appetite or picky eating, Dull abdominal pain that feels better with warmth and gentle pressure, Scanty urination
- **สิ่งกระตุ้นให้อาการแย่ลง (Worse with)**: Cold, raw, or greasy foods, Damp or cold living environment, Overeating or large meals, Excessive fluid intake
- **สิ่งช่วยให้อาการดีขึ้น (Better with)**: Warm, cooked meals, Gentle warmth on the abdomen, Small, frequent meals
- **กลไกการเกิดอาการ (Why this happens)**:
  When a child's Spleen Yang is weak, the digestive fire that transforms food into energy and warmth dims. The body cannot properly absorb nutrients or move fluids, so water builds up under the skin, causing puffiness-especially in the ankles and limbs-while the child feels constantly cold and fails to gain weight.
- **ลักษณะลิ้นและชีพจร (Tongue & Pulse)**: Tongue: Pale, puffy, and tender with teeth marks on the edges and a white slippery coating. Pulse: Deep, slow, and weak, especially soft at the right Guan position.
- **อธิบายสิ่งกระตุ้นและการทุเลาอาการ (Triggers & Relief Explanation)**: Spleen Yang is like a cooking fire under a pot-it needs steady warmth to transform food into nourishment and move fluids out of the body. Cold foods and damp environments douse that fire, allowing water to accumulate and growth to stall. Warmth, easy-to-digest meals, and rest gently stoke the digestive flame, helping the child absorb nutrients and shed excess fluid.
- **คำแนะนำด้านอาหารและการดำเนินชีวิต (Diet & Lifestyle)**: Serve small, frequent, warm meals like congees and soups that are easy to digest. Avoid raw, cold, and sugary foods, which snuff out the digestive fire. Keep the child warmly dressed, especially the belly and feet, and use gentle abdominal massage in a clockwise direction to support movement.

## คำถามที่พบบ่อย (Frequently Asked Questions)
### Is TCM safe for babies and young children?
Yes, when prescribed by a qualified TCM pediatrician, herbal formulas and acupuncture are very safe for children. Doses are carefully adjusted for age and weight. Pediatric acupuncture often uses very fine needles with minimal retention time, or non-needle techniques like acupressure and laser acupuncture, which many children tolerate well.

### How quickly will my child start gaining weight?
Most children show improved appetite and digestion within 2-4 weeks of starting herbal treatment and dietary adjustments. Measurable weight gain typically follows over the next 4-8 weeks. The timeline depends on the severity of the pattern - early-stage Spleen weakness responds faster than advanced Qi and Blood Deficiency, which may require several months of consistent treatment.

### Can my child take herbs along with formula or medications?
Yes, herbal formulas can generally be used alongside standard nutritional supplements and medications. However, always inform both your TCM practitioner and your pediatrician about everything your child is taking. Some herbs may interact with certain drugs, so professional guidance is essential.

### What if my child refuses to drink the herbal tea?
Many children dislike the taste of herbal decoctions. TCM practitioners can offer alternatives: concentrated herbal powders that are mixed into a small amount of water or food, granules that dissolve in warm water, or even honey pills for older children. The practitioner can also suggest gentle acupressure or dietary therapy if herbs are truly impossible.

### Does acupuncture hurt for children?
Pediatric acupuncture uses extremely thin needles and quick, shallow insertion. Most children feel only a tiny prick or nothing at all. For very young or anxious children, practitioners often use non-invasive methods like acupressure, laser stimulation, or tiny press needles that stay on the skin for a few hours. The goal is always to make the experience comfortable and stress-free.

### Will the results last after treatment stops?
Yes, because TCM aims to strengthen the digestive system itself, not just provide temporary calories. Once the Spleen and Stomach are functioning well, the child can maintain healthy growth on a normal diet. Follow-up dietary guidance helps prevent relapse. Some children may need occasional tune-up treatments during growth spurts or after illnesses.
