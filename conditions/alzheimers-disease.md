## Conventional Medicine View
### Description
Alzheimer's disease is a progressive neurodegenerative disorder and the most common cause of dementia. It gradually destroys memory, thinking skills, and eventually the ability to carry out simple tasks. The hallmark changes in the brain include the buildup of amyloid plaques and tau tangles, leading to the loss of connections between nerve cells and eventual cell death.
Diagnosis is based on clinical evaluation, cognitive and memory tests, and sometimes brain imaging. While there is currently no cure, medications can temporarily slow the worsening of symptoms, and supportive care helps maintain quality of life. The disease typically advances over several years, moving from mild forgetfulness to severe impairment.

### Treatments
Conventional treatment for Alzheimer's focuses on managing symptoms. Cholinesterase inhibitors such as donepezil, rivastigmine, and galantamine are commonly prescribed for mild to moderate stages to help maintain levels of a brain chemical involved in memory. Memantine, an NMDA receptor antagonist, is used for moderate to severe stages. Antidepressants or antipsychotics may be added to manage mood and behavioral symptoms, though they carry risks, especially in the elderly. Non-drug approaches like cognitive stimulation, physical exercise, and structured routines are also recommended.

## Traditional Chinese Medicine View
### TCM Understanding
In TCM, the brain is called the “sea of marrow” (髓海, suǐ hǎi), and its clarity depends on the Kidney Essence - the body’s deepest fuel. As we age, this essence naturally declines, but when it runs low too early or too fast, the brain loses its nourishment.
This is why memory fades, thinking slows, and the mind feels empty. The Kidney Essence pattern is the most common root of Alzheimer’s in TCM, and it often brings other signs of premature aging: weak low back and knees, dizziness, tinnitus, and a pale, thin tongue.
But the brain doesn’t starve alone. The Spleen must turn food into Qi and blood, the raw materials that rise to the head. When the Spleen is weak - from poor diet, worry, or chronic illness - it fails to produce enough nourishment, and it also lets fluids stagnate into phlegm.
This heavy, turbid phlegm can literally cloud the mind, causing the foggy thinking, drooling, and thick greasy tongue coating that mark the Turbid Phlegm pattern. The person seems dull, heavy, and slow, as if their thoughts are wading through mud.
Then there are the patterns of obstruction: Blood Stagnation and Liver Fire. Years of high blood pressure, emotional stress, or a previous stroke can slow blood flow, creating tiny blockages in the brain’s vessels. This leads to fixed memory loss, a dark purple tongue, and often nighttime restlessness.
Liver Fire, on the other hand, surges upward from pent-up anger or frustration, agitating the Heart spirit and causing intense irritability, red face, bitter taste, and a rapid wiry pulse. Both patterns make the person restless and difficult to calm - a very different picture from the quiet, withdrawn deficiency types.
This is why one Western diagnosis of Alzheimer’s can look so different from person to person. TCM sees not one disease but a landscape of patterns, each with its own root. The quiet forgetfulness of Kidney Essence Deficiency, the foggy dullness of Turbid Phlegm, and the agitated outbursts of Liver Fire are not variations of the same problem - they are fundamentally different imbalances, and they require fundamentally different treatments.

### Practitioner Diagnosis
In TCM, Alzheimer's disease reflects a failure to nourish the "sea of marrow" - the brain. A practitioner starts by asking about the quality and timing of memory loss, but also dizziness, lower back pain, appetite, and emotional state. These clues help separate deficiency patterns, where the brain lacks fuel, from excess patterns, where phlegm, blood stasis, or fire cloud the mind.
When memory has faded slowly and is paired with dizziness, tinnitus, and soreness in the low back and knees, the root is likely Kidney Essence Deficiency. The tongue is pale with a thin coating, and the pulse feels deep and weak. This pattern reflects a shortage of the fundamental substance that generates marrow, leaving the brain undernourished.
If the memory loss comes with a dull, apathetic expression, poor appetite, and heavy fatigue, the picture points to Spleen and Kidney Qi Deficiency. The tongue tends to be pale and puffy with a white coating, and the pulse is soft and weak. Here the digestive energy and the kidney’s essence both struggle to produce enough Qi and blood for the brain.
A person who seems mentally foggy, drools, and has a thick, greasy tongue coating is showing signs of Turbid Phlegm blocking the orifices. The pulse may feel slippery. This pattern arises when a weak Spleen fails to transform fluids, creating a cloudy obstruction that muddles thinking rather than a simple lack of nourishment.
When cognitive decline is accompanied by hesitant speech, a dark or purplish complexion, and a tongue with purple spots, Blood Stagnation is obstructing the brain’s vessels. The pulse is often rough and thready. This can develop after long-standing Qi deficiency or chronic illness, where sluggish blood flow deprives the brain’s network of clear circulation.
If the person is unusually irritable, restless, and struggles with insomnia while showing a red tongue with a yellow coating, Liver Fire Blazing may be stirring up the mind. The pulse is wiry and rapid. This pattern often grows from pent-up emotional frustration and can coexist with an underlying deficiency, creating a mixed and agitated picture.

### Kidney Essence Deficiency
*Pattern Link: [Detailed Pattern Page](/knowledge-base/patterns/kidney-essence-deficiency)*

- **Clinical Signs & Symptoms**: Progressive memory loss and difficulty concentrating, Soreness and weakness of the lower back and knees, Dizziness, tinnitus, and gradual hearing loss, Premature graying, hair loss, and loose teeth, Frequent urination, especially at night
- **Worse with**: Overwork and lack of sleep, Excessive sexual activity, Cold foods and drinks
- **Better with**: Adequate sleep and rest, Warm, nourishing foods like bone broth, Gentle exercise, Stress reduction
- **Why this happens (Mechanism)**:
  Kidney Essence is the body's deepest foundational substance, responsible for producing marrow to fill the brain and nourish the mind. When this essence runs low-often due to aging, chronic overwork, or constitutional weakness-the brain loses its nourishment, leading to a gradual decline in memory, focus, and mental sharpness. The same deficiency also causes the classic signs of premature aging like lower back weakness, hair loss, and loose teeth, reflecting the deep-seated depletion behind cognitive decline.
- **Tongue & Pulse**: Tongue: pale, thin body with little or no coating, possible fine cracks. Pulse: fine, deep, and weak, especially at the rear positions.
- **Triggers & Relief Explanation**: <p>Kidney Essence is the body's deepest reserve, and it is easily depleted by overwork, lack of sleep, and excessive sexual activity. Rest and nourishing foods help conserve and slowly replenish this essence. Cold foods can dampen the digestive fire, making it harder to transform nutrients into the essence the brain needs. Gentle movement keeps Qi moving without draining the reserves, while stress and exhaustion directly consume the Kidney's store, accelerating cognitive decline.</p>
- **Diet & Lifestyle**: Prioritize deep, restorative sleep and avoid overwork to conserve your essence. Eat warm, nourishing foods like bone broth, black sesame, walnuts, and goji berries. Gentle daily movement such as Tai Chi or walking helps keep Qi flowing without depleting reserves.

### Spleen and Kidney Qi Deficiency
*Pattern Link: [Detailed Pattern Page](/knowledge-base/patterns/spleen-and-kidney-qi-deficiency)*

- **Clinical Signs & Symptoms**: Dull, vacant facial expression, Poor memory and slow thinking, Chronic fatigue and lack of strength, Soreness and weakness of the lower back and knees, Poor appetite with loose stools, Frequent urination, especially at night
- **Worse with**: Overwork and lack of sleep, Cold, raw, or greasy foods, Irregular eating habits, Exposure to cold and damp
- **Better with**: Adequate sleep and rest, Light, easily digested meals, Gentle exercise, Keeping the lower back warm
- **Why this happens (Mechanism)**:
  The Spleen is the source of Qi and blood, and the Kidney stores essence that produces marrow to fill the brain. When both are deficient, the raw materials for clear thinking simply run low. The brain is left undernourished, so memory fades and expression becomes dull. At the same time, the body shows the classic signs of depleted Qi: persistent fatigue, poor appetite, and a sore lower back.
- **Tongue & Pulse**: Tongue: pale and swollen with teeth marks, white moist coating. Pulse: deep, weak, and fine; especially weak at the right guan (Spleen) and both chi (Kidney) positions.
- **Triggers & Relief Explanation**: The Spleen thrives on routine and warmth; irregular meals, cold foods, and overthinking weaken its ability to produce Qi and blood. Rest and warm, cooked foods allow the Spleen to rebuild. The Kidney is drained by overwork and cold, so protecting the lower back and getting enough sleep preserves its essence. Gentle movement circulates Qi without exhausting it, while cold and damp environments further strain the already weak Spleen and Kidney.
- **Diet & Lifestyle**: Eat warm, cooked foods like soups, stews, and congees to support the Spleen. Include kidney-nourishing ingredients such as black beans, walnuts, and goji berries. Avoid cold, raw, and greasy foods that tax digestion. Gentle daily movement like walking or tai chi helps Qi flow without depleting reserves. Maintain regular meal and sleep times, and protect the lower back from cold.

### Turbid Phlegm Blocks Orifices
*Pattern Link: [Detailed Pattern Page](/knowledge-base/patterns/turbid-phlegm-blocks-orifices)*

- **Clinical Signs & Symptoms**: Mental confusion and foggy thinking, Gurgling phlegm sound in the throat, Drooling or excessive saliva, Heavy-headedness or feeling the head is wrapped, Thick white greasy tongue coating
- **Worse with**: Damp, humid weather, Greasy, heavy, or sweet foods, Sedentary lifestyle, Cold foods and drinks, Overeating
- **Better with**: Warm, dry environment, Light, easily digested meals, Gentle exercise, Avoiding dairy and sweets
- **Why this happens (Mechanism)**:
  When the Spleen is weak, it fails to transform fluids, allowing dampness to accumulate and thicken into phlegm. This turbid phlegm is heavy and sticky-it rises to the head and clouds the sensory orifices of the heart and brain, literally "muddying" clear thinking. The result is the mental fog, dull expression, and slurred speech seen in this type of Alzheimer's.
- **Tongue & Pulse**: Tongue: pale, swollen body with teeth marks, thick white greasy-slippery coat. Pulse: slippery (滑 huá), like beads rolling under the fingers, and may be wiry (弦 xián).
- **Triggers & Relief Explanation**: Phlegm is a damp, heavy pathogen that thrives on stagnation and moisture. Damp weather, greasy foods, and inactivity all encourage the Spleen to produce more phlegm, which then rises to cloud the mind. Warmth, drying foods, and movement all help the body metabolize and eliminate this turbid phlegm, clearing the sensory orifices so thinking becomes sharper and more lucid.
- **Diet & Lifestyle**: Keep meals light, warm, and easy to digest-think soups, congees, and steamed vegetables. Strictly reduce dairy, fried foods, sugar, and raw-cold foods, which all generate dampness and phlegm. Gentle daily movement like walking or tai chi helps Qi circulate and prevents phlegm from stagnating.

### Blood Stagnation
*Pattern Link: [Detailed Pattern Page](/knowledge-base/patterns/blood-stagnation)*

- **Clinical Signs & Symptoms**: Purple or dark tongue with stasis spots, Fixed stabbing head discomfort that worsens at night, Speech difficulties or slow, broken speech, Dark complexion or dark circles under eyes, Restlessness and easy fright, History of stroke or hypertension
- **Worse with**: Sedentary lifestyle, Cold weather, Cold foods and drinks, Emotional stress, Greasy, heavy, or sweet foods
- **Better with**: Gentle exercise, Warmth and warm foods, Mental stimulation (puzzles, reading), Foods that move blood (turmeric, garlic)
- **Why this happens (Mechanism)**:
  Over years of chronic illness, hypertension, or emotional strain, the smooth flow of blood can slow and congeal, forming stasis that blocks the tiny vessels (collaterals) nourishing the brain. When blood cannot reach the brain, the mind loses its anchor, leading to memory loss, slow thinking, and speech difficulties. The stabbing head discomfort that worsens at night and the dark, purple tongue are hallmarks of this stuck blood.
- **Tongue & Pulse**: Tongue: purple or dusky body with stasis spots, distended dark sublingual veins, thin white coat. Pulse: choppy (涩 sè) or wiry (弦 xián).
- **Triggers & Relief Explanation**: Blood stasis obstructs the brain's collaterals, starving the brain of nourishment. Movement and warmth encourage blood flow, while cold and inactivity cause further stagnation. Foods that invigorate blood help clear the blockages, and mental activity stimulates Qi and blood circulation in the head.
- **Diet & Lifestyle**: Engage in gentle daily exercise like walking or tai chi to promote blood circulation. Avoid cold, raw foods and excessive dairy, which can congeal blood. Include blood-nourishing foods such as dark leafy greens, black sesame, and moderate amounts of lean red meat. Keep the mind active with puzzles and social interaction.

### Liver Fire Blazing
*Pattern Link: [Detailed Pattern Page](/knowledge-base/patterns/liver-fire-blazing)*

- **Clinical Signs & Symptoms**: Intense irritability and short temper, Throbbing headache at the temples or crown, Bitter taste in the mouth and dry throat, Red, flushed face and bloodshot eyes, Loud, rushing tinnitus, Constipation with dark yellow urine
- **Worse with**: Anger and frustration, Spicy or greasy food, Alcohol, Hot weather, Overwork and lack of sleep
- **Better with**: Cooling foods and drinks, Quiet, calm environment, Stress reduction, Gentle exercise
- **Why this happens (Mechanism)**:
  Emotional frustration and long-standing anger cause Liver Qi to stagnate and then transform into Fire. This blazing Fire surges upward to the head, disturbing the Heart spirit and agitating the mind. In Alzheimer's disease, this pattern intensifies restlessness, sleeplessness, and irritable outbursts, which can worsen cognitive confusion.
- **Tongue & Pulse**: Tongue: red body with redder sides, dry yellow coating; possible red prickles on the sides or tip. Pulse: wiry (弦 xián) and rapid (数 shuò), full and forceful, especially at the left middle (Liver) position.
- **Triggers & Relief Explanation**: Anger and emotional stress fuel Liver Fire, directly aggravating the pattern. Spicy, greasy foods and alcohol generate internal Heat, while hot environments add external Heat. Cooling foods and a calm environment help clear Fire and soothe the Liver, allowing the Heart spirit to settle.
- **Diet & Lifestyle**: Avoid spicy, greasy, and fried foods, alcohol, and coffee, which add heat. Favor cooling, bitter foods like celery, chrysanthemum tea, and mung beans. Practice gentle daily exercise such as tai chi or walking, and prioritize stress management to prevent emotional flare-ups.

## Frequently Asked Questions
### Can acupuncture really help with memory loss?
Acupuncture alone is not a cure for Alzheimer’s, but it can be a valuable part of a comprehensive TCM treatment plan. By stimulating specific points on the head and body, acupuncture aims to improve circulation to the brain, calm the spirit, and support the underlying organ systems - especially the Kidneys and Spleen. Many families report that regular sessions help their loved one feel more alert, less anxious, and better rested, which can indirectly support cognitive function.

### How long until we see results?
Every person responds differently, but most families notice small changes within the first 4-8 weeks: perhaps better sleep, a brighter mood, or more engagement in conversation. Deeper improvements in memory and thinking take longer, often 6-12 months of consistent herbs and acupuncture. This is a gradual process, and the goal is to stabilize and support rather than to reverse years of decline quickly.

### Is TCM safe to combine with Alzheimer’s medications?
In general, yes, TCM can be used alongside conventional Alzheimer’s drugs like donepezil or memantine. However, certain herbs may have mild blood-thinning effects or sedative properties, so it’s crucial that both your TCM practitioner and your prescribing doctor know the full list of everything you’re taking. Never stop or adjust a prescribed medication without medical supervision. With good communication, the two approaches can work together safely.

### Which TCM pattern is most common in Alzheimer’s?
Kidney Essence Deficiency is the most common root pattern because Alzheimer’s is so closely linked to aging and the natural decline of the body’s deepest reserves. However, it’s rare to see a pure, single pattern - most people have a mix. For example, a person may have an underlying Kidney deficiency that then allows phlegm to accumulate, or long-standing blood stasis from hypertension that compounds the problem. A TCM practitioner will identify the dominant pattern and any others that need attention.

### Can herbs reverse dementia?
Herbs cannot reverse the brain changes of advanced Alzheimer’s, but they can make a meaningful difference in quality of life. TCM herbal formulas aim to nourish the brain, clear obstructions, and balance the body’s systems. In some cases, this leads to improvements in alertness, mood stability, and even memory. The earlier treatment begins, the better the potential outcome, but even in later stages, herbs can help manage agitation, sleep, and appetite.

### What can I do at home to support a loved one with Alzheimer’s?
Diet is a powerful tool. Favor warm, cooked foods that are easy to digest, like soups, congees, and stews. Include brain-nourishing ingredients such as walnuts, black sesame seeds, eggs, and bone broth. Avoid cold, raw, and greasy foods, as well as dairy and sugar, which can encourage phlegm. Gentle daily movement like walking or tai chi, a calm and predictable routine, and mental stimulation through puzzles, music, or storytelling all support the TCM approach. Always work with a practitioner to tailor these suggestions to your loved one’s specific pattern.

## Modern Scientific Research
1. **A double‑blind RCT of Huannao Yicong Formula for mild‑to‑moderate Alzheimer's disease**
   *Journal/Publication: Details from a clinical trial cited in Frontiers in Pharmacology (2025); original publication not independently verified.*
   This randomized, double‑blind, placebo‑controlled trial evaluated a formula containing Panax ginseng, Polygala tenuifolia, and Acorus calamus. Patients receiving the formula showed significant improvements in ADAS‑Cog, MMSE, and MoCA scores compared to placebo, supporting its use for cognitive enhancement in Alzheimer's.

2. **Acupuncture for Alzheimer's disease: a systematic review and meta‑analysis**
   *Journal/Publication: Zhou J, Peng W, Xu M, et al. The effectiveness of acupuncture for Alzheimer's disease: a systematic review and meta‑analysis. J Alzheimers Dis. 2019;68(2):555‑570.*
   A meta‑analysis of randomized controlled trials found that acupuncture, alone or combined with medication, improved cognitive function (MMSE) and activities of daily living in Alzheimer's patients. The effect was moderate, and the safety profile was favorable.

3. **The Mechanisms of Bushen‑Yizhi Formula as a Therapeutic Agent against Alzheimer’s Disease** ([Link](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-018-21468-w))
   *Journal/Publication: Zhang Y, et al. The Mechanisms of Bushen‑Yizhi Formula as a Therapeutic Agent against Alzheimer’s Disease. Sci Rep. 2018;8:3104.*
   This preclinical study demonstrated that Bushen‑Yizhi Formula (containing Cnidii Fructus, Panax ginseng, Polygonum multiflorum, etc.) ameliorated scopolamine‑induced and age‑related cognitive deficits in mice by modulating cholinergic function, oxidative stress, and SIRT1/ER‑stress pathways, providing a mechanistic basis for its use in Alzheimer's.
