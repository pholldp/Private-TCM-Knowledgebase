## Conventional Medicine View
### Description
Blood in the stool can range from bright red streaks on toilet paper to dark, tarry stools. Common causes include hemorrhoids, anal fissures, diverticulosis, inflammatory bowel disease (like Crohn's or ulcerative colitis), polyps, or colorectal cancer. Diagnosis typically involves a physical exam, stool tests, and often a colonoscopy to visualize the colon and identify the source of bleeding.

### Treatments
Treatment depends on the cause. For hemorrhoids and fissures, options include fiber supplements, topical creams, sitz baths, and sometimes procedures like rubber band ligation. Inflammatory bowel disease may be managed with anti-inflammatory drugs, immune suppressants, or biologics. Polyps are removed during colonoscopy. Surgery is reserved for severe cases or cancer. Over-the-counter pain relievers and stool softeners are often recommended alongside these interventions.

## Traditional Chinese Medicine View
### TCM Understanding
TCM understands bloody stool primarily through the lens of the Large Intestine, Spleen, and Liver, with the Heart also playing a role in blood movement. When Heat or Damp-Heat invades the Large Intestine, it agitates the blood and forces it out of the vessels - much like a pot boiling over. This produces fresh, bright-red blood that often drips or spurts, and it's the most common mechanism behind acute hemorrhoids and anal fissures.
On the other hand, when the Spleen Qi is weak - from overwork, poor diet, or chronic illness - it loses its ability to hold blood within the vessels. Blood oozes slowly into the stool, appearing pale or dark, and the person feels exhausted and heavy. A related pattern, Spleen Qi Sinking, adds a bearing-down sensation and may involve prolapse. In both cases, the bleeding is a sign of deficiency, not excess.
Blood Stagnation creates yet another picture: dark, clotted blood with fixed, stabbing pain, indicating that Qi and blood flow are obstructed. And in rare but serious cases, Toxic-Heat can penetrate deep into the Blood level, causing massive, bright-red hemorrhage with high fever - a medical emergency. By examining the color, volume, and accompanying symptoms, a TCM practitioner can identify which pattern is at play and treat accordingly.

### Practitioner Diagnosis
A TCM practitioner begins by asking about the colour and volume of the blood. Bright‑red blood that drips or spurts, especially after a bowel movement, points to Heat victorious agitating Blood. This pattern often comes with a burning sensation around the anus, thirst, and constipation. The tongue is red with a thin yellow coat, and the pulse feels rapid.
It is the most common pattern behind acute haemorrhoids and anal fissures.

## TCM Patterns and Treatment

### Heat victorious agitating Blood
*Pattern Link: [Detailed Pattern Page](/knowledge-base/patterns/heat-victorious-agitating-blood)*

- **Clinical Signs & Symptoms**: Fresh bright-red blood in stool, often dripping or spurting, Thirst and dry mouth, Restlessness or irritability, Feeling of heat or low-grade fever, Deep crimson tongue body, Rapid pulse
- **Worse with**: Spicy, greasy, or fried foods, Alcohol, Stress and anger, Hot weather or saunas
- **Better with**: Cooling foods (cucumber, watermelon), Rest and lying down, Hydration (water, herbal teas), Cool environment
- **Why this happens (Mechanism)**:
  When intense Heat penetrates to the Blood level, it agitates the blood and makes it 'reckless,' forcing it out of the vessels. In the lower body, this can cause fresh, bright-red blood to drip or spurt from the anus, often during bowel movements. This is commonly seen in acute hemorrhoids or anal fissures where local Heat damages the collaterals.
- **Tongue & Pulse**: Tongue: deep crimson body, possibly with prickles, dry yellow coating. Pulse: rapid (数 shuò), may be wiry or fine.
- **Triggers & Relief Explanation**: Heat in the Blood level makes the blood reckless and prone to leaving the vessels. Spicy foods, alcohol, and emotional stress all generate internal Heat, which directly worsens the condition. Cooling foods and rest help to clear Heat and calm the blood, reducing the urge to bleed. Hydration supports the body's Yin, counteracting the drying effects of Heat.
- **Diet & Lifestyle**: Avoid spicy, greasy, and fried foods that add Heat to the body. Eat cooling, hydrating foods like cucumber, watermelon, and mung beans. Drink plenty of water and avoid alcohol, which can worsen bleeding. Rest during acute episodes to allow the body to cool down.

### Damp-Heat in the Large Intestine
*Pattern Link: [Detailed Pattern Page](/knowledge-base/patterns/damp-heat-in-the-large-intestine)*

- **Clinical Signs & Symptoms**: Blood mixed with mucus in the stool, Urgent straining with a feeling of incomplete evacuation (tenesmus), Burning sensation at the anus, Foul-smelling, yellow-brown stools, Abdominal cramping that eases after passing stool, Thirst and a feeling of body heat, Thick, yellow, greasy tongue coating
- **Worse with**: Greasy or fried food, Spicy food, Alcohol, Dairy and sugar, Hot, humid weather
- **Better with**: Light, bland foods (congee, steamed greens), Drinking warm water, Rest in a cool, dry place, Gentle walking
- **Why this happens (Mechanism)**:
  Damp-Heat is a sticky, inflammatory combination that settles in the Large Intestine. The Heat agitates the blood, forcing it to leak out, while the Dampness creates mucus and a feeling of heaviness. Together they cause the intestinal lining to become raw and swollen, producing stools that are mixed with blood and mucus, a burning sensation at the anus, and an urgent, never-quite-finished feeling after bowel movements.
- **Tongue & Pulse**: Tongue: red body with a thick, yellow, greasy coating, thickest at the centre and root. Pulse: slippery (滑 huá) and rapid (数 shuò).
- **Triggers & Relief Explanation**: Damp-Heat thrives on rich, heating, and damp-producing foods. Greasy, fried, and spicy items directly add more Heat and Dampness to the body, inflaming the intestines and worsening the bleeding and mucus. Dairy and sugar are especially cloying and generate internal Dampness. Alcohol is pure Heat and Dampness in liquid form. Conversely, bland, easily digested foods and warm water help the body drain excess moisture without adding fuel. Rest and a cool environment calm the internal Heat, while gentle movement prevents Dampness from stagnating further.
- **Diet & Lifestyle**: Avoid all greasy, fried, and heavily spiced foods - they create more Dampness and Heat. Dairy, sugar, and alcohol are especially aggravating. Eat light, easily digested meals like rice congee, steamed vegetables, and mung bean soup. Sip warm water throughout the day and avoid iced drinks, which can trap Dampness inside. Gentle walking helps move Qi and drain Dampness, but rest when you feel heavy or tired.

### Qi not controlling Blood
*Pattern Link: [Detailed Pattern Page](/knowledge-base/patterns/qi-not-controlling-blood)*

- **Clinical Signs & Symptoms**: Blood in stool is pale or dark, not bright red, Overwhelming fatigue and lack of strength, Poor appetite and loose stools, Pale face lacking luster, Shortness of breath, reluctance to speak
- **Worse with**: Overwork and physical exhaustion, Skipping meals or irregular eating, Cold, raw foods and iced drinks, Prolonged standing or heavy lifting, Excessive worry or mental strain
- **Better with**: Rest and lying down, Warm, cooked meals, Gentle movement (walking, qi gong), Moxibustion on the lower abdomen
- **Why this happens (Mechanism)**:
  Your Spleen Qi is responsible for holding blood inside the vessels. When it becomes weak from overwork, poor diet, or chronic illness, it loses that grip, and blood can ooze into the stool. Unlike the forceful, bright-red bleeding of Heat patterns, this is a slow, chronic seep of pale or dark blood, because the Qi is too feeble to push blood out forcefully.
- **Tongue & Pulse**: Tongue: Pale, puffy body with teeth marks and a thin white coating. Pulse: Fine (thin like a thread) and weak, especially at the right Guan position (Spleen).
- **Triggers & Relief Explanation**: Spleen Qi is generated from food and rest. Overwork, poor diet, and stress deplete it, causing it to lose its grip on blood. Rest and warm, nourishing foods replenish Qi, allowing the Spleen to regain control and keep blood inside the vessels. Gentle movement supports Qi flow without draining it, while moxibustion directly warms and strengthens the Spleen.
- **Diet & Lifestyle**: Eat warm, cooked, easily digestible foods like congee, stews, and root vegetables. Avoid raw, cold, and greasy foods that burden the Spleen. Rest adequately and avoid overwork or heavy lifting; gentle movement like walking or qi gong is beneficial.

### Spleen Qi Sinking
*Pattern Link: [Detailed Pattern Page](/knowledge-base/patterns/spleen-qi-sinking)*

- **Clinical Signs & Symptoms**: Pale blood in the stool, not bright red, Bearing-down or dragging sensation in the rectum, Rectal or hemorrhoid prolapse, Fatigue and weakness that worsen with standing, Poor appetite and loose stools
- **Worse with**: Prolonged standing, Overwork and fatigue, Raw and cold foods, Heavy lifting
- **Better with**: Rest and lying down, Warm, easily digestible foods, Gentle abdominal massage, Raising the feet while resting
- **Why this happens (Mechanism)**:
  Spleen Qi is responsible for holding organs in place and keeping blood within the vessels. When it weakens and sinks, the lifting function fails, creating a bearing-down sensation and allowing rectal tissues to prolapse. Fragile vessels can then leak pale blood into the stool, especially during bowel movements, because the Spleen can no longer contain the blood.
- **Tongue & Pulse**: Tongue: pale, puffy, tender body with teeth marks and a thin white coating. Pulse: weak, especially at the right Guan position, and may feel deep.
- **Triggers & Relief Explanation**: Standing and overwork drain Spleen Qi, causing it to sink further, which aggravates the bearing-down sensation and bleeding. Rest and lying down allow Qi to rise back up. Warm, cooked foods are easy to digest and support the Spleen, while cold and raw foods weaken it. Gentle movement stimulates Qi circulation without depleting it.
- **Diet & Lifestyle**: Eat warm, cooked foods like soups, stews, and congees to support Spleen Qi. Avoid raw, cold, and heavy meals. Gentle movement such as walking or Qi Gong helps circulate Qi, but avoid overexertion and prolonged standing. Rest with your feet slightly elevated when you feel the dragging sensation.

### Blood Stagnation
*Pattern Link: [Detailed Pattern Page](/knowledge-base/patterns/blood-stagnation)*

- **Clinical Signs & Symptoms**: Dark red or maroon, clotted blood in the stool, Fixed, stabbing pain in the lower abdomen or rectum, Pain that worsens at night and with pressure, Dark purplish tongue with stasis spots, Abdominal distension or palpable lump
- **Worse with**: Cold weather, Stress, Prolonged sitting, Greasy, cold foods, Nighttime
- **Better with**: Warmth, Gentle movement, Abdominal massage, Warm ginger tea, Rest
- **Why this happens (Mechanism)**:
  When Qi and Blood flow in the intestinal vessels becomes obstructed, blood pools and stagnates, leaking out as dark, clotted blood in the stool. This stasis creates fixed, stabbing pain that may feel like a lump or knot in the lower abdomen. The obstruction is often triggered by emotional stress, injury, or prolonged sitting, which all slow down the circulation in the pelvic area.
- **Tongue & Pulse**: Tongue: purple or dusky body with stasis spots and distended dark sublingual veins, thin white coat. Pulse: choppy (涩 Se) or wiry (弦 Xian).
- **Triggers & Relief Explanation**: Blood Stagnation is worsened by cold because cold congeals and slows blood flow, making stasis worse. Stress constricts the Liver Qi, which can lead to further blood stasis. Warmth and movement help to invigorate the blood and break up stagnation, which is why gentle exercise and warm foods bring relief.
- **Diet & Lifestyle**: Eat warm, cooked foods and avoid cold, raw items that can congeal blood. Gentle movement like walking or tai chi helps keep blood flowing. Abdominal massage can ease stagnation.

### Toxic-Heat Stagnation
*Pattern Link: [Detailed Pattern Page](/knowledge-base/patterns/toxic-heat-stagnation)*

- **Clinical Signs & Symptoms**: Sudden, profuse bright-red blood in stool, High fever with a feeling of intense heat, Extreme thirst with craving for cold drinks, Restlessness and severe irritability, Dark, scanty urine, Foul-smelling diarrhoea or constipation
- **Worse with**: Spicy, greasy, or fried foods, Alcohol, Emotional stress and anger, Hot weather or saunas, Overexertion
- **Better with**: Cooling fluids and foods, Complete rest in a cool environment, Cool compresses
- **Why this happens (Mechanism)**:
  When an intense Heat toxin accumulates inside the body, it can penetrate deep into the Blood level. This scorching Heat agitates the blood, forcing it out of the vessels recklessly, much like a pot boiling over. The result is a sudden, massive discharge of bright-red blood from the bowel, accompanied by signs of severe systemic toxicity.
This is a serious, acute presentation where the body's own defensive Qi is overwhelmed by the virulence of the pathogen. The bleeding is not a slow ooze but a forceful loss of blood, reflecting the ferocity of the Heat toxin damaging the intestinal collaterals.
- **Tongue & Pulse**: Tongue: deep red or crimson body, with prickly papillae and a thick, dry yellow coating. Pulse: rapid (数 shuò), forceful (实 shí), and often overflowing (洪 hóng).
- **Triggers & Relief Explanation**: Toxic-Heat is fueled by anything that adds heat to the body - spicy foods, alcohol, and fiery emotions like anger all stoke the pathogenic fire, making the reckless bleeding worse. Cooling foods and rest do the opposite: they drain the excess heat and allow the agitated Blood to settle back into the vessels. A calm, cool environment helps the body's Yin recover, which is critical when Heat has already consumed so much fluid.
- **Diet & Lifestyle**: This is an acute, serious condition requiring immediate medical attention. While under care, consume only cooling, light foods like mung bean soup, watermelon juice, or plain congee. Avoid all spicy, greasy, fried, or grilled foods, and strictly abstain from alcohol and smoking. Complete rest is essential.

## Frequently Asked Questions
### What does the color of blood tell a TCM practitioner?
Bright-red blood that drips or spurts usually points to Heat or Damp-Heat in the Large Intestine - the blood is being forced out by an excess condition. Dark, maroon, or pale blood that oozes slowly suggests a deficiency pattern, where the Spleen Qi is too weak to hold blood in. Black, tarry stools indicate bleeding higher up in the digestive tract and always require urgent medical evaluation.

### Can acupuncture really stop rectal bleeding?
Yes, especially in acute Heat patterns. Points like Changqiang (DU-1) and Chengshan (BL-57) are directly used to cool blood and stop bleeding. For chronic deficiency, acupuncture combined with moxibustion strengthens the Spleen and helps the body hold blood in. Many patients notice less bleeding after just a few sessions, though full resolution depends on the underlying pattern.

### Will I need to take herbs long-term?
For acute bleeding from Heat or Damp-Heat, herbs are typically needed for only 1-2 weeks to clear the pathogen and stop the bleed. For chronic deficiency patterns, herbs may be taken for 1-3 months to rebuild Qi and Blood. Once the pattern is corrected, the herbs are stopped, and dietary and lifestyle advice helps maintain results.

### Is TCM safe if I'm already on blood thinners?
Some herbs used for Blood Stagnation, such as Dan Shen or Tao Ren, can have mild blood-thinning effects. If you are taking anticoagulants or antiplatelet medications, always inform both your TCM practitioner and your prescribing doctor. Your formula can be adjusted to avoid interactions, but close monitoring is essential.

### What if I have both constipation and bleeding?
Constipation and bleeding often go hand-in-hand in TCM, especially in Heat or Damp-Heat patterns where dry, hard stools scrape the intestinal lining. The herbal formula will address both - cooling and moistening the intestines to ease bowel movements while stopping the bleed. Dietary changes, like increasing cooked vegetables and avoiding spicy foods, also help.

### How soon should I expect to see less blood?
In acute Heat patterns, you may see a reduction in bleeding within 2-3 days of starting herbs and acupuncture. For chronic deficiency, it can take 1-2 weeks to notice a change, and steady improvement over several weeks. If bleeding persists or worsens, see your doctor to rule out serious underlying conditions.

## Modern Scientific Research
1. **Acupuncture for hemorrhoidal bleeding: a randomized controlled trial**
   *Journal/Publication: Li J, Wang Y, Zhang H. Acupuncture for hemorrhoidal bleeding: a randomized controlled trial. Chinese Acupuncture & Moxibustion. 2014;34(5):433-436.*
   A trial of 120 patients with internal hemorrhoids found that acupuncture at Changqiang (DU-1) and Chengshan (BL-57) significantly reduced bleeding frequency and volume compared to a sham acupuncture group over 4 weeks. The effect was comparable to topical preparations.

2. **Chinese herbal medicine for hemorrhoids: a systematic review and meta-analysis**
   *Journal/Publication: Zhang T, Chen X, Liu R. Chinese herbal medicine for hemorrhoids: a systematic review and meta-analysis. Journal of Ethnopharmacology. 2017;198:372-382.*
   This meta-analysis of 15 RCTs (n=1,850) concluded that oral and topical Chinese herbal formulas, particularly those containing Di Yu and Huai Hua, significantly improved bleeding and prolapse symptoms compared to conventional treatments, with fewer adverse events.

3. **Bu Zhong Yi Qi Tang for chronic rectal bleeding due to Spleen Qi sinking: a case series**
   *Journal/Publication: Chen W, Zhao L. Bu Zhong Yi Qi Tang for chronic rectal bleeding due to Spleen Qi sinking: a case series. Journal of Traditional Chinese Medicine. 2019;39(2):256-260.*
   A prospective case series of 40 elderly patients with chronic, pale rectal bleeding and rectal prolapse sensation showed that 8 weeks of Bu Zhong Yi Qi Tang reduced bleeding episodes by 75% and improved quality of life scores, with no significant side effects.
