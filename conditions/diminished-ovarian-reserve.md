## Conventional Medicine View
### Description
Diminished ovarian reserve (DOR) refers to a reduction in the quantity and quality of a woman's eggs, often associated with aging but can occur prematurely. It is diagnosed through blood tests showing elevated follicle-stimulating hormone (FSH) and low anti-Müllerian hormone (AMH), along with a low antral follicle count on ultrasound. Women with DOR may experience irregular or shorter menstrual cycles, difficulty conceiving, and sometimes symptoms of perimenopause such as hot flashes or night sweats.

### Treatments
Conventional management focuses on fertility preservation and assisted reproduction. For women trying to conceive, treatments include ovarian stimulation with medications like clomiphene or gonadotropins, often combined with intrauterine insemination (IUI) or in vitro fertilization (IVF). When ovarian response is poor, donor eggs may be recommended. Hormone replacement therapy (HRT) can manage perimenopausal symptoms in women not seeking pregnancy. Supplements like DHEA and CoQ10 are sometimes used, though evidence is mixed.

## Traditional Chinese Medicine View
### TCM Understanding
In TCM, the ovaries are not an isolated organ; they are an outward expression of the Kidney system's deep reservoir of Essence (Jing). This Essence is the blueprint for growth, development, and reproduction, and it fuels Tian Gui - the reproductive vitality that governs egg quantity and quality.
When Kidney Essence becomes depleted, whether from constitutional weakness, overwork, or aging, Tian Gui wanes, and ovarian reserve diminishes. This is why most women with DOR will show signs of Kidney deficiency like lower back soreness, weak knees, and premature graying.
But the Kidneys don't work alone. The Liver stores Blood and ensures its smooth flow to the uterus and ovaries through the Chong and Penetrating vessels. Emotional stress and frustration can stagnate Liver Qi, obstructing this flow and starving the ovaries of nourishment.
The Spleen transforms food into Qi and Blood, and when it is weak, the raw materials for egg development run low. The Heart, which governs blood and houses the spirit, must also communicate with the Kidneys to maintain hormonal balance; when this connection is broken, sleep and emotions become disturbed, further taxing ovarian function.
This is why diminished ovarian reserve is never just 'low egg count.' Depending on which organ systems are most affected, a woman may experience hot flashes and night sweats (Yin deficiency), cold extremities and fatigue (Yang deficiency), irritability and breast tenderness (Liver Qi stagnation), or dark clotted periods with pelvic pain (Blood stasis). Each pattern requires a different treatment strategy, even though the Western diagnosis is the same.

### Practitioner Diagnosis
A TCM practitioner begins by listening carefully to your menstrual story - when your cycle changed, the color and volume of flow, and any pain or clots. They then ask about your whole-body experience: energy, sleep, temperature comfort, emotions, and any digestive or urinary signs. These clues, together with a look at your tongue and a feel of your pulse, help them trace the pattern beneath the diminished ovarian reserve.
When the root is Kidney Essence Deficiency (肾精亏虚, shèn jīng kuī xū), periods become very light or stop early, and the woman often feels deep fatigue, weak legs, and a foggy memory. The tongue looks pale with little or no coating, and the pulse feels deep and thready. This is the core emptiness that underlies most cases, and a practitioner spots it through the combination of early amenorrhea, lower back soreness, and that weak, deep pulse.
If Kidney and Liver Yin Deficiency (肝肾阴虚, gān shèn yīn xū) is dominant, heat signs appear alongside the deficiency. The woman may have scanty but bright‑red periods, hot flashes, night sweats, a dry vagina, and a restless, irritable feeling. Her tongue is red with little or no coating, and her pulse is thready and rapid. The practitioner differentiates this from simple Essence Deficiency by the presence of heat and dryness.
Liver Qi Stagnation (肝气郁结, gān qì yù jié) shows up as irregular cycles that shift with mood - premenstrual breast fullness, sighing, and a sense of frustration are typical. The tongue may look normal or have slightly red edges, and the pulse feels wiry. A practitioner will ask about life stress and emotional patterns, because this stagnation often rides on top of a Kidney deficiency, disrupting the smooth flow that the ovaries need.

## TCM Patterns and Treatment

### Kidney Essence Deficiency
*Pattern Link: [Detailed Pattern Page](/knowledge-base/patterns/kidney-essence-deficiency)*

- **Clinical Signs & Symptoms**: Scanty, delayed, or absent periods, Soreness and weakness of the lower back and knees, Premature greying or hair loss, Poor memory and difficulty concentrating, Dizziness and vertigo, Frequent urination, especially at night
- **Worse with**: Overwork and chronic fatigue, Excessive sexual activity, Cold, raw foods and iced drinks, Late nights and insufficient sleep, Chronic stress and anxiety
- **Better with**: Adequate deep sleep, Warm, nourishing foods (broths, stews), Gentle exercise (Qi Gong, walking), Keeping warm, especially the lower back
- **Why this happens (Mechanism)**:
  In TCM, the Kidneys store Jing (Essence), which is the deep constitutional substance that fuels growth, reproduction, and aging. This Essence gives rise to Tian Gui-the reproductive vitality that governs egg quantity and quality. When Kidney Essence is deficient, Tian Gui becomes insufficient, leading to a poor ovarian reserve, scanty or absent periods, and difficulty conceiving.
Think of it as a deep savings account for fertility: if the reserves are low, the body cannot produce the rich, nourishing environment needed for healthy follicles to develop, and the signs of premature aging-like hair loss, weak back, and memory decline-begin to appear.
- **Tongue & Pulse**: Tongue: pale, thin body with little or no coating, possible fine cracks. Pulse: fine (thin), deep, and weak especially at the rear (Kidney) positions.
- **Triggers & Relief Explanation**: Kidney Essence is a deep, slow-to-replenish resource that is easily drained by overwork, lack of sleep, and excessive sexual activity-all of which force the body to draw on its reserves. Cold and raw foods further chill the system, making it harder for the Kidneys to transform and store Essence. Rest, warmth, and nourishing foods, on the other hand, conserve and slowly rebuild that deep reservoir, while gentle movement circulates Qi without spending precious Essence.
- **Diet & Lifestyle**: Prioritize deep, restorative sleep and avoid staying up late, as the Kidneys recharge at night. Eat warm, cooked, nourishing foods like bone broths, black sesame, walnuts, and goji berries. Gentle movement like Qi Gong or walking supports Qi flow without depleting reserves. Protect your lower back from cold and avoid overwork, excessive sexual activity, and chronic stress, which all drain Essence.

### Kidney and Liver Yin Deficiency
*Pattern Link: [Detailed Pattern Page](/knowledge-base/patterns/kidney-and-liver-yin-deficiency)*

- **Clinical Signs & Symptoms**: Scanty menstruation with bright red blood, Hot flashes and night sweats, Dry eyes and blurred vision, Dizziness and ringing in the ears, Soreness and weakness of the lower back and knees, Insomnia or restless sleep with vivid dreams, Dry mouth and throat, especially at night, Flushed cheekbones in the afternoon
- **Worse with**: Late nights and sleep deprivation, Overwork and chronic stress, Spicy, fried, or drying foods, Excessive sweating from intense exercise, Hot, dry environments
- **Better with**: Early, consistent bedtime, Cooling, moistening foods, Gentle yoga or tai chi, Staying well hydrated, Stress management and calm mind
- **Why this happens (Mechanism)**:
  The Kidneys store the essence that fuels the entire reproductive system, and the Liver stores the blood that nourishes it. When Kidney Yin and Liver Yin run low, the ovaries and uterus are starved of the cooling, moistening fluids they need to grow healthy follicles. This internal dryness leads to scanty, bright-red periods, a thin uterine lining, and the classic heat signs like hot flashes and night sweats seen in diminished ovarian reserve.
- **Tongue & Pulse**: Tongue: red, thin body with cracks, little or no coating, dry surface. Pulse: fine (thin) and rapid, often with a wiry quality; the left Chi (Kidney) position is weak.
- **Triggers & Relief Explanation**: Kidney and Liver Yin are the body's cooling, moistening reserves, and they are easily depleted by overwork, stress, and heat. Late nights and intense exercise burn through yin, while spicy and drying foods add heat that further evaporates these fluids. Rest, early sleep, and cooling, moistening foods allow the body to rebuild its yin stores, which in turn calms the deficiency heat that causes hot flashes and night sweats, and restores the nourishment the ovaries need.
- **Diet & Lifestyle**: Eat cooling, moistening foods like black sesame, goji berries, mulberries, pears, and bone broths. Avoid spicy, fried, and drying foods that burn up yin. Wind down early, and protect your sleep - the hours before midnight are when yin rebuilds. Gentle, flowing movement like yoga or tai chi is better than high-intensity, sweat-drenching exercise that can further deplete fluids.

### Liver Qi Stagnation
*Pattern Link: [Detailed Pattern Page](/knowledge-base/patterns/liver-qi-stagnation)*

- **Clinical Signs & Symptoms**: Irregular or scanty periods that worsen with stress, Breast tenderness or swelling before periods, Mood swings, irritability, or low mood, Distension, bloating, or pain along the ribs and flanks, Frequent sighing or feeling of a lump in the throat
- **Worse with**: Emotional stress, anger, or frustration, Alcohol and coffee, Spicy, greasy, or heavy foods, Sedentary lifestyle
- **Better with**: Stress reduction and relaxation, Gentle exercise (yoga, walking), Warm compress on the lower abdomen, Chrysanthemum or peppermint tea
- **Why this happens (Mechanism)**:
  Emotional stress, frustration, and unexpressed anger cause the Liver's Qi to become stuck and stagnant. This stagnation disrupts the smooth flow of Qi and blood through the Chong and Ren vessels, which are the main channels supplying the uterus and ovaries. Over time, this impairs follicular development, leads to irregular or scanty periods, and contributes to a diminished ovarian reserve.
- **Tongue & Pulse**: Tongue: normal or slightly dark body with red or slightly purple sides, thin white coat. Pulse: wiry (弦 xián), felt most prominently at the left middle position.
- **Triggers & Relief Explanation**: Stress and anger are the direct emotional triggers that knot Liver Qi, so calming the mind and moving the body gently helps untie that knot. Warmth encourages Qi and blood to circulate in the pelvis, counteracting the stagnation that starves the ovaries. Avoiding stimulants and heavy foods prevents the buildup of heat and dampness that can further jam the flow of Liver Qi.
- **Diet & Lifestyle**: Prioritize stress management through meditation, gentle yoga, or walking outdoors. Avoid alcohol, coffee, and spicy or greasy foods, which can fan Liver heat and worsen stagnation. Eat plenty of lightly cooked greens, chrysanthemum tea, and small amounts of mint to gently move Qi.

### Blood Stagnation in the Directing and Penetrating Vessels
*Pattern Link: [Detailed Pattern Page](/knowledge-base/patterns/blood-stagnation-in-the-directing-and-penetrating-vessels)*

- **Clinical Signs & Symptoms**: Sharp, stabbing, fixed lower abdominal or pelvic pain, Dark purple menstrual blood with large clots, Painful periods that improve after clots are passed, Palpable lumps or masses in the lower abdomen, Breast tenderness and distension before periods
- **Worse with**: Prolonged sitting or inactivity, Emotional stress and frustration, Cold weather or cold drinks
- **Better with**: Gentle exercise and stretching, Warmth on the lower abdomen, Passing menstrual clots
- **Why this happens (Mechanism)**:
  The Directing (Ren) and Penetrating (Chong) Vessels are the main channels that bring Qi and blood to the uterus and ovaries. When emotional strain or physical trauma causes blood to stagnate in these vessels, the flow becomes blocked. This obstruction prevents fresh, nourishing blood from reaching the ovarian follicles, starving them of the resources they need to mature. Over time, the persistent stasis damages the delicate reproductive environment, resulting in fewer and lower-quality eggs, irregular or absent periods, and difficulty conceiving.
- **Tongue & Pulse**: Tongue: dark purple body with possible stasis spots, engorged sublingual veins, thin white coat. Pulse: choppy (涩 sè) like a knife scraping bamboo, often wiry at the left middle position.
- **Triggers & Relief Explanation**: Blood stasis is, by nature, a state of stuckness. Gentle movement and warmth encourage circulation, helping to break through the stagnation, which is why pain eases after clots pass. Stress tightens the Liver Qi, which can further squeeze the vessels and worsen the stasis, while cold constricts and congeals, making the obstruction more severe.
- **Diet & Lifestyle**: Gentle, consistent movement like walking or yoga helps keep blood flowing in the pelvis. Avoid prolonged sitting. Eat warm, cooked foods and include mild blood-moving spices like turmeric and black pepper. Minimize cold, raw foods and icy drinks, which can congeal blood and worsen stasis.

### Kidney and Spleen Yang Deficiency
*Pattern Link: [Detailed Pattern Page](/knowledge-base/patterns/kidney-and-spleen-yang-deficiency)*

- **Clinical Signs & Symptoms**: Feeling cold, especially in the lower back, knees, and abdomen, Scanty, pale, or delayed periods, Early-morning diarrhea or loose stools with undigested food, Frequent urination with clear, pale urine, Profound fatigue and lack of motivation, Low libido
- **Worse with**: Exposure to cold environments, Raw, cold, or frozen foods and drinks, Overwork and chronic stress, Prolonged standing or heavy lifting, Late nights and insufficient sleep
- **Better with**: Warmth on the lower back and belly, Warm cooked meals and soups, Gentle, consistent exercise, Adequate rest and early nights, Moxibustion
- **Why this happens (Mechanism)**:
  Kidney Yang is the body's pilot light, providing warmth and driving all transformation, including the maturation of eggs. When it fades, the reproductive fire (Ming Men) dims, leaving the uterus and ovaries cold and under-functioning. At the same time, Spleen Yang deficiency means food is not efficiently turned into Qi and blood, so the raw materials needed to build healthy follicles run short. Together, this deep inner cold and lack of nourishment cause a gradual decline in ovarian reserve.
- **Tongue & Pulse**: Tongue: pale, swollen, and puffy with teeth marks; coating is white and slippery. Pulse: deep, weak, and slow, especially feeble in both rear (Kidney) positions and the right middle (Spleen) position.
- **Triggers & Relief Explanation**: Kidney and Spleen Yang are like a small fire that must be protected. Cold foods, cold environments, and overexertion douse that fire, worsening the deep chill and sluggish digestion. Warmth, rest, and nourishing cooked foods gently feed the flame, allowing the body to transform essence and support reproductive function.
- **Diet & Lifestyle**: Eat warm, cooked foods like soups, stews, and congees. Include warming spices (ginger, cinnamon, black pepper) and small amounts of lamb or beef. Avoid raw, cold, and frozen foods and drinks. Keep your lower back, abdomen, and feet warm; a warm compress or moxibustion stick on the lower belly can be very helpful. Gentle daily movement like walking or yoga builds Yang without exhausting it.

### Heart and Kidney Yin Deficiency
*Pattern Link: [Detailed Pattern Page](/knowledge-base/patterns/heart-and-kidney-yin-deficiency)*

- **Clinical Signs & Symptoms**: Insomnia with vivid, restless dreams, Palpitations or a racing heartbeat, Night sweats and warm palms and soles (five-centre heat), Low back soreness with tinnitus or dizziness, Scanty menstrual flow, Mental restlessness and anxiety
- **Worse with**: Anxiety and emotional stress, Spicy, fried, or greasy foods, Coffee and alcohol, Staying up late or overwork, Hot weather or overheated rooms
- **Better with**: Deep, restful sleep, Cooling drinks and moistening foods, Gentle exercise like tai chi, Cool environment, Stress reduction
- **Why this happens (Mechanism)**:
  Kidney Yin is the foundation of reproductive essence and must ascend to nourish the Heart and keep the mind calm. When Kidney Yin becomes depleted, it fails to rise and contain the Heart, allowing Heart Fire to flare upward. This Heart-Kidney disharmony (心肾不交, xīn shèn bù jiāo) disturbs the spirit, leading to the classic symptoms of insomnia, palpitations, and anxiety. At the same time, the deficient Yin fails to nourish the Chong and Ren vessels, resulting in scanty menstruation and insufficient essence to support healthy follicle development and egg quality, which directly contributes to diminished ovarian reserve.
- **Tongue & Pulse**: Tongue: red, especially at the tip, with a thin body and little or no coating; surface may be dry with possible cracks. Pulse: fine (thin) and rapid; the left Chi (Kidney) position is weak, while the left Cun (Heart) position may feel relatively prominent and slightly floating.
- **Triggers & Relief Explanation**: Stress and anxiety stir up Heart Fire, worsening the disharmony between Heart and Kidney. Spicy, fried, and heating foods consume Yin fluids, while late nights and overwork further deplete Kidney Yin, which is already deficient. Cooling, moistening foods and deep rest nourish Yin and calm the mind, helping to restore the Heart-Kidney communication and support reproductive essence.
- **Diet & Lifestyle**: Eat cooling, moistening foods like pears, lily bulb, lotus seed, goji berries, and black sesame. Avoid spicy, fried, or overly heating foods and stimulants like coffee. Prioritize deep rest and a regular sleep schedule; avoid staying up late, as nighttime is when Yin is restored. Gentle practices like yoga, tai chi, or meditation help calm the mind and preserve Yin.

### Qi and Blood Deficiency
*Pattern Link: [Detailed Pattern Page](/knowledge-base/patterns/qi-and-blood-deficiency)*

- **Clinical Signs & Symptoms**: Profound fatigue and weakness, even at rest, Pale, sallow complexion and pale lips, Scanty, light-colored menstrual flow, Dizziness and heart palpitations, Poor appetite and spontaneous sweating
- **Worse with**: Overwork and exhaustion, Skipping meals or irregular eating, Raw, cold foods and iced drinks, Excessive exercise, Worry and mental strain
- **Better with**: Adequate sleep and rest, Warm, cooked, nourishing meals, Gentle walking or qigong, Reducing worry and overthinking
- **Why this happens (Mechanism)**:
  Your body's overall energy (Qi) and blood are depleted, so there isn't enough nourishment reaching your ovaries and uterus. The Spleen, which produces Qi and blood from food, is weak, and the Heart, which governs blood, can't properly supply the reproductive organs. Without adequate blood, the ovaries cannot develop healthy follicles, leading to fewer and lower-quality eggs, and your periods may become scanty, light, or even stop.
- **Tongue & Pulse**: Tongue: pale body, may be thin or have teeth marks, thin white coating. Pulse: fine, weak, and empty; weakest at the right Guan position (Spleen/Stomach).
- **Triggers & Relief Explanation**: The Spleen transforms food into Qi and Blood, so warm, regular meals provide the raw materials your ovaries need. Overwork and stress drain Qi and Blood directly, while cold foods weaken the Spleen's digestive fire, further reducing blood production. Rest and gentle movement conserve energy and allow the body to rebuild its reserves, which is why these habits directly improve ovarian function.
- **Diet & Lifestyle**: Eat warm, cooked, nutrient-dense foods like bone broths, stews with root vegetables, red dates, goji berries, and black sesame. Avoid raw, cold foods that weaken the Spleen. Gentle daily movement like walking or qigong is ideal-avoid exhausting workouts. Prioritize deep rest and sleep, and protect your energy by setting boundaries against overwork.

### Deficiency of both the Kidney Yin and Yang
*Pattern Link: [Detailed Pattern Page](/knowledge-base/patterns/deficiency-of-both-the-kidney-yin-and-yang)*

- **Clinical Signs & Symptoms**: Alternating chills and hot flushes, Lower back cold and aching, Deep fatigue with poor memory, Night sweats yet cold limbs, Early-morning diarrhoea
- **Worse with**: Overwork and late nights, Cold, raw foods, Exposure to cold, Excessive sexual activity, Emotional stress
- **Better with**: Warm, cooked meals, Keeping lower back warm, Rest and early nights, Gentle exercise (tai chi, walking), Acupuncture and moxibustion
- **Why this happens (Mechanism)**:
  The Kidneys are the foundation of reproductive vitality. When both Kidney Yin (the cooling, moistening, nourishing force) and Kidney Yang (the warming, activating, transformative force) are deeply depleted, the body lacks the material essence to generate healthy eggs and the metabolic fire to trigger ovulation and sustain regular menstruation. This dual deficiency leads to a profound decline in ovarian reserve, with cycles that become scanty, irregular, or stop altogether, accompanied by a mix of cold and heat signs that reflect the body's loss of balance.
- **Tongue & Pulse**: Tongue: pale, puffy-tender body with teeth marks and cracks, thin white coat that may be partially peeled. Pulse: deep, fine, weak, weakest at both Chi positions; may be slightly rapid or slow depending on which deficiency predominates.
- **Triggers & Relief Explanation**: When both Kidney Yin and Yang are depleted, the body's ability to generate warmth and maintain moisture is compromised. Warmth and rest conserve the little Yang remaining, while nourishing foods and adequate sleep allow Yin to regenerate. Cold, overwork, and excessive sexual activity drain Yang and consume Yin, worsening the dual deficiency. Stress disrupts the balance of Yin and Yang, so calming practices help stabilize the reproductive axis.
- **Diet & Lifestyle**: Eat warm, cooked foods that nourish both Yin and Yang, such as bone broths, black sesame, walnuts, goji berries, and lamb stew with angelica root. Avoid raw, cold foods and iced drinks. Keep your lower back and abdomen warm. Prioritize rest and gentle movement like tai chi or restorative yoga; overwork and late nights deplete both Yin and Yang. Manage stress with meditation or acupuncture, as emotional strain further disturbs the Kidney's delicate balance.

## Frequently Asked Questions
### Can acupuncture and herbs really improve my egg quality?
Yes, many women see measurable improvements in egg quality markers like AMH, FSH, and antral follicle count after consistent TCM treatment. Acupuncture increases blood flow to the ovaries, while herbal formulas provide the building blocks for healthy egg development. Because it takes about 90 days for a follicle to mature, a minimum of three months of treatment is recommended to see changes in egg quality. While TCM cannot reverse age-related decline indefinitely, it can often optimize the function of the eggs you still have.

### How long does TCM treatment take to work for diminished ovarian reserve?
Most women begin to notice improvements in their overall well-being, sleep, and menstrual regularity within 2-3 months. For egg quality, allow at least 3 months, and for those trying to conceive, a 6-month course is typical. Deficiency patterns take longer to rebuild reserves, while stagnation-related patterns may respond faster. Consistency with weekly acupuncture and daily herbs is the most important factor for success.

### Can I combine TCM with IVF or other fertility treatments?
Absolutely. Many women use TCM in the months leading up to an IVF cycle to improve ovarian response and uterine lining. During an IVF stimulation phase, your TCM practitioner will adjust your herbal formula to avoid interfering with hormone medications. Acupuncture can also be used on the day of embryo transfer to support implantation. Always keep both your TCM practitioner and your reproductive endocrinologist fully informed of all treatments you are using.

### What lifestyle changes should I make alongside TCM treatment?
Protecting your Kidney Essence is key. Prioritize deep, restorative sleep-ideally before 11pm-and avoid overwork. Eat warm, cooked meals and reduce cold, raw foods. Manage stress through gentle exercise like yoga, walking, or tai chi. Keep your lower back and abdomen warm. If you smoke or drink alcohol excessively, reducing or quitting will support your treatment.

### Is TCM safe during pregnancy or when trying to conceive?
Yes, when prescribed by a qualified practitioner, TCM is safe while trying to conceive. Many herbal formulas are specifically designed to support early pregnancy and prevent miscarriage. However, once you become pregnant, your formula will be adjusted or stopped depending on your needs. Acupuncture is generally safe throughout pregnancy, but certain points are avoided. Always inform your practitioner if there is any chance you could be pregnant.

### What does the tongue and pulse diagnosis reveal about my ovarian reserve?
Your tongue and pulse give your practitioner a real-time picture of your internal balance. A pale, thin tongue with little coating often points to Kidney Essence or Blood deficiency, while a red tongue with no coating suggests Yin deficiency with heat. A deep, weak pulse in the Kidney positions indicates depleted reserves, while a wiry pulse may signal Liver Qi stagnation. These signs help your practitioner tailor your treatment precisely to your pattern.

### Will I need to take herbs forever, or can I stop once my ovarian reserve improves?
TCM treatment is not meant to be lifelong. Once your menstrual cycles are regular, your symptoms resolve, and your egg quality markers improve, your practitioner will gradually reduce the frequency of acupuncture and may transition you to a maintenance herbal formula or stop herbs altogether. Many women then return for seasonal tune-ups or before an IVF cycle. The goal is to restore balance so your body can maintain it on its own.

## Modern Scientific Research
1. **Observation on therapeutic effect of acupuncture combined with Chinese herbal medicine for diminished ovarian reserve** ([Link](https://www.acumoxj.com/uploads/20241028/2bdc02f024c032392374760e81c19cee.pdf))
   *Journal/Publication: Authors not specified. Observation on therapeutic effect of acupuncture combined with Chinese herbal medicine for diminished ovarian reserve. Shanghai Journal of Acupuncture and Moxibustion, 2024.*
   This clinical study evaluated the combined effect of acupuncture and Chinese herbal medicine on women with diminished ovarian reserve. The treatment protocol included acupuncture at points such as bilateral Tianshu ST-25, Zigong (extra point), Zusanli ST-36, Sanyinjiao SP-6, Shenshu BL-23, and others, combined with a herbal formula tailored to Kidney and Spleen deficiency. Results showed significant improvements in serum AMH, FSH, and estradiol levels, as well as an increase in antral follicle count and menstrual regularity.

2. **Acupuncture for diminished ovarian reserve: a randomized controlled trial** ([Link](https://doi.org/10.1136/acupmed-2017-011584))
   *Journal/Publication: Zhou J, Qu F, Nan R. Acupuncture for diminished ovarian reserve: a randomized controlled trial. Acupuncture in Medicine. 2019;37(5):283-291.*
   This randomized controlled trial investigated the efficacy of acupuncture versus sham acupuncture in women with diminished ovarian reserve. The acupuncture group received treatment three times per week for three menstrual cycles. The primary outcomes were changes in serum AMH and FSH levels. The study found that acupuncture significantly increased AMH and reduced FSH compared to the sham group, and also improved menstrual cycle regularity and reduced anxiety scores.
