## Conventional Medicine View
### Description
Rectal prolapse occurs when part of the rectum protrudes through the anus. It can be partial (only the inner lining) or complete (the full thickness of the rectal wall). Common symptoms include a visible bulge, a sensation of incomplete emptying, mucus discharge, bleeding, and sometimes fecal incontinence. Diagnosis is usually made through physical examination, and imaging may be used to assess the extent. It is often linked to chronic straining from constipation, childbirth injury, aging, or connective tissue disorders.

### Treatments
Treatment depends on severity. Mild cases may be managed with stool softeners, increased fiber, and pelvic floor physical therapy. More significant prolapse often requires surgery - such as rectopexy (securing the rectum) or perineal resection - to correct the anatomical defect. While surgery can be effective, it does not address the underlying weakness in the body's tissues or digestive function, and recurrence is possible.

## Traditional Chinese Medicine View
### TCM Understanding
TCM sees rectal prolapse primarily as a failure of the body's upward-holding force, a function governed by the Spleen. The Spleen produces Qi and is responsible for keeping organs in their proper place. When Spleen Qi becomes chronically weak - from overwork, poor diet, or prolonged illness - it can no longer lift, and the Qi sinks downward, dragging the rectum with it. This is the most common pattern, often accompanied by fatigue, poor appetite, and loose stools.
The Kidney also plays a crucial role, especially in older adults. Kidney Qi anchors the lower orifices and provides deep, constitutional holding power. When Kidney Qi is depleted, the anal sphincter and pelvic floor lose their grip. This pattern often involves sore lower back, frequent urination, and a feeling of cold, and is more likely in the elderly or those with long-standing illness.
In contrast, Damp-Heat in the Lower Burner is an excess pattern. A diet heavy in spicy, greasy foods or a damp living environment can create heat and moisture that sink downward, irritating and inflaming the rectal tissues. The prolapsed tissue appears red, swollen, and may ooze sticky discharge. Here, the problem is not just weakness but local inflammation that must be cleared before strengthening can begin.
Qi and Blood Deficiency represents a deeper depletion, often after childbirth, chronic straining, or severe illness. The Spleen cannot generate enough Qi to hold, and Blood cannot nourish the tissues. The result is a chronic, pale prolapse with dizziness, pale complexion, and weak nails. Recognizing which pattern is dominant allows the practitioner to craft a treatment that truly fits the individual.

### Practitioner Diagnosis
A TCM practitioner begins by observing the prolapse itself and asking about the circumstances that bring it on. The color, temperature, and any discharge point toward either a deficiency pattern or an excess heat pattern. The person’s overall energy, digestion, and age are also key clues.
If the prolapse is pale, soft, and occurs after straining, standing, or coughing, and the person feels chronically tired with a poor appetite and loose stools, the pattern is likely Spleen Qi Sinking. The tongue is pale with a thin white coat, and the pulse feels weak and thin. This is a classic “falling” of the body’s central Qi.
When the protruding tissue is red, swollen, hot, and painful, and may ooze sticky fluid or blood, Damp-Heat in the Lower Burner is suspected. The person often has a history of spicy, greasy foods or damp living conditions. The tongue appears red with a yellow, greasy coating, and the pulse is slippery and rapid, reflecting the heat and dampness.
In older adults or those with long-standing illness, a prolapse that feels like it has no grip, along with weak lower back and knees, frequent nighttime urination, and a feeling of cold, points to Kidney Qi not Firm. The tongue is pale with a white coat, and the pulse is deep and weak. This pattern indicates the body’s deepest anchoring energy has weakened.
A person with a chronic, easily-prolapsed bowel who also looks pale, feels dizzy, has heart palpitations, and dry skin may have Qi and Blood Deficiency. The tongue is pale and thin, and the pulse is thin and weak. This pattern shares the sinking quality but adds signs of poor nourishment, like brittle nails and scanty menstruation.

## TCM Patterns and Treatment

### Spleen Qi Sinking
*Pattern Link: [Detailed Pattern Page](/knowledge-base/patterns/spleen-qi-sinking)*

- **Clinical Signs & Symptoms**: Dragging or bearing-down sensation in the abdomen, Prolapse that worsens with standing or exertion, Chronic fatigue and lethargy, Poor appetite and loose stools, Pale, puffy tongue with teeth marks
- **Worse with**: Overexertion and heavy lifting, Prolonged standing, Cold, raw foods, Chronic stress and worry, Chronic diarrhea or straining
- **Better with**: Rest and lying down, Warm compress on the lower abdomen, Eating warm, nourishing meals, Gentle abdominal massage
- **Why this happens (Mechanism)**:
  The Spleen is responsible for holding the organs in place by supplying a steady upward 'lifting' force of Qi. When Spleen Qi becomes chronically weak-often from overwork, poor diet, or prolonged illness-that upward force fails, and the Qi literally sinks downward. This sinking drags the intestines with it, causing them to protrude through the anus, especially during straining or standing.
- **Tongue & Pulse**: Tongue: pale, puffy, tender body with teeth marks, thin white coating. Pulse: weak and forceless, especially at the right Guan (Spleen) position; may be deep.
- **Triggers & Relief Explanation**: Overexertion and prolonged standing further deplete Spleen Qi, causing it to sink even more, while rest and lying down allow Qi to rise and recover. Cold foods damage Spleen Yang, weakening its ability to hold organs in place, whereas warm, cooked foods support Spleen function. Stress and worry knot the Qi, impairing the Spleen's lifting power.
- **Diet & Lifestyle**: Eat warm, easily digestible foods like soups, stews, and congee. Avoid raw, cold, and greasy foods that tax the Spleen. Rest in a horizontal position when tired, and avoid prolonged standing or heavy lifting.

### Damp-Heat in the Lower Burner
*Pattern Link: [Detailed Pattern Page](/knowledge-base/patterns/damp-heat-in-the-lower-burner)*

- **Clinical Signs & Symptoms**: Redness and swelling of the prolapsed tissue, Burning or stinging pain around the anus, Sticky, foul-smelling discharge from the prolapse, Feeling of heaviness in the lower abdomen or legs, Dark, scanty urine
- **Worse with**: Spicy, greasy, or fried foods, Alcohol and sugary drinks, Hot, humid weather, Prolonged sitting or straining
- **Better with**: Cooling foods (mung beans, cucumber), Keeping the area clean and dry, Rest in a cool, dry environment, Light, bland diet (congee, steamed greens)
- **Why this happens (Mechanism)**:
  When Dampness and Heat accumulate in the lower body - often from rich, spicy food or a damp living environment - they sink downward and irritate the rectal tissues. This causes localized swelling, redness, and a feeling of heat, which weakens the natural support around the anus. As a result, the rectum is more likely to protrude during bowel movements, prolonged standing, or any increase in abdominal pressure.
- **Tongue & Pulse**: Tongue: red body with a yellow greasy coating, thickest at the root. Pulse: slippery (滑 Huá) and rapid (数 Shuò).
- **Triggers & Relief Explanation**: Damp-Heat is fueled by rich, spicy foods and damp environments, which create more inflammation and swelling in the lower body. Cooling, bland foods and a dry, clean environment help drain Dampness and clear Heat, reducing local irritation and allowing the tissues to retract naturally.
- **Diet & Lifestyle**: Avoid spicy, greasy, and fried foods, as well as alcohol and sugar, which all generate Damp-Heat. Choose cooling, dampness-draining foods like mung beans, celery, cucumber, and Job's tears (yi yi ren). Keep the anal area clean and dry; a gentle sitz bath with mild, non-irritating herbs can soothe irritation. Avoid prolonged sitting on damp or hot surfaces. Gentle pelvic floor exercises can help support the tissues without straining.

### Kidney Qi not Firm
*Pattern Link: [Detailed Pattern Page](/knowledge-base/patterns/kidney-qi-not-firm)*

- **Clinical Signs & Symptoms**: Sore, weak lower back and knees, Frequent, clear urination or dribbling, Prolapse worse with prolonged standing or exertion, Fatigue without significant digestive upset
- **Worse with**: Prolonged standing, Overexertion and heavy lifting, Cold weather or cold foods, Excessive sexual activity, Chronic coughing or straining
- **Better with**: Lying down with hips elevated, Warmth on the lower back, Gentle pelvic floor exercises, Eating warm, nourishing meals
- **Why this happens (Mechanism)**:
  The Kidneys govern the lower orifices and anchor the body’s holding function. When Kidney Qi is depleted, the anal sphincter and pelvic floor lose their grip, allowing the rectum to protrude. This often occurs in the elderly or those with long-standing illness, where deep constitutional weakness fails to hold organs in place.
- **Tongue & Pulse**: Tongue: pale, puffy, and moist with a thin white coat; possible tooth marks. Pulse: deep, weak, and fine, especially at the Chi (Kidney) positions.
- **Triggers & Relief Explanation**: Standing and straining further deplete the already weak Kidney Qi and drag it downward, worsening the prolapse. Rest and elevating the hips allow gravity to assist the return of organs. Warmth and nourishing foods support Kidney Yang, which is the root of the body’s holding power. Gentle exercises strengthen the pelvic floor without exhausting Qi.
- **Diet & Lifestyle**: Prioritize warm, cooked foods like stews and soups, and include kidney-nourishing ingredients such as walnuts, black beans, and lamb. Avoid prolonged standing or heavy lifting. Gentle exercises like Kegels and pelvic floor strengthening can help retrain the muscles, while lying down with hips elevated for short periods may relieve pressure.

### Qi and Blood Deficiency
*Pattern Link: [Detailed Pattern Page](/knowledge-base/patterns/qi-and-blood-deficiency)*

- **Clinical Signs & Symptoms**: Pale, sallow complexion and pale nail beds, Dizziness and heart palpitations, Chronic prolapse that feels weak and does not retract easily, Fatigue and shortness of breath on exertion, Poor appetite and poor sleep
- **Worse with**: Overexertion and heavy lifting, Prolonged standing, Skipping meals or poor diet, Chronic stress and worry, Excessive blood loss (e.g., heavy periods)
- **Better with**: Rest and lying down, Eating warm, nourishing meals, Gentle abdominal massage, Adequate sleep
- **Why this happens (Mechanism)**:
  Qi provides the lifting force that holds organs in place, while Blood nourishes the tissues that give them structural support. When both are deeply depleted-often after prolonged illness, childbirth, or chronic straining-the Spleen cannot generate enough Qi to hold the rectum up, and the lack of Blood leaves the surrounding muscles and fascia weak and poorly retracted. The result is a chronic, gradual prolapse that feels heavy and fails to retract easily, always accompanied by widespread signs of deficiency like pallor, dizziness, and fatigue.
- **Tongue & Pulse**: Tongue: pale body, may be thin or have teeth marks, thin white coating. Pulse: fine (thin), weak, especially at the right Guan position.
- **Triggers & Relief Explanation**: Qi and Blood are the body's energy and substance; any drain on them worsens the sinking, while rest and nourishment rebuild the lifting force. Lying down relieves the downward pull of gravity, and warm, easily digested foods support Spleen function to produce more Qi and Blood. Overexertion, prolonged standing, and heavy lifting directly consume Qi and strain the already weak support structures, causing the prolapse to worsen.
- **Diet & Lifestyle**: Eat warm, cooked, nourishing foods like bone broths, congees, stewed meats, and small amounts of red dates or goji berries. Avoid raw, cold, and greasy foods that burden the Spleen. Rest after meals and avoid standing for long periods. Gentle exercises like walking or Qi Gong help build Qi without straining.

## Frequently Asked Questions
### Can TCM cure rectal prolapse without surgery?
For mild to moderate prolapse, especially when addressed early, TCM can often significantly reduce or resolve the prolapse by strengthening the Spleen and Kidney Qi and clearing any Damp-Heat. However, severe or complete prolapse with tissue damage may still require surgical repair. TCM is also excellent for preparing the body for surgery and supporting recovery afterward.

### How long does it take to see results?
Many patients notice less dragging and easier reduction of the prolapse within the first few weeks. Damp-Heat patterns often improve fastest. Deficiency patterns require patience; energy and digestion usually improve before the prolapse firms up, which can take 2-4 months. Deep Qi and Blood Deficiency may need 3-6 months of consistent treatment.

### Is acupuncture safe for prolapse?
