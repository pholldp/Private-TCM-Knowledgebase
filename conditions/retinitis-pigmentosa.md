## Conventional Medicine View
### Description
Retinitis pigmentosa (RP) is a group of inherited retinal dystrophies that cause progressive degeneration of the photoreceptor cells - the rods and cones that capture light. The earliest symptom is usually night blindness, followed by a gradual narrowing of the visual field (tunnel vision) and, in many cases, eventual central vision loss. Diagnosis is made through a dilated eye exam, electroretinography (ERG), and increasingly through genetic testing. There is currently no cure; management focuses on slowing progression with high-dose vitamin A, low-vision aids, and protective measures against light exposure.

### Treatments
Conventional care for RP centers on supportive measures. Vitamin A palmitate supplementation may modestly slow the decline of retinal function in some people. Low-vision rehabilitation, orientation and mobility training, and the use of sunglasses to reduce light damage are standard. For a small subset of patients with specific gene mutations, gene therapy (such as voretigene neparvovec) is now available. However, for the vast majority, treatment remains symptomatic, with no approved disease-modifying drug.

## Traditional Chinese Medicine View
### TCM Understanding
In Chinese medicine, the eyes are not isolated organs - they are the flowering tips of a deep root system. The Liver opens into the eyes and supplies them with Blood, while the Kidneys store Essence, the foundational substance that generates marrow and nourishes the brain and senses. When Liver Blood and Kidney Essence are profoundly depleted, the retina loses its vital nourishment, and the first sign is almost always difficulty seeing in dim light. Night is yin, and seeing in the dark requires abundant yin and blood; their deficiency creates the classic night blindness of RP.
But the story rarely ends with deficiency alone. As the disease advances, sluggish circulation in the tiny vessels of the eye can lead to blood stasis - a kind of silt that further starves the retina. If the Spleen, the engine of digestion, is weak, it may fail to produce enough clear qi and blood while also allowing dampness to accumulate. This turbid dampness can rise and cloud the eyes, adding a heavy, greasy sensation to the visual loss.
Even emotional life matters: when stress or frustration knots the Liver's qi flow, the upward delivery of nourishment to the eyes is obstructed, and vision may fog or narrow during tense periods.
This is why one Western diagnosis can unfold so differently from person to person - and why TCM looks beyond the retina. A patient who is pale, exhausted, and easily chilled with a weak digestion needs a very different approach than one who is irritable, flushed, and phlegmatic. By identifying which pattern - or combination of patterns - is driving the vision loss, the practitioner can craft a treatment that both replenishes the deep reserves and clears whatever is blocking the path to the eyes.

### Practitioner Diagnosis
A TCM practitioner first asks what you notice in dim light. Night blindness is the earliest and most telling clue, and it almost always points to a deficiency of Liver Blood and Kidney Essence - the two systems that nourish the eyes. From there, the practitioner looks at accompanying signs, tongue, and pulse to decide which pattern is dominant and whether stagnation or dampness has joined the picture.
When night blindness comes with dry eyes, dizziness, pale nails, and mild lower back soreness, the core pattern is Liver Blood and Kidney Essence Deficiency. The tongue is pale with a thin white coat, and the pulse is thready and weak. This is the foundation of most retinitis pigmentosa cases, where the eyes simply lack the nourishment they need to adapt to darkness.
If the vision loss is more rapid and accompanied by pronounced lower back and knee soreness, tinnitus, memory decline, or even nystagmus (involuntary eye movement), the picture shifts toward a deeper Kidney Essence Deficiency. Here the tongue is still pale and thinly coated, but the pulse feels thready and rapid, reflecting a more severe depletion of the body’s fundamental reserves.
When the eyes feel heavy, as if the lids are weighted, and digestion is sluggish with bloating and loose stools, Spleen Deficiency with Dampness is at play. The tongue is swollen with a thick, greasy coating, and the pulse is soft or slippery. Dampness clouds the upward flow of clear Qi to the eyes, so vision becomes murky rather than simply dim.
A person who notices a narrowing of the visual field, dark spots, and a sallow complexion, along with fatigue, likely has Blood and Qi Deficiency with Blood Stagnation. The tongue is dark or shows stasis spots, and the pulse is thready and choppy. This pattern often emerges later in the disease, when weak Qi can no longer push blood through the tiny vessels of the eye.
Emotional stress that causes vision to fluctuate, together with a sensation of distension in the chest or flanks, suggests Liver Qi Stagnation. The tongue is dark or purplish, and the pulse is wiry. This pattern rarely stands alone; it usually complicates an underlying deficiency, as constrained Liver Qi further obstructs the upward delivery of nourishment.
A less common scenario is Phlegm-Heat, where blurred vision comes with a feeling of pressure in the eyes, yellow sputum, and a sensation of heat. The tongue is red with a yellow greasy coating, and the pulse is slippery and rapid. The turbid phlegm-heat obstructs the eye collaterals, making the visual loss feel heavy and congested rather than simply weak.
An acute episode of red, painful, light-sensitive eyes is usually a superimposed Wind-Heat invasion, not the chronic driver of retinitis pigmentosa. It stands out by its sudden onset and a floating rapid pulse, and it resolves without leaving the lasting night blindness that defines the deeper deficiency patterns.

## TCM Patterns and Treatment

### Liver Blood and Kidney Essence Deficiency
*Pattern Link: [Detailed Pattern Page](/knowledge-base/patterns/liver-blood-and-kidney-essence-deficiency)*

- **Clinical Signs & Symptoms**: Night blindness, especially in dim light, Dry eyes and blurred vision, Dizziness and poor memory, Sore lower back and weak knees, Scanty or absent menstruation (in women)
- **Worse with**: Overwork and exhaustion, Prolonged screen time, Cold, raw foods and iced drinks, Emotional stress and frustration, Excessive sexual activity
- **Better with**: Rest and adequate sleep, Warm, nourishing foods, Dark, quiet environment, Gentle eye exercises and acupressure
- **Why this happens (Mechanism)**:
  In Chinese medicine, the Liver opens into the eyes and stores Blood, while the Kidneys store Essence, which produces marrow that nourishes the brain and the senses. When Liver Blood and Kidney Essence are both deeply depleted, the eyes lose their vital nourishment. This leads to the classic Retinitis Pigmentosa symptom of night blindness, because Blood and Essence are the yin substances that sustain vision in dim light. Over time, the lack of nourishment causes the visual field to narrow and sharpness to fade, much like a lamp running out of oil.
- **Tongue & Pulse**: Tongue: pale, thin body with a thin white or scanty coating. Pulse: deep, fine, and weak; the left Guan (Liver) position is fine and without strength; both Chi (Kidney) positions are deep and weak.
- **Triggers & Relief Explanation**: Liver Blood and Kidney Essence are like the deep reserves of oil in a lamp-they sustain vision over a lifetime. Overwork, poor diet, and excessive screen time drain these reserves faster, dimming the light. Rest, nourishing foods, and acupuncture help refill those reserves, while protecting the eyes from strain conserves what is left. Because the Kidneys govern reproduction, excessive sexual activity can further deplete Essence, directly impacting vision.
- **Diet & Lifestyle**: Eat warm, cooked foods that build Blood and Essence, such as bone broths, dark leafy greens, goji berries, black sesame seeds, and small amounts of liver. Avoid cold, raw foods and iced drinks, which weaken digestion and slow down the production of Blood. Protect your eyes from excessive screen time and bright light, and make rest a priority-go to bed early, as the Liver and Kidneys recharge during sleep. Gentle movement like Tai Chi or walking supports overall Qi flow without exhausting your reserves.

### Kidney Essence Deficiency
*Pattern Link: [Detailed Pattern Page](/knowledge-base/patterns/kidney-essence-deficiency)*

- **Clinical Signs & Symptoms**: Progressive vision loss, especially at night, Involuntary eye movements (nystagmus), Soreness and weakness of the lower back and knees, Dizziness and tinnitus, Frequent urination at night, Premature greying or hair loss
- **Worse with**: Overwork and exhaustion, Excessive sexual activity, Damp or humid weather, Emotional stress and frustration, Cold, raw foods and iced drinks
- **Better with**: Rest and adequate sleep, Warm, nourishing foods, Gentle exercise, Acupuncture and moxibustion, Stress reduction and meditation
- **Why this happens (Mechanism)**:
  Kidney Essence is the deepest foundational energy that supports growth, reproduction, and the health of the eyes. When it becomes depleted, the eyes lose their essential nourishment, leading to progressive vision loss, poor night vision, and sometimes involuntary eye movements (nystagmus). The lower back and knees, also governed by the Kidneys, become sore and weak, and other signs of premature aging may appear.
- **Tongue & Pulse**: Tongue: pale, thin body with little or no coating, possible fine cracks. Pulse: fine (thin), deep, and weak at the Chi positions; may be slightly rapid if Yin deficiency predominates.
- **Triggers & Relief Explanation**: Kidney Essence is depleted by overwork, late nights, and excessive sexual activity because these draw on the body's deepest reserves. Cold environments and raw foods further weaken the Kidneys, which rely on warmth to function. Rest, nourishing foods, and gentle exercise replenish Essence and support the Kidneys, while acupuncture stimulates the flow of Qi to the eyes and strengthens the foundation.
- **Diet & Lifestyle**: Eat warming, nourishing foods like black sesame seeds, walnuts, goji berries, and bone broth to support Kidney Essence. Avoid overwork and late nights, as they deplete the Kidneys. Gentle daily movement like walking or taiji helps circulate Qi without draining reserves. Protect the eyes from strain and get plenty of rest.

### Spleen Deficiency with Dampness
*Pattern Link: [Detailed Pattern Page](/knowledge-base/patterns/spleen-deficiency-with-dampness)*

- **Clinical Signs & Symptoms**: Night blindness that worsens with fatigue and poor digestion, Abdominal bloating, worse after eating, Loose or unformed stools, Heaviness of the body and limbs, Poor appetite with reduced food intake, Sticky or greasy sensation in the mouth
- **Worse with**: Cold, raw foods and iced drinks, Greasy, fried, or spicy foods, Damp or humid weather, Overthinking and worry, Sedentary habits
- **Better with**: Warm, nourishing foods, Gentle exercise, Aromatic spices (ginger, cardamom), Rest after meals
- **Why this happens (Mechanism)**:
  The Spleen is the engine that transforms food into Qi and Blood. When it is weak, the body produces less of the clear nourishment that the eyes need, so vision dims, especially at night. At the same time, a sluggish Spleen lets Dampness accumulate, and this heavy, turbid moisture rises to cloud the eyes like a fogged window, further blocking the light.
- **Tongue & Pulse**: Tongue: pale and swollen with teeth marks on the sides, covered by a white, greasy coating. Pulse: soggy (濡 ru) and slow (缓 huan), often slippery (滑 hua).
- **Triggers & Relief Explanation**: This pattern arises because the Spleen is too weak to transform food and fluids, so Dampness builds up. Cold and greasy foods directly damage the Spleen and add more Dampness, while warmth and easy-to-digest meals give the Spleen a break. Gentle movement wakes up a sluggish Spleen, and aromatic spices act like small brooms to sweep away turbid moisture. Worry knots the Spleen Qi, further impairing digestion and making the eyes dimmer.
- **Diet & Lifestyle**: Favor warm, cooked, easily digested foods like congee, stews, and roasted root vegetables. Include Spleen-friendly grains such as millet and rice, and small amounts of aromatic spices like ginger and cardamom. Minimize raw, cold, greasy, and sweet foods that create more Dampness. Gentle daily movement like walking or tai chi helps the Spleen move energy and dry out internal moisture.

### Blood and Qi Deficiency with Blood Stagnation
*Pattern Link: [Detailed Pattern Page](/knowledge-base/patterns/blood-and-qi-deficiency-with-blood-stagnation)*

- **Clinical Signs & Symptoms**: Narrowed visual fields (tunnel vision), Dark spots or floaters in vision, Sallow complexion with a dusky, purplish undertone, Fixed stabbing pain in the eyes or head, Fatigue and weakness, especially after exertion, Pale-purple tongue with stasis spots
- **Worse with**: Overwork and exhaustion, Emotional stress and frustration, Cold, raw foods and iced drinks, Prolonged screen time, Greasy, fried, or spicy foods
- **Better with**: Warm, nourishing foods, Gentle exercise, Rest and adequate sleep, Warmth on the eyes (warm compress)
- **Why this happens (Mechanism)**:
  When Qi and Blood are chronically depleted, the body lacks the driving force to circulate blood smoothly through the tiny vessels that nourish the retina. Over time, this sluggish flow leads to stagnation-like a stream that has run dry and left behind silt. The eyes, which rely on a rich supply of Blood and Qi, become undernourished and obstructed, producing the progressive narrowing of visual fields and the dark spots typical of retinitis pigmentosa.
- **Tongue & Pulse**: Tongue: pale-purple body with possible stasis spots, thin white coat, distended sublingual veins. Pulse: fine (xi) and choppy (se), uneven and hesitant like a knife scraping bamboo.
- **Triggers & Relief Explanation**: In this pattern, the body lacks the Qi and Blood needed to move blood smoothly. Overwork and stress deplete Qi further, making stagnation worse. Cold and raw foods chill the vessels, causing them to constrict and blood to congeal. Warm, nourishing meals and rest rebuild Qi and Blood, while gentle movement stimulates circulation without draining reserves. Warm compresses locally encourage blood flow to the eyes, helping to clear the stasis that blurs vision.
- **Diet & Lifestyle**: Focus on warm, cooked meals that build Qi and Blood, such as bone broths, slow-cooked stews, dark leafy greens, and small amounts of organ meats if tolerated. Goji berries and black sesame seeds are excellent snacks. Gentle daily movement like walking or qigong helps keep Blood flowing without exhausting your reserves. Avoid cold, raw foods and iced drinks, which constrict the vessels and worsen stasis.

### Liver Qi Stagnation
*Pattern Link: [Detailed Pattern Page](/knowledge-base/patterns/liver-qi-stagnation)*

- **Clinical Signs & Symptoms**: Distension or bloating along the ribs, Emotional irritability or depression, Frequent sighing, Worsening vision with stress, Feeling of a lump in the throat (plum pit sensation)
- **Worse with**: Emotional stress and frustration, Prolonged screen time, Greasy, fried, or spicy foods
- **Better with**: Gentle exercise, Open emotional expression, Warm peppermint or rose tea
- **Why this happens (Mechanism)**:
  The Liver is responsible for the smooth flow of Qi and Blood throughout the body. When emotional stress, frustration, or suppressed anger block this flow, the upward movement to the eyes is obstructed. Over time, the retina is starved of nourishment, contributing to the gradual vision loss and narrowed visual fields seen in retinitis pigmentosa. This pattern often intensifies an existing deficiency, acting as a brake on the body’s ability to repair and maintain the eye tissues.
- **Tongue & Pulse**: Tongue: normal or slightly dark body with red or purplish sides, thin white coat. Pulse: wiry (弦 xián), especially at the left middle position.
- **Triggers & Relief Explanation**: Liver Qi Stagnation is directly worsened by emotional constraint-frustration and stress cause the Qi to knot up, cutting off the nourishing flow to the eyes. Gentle movement and stretching physically unbind the Qi, while aromatic herbs and teas like peppermint and rose help disperse it. Avoiding heavy, greasy foods prevents additional dampness and heat that can further obstruct the Liver's smooth function.
- **Diet & Lifestyle**: Prioritize stress-reducing practices like tai chi, gentle yoga, or daily walks outdoors. Add small amounts of pungent, dispersing foods-fresh mint, basil, fennel, or a little turmeric-to meals. Limit or avoid alcohol, coffee, and fried foods, which can heat and further congeal stuck Qi.

### Phlegm-Heat
*Pattern Link: [Detailed Pattern Page](/knowledge-base/patterns/phlegm-heat)*

- **Clinical Signs & Symptoms**: Blurred vision with a cloudy or filmy sensation, Sensation of eye fullness or pressure, Thick yellow sputum, Chest oppression and restlessness
- **Worse with**: Greasy, fried, or spicy foods, Alcohol, Damp or humid weather, Emotional stress and frustration
- **Better with**: Light, cooling meals, Deep breathing and relaxation, Cool, dry environment
- **Why this happens (Mechanism)**:
  Phlegm-Heat is a thick, turbid substance that accumulates when digestion is weak and heat builds up. In retinitis pigmentosa, this turbidity can rise and obstruct the delicate eye collaterals, clouding the clear orifices needed for sharp vision. This leads to a sensation of pressure or fullness around the eyes and a progressive blurring of sight.
- **Tongue & Pulse**: Tongue: red body, possibly swollen, with a thick, yellow, greasy coating. Pulse: slippery (滑 huá) and rapid (数 shuò).
- **Triggers & Relief Explanation**: Greasy, spicy, and heavy foods directly create phlegm and heat in the body, while alcohol and stress stir up internal heat and stagnation, worsening the obstruction in the eyes. Light meals and a cool environment help clear heat and reduce phlegm formation, allowing the eye collaterals to open and vision to improve.
- **Diet & Lifestyle**: Avoid greasy, fried, spicy, and sweet foods that generate phlegm and heat. Eat light, easily digestible meals with cooling vegetables like cucumber and celery. Gentle exercise such as walking or tai chi helps move Qi and prevent stagnation. Keep the living environment dry and well-ventilated.

### Wind-Heat
*Pattern Link: [Detailed Pattern Page](/knowledge-base/patterns/wind-heat)*

- **Clinical Signs & Symptoms**: Sudden red, painful eyes, Photophobia (light sensitivity), Fever with mild chills, Sore throat, Red tongue tip with thin yellow coating, Floating rapid pulse
- **Worse with**: Greasy, fried, or spicy foods, Wind or cold exposure, Overwork and exhaustion, Emotional stress and frustration
- **Better with**: Dark, quiet environment, Cool compresses on eyes, Chrysanthemum or mint tea, Avoiding wind and drafts
- **Why this happens (Mechanism)**:
  Wind-Heat is an acute exterior invasion, not the root cause of retinitis pigmentosa. However, its hot, rising nature can temporarily attack the eyes, causing sudden redness, pain, and photophobia. In someone already vulnerable, this flare-up can further blur vision and worsen night blindness until the pathogen is cleared.
- **Tongue & Pulse**: Tongue: red tip and edges with a thin yellow coating (may be white-to-yellow in early stages). Pulse: floating and rapid.
- **Triggers & Relief Explanation**: Wind-Heat is an exterior pathogen that rises to the eyes. Spicy foods and wind exposure fuel Heat and aggravate the invasion, while rest and cooling measures help the body expel the pathogen. Avoiding strain conserves Qi, allowing the body's defenses to focus on clearing the acute attack.
- **Diet & Lifestyle**: During an acute flare-up, rest your eyes and avoid bright light. Drink plenty of water and eat cooling foods like pear, cucumber, and chrysanthemum tea. Avoid spicy, fried, or greasy foods that generate internal Heat, and shield your eyes from wind and cold drafts.

## Frequently Asked Questions
### Can acupuncture really help with retinitis pigmentosa?
Yes, acupuncture can play a supportive role. While it cannot regrow lost photoreceptors, it aims to improve local circulation around the eyes, reduce oxidative stress, and strengthen the underlying organ systems that nourish the retina. Many patients report less eye fatigue, better adaptation to dim light, and an overall sense of well-being. The best results come when acupuncture is combined with Chinese herbal medicine and consistent, long-term care.

### How soon will I see results?
Improvements in night vision and eye comfort are often noticed gradually, typically within 2-3 months of weekly treatment. Changes in visual field or acuity are slower and less predictable. The primary goal is to stabilize vision and slow progression, so patience and consistency are essential. Your practitioner will track subtle shifts in your energy, sleep, and digestion as early signs that the deeper pattern is shifting.

### Is it safe to combine TCM herbs with vitamin A supplements?
Generally, yes. Chinese herbs that nourish Liver Blood and Kidney Essence do not interfere with vitamin A metabolism. However, always inform both your TCM practitioner and your ophthalmologist about everything you are taking. Some herbs can affect liver function, so periodic monitoring is wise, especially if you are on high-dose vitamin A for extended periods.

### Will I need to change my diet?
Diet is a cornerstone of TCM treatment for RP. You will be guided to eat warm, cooked foods that nourish Blood and Essence - dark leafy greens, goji berries, black sesame, bone broth, and small amounts of high-quality animal protein. Cold, raw, and greasy foods are discouraged because they weaken the Spleen and generate dampness, which can cloud the eyes. Your practitioner will tailor these recommendations to your specific pattern.

### Does TCM work for all types of RP?
TCM does not treat a specific gene mutation; it treats the person's pattern of imbalance. Because almost all forms of RP share the same underlying dynamic of deficiency and often stagnation, Chinese medicine can be applied broadly. However, the rate of progression and the degree of response vary. A thorough TCM evaluation will help determine whether your constitution is likely to benefit and which pattern is dominant.

### What should I expect during an acupuncture session for my eyes?
Your practitioner will use very fine needles on points around the eyes, such as Jingming (BL-1), as well as on the hands, feet, and back. The local points are inserted gently and shallowly; you may feel a mild pressure or a dull ache, but it should not be painful. Many patients find the experience deeply relaxing. After the session, your eyes may feel temporarily heavy or watery, which is normal.

### Can TCM stop the progression completely?
Complete cessation of progression is difficult to guarantee with any treatment, conventional or complementary. However, many patients report that their vision stabilizes for extended periods with consistent TCM care. The aim is to slow the degenerative process as much as possible and to preserve the remaining visual function for as long as possible, while also improving your overall vitality and resilience.

## Modern Scientific Research
1. **Acupuncture for retinitis pigmentosa: a systematic review and meta-analysis** ([Link](https://doi.org/10.1097/MD.0000000000008839))
   *Journal/Publication: Chen J, et al. Acupuncture for retinitis pigmentosa: a systematic review and meta-analysis. Medicine (Baltimore). 2017;96(49):e8839.*
   This systematic review evaluated randomized controlled trials of acupuncture for RP. The meta-analysis suggested that acupuncture combined with conventional treatment improved visual acuity and visual field more than conventional treatment alone, but the evidence was limited by small sample sizes and high risk of bias.

   *Journal/Publication: Zhang L, et al. Clinical observation on Qi Ju Di Huang Wan combined with acupuncture for retinitis pigmentosa. Chinese Acupuncture & Moxibustion. 2015;35(8):791-794.*
   In this clinical trial, patients with RP who received Qi Ju Di Huang Wan plus acupuncture showed significant improvement in night vision and visual field indices compared to a control group receiving only conventional therapy. The study supports the TCM principle of nourishing Liver and Kidney to treat the root of RP.
