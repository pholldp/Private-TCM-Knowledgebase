## Conventional Medicine View
### Description
Keloids are benign fibrous growths that form when the body overproduces collagen during wound healing. They extend beyond the boundaries of the original injury - whether from surgery, piercing, acne, or a minor cut - and don't regress on their own. Keloids can be itchy, painful, tender, and cosmetically distressing, and they tend to run in families, especially among people with darker skin tones.
Diagnosis is usually made by a doctor simply looking at the scar and hearing the history. Because keloids are not cancerous, no biopsy is needed unless the diagnosis is uncertain.

### Treatments
Conventional treatments aim to flatten the scar and relieve symptoms. First-line options include corticosteroid injections directly into the keloid to reduce inflammation and slow collagen production. Other approaches include silicone sheets or gels, pressure therapy, cryotherapy (freezing), laser treatments, and surgical excision, though surgery alone carries a high risk of the keloid returning even larger. Often, multiple methods are combined to improve results.

## Traditional Chinese Medicine View
### TCM Understanding
In TCM, a keloid is seen as a local manifestation of a deeper disharmony - most often involving the Blood, the Spleen, and the Liver. When the body is healthy, a wound heals cleanly and leaves a flat, pale scar. But if internal Heat, Dampness, or Blood Stagnation is already present, the healing process goes awry, and the scar tissue overgrows like a crab's claw, which is exactly what the Chinese name 蟹足肿 (xiè zú zhǒng) means.
The Spleen is central to managing fluids. A diet heavy in greasy, sweet, or spicy foods can weaken the Spleen and generate Dampness, which then mixes with Heat and gets trapped in the Blood. After a skin injury, this Damp-Heat rushes to the wound, making it red, swollen, and intensely itchy. This is one of the most common patterns, especially in people who feel heavy, sluggish, and have a greasy tongue coating.
The Liver, which governs the smooth flow of Qi and Blood, also plays a key role. Emotional stress, frustration, or a constitution prone to stagnation can cause Qi to get stuck. When Qi stagnates, Blood can't move properly, and a hard, dark purple keloid forms with a fixed, stabbing pain that's worse at night - a classic Blood Stagnation pattern.
In other cases, an excess of internal Heat alone can blaze up at the wound site, creating an angry red, hot, painful lesion without much Dampness.
Less commonly, the body may be too weak to heal properly. When Qi and Blood are deficient, the repair process is sluggish and disorganized, leading to pale, hardened keloids that form slowly and resist treatment. And if a wound becomes infected with Toxic-Heat, the keloid can suppurate, ooze, and throb. Recognizing which of these five patterns is driving the scar is the key to choosing the right herbs, acupuncture points, and dietary changes.

### Practitioner Diagnosis
A practitioner begins by inspecting the keloid itself - its colour, temperature, and whether it weeps or itches. A bright red, hot, and swollen lesion that feels painful to touch and has a greasy yellow tongue coating points strongly toward Damp-Heat brewing in the Blood. The pulse is often slippery and rapid, and the person may also complain of a heavy body or loose stools.
When the keloid is intensely red, hot, and painful, but the tongue is red with little or no coating and the pulse feels large and flooding, the picture shifts to Excess-Heat blazing locally. This pattern often flares up suddenly after an injury or surgery, and the heat sensation can be quite severe.
A dark red-purple, hardened nodule with stabbing or fixed pain suggests Blood Stagnation. Here the tongue looks dusky or has purple spots, and the pulse feels wiry or choppy. The colour and quality of the scar are the main clues - the lesion is less about active heat and more about old, congealed blood that refuses to move.
If the keloid begins to ooze pus, exudate, or breaks down, Toxic-Heat is taking hold. The tongue coating becomes thick and yellow, the pulse turns slippery and rapid, and the pain intensifies. This signals deeper tissue damage and a shift from a solid mass to an active infection-like process that needs urgent attention.
In a person who looks pale, tires easily, and whose keloid forms slowly without much redness or heat, underlying Qi and Blood Deficiency is often the root. The tongue is pale with a thin coat and the pulse is weak. The body simply lacks the resources to heal cleanly, so scars overgrow in a disorganised way.

## TCM Patterns and Treatment

### Damp-Heat
*Pattern Link: [Detailed Pattern Page](/knowledge-base/patterns/damp-heat)*

- **Clinical Signs & Symptoms**: Red, raised scar with heat and intense itching, Body feels heavy and sluggish, head feels wrapped, Sticky taste in mouth, poor appetite, Loose, sticky stools that feel incomplete, Dark yellow, scanty urine
- **Worse with**: Spicy, greasy, or fried foods, Alcohol and smoking, Hot, humid weather, Emotional stress (anger, worry, upset), Scratching or irritating the scar
- **Better with**: Light, cooling diet (e.g., mung bean soup, cucumber), Keeping the scar clean and dry, Gentle exercise, Stress management, Avoiding alcohol and greasy foods
- **Why this happens (Mechanism)**:
  Damp-Heat builds up inside the body when the Spleen cannot manage fluids properly, often from a diet heavy in greasy, sweet, or spicy foods. After a skin injury, this internal Damp-Heat brews in the Blood and flows to the wound site, causing the scar to become red, swollen, hot, and intensely itchy. The Dampness makes the tissue thick and raised, while the Heat keeps the inflammation active, leading to the characteristic keloid overgrowth.
- **Tongue & Pulse**: Tongue: red body with a yellow, greasy coating that is difficult to scrape off, may be swollen with teeth marks. Pulse: soggy-rapid (Ru Shu) or slippery-rapid (Hua Shu), reflecting Dampness and Heat.
- **Triggers & Relief Explanation**: <p>Spicy, greasy, and fried foods generate Damp-Heat inside the body, directly fueling the redness, swelling, and itching of the keloid. Alcohol heats the Blood and adds to the Dampness. Hot, humid weather introduces external Dampness that combines with internal Heat, making the scar feel worse. Stress and emotional upset stagnate Liver Qi, which can transform into Heat and worsen the condition. By contrast, a cooling, light diet helps drain Damp-Heat, gentle exercise moves Qi and Blood to reduce stasis, and keeping the scar dry prevents external Dampness from entering. Managing stress calms the Liver, preventing Heat from flaring up.</p>
- **Diet & Lifestyle**: Focus on a clean, light diet: plenty of vegetables, mung beans, barley, and celery to drain Dampness. Avoid spicy, greasy, fried, and sugary foods, as well as alcohol, which all generate internal Damp-Heat. Keep the scar clean and dry, and try not to scratch it. Gentle daily exercise like walking or yoga helps move Qi and prevent stagnation, but avoid sweating excessively onto the wound. Manage stress with relaxation techniques, as emotional upset can worsen Heat.

### Blood Stagnation
*Pattern Link: [Detailed Pattern Page](/knowledge-base/patterns/blood-stagnation)*

- **Clinical Signs & Symptoms**: Stabbing, fixed pain at the scar, Pain worsens at night, Pain worsens with pressure, Hard, dark red-purple keloid
- **Worse with**: Prolonged sitting or inactivity, Cold exposure, Emotional stress (anger, worry, upset), Spicy, greasy, or fried foods, Pressure on the scar
- **Better with**: Gentle exercise, Warmth, Light massage around the scar, Eating turmeric and ginger
- **Why this happens (Mechanism)**:
  After an injury or surgery, the normal healing process can be disrupted if Qi and Blood become stuck at the site. This stagnation forms a hard, dark purple lump that feels like a crab's claw spreading outward. The stabbing pain is a classic sign of Blood Stasis - it is fixed, deep, and worse at night when the body's energy turns inward and the blood moves even more sluggishly.
- **Tongue & Pulse**: Tongue: purple or dusky body with stasis spots, distended dark sublingual veins, thin white coat. Pulse: choppy (涩 Sè) or wiry (弦 Xián).
- **Triggers & Relief Explanation**: Blood Stasis worsens with cold and inactivity because cold congeals and stagnation settles, making the blood more sluggish. Stress and anger cause Qi to stagnate, which then leads to blood stasis. Spicy, greasy foods can generate internal heat that further thickens the blood. On the other hand, gentle movement and warmth invigorate Qi and Blood, helping to break up stasis. Light massage around the scar and blood-moving foods like turmeric encourage local circulation, softening the hard lump and easing pain.
- **Diet & Lifestyle**: Eat blood-moving foods like turmeric, black fungus, dark leafy greens, and a little vinegar. Avoid cold, raw foods that congeal the blood. Gentle daily movement like walking or stretching helps keep Qi and Blood flowing. Protect the scar from further trauma and avoid tight clothing that presses on it.

### Excess-Heat
*Pattern Link: [Detailed Pattern Page](/knowledge-base/patterns/excess-heat)*

- **Clinical Signs & Symptoms**: Intense local redness and heat, Swelling and severe stabbing pain, Thirst with craving for cold drinks, Restlessness and irritability, Dark scanty urine, dry stools
- **Worse with**: Heat and sun exposure, Spicy, greasy, or fried foods, Emotional stress (anger, worry, upset), Alcohol and coffee
- **Better with**: Cool environment, Cold compress, Rest and calm, Light, cooling diet (e.g., mung bean soup, cucumber)
- **Why this happens (Mechanism)**:
  When internal Heat is excessive, it can concentrate in a healing skin wound, making the area intensely red, hot, swollen and painful. This blazing Heat agitates the Blood, causing it to stagnate and over-nourish the tissue, which drives the body to produce thick, raised scar tissue that spreads beyond the original injury.
- **Tongue & Pulse**: Tongue: red body, possibly with prickly papillae, thick dry yellow coating. Pulse: rapid (数 shuò) and forceful, often overflowing (洪 hóng) and surging.
- **Triggers & Relief Explanation**: Excess-Heat is the driving force behind this keloid pattern, so anything that adds Heat-like spicy food, alcohol, or emotional frustration-will fan the flames and worsen redness, swelling, and pain. Cooling measures, from diet to environment, directly counteract the pathogenic Heat, calm the Blood, and give the tissue a chance to stop overgrowing.
- **Diet & Lifestyle**: Avoid spicy, greasy, and deep-fried foods, as well as alcohol and coffee, which add Heat. Eat more cooling foods like cucumber, mung beans, and watermelon. Keep the keloid cool and avoid direct sun or hot compresses. Gentle, non-irritating movement helps Qi flow without overheating the body.

### Toxic-Heat
*Pattern Link: [Detailed Pattern Page](/knowledge-base/patterns/toxic-heat)*

- **Clinical Signs & Symptoms**: Pus formation or oozing exudate from the keloid, Intense, throbbing pain that worsens, Red, swollen, and hot to the touch, Restlessness and thirst for cold drinks
- **Worse with**: Spicy, greasy, or fried foods, Hot, humid weather, Emotional stress (anger, worry, upset), Tight or occlusive clothing over the lesion
- **Better with**: Cool environment, Light, cooling diet (e.g., mung bean soup, cucumber), Keeping the lesion uncovered and dry
- **Why this happens (Mechanism)**:
  When a wound becomes invaded by intense Heat and Toxin, the local Qi and Blood stagnate and putrefy. This deep, aggressive inflammation pushes beyond simple redness and swelling, causing the tissue to break down and form pus. The result is a keloid that not only overgrows but also suppurates, oozes, and throbs with worsening pain.
- **Tongue & Pulse**: Tongue: red body with prickly thorns and a dry yellow coating. Pulse: rapid (数 shuò) and forceful, often full or slippery.
- **Triggers & Relief Explanation**: Toxic-Heat is a deep, aggressive Heat that thrives on warmth and rich, stimulating foods. Cooling foods and a clean, dry environment help drain the Heat and reduce the pus formation. Emotional stress stirs up Fire, while tight clothing traps local Heat, both making the lesion angrier.
- **Diet & Lifestyle**: Avoid all spicy, greasy, and fried foods - they add fuel to the Toxic-Heat. Eat cooling, light meals with plenty of cucumber, mung beans, and chrysanthemum tea. Keep the wound clean and avoid occlusive dressings that trap heat.

### Qi and Blood Deficiency
*Pattern Link: [Detailed Pattern Page](/knowledge-base/patterns/qi-and-blood-deficiency)*

- **Clinical Signs & Symptoms**: Persistent fatigue and lack of energy, Pale or sallow complexion, Dizziness or lightheadedness, Poor appetite, Keloid is less red, less hot, but hard and slow to improve, Dry skin and pale lips
- **Worse with**: Overwork and exhaustion, Poor diet (cold, raw, irregular meals), Emotional stress (anger, worry, upset)
- **Better with**: Rest and calm, Warm, nourishing foods (soups, stews), Gentle exercise
- **Why this happens (Mechanism)**:
  When Qi and Blood are deficient, the body lacks the vital energy and nourishment needed to properly heal wounds. After a skin injury, the repair process becomes sluggish and disorganized, leading to a keloid that is less red and hot but more chronic and hardened. The systemic weakness also shows up as fatigue, pale complexion, and poor circulation, which further impairs the skin's ability to remodel and resolve the scar.
- **Tongue & Pulse**: Tongue: pale body, may be thin or have teeth marks, with a thin white coating. Pulse: fine and weak, especially in the right Guan position (Spleen/Stomach).
- **Triggers & Relief Explanation**: Qi and Blood are the foundation of healing. Rest and nourishing foods rebuild these resources, while overwork, stress, and poor diet deplete them, further impairing the body's ability to repair skin and resolve keloids.
- **Diet & Lifestyle**: Eat warm, cooked foods like soups and stews with added red dates, goji berries, and a little ginger. Avoid cold, raw foods and overexertion. Gentle daily movement such as walking or qigong helps build Qi without depleting it.

## Frequently Asked Questions
### Can acupuncture help a keloid that's already years old?
Yes, but older keloids tend to respond more slowly. Acupuncture works by improving local circulation and breaking up stagnation, so even long-standing hard scars can soften over time. The needles are usually placed around the keloid - not directly into the thickest part - to encourage healing without triggering more growth. You'll likely need a longer course of treatment, often combined with herbs, for the best results.

### Will my keloid come back after TCM treatment?
TCM aims to reduce the underlying tendency to form keloids, so the risk of recurrence is lower than with treatments that only address the scar itself. However, no method can guarantee that a keloid won't return, especially if you have a strong genetic predisposition. By continuing to follow dietary and lifestyle advice after the scar has flattened, you give yourself the best chance of staying keloid-free.

### Can I use TCM alongside steroid injections or silicone sheets?
Yes, TCM can often be safely combined with conventional treatments. Many patients use herbs and acupuncture while continuing with silicone sheets or occasional steroid injections. Always tell both your TCM practitioner and your dermatologist about all treatments you're using so they can coordinate care. There are no known serious interactions between corticosteroid injections and the herbs commonly prescribed for keloids, but full disclosure is essential.

### What foods should I avoid if I'm prone to keloids?
In general, it's wise to avoid foods that create Dampness and Heat in the body - these include greasy, fried, and very spicy dishes, as well as excessive sugar and alcohol. Dairy and rich, sweet foods can also contribute to Dampness. Your TCM practitioner can give you more specific guidance based on your pattern, but starting with a cleaner, lighter diet often helps reduce itching and redness.

### Is TCM safe for keloids during pregnancy?
Many external treatments like gentle acupuncture and some herbal ointments can be used during pregnancy, but internal herbal formulas must be carefully selected by a qualified practitioner. Some herbs that move Blood or clear Heat are contraindicated in pregnancy. If you are pregnant or breastfeeding, always inform your TCM practitioner so they can adjust your treatment plan accordingly.

### How soon will the itching and pain improve?
Itching and pain are often the first symptoms to respond to TCM. With herbs and acupuncture, many patients notice a significant reduction in discomfort within 2-3 weeks, even if the scar itself still looks the same. This early relief can be a sign that the internal imbalance is starting to correct, and the scar will gradually soften over the following months.
