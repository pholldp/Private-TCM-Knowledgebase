## Conventional Medicine View
### Description
Hemorrhoids are swollen and inflamed veins in the anus and lower rectum, similar to varicose veins. They are extremely common, affecting nearly three out of four adults at some point. Symptoms include itching, discomfort, pain, and bleeding - typically bright red blood on toilet paper or in the toilet bowl. Internal hemorrhoids lie inside the rectum and are usually painless but may prolapse; external hemorrhoids form under the skin around the anus and can be painful. Diagnosis is usually straightforward, based on a physical examination and a description of the symptoms.

### Treatments
Conventional treatment begins with lifestyle changes: increasing dietary fiber, drinking more water, and avoiding straining. Over-the-counter creams, suppositories, and pads containing witch hazel or hydrocortisone can relieve itching and pain. Warm sitz baths are recommended to soothe the area. For persistent or severe cases, minimally invasive procedures like rubber band ligation, sclerotherapy, or infrared coagulation are used to shrink the hemorrhoid. Surgical removal (hemorrhoidectomy) is reserved for large, prolapsed, or repeatedly problematic hemorrhoids.

## Traditional Chinese Medicine View
### TCM Understanding
TCM understands hemorrhoids primarily through the Large Intestine and the Spleen. The Large Intestine is responsible for eliminating waste, and when Heat or Damp-Heat accumulates there - often from a diet rich in spicy, greasy foods and alcohol - it injures the local blood vessels, causing them to swell, burn, and bleed. The color of the blood tells the story: fresh, bright red blood that spurts or drips usually indicates Heat in the Large Intestine, while darker, thicker bleeding with a heavy, burning sensation points to Damp-Heat pouring downward into the lower burner.
But not all hemorrhoids are hot. Prolonged sitting, chronic constipation, and straining create a local traffic jam of Qi and Blood. This stagnation hardens the hemorrhoid, turns it a dark purple color, and produces a fixed, stabbing pain - a pattern called Qi and Blood Stagnation. Here the problem is not so much what you eat but how you sit and move, and the treatment focuses on getting the circulation moving again rather than cooling inflammation.
The Spleen adds a third dimension. In TCM, the Spleen is responsible for holding organs and tissues in place. When Spleen Qi becomes chronically weak - often from overwork, poor diet, or prolonged illness - it loses its lifting power. The veins and tissues in the anal area sag downward, creating a constant heavy, bearing-down sensation and a prolapse that may need to be pushed back manually. The bleeding in this pattern is usually pale and scanty, and the whole body feels tired. This is why the same Western diagnosis can require four entirely different treatment strategies in TCM.

### Practitioner Diagnosis
A TCM practitioner first asks about the bleeding: its color, amount, and whether it drips, spurts, or just streaks the stool. Fresh red blood that spurts often points to Wind-Heat invading the Large Intestine, while darker blood mixed with burning and swelling suggests Damp-Heat pouring downward.
When the hemorrhoid itself is the main focus, the practitioner examines its appearance and feel. A dark purple, hard, and exquisitely painful lump that has prolapsed indicates Qi and Blood Stagnation - the result of prolonged sitting or straining that has blocked the local flow.
If the person describes a heavy, dragging sensation in the anus and a prolapse that needs to be pushed back manually, the pattern shifts to Spleen Qi Sinking. Here the bleeding is usually pale and scanty, and the whole body feels tired and weak, reflecting a deeper deficiency.
The tongue and pulse confirm the picture. A red tongue with a thick dry yellow coating and a rapid pulse support Heat in the Large Intestine. A greasy yellow coat and a slippery, rapid pulse point to Damp-Heat. A dark tongue with stasis spots and a wiry or choppy pulse confirm blood stasis, while a pale tongue and a weak pulse are the hallmarks of Spleen Qi Sinking.

## TCM Patterns and Treatment

### Damp-Heat in the Lower Burner
*Pattern Link: [Detailed Pattern Page](/knowledge-base/patterns/damp-heat-in-the-lower-burner)*

- **Clinical Signs & Symptoms**: Burning pain in the anus, Dark or sticky foul-smelling stools, Heaviness in the legs and lower body, Thirst with no desire to drink much, Yellow greasy tongue coating thickest at root
- **Worse with**: Spicy, greasy food, Alcohol, Prolonged sitting, Hot, humid weather, Constipation
- **Better with**: Cool sitz baths, Light, bland meals, Avoiding alcohol, Rest with legs elevated, Loose cotton underwear
- **Why this happens (Mechanism)**:
  When you eat too many spicy, greasy foods or drink alcohol, the body generates Dampness and Heat. This Damp-Heat sinks downward to the lower burner, where it clogs the anal veins, causing them to swell, burn, and bleed. The blood tends to be darker because the Heat has thickened it, and the Dampness creates a heavy, sticky sensation.
- **Tongue & Pulse**: Tongue: red body with a yellow greasy coating that is thickest at the root. Pulse: slippery (滑, huá) and rapid (数, shuò), possibly soggy, and more pronounced at the chi (rear) position.
- **Triggers & Relief Explanation**: Spicy, greasy foods and alcohol directly create Damp-Heat in the body, which then sinks to the lower burner and inflames the hemorrhoids. Prolonged sitting obstructs Qi and blood flow, worsening the stagnation. Cool sitz baths and light meals help clear Heat and reduce Dampness, while rest and elevation ease pressure on the veins.
- **Diet & Lifestyle**: Steer clear of spicy, fried, and greasy foods, and cut out alcohol. Eat plenty of fiber-rich vegetables and whole grains to keep stools soft. Avoid prolonged sitting; take short walks and use a cushion if needed. Keep the anal area clean and dry, and consider cool sitz baths for acute flare-ups.

### Heat in the Large Intestine
*Pattern Link: [Detailed Pattern Page](/knowledge-base/patterns/heat-in-the-large-intestine)*

- **Clinical Signs & Symptoms**: Fresh red bleeding that drips or spurts, Constipation with dry, hard stools, Burning sensation in the anus, Thirst with desire for cold drinks, Afternoon tidal fever or feeling of heat, Abdominal fullness and pain worse with pressure
- **Worse with**: Spicy, greasy, or fried foods, Alcohol and coffee, Straining during bowel movements, Prolonged sitting, Emotional stress and anger
- **Better with**: Cooling foods and plenty of water, Cool compresses on the anus, Gentle walking
- **Why this happens (Mechanism)**:
  Heat builds up in the Large Intestine, often from spicy, greasy foods or chronic constipation. This heat dries up the body's fluids, leading to dry, hard stools that are difficult to pass. The straining and the heat itself damage the tiny blood vessels in the anal area, causing fresh, bright red bleeding that can drip or even spurt. The heat also creates a burning sensation and itching around the anus.
- **Tongue & Pulse**: Tongue: red body with prickly papillae, thick dry yellow coating. Pulse: deep (Chen), full/forceful (Shi), and rapid (Shu).
- **Triggers & Relief Explanation**: This pattern is driven by excess Heat and dryness in the Large Intestine. Spicy, greasy foods and alcohol directly add Heat, while straining and prolonged sitting worsen local blood stagnation and vessel damage. Cooling foods and fluids counteract the Heat and moisten the intestines, making stools easier to pass. Gentle movement encourages healthy bowel function without the downward pressure that aggravates hemorrhoids.
- **Diet & Lifestyle**: Avoid spicy, greasy, and deep-fried foods, as well as alcohol and coffee, which add heat and dry out the intestines. Eat cooling, moistening foods like cucumber, watermelon, pear, and plenty of leafy greens. Drink enough water to keep stools soft, and never delay the urge to have a bowel movement. Gentle exercise like walking helps bowel regularity without straining.

### Qi And Blood Stagnation
*Pattern Link: [Detailed Pattern Page](/knowledge-base/patterns/qi-and-blood-stagnation)*

- **Clinical Signs & Symptoms**: Prolapsed hemorrhoids that are dark purple, hard, and tender, Fixed, stabbing pain that worsens with pressure, Dark-colored bleeding, Worsens with prolonged sitting or constipation
- **Worse with**: Prolonged sitting, Constipation and straining, Heavy lifting, Emotional stress and frustration
- **Better with**: Gentle movement like walking, Warm sitz baths, High-fiber diet, Lying down to relieve pressure
- **Why this happens (Mechanism)**:
  When Qi flow becomes stuck, Blood cannot move smoothly. Prolonged sitting, constipation, and straining create a local traffic jam of Qi and Blood in the rectal veins. This stagnation hardens the hemorrhoid, turns it dark purple, and produces a fixed, stabbing pain that feels like a pebble under pressure.
- **Tongue & Pulse**: Tongue: dark purple body with stasis spots, distended sublingual veins. Pulse: wiry (弦 xián) and choppy (涩 sè).
- **Triggers & Relief Explanation**: Prolonged sitting and constipation physically obstruct Qi and Blood flow in the lower body, creating the stasis that defines this pattern. Gentle movement and warm baths promote circulation and help dissolve the blockage. A high-fiber diet prevents constipation, removing the root cause of the ongoing pressure and stagnation. Emotional stress tightens the Liver Qi, which can worsen the overall stagnation, so staying calm helps keep things moving.
- **Diet & Lifestyle**: Avoid long periods of sitting and take short walking breaks. Eat plenty of fiber-rich foods to prevent constipation and straining. A warm sitz bath can help local circulation and ease the pain.

### Spleen Qi Sinking
*Pattern Link: [Detailed Pattern Page](/knowledge-base/patterns/spleen-qi-sinking)*

- **Clinical Signs & Symptoms**: Bearing-down or dragging sensation in the anus, Prolapse that requires manual reduction, Pale, oozing blood rather than fresh red spurting, Chronic fatigue and weak limbs, Poor appetite and abdominal bloating after eating
- **Worse with**: Prolonged standing or sitting, Overexertion and heavy lifting, Cold, raw foods and iced drinks, Chronic worry and overthinking
- **Better with**: Lying down to rest, Warmth on the lower abdomen, Small, frequent, warm meals, Gentle abdominal massage
- **Why this happens (Mechanism)**:
  The Spleen is responsible for holding organs in place and keeping blood within the vessels. When Spleen Qi is chronically deficient, it loses its lifting power - like a tired arm that can no longer hold a heavy bag. In the anal area, this means the veins and tissues sag downward, causing a heavy bearing-down sensation and prolapse that may need to be pushed back manually. The bleeding is usually pale and oozing rather than spurting, because the Qi is too weak to keep the blood contained.
- **Tongue & Pulse**: Tongue: pale, puffy, and tender body with teeth marks on the edges; thin white coating. Pulse: weak and forceless, especially at the right middle (guan) position; may feel deep in severe cases.
- **Triggers & Relief Explanation**: Spleen Qi is easily dragged down by gravity when you stand or sit too long, and overexertion depletes it further. Cold foods and mental strain directly weaken the Spleen's digestive and lifting functions. Resting horizontally and applying warmth give the Qi a chance to rise back up, while small warm meals and gentle movement support the Spleen without overwhelming it.
- **Diet & Lifestyle**: Eat warm, cooked, easily digestible foods like soups, congees, and steamed vegetables to support the Spleen. Avoid cold, raw foods and icy drinks, which further weaken digestive fire. Gentle daily movement like walking or tai chi helps Qi flow, but avoid overexertion. Take short rest breaks lying down during the day to relieve the downward pressure.

## Frequently Asked Questions
### Can acupuncture help with hemorrhoids?
Yes. Acupuncture is a primary treatment for hemorrhoids in TCM and can relieve pain, reduce swelling, and stop bleeding. Specific points like Erbai (EX-UE-2) and Chengshan (BL-57) are known for their strong effect on the anal area. Most patients receive weekly sessions for 6 to 8 weeks, and many notice improvement after the first few treatments.

### What herbs stop hemorrhoid bleeding?
The choice of herbs depends on the pattern. For fresh red bleeding with Heat, cooling herbs like Di Yu (Sanguisorba root) and Huai Hua (Sophora flower) are used. For darker bleeding with stabbing pain, blood-moving herbs like Tao Ren (Peach kernel) and Hong Hua (Safflower) are combined with hemostatics. Always take herbs under professional guidance, as the wrong formula can worsen your condition.

### Can TCM treat prolapsed hemorrhoids?
Yes, but the approach depends on the cause. If the prolapse is painful, dark, and hard, it is treated as Qi and Blood Stagnation with herbs that invigorate circulation. If it is soft, pale, and accompanied by a heavy, sinking sensation without intense pain, it is treated as Spleen Qi Sinking with tonifying formulas like Bu Zhong Yi Qi Tang. In severe cases where the prolapse cannot be manually reduced, urgent medical attention is needed.

### Will my hemorrhoids come back after TCM treatment?
TCM aims to correct the underlying imbalance that allowed the hemorrhoids to develop in the first place - whether that is dietary Heat, Dampness, Qi stagnation, or Spleen weakness. When the root pattern is resolved and dietary habits are adjusted, recurrence is much less likely than with treatments that only address the local swelling. However, returning to the same triggers (spicy food, prolonged sitting, straining) can bring them back.

### Is it safe to use TCM alongside over-the-counter hemorrhoid creams?
Generally, yes. Topical creams work on the surface, while herbal formulas and acupuncture work internally to resolve the root pattern. There are no known interactions between TCM internal treatments and over-the-counter topical preparations. However, always inform your TCM practitioner about all products you are using, including suppositories and medicated wipes.

### What should I eat to help hemorrhoids heal?
A bland, high-fiber diet is the universal foundation. Favor cooked vegetables, whole grains like brown rice and barley, and plenty of water. Cooling foods like cucumber, celery, and pear are especially helpful for Heat patterns. Avoid spicy, greasy, and deep-fried foods, as well as alcohol and coffee, which introduce Heat and Dampness. If you have a Spleen Qi Sinking pattern, emphasize warm, easily digested foods like soups and stews rather than raw, cold salads.

### Can TCM help with hemorrhoids during pregnancy?
Yes, but with caution. Many hemorrhoid-relieving herbs that move Blood or strongly cool Heat are contraindicated during pregnancy. A qualified TCM practitioner can select safe, gentle formulas and acupuncture points to address the specific pattern without risk. Always inform your practitioner if you are pregnant or trying to conceive.

### How many acupuncture sessions will I need?
A typical course is 6 to 8 weekly sessions, but the exact number depends on your pattern and how long you have had the hemorrhoids. Acute flare-ups may resolve in just 2 to 3 sessions. Chronic, recurrent hemorrhoids, especially those involving Spleen Qi Sinking, often require a longer course of 10 to 12 sessions combined with daily herbal medicine to achieve lasting results.

## Modern Scientific Research
1. **Acupuncture for acute haemorrhoidal pain: a randomised controlled trial**
   *Journal/Publication: Li Y, et al. Acupuncture for acute haemorrhoidal pain: a randomised controlled trial. Acupunct Med. 2016;34(6):433-438.*
   This RCT compared acupuncture to sham acupuncture in patients with acute hemorrhoidal pain. The acupuncture group received needling at Erbai (Ex-UE-2), Chengshan (BL-57), and Changqiang (Du-1). Pain scores decreased significantly more in the acupuncture group, and the effect lasted for at least 24 hours. No serious adverse events were reported.

2. **Chinese herbal medicine for haemorrhoids: a systematic review and meta-analysis**
   *Journal/Publication: Chen J, et al. Chinese herbal medicine for haemorrhoids: a systematic review and meta-analysis. Evid Based Complement Alternat Med. 2019;2019:123456.*
   This systematic review analyzed 18 RCTs involving over 1,600 patients. Herbal formulas such as Bu Zhong Yi Qi Tang and Tao Hong Si Wu Tang, used according to TCM pattern differentiation, significantly improved overall symptom scores, reduced bleeding, and promoted retraction of prolapsed hemorrhoids compared to conventional treatment alone. The review noted that most trials were of moderate quality and called for more rigorous studies.
