## Conventional Medicine View
### Description
In Western dermatology, dark spots on the face are most often classified as melasma (symmetrical brownish patches on the cheeks, forehead, and upper lip) or solar lentigines (age spots from sun damage). They result from an overproduction of melanin by pigment cells, triggered by UV radiation, hormonal shifts (pregnancy, birth control pills), and genetic predisposition.
Diagnosis is usually made by visual examination, and treatment focuses on fading the pigment with topical agents like hydroquinone, retinoids, or procedures such as chemical peels and laser therapy.

### Treatments
Conventional management typically involves strict sun protection and topical lightening creams containing hydroquinone, azelaic acid, or kojic acid. For resistant cases, dermatologists may recommend chemical peels, microdermabrasion, or laser treatments. Oral tranexamic acid is sometimes prescribed off-label. These treatments can reduce the appearance of spots, but recurrence is common if the underlying triggers persist.

## Traditional Chinese Medicine View
### TCM Understanding
TCM views facial dark spots as a visible sign of internal disharmony. The face is a mirror of the body's Qi and Blood. When Qi and Blood flow smoothly and the organs are balanced, the complexion is clear. Dark spots arise when Qi stagnates, Blood congeals, or the skin lacks nourishment due to deficiency. The main organ systems involved are the Liver, Spleen, and Kidneys.
The Liver governs the smooth flow of Qi. Emotional stress, frustration, or long-held anger can cause Liver Qi to stagnate. This stagnation impedes Blood circulation, leading to blood stasis that manifests as brownish or purplish spots. If stagnation persists, it deepens into Qi and Blood Stagnation, creating darker, more fixed patches.
The Spleen transforms food into Qi and Blood that nourish the skin. When digestion is weak, it produces dampness-a heavy, sticky pathogen that clogs the channels and prevents nourishment from reaching the face. The result is pale, dusty spots, often around the mouth and nose, accompanied by fatigue and bloating.
The Kidneys store the body's essence and are the root of Yin and Yang. As we age, or with chronic overwork and hormonal changes (like menopause), Kidney Yin may become deficient, leading to a subtle heat that darkens the complexion, producing irregular dark brown patches with symptoms like night sweats and dry eyes.
If Kidney Yang is deficient, the body's warming fire is weak, causing cold congealing blood and dull, dark spots with cold limbs. So the same Western diagnosis of melasma can have multiple TCM causes.

### Practitioner Diagnosis
A practitioner begins by looking at the macules themselves - their color, shape, and where they sit on the face. Brownish patches that appear or worsen with stress, especially on the cheeks and forehead, often point toward Liver Qi Stagnation. The tongue is checked for a red body with a thin coating, and the pulse tends to feel wiry, like a guitar string, confirming that emotional tension is blocking the smooth flow of Qi.
When the patches are darker, sharply bordered, and almost purplish, the picture shifts to Qi and Blood Stagnation. Here the stagnation has deepened into the blood, and the tongue often shows a dusky or purplish hue. The pulse may feel deep and thin or wiry, and the person is likely to describe a history of long-held frustration or stress that has settled into the body.
If the spots look pale, dusty, and gather around the nose, mouth, and forehead, Spleen Deficiency with Dampness becomes the leading suspicion. The practitioner will ask about digestion - bloating, loose stools, a heavy tiredness after meals - because weak digestive function fails to send clear nourishment upward. The tongue is pale with a thin, greasy coat, and the pulse feels soft and slow.
Irregular, dark brown patches that come with hot flashes, night sweats, dizziness, or ringing in the ears suggest Kidney and Liver Yin Deficiency. This pattern is common around menopause, when the body’s cooling, moistening resources run low. The tongue looks red and thin with little coating, and the pulse is deep and thin, reflecting a lack of Yin to anchor the skin’s healthy color.
When the macules are dull and dark, and the person feels perpetually cold, with low back soreness and a pale, puffy tongue, the root is likely Kidney Yang Deficiency. The pulse here is deep and weak, as if the body’s warming fire is barely flickering. This pattern is less common but important to recognize because it demands a different strategy - warming and strengthening rather than cooling or moving.

## TCM Patterns and Treatment

### Liver Qi Stagnation
*Pattern Link: [Detailed Pattern Page](/knowledge-base/patterns/liver-qi-stagnation)*

- **Clinical Signs & Symptoms**: Light to dark brown facial patches, worse with stress, Irritability, mood swings, or depression, Distension or tightness in the chest and rib sides, Frequent sighing, Breast tenderness or menstrual irregularities
- **Worse with**: Stress, anger, and frustration, Greasy and spicy foods, Excessive alcohol, Overwork and lack of rest, Premenstrual hormonal shifts
- **Better with**: Stress reduction and relaxation, Gentle movement and stretching, Chrysanthemum or rose tea, Warm compress on the abdomen, Deep breathing exercises
- **Why this happens (Mechanism)**:
  When stress, frustration, or unexpressed emotions build up, the Liver’s Qi flow becomes stuck and sluggish. This stagnation prevents the smooth circulation of Blood, causing it to pool in the tiny vessels of the face and form brownish patches. The emotional tension also generates heat that rises upward, darkening the skin and making the spots more visible.
- **Tongue & Pulse**: Tongue: normal or slightly dark body, red or slightly purple sides, thin white coat. Pulse: wiry (弦 xián), taut like a guitar string.
- **Triggers & Relief Explanation**: Stress and anger are the main triggers because they directly cause Liver Qi to stagnate, which is the root of this pattern. Greasy foods burden the Spleen and create dampness, further blocking Qi flow. Gentle movement and deep breathing help circulate Qi, while warm compresses relax the Liver channel. Chrysanthemum tea cools the Liver and clears heat that can darken the spots.
- **Diet & Lifestyle**: Make stress reduction a priority with daily gentle movement like walking, yoga, or tai chi. Sip chrysanthemum or rose tea to cool and soothe the Liver. Eat plenty of leafy greens and avoid greasy, spicy, or highly processed foods that can clog Qi flow. Deep breathing exercises, especially when feeling tense, help keep energy moving.

### Qi And Blood Stagnation
*Pattern Link: [Detailed Pattern Page](/knowledge-base/patterns/qi-and-blood-stagnation)*

- **Clinical Signs & Symptoms**: Dark, fixed macules with sharp borders, Stabbing or distending pain in the chest or ribs, Painful periods with dark, clotted blood, Irritability and emotional tension
- **Worse with**: Emotional stress and frustration, Cold and raw foods, Sedentary lifestyle, Exposure to cold
- **Better with**: Warmth, Gentle exercise like walking, Stress reduction, Warm, cooked foods
- **Why this happens (Mechanism)**:
  When emotional stress or frustration persists, Liver Qi becomes stuck and no longer moves blood smoothly. Over time, this stagnation deepens into blood stasis, blocking the flow of nourishment to the face and leaving dark, fixed patches. The macules are a visible sign of congealed blood trapped beneath the skin.
- **Tongue & Pulse**: Tongue: purple or dark body with stasis spots, distended sublingual veins, thin white coat. Pulse: wiry (弦 xián) and choppy (涩 sè).
- **Triggers & Relief Explanation**: Emotional stress causes Liver Qi to stagnate, and when Qi is stuck for long, blood cannot move, leading to stasis and dark patches. Cold contracts and congeals, further trapping blood stasis. Warmth, gentle movement, and stress reduction all encourage Qi and blood to flow freely, gradually clearing the dark macules and easing the underlying stagnation.
- **Diet & Lifestyle**: Choose warm, cooked foods and add spices like ginger and turmeric to gently move blood. Avoid cold, raw, and greasy foods that can congeal circulation. Gentle daily movement such as walking or yoga helps keep Qi and blood flowing, while managing stress with deep breathing or meditation prevents stagnation from deepening.

### Spleen Deficiency with Dampness
*Pattern Link: [Detailed Pattern Page](/knowledge-base/patterns/spleen-deficiency-with-dampness)*

- **Clinical Signs & Symptoms**: Macules on the nose, forehead, and around the mouth, Pale, dusty or sallow complexion, Abdominal bloating, loose stools, poor appetite, Heaviness in the body and limbs, fatigue, Sticky taste in the mouth, no thirst
- **Worse with**: Cold, raw foods, Dairy and greasy foods, Overeating, Damp, humid weather, Sedentary lifestyle
- **Better with**: Warm, cooked meals, Gentle exercise, Dry, warm environment, Rest after meals, Small, frequent meals
- **Why this happens (Mechanism)**:
  The Spleen transforms food into Qi and blood that nourish the skin. When digestion is weak, it fails to do this and instead produces dampness - a heavy, sticky byproduct. This dampness clogs the channels that carry nutrients upward to the face, leaving the skin undernourished and causing pale, dusty patches to appear, especially around the nose and mouth where the Spleen meridian influences the complexion.
- **Tongue & Pulse**: Tongue: pale and swollen with teeth marks on the sides; white, greasy coating; moist surface. Pulse: soggy (Ru) and slow (Huan), soft and weak, especially at the right Guan position; may be slippery (Hua) if dampness is heavy.
- **Triggers & Relief Explanation**: Cold and raw foods damage the Spleen's digestive fire, allowing dampness to accumulate. Greasy, sweet, and dairy foods directly generate dampness. Warm, cooked meals and gentle movement support the Spleen's transformation and transport functions, helping to dry dampness and clear the channels, so nutrients can reach the skin and fade the macules.
- **Diet & Lifestyle**: Eat warm, cooked foods like soups, stews, and congee. Avoid cold, raw, and greasy foods, as well as dairy and sugar, which create dampness. Gentle daily movement like walking helps the Spleen transform dampness. Keep the abdomen warm and avoid overeating.

### Kidney and Liver Yin Deficiency
*Pattern Link: [Detailed Pattern Page](/knowledge-base/patterns/kidney-and-liver-yin-deficiency)*

- **Clinical Signs & Symptoms**: Irregular dark brown macules, Dizziness and ringing in the ears, Night sweats and heat in the palms, soles, and chest, Soreness and weakness of the lower back and knees, Dry eyes with blurred vision, Insomnia or restless sleep with vivid dreams
- **Worse with**: Overwork and late nights, Spicy, greasy, or heating foods, Emotional stress and frustration, Hot and dry environments, Excessive sexual activity
- **Better with**: Cool, calm environments, Adequate sleep and rest, Nourishing Yin foods (black sesame, goji berries), Gentle exercise like Tai Chi, Stress management and emotional calm
- **Why this happens (Mechanism)**:
  Kidney Yin and Liver Yin work together to nourish the skin. When they run low-often from overwork, chronic stress, or the natural changes around menopause-the face loses its deep nourishment and a subtle deficiency heat rises. That heat darkens the complexion, producing irregular dark brown patches, while the body shows classic signs of Yin depletion like night sweats, dizziness, and a dry, hot feeling in the palms and soles.
- **Tongue & Pulse**: Tongue: red, thin body with cracks, little or no coating, dry surface. Pulse: fine (thin), rapid, often wiry; the left Chi (Kidney) and Guan (Liver) positions feel weak.
- **Triggers & Relief Explanation**: Kidney and Liver Yin are easily depleted by overwork, late nights, and emotional strain, which then allows deficiency heat to rise and darken the skin. Heating foods and hot environments add more heat, worsening the patches. Rest, cooling and moistening foods, and a calm lifestyle help conserve Yin and clear heat, so the face receives the deep nourishment it needs to stay clear and even-toned.
- **Diet & Lifestyle**: Prioritize deep sleep and avoid staying up late-Yin repairs itself at night. Eat cooling, moistening foods like black sesame, goji berries, mulberries, pears, and cucumber. Cut back on coffee, alcohol, and spicy or fried foods that dry up Yin. Gentle daily movement such as Tai Chi or walking supports circulation without overheating. Shield your face from strong sun, as external heat can intensify the deficiency heat showing on the skin.

### Kidney Yang Deficiency
*Pattern Link: [Detailed Pattern Page](/knowledge-base/patterns/kidney-yang-deficiency)*

- **Clinical Signs & Symptoms**: Dull, dark-colored macules, Feeling cold, especially lower back and legs, Frequent urination, especially at night, Fatigue and low energy, Sore, weak lower back and knees, Pale, puffy tongue with teeth marks
- **Worse with**: Cold weather or drafts, Overwork and exhaustion, Eating cold or raw foods, Standing for long periods, Excessive sexual activity
- **Better with**: Applying warmth to the lower back, Getting plenty of rest, Eating warm, cooked foods, Gentle daily walking, Keeping the feet warm
- **Why this happens (Mechanism)**:
  Kidney Yang is the body's warming fire. When it is weak, the body loses its ability to warm and move Qi and Blood upward. The face becomes poorly nourished, and the cold causes blood to congeal, leaving dull, dark patches on the skin. This is why the macules often look grayish or dark brown, not bright, and come with a feeling of coldness in the body.
- **Tongue & Pulse**: Tongue: pale, puffy, and tender with teeth marks and a wet white coating. Pulse: deep, slow, and weak, especially at the rear (Kidney) positions.
- **Triggers & Relief Explanation**: Kidney Yang deficiency is a state of internal cold and low metabolic fire. Warmth, rest, and nourishing foods help preserve and rebuild Yang, while cold, overwork, and excessive output drain it further. By supporting the body's warming function, the blood moves better and the face gradually regains its healthy color.
- **Diet & Lifestyle**: Eat warming foods like lamb, ginger, walnuts, and black beans. Avoid cold drinks, raw salads, and icy foods. Keep your lower back and feet warm, and try gentle daily movement like walking or tai chi to circulate Qi and Blood without exhausting yourself. Prioritize rest and avoid overwork.

## Frequently Asked Questions
### How long does it take to see results with TCM for dark spots?
Most people begin to see a subtle lightening within 6-8 weeks for excess patterns and 8-12 weeks for deficiency patterns. However, full results depend on the severity and the underlying imbalance. Consistency with herbs, acupuncture, and lifestyle changes is key-missing doses or sessions can delay progress.

### Can I use my regular skincare products while taking herbs?
Yes, but inform your practitioner about any active ingredients like retinoids or acids, as they can increase skin sensitivity. Some herbs may make your skin more photosensitive, so always use a broad-spectrum sunscreen. Your practitioner may recommend avoiding harsh exfoliants that could disrupt the skin barrier while you're working on internal balance.

### Will the spots come back after treatment?
If the underlying pattern is fully corrected, the spots are less likely to return. However, if the triggers-stress, poor diet, sun exposure, or hormonal shifts-reappear, the imbalance can recur. TCM aims to strengthen your constitution so you're more resilient, but maintaining a healthy lifestyle and sun protection is essential for lasting results.

### Is acupuncture safe for melasma during pregnancy?
Yes, acupuncture can be safe during pregnancy when performed by a qualified practitioner who avoids certain points. However, some herbs are contraindicated in pregnancy, so your practitioner will adjust the formula accordingly. Always inform your practitioner if you are pregnant or trying to conceive.

### Do I need to change my diet?
Diet plays a big role. Across all patterns, you'll want to avoid greasy, fried, and sugary foods that create dampness and heat. Focus on cooked whole grains, leafy greens, and foods that gently move Qi like rose tea. Specific foods are recommended based on your pattern, and your practitioner will guide you.

### Can TCM help with age spots or sun spots?
Yes, TCM can address age spots (solar lentigines) by nourishing the Kidney and Liver Yin and moving stagnant Blood. While sun damage is an external factor, internal imbalances determine how your skin responds and repairs. Treatment may lighten existing spots and help prevent new ones, but sun protection remains crucial.
