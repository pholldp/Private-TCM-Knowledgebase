## Conventional Medicine View
### Description
Uterine polyps are soft, finger-like growths that arise from the lining of the uterus (endometrium). They are common, especially during perimenopause, and while many are asymptomatic, they can cause irregular menstrual bleeding, heavy periods, spotting between cycles, or bleeding after menopause. Diagnosis is usually made by transvaginal ultrasound or hysteroscopy, and while the majority are benign, a small percentage can harbor precancerous or cancerous cells, so proper evaluation is essential.

### Treatments
Conventional management depends on symptoms and risk factors. Small, asymptomatic polyps may be watched over time. Symptomatic polyps are typically removed via hysteroscopic polypectomy, a minimally invasive procedure. In some cases, hormonal medications like progestins or GnRH agonists are used to manage bleeding, though they do not eliminate the polyp itself. Postmenopausal women or those with risk factors often have the polyp removed and biopsied to rule out malignancy.

## Traditional Chinese Medicine View
### TCM Understanding
In TCM, uterine polyps fall under the category of 'abdominal masses' (癥瘕, zhēng jiǎ) and are understood as a form of blood stasis (瘀血, yū xuè) - blood that has congealed and failed to move freely through the uterus. The Chong and Ren channels, which govern the uterus and menstrual function, become blocked by this static blood, leading to the growth of a fleshy mass. However, blood stasis is rarely the original cause; it is usually the end result of a deeper imbalance.
One common root is Qi stagnation, often driven by emotional stress and frustration. The Liver is responsible for the smooth flow of Qi and blood, and when it becomes stuck, blood circulation slows, eventually congealing in the lower burner. Another is Qi deficiency - when the body's vital energy is too weak to push blood along, it pools and stagnates. Cold can also invade the uterus, constricting the vessels and freezing the blood into stasis, while heat can scorch the blood, making it thick and sluggish. In some women, poor fluid metabolism creates phlegm-dampness, which binds with blood stasis to form the polyp.
This is why a single Western diagnosis corresponds to multiple TCM patterns. The tongue and pulse reveal which root imbalance is at play: a purple tongue with a greasy coat points to phlegm and stasis, a pale puffy tongue with teeth marks suggests Qi deficiency, and a wiry pulse with a dark red tongue indicates Qi stagnation. Treatment is never just about 'shrinking the polyp'; it is about restoring the free flow of Qi and blood, warming what is cold, clearing what is hot, and strengthening what is weak, so that the uterus no longer provides a hospitable environment for growth.

### Practitioner Diagnosis
A TCM practitioner first listens to the details of your menstrual cycle-the color, consistency, and timing of bleeding, any pain, and associated discharge. The tongue and pulse reveal deeper patterns: a sticky coating points to phlegm, a purple hue suggests blood stasis, and a pale, puffy tongue indicates Qi deficiency.
When emotional stress is prominent-irritability, breast tenderness, and a sensation of distension in the lower abdomen-the practitioner suspects Qi and Blood Stagnation. The tongue often appears red or dark red with possible purple spots, and the pulse is wiry. Menstrual flow is dark with clots, and pain may worsen with stress. This is a very common presentation.
If the tongue is purple with a thick, greasy coating and the pulse feels choppy and slippery, the likely culprit is Blood Stagnation and Phlegm binding in the uterus. Menstrual blood tends to be dark, clotted, and sticky, and there may be a persistent thick, sticky vaginal discharge. This pattern often arises when poor circulation and fluid metabolism combine.
If exhaustion is a major complaint, along with a pale, enlarged tongue that has teeth marks and a weak pulse, Qi Deficiency is failing to move blood, leading to Blood Stagnation. Bleeding may be heavy or prolonged, but the blood still contains clots; the pain is dull rather than sharp, and the woman feels drained.
A deep, slow pulse and a purplish tongue point to Cold congealing blood in the lower burner. Menstrual blood is dark and clotted, and the lower abdomen feels cold and painful, often relieved by warmth. This pattern is less common but important to identify because warming therapies are needed.
When there are signs of heat-a red tongue with a yellow, greasy coating and a rapid, slippery pulse-the pattern is Heat and Blood Stagnation. Menstrual flow may be bright or dark red, often heavy, with a foul-smelling discharge. Inflammation and a sensation of pelvic heat are typical.

## TCM Patterns and Treatment

### Qi And Blood Stagnation
*Pattern Link: [Detailed Pattern Page](/knowledge-base/patterns/qi-and-blood-stagnation)*

- **Clinical Signs & Symptoms**: Stabbing, fixed lower abdominal pain, Menstrual blood dark purple with clots, Breast distension before periods, Irritability and mood swings, Pain worsens with pressure
- **Worse with**: Emotional stress and anger, Cold or raw foods and drinks, Sedentary lifestyle or prolonged sitting, Premenstrual phase
- **Better with**: Gentle movement or walking, Abdominal warmth or warm compress, Rest and stress reduction, Warm, cooked meals
- **Why this happens (Mechanism)**:
  When stress, frustration, or emotional tension persist, Liver Qi becomes stuck and fails to keep blood moving smoothly. Over time, this stagnant Qi causes blood to congeal in the uterus, forming fixed masses that we recognize as polyps.
The obstruction creates a cycle: the more the blood pools, the more the Qi struggles to flow, leading to the stabbing pain, dark clotted periods, and breast distension typical of this pattern.
- **Tongue & Pulse**: Tongue: dark purple body with stasis spots, distended sublingual veins. Pulse: wiry (弦 xián) and choppy (涩 sè).
- **Triggers & Relief Explanation**: Emotional stress directly knots Liver Qi, which is the root cause of blood stasis in this pattern, so calming the mind and expressing feelings eases the stagnation. Cold congeals and slows circulation, while warmth and movement encourage Qi and blood to flow freely, reducing pain and preventing further accumulation. Premenstrually, Qi and blood naturally converge in the uterus, which can intensify the blockage if stagnation is already present.
- **Diet & Lifestyle**: Prioritize warm, cooked foods and avoid icy drinks or raw salads that can congeal blood. Gentle daily movement-like walking or tai chi-helps keep Qi and blood flowing. Make space to process emotions rather than bottling them up, as unresolved stress is a key driver of this pattern.

### Blood Stagnation and Phlegm in the Uterus
*Pattern Link: [Detailed Pattern Page](/knowledge-base/patterns/blood-stagnation-and-phlegm-in-the-uterus)*

- **Clinical Signs & Symptoms**: Menstrual blood dark with clots, Copious thick or sticky vaginal discharge, Fixed, stabbing lower-abdominal pain, Palpable lump or sensation of heaviness in the lower abdomen, Pain not relieved by warmth alone, Irregular or prolonged menstrual bleeding
- **Worse with**: Cold and damp environments, Greasy, fried, or spicy foods, Sedentary lifestyle or prolonged sitting, Emotional stress and anger
- **Better with**: Gentle movement or walking, Warm, cooked meals, Rest and stress reduction
- **Why this happens (Mechanism)**:
  When Qi and blood do not flow freely in the uterus, fluids can congeal into phlegm-dampness while blood stagnates and clots. These two sticky pathological products bind together over time, forming the fleshy growths recognized as polyps.
This dual blockage disrupts the normal shedding of the uterine lining, leading to heavy, clotted periods and a sense of heaviness in the lower abdomen. The phlegm component also creates the characteristic thick, sticky vaginal discharge.
- **Tongue & Pulse**: Tongue: purple body with stasis spots, swollen with teeth marks, and a white greasy coating; sublingual veins distended. Pulse: choppy (Se) and slippery (Hua), often wiry at the left Guan position.
- **Triggers & Relief Explanation**: Cold and dampness congeal fluids into phlegm and slow blood flow, making the mass more stubborn. Greasy and heavy foods directly feed phlegm production, while a sedentary lifestyle causes Qi to stagnate, worsening blood stasis. Gentle movement, warm nourishment, and calm emotions keep Qi and blood moving, preventing the two pathogenic products from binding together.
- **Diet & Lifestyle**: Keep meals warm, light, and cooked; avoid dairy, fried foods, and excess raw or cold items that create phlegm. Gentle daily movement like walking or yoga helps Qi and blood circulate, preventing stasis. Manage stress with regular relaxation, as emotional stagnation can worsen the blockage.

### Qi Deficiency causing Blood Stagnation
*Pattern Link: [Detailed Pattern Page](/knowledge-base/patterns/qi-deficiency-causing-blood-stagnation)*

- **Clinical Signs & Symptoms**: Fixed stabbing lower abdominal pain that worsens with pressure, Fatigue and lack of strength, Pale face with a greyish or dusky tinge, Heavy or prolonged menstrual bleeding with dark clots, Shortness of breath and reluctance to speak
- **Worse with**: Overwork and fatigue, Cold or raw foods and drinks, Emotional stress and anger, Sedentary lifestyle or prolonged sitting
- **Better with**: Rest and stress reduction, Warm, cooked meals, Gentle movement or walking, Abdominal warmth or warm compress
- **Why this happens (Mechanism)**:
  Qi is the body's engine for moving blood. When Qi becomes deficient, it lacks the power to keep blood flowing smoothly, especially in the uterus where circulation is already delicate. Over time, the sluggish blood congeals into stasis, forming masses like polyps. This stasis causes fixed, stabbing pelvic pain and dark, clotted menstrual flow, while the underlying Qi deficiency leaves you feeling exhausted and washed-out.
- **Tongue & Pulse**: Tongue: pale body with a purplish tinge, possible stasis spots, teeth marks, thin white coat. Pulse: deep (Chen), weak (Ruo), and choppy (Se) - rough and hesitant under the fingers.
- **Triggers & Relief Explanation**: Qi is generated from rest and nourishing food, so pushing through exhaustion depletes it further, leaving blood even more stagnant. Cold foods congeal blood and dampen the digestive fire that produces Qi. Warmth, gentle movement, and rest all support the body's ability to generate Qi and keep blood moving, which is the core deficit in this pattern.
- **Diet & Lifestyle**: Prioritise rest and avoid pushing through exhaustion. Eat warm, cooked foods like congee, stews, and small amounts of lean meat or bone broth to gently build Qi. Steer clear of raw, cold, and greasy foods that can further slow circulation and weaken digestion.

### Blood Stagnation in the Lower Burner  caused by Cold
*Pattern Link: [Detailed Pattern Page](/knowledge-base/patterns/blood-stagnation-in-the-lower-burner-caused-by-cold)*

- **Clinical Signs & Symptoms**: Fixed, stabbing cold pain in the lower abdomen, Dark or purplish menstrual blood with clots, Pain and symptoms improve with warmth, Cold sensation in the lower belly or limbs, Scanty or delayed periods
- **Worse with**: Cold weather or drafts, Cold or raw foods and drinks, Emotional stress and anger, Overwork and fatigue, Prolonged sitting on cold surfaces
- **Better with**: Abdominal warmth or warm compress, Ginger or cinnamon tea, Moxibustion, Rest and stress reduction, Warm, cooked meals
- **Why this happens (Mechanism)**:
  Cold has a freezing, constricting nature. When cold invades the lower burner, it tightens the blood vessels and slows circulation, causing blood to congeal and stagnate in the uterus. Over time, this static blood accumulates into a mass, forming a polyp. The menstrual blood turns dark and clotted because cold freezes the flow, and the pain is fixed and stabbing because stasis blocks the channels.
- **Tongue & Pulse**: Tongue: pale-purple or bluish-purple body with a white slippery coat, possible stasis spots, and engorged sublingual veins. Pulse: deep (Chen), tight (Jin), and choppy (Se).
- **Triggers & Relief Explanation**: Cold constricts and freezes, causing blood to congeal. Warmth relaxes the channels and promotes circulation, which is why a hot water bottle or warm drinks bring relief. Cold exposure, whether from weather or diet, directly worsens the stasis. Emotional stress can cause Qi stagnation, which further impedes blood flow, compounding the cold stasis.
- **Diet & Lifestyle**: Keep the lower abdomen warm; avoid sitting on cold surfaces. Eat warming, cooked foods like stews, ginger, cinnamon, and black beans. Steer clear of raw, cold, and frozen foods and drinks. Gentle daily movement such as walking or yoga helps move blood, but overexertion can worsen the cold. Moxibustion at home on the lower abdomen can be very helpful.

### Heat and Blood Stagnation in the Lower Burner
*Pattern Link: [Detailed Pattern Page](/knowledge-base/patterns/heat-and-blood-stagnation-in-the-lower-burner)*

- **Clinical Signs & Symptoms**: Fixed sharp or stabbing lower abdominal pain, Dark, clotted menstrual blood, Irritability, restlessness, or manic behaviour, Fever or feeling of heat that worsens at night, Dry mouth and throat, Reddish-purple tongue with stasis spots
- **Worse with**: Greasy, fried, or spicy foods, Emotional stress and anger, Hot and humid weather, Overwork and fatigue, Alcohol
- **Better with**: Cooling foods, Gentle movement or walking, Rest and stress reduction, Cool environment
- **Why this happens (Mechanism)**:
  When heat builds in the lower burner - often from emotional stress, chronic inflammation, or a diet rich in spicy and greasy foods - it can scorch the blood. This scorched blood becomes thick and sluggish, forming stasis that congeals in the uterus. Over time, this heat-driven stasis accumulates into a mass, creating the polyp. The heat also stirs up restlessness and irritability, and can make any bleeding dark and clotted.
- **Tongue & Pulse**: Tongue: reddish-purple body with stasis spots, distended sublingual veins, and a dry yellow coating. Pulse: deep, choppy (Se), and rapid (Shu).
- **Triggers & Relief Explanation**: Heat and blood stasis are aggravated by anything that adds more heat or slows circulation. Spicy foods, alcohol, and hot weather pour fuel on the fire, while emotional stress stirs up Liver Qi, which can turn into heat. Overwork depletes the body and allows stasis to settle. Cooling foods, rest, and gentle movement clear the heat and gently nudge the blood back into motion, helping the body dissolve the mass.
- **Diet & Lifestyle**: Avoid spicy, greasy, and fried foods that generate internal heat. Eat cooling, blood-nourishing foods like cucumber, celery, and mung beans. Gentle daily movement like walking helps keep blood flowing. Manage emotional stress through breathwork or meditation, as frustration can fan the heat.

## Frequently Asked Questions
### Can TCM actually shrink uterine polyps?
Yes, in many cases TCM can reduce the size of uterine polyps or slow their growth. The approach focuses on moving stagnant blood and correcting the underlying imbalance that allowed the polyp to form. However, results vary depending on the size and nature of the polyp, and not all polyps respond completely. Some women find their polyps disappear, while others see significant improvement in symptoms like bleeding and pain even if the polyp persists. It's important to have regular gynecological monitoring to track changes.

### How long does TCM treatment take to work?
Most women notice a change in their menstrual symptoms - lighter bleeding, less clotting, reduced pain - within 2 to 3 menstrual cycles of consistent treatment. For the polyp itself to shrink, it may take 4 to 6 months or longer. Treatment is typically weekly acupuncture sessions combined with daily customized herbal formulas. The timeline depends on the underlying pattern: excess conditions often respond faster, while deficiency patterns need more time to rebuild the body's reserves.

### Can I continue seeing my gynecologist while doing TCM?
Absolutely - and you should. TCM and conventional care work well together. Your gynecologist can monitor the polyp's size via ultrasound and perform a biopsy if needed, while your TCM practitioner addresses the root imbalance. If you have had a polypectomy, TCM can support recovery and help reduce the risk of recurrence. Always keep both providers informed of all treatments you are receiving.

### Will the polyps come back after TCM treatment?
TCM aims to reduce the risk of recurrence by changing the internal environment that allowed the polyp to grow in the first place. By moving stagnant blood, regulating hormones from a TCM perspective, and strengthening the body's Qi, the uterus becomes less hospitable to new growths. While no treatment can guarantee zero recurrence, many women find that combining TCM with healthy lifestyle habits leads to longer polyp-free intervals compared to surgery alone.

### Is TCM safe if I'm trying to conceive?
Yes, TCM is generally safe and often used to support fertility. However, if you are actively trying to conceive or might be pregnant, you must tell your TCM practitioner, as some blood-moving herbs are contraindicated during pregnancy or the luteal phase. Your practitioner will adjust the formula accordingly, focusing on gentle blood regulation and uterine health without risking a potential pregnancy.

### Do I need to change my diet?
Diet plays a supportive role. Across all patterns, it's wise to avoid cold, raw foods and icy drinks, which can constrict circulation and worsen blood stasis. Greasy, fried, and heavily spiced foods can create dampness and heat, further complicating the picture. Instead, emphasize warm, cooked meals, plenty of leafy greens, and moderate amounts of lean protein. Your practitioner may offer more specific guidance based on your individual pattern.

### What if I have very heavy bleeding - can TCM help?
Yes, TCM has effective strategies for managing heavy menstrual bleeding, but it's crucial to first rule out emergency causes. If you are soaking through a pad or tampon every hour for several hours, feel dizzy or faint, or have severe pain, seek urgent medical care. Once serious conditions are excluded, TCM can use herbs and acupuncture to stop acute bleeding and then address the underlying stasis or deficiency that caused it.
