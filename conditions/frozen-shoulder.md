## Conventional Medicine View
### Description
Frozen shoulder, or adhesive capsulitis, is a condition where the connective tissue surrounding the shoulder joint thickens and tightens, causing pain and progressive loss of movement. It typically progresses through three stages: a painful freezing stage where movement becomes increasingly limited, a frozen stage where stiffness peaks but pain may diminish, and a thawing stage where range of motion gradually returns. The exact cause is often unknown, though it is more common in people with diabetes, thyroid disorders, or after a period of immobilization following injury or surgery. Diagnosis is usually made through physical examination and medical history, with imaging sometimes used to rule out other causes.

### Treatments
Conventional management focuses on pain relief and restoring mobility. This often begins with non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs (NSAIDs) and physical therapy to gently stretch the joint capsule. If progress stalls, corticosteroid injections may reduce inflammation and pain, allowing for more effective exercise. For persistent cases, hydrodilatation (injecting sterile fluid to expand the capsule), manipulation under anesthesia, or arthroscopic surgery to release tight tissue may be considered. Treatment can be lengthy, with full recovery sometimes taking one to three years even with intervention.

## Traditional Chinese Medicine View
### TCM Understanding
TCM understands frozen shoulder as a form of Bi syndrome (painful obstruction), where the flow of Qi and Blood through the shoulder's channels and collaterals is blocked. The Liver governs the sinews and ensures smooth movement, while the Kidneys nourish the bones and joints. When external pathogens like Wind, Cold, Dampness, or Heat invade - often because the body's defensive Qi is weakened by stress, fatigue, or underlying deficiency - they lodge in the shoulder and create stagnation. This stagnation is the direct source of pain and stiffness.
The specific nature of the blockage defines the pattern. For example, if an old injury leads to poor local circulation, Blood Stagnation develops, causing a fixed, stabbing pain that worsens at night when the body is still. If Dampness and Heat combine - perhaps from a humid climate or a diet rich in greasy, spicy foods - the joint becomes hot, red, and swollen, indicating an inflammatory obstruction. When the body's internal furnace (Yang) is weak, Cold and Dampness can settle deep in the shoulder, producing a heavy, cold ache that feels better with warmth.
Other patterns arise from internal dysfunction rather than external invasion. A weak Spleen can fail to transform fluids, allowing Dampness to congeal into sticky Phlegm that gums up the joint, making it feel heavy and locked. And as we age, the natural decline of Liver Blood and Kidney Essence leaves the sinews and bones undernourished, causing a dull, chronic ache and progressive stiffness that worsens with overuse and cold weather. This is why one person's frozen shoulder may feel hot and throbbing while another's feels cold and achy - they are different TCM patterns requiring different treatments.

### Practitioner Diagnosis
A TCM practitioner first asks about the nature of the pain. If the pain is fixed, stabbing, and significantly worse at night, it points toward blood stagnation. The tongue may appear dark purple with stasis spots, and the pulse often feels choppy or wiry. This pattern often develops after a long-standing shoulder problem where poor circulation has led to local blood stasis.
When the shoulder area feels hot, looks red and swollen, and the pain is intense and possibly throbbing, damp heat is likely obstructing the channels. The tongue is red with a yellow, greasy coating, and the pulse is rapid and slippery. This pattern reflects an acute inflammatory stage where dampness and heat combine, often triggered by external pathogens or internal imbalances.
If the shoulder feels heavy and swollen rather than acutely painful, with a sensation of something being stuck and movement being restricted, phlegm obstructing the channels is suspected. The tongue is often puffy with a greasy coating, and the pulse is slippery. This arises when dampness congeals into phlegm, creating a thick, sticky obstruction that limits joint mobility.
A shoulder that feels cold, heavy, and painful, worsening in cold or damp weather, suggests yang deficiency failing to warm the channels, allowing cold-damp to lodge deeply. The person often feels generally cold and fatigued. The tongue is pale with a thin white coat, and the pulse is deep, thin, or tight. This pattern indicates an internal weakness that fails to expel external pathogens.
In middle-aged and older adults, gradual onset of chronic aching, weakness, and stiffness without obvious redness or heat points to liver and kidney deficiency. The sinews and bones are undernourished, leading to a dull ache that improves with rest. The tongue is pale with a thin coat, and the pulse is weak. This pattern reflects the natural decline of vital substances that support joint health.

## TCM Patterns and Treatment

### Blood Stagnation
*Pattern Link: [Detailed Pattern Page](/knowledge-base/patterns/blood-stagnation)*

- **Clinical Signs & Symptoms**: Fixed stabbing pain, Pain worsens at night, Dark or purplish tongue with stasis spots, Pain gets worse with pressure, Dark menstrual blood with clots (in women)
- **Worse with**: Cold and damp weather, Prolonged inactivity, Emotional stress, Spicy, greasy foods
- **Better with**: Gentle stretching and movement, Applying warmth (heat pack, warm shower), Warm cooked meals, Adequate rest
- **Why this happens (Mechanism)**:
  When a shoulder injury lingers or pain persists, the local circulation of Qi and Blood slows down and eventually gets stuck. This creates a fixed, stabbing pain because the stagnant blood blocks the channels that nourish the shoulder joint. The pain typically worsens at night because the body is still and circulation naturally slows, allowing the stagnation to become more pronounced.
- **Tongue & Pulse**: Tongue: dark purple body, possible stasis spots, distended dark sublingual veins, thin white coat. Pulse: choppy (涩 sè) or wiry (弦 xián).
- **Triggers & Relief Explanation**: Cold and damp environments constrict blood vessels and make stagnant blood even harder to move, so warmth and heat are immediate reliefs. Gentle movement acts like a pump for the channels, preventing blood from pooling and clotting. Stress knots the Liver Qi, which is the engine that moves Blood; when Qi gets stuck, Blood follows, so calming the mind helps the shoulder. Spicy and greasy foods generate internal heat and dampness that can further gum up the channels, while warm, simple meals support smooth circulation.
- **Diet & Lifestyle**: Eat warm, cooked foods and avoid icy drinks and raw salads, which can congeal Blood. Gentle, consistent shoulder movement-like pendulum swings or wall climbs-keeps blood flowing without straining the joint. A warm compress or hot shower over the shoulder in the morning can ease the night-time stiffness.

### Painful Obstruction due to Damp Heat in Channels
*Pattern Link: [Detailed Pattern Page](/knowledge-base/patterns/painful-obstruction-due-to-damp-heat-in-channels)*

- **Clinical Signs & Symptoms**: Shoulder is red, swollen, and hot to touch, Pain worsens with warmth, improves with cold, Heavy, aching sensation in the shoulder, Thirst and irritability, Fever or sensation of bodily heat
- **Worse with**: Hot, humid weather, Spicy, greasy foods, Alcohol, Heat packs or warm compress, Pressure on the shoulder
- **Better with**: Cold compresses, Cooling foods (cucumber, watermelon), Adequate rest, Light, bland diet
- **Why this happens (Mechanism)**:
  Dampness and heat are external pathogens that can invade the body, especially in humid, hot weather or from a diet rich in greasy, spicy foods. When they combine and lodge in the shoulder channels, they create inflammation, causing the joint to become red, swollen, and hot to the touch. The obstruction of Qi and blood leads to severe pain and stiffness, while the dampness adds a heavy, aching sensation. Because heat is involved, the pain typically worsens with warmth and improves with cold.
- **Tongue & Pulse**: Tongue: red body, possibly slightly swollen, with a yellow greasy coating. Pulse: slippery (滑 huá) and rapid (数 shuò), may feel soggy or wiry at the middle position.
- **Triggers & Relief Explanation**: Damp-heat is an excess condition where pathogenic heat and dampness have lodged in the channels. Heat and humidity worsen the condition by fueling the pathogens, while cold and cooling foods clear heat and reduce inflammation. Greasy, spicy foods generate more internal dampness and heat, so avoiding them helps starve the pathogens. Rest prevents further irritation of the inflamed tissues, allowing the body to resolve the obstruction.
- **Diet & Lifestyle**: Avoid greasy, fried, spicy, and sugary foods, as well as alcohol, which create dampness and heat. Eat cooling, light foods like cucumber, watermelon, mung beans, and barley. Apply cold compresses to the shoulder during acute pain, and avoid heat packs. Gentle range-of-motion exercises can help, but stop if pain increases.

### Phlegm in the Channels joints and muscles
*Pattern Link: [Detailed Pattern Page](/knowledge-base/patterns/phlegm-in-the-channels-joints-and-muscles)*

- **Clinical Signs & Symptoms**: Fixed stabbing pain, Heavy, numb sensation in the shoulder, Swelling that feels hard to the touch, Purplish discolouration around the joint, Chest tightness and excessive phlegm
- **Worse with**: Damp or humid weather, Heavy, greasy, or cold foods, Prolonged inactivity
- **Better with**: Gentle stretching and movement, Warm, dry weather, Warm cooked meals
- **Why this happens (Mechanism)**:
  When the body fails to transform dampness, it can congeal into a thicker, stickier substance called Phlegm. This Phlegm settles into the shoulder's channels and collaterals, gumming up the flow of Qi and Blood. The result is a heavy, swollen sensation and deep, fixed pain that makes the joint feel stuck and difficult to move.
- **Tongue & Pulse**: Tongue: purple body with stasis spots, white greasy coating, distended sublingual veins. Pulse: wiry (弦 xián) and choppy (涩 sè), or wiry and slippery (滑 huá) if Phlegm predominates.
- **Triggers & Relief Explanation**: Damp, heavy foods and a sedentary lifestyle generate the internal Phlegm and Dampness that congeal in the channels, making the shoulder feel heavier and stiffer. Movement and warmth help disperse the obstruction, while light, warm eating stops the supply of new Phlegm, allowing the channels to gradually clear.
- **Diet & Lifestyle**: Avoid dairy, greasy fried foods, and cold raw items that create dampness and phlegm. Eat warm, light meals with plenty of cooked vegetables and a little pungent warmth from ginger or black pepper. Gentle daily shoulder circles and walks keep Qi moving so phlegm doesn't settle.

### Yang Deficiency with Cold-Damp
*Pattern Link: [Detailed Pattern Page](/knowledge-base/patterns/yang-deficiency-with-cold-damp)*

- **Clinical Signs & Symptoms**: Deep, dull, aching shoulder pain that feels cold, Stiffness and pain worsen in cold, damp weather, Cold hands and feet, general aversion to cold, Fatigue, desire to lie down, loose stools, Pale complexion, heavy sensation in the limbs
- **Worse with**: Cold and damp weather, Prolonged inactivity, Cold and raw foods, Overexertion, Drafts or air conditioning
- **Better with**: Applying warmth (heat pack, warm shower), Gentle stretching and movement, Moxibustion on the shoulder, Warm, dry weather, Eating warming foods and spices
- **Why this happens (Mechanism)**:
  In this pattern, the body's internal yang-the warming, activating force-is depleted, so it cannot properly warm the channels and tissues. Cold and dampness from the environment then easily invade and lodge deep in the shoulder, blocking the flow of Qi and Blood. The lack of warmth and the heavy, stagnant nature of dampness combine to produce a shoulder that feels cold, heavy, and stiff, with a deep ache that worsens whenever the weather turns cold or damp.
- **Tongue & Pulse**: Tongue: pale and swollen with teeth marks, coated white and greasy-moist. Pulse: deep, slow, and may be minute or soggy.
- **Triggers & Relief Explanation**: Cold and dampness are the main external aggressors in this pattern; they further block the already weakened yang and cause Qi and Blood to stagnate in the shoulder. Warmth and gentle movement help to disperse cold and dampness, while rest conserves the body's limited yang energy. Warming foods and moxibustion add yang directly, counteracting the internal deficiency that allowed the cold-damp to lodge in the first place.
- **Diet & Lifestyle**: Keep the shoulder covered and warm at all times, especially in cold or damp weather; a silk or wool scarf can help. Use a heat pack or gentle moxibustion on the shoulder and lower back daily. Eat warming foods like ginger, cinnamon, lamb, and onions, and avoid raw, cold, or greasy foods that create dampness. Gentle, consistent movement such as Tai Chi or slow shoulder circles maintains mobility without straining depleted yang.

### Painful Obstruction with Liver and Kidney Deficiency
*Pattern Link: [Detailed Pattern Page](/knowledge-base/patterns/painful-obstruction-with-liver-and-kidney-deficiency)*

- **Clinical Signs & Symptoms**: Dull, chronic aching pain, Stiffness and pain worsen in cold, damp weather, Lower back and knee soreness and weakness, Fatigue and lack of stamina, Pale or dull complexion
- **Worse with**: Cold and damp weather, Overexertion, Prolonged inactivity, Cold and raw foods
- **Better with**: Applying warmth (heat pack, warm shower), Gentle stretching and movement, Adequate rest, Nourishing, warm foods, Moxibustion on the shoulder
- **Why this happens (Mechanism)**:
  As we age, Liver Blood and Kidney Essence naturally decline. The Liver nourishes the sinews, and the Kidneys govern the bones. When these reserves run low, the shoulder joint loses its lubrication and flexibility, leading to a dull, chronic ache and progressive stiffness. Because the body's internal warmth is diminished, cold and damp easily invade and worsen the condition, leaving the shoulder feeling weak and heavy.
- **Tongue & Pulse**: Tongue: pale, thin body with a thin white coating. Pulse: fine, weak, and slow, especially weak at the rear (Kidney) positions.
- **Triggers & Relief Explanation**: Warmth and gentle movement help circulate Qi and Blood through the depleted channels, easing stiffness and pain. Rest and nourishing foods replenish the Liver and Kidney, providing the raw materials needed to support the sinews and bones. Cold and damp environments further obstruct the already weak flow, while overexertion drains the limited reserves of Qi and Blood, making symptoms worse.
- **Diet & Lifestyle**: Nourish the Liver and Kidneys with warm, cooked foods like bone broths, black sesame, walnuts, and goji berries. Avoid cold, raw foods and iced drinks. Gentle, consistent movement such as tai chi or qigong helps maintain flexibility without straining the joint. Keep the shoulder warm, especially in cold or damp weather, and consider using a heat pack or moxibustion.

## Frequently Asked Questions
### Can acupuncture really unfreeze a frozen shoulder?
Yes, acupuncture is one of the most effective TCM tools for frozen shoulder. By inserting fine needles into local points like Jianyu (LI-15) and distal points like Yanglingquan (GB-34), it stimulates blood flow, releases muscle spasms, and signals the body to reduce inflammation. Many patients feel an immediate loosening, though lasting improvement builds over a series of treatments. Combining acupuncture with herbal medicine and gentle movement often yields the best results.

### How many acupuncture sessions will I need?
For most people, 1-2 sessions per week for 6-12 weeks is a typical course. Acute, excess-type patterns may improve faster, while chronic deficiency patterns require longer. Your practitioner will reassess your progress regularly and adjust the frequency. Consistency is key - missing sessions can slow momentum.

### Will I need to change my diet?
Dietary adjustments support your treatment by reducing internal dampness and inflammation. You'll generally be advised to avoid cold, raw, greasy, and sugary foods, which can create dampness and phlegm that stiffen the joint. Instead, favor warm, cooked meals, ginger, turmeric, and foods that nourish the Liver and Kidneys, like black sesame, walnuts, and goji berries. Specific recommendations depend on your pattern.

### Can TCM help if I've already had steroid injections?
Absolutely. Steroid injections can temporarily reduce inflammation, but they don't correct the underlying stagnation or deficiency. TCM can be safely started after an injection to address the root cause and prevent recurrence. Just inform your acupuncturist and herbalist about any recent injections or medications so they can tailor your treatment accordingly.

### Is it safe to combine TCM with physical therapy?
Yes, TCM and physical therapy work very well together. Acupuncture can reduce pain enough to make stretching exercises more tolerable, while herbs can speed healing between sessions. Coordinate with both practitioners - ideally, schedule acupuncture before physical therapy to maximize your range of motion during exercises.

### What if my shoulder pain is from an old injury?
Old injuries often lead to Blood Stagnation, a common TCM pattern for frozen shoulder. The stuck blood creates a fixed, stabbing pain that worsens at night. TCM excels at moving stagnant blood with herbs like Tao Ren and Hong Hua and acupuncture points like Geshu (BL-17). Even years after the original injury, reactivating circulation can bring significant relief and improved mobility.
