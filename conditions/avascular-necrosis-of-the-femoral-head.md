## Conventional Medicine View
### Description
Avascular necrosis (osteonecrosis) of the femoral head occurs when the blood supply to the ball of the hip joint is interrupted. Without oxygen and nutrients, bone tissue begins to die, leading to tiny fractures and eventually the collapse of the femoral head. The condition typically causes pain in the groin, thigh, or buttock that worsens with weight-bearing and can limit range of motion.
Diagnosis is confirmed by MRI, which can detect early changes before they appear on X-ray. Common triggers include hip trauma, long-term corticosteroid use, and excessive alcohol consumption, though some cases have no clear cause.

### Treatments
Treatment depends on the stage of the disease. In early stages, before collapse, options include pain management, physical therapy, and surgical procedures like core decompression or bone grafting to stimulate new blood vessel growth. Once the femoral head has collapsed, total hip replacement is often necessary to relieve pain and restore mobility. Medications such as bisphosphonates may be used to slow bone loss, but their effectiveness is limited.

## Traditional Chinese Medicine View
### TCM Understanding
In TCM, the health of bone depends on three organ systems. The Kidneys store essence (Jing), which produces marrow and nourishes bone. The Liver stores blood and ensures its smooth flow to all tissues. The Spleen transforms food into Qi and blood, providing the raw material for repair. When any of these systems is weakened - by overwork, poor diet, aging, or the toxic effects of alcohol and steroids - the femoral head becomes malnourished and vulnerable to damage.
At the heart of avascular necrosis is blood stasis. Whether caused by a traumatic injury that leaves congealed blood, by the heat and toxicity of long-term corticosteroid use, or by the sluggish circulation that comes with deep deficiency, stagnant blood blocks the tiny vessels and channels that feed the hip joint. Without a steady supply of fresh blood, bone cells die and the structural integrity of the femoral head breaks down. This is why TCM always includes herbs and acupuncture to invigorate blood and resolve stasis, no matter the underlying pattern.
But why did the stasis occur? That’s where the patterns diverge. In a younger person with a history of hip trauma or heavy drinking, the pattern is often Qi and Blood Stagnation - a pure excess condition where the body’s flow is mechanically blocked. In someone who has taken steroids for years, Blood Stagnation is often mixed with Kidney and Liver deficiency, because the medication has drained the body’s reserves. When there is heaviness, swelling, and a greasy tongue, Phlegm-Damp has accumulated in the joint. And in older or frail patients, the root may be a simple lack of Qi and blood to nourish the bone, with stasis as a secondary consequence.
A TCM practitioner listens carefully to the quality of the pain - is it sharp and stabbing, or dull and aching? Does it worsen with cold and damp weather? Are there other signs like fatigue, cold limbs, or a purple tongue? These clues, along with a detailed history of medications, lifestyle, and past injuries, allow the practitioner to identify the correct pattern. This differentiation is essential because the treatment strategy for moving stasis is different from that for nourishing deficiency, and using the wrong approach can delay healing.

### Practitioner Diagnosis
A TCM practitioner begins by listening closely to how the hip pain feels and when it started. The quality of the pain - whether it is sharp and stabbing, deep and aching, or heavy and distending - is the first clue that points toward one pattern rather than another. Questions about past injuries, alcohol use, steroid medications, and general energy levels help build the full picture.
If the pain is a fixed, stabbing sensation that feels worse with pressure and the person has a history of trauma or substance use, the practitioner suspects Qi and Blood Stagnation. The tongue often looks dark purple with possible stasis spots, and the pulse feels wiry and choppy - tight and hesitant, as if blood is struggling to move through a narrow space.
When the pain is a deep, dull ache that worsens with walking or standing, Blood Stagnation is more likely. Here the tongue appears dark purple or dusky, often with stasis spots, and the pulse is choppy or wiry. The practitioner will also ask about lower back soreness and knee weakness, because this pattern reflects a long-term lack of nourishment to the bone.
If the hip pain came on gradually and is accompanied by pronounced weakness and soreness in the lower back and knees, Painful Obstruction with Liver and Kidney Deficiency is the focus. The tongue is often pale and thin with little coating, and the pulse feels fine and weak. In contrast, a heavy, swollen sensation with a greasy tongue coating and a wiry-slippery pulse points to Phlegm obstructing the channels, often seen with obesity or chronic alcohol use.

## TCM Patterns and Treatment

### Qi And Blood Stagnation
*Pattern Link: [Detailed Pattern Page](/knowledge-base/patterns/qi-and-blood-stagnation)*

- **Clinical Signs & Symptoms**: Fixed, stabbing hip pain, Pain worsens with pressure or movement, Irritability and emotional tension, Dark purplish complexion or lips
- **Worse with**: Stress and anger, Alcohol and greasy food, Cold, damp, or windy weather, Overexertion or prolonged standing, Physical trauma
- **Better with**: Resting the hip, Gentle movement, Warmth on the hip, Foods that invigorate Blood (turmeric, black fungus)
- **Why this happens (Mechanism)**:
  The Liver is responsible for the smooth flow of Qi and Blood throughout the body. When emotional stress, physical trauma, or toxins like alcohol and corticosteroids disrupt this flow, Qi becomes stuck and Blood cannot circulate properly. This stagnation deprives the femoral head of vital nourishment, leading to the death of bone tissue and the characteristic fixed, stabbing pain.
- **Tongue & Pulse**: Tongue: dark purple body with stasis spots, distended sublingual veins, thin white coat. Pulse: wiry (弦 xián) and choppy (涩 sè), like a taut guitar string scraping bamboo.
- **Triggers & Relief Explanation**: Stress and anger stagnate Liver Qi, which is the root of this pattern; calming the mind and moving the body gently helps Qi flow. Cold and dampness constrict the channels, making Blood stasis worse, while warmth relaxes the vessels and encourages circulation. Alcohol and greasy foods create internal Dampness and Heat, further thickening the Blood and blocking the already compromised vessels.
- **Diet & Lifestyle**: Avoid alcohol, smoking, and greasy, cold foods that congeal Blood. Gentle, non-weight-bearing exercise like swimming or stationary cycling keeps Qi moving without stressing the joint. Keep the hip warm and dry. Include small amounts of turmeric, black fungus, and hawthorn in your diet to gently invigorate Blood.

### Blood Stagnation
*Pattern Link: [Detailed Pattern Page](/knowledge-base/patterns/blood-stagnation)*

- **Clinical Signs & Symptoms**: Fixed, stabbing hip pain that does not move, Pain worsens at night, Pain worse with pressure or weight-bearing, Dark purple or dusky tongue body with stasis spots
- **Worse with**: Cold, damp, or windy weather, Prolonged sitting or inactivity, Alcohol and greasy food, Re-injury or heavy lifting
- **Better with**: Gentle movement and warmth, Warmth on the hip, Resting the hip
- **Why this happens (Mechanism)**:
  When blood flow becomes sluggish and congeals, the delicate vessels feeding the femoral head become blocked. Without a steady supply of nourishment, bone tissue begins to die, and the stabbing, fixed pain of blood stasis sets in. This pattern often develops after trauma, prolonged steroid use, or chronic alcohol consumption, all of which can damage the vessels and allow stasis to take hold.
- **Tongue & Pulse**: Tongue: purple or dusky body with stasis spots; distended, dark sublingual veins; thin white coat. Pulse: choppy (Se) or wiry (Xian).
- **Triggers & Relief Explanation**: Cold constricts blood vessels and encourages stasis to congeal further, while warmth and gentle movement encourage blood to flow more freely. Alcohol, greasy foods, and trauma all damage the vessels and promote the formation of stasis, so avoiding them helps prevent the pattern from worsening.
- **Diet & Lifestyle**: Avoid cold, raw foods and alcohol, which can congeal blood and worsen stasis. Gentle, non-weight-bearing movement like swimming or stationary cycling helps circulation without overloading the joint. Warm compresses on the hip can temporarily ease pain.

### Painful Obstruction with Liver and Kidney Deficiency
*Pattern Link: [Detailed Pattern Page](/knowledge-base/patterns/painful-obstruction-with-liver-and-kidney-deficiency)*

- **Clinical Signs & Symptoms**: Dull aching hip pain that worsens with activity, Lower back and knee soreness and weakness, Fatigue and lack of stamina, Preference for warmth and aversion to cold, Numbness or tingling in the limbs, Pale or dull complexion
- **Worse with**: Cold, damp, or windy weather, Overexertion or prolonged standing, Raw, cold foods and drinks, Inadequate rest and sleep
- **Better with**: Warmth on the hip, Gentle movement, Resting the hip, Warm, nourishing foods
- **Why this happens (Mechanism)**:
  The Liver stores Blood and the Kidneys store Essence; together they nourish bone and marrow. When these reserves run low over time, the femoral head loses its structural support and begins to crumble, producing a deep, chronic ache.
Because the hip is a major weight-bearing joint, the deficiency shows up there first as weakness and soreness that worsens with activity. Blood and Essence also fail to properly warm and moisten the channels, so the pain feels better with heat and worse in cold damp weather.
- **Tongue & Pulse**: Tongue: pale, thin body with thin white coating. Pulse: fine, weak, and may be slow, with the Chi (rear) position especially weak.
- **Triggers & Relief Explanation**: This pattern is a deep deficiency of the Liver and Kidneys, which are the source of warmth, Blood, and Essence. Cold and overexertion further deplete these reserves and stagnate Qi in the channels, intensifying pain. Warmth, rest, and nourishing foods conserve Yang and support Essence, while gentle movement encourages circulation without draining what little strength remains.
- **Diet & Lifestyle**: Eat warm, nourishing foods that support Kidney Essence and Blood, such as bone broth, black beans, walnuts, and dark leafy greens. Avoid cold, raw foods and icy drinks that chill the interior. Gentle daily movement like tai chi or swimming keeps the joint mobile without overstraining. Keep the hip and lower back warm, especially in damp weather.

### Phlegm in the Channels joints and muscles
*Pattern Link: [Detailed Pattern Page](/knowledge-base/patterns/phlegm-in-the-channels-joints-and-muscles)*

- **Clinical Signs & Symptoms**: Fixed, stabbing hip pain that feels heavy, Hip swelling that is firm or hard to the touch, Numbness or heavy sensation in the leg, Chest tightness or excessive mucus
- **Worse with**: Cold, damp, or windy weather, Greasy, rich foods, Prolonged sitting or inactivity
- **Better with**: Gentle movement, Warmth on the hip, Dry, warm weather
- **Why this happens (Mechanism)**:
  When Phlegm-Dampness gathers in the hip joint, it creates a heavy, obstructing sludge that stalls the flow of Qi and blood. This stagnation starves the femoral head of nourishment and eventually leads to the death of bone tissue, which is felt as a deep, fixed, stabbing pain. The accumulation of turbid fluids also causes the joint to swell and feel firm, while the limb becomes numb and heavy.
- **Tongue & Pulse**: Tongue: purple body with stasis spots, a white greasy coating, and distended sublingual veins. Pulse: wiry (Xian) and choppy (Se), or wiry and slippery (Hua) if Phlegm predominates.
- **Triggers & Relief Explanation**: Phlegm and dampness are congealed fluids that worsen in cold, damp conditions and from foods that overtax digestion. Warmth and gentle movement help disperse the stagnation, much like stirring a thick soup so it does not settle and harden.
- **Diet & Lifestyle**: Avoid cold, raw, and greasy foods that generate dampness and phlegm. Gentle, daily movement like walking or tai chi helps keep the hip joint from stiffening, but stop before pain intensifies. Applying warmth to the area can ease the heavy ache.

### Painful Obstruction with Wind-Cold-Damp
*Pattern Link: [Detailed Pattern Page](/knowledge-base/patterns/painful-obstruction-with-wind-cold-damp)*

- **Clinical Signs & Symptoms**: Hip pain that worsens in cold, damp, or rainy weather, Heavy, aching sensation in the hip, Stiffness and difficulty moving the hip, Relief with warmth and gentle activity, Feeling of coldness in the hip and leg, No redness or heat in the joint
- **Worse with**: Cold, damp, or windy weather, Prolonged sitting or inactivity, Raw, cold foods and drinks, Overexertion in cold conditions
- **Better with**: Warmth on the hip, Gentle movement, Dry, warm weather, Moxibustion, Warming foods and spices
- **Why this happens (Mechanism)**:
  When Wind, Cold, and Dampness invade the body, they can lodge in the hip joint's channels and collaterals. The Cold contracts and obstructs the flow of Qi and Blood, while Dampness creates heaviness and stagnation. Over time, this blockage starves the femoral head of nourishment, leading to the gradual bone death seen in avascular necrosis.
The hip pain is typically deep and aching, aggravated by cold or damp weather, because these external factors reinforce the pathogenic influences already trapped in the joint. The stiffness and limited mobility reflect the obstruction of the sinews and channels.
- **Tongue & Pulse**: Tongue: pale, slightly swollen with teeth marks, white greasy coating. Pulse: wiry and tight (弦紧), or soggy (濡) if Dampness predominates.
- **Triggers & Relief Explanation**: Wind, Cold, and Dampness are the very pathogens that cause this pattern, so external cold and damp weather directly worsen the obstruction in the channels. Warmth and dryness counteract these pathogens, relaxing the constricted vessels and allowing Qi and Blood to flow. Gentle movement prevents stagnation without overstraining the joint, while warming foods and moxibustion provide internal and external heat to expel cold and dry dampness.
- **Diet & Lifestyle**: Keep the hip warm and dry; avoid sitting on cold surfaces or in drafty areas. Gentle daily movement like walking or tai chi helps keep Qi flowing, but rest when pain flares. Eat warming foods such as ginger, cinnamon, black pepper, and lamb; avoid raw, cold, or greasy foods that create dampness. Moxibustion on the hip area can be very beneficial.

### Qi and Blood Deficiency
*Pattern Link: [Detailed Pattern Page](/knowledge-base/patterns/qi-and-blood-deficiency)*

- **Clinical Signs & Symptoms**: Dull, aching hip pain that feels better with rest, Overwhelming fatigue and general weakness, Pale or sallow complexion and pale lips, Dizziness, heart palpitations, and poor sleep
- **Worse with**: Overwork and chronic stress, Raw, cold foods and drinks, Overexertion or prolonged standing
- **Better with**: Resting the hip, Warm, nourishing foods, Gentle movement
- **Why this happens (Mechanism)**:
  Qi and Blood are the body’s fundamental building blocks, responsible for nourishing every tissue, including bone. When they become deeply depleted over a long illness, the femoral head loses its source of nourishment and repair. Qi deficiency means the body lacks the energy to move blood and nutrients into the hip joint, while Blood deficiency leaves the bone dry and brittle, leading to a dull, persistent ache rather than sharp stabbing pain.
This pattern typically appears in the later stages of avascular necrosis, when the body’s reserves are exhausted. The hip pain is often accompanied by whole-body signs of emptiness: overwhelming fatigue, a pale face, and a sense of being run-down.
- **Tongue & Pulse**: Tongue: pale body, may be thin or have teeth marks on the edges, with a thin white coating. Pulse: fine, weak, and thready, lacking force.
- **Triggers & Relief Explanation**: When Qi and Blood are deficient, the body is like a nearly empty tank. Rest and sleep allow the body to conserve and rebuild its reserves, while warm, easy-to-digest foods directly supply the Spleen with the raw materials to create new Qi and Blood. Overwork, stress, and poor diet further drain these precious resources, deepening the deficiency and leaving the hip joint undernourished.
- **Diet & Lifestyle**: Focus on warm, easily digested, nourishing foods like bone broths, congees, slow-cooked stews, and dark leafy greens. Avoid cold, raw, and greasy foods that tax the Spleen. Gentle movement such as walking or tai chi helps circulate Qi without depleting it, but prioritize rest when fatigue is pronounced. Consistent, adequate sleep is essential to rebuild Blood.

## Frequently Asked Questions
### Can TCM actually regrow dead bone in the hip?
TCM cannot regrow bone that has already died and collapsed. However, by improving local blood circulation, reducing inflammation, and supporting the body’s natural repair processes, herbal medicine and acupuncture can help prevent further collapse and encourage new bone formation at the margins of the damaged area. The best results are seen in the early stages, before the femoral head has lost its shape. In more advanced cases, TCM remains valuable for managing pain and maintaining function, often as a complement to surgical planning.

### Is it safe to use Chinese herbs while I'm waiting for hip replacement surgery?
Yes, TCM can be a valuable tool to manage pain and maintain mobility before surgery. It is important to inform both your TCM practitioner and your orthopedic surgeon about all treatments you are receiving. Some blood-moving herbs (such as Dang Gui, Chuan Xiong, and Tao Ren) can affect blood clotting, so your surgeon may advise you to stop them a week or two before the operation. Never stop prescribed medications without consulting your doctor.

### What dietary changes can support TCM treatment for AVN?
A diet that supports bone health in TCM focuses on nourishing the Kidney and Liver while avoiding dampness and phlegm. Favor warm, cooked foods like bone broths, black sesame seeds, walnuts, dark leafy greens, and goji berries. Foods that gently invigorate blood, such as turmeric and black fungus, can also help. Avoid alcohol completely, as it damages the Liver and creates damp-heat. Reduce greasy, fried, and cold foods that burden the Spleen and contribute to phlegm accumulation.

### How often will I need acupuncture sessions?
A typical treatment plan begins with weekly acupuncture sessions for the first 2 to 3 months. As pain decreases and function improves, sessions may be spaced out to every two weeks, then monthly for maintenance. Each session includes local points around the hip, such as Huantiao GB-30, to directly stimulate circulation, as well as points on the lower legs and back to address the underlying organ imbalance. Moxibustion (warming therapy) may be added if cold or deficiency is present.

### My AVN was caused by long-term steroid use. Can TCM help?
Yes. In TCM, prolonged steroid use is seen as a toxin that creates heat and depletes the Kidney essence and Liver blood, leading to blood stasis and bone malnutrition. Herbal formulas are designed to clear residual heat, nourish the depleted organs, and vigorously move blood to the hip. Many patients find that TCM not only helps their hip pain but also improves other steroid-related side effects like fatigue, weight gain, and poor healing. Treatment must be coordinated with your prescribing doctor; never stop steroids abruptly.

### I have AVN on MRI but no pain yet. Should I still treat it?
Absolutely. This is the ideal time to start TCM treatment. The absence of pain often means the process is still in its earliest stages, when the bone structure is largely intact. By addressing the underlying blood stasis and any organ deficiencies now, you have the best chance of preventing progression, avoiding pain, and preserving the joint without surgery. A TCM practitioner can tailor a gentle but effective herbal formula to improve circulation and strengthen the bone.

### Can I combine TCM with my current pain medications?
Generally, yes. Acupuncture and herbs can work alongside conventional pain relievers, and many patients find they are able to reduce their medication dosage over time as pain improves. However, always inform both your TCM practitioner and your medical doctor about everything you are taking. Certain herbs have mild blood-thinning effects and should be used cautiously with NSAIDs or prescription anticoagulants. Never adjust your medication dose without medical supervision.
