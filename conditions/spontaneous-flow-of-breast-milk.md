## Conventional Medicine View
### Description
In Western medicine, leaking breast milk outside of feeding - called galactorrhea when it occurs outside of pregnancy or breastfeeding - is often linked to elevated levels of the hormone prolactin. During the postpartum period, some leakage is considered normal due to the let-down reflex, but excessive or persistent leaking may be investigated for causes like thyroid imbalance, pituitary tumors, medication side effects, or oversupply. Diagnosis typically involves blood tests, a physical exam, and sometimes imaging.

### Treatments
When no underlying pathology is found, conventional management is often supportive: breast pads, frequent feeding or pumping to manage let-down, and sometimes block feeding for oversupply. If a hormonal cause is identified, dopamine agonists like cabergoline may be used to lower prolactin levels. In many cases, however, the leaking is treated as a benign inconvenience with few targeted therapies.

## Traditional Chinese Medicine View
### TCM Understanding
TCM views breast milk as a refined fluid created from the mother's Qi and Blood. Its proper flow depends on the Spleen's ability to hold fluids in, the Liver's ability to keep Qi moving smoothly, and the Kidneys' role in storing essence. When any of these systems is out of balance, the breast network loses its grip, and milk can leak out on its own.
After childbirth, the body is often depleted. Qi and Blood deficiency is a very common cause: the Spleen is too weak to contain the milk, so it trickles out thin and watery, and the breasts feel soft and painless. On the other hand, emotional stress or frustration can cause the Liver's Qi to stagnate and generate heat, which agitates the milk and forces it to leak - here the milk is thicker, stickier, and the breasts feel distended or tender.
Less often, Spleen weakness leads to dampness that clogs the breast network, causing scanty, sticky leakage with a heavy, bloated sensation. Or Yin deficiency after birth can create empty heat that pushes out thin, warm milk, often with night sweats and a dry mouth. Each pattern has a very different feel, and TCM treatment is tailored to the specific imbalance.

### Practitioner Diagnosis
For Qi and Blood Deficiency, the leaking milk is thin and watery, and the breasts feel soft and empty with no pain or distension. The person feels very tired, dizzy, and pale, often after significant blood loss during childbirth. The tongue is pale with a thin white coat, and the pulse is weak and thin, indicating the body cannot hold fluids in.
When Liver Qi Stagnation transforms into Heat, the milk is thicker, possibly yellowish, and the breasts feel distended, tender, or even painful. Irritability, mood swings, and a sensation of heat or stress are common. The tongue is red with a yellow coating, and the pulse is wiry and rapid, reflecting heat agitating the breast channels.
Spleen Deficiency with Dampness causes scanty, sticky milk leakage and breasts that feel heavy or full but not sharply painful. The person typically has a poor appetite, bloating, and a heavy sensation in the body, especially the limbs. The tongue is pale with a greasy coating, and the pulse is slippery or soft, showing that dampness is obstructing the breast network.
Empty-Heat from Yin Deficiency leads to thin milk that may feel warm when it leaks. There is often thirst, a dry mouth, night sweats, or a feeling of heat in the palms and soles. The tongue is red with little coating, and the pulse is thin and rapid. This pattern reflects a deep loss of Yin fluids after childbirth, allowing internal heat to disturb the breast network.

## TCM Patterns and Treatment

### Qi and Blood Deficiency
*Pattern Link: [Detailed Pattern Page](/knowledge-base/patterns/qi-and-blood-deficiency)*

- **Clinical Signs & Symptoms**: Thin, watery milk that leaks easily, Soft, painless breasts without distension, Profound fatigue and weakness, Pale or sallow complexion, Poor appetite and loose stools, Dizziness or lightheadedness
- **Worse with**: Overexertion and prolonged standing, Skipping meals or eating cold raw foods, Emotional stress and worry
- **Better with**: Rest and adequate sleep, Warm, nourishing soups and stews, Gentle breast support (soft bra)
- **Why this happens (Mechanism)**:
  After childbirth, blood loss and Qi depletion weaken the Spleen and Stomach, which are responsible for holding fluids in the body. When Qi is too weak to secure the breast network, the thin, watery milk leaks out involuntarily. The breasts feel soft and painless because there is no stagnation or heat, just a lack of holding strength.
- **Tongue & Pulse**: Tongue: pale body, may be thin or have teeth marks, thin white coating. Pulse: fine (thready) and weak, especially at the right Guan position.
- **Triggers & Relief Explanation**: Qi and Blood Deficiency means the body's holding power is weak. Rest and warm, nourishing foods build Spleen Qi, strengthening its ability to secure fluids and stop leakage. Overexertion, cold foods, and stress further deplete Qi and Blood, making the leakage worse.
- **Diet & Lifestyle**: Eat warm, easily digestible foods like bone broth, congee with red dates and goji berries, and lightly cooked vegetables. Avoid cold, raw foods and icy drinks, which further weaken the Spleen. Prioritize rest and sleep to rebuild Qi and Blood.

### Liver Qi Stagnation that transforms into Heat
*Pattern Link: [Detailed Pattern Page](/knowledge-base/patterns/liver-qi-stagnation-that-transforms-into-heat)*

- **Clinical Signs & Symptoms**: Milk is thick and sticky, Breast distension or burning pain, Irritability and explosive anger, Bitter taste in the mouth, Ribcage distension or discomfort
- **Worse with**: Emotional upset (anger, frustration, overthinking), Spicy, greasy, or fried foods, Alcohol and coffee, Suppressing emotions
- **Better with**: Relaxation and stress relief, Cooling foods (like cucumber, pear, tofu), Gentle exercise, Regular breastfeeding or pumping, Deep breathing
- **Why this happens (Mechanism)**:
  Emotional frustration or suppressed anger causes the Liver’s Qi to stagnate. Over time, this stagnation generates Heat, which rises and disturbs the breast channels. The Heat agitates the milk and forces it to leak out, often making the milk thick and sticky. This is why the leakage is often accompanied by breast distension, irritability, and a bitter taste in the mouth.
- **Tongue & Pulse**: Tongue: red body, especially redder on the sides, with a yellow coating. Pulse: wiry (弦 xián) and rapid (数 shuò).
- **Triggers & Relief Explanation**: Stress and anger directly fuel Liver Qi stagnation and Heat, which is the mechanism behind this pattern. Cooling foods and relaxation help clear Heat and smooth the Liver, while regular milk expression prevents Heat from building up in the breast channels.
- **Diet & Lifestyle**: Avoid spicy, greasy, and fried foods that add Heat. Eat cooling, light foods like leafy greens, cucumber, and pear. Practice gentle stress management like walking or deep breathing. Express milk regularly to prevent stagnation.

### Spleen Deficiency with Dampness
*Pattern Link: [Detailed Pattern Page](/knowledge-base/patterns/spleen-deficiency-with-dampness)*

- **Clinical Signs & Symptoms**: Scanty, sticky or thick milk that leaks, Abdominal bloating and fullness, worse after eating, Heavy, puffy sensation in the body and limbs, Poor appetite and loose, unformed stools, Sticky taste in the mouth with no real thirst, Fatigue and drowsiness, especially after meals
- **Worse with**: Cold and raw foods, Greasy, heavy, or overly sweet meals, Damp or humid weather, Prolonged sitting or inactivity
- **Better with**: Warm, easily digested meals, Gentle exercise, Rest and adequate sleep, Warm compresses on the breast
- **Why this happens (Mechanism)**:
  The Spleen transforms food into Qi and Blood and manages the body's fluids. After childbirth, if the Spleen is weakened by poor diet or exhaustion, it fails to handle fluids properly, and dampness builds up inside. This dampness clogs the breast network, preventing normal milk production and causing the small amount of milk that forms to leak out uncontrollably. The milk is typically scanty and sticky rather than thin and clear.
- **Tongue & Pulse**: Tongue: pale and swollen with teeth marks on the sides, and a white greasy coating that looks moist. Pulse: soggy (Ru) and slowed-down (Huan), sometimes with a slippery (Hua) quality.
- **Triggers & Relief Explanation**: Cold, raw, and greasy foods damage the Spleen's ability to transform and transport fluids, directly worsening the dampness that causes milk to leak. Warm, cooked foods and gentle movement support Spleen Yang and help the body process dampness naturally. Rest allows Qi to recover, strengthening the holding function that keeps milk from escaping.
- **Diet & Lifestyle**: Eat warm, easily digested foods like rice congee with a little ginger and lean protein. Avoid raw, cold, and greasy foods that create more dampness. Gentle daily walking helps Qi move, but rest when you feel tired. Keep the breast area warm and avoid overeating, especially in the evening.

### Empty-Heat caused by Yin Deficiency
*Pattern Link: [Detailed Pattern Page](/knowledge-base/patterns/empty-heat-caused-by-yin-deficiency)*

- **Clinical Signs & Symptoms**: Breasts feel soft, not distended, Milk is thin and watery, Night sweats, Feeling of heat in palms, soles, and chest, Dry mouth and throat, especially at night, Restlessness and difficulty sleeping
- **Worse with**: Spicy, greasy, or fried foods, Alcohol and coffee, Overwork and lack of sleep, Emotional upset (anger, frustration, overthinking)
- **Better with**: Rest and adequate sleep, Cooling foods (like cucumber, pear, tofu), Sipping warm water throughout the day, Gentle exercise
- **Why this happens (Mechanism)**:
  After childbirth, the body's Yin (cooling, moistening essence) is often depleted. This deficiency allows empty heat to arise, which disturbs the breast network and pushes fluids outward. Because the milk is not properly consolidated by Yin, it leaks out thin and watery, without the breast distension seen in stagnation patterns.
- **Tongue & Pulse**: Tongue: red, thin, dry body with cracks, little or no coating. Pulse: fine (thin) and rapid, weak on deep pressure.
- **Triggers & Relief Explanation**: Yin deficiency means the body lacks cooling, moistening substance. Spicy foods, alcohol, and overwork generate heat and further consume Yin, worsening the empty heat that forces milk out. Rest, cooling foods, and hydration replenish Yin and calm the empty heat, helping the body hold fluids in. Emotional stress stirs up Liver Yang, which draws on Yin reserves, so staying calm protects the Yin.
- **Diet & Lifestyle**: Prioritise rest and sleep to rebuild Yin. Eat cooling, moistening foods like pear, tofu, black sesame, and congee. Avoid spicy, greasy, and fried foods, and limit alcohol and coffee. Gentle exercise like walking can help, but avoid overexertion.

## Frequently Asked Questions
### Why is my breast milk leaking when I'm not breastfeeding?
In TCM, milk leakage without feeding often means the body's ability to hold fluids in is weakened, or heat is agitating the breast network. If you're also very tired and pale, it's likely a Qi and Blood deficiency. If you feel irritable and your breasts are distended, it may be Liver Qi stagnation with heat. A TCM practitioner can determine which pattern fits you by looking at your milk's quality, your tongue, and your pulse.

### Can acupuncture stop breast milk leakage?
Yes, acupuncture can help restore balance by strengthening the Spleen to hold fluids, soothing the Liver to reduce heat, or nourishing Yin - depending on your pattern. Points like Zusanli (ST-36) and Sanyinjiao (SP-6) are often used to support overall energy and milk regulation, while local points on the breast can directly calm the breast network. Most women receive weekly sessions for several weeks.

### Will Chinese herbs affect my baby through breast milk?
When prescribed by a qualified TCM practitioner experienced in postpartum care, herbal formulas are safe during breastfeeding. The herbs chosen are gentle and work on your constitution, not the baby's. However, always inform your practitioner that you are nursing, and never self-prescribe - some herbs that clear heat or move Qi could reduce milk supply if not matched to your pattern.

### How long does it take for TCM to reduce leaking?
Many women notice the leakage becomes less frequent or the milk quality changes within 3 to 6 weeks. Excess patterns (stress-related, thick milk) tend to respond faster, while deficiency patterns (fatigue, thin milk) may need 2 to 3 months of consistent treatment to rebuild Qi and Blood. Acupuncture once or twice a week plus daily herbal teas is the typical protocol.

### Is leaking breast milk a sign of too much milk?
Not necessarily. In TCM, leaking is more about the body's failure to contain the milk than about an oversupply. Even women with low milk production can leak if their Qi is too weak to hold it in. The key is to look at the quality of the milk and your overall energy - thin, watery leakage with fatigue points to deficiency, not excess.

### Can I combine TCM with my doctor's advice?
Absolutely. TCM can work alongside any conventional management. If your doctor has ruled out serious causes and is simply monitoring you, herbs and acupuncture can address the underlying imbalance. Just keep both your doctor and TCM practitioner informed, especially if you are taking any medications.
