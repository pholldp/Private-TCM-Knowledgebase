## Conventional Medicine View
### Description
Trichomoniasis is a common sexually transmitted infection caused by the protozoan parasite Trichomonas vaginalis. It often causes a frothy, yellow-green vaginal discharge with a strong fishy odor, along with genital itching, burning, redness, and discomfort during urination or intercourse. However, many people - especially men - carry the infection without any symptoms.
Diagnosis is usually made through a swab test examined under a microscope or with a nucleic acid amplification test (NAAT). Standard treatment is a single dose or short course of the antibiotic metronidazole or tinidazole, which targets the parasite directly.

### Treatments
The mainstay of conventional treatment is oral metronidazole or tinidazole, typically given as a single large dose or a 5-7 day course. Sexual partners should be treated simultaneously to prevent reinfection. Topical creams and gels are sometimes used to soothe local symptoms, but they do not clear the infection throughout the body.

## Traditional Chinese Medicine View
### TCM Understanding
In TCM, the genital area is governed by the Liver channel and the Kidney system. When dampness and heat accumulate in the lower body - from diet, emotional stress, or a weakness in the body's ability to manage fluids - they create a warm, moist environment that is perfect for the Trichomonas parasite to thrive. The infection is therefore never seen as just a local problem; it always reflects a deeper imbalance in the body's internal ecosystem.
The Spleen plays a central role because it transforms and transports fluids. If the Spleen is weakened by poor diet, overwork, or worry, it fails to do its job and dampness builds up. That dampness is heavy and sinks downward, settling in the pelvic region. When it combines with heat - from emotional frustration, spicy foods, or a pre-existing tendency - the resulting Damp-Heat creates the classic symptoms of frothy, malodorous discharge and intense itching.
Emotional factors, particularly anger and frustration, can also stir up Liver Qi. Stagnant Qi generates heat, which then mixes with dampness and travels along the Liver channel to the genitals. This explains why some women notice their symptoms flare during periods of high stress. In chronic or recurrent cases, the body's Yin - the cooling, moistening energy of the Liver and Kidneys - may become depleted, leaving the tissues dry, underprotected, and prone to low-grade irritation even without heavy discharge.
Because the same parasite can present with such different underlying imbalances, TCM does not have a single 'trichomoniasis treatment.' Instead, a practitioner reads the clues in the discharge, the tongue, the pulse, and the person's overall health to identify which pattern is dominant - and then tailors the herbal formula and acupuncture points to correct that specific imbalance.

### Practitioner Diagnosis
A TCM practitioner begins by listening carefully to your description of the discharge, itching, and any burning or pain. The color, smell, and consistency of vaginal discharge are the first clues that point toward one pattern over another. The practitioner also asks about your energy, digestion, stress levels, and sleep, because trichomoniasis is never seen as just a local infection - it is always connected to the balance of your whole body.
When the discharge is frothy, yellow or greenish, and has a strong foul odor, accompanied by a burning sensation, the picture points to Damp-Heat in the Lower Burner. This is the classic acute pattern. The tongue is red with a thick, greasy yellow coating, and the pulse feels slippery and rapid. The body is reacting intensely to a heavy, hot, damp invasion.
If the itching is extremely intense, the genital area feels swollen and burning, and the discharge is thick and yellow, the Damp-Heat has likely lodged in the Liver Channel. This pattern often comes with irritability, a bitter taste in the mouth, and a wiry, rapid pulse. The tongue will also be red with a yellow greasy coat, but the emotional edge helps distinguish it.
In cases where the infection keeps coming back and the discharge is more persistent than acute, with a pale, swollen tongue and a greasy white coating, the root is Spleen Deficiency with Dampness. You might feel chronically tired, have a poor appetite, and notice loose stools. Here the body’s digestive power is too weak to manage fluids, so dampness accumulates and drips downward.
When stress, frustration, or premenstrual tension clearly makes symptoms worse, a practitioner suspects Liver Qi Stagnation that transforms into Heat. The discharge may not be as copious, but itching and burning flare with emotional upset. The pulse is wiry and rapid, and the tongue edges may be red.
In chronic, low-grade infections with dryness, night sweats, and a sore back, the pattern shifts to Kidney and Liver Yin Deficiency, where a red tongue with little coating tells the story.

## TCM Patterns and Treatment

### Damp-Heat in the Lower Burner
*Pattern Link: [Detailed Pattern Page](/knowledge-base/patterns/damp-heat-in-the-lower-burner)*

- **Clinical Signs & Symptoms**: Frothy, yellow, foul-smelling vaginal discharge, Genital itching and burning, Heavy sensation in the lower abdomen and legs, Dark, scanty urine with possible burning, Thirst with no desire to drink, Tongue coating thickest at the back
- **Worse with**: Spicy, greasy, or fried foods, Alcohol, Hot, humid weather, Emotional stress and anger, Prolonged sitting
- **Better with**: Cooling foods and drinks, Sitz baths with cooling herbs, Loose, breathable clothing, Rest and stress reduction
- **Why this happens (Mechanism)**:
  Dampness and Heat are two pernicious influences that can accumulate in the body, especially in the pelvic and genital region. When they combine and sink downward, they create a warm, moist environment that is ideal for the Trichomonas parasite to thrive. The Heat causes inflammation, burning, and irritation, while the Dampness produces the heavy sensation and the copious, frothy, foul-smelling discharge that is the hallmark of this infection.
- **Tongue & Pulse**: Tongue: Red body with a yellow greasy coating that is thickest at the root (back). Pulse: Slippery (滑 Huá) and rapid (数 Shuò), often more pronounced at the rear (Chi) position.
- **Triggers & Relief Explanation**: Hot, spicy, and greasy foods directly create more Heat and Dampness in the body, fueling the infection. Alcohol is pure Heat and stirs up Dampness. A humid environment adds external Dampness, while emotional stress causes Liver Qi to stagnate and generate Heat, making symptoms worse. Cooling foods and herbs counteract the Heat, drying herbs drain the Dampness, and rest helps calm the Liver so it does not fan the flames. Keeping the area dry and cool denies the parasite the warm, moist environment it needs to survive.
- **Diet & Lifestyle**: Focus on foods that are cooling and drying, such as cucumber, mung beans, barley, and celery. Strictly avoid greasy, fried, spicy, and sugary foods, as well as alcohol, which all generate more Dampness and Heat. Wear loose, breathable cotton underwear and avoid sitting for long periods to keep the area dry. A sitz bath with a mild herbal infusion of chrysanthemum or dandelion can provide local relief.

### Damp-Heat in the Liver Channel
*Pattern Link: [Detailed Pattern Page](/knowledge-base/patterns/damp-heat-in-the-liver-channel)*

- **Clinical Signs & Symptoms**: Intense genital itching and burning, Thick yellow, foul-smelling discharge, Bitter taste in the mouth, Pain or fullness along the ribs, Irritability or frustration
- **Worse with**: Spicy, greasy, or fried foods, Alcohol, Emotional stress and anger, Hot, humid weather, Prolonged sitting
- **Better with**: Cooling foods and drinks, Gentle exercise, Rest and stress reduction, Cool environment, Loose, breathable clothing
- **Why this happens (Mechanism)**:
  The Liver channel runs through the genital area. When Dampness and Heat accumulate in this channel-often from diet, emotional stress, or external pathogens-they pour downward, creating the hot, damp environment that allows Trichomonas to thrive. This causes the intense itching, burning, and thick yellow discharge characteristic of this pattern.
- **Tongue & Pulse**: Tongue: Red body with redder sides, yellow greasy coating thicker at centre and root. Pulse: Wiry (弦 xián), slippery (滑 huá), and rapid (数 shuò).
- **Triggers & Relief Explanation**: Spicy, greasy, and sugary foods create Dampness and Heat, which directly worsen this pattern. Emotional stress and anger stagnate Liver Qi, which then turns into Heat and combines with existing Dampness to aggravate symptoms. Cooling foods, stress reduction, and gentle exercise help clear Heat, smooth Liver Qi, and drain Dampness, relieving the burning, itching, and discharge.
- **Diet & Lifestyle**: Avoid spicy, greasy, and fried foods, alcohol, and sugar, which fuel Damp-Heat. Choose cooling, damp-resolving foods such as mung beans, bitter greens, and barley. Gentle exercise like walking or qigong helps keep Liver Qi flowing and reduces stress.

### Spleen Deficiency with Dampness
*Pattern Link: [Detailed Pattern Page](/knowledge-base/patterns/spleen-deficiency-with-dampness)*

- **Clinical Signs & Symptoms**: Whitish or clear vaginal discharge, Loose stools or diarrhoea, Abdominal bloating after eating, Fatigue and heaviness of the body, Poor appetite, Pale, swollen tongue with teeth marks and white greasy coating
- **Worse with**: Cold and raw foods, Hot, humid weather, Overeating or heavy meals, Worry and overthinking, Prolonged sitting
- **Better with**: Warm, cooked meals, Rest and stress reduction, Gentle exercise, Dry, warm environment, Light, easily digestible foods
- **Why this happens (Mechanism)**:
  When the Spleen is weak, it cannot properly transform fluids, and Dampness accumulates internally. This Dampness is heavy and sinks downward to the genital area, causing a persistent, often whitish or clear vaginal discharge and mild itching. Because the Spleen Qi is deficient, you may also feel bloated after eating, have loose stools, and feel unusually tired and heavy.
- **Tongue & Pulse**: Tongue: pale, swollen body with teeth marks, white greasy coating. Pulse: soggy (Ru) and possibly slippery (Hua).
- **Triggers & Relief Explanation**: The Spleen's ability to transform and transport fluids is easily damaged by cold, raw, and damp substances. Warm, cooked foods and rest support Spleen Qi, while gentle movement helps move Qi and dry Dampness. Emotional stress, especially worry, directly weakens the Spleen, so calm and relaxation are protective.
- **Diet & Lifestyle**: Eat warm, cooked foods like soups and stews, and avoid raw, cold, and greasy items that burden the Spleen. Gentle daily movement, such as walking or yoga, helps Qi flow and reduces Dampness. Do not overeat, and try to eat at regular times.

### Liver Qi Stagnation that transforms into Heat
*Pattern Link: [Detailed Pattern Page](/knowledge-base/patterns/liver-qi-stagnation-that-transforms-into-heat)*

- **Clinical Signs & Symptoms**: Genital itching and burning that worsens with anger, Bitter taste in the mouth, Distending or burning pain along the ribs, Irritability and explosive anger, Dry mouth and throat
- **Worse with**: Emotional stress and anger, Spicy, greasy, or fried foods, Alcohol, Hot, humid weather
- **Better with**: Rest and stress reduction, Cooling foods and drinks, Gentle exercise
- **Why this happens (Mechanism)**:
  When emotional stress, frustration, or anger is held in for too long, the Liver's job of keeping Qi moving smoothly gets stuck. That stuck Qi gradually generates heat, like friction building up in a machine.
Once heat is present, it can combine with any underlying dampness in the body and travel downward along the Liver channel, which passes through the genital area. This is what produces the itching, burning, and discharge that flare up when you feel tense or upset.
- **Tongue & Pulse**: Tongue: red body with redder sides, yellow coating. Pulse: wiry and rapid (xian shu), especially at the left Guan position.
- **Triggers & Relief Explanation**: Emotional stress directly stirs Liver Qi stagnation, which generates heat that aggravates genital symptoms. Spicy and greasy foods add internal heat, while alcohol and caffeine overstimulate the Liver. Cooling foods and calming activities help smooth the flow of Liver Qi, clear heat, and reduce irritation.
- **Diet & Lifestyle**: Build in daily stress-reducing habits like meditation, gentle yoga, or a quiet walk. Avoid spicy, greasy, and fried foods that add internal heat. Enjoy cooling foods such as celery, cucumber, and chrysanthemum tea. Keep alcohol and caffeine to a minimum.

### Kidney and Liver Yin Deficiency
*Pattern Link: [Detailed Pattern Page](/knowledge-base/patterns/kidney-and-liver-yin-deficiency)*

- **Clinical Signs & Symptoms**: Scanty or thin vaginal discharge, Persistent itching, often worse at night, Vaginal dryness, Night sweats, Soreness and weakness of the lower back and knees, Dizziness and dry eyes
- **Worse with**: Overwork and late nights, Spicy, greasy, or fried foods, Emotional stress and anger
- **Better with**: Cool environment, Rest and stress reduction, Moistening foods (pear, tofu)
- **Why this happens (Mechanism)**:
  In chronic or recurrent trichomoniasis, the underlying Yin of the Liver and Kidneys becomes depleted. Without sufficient Yin to nourish and moisten the genital tract, the area becomes dry and underprotected, leading to persistent, low-grade itching and a thin or scanty discharge. The deficiency heat that often accompanies Yin loss can further aggravate inflammation and discomfort.
- **Tongue & Pulse**: Tongue: red, thin body with cracks, little or no coating, dry surface. Pulse: fine, rapid, and wiry; left Chi position weak.
- **Triggers & Relief Explanation**: Yin is the body's cooling, moistening energy. Overwork, heat, and stress burn up Yin, making dryness and itching worse. Rest and cooling, moistening habits preserve Yin, which is exactly what this pattern needs to recover.
- **Diet & Lifestyle**: Prioritize rest and avoid late nights, as sleep restores Yin. Eat moistening foods like pears, black sesame, and tofu, while steering clear of spicy, fried, or overly heating foods. Gentle exercise such as walking or yoga can support circulation without depleting Yin.

## Frequently Asked Questions
### Can TCM cure trichomoniasis without antibiotics?
In mild to moderate cases, TCM can resolve the infection by correcting the internal environment so the parasite can no longer thrive. However, for severe or systemic infections, conventional antibiotics remain the fastest and most reliable way to clear the parasite. Many women use TCM alongside medical treatment to speed recovery and reduce the chance of recurrence.

### How long does it take for herbal treatment to stop the itching?
Most women notice a reduction in itching and discharge within the first 5-7 days of taking the correct herbal formula. The full resolution of symptoms and the underlying imbalance typically takes 2-4 weeks, though chronic cases may need longer. Acupuncture can provide almost immediate relief in some acute episodes.

### Can I use TCM alongside metronidazole?
Yes, TCM herbs and acupuncture can be safely combined with metronidazole or tinidazole. There are no known serious interactions, but always inform both your TCM practitioner and your medical doctor about all treatments you are using. Some herbs may actually help reduce the digestive side effects of the antibiotic.

### Why do I keep getting trichomoniasis even after treatment?
Recurrence often happens because the underlying damp-heat environment in your body hasn't been addressed. If the Spleen is weak or dampness continues to accumulate, the parasite can easily re-establish itself. TCM focuses on changing that internal terrain - strengthening digestion, clearing heat, and draining dampness - so future infections are less likely to take hold.

### What foods should I avoid while treating trichomoniasis?
Generally, you'll want to avoid foods that create dampness and heat: greasy or fried foods, excessive dairy, sugar, alcohol, and spicy dishes. Instead, favor lightly cooked vegetables, whole grains like rice and millet, and foods that gently support the Spleen such as yam and lentils. Your practitioner can give you more specific guidance based on your pattern.

### Is acupuncture painful for genital itching?
Acupuncture needles are extremely thin, and most points used for this condition are on the legs, abdomen, and back - not directly on sensitive areas. The sensation is usually a mild ache or tingling, and many patients find it deeply relaxing. Points are chosen to clear heat and dampness from the lower body, which calms the itching systemically.

### Can TCM treat my partner too?
Yes, TCM can address the same damp-heat patterns in men, though they may present differently (often with urethral discharge or discomfort). Treating both partners is important to break the cycle of reinfection. The herbal formula would be tailored to each person's specific pattern.
