## Conventional Medicine View
### Description
Dandruff is a very common scalp condition where the skin sheds visible white or yellowish flakes, often accompanied by itching. In Western medicine, it is considered a mild form of seborrheic dermatitis - an inflammatory reaction linked to an overgrowth of a yeast called Malassezia that lives on most people's scalps. The condition tends to flare with stress, cold dry weather, hormonal shifts, and a diet high in sugar or processed fats, but the exact reason some people are more susceptible isn't fully understood.
Diagnosis is usually straightforward: a doctor looks at the scalp and the flakes. In more stubborn cases, they may rule out other skin conditions like psoriasis or eczema. The mainstay of care is medicated shampoos that reduce yeast, slow skin cell turnover, or break down scale.

### Treatments
Standard anti-dandruff shampoos contain active ingredients like zinc pyrithione, ketoconazole, selenium sulfide, salicylic acid, or coal tar. These are used a few times a week to control yeast, reduce inflammation, and loosen flakes. For more severe seborrheic dermatitis, doctors may prescribe topical corticosteroid lotions or antifungal creams. While these treatments can effectively manage symptoms, they typically need to be used continuously - dandruff often returns within days or weeks of stopping.

## Traditional Chinese Medicine View
### TCM Understanding
TCM sees dandruff as a reflection of what's happening inside your body, particularly in the digestive system and the quality of your Blood. The most common cause is Damp-Heat in the Stomach and Spleen. When you regularly eat rich, greasy, sweet, or spicy foods, your digestive organs become overloaded and produce a sticky, hot substance called Damp-Heat. This turbid steam rises upward to your scalp, clogging hair follicles and overheating the skin. The result is an oily, red, itchy scalp covered in thick, yellowish, greasy flakes.
Another major pattern is Heat in the Blood. Emotional stress, frustration, or a diet heavy in hot, spicy foods can stir up excess Heat that enters the Blood. This Heat naturally rises to the head, scorching the scalp and creating an internal Wind that causes flaking. The flakes here are dry, fine, and white or silvery, and the scalp feels hot, red, and burning. This type often flares up after a stressful day or a spicy meal.
When the body's Blood is chronically insufficient, the scalp loses its nourishment and moisture. This Blood Deficiency leaves the skin dry and undernourished, producing fine, bran-like white flakes. At the same time, the body's defensive Qi weakens, allowing external Wind-Dryness to invade the surface and trigger persistent itching. This pattern is common in people with a pale complexion, brittle nails, and fatigue, and it worsens in dry weather or at night.
A less common but important pattern is Empty-Heat from Yin Deficiency. When the body's cooling, moistening Yin fluids are depleted, they can no longer anchor the body's warmth. This creates a deceptive heat that rises to the scalp, causing dark red patches with oily scales that feel hot and itchy, especially at night. Unlike true Damp-Heat, this heat is often accompanied by night sweats, a dry mouth, and a feeling of heat in the palms and soles.

### Practitioner Diagnosis
A TCM practitioner begins by examining the flakes and the scalp itself. The color, texture, and oiliness of the scales are the first signposts. Dry, white, powdery flakes suggest a very different internal picture than greasy, yellowish, sticky scales, so this simple observation already narrows the possibilities.
If the scalp is red and coated with oily, yellowish scales that feel greasy to the touch, Damp-Heat in Stomach and Spleen is the likely pattern. The tongue is red with a thick, yellow, greasy coating, and the pulse feels slippery or wiry and rapid. A heavy body sensation, bad breath, and loose, sticky stools often accompany this picture.
When the flakes are dry, fine, and white, and the scalp looks red with a burning or hot sensation, Heat in the Blood is usually the main driver. The tongue is red with a thin yellow or white coat, and the pulse is wiry and slippery. A preference for cold drinks, dry mouth, and dry stools reinforce this diagnosis, as does a flare-up after spicy food or emotional stress.
A pale scalp with persistent, fine dry scales and mild itching points to Blood Deficiency with External Wind. Here the tongue is pale with a thin coat, and the pulse is thready or thready-rapid. The person may also have a dull complexion, dry skin elsewhere, and feel easily fatigued, indicating that the blood is not nourishing the scalp enough to keep wind-dryness out.
Dark red patches with oily scales and itching that worsens at night, along with dry mouth, night sweats, or a warm sensation in the palms and soles, suggest Empty-Heat caused by Yin Deficiency. The tongue is red with little or no coating, and the pulse is thready and rapid. This pattern often appears in chronic cases where the body’s cooling, moistening resources have been depleted over time.

### Classical Reference
> **Original**: 「白屑风多生于头面，初起微痒，久则渐生白屑，叠叠飞起，脱去又生。此皆起于热体当风，风热所化。」

> **Translation**: "White scaling wind often arises on the head and face; at first slightly itchy, over time it gradually produces white scales that flake off in layers, and after they fall, new ones grow. This all originates from a hot constitution encountering wind, transforming into wind-heat."

## TCM Patterns and Treatment

### Damp-Heat in Stomach and Spleen
*Pattern Link: [Detailed Pattern Page](/knowledge-base/patterns/damp-heat-in-stomach-and-spleen)*

- **Clinical Signs & Symptoms**: Oily, yellowish, greasy flakes, Scalp redness and itching, Bloating and fullness in the upper abdomen, Sticky or incomplete bowel movements, Bitter or sticky taste in the mouth, Heavy feeling in the body and limbs
- **Worse with**: Greasy, fried, or spicy foods, Alcohol and coffee, Damp, humid weather, Irregular eating or overeating, Emotional stress
- **Better with**: Cooling foods and drinks, Regular, well-formed bowel movements, Dry, clean scalp care, Gentle, consistent mealtimes
- **Why this happens (Mechanism)**:
  When the Stomach and Spleen are overloaded by greasy, sweet, or spicy foods, they produce a sticky, hot excess called Damp-Heat. This turbid Damp-Heat rises like steam to the scalp, where it clogs the hair follicles and overheats the skin. The result is the classic oily, red, itchy scalp with thick, yellowish, greasy flakes.
The digestive system is the root - bloating, a sticky taste in the mouth, and heavy limbs are clues that the scalp problem is actually coming from within.
- **Tongue & Pulse**: Tongue: red body, possibly swollen with teeth marks, thick yellow greasy coating especially at the centre and root. Pulse: soggy (濡 rú) and rapid (数 shuò), often slippery (滑 huá).
- **Triggers & Relief Explanation**: Greasy, spicy, and sweet foods directly create Dampness and Heat in the Stomach and Spleen - the very substances that rise to the scalp to cause oily flakes and redness. Alcohol and irregular eating further weaken the Spleen, making it harder to transform food into clean energy and easier for turbid damp-heat to accumulate. Conversely, cooling, light foods help the body drain excess damp-heat, and regular bowel movements clear the turbidity from the system. A calm digestive routine allows the Spleen to do its job, so less damp-heat is produced to steam upward.
- **Diet & Lifestyle**: Avoid greasy, fried, spicy, and heavily sweet foods, as well as alcohol - these directly feed Damp-Heat. Instead, favour cooling, light foods like bitter greens, barley, mung beans, and lightly steamed vegetables. Keep the scalp clean with a gentle, non-drying cleanser, but avoid over-washing which can irritate the skin.
Eat at regular times and chew well; a calm, unhurried meal supports the Spleen and Stomach's ability to process food without creating dampness.

### Heat in the Blood
*Pattern Link: [Detailed Pattern Page](/knowledge-base/patterns/heat-in-the-blood)*

- **Clinical Signs & Symptoms**: Fine, dry, white or silvery flakes, Red, inflamed patches on the scalp, Burning or prickly itching, Feeling of body heat, worse at night, Thirst with desire for cool drinks, Restlessness and irritability
- **Worse with**: Greasy, fried, or spicy foods, Emotional stress, Hot weather, Alcohol and coffee
- **Better with**: Cool environment, Cooling foods and drinks, Adequate rest and sleep
- **Why this happens (Mechanism)**:
  Excess Heat can build up in the Blood from emotional stress, a diet heavy in spicy or greasy foods, or internal imbalance. This Heat rises upward to the head, scorching the skin and stirring up internal Wind. The result is a dry, red, inflamed scalp that sheds fine, flaky scales, often with a burning sensation.
Because Heat consumes the body's fluids, you may also feel thirsty, have dry stools, or notice a flushed face. The skin loses its nourishment, so the flakes are dry rather than greasy, and the itching tends to be intense and prickly.
- **Tongue & Pulse**: Tongue: red body, possibly with red prickles, and a thin yellow dry coating. Pulse: rapid (数 shuò) and wiry (弦 xián), often with a slippery quality.
- **Triggers & Relief Explanation**: Heat in the Blood is a pattern of internal excess Heat that flares upward. Spicy foods, alcohol, and emotional stress stoke that Heat, making the scalp redder and itchier. Cooling foods, a calm environment, and rest help drain the Heat and soothe the Blood, which directly eases the burning and flaking.
- **Diet & Lifestyle**: Steer clear of spicy, fried, or greasy foods, and cut back on alcohol and coffee, as they add Heat to the Blood. Load up on cooling, hydrating foods like cucumber, watermelon, mung beans, and leafy greens. Keep your living space cool and practice gentle stress-relief activities like walking or meditation. Avoid over-washing with harsh shampoos; a mild, cooling herbal rinse can soothe the scalp.

### Blood Deficiency with External Wind
*Pattern Link: [Detailed Pattern Page](/knowledge-base/patterns/blood-deficiency-with-external-wind)*

- **Clinical Signs & Symptoms**: Fine, dry white scales on the scalp, Itching that worsens at night or in dry, windy weather, Pale or sallow complexion, Brittle nails and dry, rough hair, Dizziness or light-headedness, Sensitivity to wind or drafts
- **Worse with**: Dry, windy weather, Overwork and lack of sleep, Harsh, stripping shampoos, Greasy, fried, or spicy foods, Emotional stress
- **Better with**: Moisturizing scalp oils, Adequate rest and sleep, Warm, humid environment, Nourishing blood-building foods, Gentle head massage
- **Why this happens (Mechanism)**:
  When the body's Blood is chronically insufficient, it can no longer properly nourish and moisten the scalp. The skin becomes dry and undernourished, producing fine, white, bran-like flakes. At the same time, the defensive Qi weakens, allowing external Wind-Dryness to easily invade the surface, which triggers itching that comes and goes and often worsens in dry or windy weather.
- **Tongue & Pulse**: Tongue: pale, thin body with a thin white and slightly dry coat. Pulse: fine (thin), and may feel floating at the superficial level.
- **Triggers & Relief Explanation**: Blood nourishes and moistens the skin; when it is deficient, the scalp becomes dry and the body's protective shield against Wind weakens. Wind-Dryness invades and triggers itching. Rest, blood-building foods, and moisturizing oils replenish Blood and fluids, strengthening the surface so Wind cannot easily enter. Overwork, poor sleep, and dry, windy conditions further deplete Blood and Yin, allowing the pattern to flare.
- **Diet & Lifestyle**: Focus on deeply nourishing, blood-building foods like dark leafy greens, beets, black sesame seeds, eggs, and small amounts of organ meats. Avoid overly spicy, fried, or drying foods that consume Blood and fluids. Protect your scalp from dry, cold wind with a hat or scarf, and use gentle, moisturizing shampoos rather than harsh, clarifying ones. Prioritize consistent, restful sleep, as Blood is replenished during the night.

### Empty-Heat caused by Yin Deficiency
*Pattern Link: [Detailed Pattern Page](/knowledge-base/patterns/empty-heat-caused-by-yin-deficiency)*

- **Clinical Signs & Symptoms**: Dark red patches rather than bright red, Itching that worsens in the evening or at night, Night sweats, Dry mouth and throat with desire to sip water, Feeling of heat in the palms, soles, and chest, Restlessness or difficulty falling asleep
- **Worse with**: Overwork and lack of sleep, Greasy, fried, or spicy foods, Alcohol and coffee, Emotional stress, Hot, dry weather
- **Better with**: Adequate rest and sleep, Cooling foods and drinks, Cool environment, Gentle, non-sweaty movement
- **Why this happens (Mechanism)**:
  When the body's Yin fluids are chronically depleted, they can no longer anchor the body's warmth or nourish the skin. The resulting empty heat rises to the scalp, drying and inflaming the tissues and producing dark red patches with oily scales that feel hot and itchy. Because Yin and nighttime are naturally linked, the itching and discomfort often intensify in the evening or while trying to sleep.
- **Tongue & Pulse**: Tongue: red, thin, and dry body with cracks; little or no coating. Pulse: fine and rapid.
- **Triggers & Relief Explanation**: Yin is the body's cooling, moistening energy, and it is naturally restored during deep rest. Late nights, overwork, and emotional strain consume Yin, allowing empty heat to flare upward to the scalp. Spicy, fried, or heated foods and alcohol add more heat, while cooling, moistening foods and adequate sleep help replenish Yin and settle the heat, calming the skin.
- **Diet & Lifestyle**: Prioritize rest and avoid staying up late, as sleep is the body's natural time to rebuild Yin. Eat cooling, moistening foods like pear, tofu, cucumber, black sesame, and spinach; reduce or eliminate coffee, alcohol, and spicy or deep-fried foods that fan internal heat. Gentle exercise like walking or yoga is beneficial, but avoid sweating heavily, which can further deplete fluids.

## Frequently Asked Questions
### Why does my dandruff get so much worse when I eat spicy or greasy food?
Spicy and greasy foods generate what TCM calls Damp-Heat in your digestive system. This sticky, hot excess rises like steam to your scalp, overheating the skin and clogging the hair follicles. The result is an oily, red, itchy scalp with thick yellowish flakes. Cutting back on these foods is one of the quickest ways to calm a flare-up.

### My flakes are dry and white, but my scalp still feels hot. What does that mean?
Dry, white flakes with a burning sensation usually point to Heat in the Blood. This pattern is often triggered by emotional stress or a diet heavy in hot, spicy foods. The Heat rises to your head, scorching the scalp and stirring up an internal Wind that causes flaking. Cooling herbs and stress management can help a lot.

### Can dandruff be caused by being run-down or anemic?
Yes. When your body's Blood is deficient - from poor diet, chronic fatigue, or heavy menstrual bleeding - your scalp doesn't get enough nourishment. This leads to a dry, pale scalp with fine, white, bran-like flakes and persistent itching. Nourishing the Blood with diet and herbs is key to resolving this type.

### Is it safe to use Chinese herbs on my scalp while pregnant?
Many topical Chinese herbs are gentle, but pregnancy requires extra caution. Some herbs can be absorbed through the skin and may not be recommended. Always consult a qualified TCM practitioner who knows you are pregnant before using any herbal product, even externally.

### How long does it take for TCM to clear dandruff?
You may notice less itching and flaking within a few weeks, but lasting change comes from correcting the internal imbalance, which can take one to three months. The timeline depends on your pattern, how long you've had the condition, and your consistency with herbs, diet, and lifestyle changes.

### Can acupuncture alone treat dandruff?
Acupuncture can help reduce inflammation and calm the scalp, but for chronic dandruff it's usually most effective when combined with herbal medicine and dietary adjustments. The needles help rebalance the body's energy and clear wind and heat from the head, while herbs work more deeply on the internal pattern.
