## Conventional Medicine View
### Description
Nasal polyps are soft, painless, noncancerous growths that form on the lining of the nasal passages or sinuses. They result from chronic inflammation of the mucous membrane and are often associated with conditions like asthma, allergies, recurrent infections, or aspirin sensitivity. Typical symptoms include persistent nasal congestion, a reduced or lost sense of smell, a runny nose, postnasal drip, and facial pressure. Diagnosis is usually made through nasal endoscopy, sometimes with a CT scan to assess the extent.

### Treatments
Standard Western treatment typically starts with corticosteroid nasal sprays to shrink polyps and reduce inflammation. Oral corticosteroids may be used for short-term flare-ups. If medications are insufficient, surgery - such as polypectomy or endoscopic sinus surgery - is often performed. For severe, recurrent cases, biologic medications like dupilumab may be prescribed. However, polyps frequently recur after surgery if the underlying inflammation persists.

## Traditional Chinese Medicine View
### TCM Understanding
In TCM, the nose is the opening of the Lungs, so any chronic nasal problem is seen as a reflection of Lung function. But the whole picture involves more than just the Lungs. The Spleen transforms and transports fluids, and when it's weak, dampness and phlegm can accumulate. The Kidneys govern water metabolism and anchor the Qi that the Lungs breathe in - a Kidney weakness can allow phlegm to drift upward and settle in the nose.
Polyps are essentially a form of congealed phlegm-dampness. If the body's Qi is strong and fluids move freely, this accumulation doesn't happen. But when there's a deficiency (Spleen, Lung, or Kidney) or an obstruction (heat, phlegm, blood stasis), the normal flow is disrupted. The excess fluid thickens and sticks, gradually forming the soft, pale growths we call polyps.
That's why the same Western diagnosis can have very different TCM roots. One person's polyps may be linked to chronic fatigue and weak digestion (Spleen-Lung Qi Deficiency), while another's may flare after every cold (Wind-Heat invading the Lungs), and a third's may be dark, firm, and prone to bleeding (Blood Stasis with Heat). Each root requires a different treatment strategy - tonifying, clearing, or moving - not just a one-size-fits-all approach.

### Practitioner Diagnosis
A TCM practitioner starts by asking about the quality of the nasal blockage and any discharge, then looks at your energy, digestion, and body signs. The feel of the pulse and the appearance of the tongue are essential clues, because each pattern behind nasal polyps leaves a distinct fingerprint in these areas.
If fatigue, frequent colds, a pale face, and clear or white watery nasal discharge dominate, Spleen and Lung Qi Deficiency is likely. The tongue is pale with a thin white coat, and the pulse is weak and thready. The polyps are usually soft and pale, and symptoms worsen with overwork.
When the nose produces thick, sticky, and copious mucus, and you feel a heavy sensation in the head and chest, Damp-Phlegm in the Lungs is the main driver. The tongue coating is thick and greasy, the pulse is slippery, and the polyps look boggy and swollen. This pattern often coexists with digestive sluggishness.
A more acute, irritable picture-yellow discharge, an itchy nose, sneezing, and perhaps a mild fever or facial redness-points to Wind-Heat invading the Lungs. The tongue is red with a thin yellow coating, and the pulse is floating and rapid. The polyps are inflamed and tender.
In long-standing cases with lower back and knee soreness, frequent nighttime urination, and a deep sense of cold, Kidney Qi Deficiency may be the root. The tongue is pale and the pulse is deep and weak. The nasal symptoms are stubborn and tend to recur, with a clear or thin discharge.
When the polyps feel firm, look dark, or bleed easily, and the complexion is dull or dark, Blood Stagnation with Heat is present. The tongue is purplish with a yellow coat, and the pulse feels choppy. This pattern often emerges after years of chronic inflammation.

## TCM Patterns and Treatment

### Spleen and Lung Qi Deficiency
*Pattern Link: [Detailed Pattern Page](/knowledge-base/patterns/spleen-and-lung-qi-deficiency)*

- **Clinical Signs & Symptoms**: Clear, watery or white nasal discharge, Nasal congestion that worsens with fatigue, Chronic fatigue and weakness, Poor appetite, bloating, or loose stools, Shortness of breath on exertion, Frequent colds or respiratory infections
- **Worse with**: Overwork and exhaustion, Cold, raw foods and iced drinks, Damp, cold weather, Excessive worry or overthinking, Recurrent respiratory infections
- **Better with**: Rest and adequate sleep, Warm, nourishing foods, Warm environments, Gentle exercise, Steam inhalation
- **Why this happens (Mechanism)**:
  When the Spleen and Lungs are weak, they fail to transform and transport fluids properly. This leads to an accumulation of dampness and phlegm, which congeals in the nasal passages and gradually forms soft, pale polyps. Because the body's defensive Qi is also weak, you may catch colds easily, and each infection adds more dampness, making the polyps grow or return.
- **Tongue & Pulse**: Tongue: pale, puffy, tender body with teeth marks and a thin white slippery coating. Pulse: weak and forceless, especially deficient in the right cun (Lung) and right guan (Spleen) positions.
- **Triggers & Relief Explanation**: Spleen Qi is easily damaged by cold and raw foods, overwork, and mental strain, which then fails to control dampness. Lung Qi is weakened by cold air and repeated infections, losing its ability to keep the nose clear. Rest, warmth, and gentle movement conserve and rebuild Qi, while steam directly moistens and opens the nasal passages, giving temporary relief as the underlying deficiency is corrected.
- **Diet & Lifestyle**: Eat warm, cooked foods like soups, stews, and congee, and avoid raw, cold, or greasy items that weaken the Spleen. Gentle daily movement such as walking or tai chi builds Qi without exhausting you. Keep the nose and neck warm in cold weather, and use a humidifier or steam inhalation to keep nasal passages clear. Manage worry and overthinking, as these emotions directly weaken the Spleen.

### Damp-Phlegm in the Lungs
*Pattern Link: [Detailed Pattern Page](/knowledge-base/patterns/damp-phlegm-in-the-lungs)*

- **Clinical Signs & Symptoms**: Nasal congestion with thick, white or grey-white sticky discharge, Feeling of heaviness and fullness in the chest, Cough with copious white phlegm that is easy to expectorate, Poor appetite, abdominal bloating, and loose stools, Swollen tongue with teeth marks and a thick white greasy coating, Slippery pulse that feels like beads rolling under the finger
- **Worse with**: Damp or humid weather, Cold, raw foods and iced drinks, Dairy products, Sedentary lifestyle, Overeating
- **Better with**: Warm, dry weather, Steam inhalation, Light, cooked meals, Ginger tea, Gentle exercise
- **Why this happens (Mechanism)**:
  In TCM, the Lungs open into the nose. When the Lungs are burdened by Dampness and Phlegm, the normal downward flow of Lung Qi is disrupted and the excess fluid congeals into sticky mucus. This pathological Phlegm-Dampness rises and settles in the nasal passages, causing the chronic swelling and tissue overgrowth that form polyps.
The root of the problem often lies in a weak Spleen that fails to transform and transport fluids, allowing Dampness to accumulate. This Dampness then collects in the Lungs, creating a cycle of congestion and obstruction that makes the nose feel perpetually heavy and blocked.
- **Tongue & Pulse**: Tongue: pale, swollen body with teeth marks and a thick, white, greasy coating. Pulse: slippery (滑 huá), often with a soggy quality, and particularly full in the right cun position.
- **Triggers & Relief Explanation**: Damp-Phlegm is a heavy, sticky pathogen that thrives in cool, humid environments and is directly worsened by foods that create more Dampness, such as dairy, cold raw foods, and overeating. A sluggish lifestyle further congeals fluids. Warmth, both from the environment and from cooked, spiced foods, dries Dampness and helps the Spleen transform fluids. Steam inhalation physically moistens and then drains the nasal passages, mimicking the drying action of aromatic herbs, while gentle exercise moves Qi, which is essential for draining accumulated Dampness.
- **Diet & Lifestyle**: Avoid cold, raw, and greasy foods as well as dairy, which are notoriously damp-producing. Focus on warm, cooked meals like soups and congees with a little ginger. Steaming the face over a bowl of hot water with a towel over the head can help open the nasal passages and dry local dampness. Gentle daily movement like walking supports the Spleen and keeps Qi flowing.

### Wind-Heat invading the Lungs
*Pattern Link: [Detailed Pattern Page](/knowledge-base/patterns/wind-heat-invading-the-lungs)*

- **Clinical Signs & Symptoms**: Nasal congestion with thick yellow or turbid mucus, Fever that feels stronger than any chills, Sore or painful throat, Cough with thick yellow phlegm, Thirst or dry mouth, Red tongue tip with thin yellow coating
- **Worse with**: Spicy, greasy, or fried foods, Alcohol, Exposure to wind or drafts, Overwork or stress
- **Better with**: Rest in a cool, well-ventilated room, Drinking warm fluids, Cool mist humidifier
- **Why this happens (Mechanism)**:
  When an external Wind-Heat invasion strikes the body, it first attacks the Lungs, which open into the nose. The heat and inflammation surge upward into the nasal passages, causing acute swelling, congestion, and thick yellow mucus. If this acute inflammation is not resolved or recurs frequently, the persistent heat and dampness can trigger the formation of soft, inflamed polyps.
- **Tongue & Pulse**: Tongue: Red tip, thin yellow coating, body slightly red. Pulse: Floating and rapid.
- **Triggers & Relief Explanation**: Wind-Heat invades the Lungs and rises to the nose, causing inflammation and swelling. Spicy, greasy foods and alcohol add internal heat, while wind exposure introduces more external pathogen. Rest, cooling fluids, and a calm environment help the body's defenses expel the pathogen and reduce nasal congestion.
- **Diet & Lifestyle**: Drink plenty of warm water or chrysanthemum tea. Avoid spicy, greasy, or fried foods that generate internal heat. Protect yourself from wind and drafts, especially after sweating. Use a cool-mist humidifier to soothe dry nasal passages.

### Kidney Qi Deficiency
*Pattern Link: [Detailed Pattern Page](/knowledge-base/patterns/kidney-qi-deficiency)*

- **Clinical Signs & Symptoms**: Pale, soft nasal polyps with clear or white discharge, Soreness and weakness of the lower back and knees, Frequent urination, especially at night, Fatigue and lack of stamina, Ringing in the ears (tinnitus) or dizziness
- **Worse with**: Overwork and exhaustion, Cold, raw foods and iced drinks, Chronic stress and fear
- **Better with**: Warmth and rest, Warm, nourishing foods, Gentle exercise
- **Why this happens (Mechanism)**:
  The Kidneys govern water metabolism and anchor the Qi that the Lungs breathe in. When Kidney Qi is weak, the body cannot properly transform and transport fluids, allowing phlegm-dampness to form and drift upward. This dampness settles in the nasal passages and, over time, congeals into the soft, pale growths we call nasal polyps.
Because the Kidneys are the root of all Yang energy, this deficiency also weakens the Lungs' ability to keep the nose clear, making polyps slow to resolve and prone to recurrence.
- **Tongue & Pulse**: Tongue: pale, slightly puffy body with possible tooth marks and a thin white coating. Pulse: deep (chen), weak (ruo), and possibly fine (xi), weakest at the chi (rear) position.
- **Triggers & Relief Explanation**: Kidney Qi is depleted by overwork, cold, and emotional strain, especially fear. Rest, warmth, and nourishing foods rebuild this root energy, allowing the body to properly manage fluids and prevent dampness from accumulating in the nose. Gentle movement keeps Qi flowing without further draining the Kidneys.
- **Diet & Lifestyle**: Warm, cooked foods like soups, stews, and congees are your allies. Include kidney-nourishing ingredients such as black beans, walnuts, and lamb. Avoid raw, cold, and icy foods that further weaken Kidney Qi. Gentle daily movement like walking or tai chi helps circulate Qi without depleting it.

### Blood Stagnation with Heat
*Pattern Link: [Detailed Pattern Page](/knowledge-base/patterns/blood-stagnation-with-heat)*

- **Clinical Signs & Symptoms**: Fixed stabbing pain in the nose or face, Polyps appear dark or purplish, Nasal discharge or bleeding is dark and clotted, Sensation of internal heat, worse at night, Irritability and restlessness, Thirst with a desire to rinse the mouth but not swallow
- **Worse with**: Spicy, greasy, or fried foods, Alcohol, Emotional stress, Sedentary lifestyle, Hot, dry weather
- **Better with**: Gentle exercise, Cooling foods, Deep breathing, Acupuncture
- **Why this happens (Mechanism)**:
  When blood flow slows and stagnates in the fine vessels of the nose, it creates a local blockage that can harden into tissue growths. If heat from inflammation or chronic irritation is also present, this combination "cooks" the stagnant blood, causing the polyps to become dark, firm, and prone to bleeding. The result is a persistent, stabbing nasal congestion that feels fixed and unchanging.
- **Tongue & Pulse**: Tongue: reddish-purple body with stasis spots and distended sublingual veins, thin dry yellow coating. Pulse: choppy (涩, sè) and rapid (数, shuò), often wiry at the left Guan position.
- **Triggers & Relief Explanation**: Spicy and greasy foods fuel internal heat, which congeals blood and worsens the stasis that underpins these polyps. Alcohol and emotional stress stir up Liver fire, further heating the blood and making polyps more prone to bleed. Gentle movement and deep breathing keep Qi and blood moving, preventing the local stagnation that allows polyps to harden and grow.
- **Diet & Lifestyle**: Eat cooling and blood-moving foods like eggplant, hawthorn, and a little vinegar; avoid spicy, greasy, and deep-fried foods that add heat. Gentle daily movement such as walking or tai chi helps keep blood flowing smoothly. Manage stress with relaxation practices, as emotional constraint can worsen stagnation.

## Frequently Asked Questions
### Can acupuncture really shrink nasal polyps?
Yes, acupuncture can help reduce polyp size by improving local circulation, reducing inflammation, and addressing the underlying imbalance that allowed the polyp to form. Points like Yingxiang (LI-20) directly target the nasal passages, while distal points strengthen the Lungs, Spleen, or Kidneys depending on your pattern. Results are gradual and best when combined with herbal medicine.

### Will I need to take herbs forever?
No. Herbal formulas are typically taken for several weeks to months until the polyps shrink and symptoms stabilize. Once the underlying imbalance is corrected, many people stop daily herbs and may only use them during flare-ups or seasonally. Your practitioner will adjust the plan as you improve.

### Can TCM help after polyp surgery?
Absolutely. TCM is excellent for post-surgical recovery - it can reduce inflammation, speed healing, and most importantly, address the root cause to prevent recurrence. Many patients use acupuncture and herbs after surgery to maintain clear sinuses long-term.

### Is TCM safe if I'm using corticosteroid nasal sprays?
Yes, TCM can generally be used alongside corticosteroid sprays. There are no known major interactions, but always inform both your TCM practitioner and your ENT doctor about all treatments. As your symptoms improve, your doctor may adjust the spray dosage - never stop prescribed medications abruptly without consulting them.

### How long until I can breathe better?
Many people notice improved nasal airflow within 2-4 weeks of starting acupuncture and herbs. Actual polyp shrinkage takes longer - typically 6-12 weeks for noticeable reduction. The timeline depends on your pattern and how long the polyps have been present.

### What foods should I avoid?
In general, avoid dairy products, greasy or fried foods, and cold raw foods - these all promote dampness and phlegm. Sugar and refined carbohydrates can also contribute. Favor warm, cooked meals with spices like ginger and garlic, which help dry excess dampness. For pattern-specific dietary advice, see the individual pattern tabs.

### Does TCM work for all types of nasal polyps?
TCM is most effective for inflammatory polyps linked to chronic sinusitis or allergies. It may be less effective for very large, obstructive polyps that require surgical removal. Even then, TCM can help prevent regrowth. A thorough evaluation will determine if TCM is appropriate for your case.

1. **Acupuncture for chronic rhinosinusitis: a systematic review**
   *Journal/Publication: Kim SY, Kim JI, Lee H, et al. Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews. 2015;Issue 11. Art. No.: CD011994.*
   A Cochrane systematic review assessed acupuncture as an adjunctive treatment for chronic rhinosinusitis, including patients with nasal polyps. The review found limited high-quality evidence but noted that acupuncture may improve quality of life and reduce congestion when added to usual care.
