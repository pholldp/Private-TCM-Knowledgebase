## Conventional Medicine View
### Description
Hand eczema is a chronic inflammatory skin condition that causes redness, itching, scaling, cracking, and sometimes blisters on the hands. It is often triggered by a combination of genetic susceptibility and repeated exposure to irritants like water, detergents, or chemicals, or to allergens such as nickel or latex. Diagnosis is usually made by a dermatologist based on the appearance of the skin and a history of occupational or household exposures, and sometimes patch testing is used to identify specific triggers.

### Treatments
Conventional treatment typically starts with regular use of emollients to repair the skin barrier and avoidance of known irritants. Topical corticosteroids are the mainstay for reducing inflammation during flares, while calcineurin inhibitors may be used for sensitive areas. For severe or persistent cases, phototherapy or systemic medications like oral steroids, immunosuppressants, or biologics may be prescribed.

## Traditional Chinese Medicine View
### TCM Understanding
In TCM, the skin is governed by the Lung system, but hand eczema almost always involves a deeper web of organs. The Spleen manages fluids - when it is weak, Dampness accumulates and can spill over into the skin. The Liver stores Blood, which nourishes and moistens the skin; when Blood is deficient, the skin becomes dry and brittle. The Kidneys supply the body’s fundamental Yin and Yang, and their depletion leads to chronic dryness or cold, damp eczema. Hand eczema is therefore a reflection of what is happening inside, not just a surface problem.
Acute, weepy flares are usually caused by an invasion of external pathogens - Wind, Damp, and Heat - that penetrate the skin’s defenses and combine with internal Damp-Heat from a struggling Spleen. The result is red, swollen, oozing blisters with an intense burning itch. This pattern often appears after heavy exposure to water or chemicals, or after eating rich, spicy foods that fuel internal Heat.
When the condition drags on, the internal picture shifts. Long-standing inflammation consumes Blood and Yin, drying out the skin and leading to painful cracks and thick, scaly patches. At this stage, the root is a deficiency, not an excess. The eczema may look less angry but is harder to heal because the body’s nourishing reserves are depleted. Some chronic cases also involve Blood Stagnation, where poor circulation in the hands leaves the skin thickened and purplish.
Because the same Western diagnosis can arise from such different internal landscapes - Damp-Heat, Spleen deficiency, Blood deficiency, or even Yang deficiency with Cold-Damp - TCM treatment is never one-size-fits-all. The pattern is identified through a detailed examination of the skin, the tongue, the pulse, and accompanying symptoms like digestion, thirst, and sleep. This is why two people with hand eczema may receive completely different herbal formulas and acupuncture protocols.

### Practitioner Diagnosis
A TCM practitioner begins by looking at the skin itself and asking about what makes it better or worse. The appearance of the rash-whether it is red, weeping, dry, or thickened-and the timing of the itch are the first big clues that point toward one pattern rather than another. The tongue and pulse are then checked to confirm the picture.
When the hands are red, swollen, and dotted with tiny blisters that ooze clear fluid, and the itching feels hot and relentless, the Damp-Heat pattern is likely. The tongue will often be red with a thick, yellow, greasy coating, and the pulse feels slippery and rapid. Questions about a heavy feeling in the body, thirst, or dark urine help seal this acute, fiery diagnosis.
If the rash has become dull red and scaly rather than weepy, and the person also struggles with bloating, loose stools, or a heavy sensation after eating, the focus shifts to Spleen Deficiency with Dampness. Here the tongue looks pale and puffy, sometimes with tooth marks on the sides, and the coating is white and sticky. The pulse tends to be soft or slippery-slow, reflecting a digestive system that is too weak to manage fluids.
Chronic dryness tells a different story. When the skin is rough, cracked, and peeling, and the itch flares at night, the pattern is often Blood Deficiency with External Wind. The tongue is pale with a thin coating, and the pulse feels thin and maybe slightly wiry.
If the dryness is even more extreme-with fine cracks like a dried-up riverbed, a red tongue with little coating, and thirst-the picture has shifted into Yin and Blood Deficiency, where the body’s cooling and moistening reserves are depleted.
In very long-standing cases, the skin may become thick, leathery, and almost purple-tinged, sometimes with stabbing pain. This points to Blood Stagnation from chronic inflammation.
A much rarer scenario presents with cold hands and an aversion to cold, alongside a pale tongue with a white coating, suggesting Yang Deficiency with Cold-Damp. These last two patterns usually emerge only after years of struggle and are less common.

## TCM Patterns and Treatment

### Damp-Heat
*Pattern Link: [Detailed Pattern Page](/knowledge-base/patterns/damp-heat)*

- **Clinical Signs & Symptoms**: Weeping, oozing blisters, Intense burning itch, Red, swollen skin, Bitter taste and sticky mouth, Feeling of heaviness and sluggishness, Loose, sticky stools
- **Worse with**: Hot, humid weather, Spicy, fried foods, Alcohol, Frequent hand washing or wet work, Emotional stress
- **Better with**: Rest in a cool, dry place, Cold compresses, Light, non-greasy meals, Keeping hands dry
- **Why this happens (Mechanism)**:
  Damp-Heat is a sticky mix of excess moisture and internal warmth that gets trapped under the skin. When it settles in the hands, it inflames the tissue, producing red, swollen, weeping blisters and an intense burning itch. This pattern often flares after wet work, humid weather, or eating rich, spicy foods that brew more Damp-Heat in the body.
- **Tongue & Pulse**: Tongue: red body with a yellow, greasy coating that is difficult to scrape off, may be swollen with teeth marks. Pulse: soggy-rapid or slippery-rapid.
- **Triggers & Relief Explanation**: Damp-Heat is directly fueled by anything that adds moisture or heat to the body. Humid air, greasy food, and alcohol all brew more Dampness and Heat, while stress stirs up internal Heat. Cooling, drying measures-like cold compresses and bland foods-help drain the excess and clear the pattern, and keeping hands dry stops external Dampness from seeping in.
- **Diet & Lifestyle**: Avoid alcohol, spicy, greasy, and sweet foods that generate Damp-Heat. Eat cooling, damp-draining foods like mung beans, cucumber, and celery. Keep hands dry and wear protective gloves when washing dishes or cleaning.

### Spleen Deficiency with Dampness
*Pattern Link: [Detailed Pattern Page](/knowledge-base/patterns/spleen-deficiency-with-dampness)*

- **Clinical Signs & Symptoms**: Dull red, scaly patches on hands, Bloating and loose stools, Fatigue and heaviness in limbs, Pale, swollen tongue with teeth marks, Worsens with fatigue and overwork
- **Worse with**: Cold, raw, or greasy foods, Overwork and fatigue, Damp, humid weather, Stress and worry, Prolonged water exposure
- **Better with**: Warm, cooked meals, Rest and adequate sleep, Gentle exercise, Dry, warm environment, Warm drinks like ginger tea
- **Why this happens (Mechanism)**:
  When the Spleen is weak, it cannot properly transform and transport fluids. This leads to internal Dampness that overflows to the skin, settling on the hands and producing dull red papules with scaling. Because the root is a deficiency, the eczema tends to linger and worsen when you are tired or after eating heavy foods that further burden the Spleen.
- **Tongue & Pulse**: Tongue: pale, swollen body with teeth marks and a white greasy coating. Pulse: soggy (Ru) and slow (Huan), possibly slippery.
- **Triggers & Relief Explanation**: Cold and raw foods weaken Spleen Yang and increase internal Dampness, while warm cooked meals support Spleen Qi and help transform moisture. Overwork and fatigue deplete Spleen Qi, worsening fluid metabolism, so rest is crucial. Damp environments add external Dampness to the body, burdening the already weak Spleen. Gentle exercise moves Qi and dries Dampness without exhausting the Spleen.
- **Diet & Lifestyle**: Eat warm, cooked meals and avoid cold, raw, or greasy foods. Include barley, adzuki beans, and ginger to help drain dampness. Keep hands dry but avoid harsh soaps; use gentle, moisturizing cleansers. Moderate exercise like walking helps move Qi and dry dampness. Manage stress with relaxation techniques.

### Blood Deficiency with External Wind
*Pattern Link: [Detailed Pattern Page](/knowledge-base/patterns/blood-deficiency-with-external-wind)*

- **Clinical Signs & Symptoms**: Dry, flaky, or cracked skin on hands, Itching that worsens at night, Pale or sallow complexion, Dull, brittle nails, Dizziness or light-headedness
- **Worse with**: Dry, windy weather, Stress and worry, Overwork or lack of sleep, Spicy, greasy, or fried foods, Hot showers and harsh soaps
- **Better with**: Moisturising the skin, Rest and adequate sleep, Warm, humid weather, Blood-nourishing foods
- **Why this happens (Mechanism)**:
  Long-standing hand eczema gradually consumes the body's Blood, which normally moistens and nourishes the skin. When Blood is insufficient, the skin loses its lubrication and becomes dry, flaky, and brittle. This internal dryness then generates a type of 'Wind' that stirs upward and outward, causing intense itching that shifts and worsens when the body is still, especially at night.
- **Tongue & Pulse**: Tongue: pale, thin body with a thin, white, and slightly dry coating. Pulse: thin (fine) overall, often with a wiry quality at the left middle position and a floating quality at the right front position.
- **Triggers & Relief Explanation**: Blood Deficiency means the skin lacks sufficient nourishment and moisture, so anything that further dries the body - dry winds, hot water, or depleting activities - worsens the itching. Stress and overwork consume Blood, aggravating the root deficiency. Conversely, moisturising the skin, resting, and eating Blood-tonifying foods directly replenish what is missing, while gentle, humid conditions soothe the internal Wind and dryness.
- **Diet & Lifestyle**: Eat regular meals with Blood-nourishing foods like dark leafy greens, beets, black sesame, bone broth, and moderate amounts of animal protein. Avoid overly drying habits such as excessive hot showers, harsh soaps, or prolonged exposure to wind. Keep hands well-moisturized with a gentle, fragrance-free cream, and protect them with gloves when outdoors in cold, dry weather.

### Yin and Blood Deficiency
*Pattern Link: [Detailed Pattern Page](/knowledge-base/patterns/yin-and-blood-deficiency)*

- **Clinical Signs & Symptoms**: Dry, cracked, or fissured skin on hands, Intense itching worse at night or in dry weather, Dry mouth and thirst, Night sweats or warmth in the palms and soles, Fatigue with a restless, uneasy quality, Pale or sallow complexion with possible malar flush
- **Worse with**: Dry, cold weather, Frequent hand washing with hot water, Spicy or fried foods, Stress and overwork
- **Better with**: Applying thick, fragrance-free moisturiser, Eating moistening foods like pears and congee, Rest and good sleep, Cool, humid environment
- **Why this happens (Mechanism)**:
  When hand eczema lingers for months or years, it gradually consumes the body's Yin and Blood-the deep cooling and nourishing substances that keep skin supple and resilient. Without enough Yin to moisten and Blood to nourish, the skin on the hands becomes severely dry, thin, and prone to painful cracks. The deficiency also generates a low-grade internal heat, which further parches the skin and causes thirst, night sweats, and a restless sensation.
- **Tongue & Pulse**: Tongue: pale-red, thin, dry body with cracks, scanty or peeled coating. Pulse: fine (Xi) and rapid (Shu).
- **Triggers & Relief Explanation**: Yin and Blood are the body's cooling, moistening resources. Dry environments, hot water, and heating foods deplete them further, while rest, nourishing foods, and moisturisers replenish them and calm internal heat. Stress and overwork burn through Yin, so calming the mind and resting help the skin heal.
- **Diet & Lifestyle**: Focus on moistening, Yin-nourishing foods like pears, black sesame, tofu, congee, and bone broth. Avoid spices, coffee, and alcohol, which dry out the body. Use lukewarm water for washing hands and apply a thick, fragrance-free moisturiser immediately after. Wear protective gloves for wet work, and aim for consistent, restful sleep to rebuild Yin.

### Blood Stagnation
*Pattern Link: [Detailed Pattern Page](/knowledge-base/patterns/blood-stagnation)*

- **Clinical Signs & Symptoms**: Fixed, stabbing pain in the hands, Pain worse at night, Thickened, lichenified skin with cracks, Purplish or dark discoloration of the skin, Dark menstrual blood with clots (if menstruating)
- **Worse with**: Cold exposure, Prolonged inactivity, Stress, Nighttime
- **Better with**: Warmth, Gentle movement, Rest
- **Why this happens (Mechanism)**:
  Chronic hand eczema often leaves Qi and Blood circulation sluggish. Over time, the stagnation deepens into Blood stasis, which blocks the channels in the hands. This leads to thickened, lichenified skin that may turn purplish, and the stabbing pain that is typical of Blood stasis.
- **Tongue & Pulse**: Tongue: purple or dusky body with stasis spots, distended dark sublingual veins, thin white coat. Pulse: choppy (涩, sè) or wiry (弦, xián).
- **Triggers & Relief Explanation**: Cold constricts the vessels and slows blood flow, which directly worsens stasis. Warmth and gentle movement encourage Qi and Blood to circulate, easing the obstruction. Stress tightens the Liver Qi, and since the Liver stores Blood, this stagnation further impedes blood flow, while rest and calm allow Qi to smooth out again. Nighttime is Yin time when blood flow naturally slows, so stasis pain becomes more noticeable.
- **Diet & Lifestyle**: Warm the hands and avoid cold water exposure. Gentle daily movement like walking or hand exercises promotes circulation. Include blood-moving foods such as turmeric, dark berries, and leafy greens, while minimizing cold, raw items that can congeal blood.

### Yang Deficiency with Cold-Damp

- **Clinical Signs & Symptoms**: Cold hands and feet, Aversion to cold, especially along the back, Eczema lesions are pale or skin-colored, not red, Oozing of clear, watery fluid, Fatigue and desire to lie down
- **Worse with**: Cold weather or cold water exposure, Raw, cold foods and icy drinks, Overwork and fatigue, Damp, humid conditions, Prolonged standing or sitting in cold
- **Better with**: Warmth and warm compresses, Warm drinks and soups, Rest and sleep, Dry, warm environment, Gentle movement
- **Why this happens (Mechanism)**:
  When the body's foundational Yang energy is depleted, it fails to warm the skin and transform fluids. Cold and dampness accumulate in the hand's channels, causing eczema that is pale, moist, and itchy, with cold extremities and a general aversion to cold. Unlike hot, red eczema, this pattern reflects a deep internal coldness that slows healing and leads to chronic, stubborn lesions.
- **Tongue & Pulse**: Tongue: pale, swollen body with teeth marks, white greasy moist coating. Pulse: deep, slow, and possibly soggy.
- **Triggers & Relief Explanation**: Yang deficiency means the body's warming fire is weak, so cold and dampness easily accumulate and worsen the eczema. Warmth, rest, and warm foods support Yang and help transform dampness, while cold and fatigue further deplete Yang, making the condition worse.
- **Diet & Lifestyle**: Eat warm, cooked foods like soups and stews; add ginger, cinnamon, and black pepper. Avoid raw, cold foods and icy drinks. Keep hands warm with gloves, especially in cold weather. Gentle exercise like walking or tai chi helps move Yang and dispel dampness.

## Frequently Asked Questions
### Can acupuncture really help with hand eczema?
Yes, acupuncture can be very effective for hand eczema, especially for reducing inflammation and itching. Points like Quchi (LI-11) and Hegu (LI-4) on the arms and hands are used to clear Heat and relieve itching locally, while points on the legs and abdomen like Zusanli (ST-36) and Sanyinjiao (SP-6) strengthen the Spleen to address the root of Dampness. Many patients notice less intense itching after just a few sessions, though healing the skin itself takes longer.

### How long does it take for Chinese herbs to work on hand eczema?
For acute, weepy eczema driven by Damp-Heat, you may see a reduction in oozing and redness within 1-2 weeks of taking the right herbal formula. Chronic dry eczema responds more slowly - expect gradual improvement over 4-8 weeks, with full resolution often taking several months. Herbs work by correcting the internal imbalance, so patience and consistency are key.

### Do I need to change my diet for hand eczema?
Diet plays a supporting role in TCM treatment. Generally, you’ll want to avoid foods that create Dampness and Heat - think dairy, sugar, greasy or fried foods, alcohol, and excessive spicy dishes. Focus on warm, cooked meals like congee, steamed vegetables, and easily digested proteins. Your practitioner may give more specific advice based on your pattern, such as adding moistening foods for Blood deficiency or avoiding cold, raw foods for Spleen deficiency.

### Can I use TCM alongside my steroid creams?
Yes, TCM and conventional treatments can be used together, and many people start herbs and acupuncture while still using their topical steroids. As the skin improves, you may find you need the creams less often, but always discuss any changes with both your dermatologist and TCM practitioner. Never stop prescribed medications abruptly without medical guidance.

### Is TCM safe for hand eczema during pregnancy?
Acupuncture is generally considered safe during pregnancy when performed by a qualified practitioner who avoids certain points. Herbal medicine, however, requires careful selection - some herbs are contraindicated in pregnancy. Always tell your practitioner you are pregnant so they can adjust your treatment plan. Mild dietary changes and external herbal washes may be safer options during this time.

### Does hand eczema ever go away completely with TCM?
Many people achieve long-term remission where the skin stays clear for months or years. However, because hand eczema often involves a constitutional tendency, occasional maintenance - like seasonal acupuncture or a brief course of herbs - may be needed to prevent recurrence, especially if you are exposed to triggers like frequent handwashing or cold, dry weather. TCM aims to strengthen your body so it becomes less reactive over time.

   In this RCT, patients with acute eczema treated with Xiao Feng San experienced a significant reduction in itching, erythema, and lesion area compared to a control group receiving conventional antihistamines.
