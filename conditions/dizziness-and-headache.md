## Conventional Medicine View
### Description
In conventional medicine, dizziness and headache are often evaluated as separate symptoms that may share an underlying cause, such as migraine, vestibular disorders, or tension-type headaches. Diagnosis typically involves a detailed history, neurological examination, and sometimes imaging or balance testing to rule out serious conditions. Treatment is then directed at the identified cause, but when no clear structural problem is found, the symptoms can be frustratingly difficult to manage.

### Treatments
Standard treatments may include pain relievers, anti-vertigo medications like meclizine, or preventive drugs for migraine. Physical therapy for balance disorders, stress management, and lifestyle modifications are also commonly recommended.

## Traditional Chinese Medicine View
### TCM Understanding
In TCM, the head is the meeting point of all Yang channels and the home of the clear orifices - the senses and the mind. For the head to feel light and steady, Qi and Blood must flow upward freely, and the brain must be nourished by Kidney Essence. Dizziness and headache arise when this balance is disrupted, either by something pathological rising to disturb the head or by a lack of nourishment reaching it. The quality of your symptoms - throbbing, heavy, hollow, or stabbing - tells the practitioner exactly which pattern is at play.
The Liver is often the first place a TCM practitioner looks. When Liver Yin or Blood is too weak to anchor the Yang, that fiery energy can float upward like an unanchored balloon. It rushes to the head, creating a distending, throbbing headache and a dizzy, unsteady sensation, often with irritability and a flushed face. This is Liver Yang Rising, and it is typically triggered or worsened by stress and anger.
The Spleen is equally important. If the Spleen is weak and fails to transform fluids properly, Dampness accumulates and thickens into Phlegm. This turbid substance can rise and cloud the head, causing a heavy, foggy dizziness and a dull headache, as if your head is wrapped in a wet cloth. Nausea and a stuffy chest are common, and the tongue will have a thick, greasy coating. When Heat combines with Phlegm, the dizziness becomes more severe and the person feels agitated and irritable.
Deficiency patterns are also common. In Qi and Blood Deficiency, the head simply isn't receiving enough fuel. The dizziness worsens with fatigue or standing, and the headache is dull and diffuse. The face is pale, and the pulse feels thin and weak.
Kidney Essence Deficiency, often from aging or overwork, leads to a chronic, hollow dizziness and an empty headache, because the brain's marrow is not being replenished. And in some cases, long-standing stagnation or old injuries cause Blood Stagnation, leading to a fixed, stabbing pain and dizziness that may worsen at night.

### Practitioner Diagnosis
A practitioner starts by asking what the dizziness and headache actually feel like. The quality of the pain and the spinning sensation, along with when symptoms strike and what makes them better or worse, are the first clues that point toward one pattern rather than another.
If the headache is distending or pulsating and the person feels irritable, flushed, and has a bitter taste in the mouth, the picture points to Liver Yang Rising. The tongue is usually red with a yellow coating, and the pulse feels taut like a guitar string (wiry).
When dizziness worsens with even mild exertion and the headache is dull rather than sharp, accompanied by a pale face, fatigue, and a weak pulse, the pattern is Qi and Blood Deficiency. The tongue looks pale and puffy, and the person often feels better after rest.
A heavy, wrapped-up sensation in the head with nausea, a stuffy chest, and a thick greasy tongue coating points to Phlegm-Dampness in the Middle-Burner. The dizziness here often feels like being on a boat, and the pulse is slippery, as if rolling beads are passing under the fingers.
Chronic dizziness with tinnitus, sore lower back, weak knees, and poor memory suggests Kidney Essence Deficiency. The tongue may be red with little coating (if yin is depleted) or pale and swollen (if yang is weak), and the pulse is deep and thready.
Fixed, stabbing headache with dizziness and a dark tongue showing small stasis spots signals Blood Stagnation. The pulse tends to be rough or choppy, and the pain does not move around.
In contrast, severe dizziness with headache, irritability, and a bitter taste combined with a thick yellow tongue coating and a rapid wiry pulse points to Phlegm-Heat harassing the upper orifices.

## TCM Patterns and Treatment

### Liver Yang Rising
*Pattern Link: [Detailed Pattern Page](/knowledge-base/patterns/liver-yang-rising)*

- **Clinical Signs & Symptoms**: Distending or throbbing headache, especially at the temples, Dizziness with a sense of unsteadiness, Irritability and quick temper, Flushed face and red eyes, Ringing in the ears (tinnitus), Dry mouth and throat
- **Worse with**: Anger and frustration, Spicy or greasy foods, Hot weather or stuffy rooms, Overwork
- **Better with**: Rest in a quiet, cool room, Cool, light meals, Gentle movement
- **Why this happens (Mechanism)**:
  When Liver Yin and Blood are too weak to anchor the Yang, that fiery energy drifts upward like a loose balloon. It rushes to the head, creating a distending, throbbing pain at the temples and a dizzy, unsteady sensation. The inner wind and heat also stir up irritability and a flushed face.
- **Tongue & Pulse**: Tongue: red body, redder at the sides, with a thin dry yellow or scanty coating. Pulse: wiry (弦 xián), often forceful at the left middle position; may be fine and rapid if Yin deficiency is prominent.
- **Triggers & Relief Explanation**: Liver Yang rises when stress, anger, or heat stir it upward - that is exactly what triggers the throbbing head pain and dizziness. Cooling foods, rest, and calm environments help anchor the Yang back down, while gentle movement smooths the Liver’s flow so energy doesn’t surge to the head.
- **Diet & Lifestyle**: Favor cooling, yin-nourishing foods like cucumber, celery, pear, and spinach. Keep alcohol, coffee, and spicy fried foods to a minimum. Build a wind-down routine before bed and try gentle movement like tai chi or walking to keep Qi flowing calmly.

### Qi and Blood Deficiency
*Pattern Link: [Detailed Pattern Page](/knowledge-base/patterns/qi-and-blood-deficiency)*

- **Clinical Signs & Symptoms**: Dull, diffuse headache, Dizziness worse with exertion or standing, Pale complexion and lips, Fatigue and weakness, Poor appetite, Heart palpitations
- **Worse with**: Overwork, Skipping meals, Cold or raw foods, Prolonged standing or walking, Emotional stress
- **Better with**: Rest and lying down, Warm, nourishing foods, Gentle movement
- **Why this happens (Mechanism)**:
  Your body's Qi and Blood are like the fuel and nutrients that keep your head clear and pain-free. When both are depleted, the brain and senses are not properly nourished, leading to dizziness that feels worse when you are tired or stand up. The headache is usually dull and diffuse, because there is not enough Blood to circulate and soothe the vessels in the head.
- **Tongue & Pulse**: Tongue: Pale body, may be thin or teeth-marked, thin white coating. Pulse: Fine (thin) and weak, especially at the right Guan (Spleen) position.
- **Triggers & Relief Explanation**: Rest conserves the limited Qi and Blood you have, preventing further depletion. Warm, nourishing foods directly supply the raw materials your Spleen needs to build Qi and Blood. Overwork, standing for long periods, and emotional stress burn through Qi and Blood, while cold or raw foods damage the Spleen's digestive fire, making it harder to produce the nourishment your head desperately needs.
- **Diet & Lifestyle**: Choose warm, cooked meals like soups and stews with ingredients such as chicken, bone broth, red dates, and dark leafy greens. Eat small, regular meals to support your Spleen's ability to extract nourishment. Gentle exercise like tai chi or walking builds Qi without draining it. Prioritize rest and avoid overworking.

### Phlegm-Dampness in the Middle-Burner
*Pattern Link: [Detailed Pattern Page](/knowledge-base/patterns/phlegm-dampness-in-the-middle-burner)*

- **Clinical Signs & Symptoms**: Heaviness in the head, as if wrapped in a cloth, Dizziness that feels foggy or cloudy, Nausea or queasiness, Thick, white, greasy tongue coating, Feeling of fullness in the upper abdomen, No thirst or desire to drink
- **Worse with**: Damp or rainy weather, Greasy, heavy foods, Overeating or late-night snacking, Cold or raw foods, Sedentary lifestyle
- **Better with**: Light, warm meals, Gentle movement, Dry, well-ventilated rooms, Avoiding greasy foods
- **Why this happens (Mechanism)**:
  When the Spleen is too weak to transform fluids properly, Dampness accumulates and thickens into Phlegm. This turbid Phlegm-Dampness rises to the head and blocks the clear orifices that keep your thinking and balance sharp. The result is a heavy, foggy dizziness and a dull headache, often with nausea because the Phlegm also disturbs the Stomach.
- **Tongue & Pulse**: Tongue: pale, puffy body with scalloped edges; thick, white, greasy coating that looks excessively moist. Pulse: slippery (Hua), often soft (Ru) or slow, especially at the right middle position.
- **Triggers & Relief Explanation**: Phlegm-Dampness is like a sticky internal fog that forms when the Spleen is overwhelmed. Greasy, cold, or raw foods and damp weather add to that burden, thickening the fog. Gentle movement and warm, dry foods wake up the Spleen, helping it clear the Dampness so the head feels light again.
- **Diet & Lifestyle**: Eat warm, cooked foods like soups and stews, and avoid cold, raw, or greasy meals that create more Dampness. Gentle daily movement, such as walking or stretching, helps the Spleen move Qi and dry up Phlegm. Keep your living space dry and well-ventilated.

### Kidney Essence Deficiency
*Pattern Link: [Detailed Pattern Page](/knowledge-base/patterns/kidney-essence-deficiency)*

- **Clinical Signs & Symptoms**: Dull, empty headache that feels deep inside the head, Chronic dizziness that worsens with overwork or aging, Soreness and weakness of the lower back and knees, Tinnitus or gradual hearing loss, Poor memory and difficulty concentrating, Premature greying or hair loss, Frequent urination at night
- **Worse with**: Overwork, Excessive sexual activity, Aging and long-term illness, Prolonged standing or walking, Cold or raw foods
- **Better with**: Adequate rest and sleep, Gentle warmth on the lower back, Warm, nourishing foods, Moderate, regular movement
- **Why this happens (Mechanism)**:
  Kidney Essence is the deepest substance that generates marrow and fills the brain. When it runs low - from aging, overwork, or constitutional weakness - the brain loses its nourishment. This leaves you with a chronic, hollow dizziness and a dull headache that feels empty, not pounding or heavy.
- **Tongue & Pulse**: Tongue: pale, thin body with little or no coating, possible fine cracks. Pulse: fine (thin) and deep, especially weak at both Chi positions.
- **Triggers & Relief Explanation**: Kidney Essence is the body's deepest reserve; overwork, excessive sexual activity, and aging drain it further, directly weakening the brain's nourishment and worsening dizziness and headache. Rest, nourishing warm foods, and gentle movement conserve and slowly replenish this essence, allowing the marrow to be rebuilt and the head to feel clearer.
- **Diet & Lifestyle**: Eat kidney-nourishing foods like black sesame, walnuts, goji berries, and bone broth. Avoid overwork, excessive sexual activity, and late nights. Gentle daily movement like walking or tai chi helps preserve essence, but stop before you feel drained.

### Blood Stagnation
*Pattern Link: [Detailed Pattern Page](/knowledge-base/patterns/blood-stagnation)*

- **Clinical Signs & Symptoms**: Fixed, stabbing headache that does not move, Pain worsens at night, Dark or purplish tongue with stasis spots, Dark menstrual blood with clots (in women), History of trauma or chronic pain
- **Worse with**: Cold weather, Prolonged sitting or lying down, Emotional stress, Injury or trauma, Greasy, heavy foods
- **Better with**: Gentle movement, Warm compress on head or neck, Massage of temples, Stress reduction
- **Why this happens (Mechanism)**:
  When blood flow becomes sluggish and stuck, it can no longer nourish the brain properly. This leads to dizziness and a headache that feels like a fixed, stabbing pain in one spot. The pain often worsens at night because blood circulation slows further when the body is at rest.
This pattern can arise after a head injury, from long-standing emotional stress that constricts the vessels, or from chronic illness that depletes the blood's ability to move. The stabbing quality is the hallmark of blood that is obstructed in the collaterals of the head.
- **Tongue & Pulse**: Tongue: dark purple or dusky body, stasis spots, distended dark sublingual veins, thin white coat. Pulse: choppy (涩 sè) like a knife scraping bamboo; may be wiry if Qi stagnation is present.
- **Triggers & Relief Explanation**: Cold and inactivity cause blood to congeal and slow down, which directly worsens the stasis that is at the root of this pattern. Stress constricts the Liver Qi, which in turn impedes blood flow. Warmth, gentle movement, and massage all invigorate the blood and encourage it to flow smoothly again, relieving the fixed pain and dizziness.
- **Diet & Lifestyle**: Keep warm and avoid cold, raw foods that can congeal blood. Gentle daily movement like walking or stretching helps keep blood circulating. If you sit for long periods, take breaks to move around. A warm compress on painful areas can bring immediate relief. Manage stress with breathing exercises, as frustration tightens the vessels and worsens stasis.

### Phlegm-Heat
*Pattern Link: [Detailed Pattern Page](/knowledge-base/patterns/phlegm-heat)*

- **Clinical Signs & Symptoms**: Severe dizziness with a heavy, clouded sensation, Throbbing or distending headache, Irritability and restlessness, Bitter taste in the mouth, Nausea or vomiting, Chest tightness and oppression
- **Worse with**: Spicy or greasy foods, Alcohol, Emotional stress, Overwork, Greasy, heavy foods
- **Better with**: Cool, light meals, Gentle movement, Deep breathing, Avoiding greasy foods, Hydration
- **Why this happens (Mechanism)**:
  When Dampness builds up and thickens into Phlegm, and Heat from internal stress or diet combines with it, a turbid Phlegm-Heat mixture rises to the head. This mixture clouds the clear orifices - the senses and mind - creating a heavy, dizzy feeling and a throbbing headache. The Heat agitates the mind, causing irritability and restlessness, while the Phlegm makes the head feel stuffed and the chest tight.
- **Tongue & Pulse**: Tongue: red body, possibly swollen, with a thick yellow greasy coating. Pulse: slippery (滑 huá) and rapid (数 shuò).
- **Triggers & Relief Explanation**: Phlegm-Heat forms when Dampness and Heat accumulate. Greasy, spicy, and sweet foods directly create Dampness and Heat, while alcohol and stress stir up internal fire. Avoiding these stops the mixture from thickening and rising to the head. Cooling, light foods and hydration help clear Heat and resolve Dampness, while gentle movement keeps Qi flowing so Phlegm cannot settle.
- **Diet & Lifestyle**: Avoid greasy, fried, spicy, and heavily sweet foods - they generate Dampness and Heat. Favour light, cooling meals like steamed vegetables, pear, radish, and mung bean soup. Stay hydrated with warm water or chrysanthemum tea. Gentle daily movement helps Qi flow and prevents stagnation, but avoid overheating.

## Frequently Asked Questions
### Can acupuncture help with dizziness and headache?
Yes. Acupuncture can help regulate the flow of Qi and Blood to the head, calm the Liver, resolve Phlegm, or nourish deficiencies depending on the pattern. Many people feel some relief after just one session, but a course of treatment is usually needed for lasting results.

### How long does it take for TCM to work for dizziness?
Excess patterns like Liver Yang Rising or Phlegm-Dampness often show improvement within 2 to 4 weeks of consistent herbs and acupuncture. Deficiency patterns such as Qi and Blood Deficiency or Kidney Essence Deficiency may take 3 to 6 months to rebuild the body's reserves. The timeline depends on the severity and how long you have had the symptoms.

### Can I take TCM herbs with my blood pressure medication?
Generally, yes, but it is essential to tell both your TCM practitioner and your doctor about all medications you are taking. Some herbs used for Liver Yang Rising can gently lower blood pressure, so your medication dose may need adjustment over time. Never stop or change your blood pressure medication without your doctor's guidance.

### What foods should I avoid if I have dizziness and headache?
Avoid greasy, fried, and overly sweet foods, which can generate Dampness and Phlegm and make symptoms worse. Also limit spicy foods and alcohol if your symptoms flare with stress. Iced drinks and cold foods can weaken the Spleen and should be minimized. Small, warm, easily digestible meals are best.

### Is dizziness a sign of a serious problem in TCM?
Dizziness can arise from many patterns, most of which are treatable. However, if your dizziness is sudden, severe, or accompanied by fainting, slurred speech, or weakness on one side, seek urgent medical attention. In TCM, chronic dizziness often points to an underlying deficiency that can be corrected with proper treatment.

### Will the dizziness come back after treatment?
When the underlying pattern is fully resolved, symptoms usually do not return as long as you maintain a balanced lifestyle. Some patterns, especially those related to aging or constitutional weakness, may require occasional tune-up treatments. Your practitioner will give you dietary and lifestyle advice to help prevent recurrence.

## Modern Scientific Research
1. **Acupuncture for the prevention of episodic migraine** ([Link](https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD001218.pub3))
   *Journal/Publication: Linde K, Allais G, Brinkhaus B, et al. Acupuncture for the prevention of episodic migraine. Cochrane Database Syst Rev. 2016;6:CD001218.*
   This Cochrane review included 22 trials with 4985 participants and found that acupuncture is at least as effective as prophylactic drug treatment for episodic migraine, with fewer adverse effects.

2. **The Long-term Effect of Acupuncture for Migraine Prophylaxis: A Randomized Clinical Trial** ([Link](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamainternmed.2016.9378))
   *Journal/Publication: Zhao L, Chen J, Li Y, et al. The Long-term Effect of Acupuncture for Migraine Prophylaxis: A Randomized Clinical Trial. JAMA Intern Med. 2017;177(4):508-515.*
   This randomized trial of 249 patients found that true acupuncture significantly reduced migraine frequency by 3.2 days per month compared to sham acupuncture over 24 weeks, with effects persisting through follow-up.
