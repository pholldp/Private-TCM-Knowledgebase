# Burning Sensation in Soles (足心热)
**Pinyin**: zú xīn rè | **Kind**: Symptom | **Category**: Feeling Hot & Heat Intolerance  
**Synonyms**: Hot soles, Burning Sensation In Feet | **Body Regions**: Foot — Sole

---

## Preview Summary
Not all burning soles are the same. The deep, night-time heat points to Kidney Yin, while stress-triggered burning points to Liver Qi stagnation - each has its own herbs and acupun...

---

## Conventional Medicine View
### Description
In Western medicine, a burning sensation in the soles is often classified as a type of peripheral neuropathy or paresthesia. Common causes include diabetes, vitamin B12 deficiency, alcohol overuse, hypothyroidism, and nerve compression such as tarsal tunnel syndrome. A rarer condition called erythromelalgia causes intense burning heat and redness in the feet, often triggered by warmth or exercise.
Diagnosis typically involves blood tests, nerve conduction studies, and a review of medical history. When no clear cause is found, the condition may be labeled idiopathic. Treatment focuses on managing the underlying disease (e.g., blood sugar control) and relieving symptoms with medications like gabapentin or pregabalin, topical capsaicin, or lidocaine patches.

### Treatments
Standard care aims to calm overactive nerve signals and address any underlying disorder. This often includes medications for neuropathic pain (gabapentin, pregabalin, duloxetine), topical creams, and lifestyle adjustments such as proper footwear, cool foot soaks, and avoiding prolonged standing. If a deficiency like B12 or thyroid dysfunction is identified, supplementation or hormone therapy is added.

## Traditional Chinese Medicine View
### TCM Understanding
TCM sees burning soles as a sign that the body's Yin - the cooling, moistening, anchoring force - is no longer keeping internal heat in check. The Kidney channel starts at the sole of the foot (the point Yongquan, KI-1), so when Yin is depleted, empty heat naturally travels down this channel and settles there. That's why the burning often feels deep and internal, and why it worsens during rest or at night, when Yin should be most dominant.
But the Kidneys aren't the only organ involved. The Heart houses the mind and controls blood circulation; if Heart Yin is weak, restless heat can spread to the soles and palms. The Liver stores blood and ensures smooth Qi flow - when its Yin is dry or its Qi is stuck from stress, heat can flare along the inner leg channels to the feet. Even the Spleen, which transforms food into nourishing fluids, can cause a mild, dry heat in the soles when its Yin is insufficient.
Because the same symptom can arise from so many different roots, TCM doesn't have one treatment for burning soles. A person whose heat is deepest at night with low-back weakness needs a very different approach than someone whose soles burn after an argument, or someone who feels a gentle warmth alongside poor appetite. The tongue, pulse, and a careful history of what makes the heat better or worse allow a practitioner to identify the precise imbalance and treat it directly.

### Practitioner Diagnosis
A TCM practitioner begins by asking when the burning is strongest and what makes it better or worse. The timing, quality, and accompanying sensations are the first clues. Because the Kidney channel travels through the soles, many patterns involve a deep, dry heat, but the root cause can be very different - from a genuine lack of cooling fluids to trapped emotional heat.
If the burning is most intense at night and comes with lower-back soreness, weak knees, or ringing in the ears, the practitioner looks for Kidney Yin Deficiency with Empty-Heat Blazing (肾阴虚火旺, shèn yīn xū huǒ wàng). The tongue is usually red with little or no coating, and the pulse feels rapid and thin. This picture shows that the body’s cooling Yin is too depleted to anchor Yang, so heat drifts to the soles.
When the soles and palms both feel hot and the person is restless, anxious, or has trouble sleeping, Heart Yin Deficiency (心阴不足, xīn yīn bù zú) is suspected. The tongue may be red with a peeled coating, and the pulse is thin and rapid. In this pattern, the Heart’s Yin is weakened, and because the Heart and Kidneys are linked, the empty fire often spreads downward to the feet.
A burning sensation that flares with irritability, dry eyes, or a dull ache under the ribs points toward Liver Yin Deficiency (肝阴亏虚, gān yīn kuī xū). The tongue is red with scant coating, and the pulse feels wiry, thin, and rapid. Here, the Liver’s nourishing Yin is inadequate, allowing internal heat to travel along the channels to the lower limbs, especially when emotions run high.
When stress, frustration, or long-held resentment clearly trigger the heat, a practitioner considers Liver Qi Stagnation transforming into Heat (肝郁化火, gān yù huà huǒ). The burning may come with chest or rib-side distension and a quick temper. The tongue can have a thin yellow coat, and the pulse is wiry. This pattern is less about a fluid deficit and more about compressed Qi producing fire that descends to the soles.
A milder, lingering warmth in the soles, combined with poor appetite, bloating, and dry stools, suggests Spleen Yin Deficiency (脾阴虚, pí yīn xū). The tongue may look red with small cracks on the sides and a thin, dry coat, and the pulse is often weak and thin. In this less common pattern, the digestive system’s Yin fluids are insufficient, creating a low-grade dry heat that settles in the extremities rather than blazing strongly.

## TCM Patterns and Treatment

### Kidney Yin Deficiency With Empty-Heat Blazing
*Pattern Link: [Detailed Pattern Page](/knowledge-base/patterns/kidney-yin-deficiency-with-empty-heat-blazing)*

- **Clinical Signs & Symptoms**: Deep burning in soles, worse at night, Night sweats, Lower back soreness and weakness, Tinnitus, Malar flush (red cheekbones), Dry throat, especially at night, Bone-steaming sensation (deep heat from within bones)
- **Worse with**: Overwork and late nights, Spicy, fried foods, Emotional stress, Excessive sweating, Hot weather
- **Better with**: Cool, quiet rest, Yin-nourishing foods (pears, black beans), Gentle movement (tai chi, walking), Avoiding late nights
- **Why this happens (Mechanism)**:
  The Kidney channel begins at the sole of the foot (Yongquan KI-1). When Kidney Yin is depleted, it can no longer anchor the body's Yang energy, allowing empty heat to flare. This heat travels down the channel and collects in the soles, producing a deep, internal burning that often intensifies at night when Yin is meant to be most active.
- **Tongue & Pulse**: Tongue: red, thin, and dry with little or no coating; cracks may appear. Pulse: fine (xi) and rapid (shu), weak at the chi (rear) positions.
- **Triggers & Relief Explanation**: Rest and cooling, moistening foods help rebuild depleted Kidney Yin, while overwork, stress, and heating foods further consume Yin and fan the empty fire, intensifying the burning in the soles.
- **Diet & Lifestyle**: Eat cooling, moistening foods like pears, black beans, and goji berries. Avoid spicy, fried, or overly heating foods. Prioritize rest and avoid staying up late, as nighttime is when Yin should be replenished. Gentle movement like walking or tai chi helps, but avoid intense sweating which further depletes fluids.

### Heart Yin Deficiency
*Pattern Link: [Detailed Pattern Page](/knowledge-base/patterns/heart-yin-deficiency)*

- **Clinical Signs & Symptoms**: Burning soles accompanied by heat in the palms and chest (five-palm heat), Palpitations, often worse at rest or at night, Insomnia with vivid, disturbing dreams, Night sweats, Mental restlessness and anxiety, Dry mouth and throat
- **Worse with**: Spicy fried or greasy foods, Emotional stress and anxiety, Overwork and late nights, Hot dry environments
- **Better with**: Cooling moistening foods (pear, cucumber), Calm quiet environment, Adequate sleep and rest, Meditation and deep breathing
- **Why this happens (Mechanism)**:
  Heart Yin is the cooling, moistening substance that anchors the mind and keeps the heart's fire in check. When it runs low-often from overwork, chronic stress, or emotional strain-the heart's yang becomes unmoored and turns into empty heat. Because the Heart and Kidneys are deeply connected through the body's water-fire axis, this restless heat can travel downward along the Kidney channel and settle in the soles, producing a burning sensation that frequently flares up alongside palpitations and mental agitation.
- **Tongue & Pulse**: Tongue: red body with a redder tip, little or no coating, dry surface, and possibly a midline crack. Pulse: thin and rapid, weak at the deep level especially at the left cun (Heart) position.
- **Triggers & Relief Explanation**: Heart Yin is easily consumed by emotional strain, overthinking, and staying up late, which all generate internal heat and further deplete the cooling, moistening resources of the body. Spicy and fried foods add more heat, while dry environments worsen the already parched state. In contrast, cooling foods and calming practices replenish Yin, anchor the mind, and gently guide the body back toward a cooler, more settled equilibrium.
- **Diet & Lifestyle**: Favor cooling, moistening foods like pear, cucumber, lily bulb, lotus seed, and wheat. Avoid spicy, fried, and greasy dishes, as well as coffee and alcohol, which dry out Yin. Prioritize rest and a consistent sleep schedule-late nights are especially depleting. Gentle, calming activities like walking, yoga, or meditation help settle the mind and preserve Yin.

### Liver Yin Deficiency
*Pattern Link: [Detailed Pattern Page](/knowledge-base/patterns/liver-yin-deficiency)*

- **Clinical Signs & Symptoms**: Burning soles worsen with stress and anger, Dry, gritty eyes, Dizziness and light-headedness, Irritability and short temper, Dull burning pain under the ribs
- **Worse with**: Anger and frustration, Spicy or fried foods, Overwork and late nights, Alcohol
- **Better with**: Rest and relaxation, Cooling foot baths, Cooling foods like cucumber, Gentle stretching or tai chi, Avoiding emotional stress
- **Why this happens (Mechanism)**:
  The Liver stores Blood and ensures smooth Qi flow. When its Yin fluids are depleted - often from chronic stress, overwork, or anger - the Liver becomes dry and overheated. This empty heat radiates along the Liver channel, which runs through the inner legs to the feet, causing a burning sensation in the soles. Emotional upsets further stir the heat, making the soles feel hotter.
- **Tongue & Pulse**: Tongue: red, thin, dry with little or no coating; the sides are redder. Pulse: wiry, fine, and rapid.
- **Triggers & Relief Explanation**: Anger and frustration stagnate Liver Qi and generate heat, which further depletes Yin. Spicy, fried foods and alcohol are heating and drying, directly damaging Yin fluids. Overwork and late nights consume Yin. Rest, cooling foods, and gentle movement nourish Yin, smooth the Liver, and allow empty heat to subside.
- **Diet & Lifestyle**: Eat cooling, moistening foods like cucumber, pear, tofu, and mung beans. Avoid spicy, fried, and greasy dishes that dry out Yin. Go to bed early and avoid overwork. Gentle movement such as tai chi or yoga helps smooth Liver Qi without overheating.

### Liver Qi Stagnation that transforms into Heat
*Pattern Link: [Detailed Pattern Page](/knowledge-base/patterns/liver-qi-stagnation-that-transforms-into-heat)*

- **Clinical Signs & Symptoms**: Burning soles worsens with emotional stress or anger, Irritability and explosive anger, Distending or burning pain along the ribs, Bitter taste in mouth
- **Worse with**: Anger and frustration, Spicy, greasy, or fried foods, Hot weather or hot environments, Overwork and lack of rest, Alcohol and coffee
- **Better with**: Deep breathing and meditation, Cooling foods (cucumber, chrysanthemum tea), Gentle stretching or walking, Keeping a regular daily routine
- **Why this happens (Mechanism)**:
  When emotions like anger and frustration become chronic, Liver Qi gets stuck. Over time, this stagnation generates heat. The heat can travel along the Liver channel, which connects internally to the feet, causing a burning sensation in the soles. The burning often flares up when you're stressed or upset, reflecting the underlying stagnation.
- **Tongue & Pulse**: Tongue: red body with redder sides and a yellow coating. Pulse: wiry and rapid (xian shu), especially forceful at the left middle (Liver) position.
- **Triggers & Relief Explanation**: Emotional stress is the primary trigger because it directly causes Liver Qi to stagnate, which then generates heat. Spicy and greasy foods add heat to the body, making the burning worse. Cooling foods and relaxation help clear heat and release stagnation, restoring smooth flow of Qi. Gentle exercise moves Qi and prevents it from getting stuck, while rest allows the Liver to recover.
- **Diet & Lifestyle**: Manage stress with mindfulness, meditation, or gentle exercise like yoga. Avoid spicy, greasy, and fried foods, as well as alcohol and coffee, which add heat. Eat cooling foods like cucumber, celery, and drink chrysanthemum tea. Regular, moderate movement helps keep Qi flowing and prevents stagnation.

### Spleen Yin Deficiency
*Pattern Link: [Detailed Pattern Page](/knowledge-base/patterns/spleen-yin-deficiency)*

- **Clinical Signs & Symptoms**: Poor appetite or hunger without desire to eat, Dry mouth and dry, cracked lips, Bloating after eating, Warm palms and soles, mild afternoon heat, Fatigue and dull complexion, Red tongue with small cracks on the sides
- **Worse with**: Spicy, dry, or fried foods, Irregular meal times, Overthinking and worry, Overwork and exhaustion
- **Better with**: Small, frequent meals, Moistening foods (congee, pears), Rest after eating
- **Why this happens (Mechanism)**:
  The Spleen is responsible for transforming food into nourishing fluids. When Spleen Yin is depleted, the body lacks the moisture needed to keep internal heat in check. A mild, dry heat develops and can settle in the soles, where the Spleen channel connects to the feet. This burning sensation is usually subtle and comes with digestive weakness - poor appetite, bloating, and dry stools - because the Spleen’s core function is compromised.
- **Tongue & Pulse**: Tongue: red, thin body with small cracks on the sides, little or no coating, dry surface. Pulse: fine (thin), possibly slightly rapid, weak at the right Guan (Spleen) position.
- **Triggers & Relief Explanation**: Spleen Yin is easily damaged by irregular eating, mental strain, and drying foods. Gentle, moistening meals provide the raw material the Spleen needs to rebuild its Yin fluids, while rest allows the digestive fire to focus on transformation. Spicy and fried foods create internal heat that further consumes Yin, and worry knots the Spleen Qi, blocking its ability to generate nourishing moisture.
- **Diet & Lifestyle**: Eat small, frequent meals of warm, moistening foods like rice congee, steamed pears, and honey. Avoid raw, cold, or greasy foods that tax the Spleen, and steer clear of spicy, dry, or overly roasted items that consume Yin. Rest after meals and protect your digestion from overthinking and worry.

## Frequently Asked Questions
### What causes burning soles in TCM?
In TCM, burning soles are most often caused by a lack of Yin - the body's cooling and moistening energy. When Yin is depleted, the body's Yang (warming energy) becomes unanchored and rises as empty heat, which can settle in the soles because the Kidney channel begins there. Other causes include stuck Liver Qi that turns into fire, or Spleen Yin deficiency that creates a mild, dry internal heat.

### Can acupuncture really help with burning feet?
Yes. Acupuncture can help redirect and cool the body's internal heat by stimulating points that nourish Yin and clear empty fire. Points like Taixi (KI-3) and Yongquan (KI-1) on the Kidney channel are especially effective for drawing heat down and out of the soles. Many patients feel a soothing, cooling sensation during treatment, and regular sessions can reduce both the intensity and frequency of the burning.

### How long does it take for Chinese herbs to work on burning soles?
Most people begin to notice a difference within 2-4 weeks of starting herbal treatment, especially if the pattern is excess-related. For deeper Yin deficiencies, herbs are often taken for 3-6 months to rebuild the body's reserves. Consistency is key - taking the formula daily as prescribed yields the best results.

### Can I use TCM alongside my diabetes or neuropathy medications?
Generally, yes. TCM herbs and acupuncture can complement conventional treatments for diabetes and neuropathy. However, always inform both your TCM practitioner and your prescribing doctor about all medications and supplements you are taking. Certain herbs may influence blood sugar levels or interact with blood-thinning medications, so coordination is essential.

### What foods should I avoid if I have burning soles?
It's best to avoid or minimize spicy, fried, and greasy foods, as well as alcohol and caffeine, which can all add internal heat. Instead, favor cooling, moistening foods like cucumber, pear, watermelon, black beans, and congee. Eating smaller, more frequent meals can also help if your digestion is weak.

### Is it normal for the burning to get worse at night?
Yes, this is very common in TCM patterns involving Yin deficiency. Nighttime is when Yin energy should be most active, cooling and calming the body. When Yin is depleted, the heat becomes more noticeable after dark and can disrupt sleep. If your burning soles consistently wake you up or make it hard to fall asleep, it's a strong clue that Yin needs to be nourished.
