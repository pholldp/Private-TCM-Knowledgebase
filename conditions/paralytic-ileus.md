## Conventional Medicine View
### Description
Paralytic ileus is a condition where the normal wave-like contractions of the intestines temporarily stop, preventing the movement of food, fluid, and gas. It most commonly occurs after abdominal surgery but can also result from infections, electrolyte imbalances, or certain medications. The main symptoms are abdominal distension, a feeling of fullness, nausea, and the inability to pass gas or have a bowel movement.
Diagnosis is usually based on physical exam findings - a distended, tympanic abdomen with absent bowel sounds - and confirmed with imaging such as an abdominal X-ray or CT scan that shows dilated loops of bowel without a physical blockage.

### Treatments
Standard care focuses on resting the bowel and supporting the body until normal function returns. This typically includes withholding food and drink (NPO), placing a nasogastric tube to suction out stomach contents and relieve pressure, and giving intravenous fluids to maintain hydration and electrolyte balance. In some cases, medications that stimulate bowel motility may be used, but the main approach is watchful waiting.

## Traditional Chinese Medicine View
### TCM Understanding
TCM sees paralytic ileus as a sudden halt in the downward flow of Qi through the Stomach and Large Intestine. After abdominal surgery, physical trauma, or prolonged immobility, the body's normal rhythmic propulsion can stall. The result is a blocked, distended feeling, an inability to pass gas or stool, and a belly that feels tight and full. Rather than a single mechanical problem, TCM recognizes that the cause of this Qi stagnation can vary greatly from person to person.
Cold and Dampness are frequent culprits, especially after surgery when the body is exposed to cold environments or cooling fluids. Cold has a contracting, freezing nature that can literally "shut down" the intestines, while Dampness adds heaviness and stickiness that further obstructs movement.
In other cases, the stagnation is purely from Qi disruption - the surgery itself cuts through channels and jolts the Liver's ability to keep Qi moving smoothly. If the blockage drags on, stagnant Qi can deepen into Blood stasis, causing sharp, fixed pain, or it can transform into intense Heat, drying up fluids and creating a hot, rigid abdomen.
Underlying deficiency also plays a role. A person with weak Spleen and Kidney Yang may have a chronically sluggish bowel even before surgery, and the added stress tips them into full paralysis. This is why the same Western diagnosis of paralytic ileus can show up as five distinct TCM patterns - each with its own tongue, pulse, and treatment strategy.

### Practitioner Diagnosis
A practitioner first asks about the nature of the distension and bowel sounds. When the primary issue is Large Intestine Qi Stagnation, the abdomen feels bloated and full, with absent bowel sounds and no passage of gas or stool. The pain is often diffuse rather than fixed, and it may come in waves. The tongue coating is thin and white, and the pulse is wiry. This pattern is the most common starting point, especially after abdominal surgery or prolonged immobility.
If the person reports cold‑type abdominal pain that improves with warmth or pressure, and the stools are loose or watery rather than completely absent, Cold‑Dampness in the Lower Burner is likely. This pattern often follows exposure to cold environments or surgical cooling. The tongue appears pale with a thick, white, greasy coating, and the pulse feels deep and slow or tight. The practitioner will ask about chills and the feeling of cold in the abdomen to confirm.
When the condition persists, Qi stagnation can lead to Qi and Blood Stagnation. The pain becomes fixed and sharp, and pressing on the abdomen worsens it. The patient may describe a stabbing sensation. The tongue develops a purplish hue with dark spots, and the pulse becomes wiry and choppy. A history of prolonged immobility or previous abdominal surgery with adhesions supports this pattern. Checking these signs helps differentiate it from simple Qi stagnation.
In some febrile illnesses, a Bright Yang Fire in Stomach and Intestines pattern emerges. The abdomen is severely painful and tender to touch, with thirst, a red tongue with a yellow coating, and a rapid, slippery pulse.
In chronically ill or elderly patients, Kidney and Spleen Yang Deficiency may be the root. The pain is dull, the limbs are cold, and the tongue is pale and swollen with teeth marks.
These patterns require careful differentiation because the treatment approaches are opposite-clearing heat versus warming and tonifying.

## TCM Patterns and Treatment

### Large Intestine Qi Stagnation
*Pattern Link: [Detailed Pattern Page](/knowledge-base/patterns/large-intestine-qi-stagnation)*

- **Clinical Signs & Symptoms**: Cramping pain that moves around the abdomen, Abdominal distension and bloating, Inability to pass gas or stool, Symptoms worsen with stress or anger, Bowel sounds absent or high-pitched gurgling
- **Worse with**: Stress and anger, Raw, cold, or iced foods, Heavy, greasy meals, Prolonged sitting or lying still, Suppressing the urge to pass gas
- **Better with**: Gentle walking, Warm compress on abdomen, Passing gas, Emotional calm, Warm, cooked meals
- **Why this happens (Mechanism)**:
  The Large Intestine relies on a smooth downward flow of Qi to propel waste. After abdominal surgery, emotional stress, or prolonged inactivity, this Qi can become stuck, causing the bowel's rhythmic movement to stall. The result is the hallmark distension, cramping, and inability to pass gas or stool seen in paralytic ileus.
- **Tongue & Pulse**: Tongue: normal light red body with a thin white coating. Pulse: wiry (弦 xián), especially at the left middle position.
- **Triggers & Relief Explanation**: Emotional stress causes Liver Qi to stagnate, which directly impedes the Large Intestine's ability to descend Qi. Cold and heavy foods congeal Qi and dampen digestive fire, while warmth and gentle movement encourage Qi to flow freely. Passing gas provides immediate release of trapped Qi, and avoiding suppression prevents further stagnation.
- **Diet & Lifestyle**: Eat small, warm, easily digestible meals like congee; avoid cold, raw, and greasy foods. Gentle walking and clockwise abdominal massage can help stimulate Qi flow. Manage stress with deep breathing or meditation.

### Cold-Dampness in the Lower Burner
*Pattern Link: [Detailed Pattern Page](/knowledge-base/patterns/cold-dampness-in-the-lower-burner)*

- **Clinical Signs & Symptoms**: Cold sensation in the lower abdomen, Heavy, dragging feeling in the pelvis, Loose or watery stools (or paradoxical diarrhea), Cold limbs, especially legs and feet, Pale, swollen tongue with a thick white greasy coating
- **Worse with**: Cold or damp weather, Raw, cold, or iced foods, Prolonged sitting or lying still, Heavy, greasy meals
- **Better with**: Warm compress on abdomen, Warm, cooked meals, Moxibustion on the lower belly, Gentle walking
- **Why this happens (Mechanism)**:
  When Cold and Dampness invade the lower burner - often after surgery, exposure to cold, or a prolonged illness - the intestines lose the warmth and movement they need to function. Cold contracts and freezes, while Dampness is heavy and sticky, so together they block the flow of Qi and cause the bowel to become sluggish or even paralyzed. This leaves you with a cold, heavy sensation in the lower abdomen, distension, and either loose stools or paradoxical constipation.
- **Tongue & Pulse**: Tongue: pale, swollen with teeth marks and a thick white greasy coating, excessively moist. Pulse: deep, slow, and soggy, especially in the Chi (rear) positions.
- **Triggers & Relief Explanation**: Cold and Dampness are the root cause of this pattern, so anything that adds more of them - like raw foods, cold drinks, or a chilly environment - will further congeal the Qi and worsen the ileus. Warmth, both from outside (heat packs, moxibustion) and inside (warm meals and spices), gently thaws the Cold and dries the Dampness, allowing the intestines to regain their natural rhythmic movement.
- **Diet & Lifestyle**: Keep the abdomen warm with a hot water bottle or moxibustion stick. Eat small, warm, cooked meals - think soups and stews with ginger, cinnamon, and fennel. Strictly avoid raw, cold, and greasy foods, as well as iced drinks, which will worsen the internal Cold and Dampness.

### Qi And Blood Stagnation
*Pattern Link: [Detailed Pattern Page](/knowledge-base/patterns/qi-and-blood-stagnation)*

- **Clinical Signs & Symptoms**: Fixed, stabbing abdominal pain that worsens with pressure, Severe abdominal distension with no bowel movement, Dark or purple tongue with stasis spots, Irritability and a feeling of oppression in the chest
- **Worse with**: Stress and anger, Raw, cold, or iced foods, Prolonged sitting or lying still
- **Better with**: Gentle walking, Warm compress on abdomen, Emotional calm
- **Why this happens (Mechanism)**:
  In paralytic ileus, Qi stagnation in the intestines is the first step. If it persists, the stagnant Qi eventually fails to move Blood, leading to Blood stasis. This creates a cycle where the stuck Blood blocks the intestinal passage, causing sharp, fixed pain and a complete halt in peristalsis.
The Liver is responsible for the smooth flow of Qi and Blood, so emotional tension or surgical trauma can trigger this chain. As Blood stasis deepens, the pain becomes knife-like and the tongue develops purple spots, signaling that the obstruction has moved from a functional Qi level to a structural Blood level.
- **Tongue & Pulse**: Tongue: purple or dark body with stasis spots, distended sublingual veins, thin white coat. Pulse: wiry (xian) and choppy (se).
- **Triggers & Relief Explanation**: Emotional stress and anger directly worsen Liver Qi stagnation, which is the root cause of Blood stasis in this pattern. Cold constricts the vessels and thickens Blood, making stasis worse; warmth relaxes the bowels and promotes flow. Gentle movement helps Qi and Blood circulate, breaking the cycle of stagnation, while excessive stillness allows it to pool and congeal.
- **Diet & Lifestyle**: Eat warm, easily digestible foods like congee and steamed vegetables. Avoid cold, raw, and greasy foods that congeal Blood. Gentle walking after meals helps Qi and Blood circulate, but rest when pain is sharp.

### Bright Yang Fire in Stomach and Intestines
*Pattern Link: [Detailed Pattern Page](/knowledge-base/patterns/bright-yang-fire-in-stomach-and-intestines)*

- **Clinical Signs & Symptoms**: High or spiking afternoon fever, Severe abdominal pain and distension that worsens with pressure, Intense thirst with a craving for cold drinks, Constipation with very dry, hard stools, Foul breath and body odor, Restlessness and irritability
- **Worse with**: Hot, spicy, or fried foods, Alcohol and coffee, Stress and anger, Heavy, greasy meals
- **Better with**: Sips of cool water, Cooling foods like pear or cucumber, Resting in a cool, calm environment
- **Why this happens (Mechanism)**:
  In this pattern, extreme heat accumulates in the Stomach and Large Intestine, much like an oven that has been turned up too high with no way to vent. This intense internal heat dries up the body's fluids, hardening the stool into dry lumps that cannot move and causing the bowel to grind to a halt. The trapped heat and stagnant waste then create severe pressure, distension, and pain in the abdomen.
- **Tongue & Pulse**: Tongue: Red body with prickly thorns, thick yellow dry coating, possibly cracked. Pulse: Deep, forceful, rapid, and slippery.
- **Triggers & Relief Explanation**: This pattern is driven by an excess of internal heat that has dried up fluids and paralyzed the bowel. Hot, spicy, and fried foods add more heat to the fire, while alcohol and emotional stress fan the flames upward and inward, worsening the stagnation. Cooling foods and fluids act like water on a fire, helping to clear heat and gently moisten the dried-out intestinal tract. A calm, cool environment soothes the mind and prevents the agitation that stirs up internal fire.
- **Diet & Lifestyle**: This is a serious acute condition. Once the acute phase is resolved, prevent recurrence by avoiding hot, spicy, greasy, and fried foods that stoke internal fire. Focus on cooling, moistening foods like pear, cucumber, and small amounts of prune juice. Hydration is crucial, but avoid ice-cold drinks in large volumes, as the shock can further disrupt the gut; sip cool or room-temperature water throughout the day.

### Kidney and Spleen Yang Deficiency
*Pattern Link: [Detailed Pattern Page](/knowledge-base/patterns/kidney-and-spleen-yang-deficiency)*

- **Clinical Signs & Symptoms**: Dull, cold abdominal pain better with warmth, Cold hands and feet, especially below knees, Early-morning diarrhea with undigested food, Sore and cold lower back and knees, Chronic fatigue and lack of energy, Pale, swollen tongue with teeth marks
- **Worse with**: Cold or damp weather, Raw, cold, or iced foods, Overwork and exhaustion, Early morning (around 5 AM)
- **Better with**: Warm compress on abdomen, Warm, cooked meals, Rest and gentle movement, Ginger tea
- **Why this happens (Mechanism)**:
  The Spleen and Kidney Yang provide the warmth and energy that drive the intestines' movement. When this Yang is deficient, the intestines become cold and sluggish, losing their ability to propel food and waste. This leads to chronic bloating, dull abdominal pain, and either loose stools or constipation from lack of motility.
- **Tongue & Pulse**: Tongue: pale, swollen, puffy with teeth marks, white slippery coating. Pulse: deep, weak, slow; both chi positions feeble, right guan weak.
- **Triggers & Relief Explanation**: Kidney and Spleen Yang are the body's internal furnace. Cold drains this fire, so external cold, raw foods, and exhaustion worsen the deficiency. Warmth, rest, and warming foods replenish the Yang, helping the intestines regain their natural peristaltic rhythm.
- **Diet & Lifestyle**: Eat warm, cooked foods like soups, stews, and congees. Add warming spices like ginger, cinnamon, and cloves. Avoid raw, cold, and iced foods that further chill the digestive fire. Gentle exercise like walking and keeping the abdomen warm with a hot water bottle can help.

## Frequently Asked Questions
### Can acupuncture really make my bowels move again after surgery?
Yes, acupuncture is one of the most direct ways TCM stimulates bowel function. Points like Tianshu ST-25 and Shangjuxu ST-37 have a specific effect on the Large Intestine, promoting peristalsis. Many hospitals now include acupuncture as part of enhanced recovery after surgery protocols because it can reduce the time to first bowel movement and help patients avoid prolonged ileus.

### How quickly will Chinese herbs work for paralytic ileus?
In acute cases, many people notice rumbling sounds and the passage of gas within 24 to 48 hours of starting herbal treatment. The specific formula depends on your pattern - for example, a Cold-Dampness blockage responds to warming, dispersing herbs, while a Heat pattern needs cooling, purging herbs. Your practitioner will adjust the formula daily based on your progress.

### Is it safe to use TCM while I have an NG tube?
TCM can generally be integrated with standard post-surgical care, including the use of a nasogastric tube. Herbal decoctions may sometimes be administered through the tube (after clamping, as directed by your medical team) or held until oral intake resumes. Always coordinate with your surgeon and TCM practitioner to ensure the timing and method are appropriate for your situation.

### What if I'm not passing gas or stool at all - should I still try TCM?
Absolutely. In fact, this is exactly the stage where TCM excels. The absence of bowel sounds and gas indicates severe Qi stagnation, and strong moving formulas combined with acupuncture are designed to break through that blockage. However, if you also have severe pain, fever, or vomiting, seek urgent medical evaluation - these may signal a mechanical obstruction that requires immediate intervention.

### Can TCM prevent paralytic ileus from happening after future surgeries?
If your ileus is linked to a constitutional weakness - such as Spleen and Kidney Yang Deficiency - strengthening your digestive system with herbs and acupuncture for several weeks before a planned surgery may reduce the risk. For patients who tend toward Qi stagnation, pre-operative acupuncture and stress-reduction techniques can also help keep Qi flowing smoothly through the surgical period. Discuss a pre-habilitation plan with your TCM practitioner well in advance of any scheduled procedure.

### Do I need to change my diet after recovering from ileus?
Yes, diet plays a supportive role. Once you are cleared to eat, focus on warm, cooked foods that are easy to digest, like congee, soups, and steamed vegetables. Cold drinks, raw salads, and greasy or fried foods can reintroduce Cold and Dampness, which may trigger another episode of sluggishness. Your practitioner may also recommend specific foods based on your pattern, such as ginger tea for Cold types or pear for Heat types.
