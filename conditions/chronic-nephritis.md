## Conventional Medicine View
### Description
Chronic nephritis refers to long-standing inflammation of the kidney's filtering units (glomeruli), which can lead to protein and blood in the urine, high blood pressure, and gradually declining kidney function. It is often diagnosed through urine tests showing protein or red blood cells, blood tests measuring creatinine and eGFR, and sometimes a kidney biopsy. The condition may progress silently for years before symptoms like swelling, fatigue, or foamy urine appear.
While the exact cause varies - including autoimmune diseases, infections, or genetic factors - the conventional goal is to slow progression and manage complications.

### Treatments
Standard treatment typically includes medications to control blood pressure (such as ACE inhibitors or ARBs), reduce proteinuria, and suppress the immune system if an autoimmune cause is suspected (corticosteroids, cyclophosphamide). Diuretics may be used for swelling, and dietary changes - low salt, moderate protein, and potassium/phosphorus management - are essential. In advanced stages, dialysis or kidney transplant may become necessary. These approaches can slow damage but do not restore lost kidney function.

## Traditional Chinese Medicine View
### TCM Understanding
In Traditional Chinese Medicine, chronic nephritis is understood as a disorder of the Kidney and Spleen organ systems, which together govern water metabolism and hold essential substances in the body. The Kidneys store essence and control the opening and closing of the body's water passages, while the Spleen transforms food into Qi and transports fluids. When these systems weaken - due to overwork, chronic illness, or constitutional predisposition - they fail to contain precious nutrients, and protein leaks into the urine. At the same time, water accumulates in the tissues, causing the swelling and puffiness that many patients experience.
This is a condition of 'root deficiency and branch excess.' The root is almost always a deficiency: Qi and Yin Deficiency, Spleen and Kidney Qi Deficiency, Kidney and Spleen Yang Deficiency, or Kidney and Liver Yin Deficiency. Each produces a different flavor of fatigue, swelling, and urinary changes. For example, Yang Deficiency brings a deep inner chill with cold limbs and pale, puffy skin, while Yin Deficiency creates a dry, hot sensation with night sweats and a red tongue.
On top of this deficiency, two excess factors frequently complicate the picture: Damp-Heat and Blood Stagnation. Damp-Heat sinks to the lower burner, making urine dark, turbid, and sometimes bloody, with a heavy, dragging sensation. Blood Stagnation occurs when weak Qi fails to push blood through the tiny kidney vessels, leading to fixed, stabbing pain and a dark purple tongue. These excesses accelerate kidney damage and must be cleared alongside tonifying the root.
Because TCM sees these distinct patterns, the same Western diagnosis of chronic nephritis can call for entirely different treatments. A practitioner reads the tongue and pulse to identify which pattern is dominant - a pale, swollen tongue with a white coat points to Yang Deficiency, while a red, greasy coat signals Damp-Heat. This personalized diagnosis is what allows herbal formulas and acupuncture points to be matched precisely to your body's needs.

### Practitioner Diagnosis
A TCM practitioner begins by listening carefully to how you describe your energy, urination, and any swelling. Chronic nephritis is a mixed picture of weakness and excess, so the first job is to separate root deficiency patterns from the more acute signs of damp-heat or blood stasis. The tongue and pulse offer objective clues that help confirm which pattern is dominant.
If you feel persistently drained yet also have a dry mouth, a red tongue with little or no coating, and foamy urine, the very common Qi and Yin Deficiency pattern is likely. The pulse tends to be thin and rapid, reflecting both the lack of Qi and the loss of nourishing Yin fluids.
When poor appetite, bloating, loose stools, and lower back soreness are the main complaints, Spleen and Kidney Qi Deficiency comes to the fore. The tongue is pale with a thin coating, and the pulse feels weak. This pattern highlights the digestive system’s failure to support the kidneys.
Cold hands and feet, an aversion to chill, and puffy lower limbs that pit with pressure point to Kidney and Spleen Yang Deficiency. Here the warming, transforming fire is low, so water accumulates. The tongue appears pale and swollen with a white, greasy coating, and the pulse is deep and slow.
When the urine turns dark yellow, feels heavy or uncomfortable to pass, and the tongue is red with a yellow, greasy coating, Damp-Heat in the Lower Burner is active. The pulse becomes slippery and rapid. This excess pattern often worsens proteinuria and can make the urine look turbid.
Dizziness, ringing in the ears, night sweats, and a red tongue with very little coating suggest Kidney and Liver Yin Deficiency. The pulse is thin and rapid. This pattern tends to appear when blood pressure rises or when heat signs emerge from long-standing yin damage.
As the condition drags on, Blood Stagnation may set in. A practitioner looks for fixed, stabbing pain and a tongue that is dark or purplish with stasis spots. The pulse often feels choppy. This pattern signals that the microcirculation in the kidneys is compromised, accelerating damage.

## TCM Patterns and Treatment

### Qi and Yin Deficiency
*Pattern Link: [Detailed Pattern Page](/knowledge-base/patterns/qi-and-yin-deficiency)*

- **Clinical Signs & Symptoms**: Fatigue and lack of strength, Dry mouth with little desire to drink, Foamy urine (proteinuria), Night sweats, Warm palms and soles
- **Worse with**: Overwork or prolonged standing, Stress and emotional upset, Spicy, greasy, or fried foods, Dehydration or excessive sweating
- **Better with**: Rest and adequate sleep, Gentle walking or Tai Chi, Warm, cooked meals, Moderate fluid intake
- **Why this happens (Mechanism)**:
  In chronic nephritis, the Kidneys and Spleen gradually lose their ability to produce and preserve both Qi (vital energy) and Yin (cooling, moistening fluids). When Qi is deficient, the body cannot hold essential substances, so protein leaks into the urine, creating foamy urine and fatigue. Yin deficiency then leads to a lack of moisture and generates mild heat, causing dry mouth, night sweats, and a warm sensation in the palms and soles.
- **Tongue & Pulse**: Tongue: red or pale-red body with a thin or absent coating, possibly cracked and dry. Pulse: fine (thin) and weak, may be rapid.
- **Triggers & Relief Explanation**: Qi and Yin are easily consumed by overwork, stress, and excessive sweating, which is why rest and gentle movement are essential to conserve them. Hot, spicy, and greasy foods can generate heat and further dry up Yin fluids, while dehydration directly worsens the already depleted moisture. Warm, nourishing foods and moderate hydration help gently rebuild Qi and Yin without overtaxing the weakened Spleen and Kidneys.
- **Diet & Lifestyle**: Prioritize rest and avoid overwork, as both physical and mental strain deplete Qi and Yin. Eat warm, easy-to-digest foods like congee with yam and goji berries, and keep salt and protein intake moderate to ease the burden on the kidneys. Sip water throughout the day to stay gently hydrated without overwhelming the system.

### Spleen and Kidney Qi Deficiency
*Pattern Link: [Detailed Pattern Page](/knowledge-base/patterns/spleen-and-kidney-qi-deficiency)*

- **Clinical Signs & Symptoms**: Poor appetite and loose stools, Lower back soreness and weak knees, Chronic fatigue and low stamina, Frequent urination, especially at night, Pale, puffy face and cold hands and feet
- **Worse with**: Overwork or prolonged standing, Raw, cold foods or icy drinks, Excessive fluid intake that overwhelms the Spleen, Stress and emotional upset, Cold, damp weather or environments
- **Better with**: Rest and adequate sleep, Warm, cooked meals, Gentle walking or Tai Chi, Keeping the lower back warm
- **Why this happens (Mechanism)**:
  Your Spleen transforms food into Qi and transports fluids. When Spleen Qi is weak, that process falters, so dampness builds up, leading to poor appetite, bloating, and loose stools. At the same time, Kidney Qi governs water metabolism and holds essential substances in the body. When Kidney Qi is depleted, the lower back and knees lose their support, and the body leaks vital essence into the urine, causing the foam and protein loss seen in chronic nephritis.
- **Tongue & Pulse**: Tongue: Pale, swollen body with teeth marks, white moist or slippery coating. Pulse: Deep, weak, and fine (thin), especially weak at the right Guan (Spleen) and both Chi (Kidney) positions.
- **Triggers & Relief Explanation**: Spleen Qi thrives on warmth and routine, so cooked foods and rest conserve its transformative power, while raw, cold, or greasy foods dampen it further. Kidney Qi is depleted by overwork and standing, so gentle movement and warm support protect the low back and reduce essence leakage. Stress knots the Spleen Qi, so calming habits prevent the stagnation that worsens fluid buildup.
- **Diet & Lifestyle**: Eat warm, cooked, easily digestible meals like congee with yam and a little lean protein. Avoid raw, cold, and greasy foods that burden the Spleen. Gentle daily walking helps Qi move without exhausting it, and keeping the lower back and feet warm supports Kidney Qi.

### Kidney and Spleen Yang Deficiency
*Pattern Link: [Detailed Pattern Page](/knowledge-base/patterns/kidney-and-spleen-yang-deficiency)*

- **Clinical Signs & Symptoms**: Feeling cold, especially in the limbs and lower back, Significant swelling of the legs and ankles, Chronic loose stools or early-morning diarrhea, Frequent, clear urination, Severe fatigue and lack of energy
- **Worse with**: Cold, damp weather or environments, Raw, cold foods or icy drinks, Overwork or prolonged standing
- **Better with**: Keeping the lower back warm, Warm, cooked meals, Gentle moxibustion on the lower back, Rest and adequate sleep
- **Why this happens (Mechanism)**:
  Kidney Yang is the body's pilot light, warming everything and powering fluid metabolism. When it fades and the Spleen's digestive fire also dims, a deep inner chill sets in. The body loses its ability to transform and transport water, so fluid overflows into the tissues, causing the hallmark swelling and loose stools seen in chronic nephritis.
- **Tongue & Pulse**: Tongue: Pale, swollen, and puffy with teeth marks on the edges and a white, slippery coating. Pulse: Deep, weak, and slow, especially feeble at the rear positions.
- **Triggers & Relief Explanation**: This pattern is rooted in a deep deficiency of warming Yang energy. Cold and dampness naturally extinguish what little fire remains, so external warmth and warm, cooked foods act as a lifeline. Rest conserves the body's dwindling energy, while moxibustion and warming herbs directly add Yang to the system, helping the Kidneys and Spleen to transform and transport fluids again.
- **Diet & Lifestyle**: Warmth is your best medicine. Eat only cooked, warm foods like soups, stews, and congees, and use warming spices such as ginger, cinnamon, and black pepper. Strictly avoid cold, raw foods, icy drinks, and excessive salt. Keep your lower back and feet warm, and practice gentle, grounding exercises like Tai Chi or walking to support Yang without exhausting it.

### Damp-Heat in the Lower Burner
*Pattern Link: [Detailed Pattern Page](/knowledge-base/patterns/damp-heat-in-the-lower-burner)*

- **Clinical Signs & Symptoms**: Dark, turbid or tea-colored urine, Burning or stinging during urination, Heavy, dragging sensation in the legs and lower back, Lower abdominal fullness or bloating, Foul-smelling stools or vaginal discharge, Thirst but no desire to drink much
- **Worse with**: Spicy, greasy, or fried foods, Alcohol and sweet drinks, Hot, humid weather or saunas, Prolonged sitting or standing in dampness, Stress and emotional upset
- **Better with**: Light, cooling foods (barley, mung bean), Plenty of plain water, Dry, ventilated environment, Gentle leg elevation to ease heaviness
- **Why this happens (Mechanism)**:
  In chronic nephritis, Damp-Heat sinks to the Lower Burner - the kidneys and bladder - where it creates a sticky, inflammatory environment. The Heat component makes urine dark, turbid, and sometimes bloody, while the Dampness causes a heavy, dragging sensation in the lower body and legs. This combination damages the kidney's delicate filtering structure, allowing protein and blood to leak into the urine, which is why you may see foamy or tea-colored urine.
- **Tongue & Pulse**: Tongue: red body, slightly swollen, with a thick yellow greasy coating heaviest at the root. Pulse: slippery (滑 huá) and rapid (数 shuò), more pronounced at the rear (Chi) position.
- **Triggers & Relief Explanation**: Damp-Heat is fueled by foods and environments that are either heating or damp-producing - greasy, spicy, and sweet foods, alcohol, and hot-humid weather all add to the sticky, inflammatory mix that congests the Lower Burner. Light, cooling foods and adequate hydration help to dilute and flush out the turbid heat, while dry air and gentle movement prevent dampness from accumulating. Emotional stress can stir up Liver Qi, which turns into heat and further aggravates the condition, so staying calm also helps cool the internal environment.
- **Diet & Lifestyle**: Favor light, cooling, and mildly diuretic foods such as barley, mung beans, celery, and watermelon. Avoid greasy, fried, spicy, and overly sweet foods that brew Damp-Heat. Drink plenty of plain water to help flush the urinary tract, and keep the living environment dry and ventilated.

### Kidney and Liver Yin Deficiency
*Pattern Link: [Detailed Pattern Page](/knowledge-base/patterns/kidney-and-liver-yin-deficiency)*

- **Clinical Signs & Symptoms**: Dizziness and tinnitus, Night sweats and heat in palms, soles, and chest, Dry eyes, dry mouth, and dry throat, Lower back and knee soreness, Insomnia with vivid dreams, Flushed cheekbones, especially in the afternoon
- **Worse with**: Stress and emotional upset, Spicy, greasy, or fried foods, Overwork or prolonged standing, Hot, humid weather or saunas
- **Better with**: Cool, quiet environment, Eating pears, black sesame, and goji berries, Rest and adequate sleep, Gentle walking or Tai Chi
- **Why this happens (Mechanism)**:
  The Kidneys store essence and govern water; the Liver stores blood and ensures smooth flow. When Yin becomes depleted, there is not enough cooling and moistening, and deficiency heat flares upward. This heat can damage the delicate kidney filters, leading to proteinuria and hematuria, while the lack of Yin fluids causes hypertension and the classic dry, heated symptoms. Over time, the Liver Yang may rise, causing dizziness and tinnitus.
- **Tongue & Pulse**: Tongue: red, thin body with cracks, little or no coating, dry surface. Pulse: fine, rapid, and wiry; weak at the left Chi (Kidney) position.
- **Triggers & Relief Explanation**: Yin is the body's cooling, moistening force. Spicy foods, stress, and overwork generate heat that burns up Yin fluids, making dryness and heat symptoms worse. Rest, cooling foods, and a calm environment allow Yin to rebuild, while gentle movement keeps Qi flowing without overheating. By protecting Yin, the kidneys are less damaged by deficiency heat, helping to control proteinuria and hypertension.
- **Diet & Lifestyle**: Eat cooling, moistening foods like pears, black sesame, goji berries, and tofu. Avoid spicy, fried, and grilled foods that generate heat. Prioritize rest and avoid late nights; gentle activities like Tai Chi or walking can help without depleting Yin.

### Blood Stagnation
*Pattern Link: [Detailed Pattern Page](/knowledge-base/patterns/blood-stagnation)*

- **Clinical Signs & Symptoms**: Fixed, stabbing lower-back pain, Pain worsens at night and with pressure, Dark purplish tongue with stasis spots, Dark circles under the eyes or dusky complexion, Dry, rough, scaly skin
- **Worse with**: Cold, damp weather or environments, Prolonged sitting or inactivity, Stress and emotional upset
- **Better with**: Keeping the lower back warm, Gentle walking or Tai Chi, Warm, cooked meals
- **Why this happens (Mechanism)**:
  Chronic illness gradually depletes Qi, and when Qi is too weak to push blood, circulation slows and blood pools in the tiny vessels of the kidneys. This stasis creates fixed, stabbing pain and gives the tongue its characteristic dark purple colour. Over time, the stagnant blood damages the kidney filters directly, worsening proteinuria and accelerating scarring.
- **Tongue & Pulse**: Tongue: dark purple body with stasis spots, distended dark sublingual veins, thin white coat. Pulse: choppy (涩 Sè) or wiry (弦 Xián).
- **Triggers & Relief Explanation**: Cold causes vessels to constrict and blood to congeal, so warmth and gentle movement are the simplest ways to encourage blood to flow again. Stress knots the Liver Qi, and since Qi is the commander of blood, any emotional tension directly worsens stasis. Keeping the lower back warm specifically targets the Kidney region where the stasis is most damaging.
- **Diet & Lifestyle**: Keep warm, especially the lower back and feet - cold congeals blood and makes stasis worse. Gentle daily movement like walking or tai chi is better than intense exercise; it nudges blood flow without exhausting Qi. Include a little turmeric, black fungus, or hawthorn in meals to gently move blood.

## Frequently Asked Questions
### Can TCM cure chronic nephritis?
While TCM cannot reverse advanced kidney scarring, it can significantly improve symptoms, reduce proteinuria, and slow disease progression. Many patients experience better energy, less swelling, and stable kidney function for years. The goal is to support the body's self-healing ability and correct the underlying imbalances, which may allow you to reduce medication dosages or delay dialysis. Results vary by pattern and stage, so a thorough evaluation is essential.

### How long before my proteinuria improves?
Protein in the urine often takes longer to improve than symptoms like fatigue or edema. Most patients see a noticeable reduction in foamy urine and lab values within 3-6 months of consistent herbal treatment. Deficiency patterns require time to rebuild the Kidney's ability to hold essence, while Damp-Heat patterns may respond faster once inflammation is cleared. Your practitioner will monitor urine tests regularly to track progress.

### Can I take Chinese herbs with my blood pressure medication?
Yes, but it must be done under close supervision. Many herbs used for chronic nephritis, such as Huang Qi (Astragalus) and Dan Shen (Salvia), can influence blood pressure and circulation. Your TCM practitioner will select herbs that complement your medications and avoid interactions. Never stop or adjust your blood pressure drugs on your own - coordinate with both your nephrologist and TCM provider, and bring a complete medication list to every appointment.

### Do I need to change my diet for TCM to work?
Diet plays a crucial role. A low-salt, kidney-friendly diet is the foundation across all patterns. Beyond that, TCM dietary advice depends on your pattern: if you have Damp-Heat, you'll avoid greasy, spicy foods; if you have Yang Deficiency, you'll emphasize warm, cooked meals and avoid cold raw foods. Your practitioner will give you specific guidance, but generally, eating simple, easily digested foods and staying hydrated within your fluid limits will support your treatment.

### Is acupuncture safe if I have kidney disease?
Yes, acupuncture is generally safe for people with chronic kidney disease when performed by a licensed practitioner using sterile, single-use needles. There is a very small risk of bruising, especially if you are on blood thinners, so inform your acupuncturist of all medications. Certain points on the lower back and abdomen are used to directly support kidney function, and many patients find sessions deeply relaxing and helpful for reducing stress and pain.

### Will I still need dialysis if I do TCM?
TCM is not a substitute for dialysis when kidney function has declined to end-stage renal disease. However, if started early, TCM may help slow the progression and delay the need for dialysis. In later stages, acupuncture and herbs can still be used to manage dialysis side effects like fatigue, itching, and poor appetite, improving quality of life. Always continue your prescribed dialysis schedule and discuss any complementary therapies with your nephrologist.
