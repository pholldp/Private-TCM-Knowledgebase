## Preview Summary
A bruise’s color, pain quality, and how easily it appears reveal distinct TCM patterns - and treating the root cause can speed healing and reduce future bruising.

---

## Conventional Medicine View
### Description
In Western medicine, a bruise (ecchymosis) occurs when small blood vessels break and leak blood into the tissues beneath the skin, usually after a bump or injury. The trapped blood causes the characteristic blue, purple, or black discoloration that changes color as it heals. Most bruises are harmless and fade on their own within two weeks.
When bruising happens very easily or without any remembered injury, doctors may investigate for bleeding disorders, vitamin deficiencies, or medications that thin the blood. The standard approach focuses on symptom relief and ruling out serious underlying conditions.

### Treatments
For a fresh bruise, the usual advice is rest, ice, compression, and elevation (RICE) to limit swelling. Over-the-counter pain relievers like acetaminophen can help with discomfort, though NSAIDs like ibuprofen are sometimes avoided because they can increase bleeding. For people who bruise easily, treatment targets the underlying cause - adjusting medications, supplementing vitamin C or K, or addressing a bleeding disorder.

## Traditional Chinese Medicine View
### TCM Understanding
In TCM, a bruise is understood as Blood Stagnation (瘀血, yū xuè) - blood that has leaked out of the vessels and become stuck. The Liver is the organ most responsible for the smooth flow of Qi and Blood throughout the body, while the Spleen holds blood inside the vessels. When Qi moves strongly, blood circulates; when it stagnates, blood pools and a bruise forms.
The type of bruise tells a story. A dark, fixed bruise with stabbing pain after a direct blow is classic Blood Stagnation. A bruise that comes with stress, a distending ache, and shifting pain points to Qi and Blood Stagnation - the Liver’s Qi is stuck and can’t move the blood. If bruises appear from the lightest touch and look pale, the root is often Qi and Blood Deficiency or Spleen Qi failing to hold blood in the vessels.
External factors also matter. Cold constricts the channels and slows circulation, causing stiff, aching bruises that feel worse in cold weather. Heat can turn a bruise red, hot, and swollen, indicating inflammation and possible infection. This is why the same Western symptom - a bruise - can have six different TCM patterns, each requiring a different treatment strategy.

### Practitioner Diagnosis
A TCM practitioner begins by listening carefully to how the bruise feels and how it came about. The quality of the pain - whether it is fixed, stabbing, or distending - and the color of the discoloration are the first big clues. They also ask about your energy level, any emotional stress, and whether the area feels hot or cold to the touch. These details start to separate one pattern from another.
When the pain is fixed and piercing, and the bruise is a deep, dark purple that stays in one place, the picture points to pure Blood Stagnation. The tongue often looks dark or purplish with tiny stagnant spots, and the pulse feels choppy or wiry. This pattern is very common after a direct blow, where blood has simply pooled and congealed in the local tissues.
If the bruise comes with a distending, moving pain and you feel emotionally tense or stressed, the diagnosis leans toward Qi and Blood Stagnation. Here the bruise may shift or feel more like a dull ache that spreads. The tongue may be slightly dusky, and the pulse is often wiry, reflecting the stuck Qi that is failing to move the blood smoothly.
When bruising happens easily or heals slowly, the practitioner looks for signs of deficiency. Pale bruises, general fatigue, a pale tongue with a thin white coat, and a weak or thready pulse suggest the body lacks enough Qi and Blood to repair itself. This could be Qi Deficiency causing Blood Stagnation, where the engine is too weak to push blood along, or a broader Qi and Blood Deficiency that leaves tissues undernourished and fragile.

## TCM Patterns and Treatment

### Blood Stagnation
*Pattern Link: [Detailed Pattern Page](/knowledge-base/patterns/blood-stagnation)*

- **Clinical Signs & Symptoms**: Fixed, stabbing pain at the bruise site, Pain worsens at night and with pressure, Dark purple or blackish bruise that does not fade quickly, Dark purple lips or nails, dusky complexion, Dark menstrual blood with clots (in women)
- **Worse with**: Cold exposure or cold drinks, Prolonged sitting or inactivity, Direct pressure or massaging the bruise, Stress and frustration, Cold or raw foods and drinks
- **Better with**: Gentle movement and stretching, Warm compress or heat application, Elevating the bruised area, Foods like turmeric and dark cherries, Adequate rest and sleep
- **Why this happens (Mechanism)**:
  When a bump or injury damages the small vessels, blood leaks into the tissues and gets stuck. Because it's no longer moving, it becomes stagnant and appears as a dark purple or bluish bruise. This stagnant blood presses on nerves and blocks the free flow of Qi, producing the characteristic fixed, stabbing pain that worsens at night and with pressure.
- **Tongue & Pulse**: Tongue: purple or dusky body with stasis spots and distended dark sublingual veins, thin white coat. Pulse: choppy (涩 sè) like a knife scraping bamboo, or wiry (弦 xián) if Qi stagnation is present.
- **Triggers & Relief Explanation**: Cold causes contraction and freezing of Blood, which directly worsens stagnation and pain, so warmth and gentle movement help dissolve stasis. Rest allows the body to repair, but too much inactivity causes Qi to stagnate further, preventing the bruise from clearing. Stress and frustration knot the Liver Qi, which in turn worsens Blood stasis, so keeping calm and moving gently breaks the cycle of stagnation.
- **Diet & Lifestyle**: Avoid cold and raw foods, which can congeal Blood and worsen stasis. Eat moderate amounts of Blood-moving foods like turmeric, dark cherries, eggplant, and dark leafy greens. Gentle daily movement such as walking or stretching helps keep Qi and Blood flowing, but avoid heavy impact on the bruised area. Apply a warm compress briefly after the first 24 hours to encourage circulation and speed healing.

### Qi And Blood Stagnation
*Pattern Link: [Detailed Pattern Page](/knowledge-base/patterns/qi-and-blood-stagnation)*

- **Clinical Signs & Symptoms**: Distending or pressure-like pain that may shift, Bruising worsens with emotional stress, Irritability or mood swings, Chest or rib-side tightness and discomfort, Purplish bruise with a dusky hue
- **Worse with**: Stress and frustration, Prolonged sitting or inactivity, Cold weather or drafts, Rich, greasy foods
- **Better with**: Gentle movement and stretching, Warm compress or heat application, Stress reduction and relaxation, Light massage around the area
- **Why this happens (Mechanism)**:
  When emotional stress, frustration, or trauma disrupts the smooth flow of Qi, the Liver’s ability to move blood is compromised. Stagnant Qi fails to propel blood, causing it to pool and form bruises. The result is a bruise that often feels distended or pressure-like, and its pain may shift rather than staying fixed.
Because the root is a Qi blockage, the bruising can appear or worsen after emotional upset, and it is frequently accompanied by chest tightness, rib-side discomfort, and irritability.
- **Tongue & Pulse**: Tongue: dark purple body with stasis spots, distended and tortuous sublingual veins, thin white coat. Pulse: wiry (xian) and choppy (se) - taut like a guitar string, yet rough and uneven.
- **Triggers & Relief Explanation**: Emotional stress directly knots Liver Qi, the very engine that moves blood; when Qi stalls, blood stagnates and bruises form. Inactivity and cold both slow circulation, while heavy, greasy foods create dampness that further obstructs flow. Gentle movement, warmth, and relaxation all encourage Qi to unblock and blood to circulate, allowing the stasis to resolve and the bruise to heal.
- **Diet & Lifestyle**: Keep the body gently moving with walking, stretching, or tai chi to encourage Qi flow. Use warming spices like turmeric and ginger in cooking, and avoid cold, raw foods that can congeal blood. Prioritize stress management - deep breathing, meditation, or talking things out - to prevent Liver Qi from knotting up.

### Qi Deficiency causing Blood Stagnation
*Pattern Link: [Detailed Pattern Page](/knowledge-base/patterns/qi-deficiency-causing-blood-stagnation)*

- **Clinical Signs & Symptoms**: Easy bruising from minor contact, Persistent fatigue and weakness, Bruises fade very slowly, Pale or slightly purplish tongue, Shortness of breath on exertion
- **Worse with**: Overexertion or heavy physical work, Prolonged standing, Cold or raw foods and drinks, Stress and frustration, Cold weather or drafts
- **Better with**: Adequate rest and sleep, Warm compress or heat application, Gentle movement and stretching, Qi-nourishing foods (congee, dates)
- **Why this happens (Mechanism)**:
  When Qi is deficient, it lacks the strength to push blood smoothly through the vessels. Blood then slows and pools under the skin, causing bruises to appear with minimal or no trauma. The bruise often looks pale purple and takes a long time to fade because the body lacks the vital energy to clear the stagnant blood.
- **Tongue & Pulse**: Tongue: pale body with a purplish tinge, possible stasis spots and teeth marks, thin white coat. Pulse: deep, fine, and choppy (涩, sè).
- **Triggers & Relief Explanation**: Qi is the body's engine for moving blood. Rest and nourishing foods rebuild Qi, so blood flows better and bruises clear. Overexertion and stress drain Qi further, leaving blood to stagnate. Cold constricts vessels, making it even harder for weak Qi to push blood through, so warmth helps relax and open the channels.
- **Diet & Lifestyle**: Eat warm, cooked foods like congee, stews, and soups with Qi-building ingredients such as astragalus root, dates, and ginger. Avoid raw, cold foods that weaken digestive Qi. Gentle daily exercise like walking or Tai Chi helps move Qi and blood without overexertion.

### Qi and Blood Deficiency
*Pattern Link: [Detailed Pattern Page](/knowledge-base/patterns/qi-and-blood-deficiency)*

- **Clinical Signs & Symptoms**: Pale, lingering bruises, Easy bruising from minor bumps, Fatigue and general weakness, Pale or sallow complexion and lips, Dizziness or lightheadedness, Shortness of breath on exertion
- **Worse with**: Overexertion or heavy physical work, Skipping meals or irregular eating, Cold or raw foods and drinks, Stress and frustration
- **Better with**: Adequate rest and sleep, Warm, nourishing meals, Gentle movement and stretching
- **Why this happens (Mechanism)**:
  When both Qi and Blood are deficient, the body lacks the vital force to keep blood sealed inside the vessels. Minor bumps that would barely leave a mark in a healthy person can break fragile capillaries, causing pale bruises that appear easily. Because Qi is too weak to move stagnant blood and Blood is too scanty to repair the tissue, the bruises linger and heal much more slowly than normal.
- **Tongue & Pulse**: Tongue: pale body, may be thin or have teeth marks on the sides, with a thin white coating. Pulse: fine (thin) and weak (细弱 xì ruò), lacking force.
- **Triggers & Relief Explanation**: Overexertion and stress drain Qi, leaving even less force to keep blood in the vessels and repair damaged tissue. Cold and raw foods damage Spleen Yang, impairing the production of new Qi and Blood. Rest and warmth conserve and slowly rebuild these vital substances, while gentle movement circulates Qi without depleting it, helping the body clear old bruises and prevent new ones.
- **Diet & Lifestyle**: Prioritize warm, cooked meals like soups and stews that are easy to digest and rich in nourishment - bone broth, dark leafy greens, small amounts of red meat if tolerated, and a few dates or goji berries. Avoid cold, raw foods and iced drinks which weaken the Spleen's ability to produce Qi and Blood. Gentle daily movement like walking or stretching helps circulation without draining your reserves, and getting enough deep rest is non-negotiable.

### Blood Stagnation with Heat
*Pattern Link: [Detailed Pattern Page](/knowledge-base/patterns/blood-stagnation-with-heat)*

- **Clinical Signs & Symptoms**: Bruise is red, swollen, and hot to the touch, Fixed stabbing pain that worsens at night, Sensation of internal heat or restlessness, Dark purplish-red skin with possible pus or infection, Thirst with desire to rinse mouth but not swallow
- **Worse with**: Spicy or fried food, Alcohol, Hot weather or warm rooms, Direct pressure or massaging the bruise
- **Better with**: Cool compress, Rest and elevation, Cooling foods (cucumber, pear)
- **Why this happens (Mechanism)**:
  When a bruise becomes infected or the body already carries internal heat toxins, the trapped blood turns from simple stagnation into a hot, inflammatory state. The heat accelerates tissue damage, making the bruise red, swollen, and intensely painful. This combination of static blood and heat creates a vicious cycle - the stuck blood generates more heat, and the heat further thickens and congeals the blood.
- **Tongue & Pulse**: Tongue: reddish-purple body with stasis spots, distended sublingual veins, and a thin dry yellow coating. Pulse: choppy (涩, sè) and rapid (数, shuò), often wiry (弦, xián).
- **Triggers & Relief Explanation**: Heat is the driving force behind this pattern - it inflames the bruise, thickens the blood, and intensifies pain. Anything that adds heat to the body, like spicy food, alcohol, or hot environments, will worsen the condition. Cooling measures, both external and dietary, counteract that heat, calm the inflammation, and help the stagnant blood disperse. Rest prevents further internal heat generation from activity and gives the body space to clear the stasis.
- **Diet & Lifestyle**: Avoid spicy, greasy, and fried foods that generate internal heat. Keep the bruised area clean and protected from further trauma or infection. A cool (not freezing) compress can soothe acute heat and swelling. Eat more cooling, blood-moving foods like cucumber, celery, and a little black fungus.

### Cold invading the Channels joints and muscles
*Pattern Link: [Detailed Pattern Page](/knowledge-base/patterns/cold-invading-the-channels-joints-and-muscles)*

- **Clinical Signs & Symptoms**: Bruises appear on joints or exposed limbs, Affected area feels cold to the touch, Pain and stiffness worsen in cold weather, Deep, fixed joint pain, Aversion to cold, preference for warmth
- **Worse with**: Cold weather or drafts, Cold or raw foods and drinks, Prolonged sitting or inactivity, Damp environments
- **Better with**: Warm compress or heat application, Gentle movement that warms joints, Warm foods and drinks, Avoiding cold environments, Moxibustion
- **Why this happens (Mechanism)**:
  Cold has a contracting, congealing nature. When it invades the channels, joints and muscles, it constricts blood vessels and slows circulation, causing blood to stagnate and form bruises. This bruising is not from a direct blow but from cold-induced stasis, and it typically appears on the joints or limbs that feel cold to the touch, with deep stiffness and aching pain that flare up in chilly weather.
- **Tongue & Pulse**: Tongue: pale body with a thin white coat, moist surface. Pulse: tight (Jin) and wiry (Xian), possibly slow (Chi).
- **Triggers & Relief Explanation**: Cold contracts the channels and congeals blood, worsening stasis and pain. Warmth relaxes the vessels, promotes circulation, and disperses cold, which helps blood move again and reduces bruising and stiffness.
- **Diet & Lifestyle**: Keep the bruised area warm with clothing or a heating pad. Avoid cold exposure and drafts. Eat warming foods like ginger, cinnamon, lamb and cooked vegetables, and steer clear of raw, chilled foods and icy drinks. Gentle movement that warms the joints without strain helps keep blood flowing.

## Frequently Asked Questions
### Can TCM help a bruise heal faster?
Yes. Herbs like Dang Gui and Hong Hua move blood and reduce swelling, while acupuncture points such as Xuehai SP-10 and Sanyinjiao SP-6 invigorate circulation. For a fresh bruise, apply a cool compress for the first 24 hours, then switch to warm applications and gentle movement to encourage blood flow. Many people find the discoloration fades noticeably quicker than it would on its own.

### I bruise easily without any injury. What does TCM say?
This is often a sign that your Spleen Qi is too weak to keep blood inside the vessels, or that your overall Qi and Blood are deficient. When the body lacks the vital force to seal the capillaries, even minor bumps cause leaks. A TCM practitioner will look at your tongue, pulse, and energy level to pinpoint the pattern and prescribe herbs like Huang Qi and Dang Gui to strengthen you from within.

### Is it safe to combine TCM with blood-thinning medications?
Caution is essential. Many blood-moving herbs - including Dang Gui, Chuan Xiong, and Hong Hua - can increase bleeding risk when taken alongside warfarin, aspirin, or clopidogrel. Always tell both your TCM practitioner and your prescribing doctor about every medication and supplement you use. They can adjust formulas or monitor you more closely to keep you safe.

### How long until I see results from TCM for bruising?
Acute bruises often improve within a week. Chronic easy bruising or old bruises that won’t fade may take 4-12 weeks of consistent treatment, because the goal is to rebuild your Qi and Blood rather than just clear one mark. Your practitioner will track changes in your energy, tongue, and pulse to gauge progress.

### Can acupuncture help with an old bruise that still hurts?
Yes. Stagnant blood that has been sitting for weeks or months can create a persistent ache. Acupuncture needles placed locally and at distal points like Taichong LR-3 and Hegu LI-4 help break up the stasis and restore circulation, often relieving the lingering discomfort and finally allowing the discoloration to clear.

### What foods should I eat to help bruises heal?
Warm, cooked foods that promote circulation are best. Think turmeric, ginger, black pepper, dark cherries, pineapple, and leafy greens. Avoid excessive cold or raw foods, which can constrict the vessels and slow healing. If you bruise easily, add Qi- and Blood-nourishing foods like bone broth, dates, and lean meats to your diet.
