## Conventional Medicine View
### Description
In Western medicine, retained placenta occurs when the placenta is not delivered within 30 minutes of the baby's birth. It is a leading cause of postpartum hemorrhage and can lead to severe bleeding and infection. Diagnosis is clinical, based on timing and physical examination, and may be confirmed by ultrasound. Risk factors include a previous retained placenta, preterm delivery, and uterine atony.

### Treatments
Standard management begins with controlled cord traction and uterine massage. If these fail, medications like oxytocin or prostaglandins are used to stimulate uterine contractions. When medical management is ineffective, manual removal under anesthesia or surgical intervention (such as dilation and curettage) is performed. Antibiotics may be given to prevent infection.

## Traditional Chinese Medicine View
### TCM Understanding
TCM views retained placenta through the lens of the Chong and Ren vessels - the body's deepest channels for blood and reproductive essence. After delivery, these vessels must close and the uterus must contract strongly to push the placenta out. When blood flow in these vessels becomes stagnant - often from exhaustion, cold, or emotional strain - the placenta becomes trapped. This is why blood stasis is a common thread in almost every case of retained placenta.
But stasis is rarely the whole story. In many women, the deeper problem is Qi deficiency: the body is simply too drained after labor to generate the force needed to expel the placenta. The uterus feels weak and ineffectual, and the mother may be profoundly exhausted. In other cases, heat has entered the blood chamber, making the blood thick and sticky - like glue holding the placenta in place. This pattern often comes with fever and foul-smelling lochia.
Finally, some women have a long-standing depletion of Kidney and Liver yin, leaving the uterus undernourished and unable to contract properly. This is more common after prolonged or difficult labors. Each of these four patterns - pure stasis, Qi deficiency with stasis, heat and stasis, or yin deficiency - requires a different treatment strategy, which is why TCM always tailors the approach to the individual mother.

### Practitioner Diagnosis
A TCM practitioner starts by asking about the sensation in the lower abdomen, the colour and smell of any lochia, and how tired the mother feels after delivery. The quality of pain, the presence of heat signs, and the mother’s overall strength quickly narrow the picture toward one of the four main patterns, each of which points to a different underlying imbalance.
When Blood Stagnation in the Directing and Penetrating Vessels (冲任, Chong Ren) is the driver, the pain is sharp and stabbing, and a firm lump may be felt low in the belly. Lochia is dark with clots, the tongue is purplish with possible stasis spots, and the pulse feels wiry or choppy. The absence of marked exhaustion or fever distinguishes this pattern from the others.
If Qi Deficiency causing Blood Stagnation is at play, extreme fatigue and weak, ineffectual contractions are the hallmark. The lochia tends to be pale and scant rather than dark, and there is little odour. The tongue looks pale, puffy, and tooth-marked, while the pulse is deep, weak, and choppy. The key difference is profound exhaustion rather than intense pain.
Heat and Blood Stagnation in the Lower Burner brings a very different picture: fever, restlessness, and lochia that is dark, thick, and foul-smelling. The lower abdomen is acutely tender. The tongue is reddish-purple with stasis spots and a dry yellow coating, and the pulse is deep, choppy, and rapid. The presence of heat signs is what sets this pattern apart from the others.
Kidney and Liver Yin Deficiency is less acute and often reflects long-term constitutional weakness. The mother may report lower back soreness, dizziness, night sweats, and a dry mouth. Contractions feel weak, and the retained placenta is due to insufficient essence rather than forceful stasis. The tongue is red with little coating, and the pulse is thin, rapid, and wiry.

### Classical Reference

## TCM Patterns and Treatment

### Blood Stagnation in the Directing and Penetrating Vessels
*Pattern Link: [Detailed Pattern Page](/knowledge-base/patterns/blood-stagnation-in-the-directing-and-penetrating-vessels)*

- **Clinical Signs & Symptoms**: Fixed, stabbing lower abdominal pain that worsens with pressure, Dark purple or blackish lochia with large clots, A palpable firm mass or fullness in the lower abdomen, Pain improves after passing clots, Tongue is dark purple with stasis spots and engorged veins underneath
- **Worse with**: Cold foods and drinks, Exposure to cold drafts or air conditioning, Emotional stress and frustration, Prolonged bed rest without movement
- **Better with**: Abdominal warmth, Gentle walking, Warm ginger or brown sugar drinks, Rest in a warm, calm environment
- **Why this happens (Mechanism)**:
  The Chong and Ren vessels (冲任, the Penetrating and Directing vessels) are the body's deepest channels for blood and reproductive essence. After delivery, these vessels should close and allow the uterus to contract and expel the placenta. When blood flow in these vessels becomes stagnant-often from emotional strain, cold exposure, or exhaustion during labor-the uterus loses the rhythmic movement it needs to release the placenta. The stuck blood forms clots that physically obstruct the cervix, leaving the placenta trapped inside.
- **Tongue & Pulse**: Tongue: dark purple body, possible purplish spots on the edges, engorged tortuous sublingual veins, thin white coat. Pulse: choppy (涩 sè, rough and uneven like a knife scraping bamboo) and often wiry (弦 xián), especially at the left middle position; may be deep at the rear positions.
- **Triggers & Relief Explanation**: Cold causes blood vessels to constrict and blood to congeal, which directly worsens the stasis that is trapping the placenta. Warmth does the opposite-it relaxes the vessels and encourages blood to flow again. Gentle movement helps Qi circulate, and when Qi moves, blood follows. Emotional stress stagnates Liver Qi, which is closely tied to the Chong and Ren vessels; calming the mind helps unlock the pelvic circulation and allows the uterus to complete its work.
- **Diet & Lifestyle**: Keep the lower abdomen warm with a hot water bottle or warm compress to encourage blood flow. Gentle walking around the room can help restart circulation, but avoid strenuous activity. Eat warm, cooked foods like ginger chicken soup or brown sugar water, and steer clear of iced drinks, raw salads, and cold fruit.

### Qi Deficiency causing Blood Stagnation
*Pattern Link: [Detailed Pattern Page](/knowledge-base/patterns/qi-deficiency-causing-blood-stagnation)*

- **Clinical Signs & Symptoms**: Postpartum exhaustion and weakness, Weak, ineffective uterine contractions, Dull, heavy abdominal pain that does not move, Pale face with a greyish or dusky tinge, Shortness of breath and reluctance to speak
- **Worse with**: Overexertion or standing too long, Cold foods and drinks, Emotional stress and anxiety
- **Better with**: Rest, Warm, nourishing soups or stews, Abdominal warmth
- **Why this happens (Mechanism)**:
  After delivery, the body is already drained of Qi and blood. If the mother was weak before birth or lost too much blood during labor, the remaining Qi is simply too feeble to push the placenta out. This sluggishness allows blood to congeal in the uterus, forming a stasis that traps the placenta-like a pump that has lost its power and leaves a blockage behind.
- **Tongue & Pulse**: Tongue: pale body with a purplish tinge, possible stasis spots, teeth marks, and a thin white coat. Pulse: deep, weak, and choppy (涩 sè).
- **Triggers & Relief Explanation**: Qi deficiency means the body has little power to push blood. Rest and warm, nourishing foods help rebuild that Qi, while overexertion and cold further drain and congeal it. Gentle warmth encourages blood to move without consuming Qi, but heavy pressure can aggravate the underlying stasis.
- **Diet & Lifestyle**: Absolute rest is essential-lying down conserves Qi. Take warm, easily digestible soups and congees with ginger, red dates, and a little lean protein to rebuild strength. Avoid standing or walking for long periods, and keep the lower abdomen gently warm with a cloth or warm water bottle (not heavy pressure).

### Heat and Blood Stagnation in the Lower Burner
*Pattern Link: [Detailed Pattern Page](/knowledge-base/patterns/heat-and-blood-stagnation-in-the-lower-burner)*

- **Clinical Signs & Symptoms**: Fixed sharp lower abdominal pain that worsens with pressure, Fever that worsens at night, Dark clotted blood, Mental restlessness or agitation, Dry mouth and throat
- **Worse with**: Spicy, greasy or fried foods, Emotional stress or anger, Hot environment, Strenuous activity
- **Better with**: Cooling compress on lower abdomen, Light, cooling foods like mung bean soup, Rest in a cool room, Acupuncture to invigorate blood
- **Why this happens (Mechanism)**:
  After childbirth, if heat enters the blood chamber or if stagnant blood transforms into heat, the blood becomes thick, clotted, and sticky. This sticky blood obstructs the uterus, gluing the placenta in place so it cannot detach and be expelled. The heat also agitates the blood and spirit, producing fever, restlessness, and a sense of pressure, while the stasis causes fixed, sharp pain.
- **Tongue & Pulse**: Tongue: reddish-purple body with stasis spots, distended sublingual veins, and a dry yellow coating. Pulse: deep (Chen), choppy (Se), and rapid (Shu).
- **Triggers & Relief Explanation**: Heat and blood stasis in the lower burner is a combination of excess heat and stagnant blood. Heat dries the blood and makes it sticky, while stasis obstructs the passages. Spicy foods and emotional stress add more heat, fueling the fire and making the blood thicker, which locks the placenta in place. Cooling foods and rest help clear heat and calm the blood, allowing the stasis to be resolved. Acupuncture points like Zhongji and Xuehai directly move blood in the uterus, while Taichong clears heat, so the body can let go of the retained tissue.
- **Diet & Lifestyle**: Eat light, cooling foods like mung bean soup, celery, and spinach, and avoid spicy, greasy, or heating foods that can worsen heat and stasis. Rest in a cool, calm environment and avoid emotional stress, which can stir up more heat.

### Kidney and Liver Yin Deficiency
*Pattern Link: [Detailed Pattern Page](/knowledge-base/patterns/kidney-and-liver-yin-deficiency)*

- **Clinical Signs & Symptoms**: Dizziness and ringing in the ears, Dry eyes with blurred or cloudy vision, Night sweats and heat in the palms, soles, and chest, Soreness and weakness of the lower back and knees, Insomnia or restless sleep with vivid dreams, Dry mouth and throat
- **Worse with**: Overexertion after delivery, Spicy, greasy or fried foods, Emotional stress and worry, Hot environment
- **Better with**: Rest, Warm, nourishing soups or stews, Goji berry tea, Gentle walking, Moderate warmth (not overheating)
- **Why this happens (Mechanism)**:
  The Kidneys store the essence that governs reproduction and uterine function, while the Liver provides the blood that nourishes the sinews and ensures smooth contraction. When both are chronically depleted, the uterus lacks the deep nourishment and contractile force needed to complete the third stage of labor. The resulting deficiency heat further dries the tissues, leaving the womb sluggish and unable to expel the placenta.
- **Tongue & Pulse**: Tongue: red, thin body with cracks, little or no coating, dry surface. Pulse: fine, rapid, and wiry; weak at the left Chi (Kidney) position.
- **Triggers & Relief Explanation**: Kidney and Liver Yin are deep reserves that rebuild with rest, sleep, and moistening foods. Overexertion, stress, and heat consume these reserves, leaving the uterus too weak to contract and expel the placenta. Gentle warmth and nourishing meals replenish the essence needed for uterine function, while rest prevents further depletion.
- **Diet & Lifestyle**: Focus on deeply nourishing, moistening foods like black beans, goji berries, walnuts, and bone broths. Avoid spicy, fried, and drying foods that consume precious Yin. Prioritize rest and gentle movement to rebuild deep energy reserves.

## Frequently Asked Questions
### Can TCM help if my placenta is retained right now?
Retained placenta is a medical emergency. You should seek immediate Western medical care - do not delay. TCM can be used alongside emergency measures to support uterine contraction and lochia expulsion, but it should never replace urgent manual removal or medication. Once the placenta is delivered, TCM herbs and acupuncture can help clear remaining stasis and speed recovery.

### Is it safe to take Chinese herbs while breastfeeding?
Yes, when prescribed by a qualified TCM practitioner who knows you are breastfeeding. Many postpartum herbs, such as Yi Mu Cao (Leonurus) and Dang Gui (Angelica), are traditionally used to support lactation and recovery. However, some herbs can pass into breast milk or affect milk supply, so self-prescribing is never safe. Always inform your practitioner and your baby's pediatrician.

### How quickly can acupuncture help with retained placenta?
Acupuncture can stimulate uterine contractions within minutes to hours when applied at specific points like Sanyinjiao (SP-6) and Hegu (LI-4). It is often used in hospital settings alongside conventional care. In cases of incomplete expulsion, a single session may be enough to trigger the needed contractions. For ongoing recovery, treatments are typically given daily or every other day for the first week.

### Will TCM treatment prevent retained placenta in a future birth?
Addressing the underlying pattern - whether it's Qi deficiency, blood stasis, or yin depletion - can strengthen the uterus and improve its contractile function, potentially reducing the risk of recurrence. Many women choose to work with a TCM practitioner during a subsequent pregnancy to build Qi and blood and ensure smooth delivery. However, every birth is unique, and no treatment can guarantee prevention.

### Can I use TCM if I had a manual removal?
Absolutely. After manual removal, TCM herbs and acupuncture can help clear any remaining blood stasis, reduce pain, promote uterine involution, and rebuild your energy. This can speed healing and lower the risk of infection or prolonged bleeding. Just be sure your TCM practitioner knows about all procedures and medications you received.

### What foods should I eat after a retained placenta?
Warm, easily digestible foods are best. Bone broths, congee with ginger and red dates, and lightly cooked vegetables help replenish Qi and blood. Avoid cold drinks, raw salads, and icy foods, which can constrict the uterus and worsen stasis. If you had fever or signs of heat, choose cooling but still warm foods like mung bean soup, and avoid spicy dishes.

### Does retained placenta always mean there is blood stasis?
Blood stasis is almost always involved in retained placenta, but it is often secondary to another imbalance. For example, Qi deficiency can cause sluggish blood flow that leads to stasis; heat can thicken the blood into stasis; and yin deficiency can leave the uterus too dry to expel the placenta. So while invigorating blood is a key part of treatment, the root cause must also be addressed for full recovery.
