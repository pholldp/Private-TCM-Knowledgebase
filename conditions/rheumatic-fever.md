## Conventional Medicine View
### Description
Rheumatic fever is an autoimmune reaction triggered by a Group A streptococcal infection, most often strep throat. The body's immune system mistakenly attacks its own tissues, leading to inflammation in the joints, heart, skin, and sometimes the brain. Typical symptoms include migratory arthritis - pain and swelling that moves from one joint to another - along with fever, a characteristic rash, and, in severe cases, carditis that can damage heart valves. Diagnosis relies on the Jones criteria, which combine clinical signs like arthritis, carditis, and chorea with evidence of a recent strep infection. It most commonly affects children and young adults in areas with limited access to antibiotics.

### Treatments
Standard treatment has two goals: eradicate the strep bacteria and manage inflammation. Antibiotics, usually penicillin, are given to clear the infection, followed by long-term prophylaxis to prevent recurrence. Joint pain and fever are managed with high-dose aspirin or other nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs (NSAIDs); severe heart inflammation may require corticosteroids. Once valve damage occurs, ongoing cardiac monitoring and sometimes surgery become necessary. Physical rest is also prescribed during acute episodes to reduce strain on the heart.

## Traditional Chinese Medicine View
### TCM Understanding
TCM understands rheumatic fever as a form of Bi syndrome, or painful obstruction. This occurs when external pathogenic factors - Wind, Damp, Cold, or Heat - invade the body through the skin and muscles, lodging in the joints and channels. The invasion happens because the body's protective Qi (Wei Qi) is weak, often due to overwork, poor diet, or a constitutional tendency. Once inside, these pathogens block the flow of Qi and blood, causing pain, swelling, and stiffness. The nature of the pathogen determines the character of the pain: Heat makes joints red, hot, and swollen; Cold and Damp make them achy, heavy, and worse in wet weather.
But rheumatic fever is not only about the joints. In TCM, the Heart is the emperor organ, and if Heat penetrates deeper into the blood and channels, it can disturb the Heart, leading to palpitations, chest discomfort, and the valve damage seen in carditis. This is why TCM treatment always keeps one eye on the joints and the other on the Heart, using herbs that clear Heat from the blood and calm the spirit.
Because the same Western diagnosis can present so differently - one person has hot, red knees that throb at night, another has stiff, cold ankles that ease with a warm compress - TCM sorts the condition into distinct patterns. The most common is Damp Heat in the Channels, marked by bright inflammation and a yellow tongue coat. Wind-Cold-Damp presents with pain that worsens in cold, damp weather.
When the illness lingers, Phlegm can congeal in the channels, creating fixed, knobby swellings. And in those with a weak constitution or after many recurrences, Liver and Kidney Deficiency leaves the joints chronically sore and weak. Each pattern has its own herbal formula, acupuncture strategy, and dietary advice.

### Practitioner Diagnosis
A TCM practitioner begins by feeling the temperature and appearance of your joints. Hot, red, and swollen joints that feel worse with heat and humidity point strongly toward Damp Heat in the Channels. The tongue typically shows a yellow, greasy coating and the pulse feels rapid and slippery, like beads rolling under the finger.
If instead the joints are painful but not red or hot, and the ache deepens in cold or damp weather while easing with a warm compress, the picture shifts toward Wind-Cold-Damp invasion. The tongue coating is usually white and the pulse feels tight, like a taut string, confirming cold and dampness obstructing the channels.
When pain settles in fixed, knobby spots and small lumps appear under the skin near the joints, a practitioner suspects Phlegm has congealed in the channels. The tongue may look purplish with a sticky coating, and the pulse can feel rough or slippery, reflecting the thick, stagnant quality of phlegm and blood stasis together.
In later stages or in people who have always felt frail, the joints may ache dully with a sense of weakness and heaviness rather than sharp inflammation. This suggests Liver and Kidney Deficiency failing to nourish the sinews and bones. A pale tongue and a weak, thready pulse support this pattern, where the root problem is a lack of vitality rather than an excess of pathogens.

## TCM Patterns and Treatment

### Painful Obstruction due to Damp Heat in Channels
*Pattern Link: [Detailed Pattern Page](/knowledge-base/patterns/painful-obstruction-due-to-damp-heat-in-channels)*

- **Clinical Signs & Symptoms**: Joints red, swollen, and hot to the touch, Pain relieved by cold, aggravated by warmth, Yellow greasy tongue coating, Heavy, aching sensation in limbs, Fever, thirst, and irritability
- **Worse with**: Hot, humid weather, Spicy, greasy, or rich foods, Alcohol, Overexertion, Emotional stress and frustration
- **Better with**: Cold compresses on joints, Rest and avoiding overwork, Cool, dry environment, Light, non-greasy foods, Drinking plenty of water
- **Why this happens (Mechanism)**:
  In TCM, Rheumatic Fever arises when external Dampness and Heat invade the body, often due to living in hot, humid environments or when the body's defenses are weak. These pathogens lodge in the joints and channels, blocking the flow of Qi and blood. The obstruction causes intense inflammation, resulting in joints that become red, swollen, and hot to the touch. The Heat pathogen also stirs up internal heat, leading to fever, thirst, and irritability, while the Dampness creates a heavy, aching sensation and swelling.
- **Tongue & Pulse**: Tongue: red body, possibly slightly swollen, with a yellow greasy coating. Pulse: slippery and rapid, may feel soggy or wiry at the left middle position.
- **Triggers & Relief Explanation**: Dampness and Heat are the core pathogens in this pattern. Warmth and humidity feed these pathogens, intensifying joint inflammation, while cold compresses and cool environments counteract the Heat. Spicy and greasy foods generate more internal Heat and Dampness, whereas light, hydrating foods help clear them. Overexertion strains the already obstructed channels, and emotional stress stirs up Liver Qi, which can transform into Heat, worsening the condition.
- **Diet & Lifestyle**: During acute flare-ups, rest is crucial. Apply cool compresses to affected joints. Avoid hot, humid environments. Eat light, cooling foods like cucumber, watermelon, and mung beans, and avoid spicy, greasy, or fried foods, as well as alcohol. Drink plenty of water to help clear Heat and Dampness.

### Painful Obstruction with Wind-Cold-Damp
*Pattern Link: [Detailed Pattern Page](/knowledge-base/patterns/painful-obstruction-with-wind-cold-damp)*

- **Clinical Signs & Symptoms**: Aching pain that worsens in cold or damp weather, Stiffness and limited movement, relieved by warmth, Heaviness or numbness in the limbs, Swelling without redness or heat, Aversion to cold or wind
- **Worse with**: Cold and damp weather or environments, Exposure to wind or drafts, Cold foods and drinks, Prolonged inactivity or sitting, Overexertion
- **Better with**: Applying warmth or heat, Gentle movement or stretching, Warm, dry weather, Warm, cooked foods and spices
- **Why this happens (Mechanism)**:
  When the body's protective Qi is weak, external wind, cold, and dampness can invade the channels and settle in the joints. These pathogens obstruct the normal flow of Qi and blood, creating stagnation and pain. Because the nature of cold is to contract and dampness is heavy and sticky, the pain worsens in cold or humid weather and improves with warmth. This pattern often appears in the early or subacute stages of rheumatic fever, especially in people with a constitutional tendency toward cold and dampness.
- **Tongue & Pulse**: Tongue: pale, slightly swollen body with teeth marks and a white greasy coating. Pulse: wiry and tight, or soggy and slow.
- **Triggers & Relief Explanation**: Cold and dampness are the main pathogens in this pattern. Cold contracts the channels and slows circulation, while dampness creates heaviness and stagnation. Warmth and gentle movement counteract these effects by dispersing cold, drying dampness, and promoting the smooth flow of Qi and blood. Cold foods and damp environments introduce more of the same pathogens, which is why they make symptoms worse.
- **Diet & Lifestyle**: Keep the body warm and avoid exposure to cold, wind, and dampness. Wear protective clothing and use heating pads or warm baths. Eat warm, cooked foods like soups and stews, and incorporate warming spices such as ginger, cinnamon, and black pepper. Avoid raw, cold, and greasy foods that can worsen dampness. Gentle, consistent movement like tai chi or walking helps keep Qi and blood flowing without overstraining the joints.

### Phlegm in the Channels joints and muscles
*Pattern Link: [Detailed Pattern Page](/knowledge-base/patterns/phlegm-in-the-channels-joints-and-muscles)*

- **Clinical Signs & Symptoms**: Fixed, stabbing pain that feels like a knife, Hard, firm swelling around the joints, Subcutaneous nodules or hard lumps near joints, Purple or dark discolouration of the skin, Stubborn numbness or heavy sensation in the limbs, Joint stiffness with difficulty bending and straightening
- **Worse with**: Cold and damp weather or environments, Rich, greasy, or dairy-heavy foods, Prolonged inactivity or sitting, Emotional stress and frustration
- **Better with**: Gentle movement or stretching, Warm, dry weather, Light, non-greasy foods, Applying warmth or heat
- **Why this happens (Mechanism)**:
  When dampness lingers in the body-often after an acute flare of rheumatic fever or from a constitution prone to phlegm-it can slowly condense into a thick, sticky substance called Phlegm. This Phlegm gums up the channels that run through the joints and muscles, creating hard, fixed obstructions. Over time, the stuck Phlegm also blocks the flow of Blood, leading to stabbing pain, purplish skin, and the firm nodules that can appear under the skin.
- **Tongue & Pulse**: Tongue: purple body with stasis spots, white greasy coating, distended sublingual veins. Pulse: wiry (Xian) and choppy (Se), or wiry and slippery if Phlegm predominates.
- **Triggers & Relief Explanation**: Cold and dampness congeal Qi and Blood, thickening Phlegm and worsening obstruction, while warmth and movement gently push stagnation through the channels. Greasy foods directly feed Phlegm production, so a light diet starves the obstruction. Stress knots the Liver Qi, which can further impede the flow of Blood, so keeping calm helps keep the joints supple.
- **Diet & Lifestyle**: Avoid cold, raw, and greasy foods, especially dairy, which can create more Phlegm. Eat warm, lightly spiced meals to support digestion and keep the channels open. Gentle, daily movement like walking or stretching is essential to prevent stagnation, but avoid overexerting sore joints.

### Painful Obstruction with Liver and Kidney Deficiency
*Pattern Link: [Detailed Pattern Page](/knowledge-base/patterns/painful-obstruction-with-liver-and-kidney-deficiency)*

- **Clinical Signs & Symptoms**: Chronic dull joint pain and stiffness, Lower back and knee soreness and weakness, Fatigue and lack of stamina, Numbness or tingling in limbs, Pale or dull complexion, Worse in cold and damp weather
- **Worse with**: Cold and damp weather or environments, Overexertion, Raw or cold foods, Prolonged standing or walking
- **Better with**: Applying warmth or heat, Gentle movement or stretching, Rest and avoiding overwork, Warm, nourishing foods
- **Why this happens (Mechanism)**:
  The Liver and Kidneys nourish the sinews and bones. When they are weakened by chronic illness, aging, or constitutional factors, the joints lose their foundation and become sore, weak, and stiff. This deficiency also lowers the body's defensive Qi, allowing cold and dampness to invade and further obstruct the channels, leading to chronic aching pain that worsens in cold, damp weather.
- **Tongue & Pulse**: Tongue: pale, thin body with a thin white coating. Pulse: fine, weak, and slow; the Chi (rear) position is especially weak.
- **Triggers & Relief Explanation**: This pattern is driven by deficiency of the Liver and Kidneys, which leaves the joints undernourished and vulnerable to cold and damp invasion. Warmth and nourishing foods help restore the depleted Essence and Yang, while cold and damp further obstruct the channels and worsen pain. Gentle movement encourages Qi and blood flow without depleting the body's reserves, whereas overexertion drains the already weak foundation.
- **Diet & Lifestyle**: Eat warm, cooked foods like stews, soups, and congees with ginger, cinnamon, and black beans. Avoid raw, cold, and greasy foods that weaken digestion and invite dampness. Keep joints warm and protected from drafts and damp environments. Gentle movement like Tai Chi or stretching helps maintain flexibility, but avoid overexertion that depletes Qi.

## Frequently Asked Questions
### Can TCM help with the joint pain and swelling of rheumatic fever?
Yes. TCM excels at reducing inflammation and pain by clearing the specific pathogens - Heat, Damp, Cold, or Phlegm - that are blocking your channels. Acupuncture can quickly ease joint stiffness, while herbal formulas work internally to cool Heat or dry Dampness. Many patients notice less swelling and better mobility within the first two weeks, though full resolution depends on the pattern.

### Is acupuncture safe during an acute flare, when my joints are very inflamed?
Absolutely, but your practitioner will choose points away from the most inflamed joints and use gentle needling to avoid overstimulating the area. The goal during an acute flare is to drain Heat and move Qi systemically, often using points on the hands, feet, and back. You should always tell your acupuncturist about any heart symptoms before treatment.

### Will I need to take Chinese herbs long-term?
It varies. For an acute first episode, a 4- to 6-week course is often enough to clear the pathogens and restore balance. If you have recurrent attacks or heart involvement, your practitioner may recommend longer-term, lower-dose formulas - sometimes taken seasonally - to strengthen your constitution and prevent future episodes. These maintenance herbs are generally gentler and well tolerated.

### Can TCM prevent rheumatic heart disease?
TCM cannot reverse existing valve damage, but it can help prevent further damage by clearing lingering Heat from the blood and strengthening the Heart's Qi and Yin. Early treatment during the acute phase is critical. If you already have carditis, TCM works alongside your cardiologist's care to calm inflammation and support heart function.

### How does TCM treatment differ from just taking anti-inflammatory drugs?
Anti-inflammatories suppress the symptom - the inflammation - but do not change the underlying environment that allowed the illness to develop. TCM aims to correct that environment by expelling the pathogenic factors (Damp, Heat, Cold) and strengthening the body's own defenses. This dual approach can reduce the likelihood of recurrence and, for many, lessen the need for long-term medication.

### What should I eat to support healing from rheumatic fever?
In general, avoid damp-producing foods: greasy, fried, and heavily processed items, dairy, sugar, and iced drinks. Focus on warm, cooked meals. Barley, adzuki beans, and lightly cooked vegetables help drain Dampness. If your joints are hot and red, avoid spicy foods and alcohol. If they feel cold and achy, add warming spices like ginger, cinnamon, and a little turmeric. Your practitioner will fine-tune this based on your specific pattern.

### Are there any side effects from the herbs or acupuncture?
When prescribed by a qualified TCM practitioner, herbs are generally safe and side effects are rare. Acupuncture may cause minor bruising or temporary soreness at needle sites. Because some herbs can influence blood clotting, it is essential to tell your practitioner about all medications, especially aspirin or warfarin. We cover urgent symptoms in our Safety section - please review those red flags before starting treatment.
