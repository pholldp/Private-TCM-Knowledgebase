## Conventional Medicine View
### Description
A sebaceous cyst (also called an epidermoid cyst) is a benign, slow-growing lump that forms when skin cells and keratin become trapped beneath the skin's surface. These cysts are usually round, smooth, and painless, though they can become inflamed or infected, causing redness, pain, and discharge. They commonly appear on the face, neck, upper back, and chest. Diagnosis is typically made by physical examination; imaging is rarely needed unless the cyst is unusually large or deep.

### Treatments
Small, asymptomatic cysts are often left alone. If a cyst becomes inflamed or infected, treatment may include warm compresses, incision and drainage, or antibiotics. For recurrent or bothersome cysts, surgical excision (removing the entire cyst sac) is the definitive treatment, as incomplete removal often leads to recurrence. Steroid injections can sometimes reduce inflammation.

## Traditional Chinese Medicine View
### TCM Understanding
TCM views sebaceous cysts as a form of 'phlegm nodule' (痰核, tán hé). The Spleen is responsible for transforming and transporting fluids; when its function is weakened by poor diet, stress, or constitutional weakness, dampness accumulates and eventually congeals into phlegm. This phlegm can lodge in the channels just beneath the skin, forming a smooth, movable, painless lump - the classic quiet cyst.
But a cyst doesn't always stay quiet. When toxic-heat invades - from spicy food, emotional stress, or an external infection - it combines with the phlegm-dampness, causing the cyst to become red, hot, painful, and filled with pus. This is an acute inflammatory shift, and it requires a completely different treatment approach focused on clearing heat and toxins.
Over time, if a lump persists, the local stagnation of Qi and phlegm can obstruct blood flow, leading to blood stasis. The cyst becomes firmer, more fixed, and often darker or tender. This chronic pattern is harder to resolve and reflects deeper stagnation. So the same Western diagnosis can represent three very different TCM patterns, and the right treatment must match the current phase of the cyst.

### Practitioner Diagnosis
A TCM practitioner begins by examining the cyst itself - its colour, temperature, mobility, and whether it causes pain. A painless, skin-coloured lump that glides easily under the finger points in one direction, while a red, hot, tender swelling points in another. These local clues are then matched with whole-body signs, such as the state of the tongue and pulse, to identify the underlying pattern.
When the cyst is a smooth, moveable, flesh-coloured nodule with no redness or discomfort, the picture is one of Phlegm in the Channels. This is the most common uncomplicated presentation. The tongue is often pale and slightly swollen with a thick white-greasy coating, and the pulse feels deep and slippery. The person may also feel heavy or sluggish, reflecting the phlegm-dampness that has settled under the skin.
If the cyst becomes inflamed - red, hot, swollen, and painful, perhaps oozing pus - the pattern has shifted to Toxic-Heat Stagnation. This is the body’s acute inflammatory response to local infection. The tongue turns red with a yellow greasy or thick coat, and the pulse becomes rapid and slippery. Accompanying symptoms may include thirst, irritability, and a feeling of heat in the body.
A long-standing or recurrent cyst that feels firm, fixed, and dark or purplish, often with some surrounding redness, suggests Blood Stagnation with Heat. This pattern develops when chronic phlegm obstruction damages the local blood flow. The tongue appears dark purple with possible stasis spots, and the pulse is wiry or choppy. The heat component adds a low-grade inflammatory quality that keeps the area stubborn and slow to resolve.

## TCM Patterns and Treatment

### Phlegm in the Channels joints and muscles
*Pattern Link: [Detailed Pattern Page](/knowledge-base/patterns/phlegm-in-the-channels-joints-and-muscles)*

- **Clinical Signs & Symptoms**: Painless, movable nodule under the skin, Central blackhead or plugged pore, Firm but not rock-hard, like a small rubber ball, No redness, heat, or pain, Heavy sensation in the limbs or body, May have multiple cysts
- **Worse with**: Damp living environment, Overeating rich, greasy foods, Dairy products, Lack of exercise or prolonged sitting, Stress and worry
- **Better with**: Warm compress, Gentle daily exercise, Light, warm meals, Avoiding dairy and sweets, Living in a dry environment
- **Why this happens (Mechanism)**:
  In TCM, a sebaceous cyst is considered a form of 'phlegm nodule' (痰核, tán hé). When the Spleen’s ability to transform fluids is weakened, dampness accumulates and congeals into phlegm. This phlegm can settle in the channels just beneath the skin, forming a smooth, painless, movable lump. Over time, the phlegm may obstruct the flow of Qi and blood locally, leading to a firmer texture and a slightly darker appearance, though the cyst remains non-inflamed.
- **Tongue & Pulse**: Tongue: purple body with stasis spots, white greasy coating, distended sublingual veins. Pulse: wiry and choppy, or wiry and slippery if phlegm predominates.
- **Triggers & Relief Explanation**: Phlegm-dampness forms when the Spleen is overwhelmed by rich, damp-producing foods or weakened by worry and fatigue. Warmth and gentle movement support the Spleen’s transforming function and keep Qi and fluids moving, preventing stagnation. A dry environment and light diet reduce the dampness burden, while greasy, sweet, and dairy foods directly create more phlegm, worsening the cyst.
- **Diet & Lifestyle**: Avoid dairy, greasy, fried, and sweet foods that create dampness and phlegm. Eat warm, cooked meals like congee, steamed vegetables, and moderate amounts of ginger and garlic. Regular gentle exercise such as walking or yoga helps the Spleen transform fluids and keeps Qi moving, preventing phlegm from settling under the skin.

### Toxic-Heat Stagnation
*Pattern Link: [Detailed Pattern Page](/knowledge-base/patterns/toxic-heat-stagnation)*

- **Clinical Signs & Symptoms**: Painful, hot, red cyst, Pus or cheesy discharge with foul odor, Fever or feeling of intense heat, Intense thirst with desire for cold drinks, Restlessness and irritability, Constipation or dark scanty urine
- **Worse with**: Spicy, greasy, or fried foods, Alcohol and coffee, Stress and anger, Squeezing or picking at the cyst, Hot, humid weather
- **Better with**: Cool compress, Drinking plenty of water, Eating cooling foods (cucumber, watermelon), Rest in a cool environment, Gentle cleansing
- **Why this happens (Mechanism)**:
  A sebaceous cyst is normally a painless lump of trapped phlegm-dampness under the skin. When toxic-heat builds up-often from spicy food, stress, or an external infection-it turns the cyst into a red, hot, painful, and pus-filled inflammation. This is the body's intense defensive reaction, with heat and toxin collecting locally and potentially spreading if not cleared.
- **Tongue & Pulse**: Tongue: deep red or crimson body with prickly papillae and a thick, dry, yellow coating. Pulse: rapid (shu) and forceful (shi), often overflowing (hong).
- **Triggers & Relief Explanation**: Toxic-heat is an aggressive, fiery energy that flares up with heating foods, alcohol, and emotional upset. Cooling foods and compresses directly counteract this heat, helping the body clear the toxin and reduce inflammation. Rest and gentle care prevent further irritation, while squeezing can drive the heat deeper and spread the infection.
- **Diet & Lifestyle**: Avoid all spicy, greasy, and fried foods, as well as alcohol and coffee, which fan the flames of heat. Eat plenty of cooling, water-rich foods like cucumber, watermelon, and mung beans. Keep the area clean and never squeeze the cyst-this can push the infection deeper. A cool compress can soothe the inflammation.

### Blood Stagnation with Heat
*Pattern Link: [Detailed Pattern Page](/knowledge-base/patterns/blood-stagnation-with-heat)*

- **Clinical Signs & Symptoms**: Firm, fixed lump that does not move easily, Dark or purplish skin over the cyst, Tenderness and sensation of heat over the cyst, Pain that worsens at night, Irritability and restlessness, Thirst with desire to rinse mouth but not swallow
- **Worse with**: Emotional stress and frustration, Spicy, greasy, or fried foods, Alcohol and coffee, Lack of exercise or prolonged sitting, Squeezing or picking at the cyst
- **Better with**: Cool compress, Gentle daily exercise, Avoiding tight clothing over the area, Stress reduction, Eating cooling, blood-moving foods
- **Why this happens (Mechanism)**:
  When a sebaceous cyst lingers for months or years, the local stagnation of phlegm and Qi eventually starts to block blood flow. That trapped blood becomes stasis, making the lump feel firm, fixed, and darker than the surrounding skin. Over time the stasis generates heat, so the area becomes tender, warm, and sometimes red or inflamed.
- **Tongue & Pulse**: Tongue: reddish-purple body with stasis spots, distended sublingual veins, and a thin dry yellow coating. Pulse: choppy (涩 sè) and rapid (数 shuò), often wiry (弦 xián) at the left Guan position.
- **Triggers & Relief Explanation**: Emotional stress and spicy foods generate heat and stagnate Qi, worsening blood stasis and heat. Gentle movement and cooling foods help move Qi and blood while clearing heat, which counteracts the stasis and inflammation. Pressure or squeezing aggravates local stasis and can introduce infection, so it's avoided.
- **Diet & Lifestyle**: Avoid spicy, greasy, and deep-fried foods that generate heat and phlegm. Eat more cooling, blood-moving foods such as cucumber, celery, hawthorn berry, and moderate amounts of vinegar. Gentle daily exercise like walking or tai chi keeps Qi and blood moving to prevent stasis.

## Frequently Asked Questions
### Can TCM make my cyst disappear without surgery?
Yes, especially if the cyst is small, painless, and of the phlegm type. Herbal formulas that transform phlegm and drain dampness, along with dietary changes, can gradually shrink the cyst. However, if the cyst is large, infected, or has been present for years, surgery may still be needed. TCM can be used before surgery to reduce inflammation or after to prevent recurrence.

### What should I eat to prevent sebaceous cysts?
Focus on foods that support the Spleen and reduce phlegm-dampness: cooked whole grains, vegetables (especially radish, water chestnut), and moderate lean protein. Avoid dairy, greasy or fried foods, refined sugar, and excessive raw or cold foods. If you tend toward inflammation, add cooling foods like cucumber and mung beans, and avoid spicy, hot, and alcohol-laden items.

### Is it safe to treat an infected cyst with herbs?
Yes, but only under the guidance of a qualified TCM practitioner. Herbal formulas for Toxic-Heat can help resolve infection and reduce pain. However, if you have signs of systemic infection (fever, spreading redness), you must seek urgent medical care. TCM can be used alongside antibiotics if needed - always inform both your doctor and practitioner.

### Will the cyst come back after TCM treatment?
TCM aims to correct the internal imbalance that caused the cyst, so recurrence is less likely than with drainage or incomplete surgical removal. However, if dietary and lifestyle habits that generate phlegm-dampness return, new cysts can form. Long-term prevention often involves periodic herbal 'tune-ups' and maintaining a healthy diet.

### Can acupuncture alone treat a sebaceous cyst?
Acupuncture can support treatment by improving local circulation, reducing inflammation, and strengthening the Spleen, but it is rarely sufficient on its own for a cyst. Herbal medicine is the primary tool because it directly transforms phlegm, clears heat, or moves blood stasis. A combination of acupuncture and herbs often yields the best results.

### How long does it take to see results?
For an inflamed cyst, you may see improvement in redness and pain within a few days. A small, soft cyst may start shrinking in 2-4 weeks. Hard, chronic cysts require 6-12 weeks or more. Consistent treatment and dietary changes are key; stopping too early can lead to recurrence.
