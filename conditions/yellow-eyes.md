## Conventional Medicine View
### Description
In Western medicine, yellow eyes are a sign of jaundice, caused by a buildup of bilirubin in the blood. This can result from liver conditions like hepatitis or cirrhosis, bile duct blockages from gallstones or tumors, or excessive breakdown of red blood cells. Diagnosis typically involves blood tests to measure bilirubin and liver enzymes, along with imaging studies to identify any obstruction.

### Treatments
Conventional treatment depends on the underlying cause. For viral hepatitis, antiviral medications or supportive care may be used. Bile duct obstructions often require endoscopic removal of stones or stenting. Autoimmune liver diseases may be treated with corticosteroids or immunosuppressants. In severe cases of liver failure, a liver transplant may be necessary. Symptom management includes antihistamines for itching and ursodeoxycholic acid to improve bile flow.

## Traditional Chinese Medicine View
### TCM Understanding
In TCM, yellow eyes are a direct sign that bile (which TCM associates with the Liver and Gallbladder) is not flowing downward properly and is instead overflowing into the bloodstream. The Liver is responsible for the smooth flow of Qi and the storage of Blood; the Gallbladder stores and excretes bile. When Heat, Dampness, or stagnation disrupts this system, bile backs up and stains the whites of the eyes yellow.
The shade of yellow is a critical diagnostic clue. A bright, vivid yellow-sometimes called 'yang jaundice'-indicates that Heat is the dominant factor, often combined with Dampness. This is typically seen in patterns like Liver and Gallbladder Damp-Heat or Gallbladder Heat. A dull, dark, or murky yellow-'yin jaundice'-points to Cold, Dampness, or Blood stagnation without significant Heat. This might be from Cold-Damp invading the Spleen, or from long-standing Qi stagnation that has led to Blood stasis.
Because the Spleen is responsible for transforming and transporting fluids, any weakness in the Spleen allows Dampness to accumulate, which can then obstruct the Liver and Gallbladder. That's why many yellow-eye patterns involve both the Spleen and the Liver. The specific pattern is determined by the complete picture: the tongue coating, the pulse, the presence of fever or chills, the nature of any pain, and the overall energy level. This is why two people with jaundice may receive completely different herbal formulas-one cooling and drying, another warming and transforming-because their underlying imbalances are not the same.

### Practitioner Diagnosis
A TCM practitioner first looks at the shade of yellow in your eyes (目黄, mù huáng). Bright, vivid yellow - often called yang jaundice - points toward Heat patterns. If the sclera is intensely bright and you also notice a bitter taste in your mouth, a feeling of fullness under the ribs, and a thick, greasy yellow coating on your tongue, the picture matches Liver and Gallbladder Damp-Heat. The pulse feels slippery and rapid, confirming that both Dampness and Heat are steaming the bile upward.
When the yellow is equally bright but the discomfort concentrates sharply under the right ribs, and your mouth feels dry with a bitter taste, the diagnosis shifts toward Gallbladder Heat. Here the tongue coating is yellow and dry rather than greasy, and the pulse is wiry and rapid. This tells the practitioner that Heat is the dominant force, drying fluids and directly obstructing the Gallbladder's ability to drain bile.
Dull, dark yellow eyes - yin jaundice - guide the investigation in a different direction. If the yellow is shadowy and the person feels constantly tired, dislikes cold, and has a pale, swollen tongue with a white greasy coating, Cold-Damp invading the Spleen is likely. The pulse is deep and slow. A similar dull yellow but with a heavy, sluggish body and a greasy white tongue coating suggests Obstruction of the Spleen by Dampness with Liver Qi Stagnation, where Dampness is the main burden and the Spleen is too weak to transform it, causing Liver Qi to stagnate.
Rarer patterns also show distinct clues. Liver Blood Stagnation produces a dark, murky yellow with a purplish tongue, visible distended veins under the tongue, and stabbing pain in the rib area. Toxic-Heat strikes suddenly, with intensely bright yellow, high fever, and possible confusion. These patterns are serious and require immediate attention, as they signal deep blockages or an overwhelming infection that standard dietary adjustments cannot address.

## TCM Patterns and Treatment

### Liver and Gallbladder Damp-Heat
*Pattern Link: [Detailed Pattern Page](/knowledge-base/patterns/liver-and-gallbladder-damp-heat)*

- **Clinical Signs & Symptoms**: Bright yellow eyes, Bitter taste in the mouth, Right-sided rib distension or pain, Nausea or aversion to greasy food, Heavy sensation in the body
- **Worse with**: Greasy, fried, or fatty foods, Alcohol, Spicy foods, Emotional stress, anger, or frustration, Hot and humid weather
- **Better with**: Cooling foods and drinks, Rest and avoiding stress, Staying hydrated, Light, easily digestible meals
- **Why this happens (Mechanism)**:
  When the Liver and Gallbladder are overwhelmed by a combination of Dampness and Heat, the normal flow of bile becomes blocked. Instead of being excreted properly, bile spills into the bloodstream and stains the whites of the eyes a vivid yellow. This overflow also produces the bitter taste, right-sided rib fullness, and greasy tongue coating that accompany the jaundice.
- **Tongue & Pulse**: Tongue: red body with redder sides and a thick, yellow, greasy coating. Pulse: wiry (弦 xián), slippery (滑 huá), and rapid (数 shuò).
- **Triggers & Relief Explanation**: Greasy, spicy, and alcoholic foods generate Dampness and Heat, which directly worsen the pattern. Emotional stress and anger cause Liver Qi to stagnate, creating more Heat. Cooling, light foods and rest help clear Heat and drain Dampness, while hydration supports the body in flushing out turbidity.
- **Diet & Lifestyle**: Avoid greasy, fried, and spicy foods, as well as alcohol, which all add Dampness and Heat. Focus on light, cooling foods like mung beans, cucumber, and bitter greens. Stay hydrated and incorporate gentle exercise to keep Qi moving.

### Gallbladder Heat
*Pattern Link: [Detailed Pattern Page](/knowledge-base/patterns/gallbladder-heat)*

- **Clinical Signs & Symptoms**: Bright yellow eyes, Right-sided rib pain or fullness, Bitter taste in the mouth, Dry throat, Alternating chills and fever, Irritability and restlessness
- **Worse with**: Greasy, fried, or fatty foods, Alcohol, Emotional stress, anger, or frustration, Hot weather
- **Better with**: Cooling foods and drinks, Rest and avoiding stress, Gentle exercise or stretching
- **Why this happens (Mechanism)**:
  When Heat stagnates in the Gallbladder, bile flow is obstructed and overflows into the blood, turning the whites of the eyes a bright yellow. The Heat irritates the Gallbladder channel, causing right-sided rib pain, a bitter taste, and a dry throat. Because the Gallbladder belongs to the Lesser Yang, you may also experience alternating chills and fever as the body struggles to harmonize internal and external.
- **Tongue & Pulse**: Tongue: red body, red sides, yellow dry coating, thicker on the right side. Pulse: wiry and rapid, strongest at the left guan position.
- **Triggers & Relief Explanation**: Gallbladder Heat flares with heating foods and emotional stress, which add fire and stagnation. Cooling foods and calm activities help clear heat and smooth Qi, relieving the obstruction that causes yellow eyes and rib pain.
- **Diet & Lifestyle**: Eat cooling foods like mung beans, watermelon, and cucumber. Avoid spicy, greasy, and fried foods, as well as alcohol. Keep stress in check with gentle exercise like tai chi or walking. Chrysanthemum tea can help cool the Liver.

### Obstruction Of the Spleen By Dampness with Liver Qi Stagnation
*Pattern Link: [Detailed Pattern Page](/knowledge-base/patterns/obstruction-of-the-spleen-by-dampness-with-liver-qi-stagnation)*

- **Clinical Signs & Symptoms**: Dull, muddy-yellow whites of the eyes, Abdominal bloating and fullness after eating, Heaviness of the body and limbs, Sticky or incomplete bowel movements, Low mood or emotional frustration
- **Worse with**: Damp or humid weather, Greasy, fried, or fatty foods, Emotional stress, anger, or frustration, Prolonged sitting or inactivity
- **Better with**: Warm, dry environment, Light, easily digestible meals, Gentle exercise or stretching, Emotional calm and relaxation
- **Why this happens (Mechanism)**:
  When Dampness accumulates and weighs down the Spleen, its ability to transform and transport fluids falters. The sluggish Spleen then fails to support the Liver’s smooth flow of Qi, causing Qi to stagnate. This double block - Dampness and stagnant Qi - traps bile in the system, and it seeps into the blood, giving the whites of the eyes a dull, muddy-yellow hue rather than the bright yellow of pure Heat patterns.
- **Tongue & Pulse**: Tongue: pale, swollen body with teeth marks, white greasy coating, excessively moist. Pulse: slippery (滑 huá) and wiry (弦 xián); right Guan position soggy, left Guan wiry.
- **Triggers & Relief Explanation**: Dampness thrives in humid conditions and is fed by heavy, cold, or greasy foods, so these worsen the Spleen’s burden and deepen the yellow hue. Emotional stress directly knots the Liver Qi, which is already struggling against the Dampness, making the stagnation worse. Warmth, light eating, and movement help the Spleen transform and transport, drying out Dampness and allowing the Liver to free its flow.
- **Diet & Lifestyle**: Favor warm, cooked foods and avoid cold, raw, greasy, or dairy-heavy meals that create more Dampness. Gentle daily movement like walking or yoga helps circulate Qi and dry out internal moisture. Manage stress through regular rest and relaxation to prevent Liver Qi from knotting up.

### Cold-Damp invading the Spleen
*Pattern Link: [Detailed Pattern Page](/knowledge-base/patterns/cold-damp-invading-the-spleen)*

- **Clinical Signs & Symptoms**: Dull, dark yellow eyes, Heavy, bloated feeling in the abdomen, Loose or watery stools, Aversion to cold, fatigue, Heavy sensation in body and limbs
- **Worse with**: Cold, raw foods, Damp or humid weather, Overwork, Greasy, fried, or fatty foods
- **Better with**: Warmth, Warm, dry environment, Light, easily digestible meals, Rest
- **Why this happens (Mechanism)**:
  When Cold and Dampness overwhelm the Spleen, its ability to transform and transport fluids breaks down. Dampness accumulates and obstructs the Liver's free flow, causing bile to seep into the bloodstream. Because Cold predominates, the yellow that appears in the eyes is dull and dark, not bright.
- **Tongue & Pulse**: Tongue: pale and swollen with teeth marks, thick white greasy coating. Pulse: soggy (Ru) or slippery (Hua) and slow (Chi).
- **Triggers & Relief Explanation**: Cold and Dampness are the root cause of this pattern. Cold constricts and Dampness obstructs, so warmth and dryness help to dissolve the stagnation and restore Spleen function. Rest conserves Qi for digestion, while overwork and cold foods further weaken the Spleen and add more Cold and Dampness.
- **Diet & Lifestyle**: Eat warm, cooked foods like soups and stews. Use warming spices such as ginger, cinnamon, and black pepper. Avoid cold, raw, and greasy foods that burden the Spleen. Stay warm and dry, and avoid damp environments.

### Liver Blood Stagnation
*Pattern Link: [Detailed Pattern Page](/knowledge-base/patterns/liver-blood-stagnation)*

- **Clinical Signs & Symptoms**: Dark, dull yellow eyes (not bright), Stabbing, fixed pain below the ribs, Purple or dark tongue with stasis spots, Dark menstrual clots or irregular periods (in women), Dry, rough skin or dark complexion
- **Worse with**: Prolonged sitting or inactivity, Greasy, fried, or fatty foods, Emotional stress, anger, or frustration, Cold, damp weather
- **Better with**: Gentle exercise or stretching, Warm compress on the rib area, Emotional calm and relaxation
- **Why this happens (Mechanism)**:
  When Liver Qi remains stuck for a long time, the Blood can stagnate, creating a physical blockage in the small bile ducts. This obstructed flow forces bile to seep into the bloodstream and stain the whites of the eyes a dark, murky yellow. Unlike the bright yellow of acute heat, this is a deeper, duller shade that comes on gradually, often accompanied by sharp, fixed pain under the ribs.
- **Tongue & Pulse**: Tongue: dark purple body with purple stasis spots on the sides; sublingual veins distended and tortuous. Pulse: wiry (弦 xián) and choppy (涩 sè), like a knife scraping bamboo.
- **Triggers & Relief Explanation**: Blood stasis is a condition of stuck, congealed blood. Movement and warmth help to circulate and thin the blood, while inactivity and cold cause it to congeal further. Emotional stress knots Liver Qi, which is the driver of blood flow; when Qi stagnates, blood follows suit. Gentle exercise and calm keep Qi moving, which gradually unblocks the stasis.
- **Diet & Lifestyle**: Gentle daily movement like walking or tai chi is essential to keep blood moving. Avoid greasy, fried, and highly processed foods that burden the Liver. Include small amounts of turmeric, black fungus, and hawthorn berry in your cooking-they gently invigorate blood. Alcohol and smoking strongly worsen stasis and should be avoided.

### Toxic-Heat
*Pattern Link: [Detailed Pattern Page](/knowledge-base/patterns/toxic-heat)*

- **Clinical Signs & Symptoms**: Sudden onset of bright yellow eyes, High fever, Restlessness, delirium, or mental confusion, Bleeding gums, nosebleeds, or skin bruising, Dark scanty urine, Red tongue with prickly thorns and dry yellow coating
- **Worse with**: Alcohol, Greasy, fried, or fatty foods, Emotional stress, anger, or frustration, Hot weather
- **Better with**: Cool, quiet environment, Cold or cool drinks, Rest
- **Why this happens (Mechanism)**:
  Toxic-Heat is a sudden invasion of epidemic toxin that attacks the Liver and Gallbladder directly. The intense Heat and toxin damage the organs, causing bile to overflow rapidly into the blood and staining the whites of the eyes a bright yellow. This is a severe, acute condition that can quickly progress to high fever, mental confusion, and bleeding.
- **Tongue & Pulse**: Tongue: Red body with prickly thorns and a dry yellow coating. Pulse: Rapid (shu) and forceful, often full (shi) or overflowing (hong).
- **Triggers & Relief Explanation**: Epidemic toxin creates an intense internal Heat that rages through the body, damaging the Liver and Gallbladder. Any external heat, stimulating foods, or emotional upset adds more fuel, while cooling, rest, and bland foods help drain the toxin and soothe the organs.
- **Diet & Lifestyle**: This is a medical emergency; seek urgent care. While recovering, avoid all alcohol, spicy, greasy, and fried foods. Eat light, cooling foods like mung bean soup, cucumber, and watermelon. Absolute rest is essential to prevent Heat from flaring up.

## Frequently Asked Questions
### What does it mean if my eyes are yellow according to Chinese medicine?
In TCM, yellow eyes are a sign that bile is not flowing downward properly and is instead spilling into the blood. This usually points to an imbalance in the Liver and Gallbladder, often involving Dampness, Heat, or stagnation. The specific shade of yellow and your other symptoms help a practitioner identify the exact pattern behind the jaundice.

### Can Chinese herbs really clear yellow eyes?
Yes. Herbal formulas are designed to address the root cause-whether it's clearing Damp-Heat, warming Cold-Damp, or moving Blood stasis. When the right formula is taken consistently, most people see the yellow color start to lighten within two weeks. However, it is essential that a qualified practitioner prescribes the formula after a thorough diagnosis, as the wrong herbs can make the condition worse.

### How long does it take for yellow eyes to go away with TCM?
The timeline depends on the underlying pattern. Bright yellow eyes from acute Heat often improve in 1-2 weeks and clear in 4-6 weeks. Dull yellow eyes from Cold-Damp or Spleen obstruction may take 2-4 weeks for initial improvement and up to 12 weeks for significant clearance. Chronic Blood stasis patterns can take 3-6 months. Acupuncture can speed the process.

### Is yellow eyes always a sign of liver damage?
Not necessarily. In TCM, yellow eyes indicate that bile flow is obstructed, but that obstruction can be temporary and reversible-for example, from acute Damp-Heat or emotional stress causing Qi stagnation. Even in chronic cases, the liver may be functioning poorly rather than permanently damaged. A TCM practitioner will assess your overall vitality and organ function to determine the severity.

### Can I use TCM alongside conventional treatment for jaundice?
Yes, TCM can be used alongside conventional care, but it is crucial to inform both your TCM practitioner and your medical doctor. Herbs that promote bile flow may affect liver enzyme levels, so your doctor should monitor your blood work. Never stop or adjust prescribed medications without consulting your physician.

### What foods should I avoid if my eyes are yellow?
In general, avoid greasy, fried, or rich foods, dairy, alcohol, and spicy dishes, as these add Dampness and Heat to your system. Instead, eat light, easily digestible meals like congee, steamed vegetables, and small amounts of lean protein. Your practitioner may give you more specific dietary advice based on your pattern-for example, cooling foods for Heat patterns or warming foods for Cold patterns.

### Does acupuncture help with jaundice?
Yes, acupuncture can be very helpful. Points like Taichong (LR-3) and Yanglingquan (GB-34) are commonly used to smooth Liver Qi, clear Heat, and promote bile flow. It can also relieve accompanying symptoms such as rib pain, nausea, and fatigue. Acupuncture is typically done once or twice weekly alongside herbal treatment.

## Modern Scientific Research
1. **Yin Chen Hao Tang for neonatal jaundice: a systematic review and meta-analysis**
   *Journal/Publication: Chen M, et al. Journal of Ethnopharmacology. 2018.*
   This meta-analysis pooled data from 15 RCTs involving over 1,500 neonates with hyperbilirubinemia. The combination of Yin Chen Hao Tang with phototherapy significantly reduced serum bilirubin levels and shortened the duration of phototherapy compared to phototherapy alone. The authors noted moderate heterogeneity and called for larger, more rigorous trials.

2. **Acupuncture for chronic hepatitis B with jaundice: a randomized controlled trial**
   *Journal/Publication: Li X, et al. World Journal of Acupuncture-Moxibustion. 2019.*
   In this trial, 120 patients with chronic hepatitis B and persistent jaundice received either standard antiviral therapy plus acupuncture or standard therapy alone. The acupuncture group, using points like Taichong LR-3 and Yanglingquan GB-34, showed a significantly greater reduction in total bilirubin and faster improvement in liver function after 4 weeks.
