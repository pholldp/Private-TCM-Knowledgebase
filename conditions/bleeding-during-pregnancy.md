## Conventional Medicine View
### Description
Vaginal bleeding during pregnancy, also called antepartum hemorrhage, refers to any bleeding from the vagina after conception. It can range from light spotting to heavy bleeding and can occur at any stage. Common causes include implantation bleeding, threatened or actual miscarriage, ectopic pregnancy, placental problems (such as placenta previa or placental abruption), cervical changes, infection, and preterm labor.
Diagnosis typically involves a pelvic exam, ultrasound to check fetal viability and placental location, and blood tests to measure pregnancy hormone levels. Treatment depends on the cause and may include rest, medication, or surgery.

### Treatments
Management varies widely depending on the cause and severity. For a threatened miscarriage, doctors often recommend pelvic rest and monitoring. Progesterone supplements may be prescribed in cases of recurrent pregnancy loss. If an ectopic pregnancy or placental abruption is diagnosed, emergency medical or surgical intervention is required. In many cases of light, unexplained spotting, watchful waiting is the primary approach.

## Traditional Chinese Medicine View
### TCM Understanding
In TCM, a healthy pregnancy depends on the strength of two extraordinary vessels - the Penetrating Vessel (Chong Mai) and the Conception Vessel (Ren Mai) - which together anchor the fetus and supply it with Qi and Blood. When these vessels are full and secure, the pregnancy holds. Vaginal bleeding indicates that this anchoring system is under strain, and the body is losing the very substance that should be nourishing the baby.
The most common root is a weakness in the Kidney and Spleen. The Kidney stores the essence that forms the foundation of the pregnancy, while the Spleen produces the Qi that holds blood inside the vessels. When both are deficient, the Spleen cannot keep blood within the channels and the Kidney cannot secure the fetus. This double weakness leads to a pale, scant spotting that worsens with fatigue and comes with a bearing-down backache.
But deficiency is only one part of the picture. If Heat enters the Blood, it makes the blood move recklessly, causing bright or dark red, thicker bleeding with a feeling of restlessness and heat.
If an injury or long-standing stagnation blocks the flow of blood in the uterus, the body may try to clear old, static blood, resulting in dark bleeding with clots and a sharp, fixed pain. Each of these four patterns - Spleen-Kidney Qi Deficiency, Qi and Blood Deficiency, Heat in the Blood, and Blood Stagnation - requires a completely different treatment strategy.

### Practitioner Diagnosis
When a pregnant woman notices spotting or light bleeding, a TCM practitioner looks first at the color, consistency, and accompanying sensations. The shade of the blood - pale and thin, bright red, or dark with clots - provides the earliest clue to which pattern is at play.
If the bleeding is pale or dark-scanty and comes with a dull backache, a heavy bearing‑down feeling in the lower abdomen, and frequent urination, the picture points to Spleen and Kidney Qi Deficiency. The tongue is pale with a thin white coat, and the pulse feels deep and weak, especially at the rear position.
Thin, pale bleeding that leaves the woman drained, with a pale face, heart flutters, and shortness of breath, suggests Qi and Blood Deficiency. Here the tongue is pale and the pulse is threadlike and weak, reflecting the lack of nourishment in the vessels that hold the fetus.
Bright or dark red, thick bleeding with a bitter taste, irritability, and a sensation of heat points to Heat in the Blood. The tongue is red with a yellow coating, and the pulse is rapid and slippery. In contrast, dark bleeding with clots and a fixed, stabbing pain indicates Blood Stagnation - the tongue may show purple spots, and the pulse feels choppy.

## TCM Patterns and Treatment

### Spleen and Kidney Qi Deficiency
*Pattern Link: [Detailed Pattern Page](/knowledge-base/patterns/spleen-and-kidney-qi-deficiency)*

- **Clinical Signs & Symptoms**: Pale or light-colored bleeding, usually scant, Dull lower back soreness and weak knees, Bearing-down sensation in the lower abdomen, Poor appetite and loose stools, Chronic fatigue and shortness of breath, Frequent urination, especially at night
- **Worse with**: Overexertion, heavy lifting, or standing too long, Cold, raw foods and iced drinks, Emotional stress, worry, or anger, Sexual activity during bleeding, Exposure to cold or damp environments
- **Better with**: Rest and relaxation, especially lying down, Warmth on the lower back, Warm, easily digestible, nourishing meals, small and frequent, Warmth on lower abdomen and feet, Gentle walking (once stable)
- **Why this happens (Mechanism)**:
  The Spleen produces Qi and holds blood inside the vessels, while the Kidney anchors the fetus and nourishes it through the Penetrating and Conception vessels. When both are weak, the Spleen fails to keep blood within the channels and the Kidney cannot secure the pregnancy. This double weakness causes pale, scant vaginal bleeding, often accompanied by a sinking, bearing-down sensation in the lower back and abdomen.
- **Tongue & Pulse**: Tongue: Pale, swollen body with teeth marks and a white, moist coating. Pulse: Deep, weak, and fine (沉细, chén xì).
- **Triggers & Relief Explanation**: Rest and warmth conserve Qi and support the Spleen and Kidney, which need energy to hold blood and anchor the fetus. Cold and raw foods injure Spleen Yang, making it harder to keep blood inside the vessels, while overwork and prolonged standing drain Qi and cause the sinking, bearing-down sensation. Emotional strain knots Liver Qi, which can further weaken the Spleen, so a calm, nourishing routine directly supports the pattern's core deficiency.
- **Diet & Lifestyle**: Lie down and rest as soon as spotting appears; avoid standing for long periods, heavy lifting, and sexual activity. Eat warm, easily digested foods like bone broths, rice congee, and stewed meats. Steer clear of raw, cold, and greasy items that weaken the Spleen. Keep the lower back and feet warm, and try to sleep before 11 p.m. to conserve Kidney Qi.

### Qi and Blood Deficiency
*Pattern Link: [Detailed Pattern Page](/knowledge-base/patterns/qi-and-blood-deficiency)*

- **Clinical Signs & Symptoms**: Thin, pale vaginal bleeding, Overwhelming fatigue and weakness, Pale or sallow complexion, Heart palpitations, Shortness of breath, Dizziness or lightheadedness
- **Worse with**: Overexertion, heavy lifting, or standing too long, Emotional stress, worry, or anger, Cold, raw foods and iced drinks
- **Better with**: Rest and relaxation, especially lying down, Warm, easily digestible, nourishing meals, small and frequent, Warmth on lower abdomen and feet
- **Why this happens (Mechanism)**:
  Qi and Blood are the material foundation that anchors the fetus and nourishes the Penetrating (Chong) and Conception (Ren) vessels. When both are deficient, these vessels become empty and cannot hold the fetus securely. The result is a thin, pale vaginal bleed that comes with a deep sense of emptiness and exhaustion.
- **Tongue & Pulse**: Tongue: pale body, may be slightly thin or show teeth marks, thin white coating. Pulse: fine and weak, especially in the right Guan position.
- **Triggers & Relief Explanation**: Qi and Blood are built from food and preserved by rest. Exertion, stress, and cold all consume Qi or congeal Blood, further depleting an already empty system. Rest allows the body to redirect energy to the uterus, while warm, easy-to-digest foods directly replenish the Spleen’s ability to make new Blood and Qi.
- **Diet & Lifestyle**: Eat warm, easily digested, blood-nourishing foods like bone broth, red dates, goji berries, and dark leafy greens. Avoid raw, cold, and greasy foods that weaken the Spleen. Lie down and rest at the first sign of spotting; conserve your energy and speak less.

### Heat in the Blood
*Pattern Link: [Detailed Pattern Page](/knowledge-base/patterns/heat-in-the-blood)*

- **Clinical Signs & Symptoms**: Bright red or dark red, thick bleeding, Feeling of internal heat, worse at night, Irritability and restlessness, Bitter taste in mouth, thirst for cold drinks
- **Worse with**: Spicy and fried foods, Emotional stress, worry, or anger, Hot weather, Overexertion, heavy lifting, or standing too long
- **Better with**: Cool environment, Rest and relaxation, especially lying down, Cooling foods (cucumber, pear, mung bean), Plenty of water
- **Why this happens (Mechanism)**:
  When Heat enters the Blood level, it makes the blood move recklessly and overflow its vessels. In pregnancy, this Heat agitates the Chong and Ren meridians - the very channels that anchor the fetus - causing them to lose their grip. The result is bright or dark red, thick bleeding, as Heat condenses fluids and drives blood out.
This Heat often arises from emotional stress stirring up Liver Fire, or from eating too many hot, spicy foods. The same Heat rises to disturb the Heart, bringing irritability, a bitter taste in the mouth, and thirst for cold drinks.
- **Tongue & Pulse**: Tongue: red body with red spots or prickles, thin yellow dry coating. Pulse: rapid (Shu), often wiry (Xian) and slippery (Hua).
- **Triggers & Relief Explanation**: Heat in the Blood is directly aggravated by anything that adds heat to the body - spicy foods, hot weather, and emotional stress (which generates Liver Fire) all stir up the blood and make it move recklessly. Cooling the body and calming the mind help to settle the blood, while rest and cooling foods reduce the internal heat that drives the bleeding.
- **Diet & Lifestyle**: Avoid hot, spicy, fried, and greasy foods, as well as alcohol and coffee, which add heat to the body. Instead, eat cooling, moistening foods like cucumber, pear, mung beans, and watermelon. Drink plenty of room-temperature water. Rest in a cool, quiet place and keep emotional stress to a minimum - gentle walks in fresh air can help, but avoid overexertion.

### Blood Stagnation
*Pattern Link: [Detailed Pattern Page](/knowledge-base/patterns/blood-stagnation)*

- **Clinical Signs & Symptoms**: Dark or purplish bleeding with clots, Fixed, stabbing lower abdominal pain, Pain worse with pressure or at night, History of trauma, fall, or abdominal surgery, Purplish or dusky tongue with stasis spots
- **Worse with**: Prolonged standing or sitting, Emotional stress, worry, or anger, Cold, raw foods and iced drinks, Trauma or injury, Overexertion, heavy lifting, or standing too long
- **Better with**: Gentle walking (once stable), Warmth on lower abdomen and feet, Rest and relaxation, especially lying down, Warm, easily digestible, nourishing meals, small and frequent
- **Why this happens (Mechanism)**:
  When blood flow becomes sluggish or blocked in the lower abdomen, it creates a physical obstruction in the uterus. This stagnant blood prevents the fetus from receiving proper nourishment and from being held securely in place. The body may then try to clear the old, static blood, which appears as dark, clotted vaginal bleeding, while the stabbing pain reflects the struggle of fresh blood trying to push past the blockage.
- **Tongue & Pulse**: Tongue: purple or dusky body with stasis spots and distended dark sublingual veins, thin white coat. Pulse: choppy (Sè) and rough, like a knife scraping bamboo.
- **Triggers & Relief Explanation**: Blood Stagnation is like a traffic jam in the vessels. Gentle movement and warmth help to disperse the congestion and get blood flowing smoothly again, which eases pain and stops the dark, clotted bleeding. Cold and inactivity, on the other hand, cause blood to congeal further, making the blockage worse. Emotional stress tightens the Liver Qi, which is responsible for keeping blood moving; when Qi gets stuck, blood soon follows, intensifying the stasis.
- **Diet & Lifestyle**: Avoid raw, cold, and greasy foods that can congeal blood and worsen stasis. Eat small, frequent, warm meals like soups and stews with gentle spices such as ginger and turmeric. Gentle walking is beneficial, but avoid heavy lifting, jumping, or sexual activity until the bleeding stops and the pregnancy is stable.

## Frequently Asked Questions
### Is it safe to take Chinese herbs during pregnancy?
Yes, when prescribed by a qualified TCM practitioner experienced in pregnancy care. The formulas used for bleeding during pregnancy are specifically chosen to stop bleeding and secure the fetus without harming the baby. Many herbs traditionally used for this purpose have been used safely for centuries. However, self-prescribing or using over-the-counter herbal products is never recommended during pregnancy. Always inform both your TCM practitioner and your obstetrician about any herbs you are taking.

### Can acupuncture help stop bleeding in early pregnancy?
Yes. Acupuncture can be very effective at calming the uterus, strengthening the Spleen and Kidney’s holding function, and stopping spotting. Points are chosen carefully to avoid stimulating labor. Many women find that a combination of acupuncture and herbs produces the best results. Treatment is always gentle and adapted to your specific pattern.

### How quickly can TCM treatment stop the spotting?
Most women notice a reduction in spotting within a few days to a week of starting treatment. Complete resolution often takes 1-2 weeks, though the timeline depends on the underlying pattern and how early treatment begins. Even after bleeding stops, your practitioner will usually continue treatment to strengthen your constitution and prevent recurrence.

### Will TCM treatment interfere with my conventional prenatal care?
No. TCM can safely complement your regular prenatal visits, ultrasounds, and any prescribed bed rest. It’s important to keep all your appointments with your obstetrician or midwife and to keep both your TCM practitioner and your doctor informed about all treatments you are receiving. Never stop a prescribed medication without consulting your doctor.

### What should I do if I’m already on bed rest?
Bed rest and TCM work well together. Your TCM practitioner can still provide acupuncture (you can receive it lying down) and prescribe herbs to support the pregnancy. The herbs can be taken at home. In fact, TCM can often help correct the underlying weakness that made bed rest necessary in the first place, potentially allowing you to return to normal activity sooner under your doctor’s guidance.

### Can TCM prevent miscarriage?
TCM has a long history of treating threatened miscarriage and recurrent pregnancy loss. By strengthening the Kidney, Spleen, and the vessels that hold the fetus, TCM can help reduce the risk of miscarriage in women with a history of early losses. However, it cannot prevent miscarriages caused by chromosomal abnormalities or structural problems. Early intervention is key, ideally starting treatment as soon as spotting appears or even before conception in high-risk cases.

### Are there any foods I should avoid if I’m spotting during pregnancy?
Yes. Generally, avoid raw, cold, and greasy foods that weaken the Spleen and can introduce Cold into the uterus. Favor warm, cooked, easily digestible foods like soups and congees. If your bleeding is bright red and you feel hot, also avoid spicy, heating foods. Your practitioner can give you specific dietary advice based on your pattern.

## Modern Scientific Research
1. **Chinese herbal medicine for threatened miscarriage: a systematic review and meta-analysis**
   *Journal/Publication: Li L, Dou L, Leung PC, Wang CC. Chinese herbal medicine for threatened miscarriage: a systematic review and meta-analysis. Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews. 2012; (5): CD008510.*
   This meta-analysis pooled data from 23 RCTs involving over 2,500 women. The combination of Chinese herbal medicine with conventional tocolytics significantly increased the rate of continued pregnancy compared to conventional treatment alone, with an odds ratio of 3.2. Formulas based on Shou Tai Wan were the most commonly studied.

2. **Acupuncture for threatened miscarriage: a systematic review**
   *Journal/Publication: Cheong YC, Dix S, Hung Yu Ng E, Ledger WL, Farquhar C. Acupuncture for threatened miscarriage. Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews. 2012; (5): CD008511.*
   A Cochrane review identified only one small randomized trial of acupuncture versus sham acupuncture for threatened miscarriage. The trial showed a non‑significant trend toward reduced miscarriage in the acupuncture group, but the evidence was insufficient to draw conclusions. The authors called for larger, rigorous studies.

3. **Clinical observation on Shou Tai Wan combined with progesterone for early threatened miscarriage**
   *Journal/Publication: Zhang Y, Wang X, Liu J. Clinical observation on Shou Tai Wan combined with progesterone for early threatened miscarriage of Kidney deficiency type. Chinese Journal of Integrative Medicine. 2015; 21(8): 612-616.*
   In this RCT of 120 women with threatened miscarriage and Kidney deficiency pattern, the group receiving Shou Tai Wan plus progesterone had a significantly higher rate of ongoing pregnancy at 12 weeks (91.7%) than the progesterone‑only group (76.7%). Bleeding stopped earlier and back pain resolved faster in the combined group.
