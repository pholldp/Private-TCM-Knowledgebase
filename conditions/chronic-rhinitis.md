## Conventional Medicine View
### Description
In Western medicine, chronic rhinitis is defined as persistent inflammation of the nasal lining that lasts longer than 12 weeks. It can be allergic (triggered by pollen, dust mites, or pet dander) or non-allergic (caused by irritants, weather changes, or unknown factors). The hallmark symptoms are nasal congestion, a runny nose, sneezing, and postnasal drip. Diagnosis is usually based on a patient's history and a physical exam, sometimes supported by allergy testing or imaging to rule out sinusitis. While the underlying inflammation is well understood, conventional treatment often focuses on symptom control rather than addressing why the inflammation persists in the first place.

### Treatments
Standard treatments include intranasal corticosteroid sprays to reduce inflammation, oral antihistamines for allergic symptoms, decongestant sprays (for short-term use), and saline rinses. For allergic rhinitis, allergen avoidance and immunotherapy (allergy shots or tablets) may be recommended. In severe cases, surgery may be considered to correct structural problems. These approaches can effectively manage symptoms, but they do not address the underlying constitutional tendencies that make a person prone to chronic nasal inflammation.

## Traditional Chinese Medicine View
### TCM Understanding
TCM sees the nose as the outward opening of the Lungs. The strength of your Lung Qi determines how well your nose can resist external invaders and keep itself clear. When Lung Qi is weak, the defensive shield (Wei Qi) becomes thin, so every draft and cold breeze slips right in, leaving the nose chronically stuffy with a steady trickle of clear, watery mucus. This is the classic picture of Lung Qi Deficiency, often accompanied by fatigue and a tendency to catch colds.
The Spleen is responsible for transforming food and fluids into usable energy. If the Spleen is weak, it cannot manage fluids properly, and the leftover moisture turns into a heavy, sticky substance called Dampness. This Dampness rises and settles in the nasal passages, creating thick, gluey mucus and a heavy, foggy sensation in the head. You might also notice a poor appetite, bloating, and a puffy tongue with teeth marks.
The Kidneys provide the body's fundamental warmth and govern fluid metabolism at the deepest level. When Kidney Yang is deficient, the internal thermostat runs low, and fluids are not properly warmed and moved. This leads to clear, watery nasal discharge that worsens at night, along with cold limbs, lower back soreness, and a deep, slow pulse. This pattern often makes a person feel chilled to the bone, even in mild weather.
In some cases, Heat can accumulate in the Lung channel - often from chronic inflammation, emotional stress, or dietary factors like too much spicy or greasy food. This Heat rises and disturbs the nasal passages, causing congestion with thick, yellow, sticky mucus, a burning sensation in the nose, and a dry throat.
Finally, when congestion lingers for months or years, the local flow of Qi and Blood can become sluggish and stuck, creating a stubborn, fixed blockage that feels as though the nose is physically obstructed - Blood Stagnation. Each of these patterns requires a different treatment strategy, which is why TCM's tailored approach can be so effective.

### Practitioner Diagnosis
A TCM practitioner first listens carefully to how the nose feels and what comes out of it. The quality of the discharge is the earliest clue: is it clear and watery, thick and sticky, or yellow and hot? The timing of the blockage - whether it is constant or worse at night - also helps separate one pattern from another.
If the person feels easily chilled, catches colds often, and has a pale face with a weak pulse, the picture points to Lung Qi Deficiency. Here the defensive Qi is too weak to protect the nose, so clear watery discharge and fatigue dominate. The tongue is pale with a thin white coat.
When the mucus is thick, heavy, and the person feels bloated with a poor appetite, the root is Spleen Deficiency with Dampness. The tongue is often puffy with teeth marks and a greasy white coat. The pulse feels slippery or soft, reflecting the dampness that the Spleen cannot transform.
Nighttime worsening of nasal congestion, together with cold limbs, lower back soreness, and a deep slow pulse, signals Kidney Yang Deficiency. The body lacks the warming fire to vaporize fluids, so the discharge stays clear and thin. The tongue is pale and swollen with a white coat.
In contrast, Lung Heat produces thick yellow or greenish mucus, a burning sensation inside the nose, and a dry throat. The tongue is red with a yellow coat and the pulse is rapid. This heat pattern often feels worse in warm rooms, unlike the cold patterns that improve with warmth.
Long-standing, stubborn nasal blockage that feels fixed and severe, sometimes with dark or scanty discharge, suggests Blood Stagnation. The tongue may show purplish spots, and the pulse can feel choppy. This pattern often develops after years of unresolved congestion and requires careful examination to confirm.

## TCM Patterns and Treatment

### Lung Qi Deficiency
*Pattern Link: [Detailed Pattern Page](/knowledge-base/patterns/lung-qi-deficiency)*

- **Clinical Signs & Symptoms**: Clear, thin, watery nasal discharge, Fatigue and shortness of breath, worse with exertion, Easily catches colds, sensitive to wind and drafts, Pale complexion and a low, weak voice
- **Worse with**: Overwork and pushing through fatigue, Cold, raw foods and icy beverages, Drafts, air conditioning, and cold weather, Prolonged stress and worry
- **Better with**: Rest and gentle naps, Warm, cooked meals and warm drinks, Keeping the neck and chest covered, Gentle exercise like walking or qigong
- **Why this happens (Mechanism)**:
  Your Lungs govern Qi and open into the nose. When Lung Qi is weak, the defensive shield (Wei Qi) that protects your nose and airways becomes thin, so every draft and cold breeze slips right in. The nose loses the strength to keep itself clear, leading to a chronic, stuffy sensation with a steady trickle of thin, watery discharge, and you feel tired because your whole body is running on low Qi.
- **Tongue & Pulse**: Tongue: pale, tender body with possible teeth marks and a thin white coating. Pulse: empty and weak, especially at the right Cun (Lung) position.
- **Triggers & Relief Explanation**: Lung Qi deficiency means the body's protective shield is thin. Overwork and stress further deplete that Qi, while cold and raw foods introduce a chill that the weak Lungs cannot push out. Rest and warmth conserve Qi and help the Lungs regain their strength; covering the neck and chest physically blocks the wind that easily invades. Gentle movement circulates Qi without exhausting it, and warm, cooked foods provide easily absorbed nourishment that the Spleen can turn into strong Lung Qi.
- **Diet & Lifestyle**: Eat warm, cooked foods like soups and stews, and add a little ginger or cinnamon. Avoid raw salads, iced drinks, and dairy, which can create dampness. Gentle daily movement like walking or qigong builds Qi without draining it. Keep a scarf handy - protecting your neck and upper back from wind is one of the simplest ways to stop a cold from settling in.

### Spleen Deficiency with Dampness
*Pattern Link: [Detailed Pattern Page](/knowledge-base/patterns/spleen-deficiency-with-dampness)*

- **Clinical Signs & Symptoms**: Thick, sticky, or white nasal discharge, Heavy, muzzy sensation in the head, Poor appetite and bloating after eating, Loose or unformed stools, Heaviness in the limbs and body, Fatigue and drowsiness, especially after meals, Sticky or greasy taste in the mouth
- **Worse with**: Dairy, cold, raw, or greasy foods, Damp or rainy weather, Overeating or irregular meals, Prolonged sitting or inactivity, Worry and mental overwork
- **Better with**: Warm, dry meals (soups, stews), Gentle daily exercise, Dry, airy environment, Resting after meals
- **Why this happens (Mechanism)**:
  Your Spleen is responsible for transforming food and fluids into usable energy and moisture. When the Spleen is weak, it cannot manage fluids properly, and the leftover moisture turns into a heavy, sticky substance called Dampness. This Dampness rises up and settles in the nasal passages, creating a persistent, boggy congestion that feels thick and hard to clear.
- **Tongue & Pulse**: Tongue: pale, puffy, and swollen with teeth marks on the edges, and a white, greasy coating. Pulse: soft, soggy, and slow (Ru Huan), often feeling weak at the right middle position.
- **Triggers & Relief Explanation**: A weak Spleen struggles to process rich, cold, or damp foods, which directly generates the Dampness that congests the nose. Warm, easily digested meals and gentle movement support the Spleen's function and help drain that excess moisture. Damp environments and mental strain further weaken the Spleen, so a dry, calm setting and regular rest give the body a chance to clear the phlegm and open the airways.
- **Diet & Lifestyle**: Focus on warm, cooked foods like soups, stews, and congees. Avoid cold, raw, and greasy foods, as well as dairy, which all create more Dampness. Gentle daily movement-like walking-helps the Spleen move fluids. Keep the living environment dry and avoid damp basements or humid climates when possible.

### Kidney Yang Deficiency
*Pattern Link: [Detailed Pattern Page](/knowledge-base/patterns/kidney-yang-deficiency)*

- **Clinical Signs & Symptoms**: Nasal congestion worse at night, Clear, watery nasal discharge, Cold limbs and aversion to cold, Soreness and cold pain in the lower back and knees, Frequent urination, especially at night
- **Worse with**: Cold, damp weather, Cold raw foods and iced drinks, Overwork and exhaustion, Nighttime and early morning, Excessive sexual activity
- **Better with**: Warmth and warm clothing, Moxibustion on the lower back, Warm soups and stews, Rest and adequate sleep, Gentle exercise like walking
- **Why this happens (Mechanism)**:
  The Kidneys are the root of the body's warming and fluid-transforming energy. When Kidney Yang is deficient, cold accumulates and fluids are not properly warmed and moved, leading to a buildup of clear, watery mucus that blocks the nose.
Because Yang is weakest during the night, the congestion tends to worsen then. The whole body feels cold, especially the lower back and knees, and the lack of warming fire fails to energize the Lungs to keep the nasal passages clear.
- **Tongue & Pulse**: Tongue: pale, puffy, tender body with teeth marks and a wet white coating. Pulse: deep, slow, and weak, especially at the Chi (third) position.
- **Triggers & Relief Explanation**: Kidney Yang is the body's internal fire. Cold weather and cold foods directly weaken this fire, allowing cold-damp to accumulate and rise to the nose. Overwork and excessive sexual activity further deplete Kidney essence and Yang, making symptoms worse at night when Yin energy dominates. Warmth, rest, and warm foods conserve and replenish Kidney Yang, helping to transform fluids and keep the nasal passages clear.
- **Diet & Lifestyle**: Keep your lower back and feet warm, and consider using a warm compress or moxibustion stick over the lower back area. Eat warm, cooked foods like stews, soups, and congees; add warming spices such as ginger, cinnamon, and black pepper. Avoid cold, raw foods, iced drinks, and excessive dairy, which can dampen the digestive fire and worsen congestion. Gentle daily movement like walking or tai chi helps circulate Yang Qi without exhausting it.

### Lung Heat
*Pattern Link: [Detailed Pattern Page](/knowledge-base/patterns/lung-heat)*

- **Clinical Signs & Symptoms**: Thick yellow or greenish nasal discharge, Burning sensation in the nose, Dry throat and thirst, Facial flushing or feeling of body heat, Worse in hot weather or after spicy food
- **Worse with**: Spicy or fried foods, Hot weather, Alcohol, Smoking, Stress and anger
- **Better with**: Cool fresh air, Drinking cool water or herbal teas, Rest and calm, Using a humidifier
- **Why this happens (Mechanism)**:
  The Lungs open into the nose. When Heat accumulates in the Lung channel, it rises and disturbs the nasal passages, leading to persistent congestion with thick, yellow, sticky mucus. The Heat also dries up body fluids, causing a burning sensation in the nose, dry throat, and thirst.
- **Tongue & Pulse**: Tongue: red body, especially at the tip and edges, with a yellow dry coating; possible prickly red spots on the front third. Pulse: rapid and slippery, or overflowing, particularly forceful at the right cun (Lung) position.
- **Triggers & Relief Explanation**: Lung Heat is a pattern of excess Heat in the Upper Burner. Spicy foods, alcohol, and hot environments add more Heat, aggravating the burning sensation and thick yellow discharge. Cooling foods and drinks, as well as rest, help clear the Heat and allow the Lung Qi to descend properly, opening the nasal passages. Stress and anger can stir up Heat, so staying calm is protective.
- **Diet & Lifestyle**: Avoid spicy, greasy, and fried foods that generate Heat. Drink cooling fluids like chrysanthemum tea or pear juice. Use a humidifier to keep air moist and avoid dry, smoky environments.

### Blood Stagnation
*Pattern Link: [Detailed Pattern Page](/knowledge-base/patterns/blood-stagnation)*

- **Clinical Signs & Symptoms**: Severe, fixed nasal congestion that feels stuck and does not shift, Congestion worsens noticeably at night or when lying down, Dark, scanty, or old-looking nasal discharge, Purplish or dusky tongue body, often with dark spots, Dark circles under the eyes or a dull, dusky complexion, Local distending pressure or mild stabbing pain inside the nose
- **Worse with**: Cold weather or air conditioning, Lying down for long periods, Stress and bottled-up frustration, Heavy, greasy, or cold foods, Sedentary lifestyle
- **Better with**: Warm compress on the nose, Gentle exercise and stretching, Spicy, aromatic foods, Deep breathing exercises, Acupressure around the nose
- **Why this happens (Mechanism)**:
  When nasal congestion lingers for months or years, the local flow of Qi and Blood in the nasal passages can become sluggish and eventually stuck. This creates a stubborn, fixed blockage that feels as though the nose is physically obstructed.
The stasis often develops on top of an earlier pattern-such as chronic Lung Qi Deficiency or lingering Cold-that was never fully resolved. Over time, the blood becomes dark and thick, and the congestion feels deep, heavy, and unyielding.
- **Tongue & Pulse**: Tongue: purple or dusky body with stasis spots, distended dark sublingual veins, thin white coat. Pulse: choppy (涩 sè) or wiry (弦 xián).
- **Triggers & Relief Explanation**: Blood Stagnation is like a frozen river-cold and stillness make it congeal further, while warmth and movement get it flowing again. Spicy foods and warm compresses gently heat the local area, and exercise or deep breathing pushes Qi and Blood through the stuck nasal passages. Stress tightens the Liver Qi, which can worsen stagnation, so calming the mind and staying active both help unblock the nose.
- **Diet & Lifestyle**: Choose warming, gently spicy foods like turmeric, ginger, garlic, and chives to help move Blood. Avoid icy drinks, raw foods, and heavy dairy, which can congeal Blood and worsen the stuck sensation.
Gentle daily movement-such as walking, yoga, or tai chi-keeps Qi and Blood circulating. Try a warm compress over the nose and cheeks in the evening to ease the night-time congestion.

## Frequently Asked Questions
### How does acupuncture help chronic rhinitis?
Acupuncture helps by stimulating specific points that open the nasal passages, reduce inflammation, and regulate the underlying organ systems involved. Local points on the face, like Yingxiang (LI-20), directly unblock the nose, while points on the hands, back, and legs strengthen the Lungs, Spleen, or Kidneys depending on your pattern. Many patients feel immediate, though temporary, relief after a session, with cumulative benefits over a course of treatment.

### What herbs are used for chronic nasal congestion?
The herbs chosen depend entirely on your TCM pattern. For Lung Qi Deficiency, formulas like Yu Ping Feng San (Jade Windscreen Powder) use Huang Qi (Astragalus) to strengthen the body's defenses. For Spleen Dampness, Shen Ling Bai Zhu San includes Bai Zhu and Fu Ling to dry dampness and support digestion. Kidney Yang Deficiency may be treated with Jin Gui Shen Qi Wan plus warming herbs like Ma Huang and Xi Xin. Lung Heat calls for Xin Yi San with cooling herbs like Huang Qin. Your practitioner will prescribe a customized formula based on your specific presentation.

### Can TCM cure chronic rhinitis permanently?
TCM aims to correct the underlying imbalance that causes chronic rhinitis, so many people experience long-term relief and fewer flare-ups. However, 'cure' depends on the pattern and lifestyle factors. For example, if the root is Spleen Deficiency with Dampness, dietary changes are essential to maintain the results. With consistent treatment and appropriate self-care, it's common for symptoms to resolve and not return, but it's not a guaranteed 'one-and-done' fix. A maintenance phase of herbs or occasional acupuncture may be recommended during seasonal changes.

### Is it safe to use Chinese herbs with my allergy medications?
In most cases, Chinese herbs can be used safely alongside conventional medications like nasal steroid sprays, antihistamines, and saline rinses. There are no commonly reported serious interactions. However, if you are taking blood-thinning medications, certain Blood-moving herbs (like Chuan Xiong or Hong Hua) may increase bleeding risk, so your TCM practitioner should adjust the formula accordingly. Always inform both your TCM practitioner and your medical doctor about all treatments you are using.

### Do I need to change my diet?
Diet plays a supportive role. In general, it's best to avoid cold, raw, and icy foods, which can weaken the Spleen and promote dampness. Dairy products, greasy foods, and excessive sweets tend to create phlegm and dampness, so they should be limited. If your pattern involves Heat, spicy and fried foods should also be minimized. Favor warm, cooked meals like soups and stews, and include foods that gently support the Lungs and Spleen, such as pear, white fungus, ginger, and congee. Your practitioner can give you specific advice based on your pattern.

### Can children receive TCM for chronic rhinitis?
Yes, TCM can be very helpful for children with chronic rhinitis. Pediatric acupuncture is often done with very fine needles and brief insertion, or non-needle techniques like acupressure and pediatric tuina massage may be used. Herbal formulas are adjusted to safe, age-appropriate dosages, often in powder or liquid form. Treating the root pattern early can prevent the condition from becoming entrenched and reduce the need for long-term medication.

## Modern Scientific Research
1. **Potential effectiveness of Chinese herbal medicine Yu ping feng san for adult allergic rhinitis: a systematic review and meta-analysis of randomized controlled trials** ([Link](https://doi.org/10.1155/2017/9407805))
   *Journal/Publication: Luo Q, Zhang CS, Yang L, et al. Potential effectiveness of Chinese herbal medicine Yu ping feng san for adult allergic rhinitis: a systematic review and meta-analysis of randomized controlled trials. Evid Based Complement Alternat Med. 2017;2017:9407805.*
   This meta-analysis of RCTs found that Yu Ping Feng San, used alone or as an adjunct to conventional medication, significantly improved nasal symptom scores and quality of life in adults with allergic rhinitis, with a favorable safety profile.

2. **Acupuncture for allergic rhinitis** ([Link](https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD007832.pub3))
   *Journal/Publication: Feng S, Han M, Fan Y, et al. Acupuncture for allergic rhinitis. Cochrane Database Syst Rev. 2015;(2):CD007832.*
   This Cochrane systematic review concluded that acupuncture led to a small improvement in nasal symptom scores and reduced the need for rescue medication compared with sham acupuncture, though the overall quality of evidence was low to moderate.
