## Conventional Medicine View
### Description
Hypothermia is defined as a core body temperature below 95°F (35°C). It occurs when the body loses heat faster than it can produce it, often due to prolonged cold exposure, immersion in cold water, or an underlying medical condition that impairs temperature regulation. Symptoms progress from shivering and cold, pale skin to confusion, slurred speech, and eventually loss of consciousness. Diagnosis is confirmed with a thermometer reading, and severity is classified as mild, moderate, or severe based on temperature and symptoms.

### Treatments
Treatment depends on severity. Mild hypothermia is managed with passive external rewarming - warm blankets, dry clothing, and warm drinks. Moderate to severe cases require active external rewarming (heating blankets, warm IV fluids) and, in critical cases, active internal rewarming methods such as warmed humidified oxygen or peritoneal lavage. Underlying causes, such as hypothyroidism or malnutrition, are addressed if present.

## Traditional Chinese Medicine View
### TCM Understanding
In TCM, body temperature is maintained by Yang Qi - the warm, active, metabolic force that fuels every function. The Kidneys store the deepest source of this fire, called the Gate of Life Fire, while the Spleen acts like a stove, transforming food into usable warmth and energy. When either of these organ systems is weakened, the body's thermostat drops. Chronic hypothermia or persistent coldness is almost always a sign that the internal furnace is burning too low.
External cold can also play a role, especially in acute cases. When cold penetrates deeply - as described in the Shang Han Lun - it can overwhelm the Kidney Yang and cause a life-threatening drop in temperature. This is the pattern of Lesser Yin Cold Transformation, where the body's core fire is so depleted that the limbs become icy, the mind grows dull, and the pulse nearly disappears. It's a medical emergency, but one that TCM has treated for centuries with powerful warming formulas like Si Ni Tang.
More commonly, hypothermia is a gradual, chronic issue. Spleen Yang Deficiency leads to poor digestion, bloating, loose stools, and a cold that feels centered in the abdomen and limbs. Kidney Yang Deficiency adds lower back pain, frequent nighttime urination, and a deep chill that radiates from the bones. When dampness accumulates because Yang is too weak to transform fluids, the cold becomes heavy and achy - Yang Deficiency with Cold-Damp. Each pattern has its own tongue and pulse signature, which guides the practitioner toward the right warming strategy.
This is why TCM never treats hypothermia with a one-size-fits-all approach. A person with Spleen Yang Deficiency needs their digestive fire stoked with Fu Zi Li Zhong Tang, while someone with Kidney Yang Deficiency needs the deeper warming of Jin Gui Shen Qi Wan. The goal is not just to raise the temperature temporarily, but to rebuild the body's own ability to generate and sustain heat.

### Practitioner Diagnosis
A TCM practitioner starts by asking how the cold feels, when it began, and what other signs appear. The quality and depth of the chill, along with appetite, digestion, urination, and energy levels, quickly point toward the organ system most affected and whether dampness or an acute external invasion is involved.
If the cold is deep and persistent, with achy low back, frequent nighttime urination, and a feeling that the body’s core cannot warm up, Kidney Yang Deficiency is the leading pattern. The tongue is often pale and swollen, and the pulse feels deep and weak, especially at the rear position where the Kidney is reflected.
When the chill is accompanied by poor appetite, loose stools, and fatigue that worsens after eating, Spleen Yang Deficiency is more likely. The tongue is pale with a thin white coating, and the pulse is weak and slow. This pattern often appears in milder or earlier stages, when the digestive fire is struggling.
If the cold comes with a sensation of heaviness, swelling in the legs, or a thick, greasy white tongue coating, the practitioner thinks of Yang Deficiency with Cold-Damp. Here the body’s warming power is too weak to transform fluids, so dampness accumulates. The pulse feels deep and slippery or slow, reflecting that internal stagnation.

## TCM Patterns and Treatment

### Kidney Yang Deficiency
*Pattern Link: [Detailed Pattern Page](/knowledge-base/patterns/kidney-yang-deficiency)*

- **Clinical Signs & Symptoms**: Feeling cold all over, worse in the lower body, Cold, aching lower back and knees, Frequent clear urination, especially at night, Low energy and a strong desire to sleep, Pale or dark, ashen complexion
- **Worse with**: Cold or damp weather, Cold or raw foods and drinks, Overwork or exhaustion, Excessive sexual activity, Prolonged standing
- **Better with**: Warmth (baths, heating pads, blankets), Warm meals and drinks, Rest and sleep, Moxibustion
- **Why this happens (Mechanism)**:
  Your Kidneys house the body's internal fire, called the Gate of Life Fire. This fire warms every part of you, from your core to your fingers and toes. When Kidney Yang is weak, the body's thermostat drops, and it simply cannot produce enough heat, leading to a persistent low body temperature and a deep, bone-chilling cold that no sweater can fix.
Without enough Yang, the lower back and knees-the 'palace' of the Kidneys-become cold, aching, and weak. The body also loses its ability to hold fluids, so you urinate frequently, especially at night, which further drains your warmth and energy.
- **Tongue & Pulse**: Tongue: pale, puffy, tender body with teeth marks and a wet white coating. Pulse: deep, slow, and weak, especially at the Chi (rear) position.
- **Triggers & Relief Explanation**: Kidney Yang is the body's pilot light. External warmth, hot foods, and rest all conserve and support that small flame, allowing it to gradually build. Cold, raw foods, and overwork, on the other hand, demand extra heat the body doesn't have, snuffing out the fire further. Moxibustion and warming spices directly add Yang energy to the Kidneys, helping to reset the body's thermostat from the inside out.
- **Diet & Lifestyle**: Eat warm, cooked foods like stews, soups, and congees. Use warming spices generously-ginger, cinnamon, cloves, and black pepper. Avoid all raw, cold, and frozen foods, as well as iced drinks, which directly extinguish the Kidney fire. Keep your lower back and feet warm at all times; a hot water bottle on the low back is excellent. Gentle daily movement like walking or tai chi helps circulate Yang without depleting it. If possible, practice moxibustion at home on the lower back and below the navel.

### Spleen Yang Deficiency
*Pattern Link: [Detailed Pattern Page](/knowledge-base/patterns/spleen-yang-deficiency)*

- **Clinical Signs & Symptoms**: Dull abdominal pain that feels better with warmth and pressure, Feeling cold easily, especially cold hands and feet, Poor appetite and bloating after eating, Loose stools with undigested food, Tiredness and heaviness in the limbs
- **Worse with**: Cold or raw foods and drinks, Overeating or heavy, greasy meals, Cold or damp weather, Overwork or exhaustion, Stress, worry, or fear
- **Better with**: Warm meals and drinks, Warmth (baths, heating pads, blankets), Gentle movement or walking, Rest and sleep, Warm, dry environment
- **Why this happens (Mechanism)**:
  Your Spleen is like a warm stove that turns food into Qi and body heat. When Spleen Yang is weak, that stove barely flickers-digestion slows, and the body fails to generate enough warmth. This internal cold shows up as a persistently low body temperature, cold hands and feet, and a deep-seated chill that no sweater can fix.
- **Tongue & Pulse**: Tongue: pale, puffy, and tender with tooth marks on the edges and a white slippery coating. Pulse: deep, slow, and weak, especially empty at the right Guan (Spleen) position.
- **Triggers & Relief Explanation**: Spleen Yang is your digestive fire-it thrives on warmth and gentle fuel. Cold foods and damp environments smother that fire, leaving you chilled and bloated. Warm meals, ginger, and abdominal heat directly feed the fire, while rest conserves the Yang needed to keep it burning. Stress knots the Spleen Qi, so calming the mind and avoiding overwork prevent the chill from deepening.
- **Diet & Lifestyle**: Eat warm, cooked meals like soups and stews, and add gentle spices such as ginger, cinnamon, and a pinch of black pepper. Avoid raw salads, icy drinks, and dairy, which douse the digestive fire. Keep your abdomen covered and use a hot water bottle over the navel area when resting. A daily short walk helps Qi move without depleting your reserves.

### Yang Deficiency with Cold-Damp
*Pattern Link: [Detailed Pattern Page](/knowledge-base/patterns/yang-deficiency-with-cold-damp)*

- **Clinical Signs & Symptoms**: Aching joints and muscles, Heavy, stiff sensation in the limbs, Coldness concentrated along the back, No thirst, or only desire for warm drinks, Loose stools and clear, copious urination, Pale, swollen tongue with teeth marks and a greasy white coating
- **Worse with**: Cold or damp weather, Cold or raw foods and drinks, Prolonged sitting or inactivity
- **Better with**: Warm, dry environment, Warm meals and drinks, Warmth (baths, heating pads, blankets), Gentle movement or walking
- **Why this happens (Mechanism)**:
  When the body's warming Yang Qi is weak, it cannot transform and move fluids properly. This causes cold-damp to accumulate inside, which further blocks Yang from reaching the surface and limbs. The result is a persistent low body temperature combined with a heavy, achy, swollen feeling - as if the body is cold from the inside out and waterlogged at the same time.
- **Tongue & Pulse**: Tongue: pale, swollen body with teeth marks, and a white, greasy, moist coating. Pulse: deep, slow, and minute; it may feel soggy if dampness is prominent.
- **Triggers & Relief Explanation**: Warmth helps Yang Qi circulate and evaporates the accumulated cold-damp, which is why warm drinks, dry heat, and moxibustion bring immediate relief. Cold and dampness are the very pathogens that created the problem - they further congeal fluids and block yang, worsening the heavy, achy coldness. Raw and sweet foods tax the spleen's ability to transform fluids, feeding more dampness and deepening the chill.
- **Diet & Lifestyle**: Eat warm, cooked foods and drink warm fluids. Use generous amounts of ginger, cinnamon, and black pepper. Avoid cold, raw, and damp-producing items like dairy, sugar, and raw vegetables. Keep your low back and abdomen warm with layers or a belly band. Gentle daily movement like walking helps yang circulate, but avoid sweating in cold environments. Self-moxibustion on the lower back and below the navel can be especially helpful.

### Empty-Cold
*Pattern Link: [Detailed Pattern Page](/knowledge-base/patterns/empty-cold)*

- **Clinical Signs & Symptoms**: Persistent feeling of cold, better with warmth, Cold hands and feet, Fatigue and listlessness, Pale, puffy face, Loose stools without urgency, Clear, abundant urination
- **Worse with**: Cold or raw foods and drinks, Cold or damp weather, Overwork or exhaustion, Skipping meals, Prolonged sitting or inactivity
- **Better with**: Warm meals and drinks, Gentle movement or walking, Warmth (baths, heating pads, blankets), Rest and sleep, Warm clothing and blankets
- **Why this happens (Mechanism)**:
  Empty-Cold happens when the body's Yang Qi-its internal furnace-is too weak to generate warmth. With insufficient Yang, the metabolic fire burns low, leading to a persistent drop in core temperature and a constant craving for heat. This is not an acute attack of cold, but a chronic, deep-seated chill from a lack of vital warmth, often seen when a general yang deficiency hasn't yet settled into a single organ.
- **Tongue & Pulse**: Tongue: pale, puffy, and tender with scalloped edges (teeth marks) and a thin white moist coating. Pulse: deep, slow, and weak, especially in the rear (Kidney) positions.
- **Triggers & Relief Explanation**: Empty-Cold is a state of deficient Yang, so anything cold further drains the body's already weak fire, deepening the chill. Warmth, both from food and external sources, directly supplements Yang and eases the internal cold. Rest conserves Qi, while gentle movement stimulates Yang circulation without depleting reserves. Skipping meals robs the Spleen of the fuel it needs to produce Qi and warmth, worsening fatigue and cold.
- **Diet & Lifestyle**: Eat warm, cooked foods like soups, stews, and congees. Add gentle warming spices such as ginger, cinnamon, and black pepper to meals. Avoid raw, cold, and iced foods. Keep the body warm with layers and use a hot water bottle on the abdomen. Gentle daily movement like walking or tai chi helps circulate Yang without exhausting it.

### Lesser Yin Cold Transformation
*Pattern Link: [Detailed Pattern Page](/knowledge-base/patterns/lesser-yin-cold-transformation)*

- **Clinical Signs & Symptoms**: Constant drowsiness or desire to sleep, Ice-cold hands and feet, Diarrhoea with undigested food, No thirst, preference for warm drinks, Curling up in bed, Severe chills without fever
- **Worse with**: Cold or damp weather, Cold or raw foods and drinks, Overwork or exhaustion, Stress, worry, or fear
- **Better with**: Warmth (baths, heating pads, blankets), Warm meals and drinks, Rest and sleep, Moxibustion
- **Why this happens (Mechanism)**:
  In the Shang Han Lun, this pattern develops when external cold penetrates deep into the body, overwhelming the Kidney Yang-the root of all warmth. The body's core fire is so depleted that it can no longer warm the limbs, transform fluids, or keep the mind alert, leading to a profound drop in body temperature.
Unlike milder yang deficiencies, the cold here sinks into the innermost layer, causing severe chills, ice-cold extremities, and a constant desire to sleep as the body tries to conserve its last sparks of yang.
- **Tongue & Pulse**: Tongue: Pale, puffy, tender with tooth marks, white slippery coating, excessively moist. Pulse: Fine (细 xi), faint (微 wei), deep (沉 chen), and slow (迟 chi), especially weak at the chi (Kidney) position.
- **Triggers & Relief Explanation**: This pattern is a profound depletion of the body's core Yang, leaving the interior cold and unable to generate warmth. External cold further drains the remaining Yang, while rest and warmth conserve it. Moxibustion directly adds warmth and stimulates Yang. Raw and cold foods require Yang to digest, which the body cannot spare, so warm easy-to-digest foods preserve the little fire that remains.
- **Diet & Lifestyle**: Eat warm, cooked, easily digestible foods like congee, soups, and stews. Avoid all raw, cold, or chilled foods and drinks. Keep the body warm, especially the lower back and abdomen. Gentle moxibustion on the lower abdomen and back can be done daily to support Yang.

### Collapse of Yang
*Pattern Link: [Detailed Pattern Page](/knowledge-base/patterns/collapse-of-yang)*

- **Clinical Signs & Symptoms**: Loss of consciousness or extreme drowsiness, Ice-cold hands and feet that feel cold past the elbows and knees, Profuse cold sweating, Ashen-white or greyish complexion, Blue or purple lips and fingernails, Watery diarrhea with undigested food
- **Worse with**: Cold or damp weather, Cold or raw foods and drinks, Overwork or exhaustion
- **Better with**: Warmth (baths, heating pads, blankets), Warm meals and drinks, Moxibustion
- **Why this happens (Mechanism)**:
  Yang Qi is the body's vital warmth and life force. When it collapses - often from extreme cold exposure or severe underlying deficiency - the body loses its ability to generate heat entirely. Core temperature plummets, consciousness fades, and the pulse becomes barely perceptible as the life force retreats inward.
- **Tongue & Pulse**: Tongue: pale, moist, flaccid body with a thin white slippery coating. Pulse: minute (微, wēi), on the verge of disappearing (欲绝, yù jué) - extremely faint and thread-like.
- **Triggers & Relief Explanation**: Collapse of Yang means the body's vital fire is nearly extinguished. Any cold or drain on energy pushes it further into danger, while warmth - both external and from warming foods and moxibustion - helps reignite the last spark of Yang and stabilize the life force.
- **Diet & Lifestyle**: This is a medical emergency that requires immediate professional care and external warming. Once stabilized, offer small sips of warm ginger tea or congee. Avoid all cold, raw, or hard-to-digest foods.

## Frequently Asked Questions
### Can TCM actually raise my body temperature?
Yes. By strengthening the Kidney and Spleen Yang - the body's internal sources of warmth - TCM aims to restore your natural thermostat, not just provide temporary external heat. Many patients notice their hands and feet becoming warmer within a few weeks of starting herbs and moxibustion.

### How long does it take to feel warmer?
Mild cases of Spleen Yang Deficiency often respond within 2-4 weeks of consistent treatment. Deeper Kidney Yang Deficiency or patterns with Cold-Damp may take 2-3 months to see significant improvement. Chronic, long-standing coldness can require 6 months or more to rebuild the foundational Yang.

### Is moxibustion safe?
When performed by a trained practitioner, moxibustion - the burning of dried mugwort near specific acupuncture points - is very safe and deeply warming. It is one of the most effective TCM tools for driving out cold and restoring Yang. Some patients may experience mild skin warmth or redness, which fades quickly. It should not be used on open wounds or in cases of high fever.

### Can I take warming herbs if I have high blood pressure?
Some warming herbs, particularly those containing aconite (Fu Zi) or cinnamon (Rou Gui), can raise blood pressure or heart rate. If you have hypertension or any cardiovascular condition, it is essential to inform your TCM practitioner fully. They will adjust the formula accordingly and may choose gentler warming herbs. Never self-prescribe strong warming formulas.

### What foods should I eat to help warm up?
Favor warm, cooked foods: soups, stews, porridges, and steamed vegetables. Ginger, cinnamon, cloves, lamb, chicken, walnuts, and black beans are especially warming. Avoid raw salads, iced drinks, cold smoothies, and excessive dairy, which can further cool the digestive fire. Eat meals warm and at regular times to support Spleen Yang.

### Is this the same as just being a 'cold-natured' person?
In TCM, a person's constitution can lean toward cold, but that doesn't mean it's normal or unchangeable. If you're constantly cold, have low energy, and crave warmth, it's likely a Yang deficiency pattern that can be treated. A TCM diagnosis will pinpoint which organ system is most involved and guide treatment to shift your baseline toward warmth over time.

### Can children be treated for low body temperature?
Yes. Children can also have Spleen or Kidney Yang Deficiency, leading to cold hands and feet, poor appetite, and frequent urination. Pediatric TCM uses gentler herbal dosages and often relies on pediatric tuina (massage) and moxibustion. Always consult a practitioner experienced in treating children.
