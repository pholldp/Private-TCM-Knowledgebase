## Conventional Medicine View
### Description
Long COVID, also called Post-COVID-19 Condition, refers to a wide range of symptoms that persist for weeks or months after the acute phase of COVID-19 has passed. Common complaints include crushing fatigue, shortness of breath, brain fog, chest pain, and palpitations, though over 200 different symptoms have been reported. Diagnosis is clinical - there is no single lab test - and it is based on a history of probable or confirmed SARS-CoV-2 infection and symptoms that last at least two months and cannot be explained by another condition.
The exact mechanisms are still being studied, but likely involve lingering inflammation, immune dysregulation, microclots, and damage to the autonomic nervous system. Because the presentation varies so widely from person to person, conventional management is largely supportive and symptom-focused.

### Treatments
Conventional care for Long COVID is multidisciplinary and aims to manage individual symptoms. This may include pulmonary rehabilitation for breathlessness, graded exercise or pacing strategies for fatigue, cognitive rehabilitation for brain fog, and medications for pain, sleep, or mood. Some clinics offer specialized Long COVID programs that coordinate care across cardiology, pulmonology, neurology, and physical therapy. However, no single drug or therapy has been approved to treat the condition as a whole.

## Traditional Chinese Medicine View
### TCM Understanding
TCM sees Long COVID as a complex injury left behind by a fierce external pathogen. During the acute infection, the virus acts like a fire that burns through the body's Qi and Yin fluids, while also generating dampness and phlegm that can linger long after the fever breaks. The Lungs are the first and most obvious target - they govern breathing and are the delicate organ most exposed to external invasion - so persistent cough, breathlessness, and voice weakness are common.
But the damage rarely stops there. The Spleen, which transforms food into Qi and manages fluids, is often weakened by both the virus and the dampness it creates, leading to fatigue, brain fog, poor appetite, and loose stools. When the illness digs deeper, it can exhaust the Kidney's Yang, the body's pilot light, causing a deep, bone-cold exhaustion, lower back ache, and intolerance to cold. And because Qi moves Blood, a prolonged struggle can leave Blood stagnant in the chest, producing palpitations and fixed, stabbing pain.
This is why two people with Long COVID can feel completely different - and why TCM doesn't treat them the same way. One person's pattern may be Qi and Yin Deficiency, with a red, dry tongue and a thready rapid pulse, calling for cooling, moistening herbs. Another may have Spleen and Lung Qi Deficiency, with a pale, puffy tongue and a weak pulse, needing warming, strengthening formulas. A third may have Liver Qi Stagnation generating heat on top of Spleen deficiency, with irritability and a bitter taste. Recognizing the pattern is the first and most important step.

### Practitioner Diagnosis
A TCM practitioner begins by listening to your story and examining your tongue and pulse. The quality of your fatigue, breath, and digestion are the first clues. They will ask whether your shortness of breath feels worse with exertion or is accompanied by chest tightness, and whether your appetite is poor or you feel bloated after eating.
If you feel drained, dry-mouthed, and sometimes flushed, with a red tongue that has little coating, the picture points toward Qi and Yin Deficiency. In contrast, Spleen and Lung Qi Deficiency brings more dampness: a pale, swollen tongue with teeth marks, phlegm, and digestive sluggishness like loose stools or bloating.
When deeper reserves are hit, Lung and Kidney Yang Deficiency shows up as coldness, a sore lower back, weak knees, and frequent nighttime urination. A pale, puffy tongue and a deep, weak pulse confirm it. If instead chest pain and palpitations dominate, and the tongue looks dark with tiny purple spots, the diagnosis shifts to Qi and Blood Stagnation.
Two other patterns often overlap: Phlegm clogging the Lungs with Qi Stagnation creates a tight chest and a cough with thick, sticky phlegm, while Liver Qi Stagnation with Spleen Qi Deficiency that transforms into Heat features emotional distress, rib-side tension, and poor appetite, often with a wiry-thin pulse and some mild heat signs.

## TCM Patterns and Treatment

### Qi and Yin Deficiency
*Pattern Link: [Detailed Pattern Page](/knowledge-base/patterns/qi-and-yin-deficiency)*

- **Clinical Signs & Symptoms**: Profound fatigue that doesn&#x27;t improve with rest, Dry mouth and throat with little desire to drink, Night sweats or spontaneous daytime sweating, Warm sensation in palms, soles, and chest, Shortness of breath worse with exertion, Palpitations or a racing heart
- **Worse with**: Overexertion or overwork, Spicy, fried, or drying foods, Dry, heated indoor air, Emotional stress and frustration, Insufficient sleep
- **Better with**: Rest and sleep, Gentle movement or walking, Cool, humidified air, Nourishing, moistening foods like pear and congee
- **Why this happens (Mechanism)**:
  The intense fever and inflammation of acute COVID-19 act like a fire that burns through the body's Qi and Yin fluids. The Lungs are especially vulnerable, leaving them unable to generate enough Qi to sustain energy and breath. Meanwhile, the loss of cooling, moistening Yin allows empty heat to rise, causing dry mouth, night sweats, and that warm sensation in the palms and chest.
- **Tongue & Pulse**: Tongue: red body with little or no coating, dry surface, possibly cracked. Pulse: fine (thready), weak, and often rapid.
- **Triggers & Relief Explanation**: Qi and Yin are both depleted after the viral assault, so anything that further consumes them-like overworking, stress, or spicy foods that generate heat-deepens the exhaustion and dryness. Rest and nourishing, moistening foods directly replenish what was lost, while gentle movement keeps Qi flowing without draining it. A cool, humid environment counteracts the empty heat that rises when Yin is too thin to anchor it.
- **Diet & Lifestyle**: Eat small, frequent meals of easily digestible, moistening foods such as rice congee with pear or lily bulb. Sip warm water throughout the day and avoid coffee, alcohol, and spicy or fried foods. Prioritize rest and gentle movement like slow walking or Tai Chi-never push to the point of breathlessness. A cool-mist humidifier in the bedroom can help soothe a dry throat at night.

### Spleen and Lung Qi Deficiency
*Pattern Link: [Detailed Pattern Page](/knowledge-base/patterns/spleen-and-lung-qi-deficiency)*

- **Clinical Signs & Symptoms**: Chronic cough with thin, watery phlegm, Shortness of breath that worsens with exertion, Poor appetite and abdominal bloating after eating, Loose stools, Profound fatigue and general weakness
- **Worse with**: Overexertion or overwork, Raw or cold foods, Dairy and greasy foods, Cold, damp weather, Emotional stress and frustration
- **Better with**: Warm, cooked foods, Gentle movement or walking, Rest and sleep, Warmth on the chest
- **Why this happens (Mechanism)**:
  COVID-19 first attacks the Lung, draining its Qi and leaving behind a weak cough and breathlessness. At the same time, the virus and lingering dampness damage the Spleen's ability to transform food and fluids. The result is poor appetite, bloating, and loose stools, while the Spleen's unused fluids turn into phlegm that clogs the Lungs and makes the cough wet and persistent.
Because Qi is the body's engine, this dual depletion of Lung and Spleen Qi causes deep fatigue, a weak voice, spontaneous sweating, and a tendency to catch every bug going around.
- **Tongue & Pulse**: Tongue: Pale, puffy, tender body with teeth marks; thin white slippery coating. Pulse: Weak and forceless, especially at the right Cun (Lung) and Guan (Spleen) positions.
- **Triggers & Relief Explanation**: Overexertion burns through the already depleted Qi, leaving you more exhausted. Raw and cold foods injure the Spleen's digestive fire, creating dampness that turns into phlegm and worsens bloating and loose stools. Dairy and greasy foods are phlegm-producing in TCM and directly clog the Lungs. In contrast, warm cooked meals support the Spleen's transformation, rest conserves Qi, and gentle movement encourages Qi flow without further depletion. Warmth helps dispel the cold-dampness that underlies this pattern.
- **Diet & Lifestyle**: Eat warm, cooked, easily digestible foods like congee, soups, and steamed vegetables. Avoid raw, cold, greasy, and dairy-rich foods that create dampness. Gentle daily movement like walking or qi gong helps Qi flow without draining it. Prioritize rest and keep the chest and neck covered in cool weather.

### Lung and Kidney Yang Deficiency
*Pattern Link: [Detailed Pattern Page](/knowledge-base/patterns/lung-and-kidney-yang-deficiency)*

- **Clinical Signs & Symptoms**: Feeling cold all over, especially back and limbs, Lower back soreness and weakness, Cough with thin, watery or frothy sputum, Frequent urination at night, Puffiness of face and limbs
- **Worse with**: Cold exposure, Overexertion or overwork, Raw or cold foods, Damp or humid environments, Emotional stress and frustration
- **Better with**: Rest and sleep, Warm, cooked foods, Moxibustion on lower back, Gentle movement or walking, Staying warm
- **Why this happens (Mechanism)**:
  After the acute phase of COVID-19, the virus and its treatments can exhaust the body's deep Yang, particularly in the Lung and Kidney. The Lungs govern breathing and the Kidneys grasp the Qi, anchoring it downward. When their Yang is depleted, the body fails to warm itself, fluids accumulate into thin watery phlegm, and the breath becomes short and shallow-especially with any effort. This cold-deficiency leaves you feeling chilled to the bone, with a weak lower back and frequent nighttime urination.
- **Tongue & Pulse**: Tongue: pale, puffy, and tender with tooth marks; white slippery coating. Pulse: deep, weak, and slow; the right Cun (Lung) position is especially weak, and both Chi (Kidney) positions are faint.
- **Triggers & Relief Explanation**: Yang is the body's warming, driving force. Cold and damp directly oppose it, so they worsen symptoms. Rest and warmth conserve Yang, while gentle movement stimulates its flow without depleting it. Moxibustion delivers deep, penetrating heat to rekindle the Kidney fire, which is the root of all Yang.
- **Diet & Lifestyle**: Eat warm, cooked foods like soups and stews with ginger, cinnamon, and small amounts of lamb. Avoid cold drinks, raw salads, and dairy. Keep your lower back and feet warm; moxibustion on the lower back and abdomen can be especially helpful. Gentle movement like tai chi or walking builds Yang without exhausting it.

### Qi And Blood Stagnation
*Pattern Link: [Detailed Pattern Page](/knowledge-base/patterns/qi-and-blood-stagnation)*

- **Clinical Signs & Symptoms**: Fixed, stabbing chest pain, Chest tightness and oppression, Palpitations, Irritability or mood swings, Dark or purple lips and complexion, Pain worsens with pressure
- **Worse with**: Emotional stress and frustration, Sedentary lifestyle, Cold, heavy, or greasy foods, Overexertion or overwork
- **Better with**: Gentle movement or walking, Warmth on the chest, Deep breathing and relaxation, Foods like hawthorn and turmeric
- **Why this happens (Mechanism)**:
  After the acute phase of COVID-19, lingering pathogens can damage the body's Qi. When Qi becomes depleted or stagnant, it fails to propel Blood through the vessels, leading to Blood stasis. This stagnation settles in the chest and Heart, causing fixed, stabbing pain, chest oppression, and palpitations that are characteristic of Long COVID.
- **Tongue & Pulse**: Tongue: dark purple body with stasis spots, distended sublingual veins, thin white coat. Pulse: wiry (弦 xián) and choppy (涩 sè), like a taut guitar string that feels rough and uneven.
- **Triggers & Relief Explanation**: Stress and frustration cause Qi to stagnate, which in turn worsens Blood stasis and chest pain. Gentle movement and deep breathing actively promote the smooth flow of Qi and Blood, directly countering the stagnation. Cold and heavy foods congeal Blood and slow circulation, while warming foods and external warmth help dissolve stasis and ease pain.
- **Diet & Lifestyle**: Prioritize gentle, regular movement like walking or tai chi to keep Qi and Blood flowing. Include warming, blood-moving foods such as turmeric, hawthorn, and onions. Avoid heavy, greasy meals and cold foods that can congeal Blood. Emotional calm and deep breathing exercises help prevent stagnation from building up.

### Phlegm clogging the Lungs with Qi Stagnation
*Pattern Link: [Detailed Pattern Page](/knowledge-base/patterns/phlegm-clogging-the-lungs-with-qi-stagnation)*

- **Clinical Signs & Symptoms**: Persistent productive cough with copious white phlegm, Chest stuffiness and a sensation of heaviness, Wheezing or rattling sound in the throat, Poor appetite, bloating, and nausea, Fatigue and a heavy sensation in the body
- **Worse with**: Dairy, cold, and raw foods, Greasy or fried foods, Damp or humid environments, Emotional stress and frustration, Lying down soon after eating
- **Better with**: Warm, cooked foods, Gentle movement or walking, Dry, warm weather, Sipping warm ginger tea
- **Why this happens (Mechanism)**:
  After COVID-19, the Lungs often struggle to descend and disperse Qi properly. When Lung Qi stagnates, the body's fluids are not moved and transformed correctly, so they collect and thicken into phlegm. This phlegm then clogs the airways, creating a heavy, blocked sensation in the chest, a rattling cough, and the need to bring up lots of white phlegm.
- **Tongue & Pulse**: Tongue: pale, puffy body with teeth marks and a thick, white, greasy coating that is excessively moist. Pulse: slippery (滑 huá) and wiry (弦 xián), especially at the right Guan position.
- **Triggers & Relief Explanation**: Phlegm is a Yin pathogen that congeals when cold and dampness accumulate. Warm, dry foods and environments help the body transform and thin out phlegm, while cold, raw, and greasy foods add more dampness and make phlegm thicker. Gentle movement activates Qi, which is essential for moving fluids and preventing stagnation. Stress tightens the chest and worsens Qi stagnation, so staying calm and moving regularly keeps the Lungs clear.
- **Diet & Lifestyle**: Avoid dairy, cold drinks, raw salads, and greasy or fried foods, which all encourage phlegm formation. Focus on warm, cooked meals like soups and stews with a little ginger or pepper. Gentle daily movement, such as walking or stretching, helps keep Qi and fluids moving so phlegm does not settle.

### Liver Qi Stagnation with Spleen Qi Deficiency that transforms into Heat
*Pattern Link: [Detailed Pattern Page](/knowledge-base/patterns/liver-qi-stagnation-with-spleen-qi-deficiency-that-transforms-into-heat)*

- **Clinical Signs & Symptoms**: Distending or wandering rib-side pain, Bloating after eating and loose stools, Irritability and emotional tension, Bitter taste and dry mouth, Fatigue and low energy
- **Worse with**: Emotional stress and frustration, Greasy or fried foods, Raw or cold foods, Overexertion or overwork, Alcohol
- **Better with**: Warm, cooked foods, Gentle movement or walking, Deep breathing and relaxation, Regular meal times
- **Why this happens (Mechanism)**:
  The prolonged stress and uncertainty of Long COVID can cause the Liver Qi to stagnate. When Liver Qi is stuck, it overacts on the Spleen, weakening digestion and causing bloating, poor appetite, and loose stools. Over time, the stagnant Qi can generate Heat, which rises to cause a bitter taste in the mouth, irritability, and a sensation of heat in the chest or face.
- **Tongue & Pulse**: Tongue: red body with redder sides, teeth marks, thin to moderately thick yellow and slightly greasy coating. Pulse: wiry and rapid, especially in the left Guan position.
- **Triggers & Relief Explanation**: Liver Qi stagnation is directly aggravated by emotional stress and frustration, so relaxation and stress management are essential. Greasy, spicy, and raw cold foods further weaken the Spleen and generate Heat, while warm, cooked meals support Spleen Qi and prevent stagnation. Gentle movement helps circulate Qi, and adequate rest allows the Liver to store blood and the Spleen to recover.
- **Diet & Lifestyle**: Eat regular, warm, cooked meals that are easy to digest, like soups and congees. Avoid raw, cold, greasy, and spicy foods, which tax the Spleen and add Heat. Gentle daily movement such as walking, tai chi, or yoga helps keep Liver Qi flowing. Prioritize stress management and relaxation to prevent Qi stagnation.

## Frequently Asked Questions
### Can acupuncture and herbs really help with Long COVID fatigue?
Yes, TCM directly addresses the root cause of fatigue by replenishing Qi and Yin or warming Yang, rather than just masking symptoms. Most patients report a noticeable increase in energy within a few weeks of starting treatment. The key is matching the right formula to your specific pattern - for example, Sheng Mai San for Qi and Yin Deficiency or Liu Jun Zi Tang for Spleen-Lung Qi deficiency.

### How long will I need treatment?
This depends on your pattern and how long you've been unwell. Acute patterns may resolve in a month; chronic deficiency patterns often require 2-3 months of consistent herbs and weekly acupuncture. We typically reassess after 4-6 weeks and adjust the plan based on changes in your tongue, pulse, and symptoms.

### Is it safe to take Chinese herbs with my other medications?
In most cases, yes, but you must tell your TCM practitioner about all medications you are taking. Some herbs, like Dan Shen or Chuan Xiong, can thin the blood and may interact with anticoagulants. We'll work with your doctor to ensure safety and will never ask you to stop prescribed medications abruptly.

### What diet should I follow while recovering from Long COVID?
Focus on warm, cooked, easy-to-digest foods. Avoid cold, raw, and greasy foods that weaken digestion. For dry cough or dry mouth, pears and congee help. For phlegm, add ginger. Your practitioner will give you specific advice based on your pattern, but these general principles apply to everyone.

### Can TCM help with brain fog and loss of smell?
Yes. Brain fog often relates to Spleen Qi deficiency or Phlegm misting the mind. Acupuncture points like Fenglong ST-40 and herbs like Ban Xia can clear phlegm. Loss of smell is often treated with local points like Yingxiang LI-20 and herbs that open the orifices, alongside the core pattern treatment.

### Will my symptoms return after I stop treatment?
TCM aims to correct the underlying imbalance, not just suppress symptoms. While some people may need occasional maintenance, most find lasting improvement. We'll teach you lifestyle habits to sustain your gains and can provide a gentle maintenance formula if needed.

### I have both fatigue and anxiety - can TCM treat both?
Absolutely. TCM sees the mind and body as connected. For example, Liver Qi Stagnation with Spleen Qi deficiency can cause both irritability and digestive fatigue. We treat the pattern, not individual symptoms, so both often improve together as the underlying disharmony resolves.
