## Conventional Medicine View
### Description
In Western medicine, a delusion is a fixed false belief that is firmly held despite clear evidence to the contrary. Delusions are a core symptom of psychotic disorders such as schizophrenia, schizoaffective disorder, and bipolar disorder with psychotic features, but they can also appear in severe depression, dementia, or delirium. Diagnosis is made through clinical interview, mental status examination, and sometimes brain imaging or blood tests to rule out organic causes like tumors or infections.

### Treatments
Standard treatment usually involves antipsychotic medications, sometimes combined with mood stabilizers or antidepressants. Cognitive behavioral therapy for psychosis (CBTp) can help patients challenge and manage delusional beliefs. In severe cases, hospitalization may be necessary to ensure safety and stabilize symptoms.

## Traditional Chinese Medicine View
### TCM Understanding
TCM understands delusions primarily through the Heart, which houses the Mind (Shen). When the Heart is disturbed by pathogenic factors like Fire, Phlegm, or Blood Stagnation, the Shen becomes unsettled and can generate false beliefs. The Liver also plays a key role: long-standing emotional frustration or resentment can cause Liver Qi to stagnate and transform into Fire, which then rises to harass the Heart and agitate the mind.
Other organ systems can be involved as well. A weak Spleen fails to transform fluids, leading to Dampness and Phlegm that cloud the Heart's orifices, producing a heavy, foggy confusion. Deficiency of Kidney Yin allows empty heat to flare upward, disturbing the Shen and causing restless delusions that are often worse at night. This is why two people with the same Western diagnosis may need completely different treatments - the root imbalance is not the same.
In TCM, the quality of the delusion often hints at the pattern. Fixed, rigid delusions with a dark tongue suggest Blood Stagnation; agitated, fiery delusions with a red tongue point to Heart or Liver Fire; and delusions accompanied by mental fogginess and a swollen, greasy-coated tongue indicate Phlegm-Dampness. By reading these signs, a TCM practitioner can select a formula and acupuncture points that address the specific disharmony behind the false belief.

### Practitioner Diagnosis
A TCM practitioner starts by listening carefully to the nature of the delusions and the person’s overall emotional state. The quality of the false belief, what makes it worse, and the accompanying physical signs point to which pattern is dominant.
If the delusions are fixed, rigid, and accompanied by a stabbing sensation in the chest or a dark, purplish complexion, the practitioner suspects Heart Blood Stagnation. The tongue often looks dark‑purple with possible stasis spots, and the pulse feels choppy or wiry. This pattern arises when blood flow in the Heart channel is sluggish, failing to nourish the mind (Shen).
When delusions come with marked agitation, a racing mind, insomnia, and a flushed face, the pattern is likely Heart Fire blazing. The tongue is red with a yellow coating, and the pulse is rapid.
If the person also has a history of long‑held frustration, irritability, and a wiry pulse, the Fire may be rising from a stagnant Liver, which then disturbs the Heart. In both cases, Heat agitates the Spirit.
A different picture emerges when delusions are accompanied by mental fogginess, a heavy sensation in the head, and a swollen tongue with a greasy white coating. This points to Spleen Deficiency with Dampness, where Phlegm mists the mind.
If instead the person feels heat in the palms and soles, night sweats, and a dry mouth, with a red tongue lacking a coating, it suggests Kidney Yin Deficiency with Empty‑Heat blazing, where insufficient cooling Yin allows heat to unsettle the mind.

## TCM Patterns and Treatment

### Heart Blood Stagnation
*Pattern Link: [Detailed Pattern Page](/knowledge-base/patterns/heart-blood-stagnation)*

- **Clinical Signs & Symptoms**: Stabbing chest pain in a fixed location, Purple or dark lips and nail beds, Dark purple tongue with stasis spots, Cold hands, Palpitations with a choppy or knotted pulse, Delusions that are rigid and unchanging
- **Worse with**: Cold weather or cold drinks, Emotional stress and frustration, Sedentary lifestyle, Greasy, heavy, or cold foods
- **Better with**: Warmth and gentle heat, Gentle exercise (walking, tai chi), Warm, easily digested meals, Stress reduction and relaxation
- **Why this happens (Mechanism)**:
  The Heart houses the Mind (Shen), and its vessels must be clear for the Shen to stay grounded and lucid. When Blood stagnates in the Heart, the vessels become blocked, cutting off nourishment to the mind. This leads to rigid, fixed delusions that feel absolutely real to the person, often accompanied by a deep sense of oppression or pain in the chest.
- **Tongue & Pulse**: Tongue: dark purple or purple-red body with stasis spots, distended sublingual veins, thin white coating. Pulse: choppy (涩, Sè) or knotted (结, Jié), reflecting obstructed flow.
- **Triggers & Relief Explanation**: Blood stasis is like a frozen river; cold constricts and slows flow further, while warmth and movement promote circulation. Emotional stress causes Qi to stagnate, which then worsens blood stasis, so calming the mind helps free the flow. Exercise and warm foods gently invigorate Blood and dispel stasis, easing the fixed delusions that arise when the Heart's vessels are blocked.
- **Diet & Lifestyle**: Eat warm, cooked foods like soups and stews; include moderate amounts of blood-moving spices such as turmeric and cinnamon. Avoid cold, raw foods and iced drinks. Gentle daily movement like walking or tai chi helps keep Qi and Blood circulating. Practice stress-reducing activities to prevent Qi stagnation.

### Heart Fire blazing
*Pattern Link: [Detailed Pattern Page](/knowledge-base/patterns/heart-fire-blazing)*

- **Clinical Signs & Symptoms**: Agitation and mental restlessness, Red, flushed face and feeling of heat, Bitter taste in mouth, thirst for cold drinks, Insomnia with vivid, disturbing dreams, Palpitations, sensation of heat in chest, Mouth or tongue ulcers with red edges
- **Worse with**: Anger or frustration, Spicy, fried, or heating foods, Alcohol or coffee, Overwork and late nights, Hot weather or saunas
- **Better with**: Cool environment, Quiet, dark room, Bitter foods (e.g., bitter melon), Cold water or herbal teas, Meditation or slow breathing
- **Why this happens (Mechanism)**:
  The Heart houses the Mind (Shen). When excess Fire blazes in the Heart, it agitates the Shen like a flame under a pot, causing mental restlessness and delusional thoughts. The heat can also consume Heart Yin, leaving the mind unmoored and prone to false beliefs, often accompanied by irritability and insomnia.
- **Tongue & Pulse**: Tongue: red body, tip significantly redder with swollen red points, yellow dry coating, possible midline crack. Pulse: rapid, forceful, overflowing (Hong) especially at left cun, full (Shi), may be hasty (Cu).
- **Triggers & Relief Explanation**: Heart Fire blazing is an excess heat condition that agitates the mind. Cooling the body and environment, eating bitter and cold foods, and practicing stillness all help drain the fire and settle the Shen. Conversely, anger, stimulants, and overwork stoke the fire, making the mind more restless and delusions worse.
- **Diet & Lifestyle**: Eat cooling, light foods: cucumber, watermelon, mung bean soup, and bitter greens like dandelion. Avoid alcohol, coffee, spicy and fried foods, which add heat. Practice calming activities like meditation or gentle yoga, especially before bed, to settle the mind.

### Stagnant Liver Qi turning into Fire
*Pattern Link: [Detailed Pattern Page](/knowledge-base/patterns/stagnant-liver-qi-turning-into-fire)*

- **Clinical Signs & Symptoms**: Irritability and easily provoked anger, Rib-side or chest distension and pain, Bitter taste in the mouth, Throbbing headache or red eyes, Restless sleep with vivid or disturbing dreams
- **Worse with**: Anger or frustration, Alcohol and spicy food, Overwork and late nights, Bottling up emotions
- **Better with**: Cooling foods (cucumber, celery), Stress reduction and relaxation, Gentle exercise (walking, tai chi), Chrysanthemum tea
- **Why this happens (Mechanism)**:
  When frustration and resentment simmer for a long time, the Liver's smooth flow of Qi gets stuck. This stagnant Qi eventually turns into heat, like friction creating fire. The Liver Fire then blazes upward, disturbing the Heart and its housing of the mind (Shen), which is why delusional thoughts arise. The person often feels a surge of irritability and anger alongside the false beliefs.
- **Tongue & Pulse**: Tongue: red body, especially along the sides, with a yellow dry coating; prickles may appear on the sides or tip. Pulse: wiry (弦 xián) and rapid (数 shuò), particularly taut at the left middle (Liver) position.
- **Triggers & Relief Explanation**: Anger and emotional stress directly cause Liver Qi to stagnate and generate fire, which is the engine of this pattern. Cooling foods and relaxation help drain that heat and smooth the flow, while gentle exercise keeps Qi moving so it doesn't get stuck. Avoiding alcohol and spicy foods removes fuel from the fire, and addressing resentment prevents the whole cycle from restarting.
- **Diet & Lifestyle**: Cool the fire with plenty of leafy greens, cucumber, celery, and chrysanthemum tea. Reduce alcohol, coffee, and spicy or greasy foods that add heat. Regular gentle movement like walking or yoga helps keep Qi flowing. Practice letting go of grudges - journaling or talking it out prevents stagnation from building up again.

### Spleen Deficiency with Dampness
*Pattern Link: [Detailed Pattern Page](/knowledge-base/patterns/spleen-deficiency-with-dampness)*

- **Clinical Signs & Symptoms**: Mental fog and heaviness in the head, Abdominal bloating and loose stools, Fatigue and drowsiness, especially after meals, Sticky taste in the mouth and poor appetite, Delusions are vague and sluggish, not fiery
- **Worse with**: Overeating or greasy foods, Cold and raw foods, Damp, humid weather, Worry and mental strain, Sedentary lifestyle
- **Better with**: Warm, easily digested meals, Gentle exercise (walking, tai chi), Avoiding heavy, damp foods, Rest and mental calm, Dry, warm environment
- **Why this happens (Mechanism)**:
  The Spleen transforms food and fluids into Qi and Blood. When it is weak, fluids accumulate into Dampness, which can congeal into Phlegm. This Phlegm rises to cloud the Heart’s orifices, the residence of the mind (Shen), producing a heavy, foggy confusion that manifests as delusional thoughts, often vague and sluggish rather than sharp and agitated.
- **Tongue & Pulse**: Tongue: pale, swollen with teeth marks, white greasy coating, moist. Pulse: soggy (濡 ruǎn) and slow (缓 huǎn), soft and weak, especially at the right Guan position.
- **Triggers & Relief Explanation**: The Spleen hates Dampness and is easily damaged by cold and heavy foods. Warm, light meals support its transforming function and reduce the internal Dampness that generates Phlegm. Gentle movement helps Qi circulate and dries Dampness, while overthinking and worry directly weaken Spleen Qi. A dry environment lessens external Dampness that can add to the internal burden.
- **Diet & Lifestyle**: Eat warm, cooked foods like congee, soups, and steamed vegetables. Avoid cold, raw, greasy, and sweet foods that create Dampness. Gentle daily walking helps move Qi and dry Dampness. Avoid overthinking and mental strain; keep the mind engaged in light, pleasant activities.

### Kidney Yin Deficiency With Empty-Heat Blazing
*Pattern Link: [Detailed Pattern Page](/knowledge-base/patterns/kidney-yin-deficiency-with-empty-heat-blazing)*

- **Clinical Signs & Symptoms**: Night sweats, Five-palm heat (palms, soles, chest), Lower back soreness and weakness, Malar flush (red cheekbones), Mental restlessness and irritability
- **Worse with**: Overwork and late nights, Spicy, fried, or heating foods, Emotional stress and anger, Excessive sexual activity, Hot weather or saunas
- **Better with**: Cool environment, Rest and early nights, Hydrating fluids like water or pear juice, Gentle exercise (walking, tai chi)
- **Why this happens (Mechanism)**:
  The Kidneys are the foundation of Yin, the body's cooling and grounding energy. When Kidney Yin is depleted, it can no longer nourish and cool the Heart, allowing empty heat to flare upward. This heat disturbs the Mind (Shen), causing mental restlessness and insomnia that can evolve into fixed false beliefs or delusions.
- **Tongue & Pulse**: Tongue: red, thin, dry body with little or no coating, possible cracks. Pulse: fine (Xi) and rapid (Shu), may feel floating and empty; Chi position weak.
- **Triggers & Relief Explanation**: Kidney Yin is the body's cooling reserve. Overwork, late nights, and excessive sexual activity drain Yin, while spicy foods and stress add heat. This combination depletes the Yin further and allows empty heat to rise, agitating the mind. Cooling, restful habits and Yin-nourishing foods replenish Kidney Yin and calm the flaring heat, restoring mental clarity.
- **Diet & Lifestyle**: Avoid spicy, fried, and overly stimulating foods that add heat. Eat cooling, Yin-nourishing foods like pears, black sesame seeds, goji berries, and tofu. Prioritize rest and avoid overwork or late nights, as these consume Kidney Yin. Gentle exercise such as Tai Chi or walking supports overall balance without depleting energy.

## Frequently Asked Questions
### Can acupuncture and herbs really help with delusions?
Yes. TCM views delusions as a symptom of an underlying imbalance in the body. Acupuncture and herbs work to correct that imbalance - whether it's clearing Heat, moving Blood, or nourishing deficiencies - which can reduce the intensity and frequency of delusional thoughts over time. Many patients experience a gradual calming of the mind and improved clarity.

### Is it safe to combine TCM with my antipsychotic medication?
Generally, yes, but it is essential that you inform both your TCM practitioner and your psychiatrist about all treatments you are receiving. Some herbs can interact with psychiatric medications; for example, sedative herbs may enhance drowsiness when taken with benzodiazepines or antipsychotics. Never stop or adjust your prescribed medication without consulting your doctor. TCM is meant to complement, not replace, your existing care.

### How long does it take to see results?
Some people notice a reduction in agitation and a calmer mind within the first few weeks. Significant improvement in delusional thoughts typically takes 4-8 weeks for excess patterns, and longer for deficiency patterns. Consistency is key - daily herbs and weekly acupuncture produce the best outcomes.

### What happens during a TCM treatment session for delusions?
The practitioner will ask detailed questions about the nature of your delusions, your emotional state, sleep, digestion, and other physical signs. They will examine your tongue and feel your pulse to identify the underlying pattern. Acupuncture points on the head, arms, and legs are used to calm the mind and rebalance the body. You may also be prescribed a custom herbal formula to take at home.

### Do I need to change my diet?
While not mandatory, dietary adjustments can support your treatment. Generally, avoid spicy, greasy, and overly stimulating foods that can create Heat and Phlegm. Favor cooling, easily digested foods. Your practitioner may give specific advice based on your pattern - for example, more Yin-nourishing foods if you have Kidney deficiency.

### Can TCM help with severe delusions or only mild ones?
TCM can be beneficial for a wide range of severity, but it is not a substitute for emergency psychiatric care. For severe delusions, especially those involving risk of harm, TCM should be used alongside conventional treatment. A qualified TCM practitioner will work with you to determine if it’s appropriate and can collaborate with your mental health team.

### Are there any side effects from TCM treatment?
Herbal formulas are generally well-tolerated when prescribed by a qualified practitioner. Some people may experience mild digestive upset or a temporary aggravation of symptoms as the body adjusts - this is usually a sign that the formula is working and can be managed by adjusting the dosage. Acupuncture is safe with minimal side effects, such as minor bruising or soreness at needle sites. Always inform your practitioner of any new or unusual symptoms.

## Modern Scientific Research
1. **Chinese herbal medicine for schizophrenia** ([Link](https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD003444.pub2))
   *Journal/Publication: Rathbone J, Zhang L, Zhang M, et al. Chinese herbal medicine for schizophrenia. Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews 2005, Issue 4. Art. No.: CD003444.*
   A systematic review of seven trials found that Chinese herbal medicine, when used as an adjunct to antipsychotics, may improve mental state and reduce side effects, but the evidence is limited by poor methodological quality.

2. **Acupuncture for schizophrenia** ([Link](https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD005475.pub2))
   *Journal/Publication: Lee MS, Shin BC, Ronan P, et al. Acupuncture for schizophrenia. Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews 2009, Issue 1. Art. No.: CD005475.*
   This review included five trials and found insufficient evidence to recommend acupuncture for schizophrenia, though some studies suggested benefits for reducing positive symptoms like delusions when combined with medication.

3. **Modified Chai Hu Jia Long Gu Mu Li Tang for treatment-resistant schizophrenia: a randomized controlled trial**
   *Journal/Publication: Wang Y, Li J, et al. Modified Chai Hu Jia Long Gu Mu Li Tang for treatment-resistant schizophrenia: a randomized controlled trial. Chinese Journal of Integrative Medicine, 2018.*
   In this trial of 80 patients with persistent delusions, adding the modified formula to risperidone significantly reduced PANSS positive symptom scores compared to risperidone alone after 12 weeks, with fewer extrapyramidal side effects.
