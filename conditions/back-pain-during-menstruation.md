# Back Pain During Menstruation (经行腰痛)
**Pinyin**: jīng xíng yāo tòng | **Kind**: Symptom | **Category**: Back & Neck Problems, Menstrual Problems  
**Synonyms**: Low back pain or soreness during menstruation | **Body Regions**: Back — Lower (Lumbar)

## Conventional Medicine View
### Description
Western medicine generally attributes menstrual low back pain to prostaglandins - hormone-like substances that trigger uterine contractions during your period. These contractions can radiate pain to the lower back, especially if the uterus tilts backward or if there's underlying pelvic congestion. Muscle tension from hormonal shifts can also contribute.
In some cases, menstrual back pain signals a gynecological condition like endometriosis, uterine fibroids, or pelvic inflammatory disease. Diagnosis usually involves a pelvic exam, symptom history, and sometimes ultrasound. Treatment focuses on reducing inflammation and managing pain.

### Treatments
Standard care includes over-the-counter NSAIDs like ibuprofen to block prostaglandin production, hormonal contraceptives to regulate cycles and reduce pain, and heat therapy or gentle exercise. For severe cases, stronger prescription pain relievers or surgical evaluation may be considered.

## Traditional Chinese Medicine View
### TCM Understanding
In TCM, the lower back is the "palace of the Kidneys" - the Kidneys govern the bones, the lumbar region, and the body's fundamental Yin and Yang. When Kidney energy is depleted, whether from overwork, chronic stress, or constitutional weakness, the low back loses its foundation and becomes prone to pain. Menstruation further draws on Blood and essence, which are stored by the Liver and Kidneys, so any pre-existing weakness shows up more clearly during your period.
But the Kidneys aren't the only players. The Liver ensures the smooth flow of Qi and Blood throughout the body, and emotional stress or frustration can cause Qi to stagnate, especially in the lower back channels.
The Spleen transforms fluids; a weak Spleen can lead to Dampness and Phlegm accumulation that settles heavily in the lower back. Even external pathogens like Cold and Dampness can invade the body's channels and localize in the lumbar area, creating a cold, heavy pain that worsens during menstruation when the body's defenses are lowered.
This is why the quality of your pain matters so much. A stabbing, fixed pain that worsens with pressure and comes with dark, clotty flow points to Blood Stagnation. A dull, aching soreness with dizziness and night sweats suggests Yin Deficiency. A heavy, cold ache that improves with heat signals Cold-Dampness. By listening to these details, a TCM practitioner can identify which organ systems and pathogenic factors are involved and craft a treatment that addresses the root cause, not just the pain.

### Practitioner Diagnosis
A practitioner begins by asking what the pain actually feels like. A fixed, stabbing pain that worsens with pressure and is accompanied by dark menstrual flow with clots points strongly toward Qi and Blood Stagnation. The tongue often looks dark with purple spots, and the pulse feels wiry or choppy. This pattern is about stuck energy and blood in the lower back channels, so the pain is intense and in a specific spot.
If the pain is more of a dull soreness and weakness, with dizziness, night sweats, or a dry mouth, the picture shifts to Kidney and Liver Yin Deficiency. Here the tongue is red with little coating and the pulse is thin and rapid. When that same soreness is joined by a distending, pounding sensation in the low back and head, along with irritability, it suggests Liver Yang Rising on top of the Yin deficiency. The pulse becomes wiry, and the person may feel waves of heat.
Heavy, cold pain that feels better with a heating pad or warm bath signals Cold-Dampness in the Lower Burner. The tongue coating is white and greasy, and the pulse is deep and tight. In contrast, Phlegm-Dampness in the Middle-Burner creates a heavy, dragging sensation in the low back with chest tightness and a swollen, greasy-coated tongue. The practitioner asks whether cold or damp weather makes things worse and about digestion to separate these two dampness patterns.
When the low back feels cold and aching, and the pain worsens with tiredness or chilly weather, Kidney Yang Deficiency is likely. The person may also urinate frequently, especially at night, and feel generally low in energy. The tongue is pale and puffy, and the pulse is deep and weak. This pattern reflects a lack of warming fire in the body’s core, leaving the back vulnerable to cold ache.

## TCM Patterns and Treatment

### Qi And Blood Stagnation
*Pattern Link: [Detailed Pattern Page](/knowledge-base/patterns/qi-and-blood-stagnation)*

- **Clinical Signs & Symptoms**: Fixed, stabbing low back pain, Pain that worsens with pressure, Dark menstrual blood with clots, Irritability or emotional tension
- **Worse with**: Stress, anger, or frustration, Cold, raw foods and drinks, Prolonged sitting or standing, Cold or damp weather or environment
- **Better with**: Gentle movement, stretching, or walking, Warm compress or heating pad on lower back, Deep breathing or meditation, Warm, cooked foods or warm ginger tea
- **Why this happens (Mechanism)**:
  When Qi and Blood flow is blocked in the lower back, stagnation creates a fixed, stabbing pain that intensifies during menstruation. The menstrual cycle adds extra pressure to already congested channels, causing dark, clotty blood and a pain that refuses pressure. Emotional stress or a sedentary lifestyle can worsen the stagnation, making each period more painful.
- **Tongue & Pulse**: Tongue: dark purple body with stasis spots, distended sublingual veins. Pulse: wiry (弦 xián) and choppy (涩 sè).
- **Triggers & Relief Explanation**: Stress and emotional upset cause Qi to stagnate, which worsens the blockage and pain. Cold constricts blood vessels and slows circulation, making stasis more severe. Gentle movement and warmth relax the channels and promote Blood flow, while deep breathing helps smooth Qi. Warm, cooked foods support internal warmth and prevent cold from aggravating the stagnation.
- **Diet & Lifestyle**: Eat warm, cooked meals and avoid icy drinks or raw foods during your period. Gentle daily movement like walking or yoga helps keep Qi and Blood flowing. A warm compress or heating pad on the lower back can ease stabbing pain.

### Kidney and Liver Yin Deficiency
*Pattern Link: [Detailed Pattern Page](/knowledge-base/patterns/kidney-and-liver-yin-deficiency)*

- **Clinical Signs & Symptoms**: Dull aching soreness of the low back and knees, Night sweats and heat in the palms, soles, and chest, Dizziness, dry eyes, or ringing in the ears, Red tongue with cracks and little or no coating
- **Worse with**: Overwork, fatigue, or lack of sleep, Spicy, greasy, heavy, or sweet foods, Hot weather or overheated rooms, Stress, anger, or frustration
- **Better with**: Rest and lying down or sleep, Cooling, moistening foods like cucumber and mung beans, Gentle movement, stretching, or walking, Hydration with water or herbal teas
- **Why this happens (Mechanism)**:
  The Kidneys govern the bones and the low back, and the Liver stores Blood and controls the sinews. When Kidney and Liver Yin are deficient, the low back loses its nourishing, moistening support, leading to a dull, aching soreness that becomes worse during menstruation as Blood and Yin are further depleted. This deficiency also allows empty heat to rise, causing night sweats, dizziness, and a feeling of heat in the palms and soles.
- **Tongue & Pulse**: Tongue: Red, thin body with cracks, little or no coating, dry surface. Pulse: Fine, rapid, often wiry; weak in the left Chi (Kidney) position.
- **Triggers & Relief Explanation**: Yin deficiency is a state of depleted cooling, nourishing fluids. Rest and sleep restore Yin, while overwork and late nights consume it. Cooling foods replenish Yin, whereas spicy and heating foods further dry up fluids and aggravate the empty heat. Emotional stress taxes the Liver, which stores Blood and Yin, so calming activities and gentle movement help preserve what little Yin remains.
- **Diet & Lifestyle**: Eat cooling, moistening foods like pears, black sesame, spinach, and tofu. Avoid spicy, fried, and heating foods that further deplete Yin. Prioritize rest and avoid late nights, which consume Yin fluids. Gentle exercises like yoga or tai chi help, but avoid overexertion.

### Liver Yang Rising
*Pattern Link: [Detailed Pattern Page](/knowledge-base/patterns/liver-yang-rising)*

- **Clinical Signs & Symptoms**: Distending, sore pain that may radiate upward, Flushed face and feeling of heat rising, Irritability and quick temper, Throbbing or distending headache (especially temples or top of head)
- **Worse with**: Stress, anger, or frustration, Spicy, greasy, heavy, or sweet foods, Alcohol and coffee, Overwork, fatigue, or lack of sleep
- **Better with**: Resting in a quiet, dim room, Cooling, moistening foods like cucumber and mung beans, Gentle movement, stretching, or walking
- **Why this happens (Mechanism)**:
  During menstruation, blood and Yin are already being lost. If the Liver's Yin and Blood are insufficient to anchor its Yang, that Yang energy surges upward like steam from a boiling pot. This rising pressure distends the channels in the lower back and head, creating a sore, heavy, pushing pain that often travels upward.
- **Tongue & Pulse**: Tongue: red body with redder sides and a thin dry yellow or scanty coat. Pulse: wiry (弦 xián), often more forceful at the left middle position; may be fine and rapid if Yin deficiency is prominent.
- **Triggers & Relief Explanation**: Emotional upset and stimulants cause Liver Qi to surge, which directly fuels the rising Yang that distends the low back and head. Cooling foods and a calm environment help anchor the Yang by supporting Yin, while gentle downward-focused movement encourages Qi to settle back into the lower body where it belongs.
- **Diet & Lifestyle**: Prioritize cooling, Yin-nourishing foods like cucumber, celery, spinach, and mung beans, especially in the days leading up to your period. Avoid spicy, greasy, and fried foods, as well as alcohol and coffee, which all fan the flames of rising Yang. Gentle, grounding movement such as tai chi or a slow evening walk helps anchor Qi downward; high-intensity workouts and late nights tend to stir it upward.

### Cold-Dampness in the Lower Burner
*Pattern Link: [Detailed Pattern Page](/knowledge-base/patterns/cold-dampness-in-the-lower-burner)*

- **Clinical Signs & Symptoms**: Heavy, dull, cold low back pain, Worse in cold or damp weather, Heavy dragging feeling in the pelvis, Profuse white or clear watery vaginal discharge, Aversion to cold, cold limbs
- **Worse with**: Cold or damp weather or environment, Cold, raw foods and drinks, Sitting or lying on cold surfaces, Prolonged sitting or standing, Wearing damp clothing
- **Better with**: Warm compress or heating pad on lower back, Warm, cooked foods or warm ginger tea, Rest in a dry, warm environment, Gentle movement, stretching, or walking, Moxibustion on the lower back
- **Why this happens (Mechanism)**:
  Cold and Dampness are heavy, sticky pathogens that naturally settle in the lower body. During menstruation, when Qi and Blood are already focused on the uterus, these pathogens easily invade the channels of the lower back, obstructing the flow and causing a dull, heavy ache that feels cold to the touch. The pain is often accompanied by a heavy dragging sensation in the pelvis and a feeling of coldness in the lower abdomen.
- **Tongue & Pulse**: Tongue: pale, swollen body with teeth marks and a thick, white, greasy coating. Pulse: deep (沉 chén), slow (迟 chí), and soggy (濡 rú), especially in the rear (Chi) positions.
- **Triggers & Relief Explanation**: Cold and Dampness are Yin pathogens that congeal Qi and Blood in the channels. Cold contracts and obstructs, while Dampness is heavy and sticky, so cold and damp environments worsen the obstruction. Warmth, both from external heat and warm foods, disperses Cold and dries Dampness, allowing Qi and Blood to flow freely. Gentle movement helps circulate Qi and transform Dampness, while rest in a dry, warm space prevents further invasion.
- **Diet & Lifestyle**: Avoid cold, raw foods and iced drinks; instead, enjoy warming, cooked meals like ginger tea, congee with cinnamon, and hearty soups. Keep the lower back and abdomen warm with clothing or a heat pack, especially during damp or cold weather. Gentle daily movement like walking or stretching helps Qi flow and prevents Dampness from stagnating.

### Kidney Yang Deficiency
*Pattern Link: [Detailed Pattern Page](/knowledge-base/patterns/kidney-yang-deficiency)*

- **Clinical Signs & Symptoms**: Cold, aching lower back pain, Worse with cold and fatigue, Frequent urination, especially at night, Feeling cold, especially in lower back and legs, Pale, puffy tongue with teeth marks
- **Worse with**: Cold or damp weather or environment, Cold, raw foods and drinks, Overwork, fatigue, or lack of sleep, Menstruation itself
- **Better with**: Warm compress or heating pad on lower back, Warm, cooked foods or warm ginger tea, Gentle movement, stretching, or walking, Rest and lying down or sleep
- **Why this happens (Mechanism)**:
  The Kidneys govern the low back, and Kidney Yang provides the fire that warms the entire body. When that warming energy is weak, the lower back becomes cold and aching. During menstruation, blood and Qi move downward, leaving the back even more depleted of Yang, so the dull, cold pain intensifies.
- **Tongue & Pulse**: Tongue: Pale, puffy, tender body with teeth marks, wet white coating. Pulse: Deep, slow, weak, especially at the Chi (third/foot) positions.
- **Triggers & Relief Explanation**: Kidney Yang is the body's warming fire. Cold and overwork deplete it further, so the back pain worsens. Warmth, rest, and warming foods help to restore Yang and drive out cold, easing the ache. Moxibustion directly warms the channels and points that strengthen the Kidneys.
- **Diet & Lifestyle**: Eat warming foods like lamb, ginger, cinnamon, and walnuts. Avoid cold, raw foods and icy drinks. Keep your lower back warm, especially during your period. Gentle exercise such as walking or yoga helps circulate Yang Qi, but avoid overexertion. Moxibustion at Mingmen and Guanyuan can be very beneficial.

### Phlegm-Dampness in the Middle-Burner
*Pattern Link: [Detailed Pattern Page](/knowledge-base/patterns/phlegm-dampness-in-the-middle-burner)*

- **Clinical Signs & Symptoms**: Heavy, dragging ache in the low back, Bloating and fullness in the upper abdomen, worse after eating, Nausea or queasiness, Body feels heavy and sluggish, Greasy, sticky white coating on a pale, puffy tongue
- **Worse with**: Cold or damp weather or environment, Spicy, greasy, heavy, or sweet foods, Lying down for long periods, Cold, raw foods and drinks
- **Better with**: Warm, dry weather, Gentle movement, stretching, or walking, Warm, cooked foods or warm ginger tea, Resting with knees slightly elevated
- **Why this happens (Mechanism)**:
  When the Spleen is weak, it fails to transform fluids properly, and they accumulate into Phlegm-Dampness. This heavy, sticky pathogen settles in the Middle Burner and can seep downward, clogging the channels that run through the lower back. During menstruation, Qi and blood are already moving downward to the uterus, and the obstruction from Phlegm-Dampness creates a dragging, heavy ache in the low back rather than a sharp pain.
- **Tongue & Pulse**: Tongue: pale and swollen with teeth marks, a thick white greasy coating that looks wet. Pulse: slippery (滑 huá), often slow or soggy, especially at the right Guan position.
- **Triggers & Relief Explanation**: Phlegm-Dampness is a heavy, sticky pathogen that thrives in damp environments and with poor diet. Warmth and movement help Qi to circulate and transform Dampness, while cold and heavy foods worsen the Spleen's function, making the back feel heavier. During menstruation, the downward flow of Qi and blood can be obstructed by Dampness, so clearing the Middle Burner eases the lower back.
- **Diet & Lifestyle**: Avoid cold, raw, and greasy foods that generate Dampness. Eat warm, cooked meals with aromatic spices like ginger and cardamom. Gentle daily movement like walking helps Qi circulate and dries Dampness.

## Frequently Asked Questions
### Why does my back hurt only during my period?
Your period is a time of significant energetic shift in TCM - Blood and Qi move downward to support the uterus, which can leave other areas like the lower back temporarily undernourished or congested. If you have an underlying weakness (like Kidney deficiency) or blockage (like Qi stagnation), the added demand of menstruation makes it surface as pain. Once your period ends, the body rebalances and the pain fades.

### Can acupuncture help with menstrual back pain?
Yes. Acupuncture can help relieve pain during an acute episode and, with regular treatment, reduce the frequency and intensity over time. Points are chosen based on your specific pattern - for example, moving Blood and Qi for stabbing pain, or nourishing Yin for a dull ache. Many women report noticeable improvement after 3-4 weekly sessions.

### What herbs are used for period-related back pain?
It depends entirely on your pattern. For stabbing pain with clots, formulas like Ge Xia Zhu Yu Tang with herbs like Tao Ren and Hong Hua move stagnant Blood. For a dull, aching soreness with night sweats, Liu Wei Di Huang Wan nourishes Kidney and Liver Yin. For cold, heavy pain, a warming formula like Zhen Wu Tang may be used. Never self-prescribe; a TCM practitioner will tailor the formula to your exact presentation.

### Is it safe to use heat for all types of menstrual back pain?
Heat is wonderful for patterns involving Cold or Dampness - a heating pad or warm bath can give immediate relief. But if your pain comes with a feeling of heat, irritability, and a red tongue, heat may aggravate the condition. In that case, gentle stretching or acupressure is a better choice. Let your pain's character guide you: if heat feels good, use it; if it makes you feel worse, skip it.

### Can I combine TCM with ibuprofen or birth control?
Generally yes. Acupuncture and most herbal formulas can safely complement NSAIDs or hormonal contraceptives. However, some Blood-moving herbs (like Tao Ren, Hong Hua, or Chuan Xiong) may increase the effect of anticoagulant medications, so always inform both your TCM practitioner and your doctor about everything you're taking. Over time, as TCM treatment takes effect, many women find they need less pain medication - but never adjust prescription drugs without medical guidance.

### Will dietary changes help my period back pain?
Absolutely. Across all patterns, avoiding cold, raw foods and icy drinks during your period helps prevent Qi stagnation and Cold invasion. Favor warm, cooked meals, and include kidney-nourishing foods like black beans, walnuts, and bone broth. If your pain is heavy and damp, reduce dairy, greasy, and sweet foods. Small dietary shifts can make a noticeable difference in how you feel each month.

### Does TCM treatment hurt? And how long until I see results?
Acupuncture uses hair-thin needles and is generally very comfortable - you might feel a slight pinch at insertion, then a dull ache or warmth, which is a good sign. Herbal formulas are taken as teas, powders, or pills and are usually well tolerated. Most women start noticing less intense pain within 1-2 cycles, with deeper, lasting change over 3-6 months. The timeline depends on whether your pattern is more excess (faster) or deficiency (slower to rebuild).

## Modern Scientific Research
1. **Acupuncture for dysmenorrhoea**
   *Journal/Publication: Smith CA, Armour M, Zhu X, Li X, Lu ZY, Song J. Acupuncture for dysmenorrhoea. Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews. 2016.*
   A Cochrane systematic review evaluating acupuncture for primary dysmenorrhea. It found that acupuncture may reduce menstrual pain compared to placebo, no treatment, or NSAIDs, but the evidence was limited by small sample sizes and risk of bias. The review noted that low back pain often improved alongside abdominal cramping.

2. **Elucidation of the mechanism of treating endometriosis with Ge Xia-Zhu Yu decoction by means of network pharmacology and molecular docking** ([Link](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12303444))
   *Journal/Publication: Authors not specified. Elucidation of the mechanism of treating endometriosis with Ge Xia-Zhu Yu decoction by means of network pharmacology and molecular docking. PMC. 2024.*
   This network pharmacology study explored the active compounds and targets of Ge Xia Zhu Yu Tang in treating endometriosis. It identified multiple anti-inflammatory and analgesic pathways, supporting the formula's traditional use for menstrual pain and lower back pain associated with blood stasis.

3. **Chinese herbal medicine for primary dysmenorrhoea**
   *Journal/Publication: Zhu X, Proctor M, Bensoussan A, Wu E, Smith CA. Chinese herbal medicine for primary dysmenorrhoea. Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews. 2008.*
   A Cochrane review assessing Chinese herbal medicine for primary dysmenorrhea. It concluded that Chinese herbal medicine may be more effective than NSAIDs or acupuncture for reducing menstrual pain, though most trials were of low methodological quality. Back pain, when present, responded to the same herbal interventions.
