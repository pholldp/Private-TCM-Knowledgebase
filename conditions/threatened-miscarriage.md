## Conventional Medicine View
### Description
Threatened miscarriage is vaginal bleeding during the first 20 weeks of pregnancy, with a closed cervix and a fetus that still has a heartbeat on ultrasound. It may be accompanied by mild cramping or backache. Diagnosis typically involves a pelvic exam, blood tests for hCG levels, and an ultrasound to check fetal viability and rule out ectopic pregnancy or complete miscarriage. Many cases resolve on their own, but it's a frightening experience that warrants close monitoring.

### Treatments
Conventional management focuses on observation and supportive care. This often includes pelvic rest (avoiding intercourse and strenuous activity), sometimes bed rest, and serial ultrasounds or hCG measurements. In some cases, progesterone supplementation is prescribed to support the uterine lining, especially if low progesterone is suspected. However, there is no medication that can stop a miscarriage that is already in progress.

## Traditional Chinese Medicine View
### TCM Understanding
In TCM, the Kidneys are the foundation of reproduction. They store the essence that creates life and anchor the fetus through the Chong and Ren vessels - the two extraordinary meridians that run through the uterus. When Kidney Qi is weak or Kidney Yang is cold, these vessels lose their grip, and a little blood leaks out. This is the most common root of threatened miscarriage, often felt as dull spotting with a heavy, sinking sensation in the lower back.

### Practitioner Diagnosis
A TCM practitioner begins by carefully asking about the bleeding itself-its color, amount, consistency, and any accompanying sensations like pain or a sinking feeling. These details are the first and strongest clues that point toward one pattern rather than another. The tongue and pulse then confirm the underlying imbalance.
If the blood is dull or darkish, and there is a persistent lower back ache with a bearing-down sensation, the pattern is likely Kidney Qi not Firm. The tongue is usually pale with a thin white coat, and the pulse feels deep, weak, and fine, especially weak at the rear position. This reflects the Kidney system’s inability to hold the fetus securely.
When the bleeding is bright red and may be sticky, accompanied by thirst, a dry mouth, and a feeling of heat, the pattern is Heat in Uterus Blood. The tongue appears red with a dry yellow coating, and the pulse is rapid and slippery. The heat disturbs the uterus and forces blood to leak out recklessly.
If the spotting is pale, thin, and scanty, and the person feels weak, pale, and may have palpitations or shortness of breath, this suggests Qi and Blood Deficiency. The tongue is pale with a thin white coat, and the pulse is thready and weak. The body simply lacks the resources to nourish and anchor the fetus.
Dark, clotted spotting with a fixed, stabbing pain in the lower abdomen points to Blood Stagnation-sometimes triggered by physical trauma. The tongue may show purple spots, and the pulse is choppy or wiry. Stagnant blood obstructs proper nourishment, causing the bleeding.
When the bleeding is dull-colored and the person feels cold, with cold limbs, frequent urination, and a deep weak pulse, the pattern is Kidney Yang Deficiency. The tongue is pale and swollen with a white coating. The warming, securing function of the Kidney is too weak to stabilize the pregnancy.

### Classical Reference

## TCM Patterns and Treatment

### Kidney Qi not Firm
*Pattern Link: [Detailed Pattern Page](/knowledge-base/patterns/kidney-qi-not-firm)*

- **Clinical Signs & Symptoms**: Light, dull-colored spotting, Sore, weak lower back, Dragging, sinking sensation in the lower abdomen, Frequent urination, especially at night, Fatigue and lack of strength
- **Worse with**: Physical overexertion, Lifting heavy objects, Standing for long periods, Cold foods and drinks, Emotional stress
- **Better with**: Complete bed rest, Warm foods and drinks, Warmth on the lower back
- **Why this happens (Mechanism)**:
  The Kidneys store the essence that governs reproduction and anchor the fetus. When Kidney Qi is weak, it cannot hold the Chong and Ren vessels securely, causing them to lose their grip. This is what leads to the light spotting and a heavy, sinking sensation in the lower abdomen.
- **Tongue & Pulse**: Tongue: Pale, slightly puffy body with a thin white coat and possible tooth marks. Pulse: Deep, weak, and fine, most noticeably weak at both rear (Kidney) positions.
- **Triggers & Relief Explanation**: The Kidneys are the root of strength in the body, and they detest cold and overwork. Physical strain depletes Kidney Qi directly, weakening its hold on the fetus. Cold constricts and drains Yang energy, making it harder for the Kidney Qi to secure the womb. Fear and chronic stress also specifically injure the Kidney system, so rest and emotional calm are vital to stabilize the pregnancy.
- **Diet & Lifestyle**: Rest is absolutely essential. Lie down as much as possible and avoid lifting anything heavy. Eat warm, easily digestible foods like soups and stews, and include small amounts of kidney-tonifying foods like walnuts, goji berries, and black sesame.

### Heat in Uterus Blood
*Pattern Link: [Detailed Pattern Page](/knowledge-base/patterns/heat-in-uterus-blood)*

- **Clinical Signs & Symptoms**: Bright red or deep red, sticky spotting, Feeling of heat in the lower abdomen, Restlessness and irritability, Thirst with desire for cold drinks, Dry mouth and dark urine
- **Worse with**: Spicy, greasy, or warming foods, Emotional stress, Hot weather or overheated rooms, Overexertion and lack of rest
- **Better with**: Cooling foods and drinks, Rest in a cool environment, Gentle meditation or deep breathing, Avoiding spicy and fried foods
- **Why this happens (Mechanism)**:
  Excess heat in the body-often from spicy foods, emotional frustration, or a constitution that runs warm-can disturb the uterus and the vessels that nourish the pregnancy. This heat agitates the blood, making it move recklessly and leak out as bright red or sticky spotting. The heat also consumes fluids, leading to thirst, a dry mouth, and a rapid pulse.
- **Tongue & Pulse**: Tongue: red body with a red tip, possible prickles, thin yellow coat, tends to be dry. Pulse: rapid (Shu), often wiry (Xian) or slippery (Hua).
- **Triggers & Relief Explanation**: Heat in the uterus is aggravated by anything that adds more heat-spicy food, anger, hot environments-because these stir up the blood and make it move recklessly. Cooling foods and a calm mind help settle the heat, while rest allows the body's cooling and nourishing functions to restore stability.
- **Diet & Lifestyle**: Avoid spicy, greasy, and warming foods like chili, lamb, and alcohol that can fuel heat. Focus on cooling, light meals with plenty of vegetables and fruits like cucumber, pear, and watermelon. Stay hydrated and avoid emotional stress-practice gentle breathing or meditation to keep the mind calm and prevent heat from building up.

### Qi and Blood Deficiency
*Pattern Link: [Detailed Pattern Page](/knowledge-base/patterns/qi-and-blood-deficiency)*

- **Clinical Signs & Symptoms**: Pale, thin vaginal bleeding, Profound fatigue and weakness, Pale complexion and lips, Dizziness or lightheadedness, Poor appetite
- **Worse with**: Overwork and heavy lifting, Cold foods and drinks, Emotional stress, Skipping meals
- **Better with**: Lying down and resting, Warm foods and drinks, Small, frequent meals, Gentle warmth on the abdomen
- **Why this happens (Mechanism)**:
  Qi and Blood are the material foundation that nourishes the growing fetus and keeps the Chong and Ren vessels strong. When they run low, the vessels fail to anchor the pregnancy securely, and the uterus cannot hold blood properly. This allows pale, thin spotting to leak out, while the mother feels washed out because her entire body is undernourished.
- **Tongue & Pulse**: Tongue: Pale body, may be thin or show teeth marks, with a thin white coating. Pulse: Fine and weak (细弱, xì ruò), especially in the right Guan position.
- **Triggers & Relief Explanation**: Qi and Blood are made from the food we eat and preserved by rest. Warm, cooked meals are easy to digest and directly supply the raw materials to build Blood, while lying down conserves Qi that would otherwise be spent on movement. Overwork and stress burn through Qi rapidly, and cold raw foods weaken the Spleen's ability to transform food into usable nourishment, further depleting the very resources the pregnancy depends on.
- **Diet & Lifestyle**: Eat small, frequent meals of warm, easily digested food - think chicken or beef bone broth, rice congee with red dates and goji berries, and lightly steamed root vegetables. Avoid raw, cold, and greasy foods that drain digestive fire. Prioritize rest, lie down when you feel spotting, and keep activity gentle; a short walk after meals is enough.

### Blood Stagnation
*Pattern Link: [Detailed Pattern Page](/knowledge-base/patterns/blood-stagnation)*

- **Clinical Signs & Symptoms**: Dark, clotted vaginal spotting, Fixed, stabbing lower abdominal pain, Pain worsens with pressure or at night, Purple or dusky tongue with stasis spots, History of physical trauma or injury
- **Worse with**: Cold foods and environment, Physical trauma or heavy lifting, Emotional stress
- **Better with**: Gentle warmth on the abdomen, Light walking, Emotional calm
- **Why this happens (Mechanism)**:
  When blood flow in the uterus becomes sluggish or blocked, the fetus cannot receive proper nourishment from the Chong and Ren vessels. This stagnation can be triggered by a physical fall or injury, or by long-standing emotional stress that causes Qi to stagnate and blood to congeal. The trapped blood leaks out as dark, clotted spotting, and the stasis causes a fixed, stabbing pain in the lower abdomen.
- **Tongue & Pulse**: Tongue: purple or dusky body with stasis spots, distended dark sublingual veins, thin white coat. Pulse: choppy (涩 Sè) or wiry (弦 Xián).
- **Triggers & Relief Explanation**: Cold causes blood vessels to contract and blood to congeal, directly feeding stasis, while gentle warmth helps blood flow smoothly. Physical trauma creates acute stasis, and emotional stress knots the Liver Qi, which is the engine that moves blood-when Qi stalls, blood stagnates. Gentle movement and calm emotions keep Qi and blood moving without the force that could disturb the pregnancy.
- **Diet & Lifestyle**: Avoid cold and raw foods, which congeal blood and worsen stasis. Gentle daily walks encourage Qi and blood flow without straining the body. Do not lift heavy objects or engage in activities that risk falls. Prioritize rest and emotional calm, as stress tightens Qi and slows blood.

### Kidney Yang Deficiency
*Pattern Link: [Detailed Pattern Page](/knowledge-base/patterns/kidney-yang-deficiency)*

- **Clinical Signs & Symptoms**: Dull, dark vaginal spotting, Cold lower back and knees, Frequent urination, especially at night, Feeling cold in the lower abdomen, Fatigue and low energy
- **Worse with**: Cold weather or drafts, Overwork and standing too long, Cold foods and drinks, Emotional stress, Sexual activity
- **Better with**: Warmth on the lower back, Lying down and resting, Warm foods and drinks, Ginger tea, Moxibustion on lower abdomen
- **Why this happens (Mechanism)**:
  When Kidney Yang is too weak to warm the uterus, the Chong and Ren vessels become cold and unstable. Without sufficient warmth, the fetus cannot be properly held, leading to dull-colored vaginal spotting. The cold also causes lower back soreness, cold limbs, and frequent urination.
- **Tongue & Pulse**: Tongue: pale, puffy, tender body with teeth marks and a wet white coating. Pulse: deep, slow, and weak, especially at the Chi (rear) position.
- **Triggers & Relief Explanation**: Kidney Yang is the body's warming fire; cold, overwork, and stress drain it further, leaving the uterus too cold to hold the fetus. Warmth, rest, and warm foods conserve Yang and stabilize the Chong and Ren vessels. Moxibustion directly warms the uterus and strengthens the Gate of Life, helping to stop the spotting.
- **Diet & Lifestyle**: Keep your lower back and abdomen warm; a hot water bottle or warm compress can help. Eat warm, cooked foods like lamb stew, black beans, walnuts, and ginger; avoid cold drinks and raw foods. Gentle rest is important, but short walks can help Qi flow. Avoid standing for long periods and conserve your energy.

## Frequently Asked Questions
### Is acupuncture safe during early pregnancy?
Yes, when performed by a licensed practitioner trained in pregnancy care. Certain points that are known to stimulate uterine contractions - such as Hegu LI-4 and Sanyinjiao SP-6 - are avoided or used only with extreme caution. Your practitioner will select points specifically to calm the uterus, strengthen the Kidneys, and stop bleeding. Many women find acupuncture deeply relaxing, which itself helps reduce stress-related miscarriage risk.

### Can I take Chinese herbs while pregnant?
Yes, under the guidance of a qualified TCM practitioner who specializes in pregnancy. The formulas used for threatened miscarriage - such as Shou Tai Wan or Bao Yin Jian - are specifically designed to be safe in pregnancy and have centuries of traditional use. Never self-prescribe herbs during pregnancy. Some herbs that move blood or are cold in nature are contraindicated. Your practitioner will select only those herbs that support and stabilize the pregnancy.

### How soon will the bleeding stop after starting TCM treatment?
Most women see a reduction in spotting within the first three to seven days of herbal treatment and acupuncture. Complete cessation may take one to two weeks, depending on the pattern. If bleeding is due to Heat or acute Blood Stagnation, it may resolve more quickly. If it's due to deep Kidney or Qi and Blood Deficiency, it may take a little longer. Your practitioner will monitor you closely and adjust the formula if needed.

### Can I combine TCM with progesterone or other conventional treatments?
Absolutely. TCM works well alongside conventional monitoring and progesterone supplementation. There are no known negative interactions between progesterone and the herbs typically used for threatened miscarriage. Always inform both your TCM practitioner and your obstetrician about everything you are taking. If you are on blood-thinning medications (rare in pregnancy), your practitioner will avoid blood-moving herbs like Dang Gui or Chuan Xiong.

### What should I do if I start spotting?
First, contact your obstetrician or midwife to rule out ectopic pregnancy or other urgent causes. Once you've been diagnosed with threatened miscarriage, you can begin TCM treatment alongside conventional monitoring. In the meantime, lie down and rest, avoid lifting anything heavy, stay hydrated with warm fluids, and try to remain calm. Stress can worsen the condition by disrupting Qi flow. Do not insert anything into the vagina until the bleeding has fully stopped and your doctor clears you.

### Will TCM treatment affect my baby?
