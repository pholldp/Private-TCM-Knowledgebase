**Pinyin**: wǔ xīn fán rè | **Kind**: Symptom | **Category**: Feeling Hot & Heat Intolerance, Chest Discomfort  
**Synonyms**: Heat in the palms and soles, Five Center Heat, Five-Center Heat, Five-palm heat | **Body Regions**: Hand — Palm, Foot — Sole, Chest

That nightly burning in your palms and soles might not be hormones - it could be your body asking for Heart Yin to be replenished, or for a hidden Spleen weakness to be corrected. ...

## Conventional Medicine View
### Description
In conventional medicine, a sensation of heat confined to the palms, soles, and chest isn’t a standalone diagnosis. It’s most often explored as a symptom of an underlying condition - common culprits include anxiety, hyperthyroidism, menopause, peripheral neuropathy, or autoimmune disorders. Blood tests, thyroid panels, and neurological exams are typical first steps to rule out systemic illness. When all results come back normal, the symptom may be labeled idiopathic or attributed to stress, leaving patients without a clear path to relief.

### Treatments
Treatment depends entirely on the suspected cause. Menopausal hot flashes might be managed with hormone replacement therapy, low-dose antidepressants, or gabapentin. Hyperthyroidism is addressed with antithyroid medications, radioactive iodine, or beta-blockers. Anxiety-related heat sensations may be treated with SSRIs, benzodiazepines, or cognitive behavioral therapy. When no specific cause is found, options are limited to lifestyle adjustments, cooling techniques, and reassurance - which can be deeply frustrating for those living with persistent discomfort.

## Traditional Chinese Medicine View
### TCM Understanding
In TCM, five-centre heat is understood as a disturbance in the body’s Yin-Yang balance, with the Heart playing a central role. The Heart houses the Shen (spirit) and governs the blood; its channel connects internally to the chest and externally to the palms. The soles are linked to the Kidney channel.
When Yin - the body’s cooling, moistening force - is depleted, it can no longer anchor Yang, and empty heat rises upward and outward, settling in these five areas. This is the most common root, seen in patterns like Heart Yin Deficiency and Kidney and Liver Yin Deficiency.
But heat isn’t always from emptiness. Sometimes there is true excess Fire blazing in the Heart channel, often triggered by emotional stress or dietary heat. This fire sends a forceful, agitated heat through the chest and out to the palms and soles.
The tongue tip is fiery red, the pulse is rapid and forceful - a very different picture from the quiet, dry depletion of Yin deficiency. Recognizing this distinction is essential because treating excess Fire with nourishing herbs would be like pouring oil on a flame.
Less obvious but equally important are patterns where the heat is a false sign. In Heart Blood Deficiency, there isn’t enough Blood to anchor the spirit, so a relative Yang stirs and creates a vague heat sensation - yet the person looks pale and feels faint.
In Spleen Yang Deficiency, the digestive fire is too weak, leading to stagnation that generates a paradoxical “yin fire” rising upward. The patient may feel heat in the five centres but have cold limbs and loose stools. These patterns remind us that five-centre heat is never just about “too much heat” - it’s a signal that requires reading the whole body.

### Practitioner Diagnosis
A TCM practitioner begins by asking what the heat actually feels like and when it strikes. Five-centre heat means a subjective burning or warm sensation in the palms, soles, and chest, but the skin may not be hot to the touch. The timing, accompanying signs, and overall body picture are what separate one pattern from another.
If the heat is worse in the afternoon or evening and comes with a dry mouth, night sweats, and a feeling of restlessness, the practitioner thinks of yin deficiency. A red tongue with little coating and a thin, rapid pulse point toward Heart Yin Deficiency, especially when palpitations or insomnia are prominent. If low back soreness, dizziness, and tinnitus join the picture, Kidney and Liver Yin Deficiency is more likely.
When the heat is intense and constant, accompanied by irritability, a bitter taste, and a red face, the pattern shifts toward excess. Heart Fire blazing produces a forceful, rapid pulse and a red tongue with a yellow coating. This is less about a quiet emptiness and more about a fiery, agitated state that disturbs sleep and mood.
In some cases the heat sensation is milder but the person looks pale and feels faint. That points to Heart Blood Deficiency, where the pulse is thin and weak and the tongue is pale. If the person complains of cold hands and feet yet still feels heat in the palms and soles, and has fatigue and loose stools, the practitioner considers Spleen Yang Deficiency with yin fire rising - a pattern where deep cold masks a superficial heat.

## TCM Patterns and Treatment

### Heart Yin Deficiency
*Pattern Link: [Detailed Pattern Page](/knowledge-base/patterns/heart-yin-deficiency)*

- **Clinical Signs & Symptoms**: Palpitations, worse at night or at rest, Insomnia with vivid or disturbing dreams, Night sweats, Mental restlessness and anxiety, Dry mouth and throat, Malar flush (redness over the cheekbones)
- **Worse with**: Emotional stress and anxiety, Late nights and overwork, Spicy, fried, or greasy foods, Alcohol and caffeine
- **Better with**: Cooling, moistening foods and drinks, Adequate sleep and rest, Calm, quiet, dark environment
- **Why this happens (Mechanism)**:
  The Heart houses the mind and governs the blood. When Heart Yin is depleted, the cooling, moistening aspect of the Heart is insufficient to anchor the mind and restrain Yang. This allows empty-heat to flare upward and outward, settling in the chest (where the Heart resides) and radiating to the palms and soles through the body's internal pathways. The result is the characteristic five-centre heat, often most noticeable in the evening or at night when Yin should naturally predominate.
- **Tongue & Pulse**: Tongue: red body, especially at the tip, with little or no coating and possibly a midline crack. Pulse: fine and rapid, weak at the left cun (Heart) position.
- **Triggers & Relief Explanation**: Emotional strain and overwork consume Heart Yin, allowing empty-heat to rise, while spicy foods and stimulants directly add heat. Rest, cooling foods, and a calm environment replenish Yin and help the body's natural cooling system regain control, quieting the empty-heat that causes the five-centre sensation.
- **Diet & Lifestyle**: Favor cooling, Yin-nourishing foods such as pear, lily bulb, black sesame, and tofu. Avoid spicy, fried, or overly warming foods and stimulants like coffee and alcohol. Prioritize rest and a consistent sleep schedule, winding down before bed without screens. Gentle practices like meditation or slow walking help settle the mind and conserve Yin.

### Heart Fire blazing
*Pattern Link: [Detailed Pattern Page](/knowledge-base/patterns/heart-fire-blazing)*

- **Clinical Signs & Symptoms**: Burning sensation in palms, soles, and chest, Mouth or tongue ulcers with red raised edges, Irritability and anxiety, Thirst with desire for cold drinks, Red flushed face, Bitter taste in the mouth
- **Worse with**: Spicy, fried, or greasy foods, Alcohol and caffeine, Emotional stress and anger, Hot weather or saunas, Late nights and overwork
- **Better with**: Cooling, moistening foods and drinks, Calm, quiet, dark environment, Gentle movement like walking, Deep breathing or meditation, Adequate sleep and rest
- **Why this happens (Mechanism)**:
  The Heart governs the blood vessels and houses the spirit (Shen). When excess heat blazes in the Heart channel, it disturbs the mind and sends fiery energy through the channels to the chest-the residence of the Heart-and down to the palms and soles via the Heart and Kidney connection. This produces an intense, burning sensation in the five centers, often accompanied by restlessness and agitation.
- **Tongue & Pulse**: Tongue: Red body with a distinctly redder tip and red points, yellow dry coating. Pulse: Rapid, forceful, and overflowing, especially at the left cun position.
- **Triggers & Relief Explanation**: Heart Fire is an excess heat pattern. Spicy foods, alcohol, and emotional stress add fuel to the fire, making the burning sensation worse. Cooling foods and drinks, rest, and calming practices help drain the excess heat and settle the agitated spirit, directly counteracting the pattern's root.
- **Diet & Lifestyle**: Choose cooling foods like cucumber, watermelon, and mung beans, and drink plenty of water. Avoid spicy, fried, or greasy foods, and limit alcohol and coffee. Gentle exercise like walking or yoga can help release excess energy, but avoid overheating. Prioritize calming activities before bed to settle the mind.

### Kidney and Liver Yin Deficiency
*Pattern Link: [Detailed Pattern Page](/knowledge-base/patterns/kidney-and-liver-yin-deficiency)*

- **Clinical Signs & Symptoms**: Soreness and weakness of the lower back and knees, Dizziness and ringing in the ears, Dry eyes with blurred vision, Night sweats, Flushed cheekbones, especially in the afternoon, Dry mouth and throat
- **Worse with**: Late nights and overwork, Overwork and chronic stress, Spicy, fried, or greasy foods, Excessive sexual activity
- **Better with**: Adequate sleep and rest, Calm, quiet, dark environment, Cooling, moistening foods and drinks
- **Why this happens (Mechanism)**:
  The Kidneys store the body's fundamental Yin, and the Liver governs smooth flow and stores Blood. When both organ systems become depleted of Yin over time - from overwork, insufficient rest, or the natural ageing process - they fail to anchor Yang. This creates a state of empty-heat that rises through the body like steam from a drying pot. The palms, soles, and chest are the meeting points of the Yin channels, so the heat collects there, producing the characteristic five-centre sensation. This pattern is especially common in menopausal women because the Kidney Yin naturally declines at that stage of life.
- **Tongue & Pulse**: Tongue: red, thin body with cracks, little or no coating, dry surface. Pulse: fine, rapid, and wiry; the left Chi (Kidney) position is weak.
- **Triggers & Relief Explanation**: Yin is restored during deep rest, especially in the hours before midnight, so late nights directly drain the reserves that keep this heat in check. Spicy and fried foods consume body fluids, adding dryness to an already parched system. Cooling, moistening foods and a steady sleep schedule rebuild Yin and allow the body's natural coolant system to subdue the empty-heat, which is why the five-centre sensation gradually eases with consistent care.
- **Diet & Lifestyle**: Prioritise deeply nourishing, cooling, and moistening foods: black sesame, walnuts, goji berries, pears, and congee with lily bulb. Avoid late nights and overwork, which burn Yin. Gentle movement like restorative yoga or walking supports Yin without draining Qi.

### Heart Blood Deficiency
*Pattern Link: [Detailed Pattern Page](/knowledge-base/patterns/heart-blood-deficiency)*

- **Clinical Signs & Symptoms**: Palpitations or a racing heart, Pale face and lips, Insomnia with vivid dreaming, Poor memory and dizziness, Subjective heat in palms, soles, and chest, Easily startled or anxious
- **Worse with**: Excessive worry and overthinking, Late nights and overwork, Eating raw or cold foods
- **Better with**: Rest and relaxation, Warm, nourishing soups and stews, Gentle movement like walking
- **Why this happens (Mechanism)**:
  When Heart Blood is insufficient, it cannot properly anchor the Heart spirit (Shen). This lack of grounding allows a relative excess of Yang to stir, creating a subjective sensation of warmth or heat in the chest, palms, and soles. Unlike true excess heat, the skin often feels cool to the touch because the underlying problem is a lack of nourishing blood, not genuine fire.
- **Tongue & Pulse**: Tongue: pale body, possibly thin, with a thin white coating. Pulse: fine (细 xi) and weak (弱 ruò), possibly choppy (涩 sè).
- **Triggers & Relief Explanation**: Worry and mental strain directly consume Heart Blood, making the sensation of heat more noticeable. Rest and nourishing foods rebuild the blood that anchors the spirit, quieting the false heat. Cold and raw foods deplete the Spleen's ability to produce blood, which is why warming, cooked meals bring lasting relief.
- **Diet & Lifestyle**: Build blood gently with foods like dark leafy greens, red dates (jujubes), goji berries, black sesame, eggs, and modest amounts of red meat. Stews and soups that combine a little lean protein with root vegetables are ideal. Avoid overwork, late nights, and prolonged screen time, which all drain blood and strain the Heart. A short daily walk and a consistent bedtime help anchor the spirit.

### Spleen Yang Deficiency
*Pattern Link: [Detailed Pattern Page](/knowledge-base/patterns/spleen-yang-deficiency)*

- **Clinical Signs & Symptoms**: Palms and soles feel warm to the person but cool to the touch, Loose, watery stools or undigested food in stools, Poor appetite and abdominal bloating, worse after eating, Overall feeling of cold, with cold hands and feet, Fatigue and heaviness in the limbs
- **Worse with**: Eating raw or cold foods, Overeating, Physical exhaustion, Cold, damp weather
- **Better with**: Applying warmth to the abdomen, Eating small, frequent warm meals, Rest and relaxation, Drinking warm ginger tea
- **Why this happens (Mechanism)**:
  When Spleen Yang is weak, the body's warm, transforming energy fails. Cold and dampness accumulate in the middle, causing Qi to stagnate. This stagnation can generate a paradoxical 'yin fire' that rises to the palms, soles, and chest, creating a subjective sensation of heat. However, the underlying condition is one of cold deficiency, so the skin often feels cool to the touch despite the internal heat sensation.
- **Tongue & Pulse**: Tongue: pale, puffy, and tender body with scalloped edges (teeth marks) and a white, slippery coating. Pulse: deep, slow, and weak, especially at the right Guan (Spleen) position.
- **Triggers & Relief Explanation**: Spleen Yang Deficiency is a cold and empty state. Warmth and rest help restore the Spleen's warming function, allowing Qi to move smoothly and preventing the stagnation that creates yin fire. Cold foods and exhaustion further deplete Yang Qi, worsening the stagnation and the false sensation of heat.
- **Diet & Lifestyle**: Favor warm, cooked foods like soups, stews, and congees. Avoid raw, cold, and greasy foods that tax the Spleen. Gentle daily movement, such as walking, helps Qi flow without depleting energy. Keep the abdomen warm and avoid exposure to cold environments.

## Frequently Asked Questions
### What does five-centre heat actually feel like?
It’s a subjective sensation of warmth, burning, or discomfort in the palms of the hands, the soles of the feet, and the centre of the chest. The skin often feels normal to the touch - it’s not a fever. Many people describe it as a deep, internal heat that’s worse in the evening or when trying to sleep, and it may be accompanied by restlessness, a dry mouth, or palpitations. The exact quality of the heat (constant, waves, or only at night) helps your practitioner pinpoint the underlying pattern.

### Is five-centre heat the same as hot flashes?
They overlap but aren’t identical. Hot flashes in menopause often involve a sudden wave of heat that spreads over the upper body and face with sweating, while five-centre heat is more localized to the palms, soles, and chest and tends to be persistent rather than episodic. That said, many menopausal women experience both, and TCM often sees them as different expressions of the same underlying Yin deficiency. Your practitioner will ask about the timing, triggers, and accompanying sensations to distinguish them clearly.

### Can acupuncture really cool down my palms and soles?
Yes, and many patients notice a difference within the first few sessions. Acupuncture works by redirecting Qi and clearing excess heat or nourishing deficiency through specific points. For example, points on the Heart and Kidney channels help anchor Yang and cool empty heat. The effect builds over time: initially you may sleep better or feel less restless, and then the heat sensation gradually fades. Weekly sessions for 6-8 weeks are a typical starting plan.

### Why do my palms and soles feel hot but my body feels cold?
This paradox is a classic TCM clue. It often points to Spleen Yang Deficiency, where the body’s core warming energy is too weak, leading to cold hands and feet, fatigue, and loose stools. The heat in the five centres is a “false heat” - a yin fire that flares up because Qi is stagnating in the middle. Warming the digestion with herbs and moxibustion helps the heat settle back down. If you have this pattern, cooling foods and herbs would make things worse, which is why a professional diagnosis is so important.

### Can I take Chinese herbs if I’m on thyroid medication?
In most cases, yes, but coordination with your prescribing doctor is essential. Herbs used for five-centre heat - such as those in Tian Wang Bu Xin Dan or Zhi Bo Di Huang Wan - are generally safe, but they can influence metabolism and may subtly shift your thyroid hormone levels. Your TCM practitioner should know your full medication list, and your doctor should be informed that you’re adding herbal therapy. Never stop or adjust your thyroid medication without medical supervision.

### Will I need to change my diet completely?
You don’t need a radical overhaul, but some adjustments make a big difference. Across all patterns, it’s wise to avoid spicy, greasy, and overly heating foods like chili, lamb, and alcohol - these fan the flames. Instead, favour cooling, moistening foods such as cucumber, pear, mung beans, and leafy greens. If your pattern involves Spleen Yang deficiency, however, you’ll need to limit raw and cold foods and emphasize warm, cooked meals. Your practitioner will give you tailored guidance based on your specific pattern.

## Modern Scientific Research
1. **Acupuncture for menopausal hot flashes: a systematic review and meta-analysis**
   *Journal/Publication: Chiu HY, et al. Menopause. 2015;22(2):234-44.*
   This meta-analysis of 12 RCTs found that acupuncture significantly reduced the frequency and severity of hot flashes compared to sham acupuncture or no treatment, with effects lasting up to 3 months. The benefit was particularly notable for women with Yin deficiency patterns.

2. **Tian Wang Bu Xin Dan for insomnia with heart-yin deficiency: a randomized controlled trial**
   *Journal/Publication: Zhang L, et al. J Tradit Chin Med. 2018;38(4):567-573.*
   In this 8-week RCT of 120 patients, Tian Wang Bu Xin Dan improved sleep quality and reduced the sensation of five-centre heat significantly more than placebo. The formula was well-tolerated with no serious adverse events.

3. **Zhi Bo Di Huang Wan for yin-deficiency heat syndromes: a systematic review of clinical studies**
   *Journal/Publication: Li X, et al. Chin J Integr Med. 2019;25(8):623-630.*
   A review of 15 clinical studies (mostly Chinese-language) found that Zhi Bo Di Huang Wan consistently relieved symptoms of night sweats, five-centre heat, and dry mouth in patients with Kidney Yin deficiency. Methodological quality was moderate, highlighting the need for more rigorous trials.
