## Conventional Medicine View
### Description
Beriberi is a disease caused by a deficiency of thiamine (vitamin B1). It affects the cardiovascular system, producing the swelling and heart failure of 'wet' beriberi, or the nervous system, causing peripheral neuropathy, muscle wasting, and paralysis in 'dry' beriberi. Diagnosis is based on clinical signs, dietary history, and low blood thiamine levels. It is most common in populations with polished rice diets, chronic alcoholism, or malabsorption syndromes.

### Treatments
Standard treatment is thiamine replacement therapy, given orally or intravenously depending on severity. In wet beriberi with heart involvement, urgent thiamine administration can be life-saving. Supportive care includes nutritional counseling, physical therapy for neuropathy, and addressing underlying causes such as alcohol dependence.

## Traditional Chinese Medicine View
### TCM Understanding
In TCM, beriberi is not a single disease but a manifestation of internal dampness and deficiency. The Spleen is the central organ of digestion and fluid metabolism. When the Spleen is weak, it fails to transform food into Qi and Blood, and fluids accumulate as dampness. Dampness is heavy and sinks downward, causing the leg swelling, heaviness, and numbness of wet beriberi.
If dampness lingers and combines with heat, it becomes Damp-Heat in the Lower Burner, leading to red, hot, swollen feet with oozing. This pattern often arises from a diet rich in greasy, sweet, or damp-producing foods, or from living in humid environments. The tongue shows a thick yellow greasy coat, and the pulse is rapid and slippery.
In dry beriberi, where neuropathy and heart symptoms predominate, the pattern is more about deficiency. The Spleen's long-standing weakness fails to produce enough Blood to nourish the Heart, resulting in palpitations, fatigue, and mental fog - Heart and Spleen Deficiency. In severe cases, the Yang energy of the Spleen and Kidneys is so depleted that water floods upward and oppresses the Heart, causing severe breathlessness and palpitations - a critical condition called Water Qi intimidating the Heart.
Thus, TCM sees beriberi as a spectrum from dampness excess to profound deficiency, each requiring a distinct treatment approach. The same Western diagnosis of thiamine deficiency can present very differently in TCM terms, and the treatment must match the pattern.

### Practitioner Diagnosis
When someone comes in with leg swelling, numbness, or heart flutters that suggest beriberi, a practitioner first looks at the big picture: is the body flooded with dampness, or is it more about exhaustion and poor nourishment? The legs tell one story, but the tongue and pulse tell another.
If the legs feel heavy, swollen, and cold, and the person is tired after eating and has loose stools, the pattern is likely Spleen Deficiency with Dampness. The tongue is pale and puffy with a greasy white coat, and the pulse feels soft and slippery. This is the classic "wet beriberi" picture, where the Spleen simply cannot manage fluids.
When the swelling is hot, red, and painful, with a feeling of burning and maybe a low fever, the dampness has turned into heat. This Damp-Heat in the Lower Burner pattern shows a red tongue with a thick yellow greasy coat and a rapid, slippery pulse. The person may also have scanty dark urine and a bitter taste in the mouth.
In drier forms, the legs may not swell much but the person feels palpitations, shortness of breath, and extreme fatigue. If the tongue is pale with a thin coat and the pulse is weak and thready, it points to Heart and Spleen Deficiency - the Spleen isn't making enough blood to calm the Heart.
In the most severe cases, fluid overwhelms the Heart Yang, causing chest tightness, cold limbs, and a deep, thready pulse; this Water Qi intimidating the Heart pattern is an emergency requiring immediate care.

## TCM Patterns and Treatment

### Spleen Deficiency with Dampness
*Pattern Link: [Detailed Pattern Page](/knowledge-base/patterns/spleen-deficiency-with-dampness)*

- **Clinical Signs & Symptoms**: Heavy, swollen legs with numbness, Loose stools or diarrhoea, Poor appetite, bloating after meals, Fatigue, especially after eating, Pale, puffy tongue with teeth marks
- **Worse with**: Cold or raw foods, Overeating, Damp or humid weather, Prolonged standing, Overwork or exhaustion
- **Better with**: Warm cooked meals, Resting after meals, Gentle walking or stretching, Elevating the legs, Dry, warm environment
- **Why this happens (Mechanism)**:
  The Spleen is responsible for transforming food and fluids into usable Qi. When it is weak, fluids are not processed properly and turn into internal Dampness. This Dampness is heavy and naturally sinks downward, settling in the legs to cause the swelling, heaviness, and numbness that define beriberi. The poor appetite, fatigue, and loose stools are all signs that the Spleen is struggling to generate energy and manage moisture.
- **Tongue & Pulse**: Tongue: Pale, swollen body with teeth marks, white greasy coating, moist surface. Pulse: Soggy (Ru) and slow (Huan).
- **Triggers & Relief Explanation**: The Spleen thrives on warmth and moderate activity. Cold and raw foods dampen its digestive fire, causing fluids to stagnate and pool in the legs. Warm, cooked foods and rest after meals support Spleen Yang, helping it transform and transport Dampness. Gentle movement and leg elevation assist Qi and fluid circulation, preventing Dampness from settling in the lower body.
- **Diet & Lifestyle**: Eat warm, cooked foods like congee, soups, and steamed vegetables. Avoid cold, raw, and greasy foods that burden the Spleen. Eat small, regular meals and rest briefly after eating. Keep feet dry and clean, and elevate your legs when sitting to help fluid return.

### Damp-Heat in the Lower Burner
*Pattern Link: [Detailed Pattern Page](/knowledge-base/patterns/damp-heat-in-the-lower-burner)*

- **Clinical Signs & Symptoms**: Feet red, swollen, and hot to the touch, Interdigital maceration with oozing yellow or cloudy fluid, Burning or stinging pain, worse in damp heat, Heavy, dragging sensation in the legs, Thirst with no desire to drink much, Dark, scanty urine
- **Worse with**: Hot, humid weather, Spicy or greasy food, Alcohol, Prolonged standing, Tight, non-breathable shoes
- **Better with**: Cool, dry environment, Elevating the legs, Cooling foods (mung beans, barley)
- **Why this happens (Mechanism)**:
  When dampness accumulates in the body-often from weak Spleen digestion or external humidity-it can transform into heat if it lingers. This Damp-Heat then sinks to the Lower Burner and pours down into the feet, where it causes the skin to become red, swollen, hot, and wet with oozing or maceration. The combination of Dampness and Heat blocks the local Qi and blood flow, leading to itching, burning pain, and foul-smelling discharge.
- **Tongue & Pulse**: Tongue: red body, yellow greasy coating thickest at root, slightly swollen. Pulse: slippery and rapid, more pronounced at the Chi (rear) position.
- **Triggers & Relief Explanation**: Damp-Heat is aggravated by hot, humid environments and by foods that create more internal heat and dampness. Standing for long periods encourages fluid to pool in the lower limbs, worsening the Dampness. Cooling, drying measures counteract the Heat and Dampness, allowing the feet to heal and reducing the inflammatory discharge.
- **Diet & Lifestyle**: Avoid spicy, greasy, and sweet foods that generate Damp-Heat. Choose cooling, drying foods like barley, mung beans, bitter gourd, and celery. Keep feet clean and dry; wear breathable cotton socks and open-toed shoes when possible. Soak feet in cool water with a little salt or herbal rinses containing Huang Bo or Ku Shen to reduce heat and itching.

### Heart and Spleen Deficiency
*Pattern Link: [Detailed Pattern Page](/knowledge-base/patterns/heart-and-spleen-deficiency)*

- **Clinical Signs & Symptoms**: Palpitations or racing heart, Severe fatigue and lack of energy, Poor appetite with bloating, Difficulty falling asleep or vivid dreams, Sallow or pale complexion, Shortness of breath on mild exertion, Dizziness or light-headedness
- **Worse with**: Overwork or exhaustion, Excessive worry or anxiety, Cold or raw foods, Irregular eating patterns
- **Better with**: Warm cooked meals, Gentle walking or stretching, Consistent, restful sleep, Stress reduction and calm mind
- **Why this happens (Mechanism)**:
  When the Spleen is weak for a long time, it cannot transform food into enough Qi and Blood. The Heart depends on this Blood to stay calm and steady; without it, palpitations, breathlessness, and deep fatigue appear. This mirrors the picture of dry beriberi, where nutritional deficiency starves the heart muscle.
- **Tongue & Pulse**: Tongue: pale, tender body with teeth marks, thin white coating. Pulse: fine and weak; the left cun (Heart) and right guan (Spleen) positions are especially feeble.
- **Triggers & Relief Explanation**: Overwork and emotional strain consume Heart Blood and Spleen Qi, directly deepening the deficiency. Cold and raw foods damage the Spleen’s ability to transform food into Blood, while warm, nourishing meals support it. Rest and gentle movement allow the Spleen to rebuild Qi and the Heart to settle, which is why they bring relief.
- **Diet & Lifestyle**: Eat warm, easy-to-digest meals like congee, soups, and stews with root vegetables. Include blood-nourishing foods such as dark leafy greens, dates, and small amounts of lean red meat. Avoid cold, raw, and greasy foods that weaken the Spleen. Prioritize regular, gentle movement like walking and protect your sleep, as rest directly rebuilds Heart Blood.

### Water Qi intimidating the Heart
*Pattern Link: [Detailed Pattern Page](/knowledge-base/patterns/water-qi-intimidating-the-heart)*

- **Clinical Signs & Symptoms**: Palpitations worse when lying down, Swelling of the lower legs or whole body, Feeling of cold in the limbs and body, Sensation of fullness or stuffiness in the chest, Shortness of breath, worse with exertion, Nausea or vomiting of thin watery saliva
- **Worse with**: Cold or damp environment, Overexertion or prolonged standing, Lying flat, Cold or raw foods
- **Better with**: Keeping warm, Elevating the legs, Warm cooked meals
- **Why this happens (Mechanism)**:
  In severe wet beriberi, long-standing Spleen and Kidney Yang deficiency leaves the body unable to transform and transport fluids. Water accumulates internally, and because Yang is too weak to keep it down, the excess fluid surges upward and presses on the Heart and chest. This overwhelms the Heart Yang, producing the frightening palpitations, breathlessness, and swelling that define this pattern.
- **Tongue & Pulse**: Tongue: pale, swollen, tender body with teeth marks; white slippery water-slick coating. Pulse: wiry and slippery (弦滑 xián huá), or deep and fine (沉细 chén xì).
- **Triggers & Relief Explanation**: Cold and dampness directly weaken the body's Yang, making it even harder to transform fluids, so warmth and dry conditions are protective. Overexertion and lying flat both make it easier for water to rise and press on the Heart, while resting with the legs elevated helps fluid drain downward. Warm, cooked foods are easier to digest and do not further chill the already-deficient Spleen and Kidney Yang.
- **Diet & Lifestyle**: Keep the legs elevated when resting and stay in a warm, dry environment. Eat small, warm, cooked meals-think congees, soups, and stews-and avoid raw, cold, or iced foods and drinks. Gentle movement is fine, but stop before you feel breathless or fatigued.

## Frequently Asked Questions
### Can TCM cure beriberi on its own, or do I still need thiamine?
TCM addresses the underlying patterns that made you susceptible to beriberi, but it does not replace urgent thiamine replacement in acute deficiency. If you have been diagnosed with beriberi, your doctor will prescribe thiamine. TCM herbal formulas and dietary therapy can be used alongside this to strengthen your Spleen, resolve dampness, and improve your body's ability to absorb and utilize nutrients. Never stop thiamine without your doctor's guidance, especially if you have heart involvement.

### How does acupuncture help with beriberi symptoms like leg swelling?
Acupuncture points such as Zusanli ST-36 and Yinlingquan SP-9 are chosen to strengthen the Spleen and drain dampness. By stimulating these points, acupuncture encourages the body to transform and excrete excess fluids, reducing swelling and heaviness. It also improves circulation and nerve function, which can help with numbness and tingling. Most patients find weekly sessions combined with herbs provide the best relief.

### What dietary changes should I make to support TCM treatment for beriberi?
In general, avoid cold, raw, and greasy foods that weaken the Spleen and generate dampness. Focus on warm, cooked meals like soups, stews, and congees. Foods that specifically drain dampness include barley, adzuki beans, and Job's tears. If you have a Damp-Heat pattern, also avoid spicy and fried foods. Your practitioner will give you tailored advice based on your specific pattern.
