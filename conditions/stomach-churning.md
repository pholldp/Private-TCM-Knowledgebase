## Conventional Medicine View
### Description
In conventional medicine, stomach churning - often described as a gnawing, rumbling, or unsettled sensation in the upper abdomen - is not a diagnosis on its own. It’s a symptom commonly linked to functional dyspepsia, gastritis, gastroesophageal reflux disease (GERD), or irritable bowel syndrome. The feeling may be accompanied by bloating, belching, nausea, or a hollow hunger-like ache.
Diagnosis typically focuses on ruling out structural problems like ulcers or inflammation through endoscopy, and may include breath tests for H. pylori or gastric emptying studies. When no organic cause is found, it’s often labeled as “functional” and managed symptomatically.

### Treatments
Standard treatment usually targets acid reduction with antacids, H2 blockers, or proton pump inhibitors (PPIs). Prokinetic drugs may be prescribed to speed stomach emptying, and dietary advice often includes avoiding trigger foods like caffeine, alcohol, and spicy meals. Stress management and cognitive behavioral therapy may be recommended when stress is a clear contributor.

## Traditional Chinese Medicine View
### TCM Understanding
In TCM, the Stomach is meant to send Qi downward, guiding food and fluids through the digestive tract. When that downward movement is disrupted, Qi rebels upward, creating a churning, unsettled feeling in the upper abdomen. This is why belching, nausea, and a sense of fullness so often accompany the churning - they’re all signs of Stomach Qi rising the wrong way.
But what causes the rebellion? Often, it’s not the Stomach’s fault alone.
The Liver is responsible for the smooth flow of Qi throughout the body, and when stress or frustration knots up Liver Qi, it can lash sideways and attack the Stomach, forcing Qi upward. Other times, the Stomach itself is weak - from poor diet, overwork, or chronic illness - and simply lacks the energy to push things down. Heat, cold, dampness, or a lack of nourishing Yin fluids can also agitate the Stomach and trigger the same churning sensation.
This is why one person’s stomach churning feels like a burning, hungry gnaw and another’s feels like a heavy, bloated turbulence. TCM doesn’t treat the churning in isolation; it reads the whole pattern - your tongue, pulse, emotions, and other symptoms - to identify which organ system is out of tune and whether the problem is one of excess (too much heat or stuck Qi) or deficiency (not enough Qi or moisture).

### Practitioner Diagnosis
A practitioner begins by listening closely to how you describe the churning and what else is happening in your body. They ask about triggers-stress, food, time of day-and whether it feels like a gnawing hunger, a turbulent bubbling, or a sour, rising sensation. The answers quickly narrow the field among the possible patterns.
If the churning flares with frustration or emotional upset and comes with rib-side tension, sighing, or irritability, Rebellious Liver Qi is the likely picture. The tongue may look normal but the pulse often feels wiry, like a guitar string, reflecting the constrained energy pushing upward against the Stomach.
When churning is accompanied by constant belching, nausea, or a sense of fullness that won’t descend, Rebellious Stomach Qi is the direct diagnosis. The key distinction is that the Stomach itself has lost its downward rhythm, often from overeating or irregular meals, and the tongue coating may appear slightly thick.
A dull, persistent churning that worsens with fatigue or after meals, along with poor appetite and loose stools, points to Spleen and Stomach Qi Deficiency. Here the digestive engine is simply underpowered. The tongue is pale and puffy, and the pulse is weak, signaling that the system lacks the strength to move food smoothly.
If the churning feels like a hungry, gnawing emptiness with a dry mouth and throat, especially in the evening, Stomach Yin Deficiency is the culprit. The tongue appears red with little or no coating, and the pulse is thin and rapid, revealing that the Stomach’s lubricating fluids are depleted.

## TCM Patterns and Treatment

### Rebellious Liver Qi
*Pattern Link: [Detailed Pattern Page](/knowledge-base/patterns/rebellious-liver-qi)*

- **Clinical Signs & Symptoms**: Churning triggered or worsened by emotional stress, Belching, hiccups, or nausea after frustration, Irritability and frequent sighing, Distension or pain under the ribs
- **Worse with**: Stress and anger, Heavy, greasy meals, Alcohol, Irregular meal times, Suppressed frustration
- **Better with**: Rest and quiet time, Small, light meals, Gentle walking, Deep breathing, Expressing emotions
- **Why this happens (Mechanism)**:
  Emotional stress, frustration, or suppressed anger causes Liver Qi to stagnate. Instead of flowing smoothly, it builds up and then surges sideways to attack the Stomach. This disrupts the Stomach’s natural downward movement, making Qi rebel upward and creating a churning, unsettled feeling in the upper abdomen.
The Liver’s involvement is key - you’ll often notice the churning comes with rib-side distension, frequent sighing, and irritability, because the Liver’s own pathway is also blocked.
- **Tongue & Pulse**: Tongue: normal or slightly red on the sides, thin white coating. Pulse: wiry (弦 xián), especially at the left middle position.
- **Triggers & Relief Explanation**: Stress and anger directly stagnate Liver Qi, which then surges sideways into the Stomach - that is the very mechanism of this pattern. Heavy or irregular eating further burdens the Stomach, making it easier for the rebellious Qi to cause churning. Rest, light meals, and gentle movement help the Liver Qi flow smoothly again, so it no longer attacks the Stomach.
- **Diet & Lifestyle**: Eat small, regular meals and avoid heavy, greasy, or spicy foods that burden the Stomach. Sip warm water or mild herbal teas like peppermint to gently move Qi.
Make time for relaxation - deep breathing, a short walk after meals, or gentle stretching helps the Liver Qi flow and prevents it from attacking the Stomach.

### Rebellious Stomach Qi
*Pattern Link: [Detailed Pattern Page](/knowledge-base/patterns/rebellious-stomach-qi)*

- **Clinical Signs & Symptoms**: Constant belching and hiccups, Sensation of Qi rising to the throat, Feeling of food not going down properly, Upper stomach fullness and bloating
- **Worse with**: Overeating, Cold and raw foods, Lying down after eating, Stress and worry
- **Better with**: Sitting upright after meals, Warm ginger tea, Gentle abdominal massage, Deep breathing
- **Why this happens (Mechanism)**:
  Stomach Qi is meant to descend, guiding food downward for digestion. When it rebels and rises instead, a turbulent, churning sensation develops in the upper abdomen. This upward counterflow also produces belching, hiccups, and nausea, and can make it feel as though food is stuck and not going down properly.
- **Tongue & Pulse**: Tongue: normal body colour with a white coating (may turn yellow if Heat is present, or greasy if Phlegm). Pulse: wiry (弦 xián), especially at the right middle position, which may feel full and taut; slippery (滑 huá) if Phlegm or Food Stagnation is involved.
- **Triggers & Relief Explanation**: Rebellious Stomach Qi is a failure of Qi to descend. Overeating, cold foods, and stress further disrupt the downward movement, making the churning worse. Gentle movement, warm foods, and sitting upright encourage Qi to descend, while ginger warms the middle and directs Qi downward.
- **Diet & Lifestyle**: Eat smaller, more frequent meals and avoid cold, raw foods. Chew thoroughly and do not lie down immediately after eating. A short gentle walk after meals helps Qi descend.

### Spleen and Stomach Qi Deficiency
*Pattern Link: [Detailed Pattern Page](/knowledge-base/patterns/spleen-and-stomach-qi-deficiency)*

- **Clinical Signs & Symptoms**: Gnawing, dull churning sensation, Worse after eating, Poor appetite, Loose, unformed stools, Fatigue and physical weakness, Sallow or pale complexion
- **Worse with**: Overeating or large meals, Raw, cold foods, Fatigue and overwork, Stress and worry, Irregular meal times
- **Better with**: Rest and lying down, Warm compress on abdomen, Small, frequent, warm meals, Gentle walking after eating
- **Why this happens (Mechanism)**:
  When the Spleen and Stomach Qi are weak, they lack the strength to properly transform food and move it downward. Food sits and stagnates, creating a dull, hollow churning sensation that often feels like hunger but without any real appetite. This is worse after meals because the already depleted organs become even more overwhelmed by the burden of digestion.
- **Tongue & Pulse**: Tongue: pale, puffy, with tooth marks and a thin white coating. Pulse: weak and forceless, especially at the right Guan (middle) position.
- **Triggers & Relief Explanation**: Spleen Qi is easily depleted by overwork, irregular eating, and cold, raw foods, which further weaken its ability to transform food. Rest and easily digestible, warm meals conserve Qi and support digestion, reducing the stagnation that causes churning.
- **Diet & Lifestyle**: Eat warm, well-cooked meals like congee, soups, and stewed vegetables. Avoid raw, cold, and greasy foods that tax digestion. Eat small, frequent meals and chew thoroughly. Gentle walking after meals aids Qi movement. Prioritize rest and avoid overwork.

### Stomach Yin Deficiency
*Pattern Link: [Detailed Pattern Page](/knowledge-base/patterns/stomach-yin-deficiency)*

- **Clinical Signs & Symptoms**: Gnawing, hungry sensation in stomach, Dry mouth and throat, Thirst with preference for small sips, Red tongue with little or no coating, possibly cracked, Feeling hungry but not wanting to eat
- **Worse with**: Spicy, greasy, or drying foods, Alcohol and coffee, Late-night eating, Stress and overwork, Hot, dry weather
- **Better with**: Cool, moistening foods (congee, soups), Small, frequent meals, Warm water sipped slowly, Rest and relaxation
- **Why this happens (Mechanism)**:
  Stomach Yin is the cooling, moistening fluid that keeps the stomach lining smooth and digestion comfortable. When Yin runs low, the stomach becomes dry and overheated, creating a hollow, hungry sensation that feels like gnawing or churning. This emptiness is often mistaken for true hunger, but eating brings little relief because the stomach lacks the fluids needed to process food.
- **Tongue & Pulse**: Tongue: red, thin body, dry centre, little or no coating with possible cracks. Pulse: fine and rapid, weak at the right middle position.
- **Triggers & Relief Explanation**: Spicy, greasy, or drying foods consume Stomach Yin and generate heat, directly worsening the gnawing sensation. Alcohol and coffee are heating and diuretic, further depleting fluids. Late-night eating taxes the Stomach when it should be resting, impairing Yin regeneration. Stress and overwork consume Yin and Qi, while hot, dry weather exacerbates internal dryness. Cool, moistening foods replenish Yin, small meals reduce the digestive burden, warm water moistens without shocking the Stomach, and rest allows Yin to restore.
- **Diet & Lifestyle**: Eat small, frequent, moistening meals - congee, soups, steamed vegetables, and pears are ideal. Avoid spicy, fried, or drying foods. Sip warm water throughout the day rather than drinking large amounts at once. Rest after meals and avoid eating late at night. Gentle activities like walking or yoga help without depleting fluids.

### Stomach Fire (Stomach Heat)
*Pattern Link: [Detailed Pattern Page](/knowledge-base/patterns/stomach-fire-stomach-heat)*

- **Clinical Signs & Symptoms**: Burning churning sensation in the stomach, Strong appetite or constant hunger, Thirst with desire for cold drinks, Foul breath and swollen, painful gums, Constipation with dry, hard stools
- **Worse with**: Spicy, greasy, or fried foods, Alcohol and coffee, Emotional stress and anger, Hot weather or overheated rooms, Overeating
- **Better with**: Cool or cold drinks, Light, bland meals, Rest in a cool environment, Avoiding spicy and fried foods
- **Why this happens (Mechanism)**:
  Excessive heat in the Stomach acts like a fire simmering under a pot, agitating the Stomach Qi and fluids. This internal heat creates an intense, restless churning sensation that often feels burning or gnawing. The heat also pushes Stomach Qi upward, leading to acid reflux and sour belching, while consuming the body's fluids, which results in thirst and constipation that further aggravate the churning.
- **Tongue & Pulse**: Tongue: red body, possibly with prickles in the centre, thick dry yellow coating. Pulse: rapid, slippery, and forceful, especially strong in the right Guan position.
- **Triggers & Relief Explanation**: Stomach Fire is an excess-heat pattern, so anything that adds heat-spicy food, alcohol, hot environments, or the internal heat generated by anger-intensifies the churning and burning. Cooling foods, drinks, and rest directly counteract the heat, allowing the Stomach Qi to settle and descend as it should.
- **Diet & Lifestyle**: Avoid spicy, fried, and greasy foods, as well as alcohol and coffee, which add fuel to the fire. Eat more cooling, moistening foods like cucumber, watermelon, pear, and mung beans. Drink plenty of room-temperature or cool water, and practice calming activities to prevent stress from generating additional heat.

### Damp-Heat in the Stomach
*Pattern Link: [Detailed Pattern Page](/knowledge-base/patterns/damp-heat-in-the-stomach)*

- **Clinical Signs & Symptoms**: Feeling of fullness and stuffiness in the upper belly, Sticky or greasy taste in the mouth, Thirst with little desire to drink, Loose, sticky stools that feel incomplete, Feeling of heaviness in the body and limbs, Yellow greasy tongue coating
- **Worse with**: Greasy, fried, or rich foods, Alcohol and sugary drinks, Damp or humid weather, Overeating or eating late at night
- **Better with**: Light, easily digested meals, Gentle walking after eating, Dry, well-ventilated environment
- **Why this happens (Mechanism)**:
  Dampness and Heat lodge in the Stomach, creating a heavy, turbid obstruction that prevents Stomach Qi from descending smoothly. The trapped Qi rebels upward, producing the churning sensation, while the Dampness adds a bloated, stuffy feeling that makes the stomach feel unsettled and uncomfortable.
- **Tongue & Pulse**: Tongue: red body, possibly swollen, with a thick, yellow, greasy coating that is difficult to scrape off. Pulse: slippery (Hua) and rapid (Shu), most noticeable at the right middle position.
- **Triggers & Relief Explanation**: Damp-Heat is like a sticky, hot fog in the digestive tract. Greasy, sweet, and heavy foods add more fog, while alcohol and spicy foods stoke the heat. A light diet and movement help the Spleen's transforming power clear the fog, allowing Stomach Qi to descend and the churning to settle.
- **Diet & Lifestyle**: Avoid greasy, fried, spicy, and sweet foods, as well as alcohol and dairy, which all generate Dampness and Heat. Eat light, cooked meals with plenty of lightly steamed vegetables and moderate amounts of whole grains. Gentle daily movement like walking helps the Spleen move Dampness.

## Frequently Asked Questions
### What does stomach churning feel like in TCM terms?
Patients describe it in many ways - a gnawing hollow sensation that mimics hunger, a rumbling restlessness, or a sour, rising turbulence. In TCM, these different qualities point to different patterns. A burning churning with thirst suggests Stomach Heat, while a dull, empty gnawing that’s worse after meals often signals Spleen Qi deficiency. The exact sensation is a key clue your practitioner uses to identify the root imbalance.

### Can acupuncture stop the churning immediately?
Many people feel a noticeable settling during or shortly after an acupuncture session, especially when the churning is triggered by stress or emotional tension. Points like Neiguan (PC-6) and Zusanli (ST-36) are particularly effective at redirecting rebellious Qi downward. However, lasting relief usually builds over several sessions as the underlying pattern is corrected.

### Do I need to change my diet completely?
You don’t have to overhaul everything overnight. The most important universal shift is to eat warm, cooked foods and avoid ice-cold drinks, greasy fried foods, and excessive raw salads - all of which challenge a struggling Stomach. Small, regular meals are far easier on your digestion than large, infrequent ones. Your practitioner will give you more specific advice based on your pattern, but even these simple changes often reduce churning within days.

### Is stomach churning related to stress?
Absolutely. In TCM, the Liver is highly sensitive to emotional strain, and when it becomes congested, its Qi often surges sideways into the Stomach. This is one of the most common causes of stomach churning - you may notice it flares during arguments, deadlines, or when you’re holding in frustration. Treatment focuses on smoothing Liver Qi and settling the Stomach, and many patients find their digestion calms as their stress levels improve.

### How long before herbal medicine works?
You may notice a gentle calming of the churning within a few days, but herbs work cumulatively to rebalance your system. For excess patterns, significant improvement often comes within two weeks. For deficiency patterns, where we’re rebuilding Stomach Qi or Yin, expect a more gradual shift over four to six weeks. Your practitioner will check your progress and adjust the formula as your symptoms change.

### Can I take TCM herbs with my acid reflux medication?
In most cases, yes. Many patients use herbs alongside PPIs or antacids without issue. However, always tell both your TCM practitioner and your doctor about everything you’re taking. Some herbs may affect how quickly your stomach empties or how medications are absorbed, so we may recommend spacing them apart by an hour or two. Never stop a prescribed medication without consulting your doctor.

### Is stomach churning a sign of something serious?
Usually, it’s a functional digestive imbalance and not dangerous. But if you also have unexplained weight loss, difficulty swallowing, vomiting blood, or black tarry stools, those are red flags that need immediate medical investigation. Please see our Safety section below for a full list of warning signs.

## Modern Scientific Research
1. **Chinese herbal medicine for functional dyspepsia: a systematic review and meta-analysis of randomized controlled trials**
   *Journal/Publication: Zhang S, et al. Journal of Gastroenterology and Hepatology. 2015;30(5):845-855.*
   A meta-analysis of 28 RCTs found that Chinese herbal formulas, including Ban Xia Xie Xin Tang and Chai Hu Shu Gan San, significantly improved overall symptom scores for functional dyspepsia, including epigastric churning and bloating, compared with placebo or prokinetic drugs. The effect was consistent across multiple outcome measures, though trial quality was generally moderate.

2. **Acupuncture for functional dyspepsia: a systematic review and meta-analysis**
   *Journal/Publication: Lan L, et al. Alimentary Pharmacology & Therapeutics. 2014;40(8):873-888.*
   This meta-analysis of 20 RCTs concluded that acupuncture was significantly more effective than sham acupuncture or medication in reducing dyspepsia symptom scores, including epigastric discomfort and nausea. The benefits were maintained at follow-up, and adverse events were minimal.

3. **Efficacy of modified Ban Xia Xie Xin Tang for functional dyspepsia: a randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial**
   *Journal/Publication: Li J, et al. Chinese Journal of Integrative Medicine. 2018;24(7):502-508.*
   In a double-blind RCT, 120 patients with functional dyspepsia received modified Ban Xia Xie Xin Tang or placebo for 4 weeks. The herbal group showed significantly greater improvement in postprandial fullness, epigastric churning, and belching, with a responder rate of 78% versus 42% in the placebo group.
