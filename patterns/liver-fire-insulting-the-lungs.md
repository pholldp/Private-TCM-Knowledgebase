## Clinical Signs & Symptoms
- **Key Signs**: Coughing in sudden forceful bouts, Feeling of Qi rushing upward during cough, Face turning red during coughing fits, Dry throat, Feeling of phlegm stuck in the throat that is hard to bring up, Scant sticky phlegm or phlegm in stringy clumps, Pain or distension along the ribs that worsens with coughing, Irritability and easy anger, Bitter taste in the mouth, Dry mouth, Red eyes, Dizziness, Headache, Blood-streaked sputum, Coughing up fresh blood, Feeling of heat or burning in the chest, Restlessness and agitation, Difficulty sleeping, Constipation or dry stools, Dark scanty urine, Tinnitus or ringing in the ears, Sensation of a lump in the throat, Chest tightness, Shortness of breath, Wheezing or laboured breathing, Right-side rib pain specifically, Symptoms worsening during emotional upset

### What Makes It Better/Worse
- **Worse with**: 
  - Anger or emotional frustration
  - Stress and pressure
  - Arguments or confrontation
  - Alcohol consumption
  - Spicy, fried, or greasy food
  - Smoking
  - Staying up late or sleep deprivation
  - Hot weather
  - Suppressed emotions
- **Better with**: 
  - Emotional calm and relaxation
  - Cooling foods such as pears, celery, and mung beans
  - Gentle outdoor exercise
  - Adequate sleep
  - Deep breathing exercises
  - Avoiding stimulants

**Timing/Worsening Details**: Symptoms often worsen in the late evening and during the Liver's most active time on the organ clock (1-3 AM), which may cause disturbed sleep or waking during these hours. The cough may intensify in spring, the season associated with the Liver/Wood element, when Liver Qi naturally rises more strongly. Symptoms tend to flare up noticeably after emotional episodes, sometimes within minutes of becoming angry or upset. Dry autumn weather can also aggravate the pattern because the Lungs are more vulnerable to dryness during that season.

## Traditional Chinese Medicine View
- **Pathophysiology**: Liver Fire, generated by emotional stress or dietary excess, rises along the Liver channel to invade the Lungs, disrupting their descending function and scorching their fluids, causing paroxysmal coughing, chest and flank pain, and sticky or blood-tinged phlegm.
- **Practitioner Notes**: The diagnostic key to this pattern is the combination of Liver Fire signs and Lung dysfunction appearing together, with a clear emotional trigger. The practitioner looks for two clusters of evidence: first, signs that the Liver is generating excessive Fire (irritability, bitter taste, red eyes, pain along the ribs), and second, signs that the Lungs' normal downward-directing function has been disrupted (coughing in bouts, a sensation of Qi rushing upward, chest tightness). The cough in this pattern has a distinctive quality: it comes in sudden forceful episodes, often triggered or worsened by emotional upset, and may be accompanied by a flushed face during the coughing fit. The phlegm, if present, tends to be scant, sticky, and difficult to expectorate, sometimes appearing in stringy clumps.
A hallmark diagnostic clue is that symptoms fluctuate with emotional state. When the person feels angry, frustrated, or stressed, the coughing worsens noticeably. This emotional-respiratory connection is what distinguishes it from simple Lung Heat or Phlegm-Heat patterns. The tongue and pulse together are quite telling: a red tongue body (especially along the sides, which correspond to the Liver area) with a thin, dry yellow coating points to internal Heat drying fluids, while a wiry and rapid pulse confirms both Liver involvement and Heat. In more severe cases where Liver Fire scorches the Lung's blood vessels, blood-streaked sputum or frank coughing of blood may appear, which represents a more urgent presentation.

### Four Examinations
#### Inspection
- **Tongue Summary**: Red body with redder sides, thin dry yellow coating
- **Tongue Properties**:
  - **Body colour**: Red (红 Hóng)
  - **Moisture**: Dry (干 Gān)
  - **Coating colour**: Yellow (黄 Huáng)
  - **Shape**: Prickly / Thorny (芒刺 Máng Cì)
  - **Coating quality**: Dry (干 Gān)
  - **Markings**: Red sides (舌边红)
- **Tongue Explanation**: The tongue body is red, often distinctly redder along the sides (the Liver zone in tongue diagnosis), reflecting Liver Fire. The coating is thin and yellow with reduced moisture, indicating internal Heat consuming fluids. In some cases, the tip may also be red if the Fire has affected the upper body. The coating is typically not thick or greasy unless Phlegm-Heat has developed as a secondary complication. Overall the tongue picture conveys internal Heat drying the body's fluids rather than Dampness or Cold.
- **Vitality & Complexion**:
  - **Overall vitality**: Good Shén (有神 Yǒu Shén)
  - **Complexion**: Red / Flushed (红 Hóng)
  - **Physical signs**: The face often flushes red during coughing episodes. The eyes may appear bloodshot or red. The person may appear restless and tense, with a tendency toward agitated body language. In cases where Liver Fire has scorched the Lung's blood vessels, small amounts of bright red blood may be visible in the sputum. The skin and lips may appear slightly dry due to Heat consuming body fluids. The area below the ribs on one or both sides may feel tender or taut to the touch.

#### Listening & Smelling
- **Voice**: Loud / Forceful (声高 Shēng Gāo), Hoarse (声嘶 Shēng Sī)
- **Breathing**: Coarse / Heavy Breathing (气粗 Qì Cū), Dry Cough (干咳 Gān Ké)
- **Body odour**: Rancid (臊 Sāo) — Liver/Wood

#### Palpation
- **Pulse Qualities**: Wiry (Xian), Rapid (Shu)
- **Pulse Explanation**: The pulse is characteristically wiry (taut like a guitar string, reflecting Liver involvement) and rapid (indicating Heat). The wiry quality is most prominent at the left middle position (Guan), which corresponds to the Liver. The right front position (Cun), corresponding to the Lungs, may feel relatively full or slightly overflowing, reflecting the upward rebellious movement of Qi in the chest. The overall pulse has force and tension, consistent with an Excess-Heat pattern. In cases with significant bleeding, the pulse may become slightly hasty or more rapid.
- **Channels**: Tenderness at LR-14 (Qi Men, on the rib cage below the breast), the Liver's Front-Mu point, is common and reflects Liver Qi stagnation with Fire. Tenderness or tightness along the intercostal spaces following the Liver channel pathway on the lateral torso is frequently found. LU-1 (Zhong Fu, below the outer end of the collarbone), the Lung's Front-Mu point, may also be tender, indicating the Lungs are being affected. The area around GB-24 (Ri Yue, on the lower rib cage) and the lateral rib region may feel taut or painful on palpation. LR-3 (Tai Chong, on the top of the foot between the first and second toes) may be notably tender, reflecting Liver Fire rising.

## Treatment & Recommendations
- **Treatment Principle**: Clear the Liver and drain Fire, calm the Lungs and stop coughing
- **Typical Timeline**: 2-4 weeks for acute flare-ups, 1-3 months for chronic or recurring cases

## Differential Diagnosis
### vs. Liver Fire Blazing
Liver Fire Blazing shares many of the Fire signs (red eyes, headache, irritability, bitter taste) but its primary symptoms affect the head and eyes rather than the Lungs. In Liver Fire Blazing there is no significant cough, chest tightness, or blood-streaked sputum. Once the Fire rises high enough to disrupt Lung function and cause coughing as a dominant symptom, it has progressed into the Liver Fire Insulting the Lungs pattern.

### vs. Lung Heat
Lung Heat (also called Phlegm-Heat in the Lungs) features a productive cough with copious yellow-green sputum and possibly fever, but lacks the Liver signs of rib pain, irritability, bitter taste, and emotional triggers. The cough in Lung Heat does not fluctuate with mood. The pulse in Lung Heat is typically slippery and rapid rather than wiry and rapid.

### vs. Lung Yin Deficiency
Lung Yin Deficiency can also present with dry cough and blood-tinged sputum, but it is a Deficiency pattern with a gradual, chronic course. It features night sweats, afternoon heat sensation, a thin or peeled tongue coating, and a thin rapid pulse rather than a wiry forceful one. There is no irritability, rib pain, or emotional triggering of symptoms.

### vs. Liver Qi Stagnation
Liver Qi Stagnation is the precursor to this pattern and shares the emotional volatility, rib distension, and sighing. However, in Liver Qi Stagnation there is no Heat (no red eyes, bitter taste, or yellow tongue coating) and no significant Lung symptoms like forceful coughing or blood-streaked sputum. The tongue is typically normal-coloured rather than red, and the pulse is wiry but not rapid.
