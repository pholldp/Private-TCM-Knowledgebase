# An Mian Tang (安眠汤 2)
**English Name**: Peaceful Sleep Formula  
**Category**: Spirit-calming formulas, Heart-nourishing and spirit-calming formulas  
**Dynasty/Source**: Modern clinical formula (contemporary patent medicine)  
**Temperature**: Neutral | **Form**: Decoction (Tang)

---

## Summary
An Mian Tang is a concise six-herb formula designed to promote restful sleep by nourishing Heart Blood, calming the spirit (Shen), clearing irritability arising from depressive Heat, and harmonizing the Stomach. 

It is suited for individuals whose insomnia stems from a combination of Blood deficiency failing to anchor the spirit, mild internal Heat generating restlessness, and digestive disharmony that unsettles sleep. 

Compared to larger sedative formulas, An Mian Tang takes a balanced, moderate approach—gentle enough for extended use yet effective enough to address the intertwined emotional, thermal, and digestive roots of poor sleep.

## Safety & Usage Guidelines

### Pregnancy
Use with caution; not routinely recommended during pregnancy
Yuan Zhi has documented uterine-stimulating effects in animal studies and is traditionally classified as a cautionary herb during pregnancy. Zhi Zi’s cold, downward-draining nature may theoretically promote uterine contraction. While these concerns are based on isolated herb data rather than formula-level clinical evidence, pregnancy warrants a conservative approach. Use only under qualified practitioner supervision when the benefit clearly outweighs the risk, and consider substituting safer alternatives where possible.

### Breastfeeding
Generally compatible with breastfeeding at standard doses, with one caveat:
Most herbs in the formula are compatible with breastfeeding. However, Shen Qu (Medicated Leaven) has been traditionally noted in some sources as potentially reducing milk production through its food-dispersing action. At the moderate dose used in this formula (9g), clinically significant effects on lactation are unlikely, but practitioners should monitor. Discontinue or reduce Shen Qu dosage if any change in milk supply is observed. The mother should also watch for any changes in the infant’s feeding behavior or digestive comfort.

### Children
Suitability: May consider for children aged 6 and above under qualified pediatric TCM practitioner supervision
Dosage Adjustment: One-third adult dose for ages 6–9; one-half adult dose for ages 10–14
Not Recommended: Under age 6
Use only the licorice-processed form of Yuan Zhi (Zhi Yuan Zhi) in pediatric prescriptions to minimize gastric irritation. Shen Qu contains wheat; assess for gluten sensitivity before prescribing. Administer as small, frequent sips of warm decoction rather than large doses to improve tolerability and compliance. Keep treatment duration short (1–2 weeks initially) with regular reassessment. Zhi Zi dosage should be reduced or the stir-fried form (Chao Zhi Zi) used to protect the developing digestive system.

### Drug Interactions
Interaction 1: Sedatives, Hypnotics, and Benzodiazepines
Concern: Suan Zao Ren’s demonstrated GABAergic activity may produce additive sedative effects
Medications: Lorazepam, diazepam, alprazolam, zolpidem, eszopiclone, and other sedative-hypnotic medications
Recommendation: Consult prescribing physician before combining. Monitor for excessive drowsiness, prolonged sedation, or impaired motor coordination. If combining, start with lower herb doses and titrate gradually under professional supervision.
Interaction 2: Antidepressants (SSRIs/SNRIs)
Concern: Theoretical serotonin receptor influence from Suan Zao Ren; Yuan Zhi’s documented effects on serotonin and dopamine modulation
Medications: Fluoxetine, sertraline, paroxetine, venlafaxine, duloxetine, and related antidepressants
Recommendation: Clinically significant serotonin syndrome has not been widely documented with these herbs at standard doses, but caution is warranted. Inform the prescribing physician of concurrent use. Monitor for agitation, confusion, rapid heart rate, or other signs of serotonergic excess.
Interaction 3: Anticoagulant and Antiplatelet Drugs
Concern: Gan Cao at prolonged high doses may affect platelet aggregation; Zhi Zi has theoretical additive effects with anticoagulants
Medications: Warfarin, heparin, aspirin, clopidogrel, and related medications
Recommendation: At the small Gan Cao dose (3g) in this formula, clinically significant interaction is unlikely. However, inform the healthcare provider of concurrent use, especially if the patient is on warfarin with a narrow therapeutic index.
Interaction 4: Cardiac Glycosides and Diuretics
Concern: Gan Cao can cause sodium retention and potassium depletion (pseudoaldosteronism) with prolonged use, which may interact with digoxin’s mechanism and potentiate hypokalemia from thiazide or loop diuretics
Medications: Digoxin, hydrochlorothiazide, furosemide, spironolactone
Recommendation: At 3g per day, risk is minimal for short-term use. For patients on these medications who require extended treatment, consider omitting Gan Cao or monitoring potassium levels periodically.

## Administration Details

- **Best Time to Take**: Twice daily: late afternoon (4–5 PM) and 30–60 minutes before bedtime. Take on an empty or nearly empty stomach for optimal absorption. Avoid taking immediately after heavy meals.
- **Typical Duration**: Initial course: 2–4 weeks, then reassess. Many patients experience improvement within the first week, but the underlying Blood deficiency requires sustained treatment for lasting results. Chronic insomnia: 4–8 weeks may be required to fully address the root pattern. Formula modifications may be needed during this period as the pattern evolves with treatment. The formula is non-addictive and does not produce rebound insomnia upon discontinuation, making it suitable for extended courses when indicated.
- **Dietary Advice**: Avoid
Coffee, strong black or green tea, and other caffeine sources (generate Heat and agitate the spirit)
Alcohol (generates Damp-Heat and disturbs sleep architecture)
Spicy, hot, and heavily seasoned dishes (generate Heat and deplete Yin)
Rich, greasy, or heavy meals in the evening (burden the Stomach and generate turbid Qi)
Raw, cold foods if digestion is weak (impair Spleen transformation)
Chocolate and cocoa (stimulating and Heat-generating)
Favor
Easily digestible, mildly nourishing foods: congee (zhou), cooked vegetables, light soups
Spirit-calming and Blood-nourishing foods: lotus seed soup (lian zi tang), longan fruit (long yan rou), red dates (da zao), millet porridge (xiao mi zhou)
Yin-nourishing foods: lily bulb (bai he), mulberries (sang shen), black sesame, goji berries
Light, early evening meals—ideally 3–4 hours before bedtime
